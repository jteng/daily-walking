## 这卷书在讲什么

哥林多前书是保罗写给一个问题很多的教会的信。这个教会有分党结派、有骄傲自大、有乱伦的丑事、有弟兄彼此告状、有人对婚姻和饮食的困惑、有聚会中的混乱，甚至有人不信死人复活。读这卷书，我们会发现初期教会并不是一个理想中的完美教会；它和今天的教会一样，充满了罪人。

可是保罗对这个教会的称呼是：「在基督耶稣里成圣、蒙召作圣徒的」（1:2）。他不是从一套规矩来解决问题，而是一次又一次把他们带回到福音的中心：「十字架的道理，在那灭亡的人为愚拙；在我们得救的人，却为神的大能。」（1:18）面对每一个难题，保罗都问同一个问题：在钉十字架又复活的基督面前，我们应当怎样生活？

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 作者。** 信的开头说：「奉神旨意，蒙召作耶稣基督使徒的保罗，同兄弟所提尼」（1:1）。保罗是这封信的作者，这一点在教会历史中几乎没有争议。

**2. 哥林多城。** 哥林多位于希腊南部的地峡上，控制着两个港口，是东西方贸易的要道。原来的希腊城在主前146年被罗马摧毁，主前44年由凯撒重建为罗马殖民城。保罗的时代，这是一座富裕、繁忙、移民众多、追求成功和地位的城市，像其他港口城市一样，也有道德放纵的名声。城里有许多神庙，每两年还举办一次地峡运动会（参9:24-27）。

**3. 教会的建立。** 保罗在第二次宣教旅程中来到哥林多（徒18:1），「在那里住了一年零六个月，将神的道教训他们」（徒18:11），大约是主后50-52年。这个年代的重要依据是迦流作亚该亚方伯的时间（徒18:12）：在德尔斐出土的一块碑文显示，迦流大约在主后51-52年任职。他在那里遇见了亚居拉和百基拉，起初在会堂传道，后来在提多·犹士都的家中聚会；管会堂的基利司布和全家都信了主（徒18:2-8）。教会里有犹太人，也有大量外邦人；有少数有地位的人，更多的是普通人和奴隶（1:26；7:21）。

**4. 写信的时间和原因。** 保罗写这封信时住在以弗所（16:8），大约是主后53-55年。他写信的原因至少有三个：一，革来氏家里的人告诉他教会中有纷争（1:11）；二，哥林多教会写信问了他一些问题（7:1，「论到你们信上所提的事」）；三，司提反（不是使徒行传6章的司提反）、福徒拿都、亚该古从哥林多来见他（16:17）。这封信也不是保罗写给他们的第一封信（5:9提到「我先前写信给你们」），只是先前那封信没有保存下来。

## 钥匙经文：1:18

**1. 「十字架的道理」。** 哥林多人看重口才、智慧和地位，而在罗马世界，十字架是最羞耻的刑罚，主要用在奴隶、叛乱者和最低贱的罪犯身上。保罗却说，他所传的中心，正是这个看起来愚拙、软弱的十字架。

**2. 「在那灭亡的人为愚拙」。** 同一个信息，有人听了觉得可笑，有人听了得救。区别不在信息本身，而在听的人是在「灭亡」中，还是在「得救」中；而归根结底，是因为神的呼召：「在那蒙召的……基督总为神的能力，神的智慧」（1:24；参1:26-29）。

**3. 「却为神的大能」。** 福音不是一套更高明的哲学，而是神拯救人的能力。保罗后来说：「我曾定了主意，在你们中间不知道别的，只知道耶稣基督并他钉十字架。」（2:2）

## 全书结构

哥林多前书可以大致分成五个部分：
- **教会的分裂与十字架的智慧**（1–4章）：教会中有人说「我是属保罗的」，有人说「我是属亚波罗的」；保罗用十字架的「愚拙」，拆毁人的骄傲。
- **道德上的问题**（5–6章）：教会容忍乱伦的事，弟兄彼此告到外邦人的法庭，有人以为身体的事无关紧要；保罗说：「你们的身子就是圣灵的殿」（6:19）。
- **回答信中的问题与其他传闻**（7–14章）：婚姻与独身（7章）；祭偶像之物（8–10章）；聚会中的蒙头、圣餐和属灵的恩赐（11–14章；11:17-34是回应他所听见的事，11:18），中间是著名的爱的篇章（13章）。
- **复活**（15章）：「基督照圣经所说，为我们的罪死了，而且埋葬了；又照圣经所说，第三天复活了」（15:3-4）；信徒的身体也要复活。
- **捐项、计划与问安**（16章）。

在第7章以后，保罗常用「论到……」（希腊文 *peri de*）开始一个新的话题（7:1，25；8:1；12:1；16:1，12），很可能是在回答哥林多人的来信；但也有学者认为这只是引出新题目的方式，只有7:1明确提到他们的信。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 留意问题背后的骄傲。** 哥林多教会的许多问题，表面上各不相同，根源却常常是同一个：自以为有智慧、有知识、有属灵的恩赐，于是看不起别人。保罗一再说：「知识是叫人自高自大，惟有爱心能造就人。」（8:1）

**2. 留意保罗怎样回到福音。** 无论是分党、淫乱、告状、饮食还是聚会的问题，保罗都把它连到十字架、复活和与基督联合上。这卷书教我们，福音不只是让人得救的入门道理，也是处理教会一切问题的根基。

**3. 分辨原则和处境。** 有些问题（例如蒙头、祭偶像之物）和当时的文化处境关系很深，读的时候要先明白哥林多当时的情况，再找出背后不变的原则。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们各人说：『我是属保罗的』；『我是属亚波罗的』；『我是属矶法的』；『我是属基督的』。」（1:12）今天的教会，也常常有这样的声音：我是某某牧师的追随者，我是某个宗派、某个网络讲员、某个属灵运动的人。我是否也把自己的身份，建立在跟随了一位「对的」属灵领袖、或者属于一个「更纯正」的圈子上，因而暗暗看不起别人？保罗问：「基督是分开的吗？保罗为你们钉了十字架吗？」（1:13）只有一位为我钉了十字架。在开始读哥林多前书的时候，我能不能求神叫我放下属灵上的门户之见，重新单单看见「耶稣基督并他钉十字架」？

## What This Book Is About

1 Corinthians is Paul's letter to a church with many problems. It had factions and arrogance, a case of incest, believers suing one another, confusion about marriage and food, disorder in worship, and even people who denied the resurrection of the dead. Reading it, we find that the early church was not an ideal, perfect church; like the church today, it was full of sinners.

Yet Paul addresses this church as "those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints" (1:2). He doesn't solve its problems with a set of rules; again and again he brings them back to the center of the gospel: "For the word of the cross is folly to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God" (1:18). Facing every problem, Paul asks the same question: how should we live before the crucified and risen Christ?

## Author and Setting

**1. Author.** The letter opens, "Paul, called by the will of God to be an apostle of Christ Jesus, and our brother Sosthenes" (1:1). Paul's authorship has been almost undisputed in the history of the church.

**2. The city of Corinth.** Corinth stood on the isthmus of southern Greece, controlling two ports, a key route for trade between east and west. The old Greek city was destroyed by Rome in 146 BC and refounded by Julius Caesar as a Roman colony in 44 BC. In Paul's day it was a wealthy, busy city full of migrants, driven by success and status, and, like other port cities, it had a reputation for moral license. It had many temples and hosted the Isthmian Games every two years (cf. 9:24–27).

**3. The founding of the church.** Paul came to Corinth on his second missionary journey (Acts 18:1), "and he stayed a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them" (Acts 18:11), around AD 50–52. The key anchor for this date is Gallio's term as proconsul of Achaia (Acts 18:12); an inscription found at Delphi places it around AD 51–52. There he met Aquila and Priscilla, preached first in the synagogue, and later met in the house of Titius Justus; Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, believed with his whole household (Acts 18:2–8). The church included Jews and many Gentiles; a few people of standing, and many more ordinary people and slaves (1:26; 7:21).

**4. Date and occasion.** Paul wrote from Ephesus (16:8), around AD 53–55. He had at least three reasons: people from Chloe's household reported quarrels in the church (1:11); the Corinthians had written him a letter with questions (7:1, "Now concerning the matters about which you wrote"); and Stephanas, Fortunatus and Achaicus had come to him from Corinth (16:17). Nor was this Paul's first letter to them (5:9 mentions "I wrote to you in my letter"), though that earlier letter has not survived.

## Key Verse: 1:18

**1. "The word of the cross."** The Corinthians prized eloquence, wisdom and status, and in the Roman world crucifixion was the most shameful of punishments, used mainly for slaves, rebels and the lowest criminals. Yet Paul says the center of his message is this cross that looks foolish and weak.

**2. "Folly to those who are perishing."** The same message makes some people laugh and saves others. The difference lies not in the message but in whether the hearer is "perishing" or "being saved," and behind that lies God's call: "to those who are called... Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God" (1:24; cf. 1:26–29).

**3. "The power of God."** The gospel is not a more sophisticated philosophy but God's power to save. Later Paul says, "For I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified" (2:2).

## Structure

1 Corinthians falls broadly into five parts:
- **Divisions and the wisdom of the cross** (chs. 1–4): some say "I follow Paul," others "I follow Apollos"; Paul uses the "folly" of the cross to tear down human pride.
- **Moral problems** (chs. 5–6): the church tolerates incest, believers take each other to pagan courts, and some think what is done with the body doesn't matter; Paul says, "your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit" (6:19).
- **Answers to their letter and to reports** (chs. 7–14): marriage and singleness (ch. 7); food offered to idols (chs. 8–10); head coverings, the Lord's Supper and spiritual gifts in worship (chs. 11–14; 11:17–34 responds to what Paul has heard, 11:18), with the famous "love chapter" at the center (ch. 13).
- **The resurrection** (ch. 15): "that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures" (15:3–4); believers' bodies too will be raised.
- **The collection, plans and greetings** (ch. 16).

From chapter 7 on, Paul often begins a new topic with "Now concerning..." (Greek *peri de*; 7:1, 25; 8:1; 12:1; 16:1, 12), probably answering the Corinthians' letter, though some scholars argue the phrase simply introduces a new topic, and only 7:1 explicitly cites their letter.

## How to Read This Book

**1. Watch for the pride behind the problems.** The Corinthian church's problems look different on the surface, but their root is often the same: thinking themselves wise, knowledgeable or spiritually gifted, and so looking down on others. Paul says again and again, "This 'knowledge' puffs up, but love builds up" (8:1).

**2. Watch how Paul returns to the gospel.** Whether the issue is factions, sexual immorality, lawsuits, food or worship, Paul connects it to the cross, the resurrection and union with Christ. The book teaches us that the gospel is not only the entry point of salvation but the foundation for dealing with every problem in the church.

**3. Distinguish principle from situation.** Some issues (such as head coverings and food offered to idols) are closely bound up with the culture of the time; read them by first understanding the situation in Corinth, and then finding the unchanging principle behind it.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Each one of you says, 'I follow Paul,' or 'I follow Apollos,' or 'I follow Cephas,' or 'I follow Christ'" (1:12). The church today often hears the same voices: I follow this pastor, I belong to that denomination, this online preacher, that spiritual movement. Do I build my identity on having followed the "right" spiritual leader, or belonging to a "purer" circle, and so quietly look down on others? Paul asks, "Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you?" (1:13). Only one was crucified for me. As I begin reading 1 Corinthians, can I ask God to help me lay down my spiritual partisanship and see afresh "Jesus Christ and him crucified"?
