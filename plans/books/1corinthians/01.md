## 本章脉络

第一章有三段。**问安与感恩**（1-9节）：保罗称这个问题重重的教会为「在基督耶稣里成圣、蒙召作圣徒的」，并且为他们感谢神，因为神「必坚固你们到底」。**分党的问题**（10-17节）：「你们各人说：『我是属保罗的』；『我是属亚波罗的』……」保罗问：「基督是分开的吗？保罗为你们钉了十字架吗？」**十字架的愚拙**（18-31节）：「十字架的道理，在那灭亡的人为愚拙；在我们得救的人，却为神的大能。」神拣选了世上愚拙的、软弱的、卑贱的，「使一切有血气的，在神面前一个也不能自夸」。

保罗解决分党问题的方法，不是先去调解各派，而是先把他们带回十字架面前。在十字架前，所有人的骄傲都被拆毁了。

## 深读 1:1-9 一个问题重重的「圣徒」教会

**1. 蒙召作圣徒的。** 「写信给在哥林多神的教会，就是在基督耶稣里成圣、蒙召作圣徒的，以及所有在各处求告我主耶稣基督之名的人。」（1:2）读完这卷书，我们会知道这个教会有多少问题；可是保罗一开头就说，他们是「神的教会」，是「圣徒」。这里的「成圣」首先是地位上的成圣：神已经把他们分别出来，归他自己。正因为他们已经是圣徒，保罗才要求他们活得像圣徒。

**2. 感谢神。** 「我常为你们感谢我的神，因神在基督耶稣里所赐给你们的恩惠；又因你们在他里面凡事富足，口才、知识都全备。」（1:4-5）保罗还没有责备，先感谢。值得留意的是，他感谢的「口才、知识」和「恩赐」（1:7），后来正是哥林多人骄傲的地方。恩赐本身是神的恩典；问题在于人把恩赐当作自己的功劳。

**3. 神是信实的。** 「他也必坚固你们到底，叫你们在我们主耶稣基督的日子无可责备。神是信实的，你们原是被他所召，好与他儿子－我们的主耶稣基督一同得分。」（1:8-9）保罗对这个教会有信心，不是因为他们表现得好，而是因为「神是信实的」。「一同得分」原文是「相交」：信徒蒙召，是要与基督相交；这也是他们彼此相交的根基。

> 要点：神称一个满是问题的教会为「圣徒」，因为他们的身份是神在基督里所赐的。保罗对他们有信心，不是因为他们的表现，而是因为神是信实的，必坚固他们到底。

## 深读 1:10-17 基督是分开的吗？

**1. 分党。** 「弟兄们，我藉我们主耶稣基督的名劝你们都说一样的话。你们中间也不可分党，只要一心一意，彼此相合。」（1:10）「分党」原文是「撕裂」，就像衣服被撕开。「彼此相合」原文用来指修补渔网，或者把脱臼的骨头接回去。

**2. 四个口号。** 「我的意思就是你们各人说：『我是属保罗的』；『我是属亚波罗的』；『我是属矶法的』；『我是属基督的』。」（1:12）保罗是哥林多教会的创立者；亚波罗是一位很有口才的传道人（徒18:24-28）；矶法就是彼得。至于说「我是属基督的」这一派，解经家有不同的看法：有人认为这是另一个自以为更属灵、不需要人间领袖的派别；也有人认为这是保罗自己的回答，好像在说：「你们都应该这样说！」从下一节「基督是分开的吗？」来看，多数解经家采取前一种理解：连「属基督」也可以变成一个看不起别人的口号。

**3. 三个问题。** 「基督是分开的吗？保罗为你们钉了十字架吗？你们是奉保罗的名受了洗吗？」（1:13）答案显然都是否定的。教会只有一位主，只有一位为我们钉十字架的救主，信徒也只是奉他的名受洗。人间的领袖再好，也只是仆人。

**4. 传福音，不用智慧的言语。** 「基督差遣我，原不是为施洗，乃是为传福音，并不用智慧的言语，免得基督的十字架落了空。」（1:17）保罗不是轻看洗礼（他也给一些人施过洗，1:14-16），而是说他的主要使命是传福音。「智慧的言语」很可能主要指当时希腊修辞学那种注重技巧、取悦听众的演说，也包括世人智慧的内容（1:20-21）。如果人被打动，是因为讲员的口才，十字架的大能就「落了空」。

> 要点：教会的分裂，常常是把人间的领袖放在基督的位置上。只有一位为我们钉了十字架；在他面前，所有的属灵领袖都只是仆人。

## 深读 1:18-25 神的愚拙总比人智慧

**1. 两种听众。** 「因为十字架的道理，在那灭亡的人为愚拙；在我们得救的人，却为神的大能。」（1:18）原文的「灭亡的」和「得救的」，都表示正在进行的事：人不是到死后才定局，现在就已经走在其中一条路上，不是正在灭亡，就是正在得救。

**2. 神废弃人的智慧。** 「就如经上所记：我要灭绝智慧人的智慧，废弃聪明人的聪明。」（1:19；引赛29:14）以赛亚原来说的是犹大的领袖想靠自己的谋略，不靠神。「智慧人在哪里？文士在哪里？这世上的辩士在哪里？」（1:20）无论是希腊的哲学家、犹太的文士，还是能言善辩的演说家，都不能凭自己的智慧认识神。

**3. 神迹与智慧。** 「犹太人是要神迹，希腊人是求智慧，我们却是传钉在十字架的基督，在犹太人为绊脚石，在外邦人为愚拙。」（1:22-23）在犹太人看来，一个被挂在木头上的人是受了神的咒诅（申21:23），怎么可能是弥赛亚？保罗在别处给了答案：基督确实受了咒诅，但那咒诅不是他自己的，而是我们的：「基督既为我们受了咒诅，就赎出我们脱离律法的咒诅」（加3:13）。在希腊人看来，一个被钉死的犹太工匠，怎么可能是神？可是保罗说，神偏偏用世人看为最软弱、最羞耻的方式拯救人。

**4. 神的能力，神的智慧。** 「但在那蒙召的，无论是犹太人、希腊人，基督总为神的能力，神的智慧。因神的愚拙总比人智慧，神的软弱总比人强壮。」（1:24-25）十字架看起来是失败，其实是胜利；看起来是愚拙，其实是神最深的智慧：在那里，神的公义和慈爱同时显明（参罗3:25-26）。

> 要点：十字架在人看来是愚拙和软弱，却正是神的能力和智慧。人不能凭自己的聪明找到神；神却用人看为最羞耻的十字架，拯救一切相信的人。

## 深读 1:26-31 夸口的，当指着主夸口

**1. 看看你们自己。** 「弟兄们哪，可见你们蒙召的，按着肉体有智慧的不多，有能力的不多，有尊贵的也不多。」（1:26）保罗请哥林多人看看他们自己的教会：大多数人在社会上没有地位、没有学问、没有权势。「不多」不是「没有」：教会里也有像基利司布、该犹、以拉都这样有地位的人（1:14；徒18:8；罗16:23）。

**2. 神的拣选。** 「神却拣选了世上愚拙的，叫有智慧的羞愧；又拣选了世上软弱的，叫那强壮的羞愧。神也拣选了世上卑贱的，被人厌恶的，以及那无有的，为要废掉那有的。」（1:27-28）「拣选」这个词在这两节中出现三次。神的做法和世界的价值观正好相反。

**3. 不能自夸。** 「使一切有血气的，在神面前一个也不能自夸。」（1:29）这是整段的目的。如果神拣选的都是聪明、有能力、有地位的人，人就会以为是自己的条件使他蒙拣选。

**4. 基督是我们的一切。** 「但你们得在基督耶稣里是本乎神，神又使他成为我们的智慧、公义、圣洁、救赎。」（1:30）哥林多人在追求智慧，保罗说，基督就是我们的智慧。原文的结构是「从神而来的智慧」，后面三项在原文中紧紧连成一组，所以许多解经家认为后三项是在解释这智慧：公义（神所赐、算为我们的义，参腓3:9）、圣洁（成圣）、救赎（藉基督的血得赎，并在末日身体得赎，参罗3:24；8:23）。我们所需要的一切，都在基督里。

**5. 指着主夸口。** 「如经上所记：『夸口的，当指着主夸口。』」（1:31；引耶9:23-24）耶利米说，智慧人不要因他的智慧夸口，勇士不要因他的勇力夸口，财主不要因他的财物夸口；夸口的却要因认识耶和华而夸口。这正对应1:26所说有智慧的、有能力的、有尊贵的人；耶利米说的是「耶和华」，保罗在这里却用来指主基督。

> 要点：神拣选世上愚拙、软弱、卑贱的人，叫人在他面前不能自夸。基督就是我们的智慧、公义、圣洁、救赎；我们唯一可夸的，就是主。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们蒙召的，按着肉体有智慧的不多，有能力的不多，有尊贵的也不多。」华人社会非常看重学历、出身、职位：哪所大学毕业、在哪家公司工作、住在哪里。这些标准也常常不知不觉地进入教会：选同工、请人上台分享时，我们常常先看学位和职位，自己也暗暗用这些来衡量自己的价值；在教会里，还有属灵的资历：读过神学、信主多年、在教会有职分。骄傲和自卑其实出于同一个根源：用世界的尺量自己。如果我有这些，我是否因此觉得自己更有分量？如果我没有这些，我是否觉得自己在神的家里低人一等？保罗说，神正是拣选了世上「无有的」，叫我们在他面前一个也不能自夸；而基督自己，就是我们的智慧、公义、圣洁、救赎。今天，我能不能把我最引以为傲（或最引以为耻）的那一样放下，对神说：「我唯一可夸的，就是你」？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 has three parts. **Greeting and thanksgiving** (vv. 1–9): Paul calls this troubled church "those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints," and thanks God for them, because God "will sustain you to the end." **The problem of factions** (vv. 10–17): "Each one of you says, 'I follow Paul,' or 'I follow Apollos'..." Paul asks, "Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you?" **The folly of the cross** (vv. 18–31): "For the word of the cross is folly to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God." God chose what is foolish, weak and low in the world, "so that no human being might boast in the presence of God."

Paul's way of dealing with factions is not first to mediate between the parties, but to bring them all back before the cross. At the cross, every human pride is torn down.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–9 A Troubled Church of "Saints"

**1. Called to be saints.** "To the church of God that is in Corinth, to those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints together with all those who in every place call upon the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, both their Lord and ours" (1:2). By the end of the letter we will know how many problems this church had; yet Paul begins by calling them "the church of God" and "saints." "Sanctified" here is first of all a status: God has set them apart for himself. Because they already are saints, Paul calls them to live like saints.

**2. Thanking God.** "I give thanks to my God always for you because of the grace of God that was given you in Christ Jesus, that in every way you were enriched in him in all speech and all knowledge" (1:4–5). Before any rebuke, Paul gives thanks. Interestingly, the "speech," "knowledge" and "spiritual gift" (1:7) he thanks God for are exactly where the Corinthians later grew proud. Gifts themselves are God's grace; the problem is treating them as one's own achievement.

**3. God is faithful.** "Who will sustain you to the end, guiltless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ. God is faithful, by whom you were called into the fellowship of his Son, Jesus Christ our Lord" (1:8–9). Paul is confident about this church not because they were doing well, but because "God is faithful." Believers are called into fellowship with Christ, and this is the ground of their fellowship with one another.

> The point: God calls a church full of problems "saints," because their identity is God's gift in Christ. Paul is confident about them not because of their performance, but because God is faithful and will sustain them to the end.

## Going Deeper: 1:10–17 Is Christ Divided?

**1. Divisions.** "I appeal to you, brothers, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you agree and that there be no divisions among you, but that you be united in the same mind and the same judgment" (1:10). "Divisions" translates a word for a tear, like a rip in a garment. "United" translates a word used for mending nets or setting a dislocated bone.

**2. Four slogans.** "What I mean is that each one of you says, 'I follow Paul,' or 'I follow Apollos,' or 'I follow Cephas,' or 'I follow Christ'" (1:12). Paul founded the church; Apollos was an eloquent preacher (Acts 18:24–28); Cephas is Peter. Interpreters differ about the "I follow Christ" group: some think it was another faction, one that considered itself more spiritual and above human leaders; others think it is Paul's own answer, as if to say, "That is what you should all be saying!" The next question, "Is Christ divided?", makes the first reading more widely accepted: even "I follow Christ" can become a slogan for looking down on others.

**3. Three questions.** "Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?" (1:13). The answer to all three is "no." The church has one Lord, one Savior crucified for us, and believers are baptized only in his name. However good human leaders are, they are only servants.

**4. Preaching, not with eloquent wisdom.** "For Christ did not send me to baptize but to preach the gospel, and not with words of eloquent wisdom, lest the cross of Christ be emptied of its power" (1:17). Paul isn't belittling baptism (he baptized some, 1:14–16); he is saying his main commission was to preach the gospel. "Eloquent wisdom" most likely refers mainly to the showy rhetoric of the day, which prized technique and pleasing the audience, though it also includes the content of worldly wisdom (1:20–21). If people are moved by the speaker's eloquence, the power of the cross is "emptied."

> The point: Church divisions often come from putting human leaders in Christ's place. Only one was crucified for us; before him, every spiritual leader is only a servant.

## Going Deeper: 1:18–25 The Foolishness of God Is Wiser than Men

**1. Two kinds of hearers.** "For the word of the cross is folly to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is the power of God" (1:18). "Perishing" and "being saved" are both present tense: people don't only take a direction after death; they are already on one road or the other now.

**2. God thwarts human wisdom.** "For it is written, 'I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and the discernment of the discerning I will thwart'" (1:19, quoting Isa 29:14). Isaiah was speaking of Judah's leaders, who trusted their own plans instead of God. "Where is the one who is wise? Where is the scribe? Where is the debater of this age?" (1:20). Neither Greek philosopher, nor Jewish scholar of the law, nor skilled orator can know God by his own wisdom.

**3. Signs and wisdom.** "For Jews demand signs and Greeks seek wisdom, but we preach Christ crucified, a stumbling block to Jews and folly to Gentiles" (1:22–23). To Jews, a man hanged on a tree was under God's curse (Deut 21:23); how could he be the Messiah? Paul gives the answer elsewhere: Christ did bear the curse, not his own but ours: "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us" (Gal 3:13). To Greeks, how could a crucified Jewish craftsman be divine? Yet Paul says God chose to save the world precisely through what people regard as the weakest and most shameful way.

**4. The power of God and the wisdom of God.** "But to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God. For the foolishness of God is wiser than men, and the weakness of God is stronger than men" (1:24–25). The cross looks like defeat but is victory; it looks foolish but is God's deepest wisdom: there God's justice and love are revealed together (cf. Rom 3:25–26).

> The point: The cross looks foolish and weak to people, yet it is the power and wisdom of God. No one can find God by their own cleverness; God saves all who believe through the cross people regard as most shameful.

## Going Deeper: 1:26–31 Let the One Who Boasts, Boast in the Lord

**1. Consider your calling.** "For consider your calling, brothers: not many of you were wise according to worldly standards, not many were powerful, not many were of noble birth" (1:26). Paul invites the Corinthians to look at their own church: most had no standing, learning or power in society. "Not many" is not "none": the church included people of standing such as Crispus, Gaius and Erastus (1:14; Acts 18:8; Rom 16:23).

**2. God's choice.** "But God chose what is foolish in the world to shame the wise; God chose what is weak in the world to shame the strong; God chose what is low and despised in the world, even things that are not, to bring to nothing things that are" (1:27–28). "Chose" appears three times in these two verses. God's way runs exactly opposite to the world's values.

**3. No boasting.** "So that no human being might boast in the presence of God" (1:29). This is the purpose of the whole passage. If God chose only the clever, the powerful and the well-born, people would think their own qualifications had earned his choice.

**4. Christ is everything to us.** "He is the source of your life in Christ Jesus, whom God made our wisdom and our righteousness and sanctification and redemption" (1:30). The Corinthians were chasing wisdom; Paul says Christ is our wisdom. The Greek reads "wisdom from God," and then joins the last three terms closely, so many interpreters (and the NIV) take them as explaining that wisdom, though the ESV lists all four side by side: righteousness (God's righteousness credited to us, cf. Phil 3:9), sanctification, and redemption (bought by Christ's blood and completed in the body's redemption at the last day, cf. Rom 3:24; 8:23). Everything we need is in Christ.

**5. Boast in the Lord.** "Therefore, as it is written, 'Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord'" (1:31, quoting Jer 9:23–24). Jeremiah said: let not the wise boast in wisdom, nor the mighty in might, nor the rich in riches; but let the one who boasts boast in knowing the LORD. This mirrors the wise, powerful and noble of 1:26; and "the LORD" of Jeremiah is here applied to the Lord Christ.

> The point: God chose the foolish, weak and low of this world so that no one can boast before him. Christ is our wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption; our only boast is the Lord.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Not many of you were wise according to worldly standards, not many were powerful, not many were of noble birth." Chinese culture, like many others, places great weight on education, family background and position: which university, which company, which neighborhood. These measures often slip into the church unnoticed: when choosing co-workers or asking someone to share, we often look first at degrees and titles, and quietly measure our own worth by them; and in church there are spiritual credentials too: seminary training, years as a believer, a title in the church. Pride and shame spring from the same root: measuring ourselves by the world's yardstick. If I have these things, do I feel I carry more weight? If I don't, do I feel I am second-class in God's family? Paul says God chose the very "things that are not," so that none of us can boast before him; and Christ himself is our wisdom, righteousness, sanctification and redemption. Today, can I lay down the thing I am proudest of (or most ashamed of) and say to God, "My only boast is you"?
