## 本章脉络

第二章接着第一章讲「十字架的道理」，分两段。**保罗的传道方式**（1-5节）：保罗到哥林多时，「并没有用高言大智」，而是「定了主意，在你们中间不知道别的，只知道耶稣基督并他钉十字架」；他自己「又软弱，又惧怕又甚战兢」，为要叫他们的信「不在乎人的智慧，只在乎神的大能」。**圣灵所启示的智慧**（6-16节）：福音并不是没有智慧，而是「神奥秘的智慧」，世上有权有位的人不明白，「他们若知道，就不把荣耀的主钉在十字架上了」；这智慧是「神藉着圣灵向我们显明了」，所以「属血气的人不领会神圣灵的事」，「但我们是有基督的心了」。

第一章说，十字架在世人看来是愚拙；第二章说，十字架其实是神最深的智慧，只是这智慧要靠圣灵才能明白。

## 深读 2:1-5 软弱、惧怕、甚战兢

**1. 没有用高言大智。** 「弟兄们，从前我到你们那里去，并没有用高言大智对你们宣传神的奥秘。」（2:1）在哥林多这样的城市，演说家很受欢迎，人们评价一位讲员，常常看他的口才和台风。保罗却刻意不用这种方式。

**2. 定了主意。** 「因为我曾定了主意，在你们中间不知道别的，只知道耶稣基督并他钉十字架。」（2:2）这不是说保罗只讲十字架一个题目，他在这封信里讲了婚姻、饮食、聚会、复活等许多事。意思是：十字架是他一切信息的中心和标准。「他钉十字架」用的是完成时态分词，许多解经家认为这表示钉十字架不只是过去的事件，其意义持续到如今：复活的主仍是那位被钉的主（参加3:1）。有人认为，保罗在雅典用哲学的方式讲道（徒17:22-31）效果有限，所以来到哥林多时「定了主意」；也有人认为，雅典的讲道本身就以复活的基督为中心，保罗在哥林多的决定，主要是针对哥林多人对口才的崇拜。

**3. 又软弱，又惧怕。** 「我在你们那里，又软弱，又惧怕又甚战兢。」（2:3）伟大的使徒也会害怕。主曾在哥林多的异象中对他说：「不要怕，只管讲，不要闭口，有我与你同在。」（徒18:9-10）保罗的软弱，不是他的缺点被神勉强使用，而是神刻意要显明能力在他，不在人。

**4. 圣灵和大能的明证。** 「我说的话、讲的道，不是用智慧委婉的言语，乃是用圣灵和大能的明证，叫你们的信不在乎人的智慧，只在乎神的大能。」（2:4-5）如果人信主是因为讲员的口才，他的信心就建立在人身上；有一天遇到口才更好的人，他也可能被带走。只有建立在神大能上的信心，才站立得住。

> 要点：保罗刻意不用高言大智，只传钉十字架的基督，并且在软弱中传。这样，人的信心就不建立在讲员身上，而是建立在神的大能上。

## 深读 2:6-10 神奥秘的智慧

**1. 在完全的人中讲智慧。** 「然而，在完全的人中，我们也讲智慧。但不是这世上的智慧，也不是这世上有权有位、将要败亡之人的智慧。」（2:6）「完全的人」原文是「成熟的人」。保罗不是说福音只给少数精英，而是说，福音本身有深刻的智慧，那些被圣灵带领、在属灵上成熟的人，能够领会。

**2. 隐藏的智慧。** 「我们讲的，乃是从前所隐藏、神奥秘的智慧，就是神在万世以前预定使我们得荣耀的。」（2:7）十字架不是神临时想出来的补救办法，而是「在万世以前」就定好的计划。

**3. 荣耀的主被钉。** 「这智慧世上有权有位的人没有一个知道的，他们若知道，就不把荣耀的主钉在十字架上了。」（2:8）「世上有权有位的人」，最直接是指彼拉多、希律和犹太的领袖；有些解经家认为也包括在他们背后的属灵权势（参西2:15）。「荣耀的主」被钉，这是一个惊人的说法：被钉在十字架上的，正是那位荣耀的神子。

**4. 眼睛未曾看见。** 「如经上所记：神为爱他的人所预备的是眼睛未曾看见，耳朵未曾听见，人心也未曾想到的。只有神藉着圣灵向我们显明了。」（2:9-10；参赛64:4）这节经文常被用来讲天堂的美好，这当然也包括在内。可是从上下文看，保罗的重点是：神在基督里所成就的救恩，是人凭自己永远想不到的，可是如今神「藉着圣灵向我们显明了」。

> 要点：十字架不是失败，而是神在万世以前就定好的奥秘智慧。人凭自己永远想不到，世上的掌权者也看不透；可是神已经藉着圣灵向我们显明了。

## 深读 2:10-16 圣灵与属灵的人

**1. 圣灵参透万事。** 「因为圣灵参透万事，就是神深奥的事也参透了。除了在人里头的灵，谁知道人的事？像这样，除了神的灵，也没有人知道神的事。」（2:10-11）只有一个人自己知道自己心里在想什么；同样，只有神的灵知道神的心意。我们能认识神，是因为神的灵向我们启示了他自己。只有神自己才能参透「神深奥的事」，这也显明圣灵完全的神性。

**2. 从神来的灵。** 「我们所领受的，并不是世上的灵，乃是从神来的灵，叫我们能知道神开恩赐给我们的事。」（2:12）圣灵不只是启示神的事，也叫我们「知道」神白白赐给我们的恩典。

**3. 属血气的人。** 「然而，属血气的人不领会神圣灵的事，反倒以为愚拙，并且不能知道，因为这些事惟有属灵的人才能看透。」（2:14）「属血气的人」指还没有圣灵、只凭天然本性的人。问题不在于他不够聪明，而在于他没有属灵的眼光；就像一个色盲的人，再聪明也看不出某些颜色。这也说明为什么有些很有学问的人，读圣经却觉得愚拙。

**4. 属灵的人。** 「属灵的人能看透万事，却没有一人能看透了他。」（2:15）「属灵的人」在这里不是指一群特别属灵的精英，而是指一切有圣灵的信徒（参罗8:9）。哥林多人以「属灵」自夸，保罗下一章却要说，他们其实还是「属肉体的」（3:1）。

**5. 有基督的心。** 「谁曾知道主的心去教导他呢？但我们是有基督的心了。」（2:16；引赛40:13）以赛亚书40:13的希伯来文是「耶和华的灵」（和合本作「心」，小字「或作：灵」），保罗跟随希腊文旧约译作「主的心」。他在回答「谁曾知道主的心」时说「我们是有基督的心了」：许多解经家认为，这表示基督分享并启示耶和华的心意，等于把基督放在旧约耶和华的位置上。「有基督的心」不是说我们知道神的一切，而是说，藉着圣灵，我们开始用基督的眼光看事情，包括用基督的眼光看十字架。

> 要点：我们能认识神，是因为神的灵向我们启示了他自己。没有圣灵，再聪明的人也看不透十字架的智慧；有了圣灵，我们就开始有基督的心。

## 默想

**默想：** 「属血气的人不领会神圣灵的事，反倒以为愚拙。」我读圣经的时候，是把它当作一本可以靠自己的聪明读懂的书，还是一本需要圣灵开启的书？我是否常常匆匆读完一段，就以为已经明白了？或者，我是否因为读不懂某些地方，就渐渐不读了？保罗说，「只有神藉着圣灵向我们显明了」。明白圣经不是我们自己的成就，而是神的恩赐。今天读经以前，我能不能先停一下，用诗篇119:18的话向神祷告：「求你开我的眼睛，使我看出你律法中的奇妙」？然后带着一颗渴慕的心，不只是要得着知识，更是要在这段经文中，重新看见那位钉十字架的「荣耀的主」。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 continues chapter 1's theme of "the word of the cross," in two parts. **Paul's way of preaching** (vv. 1–5): when Paul came to Corinth he did not come "with lofty speech or wisdom," but "decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified"; he himself was "in weakness and in fear and much trembling," so that their faith "might not rest in the wisdom of men but in the power of God." **Wisdom revealed by the Spirit** (vv. 6–16): the gospel is not without wisdom; it is "a secret and hidden wisdom of God," which the rulers of this age did not understand, "for if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory"; "these things God has revealed to us through the Spirit," so "the natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God," "but we have the mind of Christ."

Chapter 1 said the cross looks foolish to the world; chapter 2 says the cross is in fact God's deepest wisdom, a wisdom understood only by the Spirit.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–5 Weakness, Fear and Much Trembling

**1. Not with lofty speech.** "And I, when I came to you, brothers, did not come proclaiming to you the testimony of God with lofty speech or wisdom" (2:1). In a city like Corinth, orators were popular, and people often judged a speaker by his eloquence and stage presence. Paul deliberately refused that approach.

**2. A decision.** "For I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified" (2:2). This doesn't mean Paul preached on only one topic; in this letter he addresses marriage, food, worship, the resurrection and much else. It means the cross was the center and measure of all his message. "Him crucified" uses a perfect participle, which many take to indicate that the crucifixion's significance continues: the risen Lord remains the crucified one (cf. Gal 3:1). Some think that Paul's philosophical approach in Athens (Acts 17:22–31) had limited results, so he "decided" on a different approach in Corinth; others think the Athens sermon itself centered on the risen Christ, and that Paul's decision in Corinth was aimed mainly at the Corinthians' love of eloquence.

**3. Weakness and fear.** "And I was with you in weakness and in fear and much trembling" (2:3). The great apostle could be afraid. In Corinth the Lord told him in a vision, "Do not be afraid, but go on speaking and do not be silent, for I am with you" (Acts 18:9–10). Paul's weakness was not a flaw God reluctantly put up with, but something God used on purpose to show that the power was his, not human.

**4. Demonstration of the Spirit and of power.** "And my speech and my message were not in plausible words of wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power, that your faith might not rest in the wisdom of men but in the power of God" (2:4–5). If people believe because of a preacher's eloquence, their faith rests on a person; one day someone more eloquent may carry them away. Only faith resting on God's power will stand.

> The point: Paul deliberately refused lofty speech, preached only Christ crucified, and did so in weakness. So people's faith rests not on the preacher but on the power of God.

## Going Deeper: 2:6–10 The Hidden Wisdom of God

**1. Wisdom among the mature.** "Yet among the mature we do impart wisdom, although it is not a wisdom of this age or of the rulers of this age, who are doomed to pass away" (2:6). Paul is not saying the gospel is only for an elite few, but that the gospel itself contains deep wisdom, which those led by the Spirit and spiritually mature can grasp.

**2. Hidden wisdom.** "But we impart a secret and hidden wisdom of God, which God decreed before the ages for our glory" (2:7). The cross was not a last-minute repair God came up with, but a plan decreed "before the ages."

**3. The Lord of glory crucified.** "None of the rulers of this age understood this, for if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory" (2:8). "The rulers of this age" most directly means Pilate, Herod and the Jewish leaders; some interpreters think it also includes the spiritual powers behind them (cf. Col 2:15). "The Lord of glory" crucified is an astonishing phrase: the one nailed to the cross was the glorious Son of God himself.

**4. What no eye has seen.** "But, as it is written, 'What no eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor the heart of man imagined, what God has prepared for those who love him'—these things God has revealed to us through the Spirit" (2:9–10; cf. Isa 64:4). This verse is often used to describe the glories of heaven, and those are certainly included. But in context Paul's point is that the salvation God has accomplished in Christ is something no one could ever have imagined on their own, and yet "God has revealed [it] to us through the Spirit."

> The point: The cross is not a defeat but God's hidden wisdom, decreed before the ages. No one could have imagined it, and the rulers of this age did not understand it; but God has revealed it to us by the Spirit.

## Going Deeper: 2:10–16 The Spirit and the Spiritual Person

**1. The Spirit searches everything.** "For the Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God. For who knows a person's thoughts except the spirit of that person, which is in him? So also no one comprehends the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God" (2:10–11). Only a person knows what they themselves are thinking; in the same way, only God's Spirit knows God's mind. We can know God because God's Spirit has revealed him to us. Only one who is God can search "the depths of God," which shows the Spirit's full deity.

**2. The Spirit from God.** "Now we have received not the spirit of the world, but the Spirit who is from God, that we might understand the things freely given us by God" (2:12). The Spirit not only reveals the things of God but enables us to "understand" the grace God has freely given us.

**3. The natural person.** "The natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God, for they are folly to him, and he is not able to understand them because they are spiritually discerned" (2:14). "The natural person" means someone without the Spirit, living by natural capacities alone. The problem is not lack of intelligence but lack of spiritual sight; like someone who is colorblind, however clever, who cannot see certain colors. This also explains why some very learned people find the Bible foolish.

**4. The spiritual person.** "The spiritual person judges all things, but is himself to be judged by no one" (2:15). "The spiritual person" here does not mean an especially spiritual elite but every believer who has the Spirit (cf. Rom 8:9). The Corinthians prided themselves on being "spiritual"; in the next chapter Paul will say they are still "of the flesh" (3:1).

**5. The mind of Christ.** "'For who has understood the mind of the Lord so as to instruct him?' But we have the mind of Christ" (2:16, quoting Isa 40:13). In Isaiah 40:13 the Hebrew reads "the Spirit of the LORD" (so the ESV); Paul follows the Greek Old Testament, "the mind of the Lord." To the question "who has understood the mind of the Lord?" he answers "we have the mind of Christ," which many interpreters take to mean that Christ shares and reveals the LORD's mind, setting Christ in the place the LORD occupies in Isaiah. Having the mind of Christ doesn't mean we know everything God knows, but that through the Spirit we begin to see things as Christ sees them, including seeing the cross as he does.

> The point: We can know God because God's Spirit has revealed him to us. Without the Spirit, even the cleverest cannot see the wisdom of the cross; with the Spirit, we begin to have the mind of Christ.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "The natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God, for they are folly to him." When I read the Bible, do I treat it as a book I can understand by my own cleverness, or as a book the Spirit must open to me? Do I often skim a passage and assume I've understood it? Or have I slowly stopped reading because some parts are hard? Paul says, "these things God has revealed to us through the Spirit." Understanding Scripture is not my achievement but God's gift. Before I read today, can I pause and pray the words of Psalm 119:18: "Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law"? And then read with a longing heart, not only to gain knowledge, but to see afresh in this passage the crucified "Lord of glory."
