## 本章脉络

第三章把第一、二章的道理用在教会的分裂上，分四段。**仍是属肉体的**（1-4节）：哥林多人以「属灵」自居，保罗却说：「你们仍是属肉体的，因为在你们中间有嫉妒、纷争。」**仆人与神**（5-9节）：「亚波罗算什么？保罗算什么？无非是执事」；「我栽种了，亚波罗浇灌了，惟有神叫他生长。」**根基与工程**（10-15节）：「那已经立好的根基就是耶稣基督」；每个人在这根基上建造的工程，都要被火试验。**神的殿与万有**（16-23节）：「你们是神的殿」；「不可拿人夸口，因为万有全是你们的……并且你们是属基督的，基督又是属神的。」

保罗用了三个画面来描写教会：田地（6-9节）、房屋（9-15节）、圣殿（16-17节）。三个画面都说明同一件事：教会是神的，不是任何领袖的。

## 深读 3:1-4 仍是婴孩

**1. 属肉体的。** 「弟兄们，我从前对你们说话，不能把你们当作属灵的，只得把你们当作属肉体，在基督里为婴孩的。」（3:1）哥林多人自以为是「属灵的人」（2:15），保罗却说他们仍是「婴孩」。这里的「属肉体」不是说他们不是信徒（他们是「在基督里」的），而是说他们的行为仍然像没有圣灵的人。

**2. 奶与饭。** 「我是用奶喂你们，没有用饭喂你们。那时你们不能吃，就是如今还是不能。」（3:2）保罗只能用「奶」喂他们，不能用「饭」。有些解经家（如加尔文）认为，奶和饭不是两套不同的福音，而是同一位钉十字架的基督，按着人的成熟程度讲得浅或深（参2:2，6）；也有人参照来5:12-14，认为这里确有教导上的进深。无论哪一种理解，属灵的成熟都不是离开十字架去追求更高深的东西，而是更深地明白和活出十字架。

**3. 成熟的记号。** 「你们仍是属肉体的，因为在你们中间有嫉妒、纷争，这岂不是属乎肉体、照着世人的样子行吗？」（3:3）保罗衡量属灵成熟的标准，不是知识有多少、恩赐有多显著，而是有没有嫉妒和纷争。一个人可以很会讲道理，在关系上却仍是婴孩。

> 要点：属灵的成熟不在于知识或恩赐，而是看十字架有没有改变我们彼此相待的方式：在基督里已经蒙接纳的人，不必再靠嫉妒和争竞来证明自己。成熟不是离开十字架，而是更深地活出十字架。

## 深读 3:5-9 惟有神叫他生长

**1. 无非是执事。** 「亚波罗算什么？保罗算什么？无非是执事，照主所赐给他们各人的，引导你们相信。」（3:5）「执事」原文就是「仆人」。保罗问的是「算什么」，不是「是谁」：领袖不是教会的主人，只是神用来引导人相信的器皿。

**2. 栽种与浇灌。** 「我栽种了，亚波罗浇灌了，惟有神叫他生长。可见栽种的，算不得什么，浇灌的，也算不得什么；只在那叫他生长的神。」（3:6-7）保罗建立了教会，亚波罗后来在那里教导（徒18:27-19:1）。两个人都有工作，可是生命的成长只有神能给。传道人可以撒种、可以浇水，却不能叫一颗心重生，也不能叫一个人在属灵上长大。

**3. 都是一样。** 「栽种的和浇灌的，都是一样，但将来各人要照自己的工夫得自己的赏赐。」（3:8）「都是一样」原文直译是「是一」，意思是他们本是一体：他们不是竞争者，而是同一项工作的同工。每个人都要向神交账。赏赐是照「工夫」，就是劳苦和忠心，不是照看得见的果效大小。当然，工程的质量也要被火试验（3:12-15）。

**4. 神的同工，神的田地。** 「因为我们是与神同工的；你们是神所耕种的田地，所建造的房屋。」（3:9）「与神同工」可以理解为「与神一同工作」，也可以理解为「在神手下彼此同工」；从上下文强调「惟有神」来看，许多解经家倾向后一种理解。无论哪一种，教会都是「神的」田地、「神的」房屋。

> 要点：传道人只是栽种和浇灌的仆人，惟有神叫人生长。教会是神的田地，不是任何领袖的；每个人都要照自己的劳苦，在神面前得赏赐。

## 深读 3:10-15 根基与火

**1. 唯一的根基。** 「我照神所给我的恩，好像一个聪明的工头，立好了根基，有别人在上面建造；只是各人要谨慎怎样在上面建造。因为那已经立好的根基就是耶稣基督，此外没有人能立别的根基。」（3:10-11）教会只有一个根基，就是耶稣基督，特别是钉十字架的基督。任何建造，若不是建立在他身上，都不是神的教会。

**2. 两类材料。** 「若有人用金、银、宝石、草木、禾秸在这根基上建造，各人的工程必然显露，因为那日子要将它表明出来，有火发现；这火要试验各人的工程怎样。」（3:12-13）金、银、宝石经得起火；草木、禾秸一烧就没有了。「那日子」是基督再来审判的日子。这些材料所指的，可能是传道人所教导的内容，也可能是他们服事的动机和方式；从上下文看，保罗特别是对那些在教会中教导和带领的人说的，但原则适用于每一个信徒。

**3. 得赏赐与受亏损。** 「人在那根基上所建造的工程若存得住，他就要得赏赐。人的工程若被烧了，他就要受亏损，自己却要得救；虽然得救，乃像从火里经过的一样。」（3:14-15）这里讨论的是信徒的工作，不是人能否得救：建造在基督根基上的人，「自己却要得救」。被火试验的是工程，不是人的救恩。

天主教的教导（《天主教教理》1030-1031条）引用这节经文支持「炼狱」的说法：死时已在神恩典中、却尚未完全洁净的人，死后要经过炼净。更正教的解经家有几点回应。第一，这里被火试验的是工程，不是人。第二，试验发生在「那日子」，不是死后的某个中间阶段。第三，「像从火里经过的一样」是比喻仅仅得救（参摩4:11；亚3:2）。最重要的是，基督一次献上，已经叫信徒永远完全（来10:14），不再需要另外的补赎。信徒的盼望不在死后的炼净，而在基督已经成就的工作；这也是我们可以温柔地与天主教亲友分享的好消息。

> 要点：教会唯一的根基是耶稣基督。每个人在这根基上的建造都要被火试验；我们的得救是靠恩典，但我们的工作是否存得住，要在主面前显明。

## 深读 3:16-23 神的殿与万有

**1. 你们是神的殿。** 「岂不知你们是神的殿，神的灵住在你们里头吗？若有人毁坏神的殿，神必要毁坏那人；因为神的殿是圣的，这殿就是你们。」（3:16-17）这里的「你们」是复数：整个哥林多教会是神的殿（到6:19保罗才用同样的画面讲每个人的身体）。在旧约，神的同在住在圣殿里；如今神的灵住在他的教会中。所以，分裂教会、毁坏教会的人，是在毁坏神的殿，这是非常严重的罪。这警告之所以这样重，是因为神看他的教会为宝贵，他亲自保护他所住的殿。

**2. 变作愚拙。** 「人不可自欺。你们中间若有人在这世界自以为有智慧，倒不如变作愚拙，好成为有智慧的。」（3:18）保罗引用约伯记5:13和诗篇94:11说明，神看世上的智慧为愚拙（3:19-20）。

**3. 万有全是你们的。** 「所以无论谁，都不可拿人夸口，因为万有全是你们的。或保罗，或亚波罗，或矶法，或世界，或生，或死，或现今的事，或将来的事，全是你们的；并且你们是属基督的，基督又是属神的。」（3:21-23）哥林多人说「我是属保罗的」，保罗却说：不是你属保罗，是保罗属你！神赐给教会的每一位仆人，都是为了全教会的益处。你们不需要选一个领袖来夸口，因为一切都是你们的；而你们是属基督的。「基督又是属神的」是指基督道成肉身、作我们的中保，在救恩的工作上顺服父（参11:3；15:28）。这不是说子在本质上比父低：保罗在8:6同样称他为「主」，万物都是藉着他有的。

> 要点：教会是神的殿，毁坏教会就是毁坏神的殿。我们不必拿人夸口，因为一切都是神赐给我们的；而我们是属基督的，基督又是属神的。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我栽种了，亚波罗浇灌了，惟有神叫他生长。」我在教会里是否有一份服事，做了很久，却看不见什么果效：一个主日学班、一个小组、一位我多年来向他传福音的同事、一位一直没有起色的弟兄或姊妹？我是否因此灰心，觉得自己的劳苦白费了？或者，我是否把别人的成长看作自己的功劳？保罗说，栽种的和浇灌的都「算不得什么」，因为叫人生长的是神；而将来的赏赐，是照「自己的工夫」，就是劳苦，不是照看得见的果效。这既拆掉我的骄傲，也安慰我的灰心。主耶稣自己也曾这样劳苦：他传道三年，到最后连门徒都离开了他；可是他像一粒麦子落在地里死了，神却叫它结出许多子粒来（约12:24）。我的劳苦在他里面，也不会白费。今天，我能不能把我所服事的那个人或那群人重新交给神，对他说：「主啊，我能做的只是撒种、浇水；叫他生长的，是你」？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 applies the teaching of chapters 1–2 to the church's divisions, in four parts. **Still of the flesh** (vv. 1–4): the Corinthians considered themselves "spiritual," but Paul says, "you are still of the flesh. For while there is jealousy and strife among you..." **Servants and God** (vv. 5–9): "What then is Apollos? What is Paul? Servants"; "I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth." **The foundation and the work** (vv. 10–15): "no one can lay a foundation other than that which is laid, which is Jesus Christ"; each person's work on this foundation will be tested by fire. **God's temple and all things** (vv. 16–23): "you are God's temple"; "let no one boast in men. For all things are yours... and you are Christ's, and Christ is God's."

Paul uses three pictures for the church: a field (vv. 6–9), a building (vv. 9–15) and a temple (vv. 16–17). All three make the same point: the church belongs to God, not to any leader.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–4 Still Infants

**1. People of the flesh.** "But I, brothers, could not address you as spiritual people, but as people of the flesh, as infants in Christ" (3:1). The Corinthians thought themselves "spiritual" (2:15), but Paul says they are still "infants." "Of the flesh" here doesn't mean they were not believers (they are "in Christ"), but that they were behaving like people without the Spirit.

**2. Milk and solid food.** "I fed you with milk, not solid food, for you were not ready for it. And even now you are not yet ready" (3:2). Paul could feed them only milk, not solid food. Some interpreters (Calvin among them) hold that milk and solid food are not two different gospels but the same Christ crucified, taught at a shallower or deeper level (cf. 2:2, 6); others, comparing Heb 5:12–14, see a real progression in teaching. Either way, spiritual maturity is not leaving the cross behind but understanding and living it out more deeply.

**3. The mark of maturity.** "For you are still of the flesh. For while there is jealousy and strife among you, are you not of the flesh and behaving only in a human way?" (3:3). Paul measures spiritual maturity not by how much knowledge or how impressive the gifts, but by whether there is jealousy and strife. A person can argue doctrine well and still be an infant in relationships.

> The point: Spiritual maturity is measured not by knowledge or gifts but by whether the cross is reshaping how we treat one another: people already accepted in Christ no longer need jealousy and rivalry to prove themselves. Maturity is not leaving the cross behind but living it out more deeply.

## Going Deeper: 3:5–9 Only God Gives the Growth

**1. Only servants.** "What then is Apollos? What is Paul? Servants through whom you believed, as the Lord assigned to each" (3:5). Paul asks "what," not "who": leaders are not the church's masters, only instruments God uses to bring people to faith.

**2. Planting and watering.** "I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. So neither he who plants nor he who waters is anything, but only God who gives the growth" (3:6–7). Paul founded the church; Apollos later taught there (Acts 18:27–19:1). Both had work to do, but only God can give the growth of life. Preachers can sow and water, but they cannot cause a heart to be born again or a person to grow spiritually.

**3. One.** "He who plants and he who waters are one, and each will receive his wages according to his labor" (3:8). They are not competitors but co-workers in a single task. Each answers to God, and is rewarded according to "labor," that is, faithful toil, not the visible size of the results; though the quality of the work will also be tested (3:12–15).

**4. God's fellow workers, God's field.** "For we are God's fellow workers. You are God's field, God's building" (3:9). "God's fellow workers" can mean "working together with God" or "fellow workers under God"; given the context's stress on "only God," many interpreters lean toward the second. Either way, the church is "God's" field and "God's" building.

> The point: Preachers are only servants who plant and water; only God gives the growth. The church is God's field, not any leader's; each will be rewarded before God according to their labor.

## Going Deeper: 3:10–15 The Foundation and the Fire

**1. The only foundation.** "According to the grace of God given to me, like a skilled master builder I laid a foundation, and someone else is building upon it. Let each one take care how he builds upon it. For no one can lay a foundation other than that which is laid, which is Jesus Christ" (3:10–11). The church has one foundation, Jesus Christ, and especially Christ crucified. Anything not built on him is not God's church.

**2. Two kinds of material.** "Now if anyone builds on the foundation with gold, silver, precious stones, wood, hay, straw, each one's work will become manifest, for the Day will disclose it, because it will be revealed by fire, and the fire will test what sort of work each one has done" (3:12–13). Gold, silver and precious stones survive fire; wood, hay and straw burn up. "The Day" is the day of Christ's return and judgment. The "materials" may mean the content of what teachers teach, or the motives and manner of their service; in context Paul is speaking especially to those who teach and lead in the church, but the principle applies to every believer.

**3. Reward and loss.** "If the work that anyone has built on the foundation survives, he will receive a reward. If anyone's work is burned up, he will suffer loss, though he himself will be saved, but only as through fire" (3:14–15). The subject is believers' work, not whether a person is saved: whoever builds on the foundation of Christ "will be saved." What the fire tests is the work, not the person's salvation. Roman Catholic teaching (Catechism 1030–1031) cites this verse in support of purgatory, a purification after death for those who die in God's grace but are not yet fully purified. Protestant interpreters respond that what the fire tests here is the work, not the person; that the testing happens on "the Day," not in an intermediate state after death; that "as through fire" pictures a narrow escape (cf. Amos 4:11; Zech 3:2); and that Christ's single offering has perfected believers for all time (Heb 10:14), leaving no further satisfaction to be made. Our hope does not rest on purification after death but on the work Christ has already finished; this is good news we can share gently with Catholic friends and relatives.

> The point: The church's only foundation is Jesus Christ. Everyone's building on that foundation will be tested by fire; we are saved by grace, but whether our work survives will be shown before the Lord.

## Going Deeper: 3:16–23 God's Temple and All Things

**1. You are God's temple.** "Do you not know that you are God's temple and that God's Spirit dwells in you? If anyone destroys God's temple, God will destroy him. For God's temple is holy, and you are that temple" (3:16–17). "You" here is plural: the whole Corinthian church is God's temple (only at 6:19 does Paul use the same picture for each person's body). In the Old Testament God's presence dwelt in the temple; now God's Spirit dwells in his church. So whoever divides and destroys the church is destroying God's temple, a very serious sin. The warning is this severe because God treasures his church and guards the temple where he dwells.

**2. Becoming a fool.** "Let no one deceive himself. If anyone among you thinks that he is wise in this age, let him become a fool that he may become wise" (3:18). Paul quotes Job 5:13 and Psalm 94:11 to show that God regards the wisdom of this world as folly (3:19–20).

**3. All things are yours.** "So let no one boast in men. For all things are yours, whether Paul or Apollos or Cephas or the world or life or death or the present or the future, all are yours, and you are Christ's, and Christ is God's" (3:21–23). The Corinthians said, "I follow Paul"; Paul says, in effect, "No, Paul belongs to you!" Every servant God gives the church is for the good of the whole church. You don't need to pick one leader to boast in, because everything is yours; and you are Christ's. "Christ is God's" speaks of Christ as the incarnate Mediator, who in the work of salvation submits to the Father (cf. 11:3; 15:28), not of any inferiority in his being; in 8:6 Paul calls him the one Lord, through whom are all things.

> The point: The church is God's temple, and to destroy the church is to destroy God's temple. We need not boast in people, for all things are God's gifts to us; and we are Christ's, and Christ is God's.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth." Do I have a ministry in church that I have carried for a long time without seeing much fruit: a Sunday school class, a small group, a coworker I have shared the gospel with for years, a brother or sister who never seems to change? Have I grown discouraged, feeling my labor has been wasted? Or do I take credit for others' growth? Paul says that neither the one who plants nor the one who waters "is anything," for it is God who gives the growth; and the coming reward is "according to his labor," not according to visible results. This tears down my pride and comforts my discouragement. The Lord Jesus himself labored like this: after three years of ministry even his disciples deserted him; yet he was the grain of wheat that fell into the earth and died, and God made it bear much fruit (John 12:24). In him my labor is not in vain either. Today, can I hand the person or group I serve back to God and say, "Lord, all I can do is sow and water; you are the one who gives the growth"?
