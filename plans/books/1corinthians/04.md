## 本章脉络

第四章是第一至四章的总结，分三段。**基督的执事，神的管家**（1-5节）：「人应当以我们为基督的执事，为神奥秘事的管家」；管家所需要的是「忠心」；保罗把人的论断看为极小的事，连自己也不论断自己，「判断我的乃是主」。**骄傲与使徒的样子**（6-13节）：「你有什么不是领受的呢？若是领受的，为何自夸？」哥林多人以为自己「已经丰富了」，「自己就作王了」，使徒们却像「定死罪的囚犯」，被人看作「世界上的污秽，万物中的渣滓」。**属灵的父亲**（14-21节）：「师傅虽有一万，为父的却是不多」；保罗求他们效法他，并且打发提摩太去提醒他们。

## 深读 4:1-5 判断我的乃是主

**1. 执事与管家。** 「人应当以我们为基督的执事，为神奥秘事的管家。」（4:1）「执事」原文指助手、随从，是服事人的；「管家」是替主人管理家业的奴仆，虽然有责任，却不是主人。神的「奥秘事」，就是第二章所说、如今藉着福音显明的神的智慧（2:7）。

**2. 要他有忠心。** 「所求于管家的，是要他有忠心。」（4:2）主人对管家的要求，不是才干、口才或受欢迎，而是忠心。这正好回应了哥林多人用口才和智慧来比较领袖的风气。

**3. 不论断自己。** 「我被你们论断，或被别人论断，我都以为极小的事；连我自己也不论断自己。我虽不觉得自己有错，却也不能因此得以称义；但判断我的乃是主。」（4:3-4）保罗不是说人的意见完全不重要，也不是说他不需要省察自己（参11:28）。他是说：最终的判决不在人手里，也不在自己的良心里，而在主手里。良心没有不安，不等于在神面前无可指摘。反过来，良心不安，也不一定表示神已经定了我们的罪：有时那是圣灵叫我们悔改，有时却只是过度的自责，而「神比我们的心大」（约壹3:20）。提摩太·凯勒（Timothy Keller）在《忘我的自由》（*The Freedom of Self-Forgetfulness*，暂译）一书中说，福音给人的自由，是不再需要不断地评估自己，因为判决已经宣告了：在基督里，我们已经被称义。当然，保罗在第5节也说，主来的时候还要「照出暗中的隐情」，察验我们作管家的忠心（参3:12-15）。可是对在基督里的人，这审判不会定我们的罪（罗8:1），而是「各人要从神那里得着称赞」的日子。

**4. 等主来。** 「所以，时候未到，什么都不要论断，只等主来，他要照出暗中的隐情，显明人心的意念。那时，各人要从神那里得着称赞。」（4:5）这里的「论断」是指对别人最终价值和动机的判定，不是说教会不可以分辨是非（参5:12）。只有主能照出「人心的意念」。而且，那一天不只是审判的日子，也是「各人要从神那里得着称赞」的日子。

> 要点：管家所需要的是忠心，不是受欢迎。最终的判决不在人的意见里，也不在自己的良心里，而在主手里；在基督里，我们可以从不断评估自己的重担中得着自由。

## 深读 4:6-13 你有什么不是领受的呢？

**1. 不可过于圣经所记。** 「弟兄们，我为你们的缘故，拿这些事转比自己和亚波罗，叫你们效法我们不可过于圣经所记，免得你们自高自大，贵重这个，轻看那个。」（4:6）「不可过于圣经所记」是这封信中最难解的句子之一。解经家的看法不一：有人认为是一句熟悉的格言，指要照着圣经（旧约）来衡量人，特别是前面引用、拆毁人自夸的经文（1:19，31；3:19-20）；有人认为是指保罗在这封信里已经写的话；也有人认为是早期抄写时混进正文的旁注。第一种看法最为常见，也最合上下文。

**2. 三个问题。** 「使你与人不同的是谁呢？你有什么不是领受的呢？若是领受的，为何自夸，仿佛不是领受的呢？」（4:7）这三个问题是对一切骄傲的致命一击。我的才干、学识、恩赐、性格、机会，甚至我的信心，有哪一样不是领受的？奥古斯丁在《论圣徒的预定》中说，特别是这节经文，使他明白连信心本身也是神的恩赐。严格说来，保罗在这里讲的是恩赐；奥古斯丁把它应用到信心上，是合乎圣经的神学引申。

**3. 已经作王了。** 「你们已经饱足了！已经丰富了！不用我们，自己就作王了！我愿意你们果真作王，叫我们也得与你们一同作王。」（4:8）这是带着讽刺的话。哥林多人好像以为，神国的一切福气现在都已经完全得着了：属灵的知识、恩赐、能力，都已经满足。许多解经家称之为「过度实现的末世观」：他们忘了，在基督再来以前，信徒仍然走在十字架的道路上。

**4. 一台戏。** 「我想神把我们使徒明明列在末后，好像定死罪的囚犯；因为我们成了一台戏，给世人和天使观看。」（4:9）这个画面可能来自罗马的凯旋游行（队伍的最后是要被处死的俘虏，参林后2:14），也可能来自竞技场（「一台戏」原文是 *theatron*（剧场），定了死罪的犯人常常被安排在最后出场）。无论哪一种，意思都一样：哥林多人自以为是作王的，使徒们却像排在最后、要被处死的人。

**5. 使徒的样子。** 「我们为基督的缘故算是愚拙的，你们在基督里倒是聪明的；我们软弱，你们倒强壮；你们有荣耀，我们倒被藐视……被人咒骂，我们就祝福；被人逼迫，我们就忍受；被人毁谤，我们就善劝。」（4:10-13）保罗描写的，正是十字架的样子：被藐视、受苦、以善报恶（参太5:44；罗12:14）。真正属基督的人，生命会渐渐带出十字架的样子。

> 要点：我们所有的一切都是领受的，所以没有可夸的。在基督再来以前，跟随一位钉十字架的主，意思是走一条被看为愚拙、软弱，却以善报恶的路；他先为我们走了这条路，我们才能跟着走。

## 深读 4:14-21 为父的心

**1. 不是叫你们羞愧。** 「我写这话，不是叫你们羞愧，乃是警戒你们，好像我所亲爱的儿女一样。」（4:14）保罗刚刚用了很重的话，可是他的动机是爱。属灵的责备，目的不是羞辱人，而是挽回人。

**2. 师傅与父亲。** 「你们学基督的，师傅虽有一万，为父的却是不多，因我在基督耶稣里用福音生了你们。」（4:15）「师傅」原文是古代负责照管孩子的家奴，带孩子上学，监督他的行为。这样的人可以很多，可是父亲只有一个。保罗用福音把他们「生」出来，所以他对他们的关心，是父亲的关心。

**3. 效法我。** 「所以，我求你们效法我。」（4:16）这听起来好像很骄傲，可是从上文看，保罗要他们效法的，正是刚才所描写那种被藐视、受苦、以善报恶的生活；他后来又说：「你们该效法我，像我效法基督一样。」（11:1）

**4. 神的国在乎权能。** 「因为神的国不在乎言语，乃在乎权能。」（4:20）哥林多人很会说话，可是神国的记号，不是漂亮的言语，而是神改变人生命的能力。

**5. 刑杖或温柔。** 「你们愿意怎么样呢？是愿意我带着刑杖到你们那里去呢？还是要我存慈爱温柔的心呢？」（4:21）保罗把选择交给他们。他宁愿温柔，可是如果需要，他也会管教。爱与管教并不冲突。

> 要点：属灵的责备，出于父亲的爱，目的是挽回，不是羞辱。神的国不在乎漂亮的言语，而在乎神改变人生命的能力。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我被你们论断，或被别人论断，我都以为极小的事；连我自己也不论断自己……但判断我的乃是主。」我今天花了多少心思，在意别人怎样看我？同事的评价、长辈的期望、朋友圈的点赞、教会里别人的眼光……又有多少时候，我在心里不断地审问自己：我够好吗？我是不是失败了？保罗说，最终的判决不在这些地方，而在主手里；而在基督里，主已经宣告了我们的身份：「如今，那些在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了」（罗8:1）。这不是叫我不在乎对错，而是叫我从必须不断证明自己的重担中得释放。今天，我能不能把一件我一直在意别人怎么看的事交给主，对他说：「判断我的乃是你；你既已在基督里接纳了我，我就可以忘了自己，单单忠心地服事你；因为那一天，我要听的称赞是从你来的」？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 sums up chapters 1–4, in three parts. **Servants of Christ, stewards of God** (vv. 1–5): "This is how one should regard us, as servants of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God"; what stewards need is to be "found trustworthy"; Paul counts human judgment a very small thing, doesn't even judge himself: "It is the Lord who judges me." **Pride and the apostolic pattern** (vv. 6–13): "What do you have that you did not receive? If then you received it, why do you boast?" The Corinthians thought that "without us you have become kings," while the apostles were "like men sentenced to death," treated as "the scum of the world, the refuse of all things." **A spiritual father** (vv. 14–21): "though you have countless guides in Christ, you do not have many fathers"; Paul urges them to imitate him and sends Timothy to remind them.

## Going Deeper: 4:1–5 It Is the Lord Who Judges Me

**1. Servants and stewards.** "This is how one should regard us, as servants of Christ and stewards of the mysteries of God" (4:1). The word for "servants" was used of assistants and attendants; a "steward" was a slave who managed his master's household, responsible but not the owner. God's "mysteries" are the wisdom of God described in chapter 2, now revealed through the gospel (2:7).

**2. Found trustworthy.** "Moreover, it is required of stewards that they be found trustworthy" (4:2). What a master requires of a steward is not talent, eloquence or popularity, but faithfulness. This answers directly the Corinthian habit of comparing leaders by eloquence and wisdom.

**3. Not judging myself.** "But with me it is a very small thing that I should be judged by you or by any human court. In fact, I do not even judge myself. For I am not aware of anything against myself, but I am not thereby acquitted. It is the Lord who judges me" (4:3–4). Paul isn't saying human opinion is completely unimportant, or that he needn't examine himself (cf. 11:28). He is saying that the final verdict rests neither in human hands nor in his own conscience, but with the Lord. A clear conscience doesn't mean a person is blameless before God; likewise, a troubled conscience does not necessarily mean God has condemned them; sometimes it is the Spirit calling us to repent, sometimes it is misplaced self-accusation, and "God is greater than our heart" (1 John 3:20). Timothy Keller, in *The Freedom of Self-Forgetfulness*, says the freedom the gospel gives is no longer needing to keep assessing ourselves, because the verdict is already in: in Christ we are justified. Paul does say in verse 5 that when the Lord comes he will still "bring to light the things now hidden" and weigh our faithfulness as stewards (cf. 3:12–15). But for those in Christ this is not a judgment that can condemn (Rom 8:1); it is the day when "each one will receive his commendation from God."

**4. Wait for the Lord.** "Therefore do not pronounce judgment before the time, before the Lord comes, who will bring to light the things now hidden in darkness and will disclose the purposes of the heart. Then each one will receive his commendation from God" (4:5). "Judgment" here means a final verdict on another's worth and motives; it does not mean the church may not discern right from wrong (cf. 5:12). Only the Lord can disclose "the purposes of the heart." And that day is not only a day of judgment, but the day when "each one will receive his commendation from God."

> The point: What a steward needs is faithfulness, not popularity. The final verdict lies not in human opinion or in our own conscience but with the Lord; in Christ we can be free from the burden of endlessly assessing ourselves.

## Going Deeper: 4:6–13 What Do You Have That You Did Not Receive?

**1. Not beyond what is written.** "I have applied all these things to myself and Apollos for your benefit, brothers, that you may learn by us not to go beyond what is written, that none of you may be puffed up in favor of one against another" (4:6). "Not beyond what is written" is one of the hardest phrases in the letter. Interpreters differ. Some take it as a familiar maxim: measure people by Scripture (the Old Testament), especially the texts quoted earlier that tear down boasting (1:19, 31; 3:19–20). Others think it refers to what Paul has already written in this letter. A few suggest it began as a copyist's marginal note. The first view is the most common and fits the context best.

**2. Three questions.** "For who sees anything different in you? What do you have that you did not receive? If then you received it, why do you boast as if you did not receive it?" (4:7). These three questions are a death blow to all pride. My talents, learning, gifts, personality, opportunities, even my faith: which of them did I not receive? Augustine said (*On the Predestination of the Saints*) that it was "chiefly" this verse that convinced him that even faith itself is God's gift (a theological extension from gifts to faith).

**3. Already kings.** "Already you have all you want! Already you have become rich! Without us you have become kings! And would that you did reign, so that we might share the rule with you!" (4:8). This is irony. The Corinthians seemed to think that all the blessings of God's kingdom were already fully theirs: spiritual knowledge, gifts and power, all complete. Many interpreters call this an "over-realized eschatology": they forgot that until Christ returns, believers still walk the way of the cross.

**4. A spectacle.** "For I think that God has exhibited us apostles as last of all, like men sentenced to death, because we have become a spectacle to the world, to angels, and to men" (4:9). The picture may come from the Roman triumphal procession, where captives destined for execution came last (cf. 2 Cor 2:14), or from the arena: "spectacle" is literally "theater," and condemned criminals were often the final act of the show. Either way the point is the same: the Corinthians thought themselves kings, while the apostles were like the condemned men brought out last to die.

**5. The apostolic pattern.** "We are fools for Christ's sake, but you are wise in Christ. We are weak, but you are strong. You are held in honor, but we in disrepute... When reviled, we bless; when persecuted, we endure; when slandered, we entreat" (4:10–13). Paul is describing the shape of the cross: despised, suffering, returning good for evil (cf. Matt 5:44; Rom 12:14). Those who truly belong to Christ will increasingly bear the shape of the cross in their lives.

> The point: Everything we have, we have received, so we have nothing to boast of. Until Christ returns, following a crucified Lord means walking a road that looks foolish and weak, and that returns good for evil; he walked that road for us first, and that is why we can follow.

## Going Deeper: 4:14–21 A Father's Heart

**1. Not to shame you.** "I do not write these things to make you ashamed, but to admonish you as my beloved children" (4:14). Paul has just used very strong words, but his motive is love. Spiritual rebuke aims not to humiliate but to restore.

**2. Guides and a father.** "For though you have countless guides in Christ, you do not have many fathers. For I became your father in Christ Jesus through the gospel" (4:15). "Guides" translates a word for the household slave who looked after a child, took him to school and supervised his behavior. There could be many such guides, but only one father. Paul "fathered" them through the gospel, so his care for them is a father's care.

**3. Imitate me.** "I urge you, then, be imitators of me" (4:16). This sounds proud, but in context what Paul wants them to imitate is exactly the despised, suffering, good-for-evil life he has just described; and later he says, "Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ" (11:1).

**4. The kingdom is power.** "For the kingdom of God does not consist in talk but in power" (4:20). The Corinthians were good talkers, but the mark of God's kingdom is not fine words but God's power to change lives.

**5. A rod or gentleness.** "What do you wish? Shall I come to you with a rod, or with love in a spirit of gentleness?" (4:21). Paul leaves the choice to them. He would rather come gently, but if necessary he will discipline. Love and discipline do not conflict.

> The point: Spiritual rebuke comes from a father's love; its aim is restoration, not humiliation. The kingdom of God is not a matter of fine words but of God's power to change lives.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But with me it is a very small thing that I should be judged by you or by any human court. In fact, I do not even judge myself... It is the Lord who judges me." How much of my mind today goes on how others see me: a coworker's evaluation, an elder's expectations, likes on social media, how people at church regard me? And how often do I keep interrogating myself: Am I good enough? Have I failed? Paul says the final verdict lies not in any of these places but with the Lord; and in Christ the Lord has already pronounced who we are: "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Rom 8:1). This doesn't make me indifferent to right and wrong; it frees me from the burden of always having to prove myself. Today, can I hand to the Lord one thing I have been anxious about others' opinion of, and say, "You are the one who judges me; since you have accepted me in Christ, I can forget myself and simply serve you faithfully, because the commendation I long to hear on that day is yours"?
