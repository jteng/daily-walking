## 本章脉络

第五章开始处理哥林多教会的道德问题，分三段。**一件可耻的事**（1-5节）：「有人收了他的继母」，教会却「还是自高自大，并不哀痛」；保罗要他们在聚会时「把这样的人交给撒但」，目的是「使他的灵魂在主耶稣的日子可以得救」。**旧酵与逾越节**（6-8节）：「一点面酵能使全团发起来」；「我们逾越节的羔羊基督已经被杀献祭了」，所以要除去旧酵。**教会内外**（9-13节）：保罗不是叫他们离开世界，而是说「若有称为弟兄」却活在明显的罪中，就不可与他相交；「至于外人有神审判他们」。

这一章不容易读，因为它讲的是教会的管教。可是保罗的出发点不是严厉，而是出于两样关切：神的圣洁，以及那个犯罪之人的灵魂。

## 深读 5:1-5 并不哀痛

**1. 连外邦人中也没有。** 「风闻在你们中间有淫乱的事。这样的淫乱连外邦人中也没有，就是有人收了他的继母。」（5:1）「收了」可能是同居，也可能是结婚。旧约明令禁止（利18:8；申22:30），罗马法律也不容许。这个人的父亲可能已经去世，或者已经离婚；继母看来不是信徒，因为保罗只处理那个男人。

**2. 自高自大，并不哀痛。** 「你们还是自高自大，并不哀痛，把行这事的人从你们中间赶出去。」（5:2）令保罗最痛心的，不只是这个人的罪，更是教会的反应。他们「自高自大」：也许是以为自己有「自由」、有「知识」，超越了旧的道德规范（参6:12）；也许只是因为这个人有地位，教会不敢处理。保罗说，他们应当「哀痛」，就像为死人哀哭一样。

**3. 交给撒但。** 「奉我们主耶稣的名，并用我们主耶稣的权能，要把这样的人交给撒但，败坏他的肉体，使他的灵魂在主耶稣的日子可以得救。」（5:4-5）这是本章最难解的一句话。多数解经家认为，「交给撒但」就是把他逐出教会的团契，交回到撒但掌权的世界里（参约壹5:19；提前1:20）。「败坏他的肉体」有不同的理解：

- 有人（例如戈登·费依〔Gordon Fee〕）认为，「肉体」是指他属肉体的罪性，在被逐出团契的痛苦中被除灭，使他悔改（参提前1:20「使他们受责罚就不再谤渎」）。
- 也有人认为是指身体上的疾病、苦难，甚至死亡（参11:30「死的也不少」）。

无论哪一种理解，最终的目的都很清楚：「使他的灵魂在主耶稣的日子可以得救」。教会的管教不是报复，而是一种严厉的爱，盼望犯罪的人回转。

**4. 在聚会中执行。** 「就是你们聚会的时候，我的心也同在。」（5:4）管教不是某一个领袖私下的决定，而是全教会在主的名下、在聚会中一同承担的事。

> 要点：教会对明显的罪不可以自高自大、视而不见，而应当哀痛。教会的管教不是报复，而是严厉的爱，最终的目的是叫犯罪的人在主的日子得救。

## 深读 5:6-8 基督我们逾越节的羔羊

**1. 一点面酵。** 「你们这自夸是不好的。岂不知一点面酵能使全团发起来吗？」（5:6）面酵在圣经中常常象征罪和败坏的影响（参太16:6；加5:9）。容忍明显的罪，不会让罪停留在一个人身上；它会渐渐影响整个群体。

**2. 你们既是无酵的面。** 「你们既是无酵的面，应当把旧酵除净，好使你们成为新团；因为我们逾越节的羔羊基督已经被杀献祭了。」（5:7）保罗先说他们是什么，再吩咐他们做什么：「应当把旧酵除净」的根据，是「你们既是无酵的面」（原文用 *kathōs*，意思是「正如」）。在基督里，他们已经是新的、圣洁的；所以要活得名副其实。

**3. 逾越节的羔羊。** 犹太人在逾越节之前，要把家里所有的酵除净（出12:15-20），然后宰杀逾越节的羔羊。保罗说，羔羊已经被杀了：基督已经为我们死了。逾越节的羔羊替头生的死：「我一见这血，就越过你们去」（出12:13）。所以我们的整个生命，就像一个长久的无酵节，「不可用旧酵，也不可用恶毒、邪恶的酵，只用诚实真正的无酵饼」（5:8）。圣洁的动力，不是怕被赶出去，而是基督已经为我们流了血。

> 要点：在基督里，我们已经是无酵的面；所以要除去旧酵，活出与这新身份相称的生命。圣洁的动力，是逾越节的羔羊基督已经为我们被杀献祭了。

## 深读 5:9-13 教会内与教会外

**1. 不是叫你们离开世界。** 「我先前写信给你们说，不可与淫乱的人相交。此话不是指这世上一概行淫乱的，或贪婪的，勒索的，或拜偶像的；若是这样，你们除非离开世界方可。」（5:9-10）哥林多人误解了保罗先前的信，以为他要他们远离一切不信的罪人。保罗说，那是不可能的，也不是他的意思。基督徒要活在世界中，像耶稣一样与罪人来往（参路15:1-2）。

**2. 称为弟兄的。** 「但如今我写信给你们说，若有称为弟兄是行淫乱的，或贪婪的，或拜偶像的，或辱骂的，或醉酒的，或勒索的，这样的人不可与他相交，就是与他吃饭都不可。」（5:11）保罗所说的，是一个自称为信徒、却在明显而不肯悔改的罪中继续生活的人。要留意，这份清单里不只有淫乱，也有「贪婪」和「辱骂」：教会常常只严厉对待性方面的罪，却容忍贪心和恶言。

**3. 教内与教外。** 「因为审判教外的人与我何干？教内的人岂不是你们审判的吗？至于外人有神审判他们。你们应当把那恶人从你们中间赶出去。」（5:12-13；引自申17:7等）教会不是社会的道德警察，不需要去审判不信的人；可是对教会内部的罪，教会有责任。

**4. 今天的应用。** 教会的管教在华人教会中常常有两种偏差：一种是顾全面子、碍于人情，或者因为犯罪的是重要的长执、大奉献者，就不了了之；另一种是缺乏爱心，也不按程序，当众点名羞辱，叫人连同家人都抬不起头，然后一赶了之。圣经的管教要有耐心的劝勉（太18:15-17），目的是挽回（加6:1），而且犯罪的人悔改以后，教会要赦免、安慰他（参林后2:6-8）。林后二章的那个人是否就是这里的人，解经家看法不一，但原则相同。另外，若涉及性侵、虐待儿童等犯罪行为，教会不可以「家丑不可外扬」为理由私下处理，更不可劝受害人忍耐、饶恕了事；必须保护受害人，并按当地法律向警方报案（参罗13:4）。报了案，教会仍要按圣经执行自己的管教。悔改的人当蒙赦免，但赦免不等于恢复原来的职分，更不等于让他重新接近受害人或儿童；教会也不可以要求受害人「快点饶恕」、与加害者和好。

> 要点：教会不是社会的道德警察，却要为内部的罪负责。圣经的管教既不是为了面子而不了了之，也不是冷冷地把人赶走，而是在耐心和爱中，盼望人悔改回转。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们还是自高自大，并不哀痛。」当我听见教会里有人跌倒的时候，我的第一个反应是什么？是好奇、议论（为受害人发声、向该知道的人报告，不是议论）、暗暗觉得自己比他好，还是像保罗所说的「哀痛」？当我看见自己心里的罪时，我是轻轻带过，还是为它忧伤？保罗说，我们「既是无酵的面」，因为「逾越节的羔羊基督已经被杀献祭了」。我不是为了让自己变成圣洁才去除罪，而是因为基督已经用他的血使我成为圣洁，所以我不愿意再让旧酵留在生命里。今天，我生命中有没有一样「旧酵」（也许是一个习惯、一段不该有的关系、一份苦毒），需要我为它哀痛，并靠着基督把它除净？如果我就是那个跌倒的人，这羔羊的血也是为我流的，回转的路是开着的。我为罪哀痛，却不必绝望，因为神说：「我一见这血，就越过你们去。」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 begins to deal with moral problems in the Corinthian church, in three parts. **A shameful case** (vv. 1–5): "a man has his father's wife," yet the church is "arrogant" and does not "mourn"; Paul tells them, when assembled, "to deliver this man to Satan," "so that his spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord." **Old leaven and Passover** (vv. 6–8): "a little leaven leavens the whole lump"; "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed," so they must clean out the old leaven. **Inside and outside the church** (vv. 9–13): Paul isn't telling them to leave the world, but not to associate with "anyone who bears the name of brother" while living in open sin; "God judges those outside."

This is not an easy chapter, because it deals with church discipline. Yet Paul's starting point is not severity but two things: the holiness of God and the soul of the man who has sinned.

## Going Deeper: 5:1–5 You Did Not Mourn

**1. Not tolerated even among pagans.** "It is actually reported that there is sexual immorality among you, and of a kind that is not tolerated even among pagans, for a man has his father's wife" (5:1). "Has" may mean living together or marriage. The Old Testament explicitly forbids it (Lev 18:8; Deut 22:30), and Roman law did not allow it either. The man's father may have died or divorced; the stepmother was apparently not a believer, since Paul deals only with the man.

**2. Arrogant rather than mourning.** "And you are arrogant! Ought you not rather to mourn? Let him who has done this be removed from among you" (5:2). What grieves Paul most is not only the man's sin but the church's response. They were "arrogant": perhaps thinking their "freedom" or "knowledge" put them above old moral rules (cf. 6:12); perhaps simply afraid to act because the man had standing. Paul says they should "mourn," as one mourns for the dead.

**3. Delivered to Satan.** "When you are assembled in the name of the Lord Jesus and my spirit is present, with the power of our Lord Jesus, you are to deliver this man to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that his spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord" (5:4–5). This is the hardest sentence in the chapter. Most interpreters take "deliver to Satan" to mean putting him out of the church's fellowship, back into the world where Satan holds sway (cf. 1 John 5:19; 1 Tim 1:20). "The destruction of the flesh" is understood in different ways:

- Some (e.g. Gordon Fee) take "flesh" as his sinful nature, destroyed through the pain of exclusion so that he repents (cf. 1 Tim 1:20, "that they may learn not to blaspheme").
- Others take it as physical sickness, suffering, or even death (cf. 11:30, "and some have died").

Either way, the final goal is clear: "so that his spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord." Church discipline is not revenge but a severe love that hopes for the sinner's return.

**4. Carried out in the assembly.** "When you are assembled..." (5:4). Discipline is not one leader's private decision but something the whole church bears together, in the Lord's name, when gathered.

> The point: The church must not be proud or turn a blind eye to open sin, but should mourn. Church discipline is not revenge but severe love, whose final goal is that the sinner may be saved in the day of the Lord.

## Going Deeper: 5:6–8 Christ Our Passover Lamb

**1. A little leaven.** "Your boasting is not good. Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the whole lump?" (5:6). In Scripture leaven often pictures the spreading influence of sin and corruption (cf. Matt 16:6; Gal 5:9). Tolerating open sin doesn't keep it confined to one person; it slowly affects the whole community.

**2. As you really are unleavened.** "Cleanse out the old leaven that you may be a new lump, as you really are unleavened. For Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (5:7). The logic matters greatly: the command ("Cleanse out the old leaven") rests on what they already are ("as you really are unleavened"). The indicative grounds the imperative. In Christ they already are new and holy; so they must live out what they already are.

**3. The Passover lamb.** Before Passover, Jews removed all leaven from their homes (Exod 12:15–20) and then sacrificed the Passover lamb. Paul says the lamb has already been sacrificed: Christ has died for us. The Passover lamb died in place of the firstborn: "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exod 12:13). So our whole life is like an unending Feast of Unleavened Bread: "Let us therefore celebrate the festival, not with the old leaven, the leaven of malice and evil, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (5:8). The motive for holiness is not fear of being put out, but that Christ has shed his blood for us.

> The point: In Christ we already are unleavened; so we clean out the old leaven and live out what we already are. The motive for holiness is that Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed for us.

## Going Deeper: 5:9–13 Inside and Outside the Church

**1. Not leaving the world.** "I wrote to you in my letter not to associate with sexually immoral people, not at all meaning the sexually immoral of this world, or the greedy and swindlers, or idolaters, since then you would need to go out of the world" (5:9–10). The Corinthians had misunderstood Paul's earlier letter, thinking he meant they should avoid all unbelieving sinners. Paul says that would be impossible, and it was not what he meant. Christians are to live in the world, mixing with sinners as Jesus did (cf. Luke 15:1–2).

**2. One who bears the name of brother.** "But now I am writing to you not to associate with anyone who bears the name of brother if he is guilty of sexual immorality or greed, or is an idolater, reviler, drunkard, or swindler, not even to eat with such a one" (5:11). Paul means someone who calls himself a believer yet keeps living in open, unrepentant sin. Notice that the list includes not only sexual sin but "greed" and "reviling": churches often deal severely with sexual sins while tolerating greed and abusive speech.

**3. Inside and outside.** "For what have I to do with judging outsiders? Is it not those inside the church whom you are to judge? God judges those outside. 'Purge the evil person from among you'" (5:12–13, quoting Deut 17:7 and others). The church is not society's moral police and need not judge unbelievers; but it is responsible for sin within its own fellowship.

**4. Applying this today.** Chinese churches often err in two directions with discipline: either letting things drop to protect face or relationships, or because the sinner is an important elder or major donor; or acting without love or process, shaming the person publicly so that they and their family cannot lift their heads, and simply driving them out. Biblical discipline involves patient appeal (Matt 18:15–17), aims at restoration (Gal 6:1), and when the sinner repents, the church must forgive and comfort him (cf. 2 Cor 2:6–8; whether that is the same man is debated, but the principle is the same). And where sexual assault, child abuse or other crimes are involved, the church must not deal with it privately to avoid "airing family shame," nor press victims simply to endure and forgive; it must protect the victims and report to the police in accordance with local law (cf. Rom 13:4). Reporting does not replace the church's own discipline. A repentant offender is to be forgiven, but forgiveness does not mean restoring him to office, still less giving him renewed access to victims or children; nor may the church press victims to "forgive quickly" or reconcile with the one who harmed them.

> The point: The church is not society's moral police, but it is responsible for sin within. Biblical discipline neither lets things drop to save face nor drives people out without love; it waits in patience and love for repentance and return.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And you are arrogant! Ought you not rather to mourn?" When I hear that someone in the church has fallen, what is my first reaction? Curiosity, gossip (speaking up for a victim, or telling those who need to know, is not gossip), a secret sense that I'm better than they are, or the "mourning" Paul speaks of? When I see sin in my own heart, do I brush past it or grieve over it? Paul says we "really are unleavened," because "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed." I don't put away sin to make myself holy; I put it away because Christ has already made me holy by his blood, and I no longer want the old leaven in my life. Today, is there some "old leaven," a habit, a relationship I should not be in, a bitterness, that I need to grieve over and, in Christ's strength, clean out? If I am the one who has fallen, this Lamb's blood was shed for me too; the way back is open. I grieve over my sin, but not without hope, for God says, "When I see the blood, I will pass over you."
