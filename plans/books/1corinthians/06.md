## 本章脉络

第六章继续处理哥林多教会的问题，分三段。**弟兄告弟兄**（1-8节）：信徒彼此告到不信主的法官面前；保罗问：「为什么不情愿受欺呢？为什么不情愿吃亏呢？」**从前与如今**（9-11节）：「不义的人不能承受神的国」，「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今你们……已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」**身子是为主**（12-20节）：哥林多人说「凡事我都可行」，保罗却说「身子不是为淫乱，乃是为主」，「你们的身子就是圣灵的殿」，「你们是重价买来的。所以，要在你们的身子上荣耀神。」

这一章的中心是第11节：信徒不再是从前的样子，因为他们已经被洗净、成圣、称义。所有的劝勉，都从这个事实出发。

## 深读 6:1-8 为什么不情愿吃亏呢？

**1. 告在不义的人面前。** 「你们中间有彼此相争的事，怎敢在不义的人面前求审，不在圣徒面前求审呢？」（6:1）在罗马的哥林多，民事诉讼常常是有钱有势的人用来压制别人、提高自己地位的工具。保罗所说的「不义的人」，不是说每一个外邦法官都腐败，而是说他们不在神的国里。

**2. 圣徒要审判世界。** 「岂不知圣徒要审判世界吗？……岂不知我们要审判天使吗？何况今生的事呢？」（6:2-3）这是指末日时信徒要与基督一同作王、一同审判（参但7:22；太19:28；启20:4）。既然将来要参与这么大的审判，难道今天连弟兄之间的小事都处理不了吗？

**3. 已经是大错。** 「你们彼此告状，这已经是你们的大错了。为什么不情愿受欺呢？为什么不情愿吃亏呢？你们倒是欺压人、亏负人，况且所欺压所亏负的就是弟兄。」（6:7-8）「大错」原文是「失败」：不论官司打赢还是打输，弟兄彼此告状，本身就已经是失败了。这让人想起耶稣的教导：「有人想要告你，要拿你的里衣，连外衣也由他拿去。」（太5:40）

**4. 这段经文的界限。** 保罗在这里讲的是信徒之间的民事纠纷，例如钱财和生意上的争执。他并不是说信徒在任何情况下都不可以诉诸法律：保罗自己也曾为了福音的缘故上诉于凯撒（徒25:11）；在涉及暴力、虐待、性侵等刑事罪行时，受害人寻求法律保护，正是神设立掌权者的目的（罗13:4）。教会也不可以用「不要告弟兄」来掩盖罪行、压制受害人。

> 要点：在基督里，弟兄之间的纠纷首先应当在教会中以智慧和恩典处理；宁可吃亏，也不愿意叫基督的名受羞辱。但这不是要受害人在罪行面前沉默。

## 深读 6:9-11 但如今你们已经洗净

**1. 不能承受神的国。** 「你们岂不知不义的人不能承受神的国吗？不要自欺！无论是淫乱的、拜偶像的、奸淫的、作娈童的、亲男色的、偷窃的、贪婪的、醉酒的、辱骂的、勒索的，都不能承受神的国。」（6:9-10）这份清单很长，而且包括了各种各样的罪：性方面的罪、拜偶像、偷窃、贪婪、醉酒、恶言。保罗不是要人觉得某一类罪人特别可怕，而是要说明：一个继续活在这些罪中、不肯悔改的人，不能承受神的国。

**2. 关于同性性行为。** 「作娈童的、亲男色的」原文是两个词（*malakoi*、*arsenokoitai*）。历代教会和今天大多数福音派学者认为，这两个词是指同性之间的性行为，后一个词很可能出自利未记18:22和20:13的希腊文旧约。近代也有学者提出不同的解释，认为这里只是指剥削性的关系，例如娈童或性交易。福音派普遍认为，保罗在这里谴责的是同性性行为本身，而不只是剥削性的关系。要留意：保罗把这些罪和贪婪、醉酒、辱骂放在同一份清单里；在神面前，没有哪一种罪人比别人更不需要恩典，也没有哪一种罪人比别人更不能得着恩典。

**3. 从前是这样。** 「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今你们奉主耶稣基督的名，并藉着我们神的灵，已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」（6:11）这是全章最美的一句话。哥林多教会里，正有一些从前就是清单上那样的人：淫乱的、拜偶像的、亲男色的、偷窃的、醉酒的。可是保罗说：「从前是」，「但如今」。三个动词都是过去时态：「洗净」（罪被洗去）、「成圣」（被分别出来归神）、「称义」（在神面前被宣告为义）。这是父（「我们神」）、子（「主耶稣基督的名」）、灵（「神的灵」）一同成就的工作。

> 要点：没有哪一种罪人在神面前比别人更好，也没有哪一种罪人比别人离恩典更远。「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今」已经洗净，成圣，称义了。

## 深读 6:12-20 在你们的身子上荣耀神

**1. 哥林多人的口号。** 「凡事我都可行，但不都有益处。凡事我都可行，但无论哪一件，我总不受它的辖制。」（6:12）许多解经家认为，「凡事我都可行」和「食物是为肚腹，肚腹是为食物」（6:13）是哥林多人自己的口号（ESV用引号标出）。他们可能误解了保罗所传的恩典与自由，以为既然得救是靠恩典，身体做什么都无所谓。保罗没有否定自由，却加上了两个限制：是否「有益处」，以及是否会「受它的辖制」。

**2. 身子是为主。** 「身子不是为淫乱，乃是为主；主也是为身子。并且神已经叫主复活，也要用自己的能力叫我们复活。」（6:13-14）希腊思想常常轻看身体，认为只有灵魂才重要。保罗却说，身体是重要的，因为神要叫它复活（参15章）。神既然要救赎我们的身体，我们今天用身体做什么，就很重要。

**3. 基督的肢体。** 「岂不知你们的身子是基督的肢体吗？我可以将基督的肢体作为娼妓的肢体吗？断乎不可！」（6:15）哥林多城里有许多娼妓，有些与神庙有关。保罗说，信徒的身体已经与基督联合了；与娼妓联合，就是把基督的肢体拿去与她「成为一体」（6:16；引创2:24）。性不只是身体的行为，而是全人的联合。

**4. 逃避淫行。** 「你们要逃避淫行。人所犯的，无论什么罪，都在身子以外，惟有行淫的，是得罪自己的身子。」（6:18）面对性的试探，圣经的建议不是去和它较量，而是「逃避」，像约瑟逃离波提乏的妻子一样（创39:12）。

**5. 圣灵的殿。** 「岂不知你们的身子就是圣灵的殿吗？这圣灵是从神而来，住在你们里头的；并且你们不是自己的人，因为你们是重价买来的。所以，要在你们的身子上荣耀神。」（6:19-20）3:16说整个教会是神的殿；这里说每一个信徒的身体也是圣灵的殿。「重价买来的」是奴隶市场的用语：我们原是罪的奴仆，基督用自己的血把我们买了回来（参彼前1:18-19）。所以我们的身体不再属于自己，而属于那位为我们付了重价的主。

> 要点：身体不是无关紧要的，因为神要叫它复活，圣灵也住在其中。我们是基督用重价买来的，所以要逃避淫行，在身子上荣耀神。

## 默想

**默想：** 「为什么不情愿受欺呢？为什么不情愿吃亏呢？」在华人家庭和教会中，最伤感情的，常常是钱的事：兄弟姊妹之间分家产、合伙做生意的弟兄翻脸、借了钱不还的教会朋友。我心里有没有一笔这样的账，让我和某一位信主的弟兄姊妹已经很久没有说话了？保罗说，弟兄彼此相争，不论谁赢，都已经是失败了。我能宁可吃亏，是因为我知道我已经得着了什么：「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今你们……已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」我的价值、我的保障，不在于这笔钱，而在于那位用重价把我买回来的主。今天，我能不能为这件事向神祷告，并且考虑请一位成熟、公正的弟兄姊妹来帮助我们和好？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 continues with the Corinthian church's problems, in three parts. **Brother suing brother** (vv. 1–8): believers were taking one another before unbelieving judges; Paul asks, "Why not rather suffer wrong? Why not rather be defrauded?" **Then and now** (vv. 9–11): "the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God," "and such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified." **The body is for the Lord** (vv. 12–20): the Corinthians said "All things are lawful for me," but Paul says "the body is not meant for sexual immorality, but for the Lord," "your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit," "you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body."

The center of the chapter is verse 11: believers are no longer what they were, because they have been washed, sanctified and justified. Every exhortation starts from this fact.

## Going Deeper: 6:1–8 Why Not Rather Be Wronged?

**1. Going to law before the unrighteous.** "When one of you has a grievance against another, does he dare go to law before the unrighteous instead of the saints?" (6:1). In Roman Corinth, civil lawsuits were often a tool the wealthy and powerful used to crush others and raise their own standing. By "the unrighteous" Paul doesn't mean every pagan judge was corrupt, but that they were outside God's kingdom.

**2. The saints will judge the world.** "Or do you not know that the saints will judge the world?... Do you not know that we are to judge angels? How much more, then, matters pertaining to this life!" (6:2–3). This refers to believers reigning and judging with Christ at the end (cf. Dan 7:22; Matt 19:28; Rev 20:4). If they are to share in so great a judgment then, can they not settle small matters between brothers now?

**3. Already a defeat.** "To have lawsuits at all with one another is already a defeat for you. Why not rather suffer wrong? Why not rather be defrauded? But you yourselves wrong and defraud, even your own brothers!" (6:7–8). Whether the suit is won or lost, brothers suing each other is already a defeat. This recalls Jesus' teaching: "if anyone would sue you and take your tunic, let him have your cloak as well" (Matt 5:40).

**4. The limits of this passage.** Paul is speaking here of civil disputes between believers, such as quarrels over money and business. He is not saying believers may never turn to the law: Paul himself appealed to Caesar for the gospel's sake (Acts 25:11); and where violence, abuse, sexual assault or other crimes are involved, a victim seeking legal protection is exactly what God established governing authorities for (Rom 13:4). Nor may the church use "don't sue a brother" to cover up crimes or silence victims.

> The point: In Christ, disputes between believers should first be handled within the church with wisdom and grace; better to be wronged than to bring shame on Christ's name. But this does not ask victims to stay silent in the face of crime.

## Going Deeper: 6:9–11 But You Were Washed

**1. Not inheriting the kingdom.** "Or do you not know that the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God? Do not be deceived: neither the sexually immoral, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor men who practice homosexuality, nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor swindlers will inherit the kingdom of God" (6:9–10). The list is long and covers many kinds of sin: sexual sin, idolatry, theft, greed, drunkenness, abusive speech. Paul isn't singling out one kind of sinner as especially dreadful; he is saying that anyone who continues in these sins without repentance will not inherit the kingdom of God.

**2. On same-sex sexual behavior.** "Men who practice homosexuality" translates two Greek words (*malakoi* and *arsenokoitai*; the ESV footnote explains that both are included). The church through history and most evangelical scholars today understand these words to refer to same-sex sexual acts; the second very likely derives from the Greek Old Testament of Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13. Some recent scholars have proposed other readings, limiting the words to exploitative relationships such as pederasty or prostitution. Evangelicals generally hold that Paul is condemning same-sex sexual acts as such, not only exploitative ones. Notice too that Paul puts these sins on the same list as greed, drunkenness and reviling: before God no kind of sinner needs grace less than another, and no kind of sinner is beyond its reach.

**3. Such were some of you.** "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God" (6:11). This is the most beautiful sentence in the chapter. The Corinthian church included people who had been exactly what the list describes: sexually immoral, idolaters, men who practiced homosexuality, thieves, drunkards. But Paul says "were," and then "but." All three verbs are past tense: "washed" (sins cleansed), "sanctified" (set apart for God), "justified" (declared righteous before God). It is the work of the Father ("our God"), the Son ("the name of the Lord Jesus Christ") and the Spirit ("the Spirit of our God") together.

> The point: No kind of sinner is better than another before God, and none is farther from grace than another. "Such were some of you. But" you were washed, sanctified and justified.

## Going Deeper: 6:12–20 Glorify God in Your Body

**1. Corinthian slogans.** "'All things are lawful for me,' but not all things are helpful. 'All things are lawful for me,' but I will not be enslaved by anything" (6:12). Many interpreters think "All things are lawful for me" and "Food is meant for the stomach and the stomach for food" (6:13) were the Corinthians' own slogans (the ESV puts them in quotation marks). They may have misunderstood the grace and freedom Paul preached, thinking that since salvation is by grace, what one does with the body doesn't matter. Paul doesn't deny freedom, but he adds two limits: is it "helpful," and will I be "enslaved" by it?

**2. The body is for the Lord.** "The body is not meant for sexual immorality, but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body. And God raised the Lord and will also raise us up by his power" (6:13–14). Greek thought often looked down on the body, as if only the soul mattered. Paul says the body matters, because God will raise it (cf. ch. 15). Since God intends to redeem our bodies, what we do with them now matters.

**3. Members of Christ.** "Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ? Shall I then take the members of Christ and make them members of a prostitute? Never!" (6:15). Corinth had many prostitutes, some connected with temples. Paul says believers' bodies have been united with Christ; to unite with a prostitute is to take a member of Christ and "become one body with her" (6:16, quoting Gen 2:24). Sex is not merely a bodily act but a union of the whole person.

**4. Flee.** "Flee from sexual immorality. Every other sin a person commits is outside the body, but the sexually immoral person sins against his own body" (6:18). Faced with sexual temptation, Scripture's counsel is not to wrestle with it but to "flee," as Joseph fled from Potiphar's wife (Gen 39:12).

**5. A temple of the Holy Spirit.** "Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body" (6:19–20). In 3:16 the whole church is God's temple; here each believer's body is also a temple of the Spirit. "Bought with a price" is the language of the slave market: we were slaves of sin, and Christ bought us back with his own blood (cf. 1 Pet 1:18–19). So our bodies no longer belong to ourselves, but to the Lord who paid that price for us.

> The point: The body is not unimportant, because God will raise it and the Spirit dwells in it. We were bought by Christ at a great price, so we flee sexual immorality and glorify God in our bodies.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Why not rather suffer wrong? Why not rather be defrauded?" In Chinese families and churches, what wounds relationships most is often money: siblings dividing an inheritance, brothers in business together falling out, a church friend who borrowed money and never paid it back. Is there such an account in my heart, one that has kept me from speaking to a Christian brother or sister for a long time? Paul says that when brothers fight, whoever wins, it is already a defeat. I can be willing to lose because I know what I have already gained: "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified." My worth and my security lie not in this money but in the Lord who bought me back at a great price. Today, can I pray about this, and consider asking a mature, fair-minded brother or sister to help us be reconciled?
