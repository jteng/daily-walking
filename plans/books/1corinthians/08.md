## 本章脉络

第八至十章是一个整体，回答哥林多人问的另一件事：「论到祭偶像之物」（8:1）。在哥林多，许多肉类来自神庙的献祭；神庙旁边也有宴会厅，朋友聚餐、庆祝生日或生意往来，常常在那里坐席。信了主的人还可以吃这些食物吗？第八章分三段。**知识与爱心**（1-3节）：「知识是叫人自高自大，惟有爱心能造就人。」**一位神，一位主**（4-6节）：偶像算不得什么，「我们只有一位神，就是父……并有一位主，就是耶稣基督」。**为软弱的弟兄着想**（7-13节）：不是人人都有这知识；有知识的人若不顾软弱的弟兄，就是「得罪基督」。

这一章的中心是第1节：问题不只是我知道什么，更是我怎样爱人。保罗在第九章用自己的榜样说明这个原则，在第十章再回到祭偶像的问题，并且说得更直接：信徒不可参与拜偶像（10:14-22）。

## 深读 8:1-3 知识与爱心

**1. 「我们都有知识」。** 「论到祭偶像之物，我们晓得我们都有知识。但知识是叫人自高自大，惟有爱心能造就人。」（8:1）许多解经家认为，「我们都有知识」是哥林多人的口号（ESV加上了引号）。他们以为：既然我们知道偶像是假的，吃祭偶像之物有什么关系？保罗没有说他们的知识是错的，他说的是：知识若没有爱，就只会叫人「自高自大」（直译是吹胀起来，第4章和第5章已经用过这个词）。

**2. 以为自己知道。** 「若有人以为自己知道什么，按他所当知道的，他仍是不知道。」（8:2）真正认识神的人，会越来越知道自己所知道的有限。一个以为自己什么都明白、因此看不起别人的人，正说明他还不知道该怎样知道。

**3. 被神知道。** 「若有人爱神，这人乃是神所知道的。」（8:3）我们以为保罗会说：若有人爱神，这人就是认识神的。保罗却把重点倒转过来：最重要的不是我认识神多少，而是神认识我。「知道」在圣经里常常是指拣选和亲密的关系（参加4:9；提后2:19）。我们能够爱神，是因为神先认识了我们、爱了我们（约一4:19）。

> 要点：最重要的不是我知道多少，而是神认识我。被神认识、被神爱的人，会用爱心造就人，而不是用知识压人。

## 深读 8:4-6 一位神，一位主

**1. 偶像算不得什么。** 「论到吃祭偶像之物，我们知道偶像在世上算不得什么，也知道神只有一位，再没有别的神。」（8:4）这是正确的：木头石头做的像不是神。可是保罗在10:20还要补充：「外邦人所献的祭是祭鬼，不是祭神」。偶像本身虽然算不得什么，偶像背后却有真实的属灵势力。

**2. 许多的神，许多的主。** 「虽有称为神的，或在天，或在地，就如那许多的神，许多的主。」（8:5）哥林多城里有阿波罗、爱神阿芙罗狄蒂、医神亚斯克勒庇俄斯等许多神的庙宇，城中也有对罗马皇帝的崇拜。对哥林多的信徒来说，「许多的神，许多的主」是每天都看得见的。

**3. 改写的「示玛」。** 「然而我们只有一位神，就是父－万物都本于他；我们也归于他－并有一位主，就是耶稣基督－万物都是藉着他有的；我们也是藉着他有的。」（8:6）以色列人每天要背诵申命记6:4：「以色列啊，你要听！耶和华－我们神是独一的主。」许多学者（如包衡〔Richard Bauckham〕、赖特〔N. T. Wright〕）认为，保罗在这里把这句话分成两半：「神」是父，「主」是耶稣基督。他没有在独一的神之外，再加上第二位神，而是把耶稣放在独一真神的身分之内：万物都是藉着他造的（参约1:3；西1:16）。这是新约很早就有的三一真理的根据：父与子是两个不同的位格，却是同一位神；圣灵也一样（参12:4-6）。

> 要点：世上有「许多的神，许多的主」，但我们只有一位神，就是父，并有一位主，就是耶稣基督。我们的生命本于他，也归于他。

## 深读 8:7-13 为软弱的弟兄着想

**1. 软弱的良心。** 「但人不都有这等知识。有人到如今因拜惯了偶像，就以为所吃的是祭偶像之物。他们的良心既然软弱，也就污秽了。」（8:7）「软弱的人」是那些从前拜惯了偶像、刚信主不久的人。对他们来说，吃祭偶像之物仍然好像在拜那个偶像；他们若勉强去吃，就是违背自己的良心。

**2. 食物不能叫神看中我们。** 「其实食物不能叫神看中我们，因为我们不吃也无损，吃也无益。」（8:8）食物本身是中性的（参提前4:4）。可是正因为吃不吃都「无损」、「无益」，有知识的人就更不应该坚持自己吃的权利。

**3. 绊脚石。** 「只是你们要谨慎，恐怕你们这自由竟成了那软弱人的绊脚石。若有人见你这有知识的，在偶像的庙里坐席，这人的良心若是软弱，岂不放胆去吃那祭偶像之物吗？」（8:9-10）「绊脚石」不是指别人看了不高兴，而是指别人因此跌倒：违背良心，甚至回到拜偶像的路上。

**4. 「基督为他死的」。** 「因此，基督为他死的那软弱弟兄，也就因你的知识沉沦了。你们这样得罪弟兄们，伤了他们软弱的良心，就是得罪基督。」（8:11-12）「沉沦」原文是 *apollymi*，有人理解为最终的灭亡，是真实而严肃的警告；也有人理解为严重的属灵伤害，叫他的信心破碎。无论哪一种，保罗都要有知识的人看见：那个软弱的人，是「基督为他死的」。基督为他舍了命，我难道不能为他放下一块肉吗？

**5. 「永远不吃肉」。** 「所以，食物若叫我弟兄跌倒，我就永远不吃肉，免得叫我弟兄跌倒了。」（8:13）这是保罗为自己作的决定，是爱的夸张说法，不是一条要求所有人遵守的规条。

**6. 今天的华人处境。** 对许多华人信徒来说，祭偶像之物不是古代的问题：家里拜祖先的供品、中元节的普渡、丧礼上的祭品、庙会的食物，常常就摆在面前。这里要分清两件事：
- **参与祭拜本身**，例如上香、跪拜祖先牌位或神明、烧纸钱，不是良心强弱的问题，而是拜偶像，信徒不可参与（10:20-21）。
- **祭拜以后的食物**，例如拜过之后家人一起吃的饭菜，食物本身并没有被污染（8:8；10:25-26）；但保罗要我们顾念别人的良心，以及在场的人怎样理解我们的举动（10:28）。第十章会更详细地讨论这一点。

从民间信仰背景信主的弟兄姊妹，若对这些食物特别敏感，教会不可以笑他们「迷信」，也不可以勉强他们去吃。信徒也可以用别的方式孝敬父母、尊荣祖先：在父母在世时尽心照顾，在丧礼和追思时献花、致辞、扫墓，并用温柔敬畏的心向家人说明自己的信仰（出20:12；彼前3:15）。

> 要点：自由要受爱心的约束。那个软弱的弟兄，是基督为他死的；为了他，我可以放下自己的权利。

## 默想

**默想：** 「知识是叫人自高自大，惟有爱心能造就人。」我可能读过很多神学书，对圣经有很清楚的立场，在网上或小组里讨论时，常常是那个「对」的人。可是我说话的方式，是让人更爱主，还是让人觉得自己笨、不属灵？有没有一位弟兄姊妹，因为我的「知识」，在教会里不敢再开口？保罗没有叫我放弃真理，却提醒我两件事：第一，我所知道的，远比我以为的少（8:2）；第二，我最大的安慰，不是我认识神有多少，而是「这人乃是神所知道的」（8:3）。我在神面前站得住，不是因为我比别人懂得多，而是因为神在我还不认识他的时候，就先认识我、爱我，并且差他的儿子为我死。那个被我看不起的弟兄，也是「基督为他死的」（8:11）。今天，我能不能为那些曾经被我的「知识」伤害的人祷告，并且下一次开口之前，先问自己：这句话能造就他吗？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapters 8–10 form one unit, answering another of the Corinthians' questions: "Now concerning food offered to idols" (8:1). In Corinth much of the meat came from temple sacrifices, and temples had dining rooms where people ate together with friends, celebrated birthdays or did business. Could believers still eat such food? Chapter 8 has three parts. **Knowledge and love** (vv. 1–3): "'knowledge' puffs up, but love builds up." **One God, one Lord** (vv. 4–6): an idol is nothing, and "for us there is one God, the Father... and one Lord, Jesus Christ." **Thinking of the weak brother** (vv. 7–13): not everyone has this knowledge, and those who do, if they disregard the weak brother, "sin against Christ."

The center of the chapter is verse 1: the question is not only "what do I know?" but "how do I love?" In chapter 9 Paul illustrates this principle from his own example, and in chapter 10 he returns to idol food and speaks more directly still: believers may not take part in idolatry (10:14–22).

## Going Deeper: 8:1–3 Knowledge and Love

**1. "All of us possess knowledge."** "Now concerning food offered to idols: we know that 'all of us possess knowledge.' This 'knowledge' puffs up, but love builds up" (8:1). Many interpreters take "all of us possess knowledge" as a Corinthian slogan (the ESV puts it in quotation marks). The Corinthians reasoned: since we know idols are false, what does it matter if we eat food offered to them? Paul doesn't say their knowledge is wrong. He says that knowledge without love only "puffs up," the same word he used in chapters 4 and 5.

**2. Imagining that one knows.** "If anyone imagines that he knows something, he does not yet know as he ought to know" (8:2). Someone who truly knows God grows more aware of how limited his knowledge is. A person who thinks he understands everything and so looks down on others shows that he does not yet know as he ought.

**3. Known by God.** "But if anyone loves God, he is known by God" (8:3). We expect the sentence to say, "if anyone loves God, he knows God," but Paul turns the emphasis around: what matters most is not how much I know God, but that God knows me. In Scripture "to know" often refers to choosing and intimate relationship (cf. Gal 4:9; 2 Tim 2:19). We are able to love God because God first knew us and loved us (1 John 4:19).

> The point: What matters most is not how much I know, but that God knows me. Those whom God knows and loves will build others up with love, not crush them with knowledge.

## Going Deeper: 8:4–6 One God, One Lord

**1. An idol is nothing.** "Therefore, as to the eating of food offered to idols, we know that 'an idol has no real existence,' and that 'there is no God but one'" (8:4). This is true: an image of wood or stone is not a god. But in 10:20 Paul will add, "what pagans sacrifice they offer to demons and not to God." The idol itself is nothing, but there are real spiritual powers behind idolatry.

**2. Many gods and many lords.** "For although there may be so-called gods in heaven or on earth, as indeed there are many 'gods' and many 'lords'" (8:5). Corinth had shrines to Apollo, to Aphrodite, to Asclepius the god of healing and to many others, and the worship of the Roman emperors was also present in the city. For the believers in Corinth, "many gods and many lords" were visible every day.

**3. The Shema reworked.** "Yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things and for whom we exist, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things and through whom we exist" (8:6). Israel recited Deuteronomy 6:4 daily: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one." Many scholars (e.g. Richard Bauckham, N. T. Wright) see Paul here dividing this confession in two: "God" is the Father, "Lord" is Jesus Christ. He does not add a second god alongside the one God; he places Jesus within the identity of the one true God, the one through whom all things were made (cf. John 1:3; Col 1:16). This is an early New Testament foundation for the truth of the Trinity: the Father and the Son are distinct persons, yet one God, and so too the Spirit (cf. 12:4–6).

> The point: The world has "many gods and many lords," but for us there is one God, the Father, and one Lord, Jesus Christ. Our life is from him and for him.

## Going Deeper: 8:7–13 Thinking of the Weak Brother

**1. The weak conscience.** "However, not all possess this knowledge. But some, through former association with idols, eat food as really offered to an idol, and their conscience, being weak, is defiled" (8:7). "The weak" are those who were long accustomed to idols and are new to the faith. For them, eating idol food still feels like worshiping the idol; if they force themselves to eat, they violate their own conscience.

**2. Food will not commend us to God.** "Food will not commend us to God. We are no worse off if we do not eat, and no better off if we do" (8:8). Food itself is neutral (cf. 1 Tim 4:4). But precisely because eating or not eating makes us "no worse off" and "no better off," those with knowledge have all the less reason to insist on their right to eat.

**3. A stumbling block.** "But take care that this right of yours does not somehow become a stumbling block to the weak. For if anyone sees you who have knowledge eating in an idol's temple, will he not be encouraged, if his conscience is weak, to eat food offered to idols?" (8:9–10). A "stumbling block" is not something that merely displeases others; it is something that makes them fall: to act against conscience, even to return to idolatry.

**4. "The brother for whom Christ died."** "And so by your knowledge this weak person is destroyed, the brother for whom Christ died. Thus, sinning against your brothers and wounding their conscience when it is weak, you sin against Christ" (8:11–12). "Destroyed" translates *apollymi*. Some take it as final ruin, a real and serious warning; others as grave spiritual harm that shatters his faith. Either way, Paul wants the one with knowledge to see that the weak person is "the brother for whom Christ died." Christ gave his life for him; can I not give up a piece of meat for him?

**5. "I will never eat meat."** "Therefore, if food makes my brother stumble, I will never eat meat, lest I make my brother stumble" (8:13). This is Paul's decision for himself, love's hyperbole, not a rule binding on everyone.

**6. The Chinese setting today.** For many Chinese believers, idol food is not an ancient issue: offerings to ancestors at home, Ghost Festival offerings, funeral offerings and temple-festival food are often right in front of them. Two things need to be distinguished:
- **Taking part in the rite itself**, such as offering incense, bowing before ancestral tablets or gods, or burning paper money, is not a question of strong or weak conscience; it is idolatry, and believers may not take part (10:20–21).
- **Food after the rite**, such as the dishes the family eats together once the offering is over, is not itself defiled (8:8; 10:25–26); but Paul asks us to consider the conscience of others and how those present will understand what we do (10:28). Chapter 10 takes this up in more detail.

When brothers and sisters who came to faith from folk-religion backgrounds are especially sensitive about such food, the church must not laugh at them as "superstitious," nor pressure them to eat. Believers can also honor their parents and ancestors in other ways: caring for parents wholeheartedly while they live; bringing flowers, speaking and tending graves at funerals and memorials; and explaining their faith to their families with gentleness and respect (Exod 20:12; 1 Pet 3:15).

> The point: Freedom is governed by love. The weak brother is one for whom Christ died; for his sake, I can lay down my rights.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "'Knowledge' puffs up, but love builds up." I may have read many theology books and hold clear positions on Scripture; in online discussions or small group, I am often the one who is "right." But does the way I speak help others love the Lord more, or leave them feeling foolish and unspiritual? Is there a brother or sister who no longer dares to speak up in church because of my "knowledge"? Paul doesn't ask me to give up the truth, but he reminds me of two things. First, I know far less than I think (8:2). Second, my greatest comfort is not how much I know God, but that I am "known by God" (8:3). I stand before God not because I understand more than others, but because God knew me and loved me before I knew him, and sent his Son to die for me. The brother I look down on is also one "for whom Christ died" (8:11). Today, can I pray for those my "knowledge" has hurt, and before I speak next time, ask myself: will this build him up?
