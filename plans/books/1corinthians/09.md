## 本章脉络

第九章看起来离开了祭偶像之物的话题，其实是用保罗自己的生命，说明第八章的原则：有权利，却为了爱和福音的缘故不用。这一章分三段。**使徒的权利**（1-14节）：保罗有权柄靠福音养生，这有常理、律法和主的命令为根据。**为福音不用这权柄**（15-23节）：保罗宁可自己做工，叫人「不花钱得福音」；他「甘心作了众人的仆人」，「向什么样的人，我就作什么样的人。无论如何，总要救些人」。**攻克己身**（24-27节）：用运动员作比喻，说明为福音的缘故，要有节制、有方向地奔跑。

这一章的中心是19节和22节：「我虽是自由的，无人辖管；然而我甘心作了众人的仆人，为要多得人。」「向什么样的人，我就作什么样的人。无论如何，总要救些人。」保罗不是被迫放下权利，而是为了福音，甘心放下。

## 深读 9:1-14 使徒的权利

**1. 「我不是自由的吗？」** 「我不是自由的吗？我不是使徒吗？我不是见过我们的主耶稣吗？你们不是我在主里面所做之工吗？」（9:1）哥林多教会里有人质疑保罗的使徒身分（9:3）。保罗的回答是：他亲眼见过复活的主（参15:8；徒9:3-6），而哥林多教会本身就是他「作使徒的印证」（9:2）。

**2. 「娶信主的姊妹为妻」。** 「难道我们没有权柄娶信主的姊妹为妻，带着一同往来，仿佛其余的使徒和主的弟兄并矶法一样吗？」（9:5）彼得（矶法）是结了婚的人（参可1:30），而且他的妻子与他一同出外服事。保罗自己没有结婚（7:7-8），但他说他有同样的权柄。

**3. 三个理由。** 保罗用三个理由说明传道的人可以靠福音养生：
- **常理**：「有谁当兵自备粮饷呢？有谁栽葡萄园不吃园里的果子呢？」（9:7）
- **律法**：「牛在场上踹谷的时候，不可笼住它的嘴。」（9:9；引申25:4）保罗问：「难道神所挂念的是牛吗？」他不是说神不在乎牛，而是说这条律法背后的原则（作工的人应当分享他工作的果子）更是为我们写的（9:10；参罗15:4）。在申命记中，这节经文正夹在保护人的律例当中（申24-25章）。
- **主的命令**：「主也是这样命定，叫传福音的靠着福音养生。」（9:14；参路10:7）

**4. 今天的应用。** 华人教会有时会不自觉地认为，传道人越清贫越属灵，薪水低一点也是应该的。保罗却说，供应传道人，是「主……命定」的。教会应当让忠心的传道人和他的家人不必为基本的生活忧虑（参提前5:17-18）。

> 要点：传道人靠福音养生，是神所命定的，教会应当甘心乐意地供应。

## 深读 9:15-18 不花钱得福音

**1. 宁可死。** 「但这权柄我全没有用过。我写这话，并非要你们这样待我，因为我宁可死也不叫人使我所夸的落了空。」（9:15）保罗在哥林多的时候，和亚居拉、百基拉一同制造帐棚（徒18:3）。他为什么不接受哥林多人的供应？保罗自己的回答是：「免得基督的福音被阻隔」（9:12）。许多学者补充说，在当时的哥林多，接受富人的钱，常常就意味着成了他的门客，要听他的话、替他争面子（参林后11:7-12）。保罗却接受了马其顿教会的供给（林后11:8-9），可见这是他在哥林多的选择，不是一条规条。他不愿意福音被人当作可以用钱买来的东西。

**2. 「我便有祸了」。** 「我传福音原没有可夸的，因为我是不得已的。若不传福音，我便有祸了。」（9:16）保罗传福音，不是因为这是他自己挑选的职业，而是因为神在大马士革路上抓住了他，把这责任托付给他（9:17）。

**3. 我的赏赐。** 「既是这样，我的赏赐是什么呢？就是我传福音的时候叫人不花钱得福音。」（9:18）保罗的「赏赐」不是钱，也不是名声，而是能够白白地把福音传给人。这正配得上福音本身：福音是神白白赐给我们的恩典（罗3:24）。

> 要点：福音是白白得来的，保罗也愿意白白地传给人。他最大的赏赐，是看见人不花钱得着福音。

## 深读 9:19-23 向什么样的人，我就作什么样的人

**1. 自由人甘心作仆人。** 「我虽是自由的，无人辖管；然而我甘心作了众人的仆人，为要多得人。」（9:19）这正是福音的样式：基督「本有神的形象」，却「虚己，取了奴仆的形象」（腓2:6-7）；「他本来富足，却为你们成了贫穷」（林后8:9）。

**2. 向犹太人，向没有律法的人。** 「向犹太人，我就作犹太人，为要得犹太人……向没有律法的人，我就作没有律法的人，为要得没有律法的人；其实我在神面前，不是没有律法；在基督面前，正在律法之下。」（9:20-21）在犹太人中间，保罗可以守犹太人的规矩（例如徒16:3给提摩太行割礼）；在外邦人中间，他可以与他们一同吃饭。可是有两条界线：第一，他「不在律法以下」：他已不在摩西律法的约之下，这律法不再是管辖他生命的制度，更不是他得救的根据；所以守犹太人的规矩，对他是为福音而有的自由选择；第二，他「在基督面前，正在律法之下」（参加6:2），不会为了得人而犯罪。

**3. 这不是妥协。** 「向什么样的人，我就作什么样的人」不是说为了让人接受，什么都可以改。当有人要求外邦信徒受割礼才能得救时，保罗就「没有勉强」提多受割礼（加2:3），甚至当面抵挡彼得（加2:11-14）。在不影响福音的事上，保罗愿意改变自己；在福音的核心上，他一步也不让。十九世纪来华的宣教士戴德生，穿上中国人的衣服、梳起辫子，为的是让中国人能听福音，而不是先被他的洋装挡在门外；但他所传的，仍然是同一个福音。

**4. 「总要救些人」。** 「向软弱的人，我就作软弱的人，为要得软弱的人。向什么样的人，我就作什么样的人。无论如何，总要救些人。凡我所行的，都是为福音的缘故，为要与人同得这福音的好处。」（9:22-23）保罗不是以为自己能够救人，他知道救人的是神（3:6-7）；但神使用他的委身和弹性，作为救人的途径。

> 要点：在不影响福音的事上，我愿意放下自己的习惯和权利；在福音的核心上，我一步也不让。

## 深读 9:24-27 攻克己身

**1. 地峡运动会。** 「岂不知在场上赛跑的都跑，但得奖赏的只有一人？」（9:24）哥林多附近每两年举行一次地峡运动会，是希腊最重要的运动会之一，哥林多人都很熟悉。保罗不是说只有一个信徒能得救，而是说：要像那个决心得奖的人一样，全力以赴。

**2. 不能坏的冠冕。** 「凡较力争胜的，诸事都有节制，他们不过是要得能坏的冠冕；我们却是要得不能坏的冠冕。」（9:25）运动会的冠冕是用松枝或芹菜编成的，过几天就枯干了。运动员为了这样的冠冕，尚且长时间节制饮食、严格训练；我们所盼望的是永不衰残的冠冕（参彼前5:4）。

**3. 「被弃绝」。** 「我是攻克己身，叫身服我，恐怕我传福音给别人，自己反被弃绝了。」（9:27）「被弃绝」原文是 *adokimos*，意思是经不起考验、不合格。这句话有几种理解：
- 阿民念派与卫斯理宗的传统认为，真信徒有可能最终离弃信仰而失丧，所以这是对真信徒的警告（参10:12）。
- 改革宗传统认为，这是真实的警告，而神正是使用这样的警告，保守他的子民坚忍到底（参1:8-9）；那些最终离开的人，显明他们原本就不是真属主的（约一2:19）。
- 也有人（包括一些时代论的解经家）认为，这里讲的是失去服事的资格或赏赐，而不是失去救恩。

紧接着的10:1-12用以色列人「在旷野倒毙」作鉴戒，并且说「自己以为站得稳的，须要谨慎，免得跌倒」（10:5，12），每一种理解都要面对这段上下文。

无论哪一种理解，保罗都不是靠自己的努力赚得救恩。他认真奔跑，正是因为他已经被基督得着（腓3:12）；他的确据，在于「神是信实的」（1:9）。

> 要点：我们认真奔跑，不是为了赚得救恩，而是因为那位信实的神已经召了我们，也必坚固我们到底。

## 默想

**默想：** 「向什么样的人，我就作什么样的人。无论如何，总要救些人。」许多海外的华人教会，讲的是自己的语言，吃的是家乡的饭菜，彼此之间很亲切，这是神给的恩典。可是我们也可能在不知不觉中，只在自己熟悉的圈子里打转：同事、邻居、孩子同学的父母，若不是华人，或者不会说我们的语言，就好像和教会没有什么关系。我自己呢？我身边有没有一个和我很不一样的人，背景、语言、年龄、生活方式都不同，我却从来没有想过要走进他的世界，与他做朋友，让他有机会听见福音？保罗之所以能够这样做，是因为他先经历了这样的爱：我们这些「从前远离神的人」，原是「在所应许的诸约上是局外人」，基督却亲自来到我们中间，「如今……靠着他的血，已经得亲近了」（弗2:12-13）。今天，我能不能求主让我看见一个这样的人，并且为他祷告，想一想我可以怎样为他放下一点自己的方便？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 9 seems to leave the subject of idol food, but in fact it illustrates chapter 8's principle from Paul's own life: having a right, yet not using it, for the sake of love and the gospel. The chapter has three parts. **The rights of an apostle** (vv. 1–14): Paul has the right to get his living by the gospel, grounded in common sense, the Law and the Lord's command. **Not using that right for the gospel** (vv. 15–23): Paul would rather work with his own hands and "present the gospel free of charge"; he has "made myself a servant to all," and "I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some." **Self-discipline** (vv. 24–27): using the picture of an athlete, he shows that for the gospel's sake we run with self-control and purpose.

The center of the chapter is verses 19 and 22: "For though I am free from all, I have made myself a servant to all, that I might win more of them." "I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some." Paul was not forced to lay down his rights; he laid them down gladly for the gospel.

## Going Deeper: 9:1–14 The Rights of an Apostle

**1. "Am I not free?"** "Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen Jesus our Lord? Are not you my workmanship in the Lord?" (9:1). Some in the Corinthian church questioned Paul's apostleship (9:3). His answer is that he saw the risen Lord with his own eyes (cf. 15:8; Acts 9:3–6), and that the Corinthian church itself is "the seal of my apostleship in the Lord" (9:2).

**2. "A believing wife."** "Do we not have the right to take along a believing wife, as do the other apostles and the brothers of the Lord and Cephas?" (9:5). Peter (Cephas) was married (cf. Mark 1:30), and his wife traveled with him in ministry. Paul himself was unmarried (7:7–8), but he says he has the same right.

**3. Three reasons.** Paul gives three reasons why those who preach may get their living from the gospel:
- **Common sense**: "Who serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard without eating any of its fruit?" (9:7).
- **The Law**: "You shall not muzzle an ox when it treads out the grain" (9:9, quoting Deut 25:4). Paul asks, "Is it for oxen that God is concerned?" He is not saying God doesn't care about oxen, but that the principle behind the law (the worker should share in the fruit of his work) was written even more for our sake (9:10; cf. Rom 15:4). In Deuteronomy the verse sits among laws protecting people (Deut 24–25).
- **The Lord's command**: "In the same way, the Lord commanded that those who proclaim the gospel should get their living by the gospel" (9:14; cf. Luke 10:7).

**4. For today.** Chinese churches sometimes assume, without quite saying so, that the poorer a pastor is the more spiritual he is, and that a low salary is only to be expected. Paul says that supporting those who preach is something "the Lord commanded." Churches should see to it that faithful pastors and their families need not worry about the basic needs of life (cf. 1 Tim 5:17–18).

> The point: That those who preach get their living by the gospel is God's own command, and the church should provide for them gladly.

## Going Deeper: 9:15–18 The Gospel Free of Charge

**1. Rather die.** "But I have made no use of any of these rights, nor am I writing these things to secure any such provision. For I would rather die than have anyone deprive me of my ground for boasting" (9:15). In Corinth Paul made tents with Aquila and Priscilla (Acts 18:3). Why did he refuse the Corinthians' support? Paul's own answer is "rather than put an obstacle in the way of the gospel of Christ" (9:12). Many scholars add that in Corinth at the time, accepting a wealthy person's money often meant becoming his "client," obliged to do his bidding and enhance his standing (cf. 2 Cor 11:7–12). Yet Paul did accept support from the Macedonian churches (2 Cor 11:8–9), so this was a choice for Corinth, not a rule. He refused to let the gospel be treated as something money could buy.

**2. "Woe to me."** "For if I preach the gospel, that gives me no ground for boasting. For necessity is laid upon me. Woe to me if I do not preach the gospel!" (9:16). Paul preached not because it was a career he had chosen, but because God had seized him on the Damascus road and entrusted him with this stewardship (9:17).

**3. My reward.** "What then is my reward? That in my preaching I may present the gospel free of charge" (9:18). Paul's "reward" is not money or fame, but being able to give the gospel away freely. This fits the gospel itself: the gospel is grace God gives us freely (Rom 3:24).

> The point: The gospel is received freely, and Paul gladly gave it away freely. His greatest reward was seeing people receive it free of charge.

## Going Deeper: 9:19–23 All Things to All People

**1. The free man who becomes a servant.** "For though I am free from all, I have made myself a servant to all, that I might win more of them" (9:19). This is the shape of the gospel itself: Christ, "though he was in the form of God," "made himself nothing, taking the form of a servant" (Phil 2:6–7); "though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor" (2 Cor 8:9).

**2. To the Jews, to those outside the law.** "To the Jews I became as a Jew, in order to win Jews... To those outside the law I became as one outside the law (not being outside the law of God but under the law of Christ) that I might win those outside the law" (9:20–21). Among Jews Paul could keep Jewish customs (e.g. circumcising Timothy, Acts 16:3); among Gentiles he could eat with them. But there are two boundaries. First, he is "not being myself under the law": he is no longer under the Mosaic law-covenant as the authority that governs his life, still less as a way of being right with God; so keeping Jewish customs is, for him, a free choice for the gospel. Second, he is "under the law of Christ" (cf. Gal 6:2): he will not sin in order to win people.

**3. Not compromise.** "All things to all people" does not mean changing anything at all to win acceptance. When some demanded that Gentile believers be circumcised to be saved, Paul would not have Titus "forced to be circumcised" (Gal 2:3), and he even opposed Peter to his face (Gal 2:11–14). In matters that do not touch the gospel, Paul was willing to change himself; at the gospel's core, he would not give an inch. In the nineteenth century the missionary Hudson Taylor put on Chinese clothes and adopted the queue so that Chinese people could hear the gospel without first being put off by his Western dress; but what he preached was the same gospel.

**4. "That by all means I might save some."** "To the weak I became weak, that I might win the weak. I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some. I do it all for the sake of the gospel, that I may share with them in its blessings" (9:22–23). Paul doesn't think he can save anyone himself; he knows it is God who saves (3:6–7). But God uses his commitment and flexibility as the means of saving people.

> The point: In matters that do not touch the gospel, I am willing to lay down my habits and rights; at the gospel's core, I will not give an inch.

## Going Deeper: 9:24–27 Self-Discipline

**1. The Isthmian Games.** "Do you not know that in a race all the runners compete, but only one receives the prize?" (9:24). The Isthmian Games, held every two years near Corinth, were among the most important games in Greece, and every Corinthian knew them. Paul isn't saying only one believer will be saved, but that we should give ourselves fully, like the runner determined to win.

**2. An imperishable wreath.** "Every athlete exercises self-control in all things. They do it to receive a perishable wreath, but we an imperishable" (9:25). The victor's wreath at the games was made of pine or celery and withered within days. Athletes would discipline their diet and train hard for a long time for such a wreath; what we hope for is a crown that never fades (cf. 1 Pet 5:4).

**3. "Disqualified."** "But I discipline my body and keep it under control, lest after preaching to others I myself should be disqualified" (9:27). "Disqualified" translates *adokimos*: failing the test, not approved. The sentence is read in several ways:
- The Arminian and Wesleyan traditions hold that a true believer may finally abandon the faith and be lost, so this is a warning to true believers (cf. 10:12).
- The Reformed tradition holds that this is a real warning, and that God uses such warnings as the very means by which he keeps his people persevering to the end (cf. 1:8–9); those who finally depart show that they never truly belonged (1 John 2:19).
- Others, including some dispensational interpreters, take it as losing one's qualification for ministry or one's reward, not losing salvation.

The next verses (10:1–12) set this warning beside Israel, "overthrown in the wilderness," and add, "let anyone who thinks that he stands take heed lest he fall" (10:5, 12); every view must reckon with that context.

On any reading, Paul is not earning his salvation by his own effort. He runs in earnest precisely because Christ has already made him his own (Phil 3:12), and his assurance rests on this: "God is faithful" (1:9).

> The point: We run in earnest, not to earn salvation, but because the faithful God has called us and will sustain us to the end.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some." Many overseas Chinese churches speak their own language, eat the food of home and are warmly close to one another; this is a gift from God. But without noticing, we can keep circling within our own familiar world: colleagues, neighbors, the parents of our children's classmates, if they aren't Chinese or don't speak our language, seem to have nothing to do with church. What about me? Is there someone near me who is very different from me, in background, language, age or way of life, whose world I have never thought of entering, to become their friend and give them a chance to hear the gospel? Paul could do this because he had first experienced this kind of love: we who "once were far off" were "strangers to the covenants of promise," yet Christ himself came among us, and "now in Christ Jesus" we "have been brought near by the blood of Christ" (Eph 2:12–13). Today, can I ask the Lord to show me one such person, pray for them, and think about how I might give up a little of my own convenience for their sake?
