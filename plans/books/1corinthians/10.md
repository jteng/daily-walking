## 本章脉络

第十章是第八至十章的结尾，保罗回到祭偶像之物的问题，这一次说得更直接。这一章分三段。**以色列人的鉴戒**（1-13节）：以色列人都经历了神的恩典，却多半倒毙在旷野；「自己以为站得稳的，须要谨慎，免得跌倒」，但「神是信实的」。**主的筵席与鬼的筵席**（14-22节）：「你们要逃避拜偶像的事」；信徒在圣餐中与基督相交，就不能同时参与拜偶像的筵席。**为荣耀神而行**（10:23-11:1）：市场上的肉和不信的人家里的饭，信徒可以吃；但若有人特别指出「这是献过祭的物」，就为别人的良心不吃。「你们或吃或喝，无论做什么，都要为荣耀神而行。」

第八章讲的是爱心：为软弱的弟兄放下自己的权利；第十章讲的是忠诚：信徒只属于一位主，不能「喝主的杯又喝鬼的杯」。

## 深读 10:1-13 以色列人的鉴戒

**1. 他们都经历了恩典。** 「我们的祖宗从前都在云下，都从海中经过，都在云里、海里受洗归了摩西；并且都吃了一样的灵食，也都喝了一样的灵水。所喝的，是出于随着他们的灵磐石；那磐石就是基督。」（10:1-4）保罗连用了五次「都」：以色列人「都」蒙了拯救，「都」吃喝了神所供应的。「那磐石就是基督」，是说在旷野供应以色列人的，正是那位先存的基督（参出17:6；民20:11）。后来的犹太传统中有一口水井随着以色列人走的说法，保罗是否在暗用这个传统，学者看法不一；他的重点是：基督当时就与他的百姓同在。

**2. 「多半是神不喜欢的人」。** 「但他们中间多半是神不喜欢的人，所以在旷野倒毙。」（10:5）哥林多人可能以为：我们受了洗，也领了圣餐，所以可以放心去神庙坐席，不会有事。保罗说：以色列人也有他们的「洗礼」和「灵食」，却仍然因拜偶像、行奸淫、试探主、发怨言而倒毙（10:6-10）。圣礼不是护身符；领受圣礼却继续活在拜偶像中的人，是在自欺。

**3. 四个例子。** 保罗举了四个例子：
- **拜偶像**：「百姓坐下吃喝，起来玩耍。」（10:7；引出32:6）这是拜金牛犊的事，正好是在偶像面前吃喝。
- **行奸淫**：「一天就倒毙了二万三千人。」（10:8）民数记25:9说死了二万四千人。有人认为保罗算的是「一天」之内死的人数，也有人认为两处都是约数；这不影响保罗的论点。
- **试探主**：「就被蛇所灭。」（10:9；参民21:5-6）有些抄本作试探基督（有些现代译本采用这个读法），和合本作「试探主」。
- **发怨言**：「就被灭命的所灭。」（10:10；参民16:41-49）

**4. 「自己以为站得稳的」。** 「他们遭遇这些事都要作为鉴戒，并且写在经上，正是警戒我们这末世的人。所以，自己以为站得稳的，须要谨慎，免得跌倒。」（10:11-12）这是给自信的人的警告：最危险的，常常是那个以为自己不会跌倒的人。

**5. 「神是信实的」。** 「你们所遇见的试探，无非是人所能受的。神是信实的，必不叫你们受试探过于所能受的；在受试探的时候，总要给你们开一条出路，叫你们能忍受得住。」（10:13）这节经文常被误解为「神给你的苦难，一定不会超过你能承受的」。可是保罗自己就说过，他在亚细亚「被压太重，力不能胜，甚至连活命的指望都绝了」（林后1:8）。这里的「试探」，在上下文中主要是指引人犯罪、特别是引人拜偶像的试探。保罗的应许是：没有一种试探是独特到让你非犯罪不可的；神是信实的，必定开一条出路。在这里，出路就在下一节：「你们要逃避拜偶像的事。」（10:14）这个应许的根据，不是我们有多坚强，而是「神是信实的」；而且我们有一位大祭司，「也曾凡事受过试探，与我们一样，只是他没有犯罪」（来4:15）。

> 要点：圣礼不是护身符，自以为站得稳的要谨慎。但神是信实的：没有一种试探是我们非犯罪不可的，他必开一条出路。

## 深读 10:14-22 主的筵席与鬼的筵席

**1. 逃避拜偶像的事。** 「我所亲爱的弟兄啊，你们要逃避拜偶像的事。」（10:14）面对淫行，保罗说要「逃避」（6:18）；面对拜偶像，他也说要「逃避」。这不是要人去辩论、去试探自己能走多近，而是要远远离开。

**2. 同领基督的血和身体。** 「我们所祝福的杯，岂不是同领基督的血吗？我们所擘开的饼，岂不是同领基督的身体吗？我们虽多，仍是一个饼，一个身体，因为我们都是分受这一个饼。」（10:16-17）「同领」原文是 *koinōnia*，意思是相交、有分。在圣餐中，信徒真实地与基督相交，也彼此联合成为一个身体。基督怎样临在于圣餐中，历代教会有不同的理解：天主教主张「变质说」；路德宗主张基督的身体和血真实地临在于饼和杯之中；改革宗（如加尔文）主张基督藉着圣灵真实地、属灵地临在；慈运理传统则强调圣餐是记念。不论哪一种理解，保罗在这里的重点都一样：吃喝圣餐，就是与基督有分。

**3. 祭鬼，不是祭神。** 「我乃是说，外邦人所献的祭是祭鬼，不是祭神。我不愿意你们与鬼相交。你们不能喝主的杯又喝鬼的杯，不能吃主的筵席又吃鬼的筵席。」（10:20-21）第八章说「偶像在世上算不得什么」，这里却说偶像背后有鬼魔（参申32:17）。所以，在神庙里吃祭偶像的筵席，不是一件可以凭「知识」决定的中性小事，而是与鬼相交。

**4. 惹主的愤恨。** 「我们可惹主的愤恨吗？我们比他还有能力吗？」（10:22）这句话出自申命记32:21：「他们以那『不算为神』的触动我的愤恨」。神的「愤恨」（或作忌邪）不是小气，而是像一位丈夫不能容忍妻子不忠那样的爱。他为我们舍了自己的儿子，就不愿意与任何偶像分享我们的心。

**5. 今天的应用。** 第八章已经分出三种情形：参与祭拜本身（上香、跪拜祖先牌位或神明、烧纸钱）是拜偶像，信徒不可参与；祭拜仪式中的筵席，例如在庙里、庙会或普渡仪式中的聚餐，接近这里所说的「鬼的筵席」，信徒应当婉拒（参罗14章，那里也说这些不是次要的事）。至于丧礼上向逝者鞠躬，华人教会的看法并不一致：有些教会认为，在追思礼拜或公祭中鞠躬，就像向在世的长辈鞠躬一样，是表达敬意的礼节；也有些教会认为，在设有香案、牌位的场合，鞠躬容易被理解为祭拜，所以劝信徒改用默哀或献花。在这样的事上，信徒可以按照清楚的良心和处境来决定，也要彼此尊重，不要互相论断。

> 要点：在圣餐中，我们与基督有分，也彼此联合。神爱我们，所以不愿与任何偶像分享我们的心；我们不能喝主的杯又喝鬼的杯。

## 深读 10:23-11:1 为荣耀神而行

**1. 「凡事都可行」。** 「凡事都可行，但不都有益处。凡事都可行，但不都造就人。无论何人，不要求自己的益处，乃要求别人的益处。」（10:23-24）保罗再一次引用哥林多人的口号（参6:12），并加上限制：问题不只是可不可以，更是能不能造就人。

**2. 市场上的肉。** 「凡市上所卖的，你们只管吃，不要为良心的缘故问什么话，因为地和其中所充满的都属乎主。」（10:25-26；引诗24:1）市场上卖的肉，有些是从神庙来的，但在市场上，它只是食物。保罗说：不必追问来历，因为一切食物都是神所造、属乎主的。

**3. 不信的人请你赴席。** 「倘有一个不信的人请你们赴席，你们若愿意去，凡摆在你们面前的，只管吃，不要为良心的缘故问什么话。若有人对你们说：『这是献过祭的物』，就要为那告诉你们的人，并为良心的缘故不吃。我说的良心不是你的，乃是他的。」（10:27-29）这是在私人家中的饭局，不是在神庙里。保罗允许信徒去，也允许他吃。但若有人特别指出「这是献过祭的物」，就表示在他眼中，这顿饭和祭拜有关；这时信徒若照样吃，那人就可能以为信徒也认同那个祭拜。所以，为了他的良心，就不吃。这个「告诉你们的人」是谁，有人认为是不信的主人或客人，也有人认为是在场的软弱信徒；两种情形，原则都一样。

**4. 在华人家庭里。** 祭拜之后家人一起吃的饭菜，食物本身没有因此变为不洁，信徒可以存感谢的心吃（10:30）。但如第八章所说，在华人家庭里，大家通常都知道这些饭菜拜过祖先，有时长辈还会特别说：「吃了，祖先会保佑你。」这时，信徒的吃就被赋予了祭拜的意义，正是10:28所说的情形，可以温和地婉拒，并找机会说明自己的信仰。若没有人赋予这样的意义，只是一家人吃饭，信徒就可以安心吃，不必自寻烦恼。在这些事上，华人教会的劝勉也有宽严之分；重要的是存清洁的良心，又顾念别人的良心。

**5. 为荣耀神而行。** 「所以，你们或吃或喝，无论做什么，都要为荣耀神而行。不拘是犹太人，是希腊人，是神的教会，你们都不要使他跌倒；就好像我凡事都叫众人喜欢，不求自己的益处，只求众人的益处，叫他们得救。你们该效法我，像我效法基督一样。」（10:31-11:1）这是第八至十章的总结：信徒的自由，是为了荣耀神、为了别人得救。保罗请哥林多人效法他，因为他效法的是那位「不求自己的益处」、为众人舍命的基督。

> 要点：「地和其中所充满的都属乎主」，所以信徒可以存感谢的心吃喝。但我们的自由是为了荣耀神、为了别人得救。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们不能喝主的杯又喝鬼的杯。」今天的华人信徒，也许不会去庙里上香，却可能有别的偶像。一个常见的偶像，是孩子的成功：成绩、名校、出路。我们会说这是为孩子好，可是当孩子的分数决定了全家的喜怒哀乐，当主日崇拜可以为补习班让路，当我们的平安取决于孩子能不能成龙成凤，那个位置就已经不是神的了。我心里有没有这样一张「筵席」，在那里我每天献上时间、金钱和焦虑？也许我自己就是那个在这样的期望下长大、至今仍觉得不够好的孩子。神对我们的「愤恨」，不是要压垮我们，而是一位不愿与偶像分享我们的父的爱；他为我们舍了自己的儿子，我们在圣餐中所喝的，正是「同领基督的血」。我的价值，孩子的价值，都不在成绩单上，而在那位「随着他们的灵磐石」，就是基督身上。今天，我能不能在主面前承认这个偶像，并且相信「神是信实的」：他会为我开一条出路，叫我和我的孩子都能在他里面得着安息？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 10 closes the unit of chapters 8–10. Paul returns to food offered to idols, and this time he speaks more directly. The chapter has three parts. **Israel's example** (vv. 1–13): all Israel experienced God's grace, yet most of them were overthrown in the wilderness; "let anyone who thinks that he stands take heed lest he fall," but "God is faithful." **The Lord's table and the table of demons** (vv. 14–22): "flee from idolatry"; believers who share in Christ at the Lord's Supper cannot also share in idol feasts. **To the glory of God** (10:23–11:1): believers may eat meat from the market and food at an unbeliever's dinner; but if someone points out, "This has been offered in sacrifice," they refrain for the other person's conscience. "Whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God."

Chapter 8 was about love: laying down one's rights for the weak brother. Chapter 10 is about loyalty: believers belong to one Lord and cannot "drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons."

## Going Deeper: 10:1–13 Israel's Example

**1. They all experienced grace.** "Our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, and all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank from the spiritual Rock that followed them, and the Rock was Christ" (10:1–4). Paul says "all" five times: all Israel was rescued, all ate and drank what God provided. "The Rock was Christ" means that the one who provided for Israel in the wilderness was the preexistent Christ (cf. Exod 17:6; Num 20:11). Later Jewish tradition spoke of a well that followed Israel; scholars differ on whether Paul is alluding to it. His point is that Christ was with his people even then.

**2. "With most of them God was not pleased."** "Nevertheless, with most of them God was not pleased, for they were overthrown in the wilderness" (10:5). The Corinthians may have thought: we are baptized and we share the Lord's Supper, so we can safely dine in the temples. Paul says Israel had its own "baptism" and "spiritual food," yet they fell through idolatry, sexual immorality, testing the Lord and grumbling (10:6–10). The sacraments are not a charm; to receive them while continuing in idolatry is self-deception.

**3. Four examples.** Paul gives four examples:
- **Idolatry**: "The people sat down to eat and drink and rose up to play" (10:7, quoting Exod 32:6). This is the golden calf, people eating and drinking before an idol.
- **Sexual immorality**: "twenty-three thousand fell in a single day" (10:8). Numbers 25:9 gives twenty-four thousand. Some think Paul counts those who died "in a single day"; others think both are round numbers. Either way Paul's point stands.
- **Testing the Lord**: "and were destroyed by serpents" (10:9; cf. Num 21:5–6). Some manuscripts read "put Christ to the test" (followed by the ESV); the CUV reads "the Lord."
- **Grumbling**: "and were destroyed by the Destroyer" (10:10; cf. Num 16:41–49).

**4. "Anyone who thinks that he stands."** "Now these things happened to them as an example, but they were written down for our instruction, on whom the end of the ages has come. Therefore let anyone who thinks that he stands take heed lest he fall" (10:11–12). This is a warning to the self-confident: often the person in greatest danger is the one who thinks he cannot fall.

**5. "God is faithful."** "No temptation has overtaken you that is not common to man. God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond your ability, but with the temptation he will also provide the way of escape, that you may be able to endure it" (10:13). This verse is often misread as "God will never give you more suffering than you can bear." But Paul himself says that in Asia he was "so utterly burdened beyond our strength that we despaired of life itself" (2 Cor 1:8). In context, "temptation" here chiefly means enticement to sin, especially to idolatry. Paul's promise is that no temptation is so unique that you must sin; God is faithful and will provide a way out. Here the way out is the very next verse: "flee from idolatry" (10:14). The ground of the promise is not how strong we are, but that "God is faithful," and that we have a high priest "who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin" (Heb 4:15).

> The point: The sacraments are not a charm, and those who think they stand must take heed. But God is faithful: no temptation forces us to sin, and he will provide the way of escape.

## Going Deeper: 10:14–22 The Lord's Table and the Table of Demons

**1. Flee from idolatry.** "Therefore, my beloved, flee from idolatry" (10:14). Facing sexual immorality, Paul said "flee" (6:18); facing idolatry, he says "flee" again. This is not a call to debate, or to test how close one can get, but to keep well away.

**2. Participation in Christ's blood and body.** "The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a participation in the blood of Christ? The bread that we break, is it not a participation in the body of Christ? Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread" (10:16–17). "Participation" translates *koinōnia*: fellowship, sharing. At the Lord's Supper believers truly share in Christ and are joined to one another as one body. How Christ is present in the Supper has been understood in different ways: Catholic teaching holds transubstantiation; Lutherans hold that Christ's body and blood are "in, with and under" the bread and wine; the Reformed (e.g. Calvin) hold that Christ is truly and spiritually present by the Spirit; the Zwinglian tradition stresses the Supper as a memorial. On any of these views Paul's point here is the same: to eat and drink the Supper is to share in Christ.

**3. Sacrifices to demons, not to God.** "No, I imply that what pagans sacrifice they offer to demons and not to God. I do not want you to be participants with demons. You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons. You cannot partake of the table of the Lord and the table of demons" (10:20–21). Chapter 8 said "an idol has no real existence," but here Paul says there are demons behind idols (cf. Deut 32:17). So eating sacrificial feasts in a temple is not a neutral matter to be settled by "knowledge"; it is fellowship with demons.

**4. Provoking the Lord to jealousy.** "Shall we provoke the Lord to jealousy? Are we stronger than he?" (10:22). The words echo Deuteronomy 32:21: "They have made me jealous with what is no god." God's jealousy is not pettiness; it is the love of a husband who cannot tolerate his wife's unfaithfulness. He gave his own Son for us, and he will not share our hearts with any idol.

**5. For today.** Chapter 8 distinguished three situations. Taking part in the rite itself (offering incense, bowing before ancestral tablets or gods, burning paper money) is idolatry, and believers may not take part. Meals that are part of the worship, such as a feast in a temple, at a temple festival or at the Ghost Festival rite, are close to what Paul here calls "the table of demons," and believers should decline (cf. Rom 14, which also says these are not "secondary matters"). As for bowing to the deceased at a funeral, Chinese churches are not of one mind. Some hold that a bow at a memorial service or public funeral is a courtesy of respect, like bowing to a living elder. Others hold that where there is an incense table and an ancestral tablet, a bow is easily understood as worship, and counsel believers to stand in silence or lay flowers instead. In such matters believers may decide with a clear conscience in light of the setting, and should respect one another without passing judgment.

> The point: At the Lord's Supper we share in Christ and in one another. God loves us and will not share our hearts with any idol; we cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons.

## Going Deeper: 10:23–11:1 To the Glory of God

**1. "All things are lawful."** "'All things are lawful,' but not all things are helpful. 'All things are lawful,' but not all things build up. Let no one seek his own good, but the good of his neighbor" (10:23–24). Paul again quotes the Corinthians' slogan (cf. 6:12) and adds limits: the question is not only "may I?" but "does it build others up?"

**2. Meat from the market.** "Eat whatever is sold in the meat market without raising any question on the ground of conscience. For 'the earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof'" (10:25–26, quoting Ps 24:1). Some meat in the market came from the temples, but in the market it was simply food. Paul says: don't investigate where it came from, for all food is created by God and belongs to the Lord.

**3. An invitation from an unbeliever.** "If one of the unbelievers invites you to dinner and you are disposed to go, eat whatever is set before you without raising any question on the ground of conscience. But if someone says to you, 'This has been offered in sacrifice,' then do not eat it, for the sake of the one who informed you, and for the sake of conscience, I do not mean your conscience, but his" (10:27–29). This is a dinner in a private home, not in a temple. Paul allows the believer to go and to eat. But if someone specifically points out, "This has been offered in sacrifice," it shows that in that person's eyes the meal is bound up with the sacrifice. If the believer eats anyway, that person may think the believer endorses the sacrifice too. So for his conscience's sake, the believer refrains. Who "the one who informed you" is, some take as the unbelieving host or a guest, others as a weak believer present; the principle is the same either way.

**4. In Chinese families.** The dishes a family eats together after an offering are not made unclean by it, and believers may eat them with thanksgiving (10:30). But as chapter 8 noted, in Chinese families everyone usually knows the food was offered to the ancestors, and sometimes an elder will say, "Eat it, and the ancestors will protect you." Then the believer's eating has been given the meaning of worship, which is exactly the situation of 10:28, and the believer may gently decline and look for a chance to explain his faith. If no one gives it such a meaning and it is simply a family meal, the believer may eat in peace without troubling himself. Chinese churches counsel more strictly or more freely in these matters; what matters is a clear conscience and care for the conscience of others.

**5. To the glory of God.** "So, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God. Give no offense to Jews or to Greeks or to the church of God, just as I try to please everyone in everything I do, not seeking my own advantage, but that of many, that they may be saved. Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ" (10:31–11:1). This sums up chapters 8–10: the believer's freedom is for God's glory and for others' salvation. Paul asks the Corinthians to imitate him because he imitates Christ, who did not seek his own advantage but gave his life for many.

> The point: "The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof," so believers may eat and drink with thanksgiving. But our freedom is for the glory of God and the salvation of others.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons." Chinese believers today may never offer incense in a temple, yet we can have other idols. A common one is our children's success: grades, top schools, prospects. We say it is for the children's good. But when a child's scores decide the whole family's moods, when Sunday worship gives way to cram school, when our peace depends on whether our children "become dragons and phoenixes," that place no longer belongs to God. Is there such a "table" in my heart, where every day I offer up time, money and anxiety? Or perhaps I am the child who grew up under such expectations and still feel I am never good enough. God's jealousy is not meant to crush us; it is the love of a Father who will not share us with an idol. He gave his own Son for us, and what we drink at the Lord's table is "a participation in the blood of Christ." My worth, and my children's worth, is not on a report card but in the one who was "the spiritual Rock that followed them," Christ himself. Today, can I confess this idol before the Lord and trust that "God is faithful": that he will provide a way out, so that my children and I may find our rest in him?
