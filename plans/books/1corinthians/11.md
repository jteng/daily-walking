## 本章脉络

第十一章开始讨论教会聚会中的问题（11-14章）。这一章分两段。**蒙头**（2-16节）：男人祷告或讲道时不可蒙头，女人祷告或讲道时要蒙头；保罗从「头」的次序、创造和「本性」来说明，又说「照主的安排，女也不是无男，男也不是无女」。**主的晚餐**（17-34节）：哥林多人聚会时「各人先吃自己的饭，甚至这个饥饿，那个酒醉」；保罗提醒他们，主耶稣在被卖的那一夜怎样设立圣餐，并且要他们「自己省察，然后吃这饼、喝这杯」。

两段都关乎聚会中的次序与彼此尊重，第二段更是全章的重心：「你们每逢吃这饼，喝这杯，是表明主的死，直等到他来。」（11:26）

## 深读 11:2-16 蒙头

**1. 「头」是什么意思？** 「我愿意你们知道，基督是各人的头；男人是女人的头；神是基督的头。」（11:3）「头」原文是 *kephalē*。这个词在这里的意思，学者有不同的看法：
- **权柄**：许多主张男女角色有别的学者（如古德恩、派博）认为，「头」是指带领和权柄，正如以弗所书5:23所说：「丈夫是妻子的头，如同基督是教会的头」。
- **源头**：许多主张男女在教会和家庭中角色平等的学者（如戈登·费依）认为，「头」是指生命的来源，与11:8「女人乃是由男人而出」相呼应。
- 也有学者（如锡塞尔顿）认为，「头」是指显要、代表的地位。

「神是基督的头」也要小心理解：子与父同等（腓2:6；约10:30），子的顺服是在救赎的工作中甘心的顺服（参15:28），不表示子在本质上次于父（子的顺服在什么范围内，福音派中也有讨论，但都承认子与父同质同等）。无论哪一种看法，「头」都不是丈夫辖制、苛待妻子的理由；圣经所说的「头」，是像基督那样「为教会舍己」（弗5:25）。家庭暴力是罪，受害人可以寻求保护，包括报警（罗13:4）；受害人不必留在危险之中，教会也不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩。

**2. 女人也祷告、讲道。** 「凡女人祷告或是讲道，若不蒙着头，就羞辱自己的头。」（11:5）「讲道」原文是说预言。大多数解经家认为，这节经文表明，在哥林多教会的聚会中，女人也公开祷告、说预言，保罗并没有禁止，只是要她们蒙头。所以，14:34「妇女在会中要闭口不言」不能理解为女人在聚会中完全不可以开口；那节经文的意思，要到第十四章再讨论。

**3. 蒙头是什么？** 在当时的希腊罗马社会，已婚的妇女在公开场合用外衣或头巾盖头，表示端庄，也表示自己是有夫之妇；不蒙头，可能被看作轻浮，甚至羞辱丈夫。也有学者认为，保罗说的不是头巾，而是把长发盘起来，不披头散发（参11:15「这头发是给她作盖头的」）。11:10「因此，女人为天使的缘故，应当在头上有服权柄的记号」是全段最难解的一句：有人认为天使在敬拜中观看，有人认为是指天使维护神所定的次序，也有人认为「权柄」是指女人有权柄祷告、说预言的记号。

**4. 今天还要蒙头吗？** 华人教会在这件事上有不同的做法：
- 有些教会（例如弟兄会的传统，以及倪柝声所建立的聚会处和地方教会，还有真耶稣教会和中国大陆的不少家庭教会）认为这是神在创造中定下的次序（11:8-9），所以姊妹在聚会中至今仍然蒙头。
- 大多数福音派教会认为，保罗所教导的原则是长久的，但蒙头这个表达方式与当时的文化有关。至于这原则是什么，主张男女角色有别的人认为是尊重神所定的带领次序，主张角色平等的人认为是在敬拜中不要让性别的分别被抹去、不要叫人羞愧。

不论我们采用哪一种做法，都要记住保罗在11:11-12所说的：「然而照主的安排，女也不是无男，男也不是无女。因为女人原是由男人而出，男人也是由女人而出；但万有都是出乎神。」在主里，男女彼此需要，同是照着神的形象造的（创1:27），也「一同承受生命之恩」（彼前3:7）。

> 要点：在敬拜中，男女都要以尊荣神、尊重彼此的方式来到神面前。在主里，男女彼此需要，一同承受生命之恩。

## 深读 11:17-22 不是吃主的晚餐

**1. 聚会反而招损。** 「我现今吩咐你们的话，不是称赞你们；因为你们聚会不是受益，乃是招损。」（11:17）早期教会的圣餐，是在一顿共同的饭中举行的（犹大书12节称这样的聚餐为爱席），地点通常是比较富有的信徒的家。

**2. 「这个饥饿，那个酒醉」。** 「你们聚会的时候，算不得吃主的晚餐；因为吃的时候，各人先吃自己的饭，甚至这个饥饿，那个酒醉。」（11:20-21）许多学者（如泰森〔Gerd Theissen〕、墨菲-奥康纳〔Jerome Murphy-O'Connor〕）根据当时房屋的格局推想：富有的信徒可以早到，在主人家的餐厅里吃好的；奴隶和穷人做完工才赶来，只能在外面的庭院里吃剩下的，甚至没有东西吃。也有学者（如温特〔Bruce Winter〕）认为，问题不在先到后到，而在富人独自享用自己的饭，所以11:33也可以理解为彼此接待。无论哪一种，都是把社会上的阶级分别，带进了主的晚餐。

**3. 藐视神的教会。** 「你们要吃喝，难道没有家吗？还是藐视神的教会，叫那没有的羞愧呢？」（11:22）保罗说，这样做不只是不礼貌，而是「藐视神的教会」。今天的华人教会也可能有这样的分别：主日崇拜后的午餐，说不同方言的、新移民和老移民、有钱的和没钱的，各坐各的桌子；有些弟兄姊妹总是一个人吃饭，没有人理会。

> 要点：主的晚餐是基督为他所有的子民，不论贫富，舍身的筵席；在主的桌前，不能有贫富、地位的高低。

## 深读 11:23-26 主耶稣被卖的那一夜

**1. 从主领受的。** 「我当日传给你们的，原是从主领受的，就是主耶稣被卖的那一夜，拿起饼来，祝谢了，就擘开，说：『这是我的身体，为你们舍的，你们应当如此行，为的是记念我。』」（11:23-24）「领受」和「传给」是传递传统的用语（参15:3）。哥林多前书大约写于主后五十三至五十五年，所以这段经文是新约中最早的圣餐记载之一，与路加福音22:19-20非常接近。

**2. 新约的血。** 「饭后，也照样拿起杯来，说：『这杯是用我的血所立的新约，你们每逢喝的时候，要如此行，为的是记念我。』」（11:25）按照对观福音的记载，耶稣设立圣餐，是在逾越节的晚餐中（参5:7「我们逾越节的羔羊基督已经被杀献祭了」）。他的血成就了耶利米所应许的新约：神要赦免他们的罪孽，不再记念他们的罪恶（耶31:31-34）。

**3. 记念与宣告。** 「你们每逢吃这饼，喝这杯，是表明主的死，直等到他来。」（11:26）「记念」不只是心里想起一件过去的事，更像以色列人守逾越节，是在神面前重新承认那拯救的作为如今仍然与我们有关。每一次领圣餐，教会都在「表明」（宣告）主的死；而且是「直等到他来」，圣餐也是对主再来的盼望。

> 要点：圣餐回头看十字架，看见基督「为你们舍的」身体；也向前看，「直等到他来」。

## 深读 11:27-34 自己省察

**1. 「不按理」。** 「所以，无论何人，不按理吃主的饼，喝主的杯，就是干犯主的身、主的血了。」（11:27）「不按理」原文是一个副词（*anaxiōs*），讲的是吃喝的方式，不是吃的人配不配。在上下文中，「不按理」就是17-22节所说的：自己吃饱喝醉，却藐视饥饿的弟兄。没有一个人「配」领圣餐；圣餐正是为那些知道自己不配、却投靠基督的罪人预备的。

**2. 「分辨是主的身体」。** 「人应当自己省察，然后吃这饼、喝这杯。因为人吃喝，若不分辨是主的身体，就是吃喝自己的罪了。」（11:28-29）「主的身体」有几种理解：有人认为是指在饼和杯中所纪念的基督的身体；也有人（如戈登·费依）认为是指教会，就是基督的身体（参10:17；12:27），也就是不顾念弟兄姊妹；也有人认为两者都包括在内。要注意：保罗说「自己省察，然后吃」，省察的目的是来领受，不是叫人离开主的桌子。

**3. 被主惩治。** 「因此，在你们中间有好些软弱的与患病的，死的也不少。我们若是先分辨自己，就不至于受审。我们受审的时候，乃是被主惩治，免得我们和世人一同定罪。」（11:30-32）保罗说，哥林多教会中有些疾病和死亡，是神的管教。但这不表示每一次生病都是因为某个罪（参约9:3）。而且保罗特别说，这是「惩治」（管教），目的是「免得我们和世人一同定罪」：神管教的，正是他所爱的儿女（来12:6）。

**4. 软弱的良心。** 有些敏感的信徒，因为想起自己的罪，就不敢领圣餐，一次又一次地让饼和杯从面前经过。据说十九世纪苏格兰有一位牧者（约翰·邓肯〔John Duncan〕），看见一位妇人流着泪推开杯，就对她说：「拿去吧，这是为罪人预备的。」若我们为罪忧伤，又投靠基督，主的桌子正是为我们预备的。真正需要停下来的，是那些继续活在明知的罪中、不肯悔改，或者正在与弟兄姊妹结怨、却不愿和好的人（参太5:23-24。这不是要受害人与伤害他的人和好；饶恕不等于恢复关系，受害人不必回到危险之中）；就算是这样的人，主的呼召也是悔改，然后来到桌前。

**5. 彼此等待。** 「所以我弟兄们，你们聚会吃的时候，要彼此等待。」（11:33）这是这一段实际的结论：不要自己先吃，要等那些晚来的、穷乏的弟兄姊妹。

> 要点：没有人配领圣餐；圣餐是为投靠基督的罪人预备的。省察自己，是为了悔改、和好，然后来到主的桌前。

## 默想

**默想：** 「这是我的身体，为你们舍的。」也许我已经有好几次在圣餐时，让饼和杯从面前经过，因为我想起这个星期发的脾气、看过不该看的东西、心里的怨恨和冷淡，觉得自己不配。也许我也曾在主日午餐时，只和自己熟悉的人坐在一起，没有看见那个总是一个人吃饭的弟兄。保罗说：「人应当自己省察，然后吃这饼、喝这杯。」省察不是要我把自己关在门外，而是要我带着这一切来到十字架前，承认自己的罪，与得罪的人和好，然后伸手领受。主耶稣在被卖的那一夜设立这饼和杯，那一夜他的门徒都要离开他，彼得也要三次不认他；他却仍然说：「这是我的身体，为你们舍的。」我能领圣餐，从来不是因为我这个星期表现得够好，而是因为他的身体已经为我舍了，他的血已经为我流了。我领受了这样的恩典，也就能邀请那个一个人吃饭的弟兄姊妹，与我同坐一桌。下一次领圣餐的时候，我能不能不再把手缩回来，而是相信主自己的应许：「到我这里来的，我总不丢弃他」（约6:37）？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 11 begins Paul's treatment of problems in the church's gatherings (chs. 11–14). The chapter has two parts. **Head coverings** (vv. 2–16): a man should not cover his head when he prays or prophesies, and a woman should cover hers; Paul argues from the order of "heads," from creation and from "nature," and adds that "in the Lord woman is not independent of man nor man of woman." **The Lord's Supper** (vv. 17–34): when the Corinthians gather, "each one goes ahead with his own meal. One goes hungry, another gets drunk"; Paul reminds them how the Lord Jesus instituted the Supper on the night he was betrayed, and tells them to "examine himself, then, and so eat of the bread and drink of the cup."

Both parts concern order and mutual honor in worship, and the second is the heart of the chapter: "For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes" (11:26).

## Going Deeper: 11:2–16 Head Coverings

**1. What does "head" mean?** "But I want you to understand that the head of every man is Christ, the head of a wife is her husband, and the head of Christ is God" (11:3). "Head" translates *kephalē*. Scholars differ on its meaning here:
- **Authority**: many complementarian scholars (e.g. Wayne Grudem, John Piper) take "head" to mean leadership and authority, as in Ephesians 5:23: "the husband is the head of the wife even as Christ is the head of the church."
- **Source**: many egalitarian scholars (e.g. Gordon Fee) take "head" to mean source of life, echoing 11:8, "woman from man."
- Others (e.g. Anthony Thiselton) take it to mean prominence or representative standing.

"The head of Christ is God" must also be read with care: the Son is equal with the Father (Phil 2:6; John 10:30), and the Son's submission is a willing submission in the work of redemption (cf. 15:28); it does not mean the Son is lesser in nature than the Father (evangelicals debate how far this submission extends, but all confess the Son is of one being and equal with the Father). On any view, "head" gives no husband a reason to dominate or mistreat his wife; the headship Scripture describes is that of Christ, who "gave himself up for her" (Eph 5:25). Domestic violence is sin, and victims may seek protection, including from the police (Rom 13:4); victims need not remain in danger, and the church must not hide abuse to save face.

**2. Women also prayed and prophesied.** "But every wife who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonors her head" (11:5). Most interpreters take this verse to show that in the Corinthian gatherings women also prayed and prophesied publicly; Paul doesn't forbid it, only asks that they be covered. So 14:34, "the women should keep silent in the churches," cannot mean that women may never speak at all in the gathering; what that verse means will be taken up in chapter 14.

**3. What was the covering?** In Greco-Roman society married women covered their heads in public with a mantle or veil, signaling modesty and marriage; to go uncovered could be seen as loose, even as shaming one's husband. Some scholars think Paul has in mind not a veil but long hair bound up rather than let down (cf. 11:15, "her hair is given to her for a covering"). Verse 10, "a wife ought to have a symbol of authority on her head, because of the angels," is the hardest sentence in the passage: some think angels watch over worship, some that they guard God's order, and some that the "authority" is the woman's own authority to pray and prophesy.

**4. Should women cover their heads today?** Chinese churches differ in practice:
- Some churches (e.g. in the Brethren tradition, the assemblies and local churches associated with Watchman Nee, the True Jesus Church and many house churches in mainland China) hold that this reflects an order God set at creation (11:8–9), so sisters still cover their heads in the gathering.
- Most evangelical churches hold that the principle Paul teaches is lasting, but that the head covering as its expression belongs to that culture. As to what the principle is, complementarians see honoring the order of leadership God has set, while egalitarians see not erasing the distinction between the sexes in worship, and not bringing shame.

Whatever our practice, we must remember 11:11–12: "Nevertheless, in the Lord woman is not independent of man nor man of woman; for as woman was made from man, so man is now born of woman. And all things are from God." In the Lord, men and women need each other; both are made in the image of God (Gen 1:27) and are "heirs with you of the grace of life" (1 Pet 3:7).

> The point: In worship, men and women alike come before God in ways that honor him and one another. In the Lord, men and women need each other and share together in the grace of life.

## Going Deeper: 11:17–22 Not the Lord's Supper

**1. Gatherings that do harm.** "But in the following instructions I do not commend you, because when you come together it is not for the better but for the worse" (11:17). The early church held the Lord's Supper within a shared meal (Jude 12 calls such meals "love feasts"), usually in the home of a wealthier believer.

**2. "One goes hungry, another gets drunk."** "When you come together, it is not the Lord's supper that you eat. For in eating, each one goes ahead with his own meal. One goes hungry, another gets drunk" (11:20–21). Many scholars (e.g. Gerd Theissen, Jerome Murphy-O'Connor), reconstructing from the layout of houses at the time, think the wealthy could arrive early and eat well in the host's dining room, while slaves and the poor came after work, ate leftovers in the courtyard, or had nothing at all. Others (e.g. Bruce Winter) think the problem was not timing but the rich devouring their own meals, so that 11:33 can mean "receive one another." Either way, society's class divisions were being carried into the Lord's Supper.

**3. Despising the church of God.** "What! Do you not have houses to eat and drink in? Or do you despise the church of God and humiliate those who have nothing?" (11:22). Paul says this isn't just bad manners; it is "despising the church of God." Chinese churches today can have such divisions too: at lunch after Sunday worship, people who speak different dialects, new and old immigrants, the well-off and the poor each sit at their own tables, and some brothers and sisters always eat alone with no one noticing.

> The point: The Lord's Supper is the meal of Christ, who gave his body for all his people, rich and poor alike; at the Lord's table there is no high or low by wealth or status.

## Going Deeper: 11:23–26 The Night He Was Betrayed

**1. Received from the Lord.** "For I received from the Lord what I also delivered to you, that the Lord Jesus on the night when he was betrayed took bread, and when he had given thanks, he broke it, and said, 'This is my body which is for you. Do this in remembrance of me'" (11:23–24). "Received" and "delivered" are the language of handing on tradition (cf. 15:3). First Corinthians was written around AD 53–55, so this is among the earliest written accounts of the Lord's Supper in the New Testament, very close to Luke 22:19–20.

**2. The blood of the new covenant.** "In the same way also he took the cup, after supper, saying, 'This cup is the new covenant in my blood. Do this, as often as you drink it, in remembrance of me'" (11:25). According to the Synoptic Gospels, Jesus instituted the Supper at the Passover meal (cf. 5:7, "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed"). His blood brings in the new covenant Jeremiah promised, in which God forgives his people's iniquity and remembers their sin no more (Jer 31:31–34).

**3. Remembering and proclaiming.** "For as often as you eat this bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes" (11:26). "Remembrance" is not merely calling a past event to mind; like Israel keeping the Passover, it is acknowledging before God that his saving act still concerns us now. Each time the church takes the Supper it "proclaims" the Lord's death, "until he comes," so the Supper is also a hope for the Lord's return.

> The point: The Supper looks back to the cross and sees Christ's body "which is for you"; it looks forward, "until he comes."

## Going Deeper: 11:27–34 Let a Person Examine Himself

**1. "In an unworthy manner."** "Whoever, therefore, eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord in an unworthy manner will be guilty of profaning the body and blood of the Lord" (11:27). "In an unworthy manner" translates an adverb (*anaxiōs*); it describes the way one eats, not whether the person is worthy. In context it is what verses 17–22 describe: eating and drinking one's fill while despising the hungry brother. No one is "worthy" of the Supper; it is set for sinners who know they are unworthy and take refuge in Christ.

**2. "Discerning the body."** "Let a person examine himself, then, and so eat of the bread and drink of the cup. For anyone who eats and drinks without discerning the body eats and drinks judgment on himself" (11:28–29). "The body" is read in several ways: some take it as Christ's body remembered in the bread and cup; others (e.g. Gordon Fee) as the church, the body of Christ (cf. 10:17; 12:27), so that the fault is disregarding one's brothers and sisters; others think both are included. Notice that Paul says "examine himself, then, and so eat": the purpose of self-examination is to come and receive, not to keep people away from the table.

**3. Disciplined by the Lord.** "That is why many of you are weak and ill, and some have died. But if we judged ourselves truly, we would not be judged. But when we are judged by the Lord, we are disciplined so that we may not be condemned along with the world" (11:30–32). Paul says some illness and death in the Corinthian church was God's discipline. That does not mean every illness is caused by a particular sin (cf. John 9:3). And Paul stresses that this is discipline, "so that we may not be condemned along with the world": God disciplines the children he loves (Heb 12:6).

**4. Tender consciences.** Some sensitive believers, remembering their sins, dare not take the Supper and let the bread and cup pass them by time after time. It is said that a nineteenth-century Scottish minister, John Duncan, saw a woman in tears push the cup away, and said to her, "Take it, woman; it's for sinners." If we grieve over our sin and take refuge in Christ, the Lord's table is set precisely for us. Those who truly need to pause are those who persist in known sin without repentance, or who hold a grudge against a brother or sister and refuse to be reconciled (cf. Matt 5:23–24; this does not require a victim to reconcile with someone who has harmed them: forgiveness is not the same as restored relationship, and no one need return to danger); and even to them the Lord's call is to repent, and then come to the table.

**5. Wait for one another.** "So then, my brothers, when you come together to eat, wait for one another" (11:33). This is the practical conclusion: don't eat first; wait for the brothers and sisters who come late and have little.

> The point: No one is worthy of the Supper; it is set for sinners who take refuge in Christ. We examine ourselves in order to repent and be reconciled, and then to come to the Lord's table.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "This is my body which is for you." Perhaps several times at the Lord's Supper I have let the bread and cup pass me by, because I remembered the temper I lost this week, the things I should not have looked at, the resentment and coldness in my heart, and felt unworthy. Perhaps at Sunday lunch I have sat only with people I know, and never noticed the brother who always eats alone. Paul says, "Let a person examine himself, then, and so eat of the bread and drink of the cup." Self-examination is not meant to shut me out, but to bring all of this to the cross: to confess my sin, to be reconciled with those I have wronged, and then to reach out my hand and receive. The Lord Jesus instituted this bread and cup on the night he was betrayed, the night all his disciples would desert him and Peter would deny him three times; yet he still said, "This is my body which is for you." I can come to the Supper not because I did well enough this week, but because his body has been given for me and his blood poured out for me. Having received such grace, I can also invite the brother or sister who eats alone to sit at my table. Next time I come to the Supper, can I stop pulling my hand back, and trust the Lord's own promise: "whoever comes to me I will never cast out" (John 6:37)?
