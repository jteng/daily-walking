## 本章脉络

第十二至十四章讨论「属灵的恩赐」（12:1），特别是说方言和说预言的问题。第十二章奠定基础，分三段。**认耶稣为主**（1-3节）：辨别是不是出于圣灵，标准不是现象多么奇妙，而是承认「耶稣是主」。**同一位圣灵，许多恩赐**（4-11节）：「恩赐原有分别，圣灵却是一位」，「圣灵显在各人身上，是叫人得益处」。**一个身体，许多肢体**（12-31节）：「你们就是基督的身子，并且各自作肢体」；没有一个肢体可以说自己「不属乎身子」，也没有一个肢体可以说「我用不着你」。

这一章的中心是27节：教会不是一群各自追求恩赐的人，而是基督的身体。恩赐的目的，是叫整个身体得益处；第十三章随即指出那「最妙的道」，就是爱。

## 深读 12:1-3 认耶稣为主

**1. 从哑巴偶像到圣灵。** 「弟兄们，论到属灵的恩赐，我不愿意你们不明白。你们作外邦人的时候，随事被牵引，受迷惑，去服事那哑巴偶像，这是你们知道的。」（12:1-2）哥林多人从前拜偶像的时候，可能也有过出神、狂喜之类的宗教经验。保罗提醒他们：不是一切奇妙的灵性经验都出于神。

**2. 「耶稣是主」。** 「所以我告诉你们，被神的灵感动的，没有说『耶稣是可咒诅』的；若不是被圣灵感动的，也没有能说『耶稣是主』的。」（12:3）「耶稣是可咒诅」是什么情形，学者有不同的猜测；但保罗的重点是清楚的：圣灵的工作，总是叫人尊耶稣为主。这不是说任何人口里说了这几个字，就证明有圣灵；而是说，一个人真心承认耶稣是他的主，本身就是圣灵的工作。每一个真信徒，都已经有圣灵（参罗8:9）。

> 要点：辨别属灵恩赐的标准，不是现象多么奇妙，而是是否高举耶稣为主。每一个真心承认耶稣是主的人，都是被圣灵感动的。

## 深读 12:4-11 同一位圣灵，许多恩赐

**1. 三位一体的神。** 「恩赐原有分别，圣灵却是一位。职事也有分别，主却是一位。功用也有分别，神却是一位，在众人里面运行一切的事。」（12:4-6）圣灵、主（耶稣）、神（父），三位一同在教会中工作。教会中恩赐多样又彼此合一，正反映出三一神的合一与丰富：恩赐不同，却出于同一位神。

**2. 叫人得益处。** 「圣灵显在各人身上，是叫人得益处。」（12:7）这里有两个重点：第一，「各人」都有圣灵的显现，没有一个信徒是没有恩赐的；第二，恩赐的目的是「叫人得益处」，原文是为了共同的益处，不是为了显出自己属灵。

**3. 恩赐的清单。** 8-10节列出了九种恩赐：智慧的言语、知识的言语、信心、医病、行异能、作先知、辨别诸灵、说方言、翻方言。这不是全部的恩赐。比较罗马书12:6-8、以弗所书4:11和彼得前书4:10-11，就知道圣经中还有教导、劝化、施舍、治理、怜悯、传福音、牧养等等。

**4. 随己意分给各人。** 「这一切都是这位圣灵所运行、随己意分给各人的。」（12:11）恩赐不是我们靠努力赚来的，也不是用来比较的，而是圣灵按着自己的主权白白分给的。

**5. 恩赐今天还有吗？** 关于说方言、说预言、医病这一类恩赐今天是否仍然存在，福音派中有两种主要看法：
- **终止论**：许多学者，特别是在改革宗传统中（如华腓德〔B. B. Warfield〕、加芬〔Richard Gaffin〕），认为这些恩赐主要是为使徒时代立定根基（弗2:20），在使徒时代结束、根基立定以后就已经停止；神今天仍然按着他的旨意医治人、垂听祷告，但不再藉着这样的恩赐给人新的启示。
- **延续论**：另一些学者，包括一些改革宗学者（如古德恩、派博），认为新约没有说这些恩赐会在主再来以前停止（参13:8-10），所以今天仍然存在；但一切都要按圣经察验（14:29；帖前5:20-21），不能与圣经同等。

此外，古典五旬节派认为说方言是受圣灵洗的最初凭据；可是保罗在12:30问：「岂都是说方言的吗？」这个问题所期待的答案是「不是」。五旬节派的回应是：12:30讲的是在聚会中公开说方言的恩赐，与受灵洗时的方言不同；但大多数福音派学者认为，经文本身并没有作这样的区分。也有一些学者（如卡森）采取开放而谨慎的立场。华人教会中，这几种看法都有：有的教会认为非说方言不可，有的则对灵恩运动十分戒备，灵恩和非灵恩的教会彼此猜疑、互相论断的情形并不少见。若有人对我说，我不说方言，所以还没有圣灵，12:3和12:13告诉我：真心承认耶稣是主的人，都已经在一位圣灵里受洗，进了这身体；反过来，有不同经历的弟兄姊妹，也不该被看作异类。不论采取哪一种看法，第十二章的教导都一样适用：恩赐出于同一位圣灵，是为了造就整个身体，不是为了分辨谁比较属灵。

> 要点：每一个信徒都有圣灵所赐的恩赐，没有一种恩赐是为了显出自己。恩赐不同，却出于同一位神，为了叫众人得益处。

## 深读 12:12-26 一个身体，许多肢体

**1. 基督也是这样。** 「就如身子是一个，却有许多肢体；而且肢体虽多，仍是一个身子；基督也是这样。我们不拘是犹太人，是希腊人，是为奴的，是自主的，都从一位圣灵受洗，成了一个身体，饮于一位圣灵。」（12:12-13）保罗没有说教会也是这样，而是说「基督也是这样」：教会与基督联合，是他的身体。「都从一位圣灵受洗」，大多数福音派学者认为是指每一个信徒在信主时都经历的事；五旬节派则常常把「灵洗」理解为信主以后的另一次经历。保罗在这里强调的是「都」：没有一个信徒在这个身体以外。

**2. 「不属乎身子」？** 「设若脚说：『我不是手，所以不属乎身子，』它不能因此就不属乎身子。」（12:15）这是对自卑的人说的。有人觉得自己的恩赐不显眼，不能讲道、不能领诗，就以为自己在教会中可有可无。保罗说：你属不属于这个身体，不是由你自己的感觉决定的，而是神「随自己的意思把肢体俱各安排在身上了」（12:18）。

**3. 「我用不着你」。** 「眼不能对手说：『我用不着你』；头也不能对脚说：『我用不着你。』」（12:21）这是对骄傲的人说的。有恩赐、有地位的人，很容易觉得别人可有可无。可是保罗说：「身上肢体人以为软弱的，更是不可少的。」（12:22）保罗说的是「人以为」软弱的：软弱常常在我们的眼光里，不在他们身上。年长的、患病的、残障的、不善言辞的弟兄姊妹，不只是教会照顾的对象，更是神放在身体里、有恩赐可以给出来的肢体；他们若进不了门、听不见、跟不上，缺欠的是整个身体。

**4. 彼此相顾。** 「免得身上分门别类，总要肢体彼此相顾。若一个肢体受苦，所有的肢体就一同受苦；若一个肢体得荣耀，所有的肢体就一同快乐。」（12:25-26）这正击中哥林多教会的问题：他们在领袖的事上分党（1:10-12），在主的晚餐上「彼此分门别类」（11:18）。在基督的身体里，别人的痛苦就是我的痛苦，别人的荣耀也是我的快乐；不是嫉妒，也不是冷漠。

> 要点：在基督的身体里，没有一个肢体是多余的，也没有一个肢体可以不需要别人。软弱的，更是不可少的。

## 深读 12:27-31 你们就是基督的身子

**1. 各自作肢体。** 「你们就是基督的身子，并且各自作肢体。」（12:27）这是全章的总结。「你们」是复数，是指整个教会；「各自」是说每一个人都在其中。

**2. 第一是使徒。** 「神在教会所设立的：第一是使徒，第二是先知，第三是教师，其次是行异能的，再次是得恩赐医病的，帮助人的，治理事的，说方言的。」（12:28）保罗列出的头三项，都是与神的话语有关的职事；说方言被列在最后，可能是因为哥林多人过分看重它，保罗有意把它放在后面。「帮助人的，治理事的」也在清单中：那些在幕后默默服事的人，同样是神在教会中「设立」的。

**3. 岂都是？** 「岂都是使徒吗？岂都是先知吗？……岂都是说方言的吗？岂都是翻方言的吗？」（12:29-30）这些问题在原文中都期待「不是」的回答。神从来没有要每一个信徒都有同样的恩赐。

**4. 更大的恩赐与最妙的道。** 「你们要切切地求那更大的恩赐。我现今把最妙的道指示你们。」（12:31）「切切地求」大多数译本理解为命令，就是要追求那些更能造就人的恩赐（参14:1，5，12）；也有人理解为保罗在描述哥林多人的现况：你们正热切地追求那些看起来更大的恩赐。无论哪一种，保罗接着要指出的，都比任何恩赐更美：那就是爱（13章）。

> 要点：神在教会中设立了不同的恩赐，没有人拥有全部，也没有人一无所有。比一切恩赐更美的，是爱。

## 默想

**默想：** 「设若脚说：『我不是手，所以不属乎身子，』它不能因此就不属乎身子。」我从小学到的是：有用的人才有价值。考试要考好，工作要有成就，在教会里也要有拿得出手的恩赐。所以，当我不会讲道、不会弹琴、不会带小组，或者年纪大了、身体不好、刚刚失业，觉得什么都给不了的时候，我就以为自己在教会里可有可无。也许我也曾经是那只「眼」，心里对某些弟兄姊妹说：「我用不着你」；其实我看不起别人，常常是因为我需要觉得自己重要。保罗说，我属于基督的身体，不是因为我有用，而是因为我已经在一位圣灵里「受洗，成了一个身体」，是神「随自己的意思」把我安排在这里（12:13，18）。如果我曾经看不起别人，我可以向主认罪，他必赦免我；他也差我去告诉一位看起来软弱的弟兄姊妹：「这个家需要你。」我们的主耶稣「因软弱被钉在十字架上」（林后13:4），他最荣耀的工作，正是在最软弱的时候完成的。今天，我能不能相信：那位为我软弱、为我死的主，正是「把加倍的体面给那有缺欠的肢体」的神（12:24）；在他的身体里，我不是可有可无的？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapters 12–14 address "spiritual gifts" (12:1), especially tongues and prophecy. Chapter 12 lays the foundation, in three parts. **Confessing Jesus as Lord** (vv. 1–3): the test of what comes from the Spirit is not how wonderful an experience seems, but the confession "Jesus is Lord." **One Spirit, many gifts** (vv. 4–11): "there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit," and "to each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good." **One body, many members** (vv. 12–31): "you are the body of Christ and individually members of it"; no member may say "I do not belong to the body," and none may say "I have no need of you."

The center of the chapter is verse 27: the church is not a crowd of individuals each pursuing gifts, but the body of Christ. The purpose of the gifts is the good of the whole body, and chapter 13 then shows the "still more excellent way," which is love.

## Going Deeper: 12:1–3 Confessing Jesus as Lord

**1. From mute idols to the Spirit.** "Now concerning spiritual gifts, brothers, I do not want you to be uninformed. You know that when you were pagans you were led astray to mute idols, however you were led" (12:1–2). When the Corinthians worshiped idols, they may well have had religious experiences such as trance or ecstasy. Paul reminds them that not every remarkable spiritual experience comes from God.

**2. "Jesus is Lord."** "Therefore I want you to understand that no one speaking in the Spirit of God ever says 'Jesus is accursed!' and no one can say 'Jesus is Lord' except in the Holy Spirit" (12:3). Scholars offer different guesses about the situation behind "Jesus is accursed," but Paul's point is clear: the Spirit's work always leads people to honor Jesus as Lord. This doesn't mean anyone who pronounces the words thereby proves he has the Spirit; it means that genuinely confessing Jesus as one's Lord is itself the Spirit's work. Every true believer already has the Spirit (cf. Rom 8:9).

> The point: The test of spiritual gifts is not how remarkable they appear, but whether they exalt Jesus as Lord. Everyone who truly confesses Jesus as Lord does so by the Holy Spirit.

## Going Deeper: 12:4–11 One Spirit, Many Gifts

**1. The triune God.** "Now there are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit; and there are varieties of service, but the same Lord; and there are varieties of activities, but it is the same God who empowers them all in everyone" (12:4–6). The Spirit, the Lord (Jesus) and God (the Father) work together in the church. The church's variety reflects the unity and richness of the triune God: the gifts differ, but they come from the one God.

**2. For the common good.** "To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common good" (12:7). There are two points here. First, "to each": every believer has a manifestation of the Spirit; no believer is without a gift. Second, the purpose of the gifts is "the common good," not displaying one's own spirituality.

**3. The list of gifts.** Verses 8–10 list nine gifts: the utterance of wisdom, the utterance of knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, distinguishing between spirits, tongues and the interpretation of tongues. This is not every gift. Comparing Romans 12:6–8, Ephesians 4:11 and 1 Peter 4:10–11 shows that Scripture also names teaching, exhortation, giving, leading, mercy, evangelism, shepherding and more.

**4. As he wills.** "All these are empowered by one and the same Spirit, who apportions to each one individually as he wills" (12:11). Gifts are not earned by effort, nor meant for comparison; the Spirit gives them freely by his own sovereign choice.

**5. Do these gifts continue today?** Evangelicals hold two main views on whether gifts such as tongues, prophecy and healing continue today:
- **Cessationism**: many scholars, especially in the Reformed tradition (e.g. B. B. Warfield, Richard Gaffin), hold that these gifts served mainly to lay the foundation in the apostolic age (Eph 2:20) and ceased once that apostolic foundation was laid; God still heals and answers prayer according to his will today, but no longer gives new revelation through such gifts.
- **Continuationism**: other scholars, including some in the Reformed tradition (e.g. Wayne Grudem, John Piper), hold that the New Testament does not say these gifts will cease before the Lord returns (cf. 13:8–10), so they continue today; but everything must be tested by Scripture (14:29; 1 Thess 5:20–21) and can never stand on a level with it.

In addition, classical Pentecostals hold that speaking in tongues is the initial evidence of baptism in the Spirit; yet in 12:30 Paul asks, "Do all speak with tongues?", a question that expects the answer "No." Pentecostals reply that 12:30 concerns the gift of tongues exercised in the assembly, distinct from tongues as the sign of Spirit baptism; most evangelical scholars find no such distinction in the text. Some (e.g. D. A. Carson) take an "open but cautious" position. All these views are found in Chinese churches: some insist that one must speak in tongues, others are deeply wary of the charismatic movement, and charismatic and non-charismatic churches often regard each other with suspicion and judgment. If someone has told me that because I don't speak in tongues I lack the Spirit, 12:3 and 12:13 say that everyone who truly confesses Jesus as Lord has been baptized in the one Spirit into the body; and brothers and sisters with different experiences should not be treated as outsiders. Whichever view one holds, the teaching of chapter 12 applies equally: the gifts come from one Spirit to build up the whole body, not to sort out who is more spiritual.

> The point: Every believer has a gift from the Spirit, and no gift is for showing off. The gifts differ, but they come from the one God for the good of all.

## Going Deeper: 12:12–26 One Body, Many Members

**1. So it is with Christ.** "For just as the body is one and has many members, and all the members of the body, though many, are one body, so it is with Christ. For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, Jews or Greeks, slaves or free, and all were made to drink of one Spirit" (12:12–13). Paul doesn't say "so it is with the church," but "so it is with Christ": the church is united to Christ as his body. "In one Spirit we were all baptized" is taken by most evangelical scholars to describe what every believer experiences at conversion; Pentecostals often understand "Spirit baptism" as a further experience after conversion. Paul's emphasis here is on "all": no believer is outside this body.

**2. "I do not belong to the body."** "If the foot should say, 'Because I am not a hand, I do not belong to the body,' that would not make it any less a part of the body" (12:15). This is addressed to those who feel inferior. Some feel that their gifts are not visible: they can't preach or lead worship, so they think they make no difference in the church. Paul says that whether you belong to the body isn't decided by your feelings; "God arranged the members in the body, each one of them, as he chose" (12:18).

**3. "I have no need of you."** "The eye cannot say to the hand, 'I have no need of you,' nor again the head to the feet, 'I have no need of you'" (12:21). This is addressed to the proud. People with gifts and standing can easily feel that others make no difference. But Paul says, "the parts of the body that seem to be weaker are indispensable" (12:22). Paul says the parts that seem weaker: the weakness is often in our eyes, not in them. Elderly, sick and disabled brothers and sisters, and those who are not good with words, are not only people for the church to care for; they are members God has placed in the body, with gifts to give. If they cannot get in the door, hear, or keep up, it is the whole body that is lacking.

**4. Caring for one another.** "That there may be no division in the body, but that the members may have the same care for one another. If one member suffers, all suffer together; if one member is honored, all rejoice together" (12:25–26). This speaks directly to the Corinthian church's problems: "divisions" over leaders (1:10–12) and at the Lord's Supper (11:18). In the body of Christ, another's pain is my pain, and another's honor is my joy: neither envy nor indifference.

> The point: In the body of Christ no member is superfluous, and no member can do without the others. The weaker members are indispensable.

## Going Deeper: 12:27–31 You Are the Body of Christ

**1. Individually members.** "Now you are the body of Christ and individually members of it" (12:27). This sums up the chapter. "You" is plural, meaning the whole church; "individually" means each person has a place in it.

**2. First apostles.** "And God has appointed in the church first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then miracles, then gifts of healing, helping, administrating, and various kinds of tongues" (12:28). The first three are all ministries of God's word; tongues come last, perhaps deliberately, because the Corinthians prized them too highly. "Helping" and "administrating" are on the list too: those who serve quietly behind the scenes are equally "appointed" by God in the church.

**3. Are all...?** "Are all apostles? Are all prophets?... Do all speak with tongues? Do all interpret?" (12:29–30). In Greek each of these questions expects the answer "No." God never intended every believer to have the same gift.

**4. Higher gifts and a more excellent way.** "But earnestly desire the higher gifts. And I will show you a still more excellent way" (12:31). Most translations take "earnestly desire" as a command: pursue the gifts that build others up more (cf. 14:1, 5, 12). Others take it as Paul describing the Corinthians' present state: you are eagerly pursuing the gifts that look greater. Either way, what Paul goes on to show is better than any gift: love (ch. 13).

> The point: God has appointed different gifts in the church; no one has them all, and no one has none. Better than every gift is love.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "If the foot should say, 'Because I am not a hand, I do not belong to the body,' that would not make it any less a part of the body." I learned young that only useful people are worth anything. I had to do well in exams, achieve at work, and have a gift at church I could point to. So when I can't preach, can't play an instrument, can't lead a small group, or I'm getting old, my health is poor, I've just lost my job and feel I have nothing to give, I assume I make no difference at church. Or perhaps I have been the "eye," saying in my heart to certain brothers and sisters, "I have no need of you"; and often I look down on others because I need to feel important. Paul says I belong to the body of Christ not because I am useful, but because in one Spirit I was "baptized into one body," and God has placed me here "as he chose" (12:13, 18). If I have looked down on others, I can confess it to the Lord, and he will forgive me; and he sends me to tell a brother or sister who seems weak, "This family needs you." Our Lord Jesus "was crucified in weakness" (2 Cor 13:4); his most glorious work was accomplished in his weakest hour. Today, can I believe that the Lord who became weak and died for me is the God who gives "greater honor to the part that lacked it" (12:24), and that in his body I am not dispensable?
