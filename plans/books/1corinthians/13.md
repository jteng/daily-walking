## 本章脉络

第十三章夹在讨论属灵恩赐的第十二章和第十四章中间，是保罗所说的「最妙的道」（12:31）。这一章分三段。**没有爱，就算不得什么**（1-3节）：说方言、作先知、有信心、舍己，若没有爱，都毫无益处。**爱是什么**（4-7节）：保罗用十五个动词描写爱，有些说爱做什么，有些说爱不做什么。**爱是永不止息**（8-13节）：恩赐都是暂时的、有限的，「等那完全的来到」就要归于无有；「如今常存的有信，有望，有爱这三样，其中最大的是爱」。

这一章常在婚礼上诵读，这样用并没有错；但保罗原是写给一个彼此嫉妒、自高自大、争夺恩赐的教会。每一句描写爱的话，都在照出哥林多人的问题，也照出我们的问题。

## 深读 13:1-3 没有爱

**1. 鸣的锣，响的钹。** 「我若能说万人的方言，并天使的话语，却没有爱，我就成了鸣的锣，响的钹一般。」（13:1）哥林多人最看重说方言。保罗说：就算能说天使的话语，若没有爱，也只是噪音，像异教神庙里敲锣打钹的声音。

**2. 算不得什么。** 「我若有先知讲道之能，也明白各样的奥秘，各样的知识，而且有全备的信，叫我能够移山，却没有爱，我就算不得什么。」（13:2）保罗不是说这些恩赐不好（他在14:1还要人「切慕」），而是说：一个人可能有很大的恩赐，却在神面前「算不得什么」。

**3. 舍己身叫人焚烧。** 「我若将所有的周济穷人，又舍己身叫人焚烧，却没有爱，仍然与我无益。」（13:3）连最大的牺牲，也可能不是出于爱，而是出于骄傲。这节有一个抄本上的差异：有些抄本作「叫人焚烧」，有些作「叫我可以夸口」，两个希腊字只差一个字母；今天通行的希腊文本多半采用后者。无论哪一种，意思都是：外表的舍己，不等于爱。

> 要点：恩赐、知识和牺牲本身都不能证明我们属灵；没有爱，一切都算不得什么。

## 深读 13:4-7 爱是什么

**1. 爱是行动。** 「爱是恒久忍耐，又有恩慈；爱是不嫉妒；爱是不自夸，不张狂，不做害羞的事，不求自己的益处，不轻易发怒，不计算人的恶，不喜欢不义，只喜欢真理；凡事包容，凡事相信，凡事盼望，凡事忍耐。」（13:4-7）在原文中，这些都是动词。保罗没有给爱下一个抽象的定义，而是描写爱怎样对待人。

**2. 一面镜子。** 这些描写几乎都对着哥林多人的问题：
- 他们有「嫉妒、纷争」（3:3），爱却「不嫉妒」；
- 他们「自高自大」（4:6；8:1），爱却「不张狂」；
- 他们在主的晚餐上各吃各的，在法庭上彼此相告，爱却「不求自己的益处」（参10:24）。

这一段不只是一首美丽的诗，更是一面镜子。

**3. 「凡事包容」不是纵容恶。** 「凡事包容，凡事相信」不是说要相信一切谎言、容忍一切恶行。同一段经文说，爱「不喜欢不义，只喜欢真理」（13:6）。爱不会为了表面的和平，把罪掩盖起来；面对家庭暴力或虐待，爱的做法是揭露恶、保护受害人。受害人可以，也应当寻求保护，包括报警（罗13:4），不必为了「包容」而留在危险中；教会也不可以拿「凡事包容」作理由，要受害人沉默，或为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩。「凡事包容」的意思是：爱愿意为人承担，不轻易放弃人，也不急着把人往坏处想。

**4. 只有一位完全符合。** 有人建议，读这段经文时，把「爱」换成自己的名字：「某某是恒久忍耐，又有恩慈……」读不了几句，我们就读不下去了。可是若换成「耶稣」，每一句都完全符合：他「爱世间属自己的人，就爱他们到底」（约13:1）。这段经文先是在描写基督，然后才是在描写那些被他爱、被他改变的人。我们能这样爱，是因为「不是我们爱神，乃是神爱我们，差他的儿子为我们的罪作了挽回祭，这就是爱了」（约一4:10）。

> 要点：爱不是一种感觉，而是怎样对待人。只有基督完全活出了这样的爱；我们被他所爱，才能开始这样爱人。

## 深读 13:8-13 爱是永不止息

**1. 恩赐终必止息。** 「爱是永不止息。先知讲道之能终必归于无有；说方言之能终必停止；知识也终必归于无有。我们现在所知道的有限，先知所讲的也有限，等那完全的来到，这有限的必归于无有了。」（13:8-10）哥林多人以为恩赐最重要，保罗却说：恩赐都是暂时的，爱却永远长存。

**2. 「那完全的」是什么？** 这里有几种主要理解：
- **基督再来、我们面对面见主的时候**：大多数解经家都这样理解，因为12节说「到那时就要面对面了」。持这种看法的人中，有延续论者（如古德恩），也有许多终止论者（如加芬），后者认为恩赐的止息要根据别的经文来判断，不是根据这一节。
- **新约正典的完成**：有些终止论者认为，「那完全的」是指圣经全部写成。但多数学者认为，「面对面」和「全知道，如同主知道我一样」很难用来描写正典完成以后的今天。
- 也有人认为是指教会的成熟。

这与第十二章所说终止论和延续论的讨论相关；但不论哪一种看法，保罗的重点都是：今天我们所知道的都是有限的，惟有爱永远长存。

**3. 对着镜子观看。** 「我们如今仿佛对着镜子观看，模糊不清，到那时就要面对面了。我如今所知道的有限，到那时就全知道，如同主知道我一样。」（13:12）古代的镜子是用磨光的铜做的，哥林多正以出产铜器闻名。铜镜照出来的像，总是模糊不清。我们今天对神的认识是真实的，却是有限的；到那日，我们要「面对面」见他（参民12:8；约一3:2）。最安慰人的是后半句：在我还不完全认识神的时候，神早已完全认识我（参8:3）。

**4. 其中最大的是爱。** 「如今常存的有信，有望，有爱这三样，其中最大的是爱。」（13:13）为什么爱最大？到那日，信要变成眼见，望要变成实现，爱却永远不会过去，因为「神就是爱」（约一4:16）。我们在天上要做的，就是永远爱神、爱人；今天在地上学习爱，就是在预备那永远的生活。

> 要点：恩赐会止息，知识是有限的，但爱永不止息。我们今天对神的认识模糊不清，神对我们的认识却是完全的。

## 默想

**默想：** 「爱是恒久忍耐，又有恩慈。」请想一个在我生活中最难爱的人：也许是一位常常挑剔我的长辈，一位得了失智症、天天重复同样问题的父母，一位让我失望的配偶，或者一个怎么说都不听的孩子。现在，把13:4-7读一遍，把「爱」换成我的名字，想着这个人：「某某是恒久忍耐……不轻易发怒，不计算人的恶……」我能读到哪一句？也许不到第二句，我就要停下来，向主承认：我做不到。然后，请再读一遍，这次把「爱」换成「耶稣」，想着我自己：耶稣对我恒久忍耐，耶稣不计算我的恶，耶稣凡事为我盼望。正当我还是罪人的时候，基督为我死（罗5:8）；他对我的忍耐，比我对任何人的忍耐都更长。今天我面对那个难爱的人，不是靠咬紧牙关，而是带着这样被爱的心，求圣灵在我里面结出「忍耐、恩慈」的果子（加5:22）。若这个人正在伤害我，爱也不是要我留在危险中；但无论如何，我可以先在主面前，领受他对我那「永不止息」的爱。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 13 sits between chapters 12 and 14 on spiritual gifts; it is the "still more excellent way" (12:31). It has three parts. **Without love, nothing** (vv. 1–3): tongues, prophecy, faith and self-sacrifice gain nothing without love. **What love is** (vv. 4–7): Paul describes love with fifteen verbs, some saying what love does and some what it does not do. **Love never ends** (vv. 8–13): the gifts are temporary and partial and will pass away "when the perfect comes"; "So now faith, hope, and love abide, these three; but the greatest of these is love."

This chapter is often read at weddings, and that is not wrong; but Paul first wrote it to a church full of envy and arrogance, competing over spiritual gifts. Every line describing love exposes the Corinthians' problems, and ours.

## Going Deeper: 13:1–3 Without Love

**1. A noisy gong, a clanging cymbal.** "If I speak in the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I am a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal" (13:1). The Corinthians prized tongues above all. Paul says that even speaking the language of angels without love is only noise, like the gongs and cymbals of pagan temple worship.

**2. Nothing.** "And if I have prophetic powers, and understand all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so as to remove mountains, but have not love, I am nothing" (13:2). Paul isn't saying these gifts are bad (in 14:1 he tells them to "earnestly desire" them); he is saying a person can have great gifts and still be "nothing" before God.

**3. Delivering up my body to be burned.** "If I give away all I have, and if I deliver up my body to be burned, but have not love, I gain nothing" (13:3). Even the greatest sacrifice can come from pride rather than love. There is a manuscript difference here: some manuscripts read "to be burned," others "that I may boast"; the two Greek words differ by a single letter, and the standard Greek text today mostly prefers "that I may boast." Either way the point is that outward self-sacrifice is not the same as love.

> The point: Gifts, knowledge and sacrifice cannot by themselves prove that we are spiritual; without love, all of it is nothing.

## Going Deeper: 13:4–7 What Love Is

**1. Love acts.** "Love is patient and kind; love does not envy or boast; it is not arrogant or rude. It does not insist on its own way; it is not irritable or resentful; it does not rejoice at wrongdoing, but rejoices with the truth. Love bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things" (13:4–7). In Greek these are all verbs. Paul doesn't give love an abstract definition; he describes how love treats people.

**2. A mirror.** Nearly every line answers a Corinthian problem:
- they had "jealousy and strife" (3:3), but love "does not envy";
- they were "puffed up" (4:6; 8:1), but love "is not arrogant";
- they ate their own meals at the Lord's Supper and sued each other in court, but love "does not insist on its own way" (cf. 10:24).

This passage is not only a beautiful poem; it is a mirror.

**3. "Bears all things" does not excuse evil.** "Bears all things, believes all things" does not mean believing every lie or tolerating every wrong. The same passage says love "does not rejoice at wrongdoing, but rejoices with the truth" (13:6). Love does not cover sin for the sake of surface peace; faced with domestic violence or abuse, love exposes the evil and protects the victim. Victims may, and should, seek protection, including from the police (Rom 13:4), and need not stay in danger in the name of "bearing all things"; nor may the church use "love bears all things" to silence victims or hide abuse to save face. "Bears all things" means that love is willing to carry burdens for others, does not give up on people easily, and is slow to think the worst.

**4. Only one fits.** Some suggest reading this passage with your own name in place of "love": "So-and-so is patient and kind..." We can't get far before we have to stop. But put "Jesus" in its place and every line fits perfectly: "having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end" (John 13:1). This passage describes Christ first, and only then those whom he loves and changes. We can love like this because "in this is love, not that we have loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins" (1 John 4:10).

> The point: Love is not a feeling but a way of treating people. Only Christ has lived this love perfectly; loved by him, we can begin to love like this.

## Going Deeper: 13:8–13 Love Never Ends

**1. The gifts will cease.** "Love never ends. As for prophecies, they will pass away; as for tongues, they will cease; as for knowledge, it will pass away. For we know in part and we prophesy in part, but when the perfect comes, the partial will pass away" (13:8–10). The Corinthians thought the gifts mattered most; Paul says the gifts are temporary, but love lasts forever.

**2. What is "the perfect"?** There are several main readings:
- **Christ's return, when we see the Lord face to face**: most interpreters take it this way, because verse 12 says "then face to face." Those who hold this view include continuationists (e.g. Wayne Grudem) and many cessationists (e.g. Richard Gaffin), who hold that whether the gifts have ceased must be decided from other texts, not this one.
- **The completion of the New Testament canon**: some cessationists take "the perfect" to mean the completed Scriptures. But most scholars find "face to face" and "I shall know fully, even as I have been fully known" hard to apply to the present age after the canon was completed.
- Others take it to mean the church's maturity.

This connects with the cessationist and continuationist discussion in chapter 12; but on any view Paul's point is that all we know now is partial, and only love lasts forever.

**3. Seeing in a mirror.** "For now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part; then I shall know fully, even as I have been fully known" (13:12). Ancient mirrors were made of polished bronze, and Corinth was famous for its bronze work. A bronze mirror always gave a dim image. Our knowledge of God today is real but partial; on that day we will see him "face to face" (cf. Num 12:8; 1 John 3:2). The most comforting words are the last ones: while I do not yet fully know God, God already fully knows me (cf. 8:3).

**4. The greatest of these is love.** "So now faith, hope, and love abide, these three; but the greatest of these is love" (13:13). Why is love the greatest? On that day faith will become sight and hope will be fulfilled, but love will never pass away, because "God is love" (1 John 4:16). What we will do in heaven forever is love God and love one another; learning to love on earth now is preparation for that eternal life.

> The point: The gifts will cease and our knowledge is partial, but love never ends. Our knowledge of God today is dim, but his knowledge of us is complete.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Love is patient and kind." Think of the person in my life who is hardest to love: perhaps an elder who constantly criticizes me, a parent with dementia who asks the same question all day, a spouse who has disappointed me, or a child who won't listen however I say it. Now read 13:4–7 with my own name in place of "love," thinking of this person: "So-and-so is patient... is not irritable or resentful..." How far can I read? Perhaps before the second line I have to stop and admit to the Lord: I can't do this. Then read it again, this time putting "Jesus" in place of "love," and thinking of myself: Jesus is patient with me, Jesus keeps no record of my wrongs, Jesus hopes all things for me. While I was still a sinner, Christ died for me (Rom 5:8); his patience with me is longer than my patience with anyone. Today I face that hard-to-love person not by gritting my teeth but with a heart that has been loved like this, asking the Spirit to bear in me the fruit of "patience, kindness" (Gal 5:22). If this person is harming me, love does not ask me to stay in danger; but whatever the case, I can first receive, before the Lord, his love for me that "never ends."
