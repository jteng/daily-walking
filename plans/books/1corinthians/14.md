## 本章脉络

第十四章把第十二、十三章的原则，应用在哥林多教会的聚会上。这一章分三段。**作先知讲道比说方言更能造就教会**（1-19节）：「说方言的，是造就自己；作先知讲道的，乃是造就教会。」保罗宁可「用悟性说五句教导人的话，强如说万句方言」。**为不信的人着想**（20-25节）：若外人进来，听见众人说方言，会以为「你们癫狂了」；若听见先知讲道，就可能「将脸伏地，敬拜神」。**聚会的次序**（26-40节）：「凡事都当造就人」，说方言要轮流，并要有人翻出来；作先知讲道也要轮流，其余的人要慎思明辨，因为「神不是叫人混乱，乃是叫人安静」。中间有一段关于「妇女在会中要闭口不言」的话（33-36节），是新约中较难解的经文之一。

这一章的中心是26节：「凡事都当造就人。」聚会的目的，不是让人显出自己的恩赐，而是让众人被建立。这正是第十三章所说的爱。

## 深读 14:1-19 造就教会

**1. 追求爱，切慕恩赐。** 「你们要追求爱，也要切慕属灵的恩赐，其中更要羡慕的，是作先知讲道。」（14:1）保罗先说「追求爱」，正是接续第十三章。他没有贬低恩赐，却把恩赐放在爱的底下：恩赐是用来表达爱、服事人的。

**2. 先知讲道是什么？** 「但作先知讲道的，是对人说，要造就、安慰、劝勉人。」（14:3）「作先知讲道」原文是说预言（和合本在11:4-5只作「讲道」），不等于今天讲台上的讲道。它是什么，有不同的理解：
- 许多终止论者（如加芬）认为，这是神直接的启示，带着神自己的权威，属于使徒时代的根基（弗2:20）。
- 许多延续论者（如古德恩）认为，这是人受圣灵感动所说、能帮助会众的话，但权威低于圣经，所以「其余的就当慎思明辨」（14:29）。
- 也有人（如加尔文）认为，在今天，这恩赐主要表现在解释和应用圣经上。

不论哪一种理解，保罗的重点都是：作先知讲道是用人听得懂的话，叫人得造就、安慰、劝勉。

**3. 说方言是什么？** 「那说方言的，原不是对人说，乃是对神说，因为没有人听出来。然而，他在心灵里却是讲说各样的奥秘。」（14:2）方言是什么，也有不同的看法：有人认为是说话的人没有学过的外国语言，像五旬节那天一样（徒2:6-8）；也有人认为是一种不属于人间语言的祷告和赞美（参13:1「天使的话语」）。保罗承认说方言有价值：「说方言的，是造就自己」（14:4），他自己「说方言比你们众人还多」（14:18）。但在聚会中，没有翻出来的方言不能造就别人。

**4. 用灵，也用悟性。** 「我要用灵祷告，也要用悟性祷告；我要用灵歌唱，也要用悟性歌唱。不然，你用灵祝谢，那在座不通方言的人，既然不明白你的话，怎能在你感谢的时候说『阿们』呢？」（14:15-16）敬拜不只是情感的经验，也需要头脑明白。别人要能跟着说「阿们」，才算是一同敬拜。

**5. 五句与万句。** 「但在教会中，宁可用悟性说五句教导人的话，强如说万句方言。」（14:19）这句话对今天的教会仍然适用：讲台上无论用了多少术语、说得多么高深，都不如几句让人听得懂、能带回家的话。

> 要点：聚会中的恩赐，要用人听得懂的方式，造就、安慰、劝勉别人。五句造就人的话，强如一万句只造就自己的话。

## 深读 14:20-25 为不信的人着想

**1. 在心志上作大人。** 「弟兄们，在心志上不要作小孩子。然而，在恶事上要作婴孩，在心志上总要作大人。」（14:20）哥林多人看重方言，却只看重它的新奇和热闹，像小孩子一样。保罗要他们在思想上成熟。

**2. 方言是给不信的人的记号。** 「律法上记着：主说：我要用外邦人的舌头和外邦人的嘴唇向这百姓说话；虽然如此，他们还是不听从我。这样看来，说方言不是为信的人作证据，乃是为不信的人。」（14:21-22）保罗引用以赛亚书28:11-12：以色列人不肯听先知的话，神就让他们听见听不懂的亚述话，作为审判的记号。这段经文很难解。常见的理解是：在聚会中，不信的人听见没有翻出来的方言，不会因此悔改，反倒说「你们癫狂了」（14:23）；这方言就成了一个记号，显明他们的不信。

**3. 「神真是在你们中间了」。** 「若都作先知讲道，偶然有不信的，或是不通方言的人进来，就被众人劝醒，被众人审明，他心里的隐情显露出来，就必将脸伏地，敬拜神，说：『神真是在你们中间了。』」（14:24-25）这是聚会最好的结果：一个外人进来，不是被奇怪的现象吓走，而是听见神的话，被神光照，认罪悔改，敬拜神。

> 要点：聚会最美的时刻，不是我们显得多属灵，而是慕道友和新朋友也能说：「神真是在你们中间了。」为此，我们要用他听得懂的话，把神的话讲给他听。

## 深读 14:26-40 聚会的次序

**1. 凡事都当造就人。** 「弟兄们，这却怎么样呢？你们聚会的时候，各人或有诗歌，或有教训，或有启示，或有方言，或有翻出来的话，凡事都当造就人。」（14:26）从这里可以看见，早期教会的聚会有许多人参与。

**2. 次序。** 说方言的「只好两个人，至多三个人，且要轮流着说，也要一个人翻出来。若没有人翻，就当在会中闭口」；作先知讲道的「只好两个人或是三个人，其余的就当慎思明辨」（14:27-29）。「先知的灵原是顺服先知的；因为神不是叫人混乱，乃是叫人安静。」（14:32-33）和合本译作「安静」的这个词，原文是平安。圣灵的工作不会叫人失控，属灵的人可以安静、等候、轮流。

**3. 「妇女在会中要闭口不言」。** 「妇女在会中要闭口不言，像在圣徒的众教会一样，因为不准她们说话。她们总要顺服，正如律法所说的。她们若要学什么，可以在家里问自己的丈夫，因为妇女在会中说话原是可耻的。」（14:34-35）大多数解经家认为，这段话不能理解为妇女在聚会中完全不可以开口，因为保罗在11:5已经提到妇女在聚会中祷告、说预言（和合本作「讲道」）；所以这里的「闭口」是指某种特定的说话。主要有以下几种理解：
- **不参与评断先知讲道**：许多互补派学者（如卡森、古德恩）认为，这段话紧接在29节「其余的就当慎思明辨」之后，指的是妇女不在会众面前公开评断先知的话，因为这涉及教导的权柄（参提前2:12）。
- **不在聚会中插嘴发问**：许多学者，特别是平等派学者（如克雷格·基纳〔Craig Keener〕）认为，当时的妇女大多没有受过教育，可能在聚会中大声发问、打断讲话，所以保罗说「可以在家里问自己的丈夫」；这是为了聚会的秩序，不是一条永远禁止妇女说话的命令。
- **后来加进去的经文**：也有一些学者（如戈登·费依，多属平等派）认为，这两节可能是后来抄写的人加进去的，因为少数西方抄本把这两节放在第40节之后；他们也认为，这两节与11:5和上下文不协调。但现存的抄本都有这两节，今天通行的希腊文本和大多数福音派解经家都认为这是保罗原来的话。

「正如律法所说的」也不容易理解，因为旧约没有一节经文直接这样说。保罗在14:21把以赛亚书也称为「律法」，可见「律法」可以泛指旧约。有人认为是指创世记第二章的创造次序（参11:8-9；提前2:13），也有人认为是指创世记3:16，或泛指旧约的原则。华人教会在这件事上的做法也不一样：有的教会有女传道人讲道、教导，有的教会讲台只由弟兄负责，姊妹仍然祷告、作见证，也教导妇女和孩子。无论采取哪一种做法，都要记得：神的灵「浇灌凡有血气的」，「你们的儿女要说预言」（珥2:28；徒2:17-18）。彼得在五旬节宣告，这应许已经应验；腓利的四个女儿也是说预言的（徒21:9）。主耶稣让马利亚坐在他脚前听道（路10:39），又让妇女作复活的第一批见证人（约20:17-18）；保罗在这里也假定姊妹要学道。姊妹的恩赐是神给教会的礼物，不可轻看；持不同做法的教会，也不必因此彼此论断。

不论怎样理解这里的「顺服」和「闭口」，这段经文都不是要姊妹忍受丈夫的暴力，也不是要她在教会里对虐待保持沉默。受害的姊妹可以离开危险，也应当报警（罗13:4）；教会必须聆听、保护她，不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩，也不可让施害的人保留职分，或再接近受害人和孩子。

**4. 主的命令。** 「若有人以为自己是先知，或是属灵的，就该知道，我所写给你们的是主的命令。」（14:37）保罗所写的，不只是他个人的意见，而是主的命令。

**5. 不要禁止，也要规矩。** 「所以我弟兄们，你们要切慕作先知讲道，也不要禁止说方言。凡事都要规规矩矩地按着次序行。」（14:39-40）这一句平衡了两边：不要因为怕混乱就禁止圣灵的恩赐；也不要因为追求恩赐，就让聚会失去次序。放在今天的华人教会，这几节对两边都有话说。灵恩的教会要问：众人同时大声说方言、没有人翻出来，外人进来能说「阿们」吗？有人说「主告诉我」，有没有人按圣经「慎思明辨」？若有人藉「神的启示」要人嫁给谁、奉献多少，或不可离开教会，那就是辖制，不是先知讲道。非灵恩的教会也要问：我们是不是因为怕，就一概「禁止」，或者看不起有不同经历的弟兄姊妹？讲台上满是术语、外人听不懂的，也是另一种没有翻出来的方言。两边要记得，量度的尺是同一把：这能不能造就人，能不能叫人更认识基督？

> 要点：神不是叫人混乱的神，乃是赐平安的神。聚会中的一切，都要规规矩矩、按着次序，好叫众人在基督这根基上被建立（参3:11）。

## 默想

**默想：** 「作先知讲道的，是对人说，要造就、安慰、劝勉人。」请回想一句曾经扶持过我的话：也许是在我最灰心的时候，一位长辈拍拍我的肩膀说：「神没有忘记你」；也许是小组里一位弟兄姊妹为我祷告时说的一句经文；也许只是一条短信。那几句话不长，却在我心里留了很多年。也许我想不起有谁这样扶持过我；也许我在教会里听到的，更多是批评和论断。可是有一句话，主已经亲口对我说了：神在这末世藉着他的儿子向我们说话（来1:2），他对我说的，不是定罪，而是「你的罪赦了」（可2:5）。我也想起自己说过的一些话，不是造就人，而是伤人：在家里对孩子说过的重话，在教会里传过的闲话。这是罪；但这些罪也在他的赦免里面，所以我可以不再为自己辩护，先向主认罪，也去向那人认错。我常常以为，造就人是牧师和讲员的事。可是保罗说「各人或有诗歌，或有教训」，每个人都可以说几句造就人的话；即使我没有说预言的恩赐，我说话的目标也和先知讲道一样：造就人（弗4:29；帖前5:11）。今天，我能不能求主把一个人放在我心里，把我从主那里听见的恩典，用几句简单的话说给他听？我能说造就人的话，不是因为我口才好，而是因为我先被主的话造就了。他对我说的那句「你的罪赦了」，就是我一切话语的源头。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 14 applies the principles of chapters 12 and 13 to the Corinthian church's gatherings. It has three parts. **Prophecy builds the church more than tongues** (vv. 1–19): "The one who speaks in a tongue builds up himself, but the one who prophesies builds up the church." Paul "would rather speak five words with my mind in order to instruct others, than ten thousand words in a tongue." **Thinking of unbelievers** (vv. 20–25): if outsiders come in and hear everyone speaking in tongues, they will say "you are out of your minds"; if they hear prophecy, they may fall "on his face" and "worship God." **Order in the gathering** (vv. 26–40): "Let all things be done for building up"; tongues and prophecy should take turns, with interpretation or weighing, because "God is not a God of confusion but of peace." In the middle is a passage on women keeping "silent in the churches" (vv. 33–36), among the harder texts in the New Testament.

The center of the chapter is verse 26: "Let all things be done for building up." The purpose of the gathering is not for people to display their gifts, but for everyone to be built up. This is the love of chapter 13.

## Going Deeper: 14:1–19 Building Up the Church

**1. Pursue love, desire the gifts.** "Pursue love, and earnestly desire the spiritual gifts, especially that you may prophesy" (14:1). Paul begins with "pursue love," continuing chapter 13. He doesn't belittle the gifts, but he puts them under love: gifts are tools of love.

**2. What is prophecy?** "On the other hand, the one who prophesies speaks to people for their upbuilding and encouragement and consolation" (14:3). What New Testament prophecy is has been understood in different ways:
- Many cessationists (e.g. Richard Gaffin) hold that it was direct revelation from God, carrying God's own authority, belonging to the foundation laid in the apostolic age (Eph 2:20).
- Many continuationists (e.g. Wayne Grudem) hold that it is Spirit-prompted speech that helps the congregation, with less authority than Scripture, which is why "the others weigh what is said" (14:29).
- Others (e.g. Calvin) hold that today this gift is expressed mainly in explaining and applying Scripture.

On any view, Paul's point is that prophecy uses words people can understand, for their upbuilding, encouragement and consolation.

**3. What are tongues?** "For one who speaks in a tongue speaks not to men but to God; for no one understands him, but he utters mysteries in the Spirit" (14:2). Views differ on what tongues are. Some take them to be foreign languages the speaker had never learned, as at Pentecost (Acts 2:6–8); others take them to be prayer and praise not in any human language (cf. 13:1, "the tongues of men and of angels"). Paul grants that tongues have value: "The one who speaks in a tongue builds up himself" (14:4), and he himself speaks "in tongues more than all of you" (14:18). But in the gathering, uninterpreted tongues cannot build others up.

**4. With the spirit and with the mind.** "I will pray with my spirit, but I will pray with my mind also; I will sing praise with my spirit, but I will sing with my mind also. Otherwise, if you give thanks with your spirit, how can anyone in the position of an outsider say 'Amen' to your thanksgiving when he does not know what you are saying?" (14:15–16). Worship is not only an emotional experience; it also needs the mind to understand. Others must be able to say "Amen" for it to be shared worship.

**5. Five words and ten thousand.** "Nevertheless, in church I would rather speak five words with my mind in order to instruct others, than ten thousand words in a tongue" (14:19). This still applies to the church today: however learned a sermon sounds, however much specialized vocabulary it uses, a few words people can understand and take home are worth more.

> The point: Gifts in the gathering are to be used in ways others can understand, for their upbuilding, encouragement and consolation. Five words that build others up are better than ten thousand that build up only oneself.

## Going Deeper: 14:20–25 Thinking of Unbelievers

**1. Mature in thinking.** "Brothers, do not be children in your thinking. Be infants in evil, but in your thinking be mature" (14:20). The Corinthians prized tongues, but like children they prized the novelty and the excitement. Paul wants them to mature in their thinking.

**2. Tongues as a sign for unbelievers.** "In the Law it is written, 'By people of strange tongues and by the lips of foreigners will I speak to this people, and even then they will not listen to me, says the Lord.' Thus tongues are a sign not for believers but for unbelievers" (14:21–22). Paul cites Isaiah 28:11–12: when Israel would not listen to the prophets, God made them hear unintelligible Assyrian speech, a sign of judgment. The passage is difficult; a common reading is that in the gathering, unintelligible tongues bring unbelievers not to repentance but to a sign of their unbelief, leaving them to say "you are out of your minds" (14:23).

**3. "God is really among you."** "But if all prophesy, and an unbeliever or outsider enters, he is convicted by all, he is called to account by all, the secrets of his heart are disclosed, and so, falling on his face, he will worship God and declare that God is really among you" (14:24–25). This is the best outcome of a gathering: an outsider comes in and, rather than being driven away by strange phenomena, hears God's word, is searched by God, confesses and repents, and worships God.

> The point: A gathering is at its best not when we look spiritual, but when an outsider says, "God is really among you." So we speak God's word in words he can understand.

## Going Deeper: 14:26–40 Order in the Gathering

**1. All things for building up.** "What then, brothers? When you come together, each one has a hymn, a lesson, a revelation, a tongue, or an interpretation. Let all things be done for building up" (14:26). Here we see that the early church's gatherings involved many participants.

**2. Order.** For tongues, "let there be only two or at most three, and each in turn, and let someone interpret. But if there is no one to interpret, let each of them keep silent in church"; for prophecy, "let two or three prophets speak, and let the others weigh what is said" (14:27–29). "The spirits of prophets are subject to prophets. For God is not a God of confusion but of peace" (14:32–33). The Spirit's work does not make people lose control; spiritual people can be quiet, wait and take turns.

**3. "The women should keep silent in the churches."** "The women should keep silent in the churches. For they are not permitted to speak, but should be in submission, as the Law also says. If there is anything they desire to learn, let them ask their husbands at home. For it is shameful for a woman to speak in church" (14:34–35). Most interpreters conclude that this cannot mean women may never speak at all in the gathering, since in 11:5 Paul has already mentioned women praying and prophesying there; so the "silence" here concerns a particular kind of speech. The main readings are these:
- **Not taking part in weighing prophecies**: many complementarian scholars (e.g. D. A. Carson, Wayne Grudem) note that this follows directly on verse 29, "let the others weigh what is said," and take it to mean that women should not publicly judge the prophets' words before the congregation, since that involves the authority to teach (cf. 1 Tim 2:12).
- **Not interrupting with questions**: many scholars, especially egalitarians (e.g. Craig Keener), hold that most women then had little education and may have been calling out questions and interrupting, so Paul says "let them ask their husbands at home"; this is for the order of the gathering, not a permanent command forbidding women to speak.
- **A later addition**: some scholars, mostly egalitarian (e.g. Gordon Fee), think these two verses may have been added later by a copyist, because in a few Western manuscripts they appear after verse 40; they also argue the verses fit poorly with 11:5 and the flow from verse 33 to verse 36. But no extant manuscript lacks them, and the standard Greek text (NA28) and most evangelical commentators retain them as Paul's own words.

"As the Law also says" is not easy either, since no Old Testament verse says this directly. In 14:21 Paul calls Isaiah "the Law," so the word can refer to the Old Testament generally. Some take it as the creation order of Genesis 2 (cf. 11:8–9; 1 Tim 2:13), others as Genesis 3:16, others as a general Old Testament principle. Chinese churches differ in practice: some have women pastors who preach and teach, others reserve the pulpit for men while sisters still pray, testify, and teach women and children. Whatever the practice, we must remember that God pours out his Spirit "on all flesh," and "your sons and your daughters shall prophesy" (Joel 2:28; Acts 2:17–18). Peter declared this promise fulfilled at Pentecost, and Philip's four daughters prophesied (Acts 21:9). Jesus let Mary sit at his feet as a learner (Luke 10:39) and made women the first witnesses of his resurrection (John 20:17–18); Paul here also assumes that sisters will learn. Sisters' gifts are God's gift to the church and must not be despised; and churches with different practices need not judge one another over this.

However "submission" and "silence" here are understood, this passage never requires a wife to endure her husband's violence, or a sister to keep quiet about abuse in the church. A woman who is being harmed may leave danger, and should report it to the police (Rom 13:4). The church must listen to her and protect her; it may not hide abuse to save face, and it may not leave the abuser in office or give him access again to victims or children.

**4. A command of the Lord.** "If anyone thinks that he is a prophet, or spiritual, he should acknowledge that the things I am writing to you are a command of the Lord" (14:37). What Paul writes is not merely his personal opinion but the Lord's command.

**5. Do not forbid, and keep order.** "So, my brothers, earnestly desire to prophesy, and do not forbid speaking in tongues. But all things should be done decently and in order" (14:39–40). This sentence balances both sides: don't forbid the Spirit's gifts out of fear of disorder, and don't let the gathering lose its order in pursuit of gifts. In Chinese churches today these verses speak to both sides. Charismatic churches should ask: when everyone prays aloud in tongues at once with no interpretation, can an outsider say "Amen"? When someone says "the Lord told me," does anyone weigh it by Scripture? If someone uses a "revelation" to tell a person whom to marry, how much to give or that they may not leave the church, that is control, not prophecy. Non-charismatic churches should ask: have we simply "forbidden" out of fear, or looked down on brothers and sisters whose experience differs? A pulpit full of jargon outsiders cannot follow is another kind of uninterpreted tongue. Both sides use the same measure: does this build people up, and does it help them know Christ?

> The point: God is not a God of confusion but of peace. Everything in the gathering should be done decently and in order, so that all are built up on Christ the foundation (cf. 3:11).

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "The one who prophesies speaks to people for their upbuilding and encouragement and consolation." Recall a few words that once held me up: perhaps when I was most discouraged, an older believer patted my shoulder and said, "God has not forgotten you"; perhaps a verse a brother or sister prayed over me in small group; perhaps just a short text message. Those words were few, but they stayed with me for years. Perhaps I can't remember anyone speaking to me like that; perhaps what I have mostly heard at church is criticism. Yet there is one word the Lord has spoken to me himself: in these last days God has spoken to us by his Son (Heb 1:2), and what he says to me is not condemnation but "your sins are forgiven" (Mark 2:5). I also remember words I have spoken that did not build up but wounded: harsh words to my children at home, gossip passed on at church. This is sin; but these sins too are covered by his forgiveness, so I can stop defending myself, confess them to the Lord, and go and own them to the person I hurt. I often think building others up is the work of pastors and speakers. But Paul says "each one has a hymn, a lesson," and everyone can speak a few words that build others up; even if I do not prophesy, the aim of my speech is the same as prophecy's: building up (Eph 4:29; 1 Thess 5:11). Today, can I ask the Lord to lay one person on my heart, and pass on to them, in a few simple words, the grace I have heard from him? I can speak words that build up not because I am eloquent, but because his word has built me up first. His "your sins are forgiven" is the source of every word I speak.
