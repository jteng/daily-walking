## 本章脉络

第十五章是全书的高峰，讲死人复活。哥林多教会中「有人说没有死人复活的事」（15:12），保罗用全章来回答。这一章分五段。**福音的核心**（1-11节）：基督照圣经所说为我们的罪死了，埋葬了，第三天复活了，并且显给许多人看。**若基督没有复活**（12-19节）：我们所传的、所信的都是枉然，我们仍在罪里。**基督已经复活**（20-28节）：他是「睡了之人初熟的果子」，他必作王，直到「尽末了所毁灭的仇敌就是死」。**为什么天天冒死**（29-34节）：若没有复活，信徒的受苦就没有意义。**复活的身体与最后的得胜**（35-58节）：所种的是必朽坏的，复活的是不朽坏的；「死啊！你得胜的权势在哪里？」

这一章的转折点在20节：「但基督已经从死里复活。」前面的「若」字，都在这一句面前倒塌了。复活不是福音的附录，而是福音的中心：没有复活，就没有基督教的信仰。

## 深读 15:1-11 福音的核心

**1. 「第一」要紧的事。** 「我当日所领受又传给你们的：第一，就是基督照圣经所说，为我们的罪死了，而且埋葬了；又照圣经所说，第三天复活了。」（15:3-4）「第一」是指最重要的事。这几句话有固定的格式，「领受」和「传给」又是传递传统的用语（参11:23），所以许多学者认为，这是保罗信主后不久就领受的一段早期教会的信条，可能是在耶稣受死后几年之内就形成的；保罗信主大约三年后，曾在耶路撒冷见过矶法和雅各（加1:18-19），很可能就是那时领受的。福音的核心是历史事件：基督死了，埋葬了，复活了，而且这一切都是「照圣经所说」，应验了旧约（参赛53:5-12；诗16:10；何6:2）。「为我们的罪」说明了基督受死的意义：他是替我们承担罪的刑罚。

**2. 许多见证人。** 「并且显给矶法看，然后显给十二使徒看；后来一时显给五百多弟兄看，其中一大半到如今还在，却也有已经睡了的。以后显给雅各看，再显给众使徒看，末了也显给我看。」（15:5-8）「其中一大半到如今还在」，等于是说：你们可以去问他们。雅各是主的弟弟，耶稣在世的时候，连他的弟兄也不信他（约7:5），后来却成了耶路撒冷教会的领袖（徒15:13）。这份名单没有提到妇女，许多学者认为，这可能是因为当时妇女的见证在法庭上不太被看重，而这是一段公开传讲的信条；但四福音都记载，最先来到空坟墓的是妇女，马太福音和约翰福音也记载，复活的主最先向她们显现（太28:9-10；约20:14-18）。

**3. 「是蒙神的恩才成的」。** 「我原是使徒中最小的，不配称为使徒，因为我从前逼迫神的教会。然而，我今日成了何等人，是蒙神的恩才成的。」（15:9-10）保罗称自己「如同未到产期而生的人一般」（15:8）。这个词的意思有不同理解，许多人认为是因为他不是在耶稣地上的时候跟随他，而是在逼迫教会的时候被主拦住。他比众使徒格外劳苦，「这原不是我，乃是神的恩与我同在」。

> 要点：福音的核心是历史事实：基督为我们的罪死了，埋葬了，第三天复活了，并且有许多见证人。

## 深读 15:12-19 若基督没有复活

**1. 「没有死人复活的事」。** 「既传基督是从死里复活了，怎么在你们中间有人说没有死人复活的事呢？」（15:12）这些哥林多人可能受了希腊哲学（特别是柏拉图一派）的影响，认为身体是灵魂的监牢，死后灵魂得释放就好了，身体复活是不可思议的事（参徒17:32）。也有学者认为，他们以为自己已经进入了完全属灵的境界，不再需要身体的复活（参4:8；提后2:18）。

**2. 一连串的「若」。** 保罗从反面推论：「若基督没有复活，我们所传的便是枉然，你们所信的也是枉然」（15:14）；「基督若没有复活，你们的信便是徒然，你们仍在罪里。就是在基督里睡了的人也灭亡了。」（15:17-18）基督教的信仰不是一套道德教训，也不是一种心理安慰；它建立在一件历史事件上。若基督没有复活，十字架就只是一场悲剧，我们的罪也没有被除去。

**3. 比众人更可怜。** 「我们若靠基督只在今生有指望，就算比众人更可怜。」（15:19）这句话很诚实：信徒为主受苦、舍弃、牺牲，若死后什么都没有，那确实是白白受苦。基督教的盼望，从来不只是今生过得更好。

> 要点：基督教的信仰建立在复活这件历史事件上。若基督没有复活，我们仍在罪里；但他若复活了，一切都改变了。

## 深读 15:20-28 初熟的果子

**1. 「但基督已经从死里复活」。** 「但基督已经从死里复活，成为睡了之人初熟的果子。」（15:20）「初熟的果子」是收割时先献给神的第一捆庄稼（利23:10-11），它保证后面还有整个收成。基督的复活不只是一件孤立的神迹，而是保证：所有属他的人，将来也必复活。

**2. 亚当与基督。** 「死既是因一人而来，死人复活也是因一人而来。在亚当里众人都死了；照样，在基督里众人也都要复活。」（15:21-22；参罗5:12-21）这不是说所有人都要得救，因为23节接着说：「以后，在他来的时候，是那些属基督的。」在亚当里的众人，因为与亚当联合而死；在基督里的众人，因为与基督联合而活。圣经在别处也说，所有的人都要复活，有的复活得生，有的复活定罪（约5:28-29）；这里的「在基督里……都要复活」，是指复活得生命。

**3. 尽末了的仇敌。** 「因为基督必要作王，等神把一切仇敌都放在他的脚下。尽末了所毁灭的仇敌就是死。」（15:25-26；参诗110:1；8:6）今天我们仍然面对死亡，死仍是仇敌，不是朋友；但它已经是一个注定要被毁灭的仇敌。

**4. 「子也要自己服」。** 「万物既服了他，那时子也要自己服那叫万物服他的，叫神在万物之上，为万物之主。」（15:28）这节经文不是说子在本质上次于父，或说子的国会结束（「他的国也没有穷尽」，路1:33）。许多解经家（如奥古斯丁、加尔文）认为，这是指子以成了肉身的中保身份，完成救赎的工作以后，把国交与父，叫三一神得着一切的荣耀（参第十一章关于「神是基督的头」的说明）；在新天新地里，宝座仍是「神和羔羊的宝座」（启22:3）。子与父同质、同等、同荣。

> 要点：基督的复活是初熟的果子，保证属他的人都要复活。死仍是仇敌，但已经是注定被毁灭的仇敌。

## 深读 15:29-34 为什么天天冒死

**1. 为死人受洗。** 「不然，那些为死人受洗的，将来怎样呢？若死人总不复活，因何为他们受洗呢？」（15:29）这是新约中最难解的经文之一，历来有几十种解释。主要的有：
- 哥林多有些人为已经去世的人代受洗礼；保罗只是提到这个做法，用来说明连他们也相信复活，并没有赞同它（如戈登·费依）。
- 有人（如锡塞尔顿）认为，有人因为已经去世的信徒（例如亲人临终时的见证），盼望将来与他们重聚，就信主受洗。
- 一些较早的解经家认为，「死人」指受洗的人自己必死的身体：受洗就是承认自己将来要死，也盼望复活。

耶稣基督后期圣徒教会（俗称摩门教）根据这节经文，为去世的人举行代洗，并认为逝者可以接受或拒绝这代行的仪式。可是圣经没有一处吩咐这样做，而且圣经清楚地教导：「人人都有一死，死后且有审判」（来9:27），每一个人都要亲自信靠基督。

**2. 天天冒死。** 「我们又因何时刻冒险呢？……我是天天冒死。我若当日像寻常人，在以弗所同野兽战斗，那于我有什么益处呢？若死人不复活，我们就吃吃喝喝吧！因为明天要死了。」（15:30-32；引赛22:13）「同野兽战斗」可能是比喻，指在以弗所遇见凶猛的敌人（参16:9；徒19章）；也有人认为是实际的经历。保罗的意思是：若没有复活，他为福音所受的苦就毫无意义，不如及时行乐。

**3. 滥交是败坏善行。** 「你们不要自欺；滥交是败坏善行。」（15:33）这句话出自希腊诗人米南德的诗句，当时已成为俗语。「滥交」是指与坏人来往，在上下文中就是与那些否认复活的人来往。错误的教导会带来错误的生活。

> 要点：复活的盼望使信徒的受苦有意义。若没有复活，人就只剩下「吃吃喝喝」；但因为有复活，为主受苦不是徒然的。

## 深读 15:35-49 复活的身体

**1. 种子的比喻。** 「或有人问：『死人怎样复活，带着什么身体来呢？』无知的人哪，你所种的，若不死就不能生。并且你所种的不是那将来的形体，不过是子粒……但神随自己的意思给他一个形体。」（15:35-38）种子和长出来的植物，是同一个生命，却是很不一样的形体。复活的身体也是这样：与现在的身体有连续（是同一个人），也有改变（是全新的样式）。

**2. 四个对比。** 「所种的是必朽坏的，复活的是不朽坏的；所种的是羞辱的，复活的是荣耀的；所种的是软弱的，复活的是强壮的；所种的是血气的身体，复活的是灵性的身体。」（15:42-44）「灵性的身体」不是说没有形体、只有灵魂，而是指完全被圣灵掌管、充满圣灵生命的身体（参罗8:11）。复活的主可以被摸、可以吃东西（路24:39-43），我们将来的身体也要像他「荣耀的身体」（腓3:21）。

**3. 不只是灵魂得释放，也不是轮回。** 信徒死后，灵魂会立刻与主同在（腓1:23；林后5:8），但这还不是最终的盼望；最终的盼望是身体复活。圣经的盼望不是希腊思想所说的灵魂脱离身体就好，也不是佛教和民间信仰所说的轮回转世。轮回是一次又一次地回到这个充满苦难的世界；复活是神亲自使我们这同一个人，带着改变了的身体，进入永远不再有死亡的新天新地。

**4. 两个亚当。** 「首先的人亚当成了有灵的活人」；「末后的亚当成了叫人活的灵」……「我们既有属土的形状，将来也必有属天的形状。」（15:45-49）我们从亚当那里得了必死的身体；从基督那里，我们要得着复活的身体。

> 要点：复活的身体与现在的身体是同一个人，却完全改变：不朽坏、荣耀、强壮，被圣灵充满，像基督荣耀的身体。

## 深读 15:50-58 死被得胜吞灭

**1. 都要改变。** 「我如今把一件奥秘的事告诉你们：我们不是都要睡觉，乃是都要改变，就在一霎时，眨眼之间，号筒末次吹响的时候。」（15:51-52；参帖前4:16）主再来的时候，已经死了的信徒要复活，还活着的信徒要改变。15:50「血肉之体不能承受神的国」，是指现在必朽坏、必死的身体（同节下半：「必朽坏的不能承受不朽坏的」），不是说复活的身体没有形体。

**2. 死啊，你的毒钩在哪里？** 「那时经上所记『死被得胜吞灭』的话就应验了。死啊！你得胜的权势在哪里？死啊！你的毒钩在哪里？死的毒钩就是罪，罪的权势就是律法。」（15:54-56；引赛25:8；何13:14）死之所以可怕，是因为罪；罪之所以有权势，是因为律法显明、甚至激动人的罪（罗7:8-11），又定人的罪（加3:10）。可是基督已经担当了我们的罪，满足了律法的要求，「为我们受了咒诅」（加3:13），所以死的毒钩已经被拔除了。

**3. 感谢神。** 「感谢神，使我们藉着我们的主耶稣基督得胜。」（15:57）得胜不是我们自己争取来的，是神「使我们……得胜」。

**4. 不是徒然的。** 「所以，我亲爱的弟兄们，你们务要坚固，不可摇动，常常竭力多做主工；因为知道，你们的劳苦在主里面不是徒然的。」（15:58）复活的盼望不是叫人逃避现实，而是叫人在今天更坚定地事奉。因为有复活，今天为主所做的每一件小事，都不会白费。

**5. 面对亲人的离世。** 华人的丧礼常常充满了恐惧和忌讳，也有许多祭拜的仪式。信徒在丧礼中不参与上香、跪拜，但可以用追思礼拜（或称安息礼拜）、献花、诗歌和见证，来纪念逝者、安慰家人（参第八章和第十章）。信徒可以哀哭，耶稣在拉撒路的坟前也「哭了」（约11:35）；但我们的忧伤，不「像那些没有指望的人一样」（帖前4:13）。至于那些没有信主就离世的亲人，圣经清楚地说，人得救只有靠着基督（徒4:12）；但我们不知道每一个人临终时在神面前的光景，定人结局的也不是我们。我们可以把他们交托给那位「审判全地的主」，他必行公义（创18:25），而我们今天要做的，是把这复活的盼望告诉还活着的人。

> 要点：基督已经胜过死亡，死的毒钩已经被拔除。所以我们可以带着盼望哀哭，也可以坚固不摇动地事奉主。

## 默想

**默想：** 「若基督没有复活……就是在基督里睡了的人也灭亡了。」想起我所爱的一位已经在主里睡了的人：也许是祖母，也许是父亲，也许是一位朋友。我记得那天的灵堂，记得盖棺时的哭声，记得回家后那个空出来的位子。如果保罗只写到第19节，我的盼望就停在那里：他走了，我只剩下回忆，我们「比众人更可怜」。可是保罗接着写下了一个「但」字：「但基督已经从死里复活，成为睡了之人初熟的果子。」（15:20）这个「但」字，不是建立在我的感觉上，而是建立在那个空了的坟墓上，建立在矶法、雅各、五百多位弟兄亲眼看见的那一位身上。他复活了，所以那位在主里睡了的人，也必复活；我们还要在主面前重聚，带着不朽坏、荣耀的身体。我今天也许仍然会哭，这不是没有信心；耶稣也哭过。但我可以在眼泪中，和保罗一同说：「感谢神，使我们藉着我们的主耶稣基督得胜。」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 15 is the summit of the letter: the resurrection of the dead. Some in the Corinthian church were saying "that there is no resurrection of the dead" (15:12), and Paul gives the whole chapter to answering them. It has five parts. **The heart of the gospel** (vv. 1–11): Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared to many. **If Christ has not been raised** (vv. 12–19): our preaching and your faith are in vain, and you are still in your sins. **Christ has been raised** (vv. 20–28): he is "the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep," and he must reign until "the last enemy to be destroyed is death." **Why face danger every hour?** (vv. 29–34): without resurrection, believers' suffering has no meaning. **The resurrection body and the final victory** (vv. 35–58): what is sown is perishable, what is raised is imperishable; "O death, where is your victory?"

The turning point of the chapter is verse 20: "But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead." All the "ifs" before it collapse before this sentence. The resurrection is not an appendix to the gospel but its center: without the resurrection there is no Christian faith.

## Going Deeper: 15:1–11 The Heart of the Gospel

**1. Of first importance.** "For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures" (15:3–4). These lines have a fixed form, and "received" and "delivered" are the language of handing on tradition (cf. 11:23), so many scholars think this is an early church creed that Paul received soon after his conversion, perhaps formed within a few years of Jesus' death; about three years after his conversion Paul met Cephas and James in Jerusalem (Gal 1:18–19), a likely occasion for receiving it. The heart of the gospel is historical events: Christ died, was buried and was raised, all "in accordance with the Scriptures," fulfilling the Old Testament (cf. Isa 53:5–12; Ps 16:10; Hos 6:2). "For our sins" gives the meaning of his death: he bore the penalty of our sins in our place.

**2. Many witnesses.** "And that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve. Then he appeared to more than five hundred brothers at one time, most of whom are still alive, though some have fallen asleep. Then he appeared to James, then to all the apostles. Last of all, as to one untimely born, he appeared also to me" (15:5–8). "Most of whom are still alive" amounts to saying: you can go and ask them. James was the Lord's brother; during Jesus' ministry "not even his brothers believed in him" (John 7:5), yet James became a leader of the Jerusalem church (Acts 15:13). This list does not mention the women, perhaps, as many suggest, because women's testimony carried little legal weight then and this was a creed for public proclamation. But all four Gospels record that women were first at the empty tomb, and Matthew and John record that the risen Lord appeared first to them (Matt 28:9–10; John 20:14–18).

**3. "By the grace of God I am what I am."** "For I am the least of the apostles, unworthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the church of God. But by the grace of God I am what I am" (15:9–10). Paul calls himself "one untimely born" (15:8). The word's sense is debated; many take it to mean that he did not follow Jesus during his earthly ministry but was stopped by the Lord while persecuting the church. He worked harder than any of them, "though it was not I, but the grace of God that is with me."

> The point: The heart of the gospel is historical fact: Christ died for our sins, was buried, was raised on the third day, and was seen by many witnesses.

## Going Deeper: 15:12–19 If Christ Has Not Been Raised

**1. "No resurrection of the dead."** "Now if Christ is proclaimed as raised from the dead, how can some of you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?" (15:12). These Corinthians may have been influenced by Greek philosophy (especially the Platonic strand), which saw the body as the prison of the soul: at death the soul is released, and a bodily resurrection is unthinkable (cf. Acts 17:32). Others think they believed they had already arrived at full spiritual life and needed no bodily resurrection (cf. 4:8; 2 Tim 2:18).

**2. A chain of "ifs."** Paul argues from the opposite side: "if Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain and your faith is in vain" (15:14); "if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in your sins. Then those also who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished" (15:17–18). The Christian faith is not a set of moral teachings or a psychological comfort; it rests on a historical event. If Christ has not been raised, the cross is only a tragedy and our sins have not been taken away.

**3. Most to be pitied.** "If in this life only we have hoped in Christ, we are of all people most to be pitied" (15:19). This is honest: if believers suffer, give up and sacrifice for the Lord, and there is nothing after death, they have truly suffered for nothing. Christian hope has never been merely "a better life now."

> The point: The Christian faith rests on the historical event of the resurrection. If Christ has not been raised, we are still in our sins; but if he has, everything has changed.

## Going Deeper: 15:20–28 The Firstfruits

**1. "But in fact Christ has been raised."** "But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep" (15:20). The "firstfruits" were the first sheaf of the harvest offered to God (Lev 23:10–11), the guarantee that the whole harvest would follow. Christ's resurrection is not an isolated miracle but a guarantee: all who belong to him will also be raised.

**2. Adam and Christ.** "For as by a man came death, by a man has come also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive" (15:21–22; cf. Rom 5:12–21). This does not mean everyone will be saved, for verse 23 goes on: "then at his coming those who belong to Christ." All who are in Adam die because they are united to Adam; all who are in Christ live because they are united to Christ. Elsewhere Scripture says that all will be raised, some to life and some to judgment (John 5:28–29); "made alive" in Christ here means resurrection to life.

**3. The last enemy.** "For he must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet. The last enemy to be destroyed is death" (15:25–26; cf. Ps 110:1; 8:6). We still face death today, and death is still an enemy, not a friend; but it is an enemy already doomed to destruction.

**4. "The Son himself will also be subjected."** "When all things are subjected to him, then the Son himself will also be subjected to him who put all things in subjection under him, that God may be all in all" (15:28). This verse does not mean that the Son is lesser in nature than the Father, or that the Son's kingdom will end ("of his kingdom there will be no end," Luke 1:33). Many interpreters (so Augustine and Calvin) take it to refer to the Son, as the incarnate Mediator, handing over the kingdom to the Father once the work of redemption is complete, so that the triune God receives all the glory (cf. the note in chapter 11 on "the head of Christ is God"); in the new creation the throne is still "the throne of God and of the Lamb" (Rev 22:3). The Son is of one being with the Father, equal in power and glory.

> The point: Christ's resurrection is the firstfruits, guaranteeing that all who belong to him will be raised. Death is still an enemy, but an enemy already doomed.

## Going Deeper: 15:29–34 Why Face Danger Every Hour?

**1. Baptized on behalf of the dead.** "Otherwise, what do people mean by being baptized on behalf of the dead? If the dead are not raised at all, why are people baptized on their behalf?" (15:29). This is among the hardest verses in the New Testament, with dozens of proposed explanations. The main ones are:
- Some in Corinth were being baptized in place of people who had died; Paul mentions the practice only to show that even they believed in resurrection, without approving it (e.g. Gordon Fee).
- Some (e.g. Anthony Thiselton) think people came to faith and were baptized because of believers who had died (for example, a relative's testimony on their deathbed), hoping to be reunited with them.
- Some older interpreters hold that "the dead" refers to the baptized person's own mortal body: baptism acknowledges that one will die and hopes for resurrection.

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints draws on this verse to perform baptisms on behalf of the dead, holding that the deceased may accept or reject the proxy ordinance. But Scripture nowhere commands this, and it teaches plainly that "it is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment" (Heb 9:27); each person must personally trust in Christ.

**2. Dying every day.** "Why am I in danger every hour?... I die every day! What do I gain if, humanly speaking, I fought with beasts at Ephesus? If the dead are not raised, 'Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die'" (15:30–32, quoting Isa 22:13). "Fought with beasts" may be a figure for fierce opponents in Ephesus (cf. 16:9; Acts 19); others take it as a literal experience. Paul's point is that without resurrection his suffering for the gospel would be meaningless, and he might as well live for pleasure.

**3. Bad company.** "Do not be deceived: 'Bad company ruins good morals'" (15:33). These words come from the Greek poet Menander and had become a proverb. Wrong teaching leads to wrong living.

> The point: The hope of resurrection gives believers' suffering its meaning. Without resurrection, all that is left is "eat and drink"; but because there is resurrection, suffering for the Lord is not in vain.

## Going Deeper: 15:35–49 The Resurrection Body

**1. The seed.** "But someone will ask, 'How are the dead raised? With what kind of body do they come?' You foolish person! What you sow does not come to life unless it dies. And what you sow is not the body that is to be, but a bare kernel... But God gives it a body as he has chosen" (15:35–38). The seed and the plant are the same life in very different forms. So it is with the resurrection body: there is continuity (it is the same person) and transformation (an entirely new kind of body).

**2. Four contrasts.** "What is sown is perishable; what is raised is imperishable. It is sown in dishonor; it is raised in glory. It is sown in weakness; it is raised in power. It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body" (15:42–44). A "spiritual body" does not mean a bodiless soul, but a body wholly governed by the Holy Spirit and filled with his life (cf. Rom 8:11). The risen Lord could be touched and could eat (Luke 24:39–43), and our future bodies will be like "his glorious body" (Phil 3:21).

**3. Not merely the soul set free, not reincarnation.** Believers who die are at once with the Lord (Phil 1:23; 2 Cor 5:8), but that is not yet the final hope; the final hope is the resurrection of the body. The hope of Scripture is not the Greek idea of the soul escaping the body, nor the cycle of rebirth in Buddhism and the transmigration of souls in folk religion. Reincarnation means returning again and again to this world of suffering; resurrection is God himself bringing the same person, with a transformed body, into a new heavens and new earth where death will be no more.

**4. Two Adams.** "'The first man Adam became a living being'; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit... Just as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we shall also bear the image of the man of heaven" (15:45–49). From Adam we received mortal bodies; from Christ we will receive resurrection bodies.

> The point: The resurrection body is the same person, yet wholly transformed: imperishable, glorious, powerful, filled with the Spirit, like Christ's glorious body.

## Going Deeper: 15:50–58 Death Swallowed Up in Victory

**1. All changed.** "Behold! I tell you a mystery. We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet" (15:51–52; cf. 1 Thess 4:16). When the Lord returns, believers who have died will be raised, and believers still living will be changed. "Flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God" (15:50) refers to our present perishable, mortal state ("nor does the perishable inherit the imperishable"), not to bodily existence as such.

**2. O death, where is your sting?** "Then shall come to pass the saying that is written: 'Death is swallowed up in victory.' 'O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting?' The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law" (15:54–56, quoting Isa 25:8; Hos 13:14). Death is terrible because of sin; sin has power because the law exposes and even provokes sin (Rom 7:8–11) and condemns us (Gal 3:10). But Christ has borne our sin and satisfied the law's demands, "becoming a curse for us" (Gal 3:13), so death's sting has been drawn.

**3. Thanks be to God.** "But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ" (15:57). The victory is not something we win; God "gives us the victory."

**4. Not in vain.** "Therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that in the Lord your labor is not in vain" (15:58). The hope of resurrection doesn't make us escape reality; it makes us serve more steadfastly now. Because of the resurrection, every small thing done for the Lord today counts.

**5. When a loved one dies.** Chinese funerals are often full of fear and taboo, and of many rites of offering. Believers do not take part in offering incense or bowing in the rite, but can remember the departed and comfort the family with a memorial service, flowers, hymns and testimony (cf. chapters 8 and 10). Believers may weep; Jesus too "wept" at Lazarus's tomb (John 11:35); but we do not grieve "as others do who have no hope" (1 Thess 4:13). As for relatives who died without coming to faith, Scripture is clear that salvation is in Christ alone (Acts 4:12); but we do not know how things stood between each person and God at the end, and judging them is not ours to do. We can entrust them to "the Judge of all the earth," who will do what is just (Gen 18:25), and our task today is to tell the living about this resurrection hope.

> The point: Christ has conquered death, and its sting has been drawn. So we can weep with hope, and serve the Lord steadfast and immovable.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "If Christ has not been raised... then those also who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished." I think of someone I loved who has fallen asleep in the Lord: perhaps a grandmother, a father, a friend. I remember the funeral hall, the weeping when the coffin was closed, the empty place at home afterward. If Paul had stopped at verse 19, my hope would stop there too: they are gone, I have only memories, and we are "of all people most to be pitied." But Paul goes on to write one word, "But": "But in fact Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep" (15:20). That "but" does not rest on my feelings; it rests on an empty tomb, on the one whom Cephas, James and more than five hundred brothers saw with their own eyes. He is risen, so the one who fell asleep in him will rise too, and we will meet again before the Lord, with imperishable and glorious bodies. Today I may still weep, and that is not a lack of faith; Jesus wept too. But through my tears I can say with Paul: "Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."
