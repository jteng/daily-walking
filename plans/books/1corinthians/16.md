## 本章脉络

第十六章是全书的结尾，看起来只是一些实际的安排和问安，却处处显出福音带来的新关系。这一章分四段。**为圣徒捐钱**（1-4节）：每逢七日的第一日，各人照自己的进项抽出来留着，由众人举荐的人送到耶路撒冷。**保罗和同工的行程**（5-12节）：保罗要住在以弗所，因为「有宽大又有功效的门」为他开了；提摩太要来，亚波罗暂时不来。**最后的劝勉**（13-18节）：「你们务要警醒，在真道上站立得稳，要作大丈夫，要刚强。凡你们所做的都要凭爱心而做。」又要敬重司提反一家这样服事圣徒的人。**问安与祝福**（19-24节）：「若有人不爱主，这人可诅可咒。主必要来！」最后以恩典和爱作结。

这一章的中心是14节。一封充满责备的信，最后落在「爱」字上：保罗不是要赢得辩论，而是要哥林多人在基督里彼此相爱，等候主再来。

## 深读 16:1-4 为圣徒捐钱

**1. 为耶路撒冷的穷人。** 「论到为圣徒捐钱，我从前怎样吩咐加拉太的众教会，你们也当怎样行。」（16:1）这笔捐项是为「耶路撒冷圣徒中的穷人」（罗15:26；参林后8-9章）。外邦教会为犹太信徒捐钱，不只是救济，更是表明犹太人和外邦人在基督里是一家。

**2. 有计划、按比例。** 「每逢七日的第一日，各人要照自己的进项抽出来留着，免得我来的时候现凑。」（16:2）这里有几个原则：
- **定期**：「每逢七日的第一日」。「留着」可能是在自己家里存起来；但保罗特别指定这一天，也显示早期教会已经在这一天（主复活的日子）聚会（参徒20:7）。
- **人人有分**：「各人」都要参与，不只是有钱的人。
- **按比例**：「照自己的进项」，收入多的多给，收入少的少给（参林后8:12）。
- **有计划**：平时就预备，不是临时被情绪推动。

**3. 透明与问责。** 「及至我来到了，你们写信举荐谁，我就打发他们，把你们的捐资送到耶路撒冷去。」（16:3）保罗不自己经手这笔钱，而是让教会举荐的人一同送去，「免得有人……挑我们的不是。我们留心行光明的事，不但在主面前，就在人面前也是这样」（林后8:20-21）。今天的教会处理奉献，也要公开、有多人负责、经得起查账。

**4. 这不是成功神学。** 保罗从来没有说，奉献是一种投资，给得越多，神就会让你越富有。奉献的动力是恩典：「他本来富足，却为你们成了贫穷，叫你们因他的贫穷，可以成为富足」（林后8:9）；奉献的态度是甘心，「捐得乐意的人是神所喜爱的」（林后9:7）。

> 要点：奉献是对基督恩典的回应：定期、按比例、有计划，也要公开透明，使人在主面前和人面前都看为光明。

## 深读 16:5-12 保罗和同工

**1. 「主若许我」。** 「我如今不愿意路过见你们；主若许我，我就指望和你们同住几时。」（16:7）保罗有计划，却把计划交在主手里（参雅4:15）。后来他的行程果然有所改变（参林后1:15-17）。

**2. 宽大的门，也有许多反对的人。** 「但我要仍旧住在以弗所，直等到五旬节；因为有宽大又有功效的门为我开了，并且反对的人也多。」（16:8-9）开了的门和反对的人同时存在（参徒19章）。保罗没有因为反对的人多，就认为门没有开。

**3. 提摩太。** 「若是提摩太来到，你们要留心，叫他在你们那里无所惧怕；因为他劳力做主的工，像我一样。所以，无论谁都不可藐视他。」（16:10-11）提摩太年轻，也可能比较胆怯（参提前4:12；提后1:7）。保罗请一个骄傲的教会，好好接待一位年轻的同工。

**4. 亚波罗。** 「至于兄弟亚波罗，我再三地劝他同弟兄们到你们那里去；但这时他决不愿意去，几时有了机会他必去。」（16:12）哥林多教会中有人说「我是属亚波罗的」（1:12），可是保罗称他为「兄弟」，还再三劝他去。保罗和亚波罗之间没有竞争（参3:5-9）；亚波罗也有自己的判断，保罗尊重他（这里「他决不愿意」的「愿意」，也有人理解为神的旨意）。

> 要点：福音的同工不是彼此竞争的对手，而是一同在主手中、按主的时间服事的弟兄。

## 深读 16:13-18 凡事凭爱心

**1. 四个命令。** 「你们务要警醒，在真道上站立得稳，要作大丈夫，要刚强。」（16:13）「要作大丈夫」原文的意思是要勇敢、要有担当，不是只对弟兄说的，姊妹也同样要在真道上刚强。

**2. 凭爱心而做。** 「凡你们所做的都要凭爱心而做。」（16:14）前一节的四个命令，可能让人变得刚硬；这一节却说，一切都要凭爱心。刚强而没有爱，会变成好斗；爱心而没有真道，会变成软弱。第十三章所说的爱，在这里成了全书最后的命令。

**3. 司提反一家。** 「你们晓得司提反一家，是亚该亚初结的果子，并且他们专以服事圣徒为念。我劝你们顺服这样的人，并一切同工同劳的人。」（16:15-16）司提反一家是保罗亲自施洗的（1:16）。他们得到尊重，不是因为口才或地位，而是因为「专以服事圣徒为念」。「顺服」这样的人，是指尊重、跟随那些忠心服事的人，不是盲目服从，更不是给任何人辖制别人的权柄。若有带领的人滥用属灵权柄、欺压或伤害人，信徒不必顺服，教会应当处理，涉及犯罪的也应当报警（罗13:4；参第十一章的说明）。

**4. 叫我心里快活。** 「他们叫我和你们心里都快活。这样的人，你们务要敬重。」（16:18）教会中那些默默服事、使别人「心里都快活」的人，应当被看见、被感谢。

> 要点：在真道上站立得稳，与凭爱心而做，是同一个呼召。教会要敬重那些默默服事圣徒的人。

## 深读 16:19-24 主必要来！

**1. 家里的教会。** 「亚居拉和百基拉并在他们家里的教会，因主多多地问你们安。」（16:19）亚居拉和百基拉曾在哥林多与保罗一同制造帐棚（徒18:2-3），现在在以弗所，他们的家就是教会聚会的地方。今天许多华人教会，也是从一个家庭查经开始的。

**2. 圣洁的亲嘴问安。** 「你们要亲嘴问安，彼此务要圣洁。」（16:20）这是当时家人和亲友之间问安的方式；今天在华人教会，可能是握手、拥抱或一句真诚的问候。重点是：信徒彼此是一家人。

**3. 亲笔问安。** 「我－保罗亲笔问安。」（16:21）保罗常请人代笔，最后亲手写几句，表明这封信是他写的（参罗16:22；加6:11；帖后3:17）。

**4. 「主必要来！」** 「若有人不爱主，这人可诅可咒。主必要来！」（16:22）「可诅可咒」原文是 *anathema*，是很重的话：拒绝爱主的人，是在神的审判之下。这是严肃的警告，不是许可信徒去咒诅人，而且紧接着就是「主必要来」和恩典（16:23）。这句亚兰文可以分成 *maran atha*（我们的主已经来了，或必要来；和合本这样理解），也可以分成 *marana tha*（「我们的主啊，请来！」许多现代译本这样理解）。两种读法都可能；《十二使徒遗训》10:6 记载早期一些教会在圣餐中用这句祷告，启示录22:20 也说「主耶稣啊，我愿你来！」。无论哪一种读法，保罗在说希腊话的教会里保留这句亚兰文，都显示最早说亚兰话的犹太信徒已经称耶稣为「主」；若是祷告，他们也已经向他祷告。

**5. 恩典与爱。** 「愿主耶稣基督的恩常与你们众人同在！我在基督耶稣里的爱与你们众人同在。阿们！」（16:23-24）这封信责备了分门结党、淫乱、诉讼、骄傲、在主的晚餐上的自私，最后的一句话却是：我的爱与你们「众人」同在。保罗爱这个让他头痛的教会，因为基督先爱了他们。

> 要点：全书以恩典和爱作结，也以盼望作结：「主必要来！」我们今天在爱中等候他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「主必要来！」读完哥林多前书，我好像看见了一个和我的教会很像的教会：有分门结党，有骄傲，有对性的混乱，有为钱打官司，有在聚会中各顾各的，也有对复活的怀疑。保罗没有放弃他们，最后的一句话是：「我在基督耶稣里的爱与你们众人同在。」我也可以回想这十几章，哪一章最像在说我？是第一章的「我是属保罗的」，第八章自以为有知识的骄傲，第十一章对一个人吃饭的弟兄视而不见，还是第十三章那段我读不下去的爱？这些都不是要把我推开，而是要把我带回十字架前，带到那位「为我们的罪死了，而且埋葬了」、「第三天复活了」的主面前（15:3-4）。今天，我不用一个计划来结束这卷书，而是用早期教会的祷告来结束：

主耶稣啊，我愿你来。在你来以前，求你使我在真道上站立得稳，凡我所做的，都凭爱心而做。愿你的恩常与我们众人同在。阿们。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 16 closes the letter. It looks like nothing more than practical arrangements and greetings, yet everywhere it shows the new relationships the gospel creates. It has four parts. **The collection for the saints** (vv. 1–4): on the first day of every week, each puts something aside as he may prosper, to be carried to Jerusalem by people the church approves. **Plans for Paul and his co-workers** (vv. 5–12): Paul will stay in Ephesus, because "a wide door for effective work has opened"; Timothy will come, and Apollos will not come yet. **Final exhortations** (vv. 13–18): "Be watchful, stand firm in the faith, act like men, be strong. Let all that you do be done in love." And honor people like the household of Stephanas who serve the saints. **Greetings and blessing** (vv. 19–24): "If anyone has no love for the Lord, let him be accursed. Our Lord, come!" The letter ends with grace and love.

The center of the chapter is verse 14. A letter full of rebuke ends on the word "love": Paul is not trying to win an argument, but to see the Corinthians love one another in Christ as they wait for the Lord's return.

## Going Deeper: 16:1–4 The Collection for the Saints

**1. For the poor in Jerusalem.** "Now concerning the collection for the saints: as I directed the churches of Galatia, so you also are to do" (16:1). This collection was "for the poor among the saints at Jerusalem" (Rom 15:26; cf. 2 Cor 8–9). Gentile churches giving for Jewish believers was not only relief; it showed that Jews and Gentiles are one family in Christ.

**2. Planned and proportionate.** "On the first day of every week, each of you is to put something aside and store it up, as he may prosper, so that there will be no collecting when I come" (16:2). Several principles appear here:
- **Regular**: "on the first day of every week." The money was probably set aside at home ("store it up"), but the singling out of this day also suggests the early church was already gathering on it, the day of the Lord's resurrection (cf. Acts 20:7).
- **Everyone takes part**: "each of you," not only the wealthy.
- **Proportionate**: "as he may prosper"; those who earn more give more, those who earn less give less (cf. 2 Cor 8:12).
- **Planned**: prepared beforehand, not moved by emotion on the spot.

**3. Transparency and accountability.** "And when I arrive, I will send those whom you accredit by letter to carry your gift to Jerusalem" (16:3). Paul does not handle the money alone; people the church accredits carry it together, "so that no one should blame us about this generous gift... for we aim at what is honorable not only in the Lord's sight but also in the sight of man" (2 Cor 8:20–21). Churches today should handle giving openly too, with several people responsible and books that can bear an audit.

**4. Not prosperity teaching.** Paul never says giving is an investment, so that the more you give, the richer God will make you. The motive for giving is grace: "though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you by his poverty might become rich" (2 Cor 8:9); and the attitude is gladness, for "God loves a cheerful giver" (2 Cor 9:7).

> The point: Giving is a response to Christ's grace: regular, proportionate and planned, and handled openly, so that it is honorable before the Lord and before people.

## Going Deeper: 16:5–12 Paul and His Co-workers

**1. "If the Lord permits."** "For I do not want to see you now just in passing. I hope to spend some time with you, if the Lord permits" (16:7). Paul makes plans but holds them in the Lord's hand (cf. Jas 4:15). His plans did in fact change later (cf. 2 Cor 1:15–17).

**2. A wide door, and many adversaries.** "But I will stay in Ephesus until Pentecost, for a wide door for effective work has opened to me, and there are many adversaries" (16:8–9). The open door and the many adversaries exist side by side (cf. Acts 19). Paul did not conclude from the opposition that the door was closed.

**3. Timothy.** "When Timothy comes, see that you put him at ease among you, for he is doing the work of the Lord, as I am" (16:10), and no one is to despise him (16:11). Timothy was young and perhaps timid (cf. 1 Tim 4:12; 2 Tim 1:7). Paul asks a proud church to receive a young co-worker well.

**4. Apollos.** "Now concerning our brother Apollos, I strongly urged him to visit you with the other brothers, but it was not at all his will to come now. He will come when he has opportunity" (16:12). Some in Corinth said "I follow Apollos" (1:12), yet Paul calls him "brother" and urged him to go. There was no rivalry between Paul and Apollos (cf. 3:5–9), and Apollos had his own judgment, which Paul respected (some take "will" here as God's will).

> The point: Gospel co-workers are not rivals but brothers, serving together in the Lord's hands and on the Lord's timetable.

## Going Deeper: 16:13–18 All in Love

**1. Four commands.** "Be watchful, stand firm in the faith, act like men, be strong" (16:13). "Act like men" means be courageous and take responsibility; it is not addressed only to brothers, for sisters too are to be strong in the faith.

**2. Done in love.** "Let all that you do be done in love" (16:14). The four commands before it could make people hard; this verse says that everything is to be done in love. Strength without love becomes combativeness; love without the truth becomes weakness. The love of chapter 13 becomes the letter's final command.

**3. The household of Stephanas.** "You know that the household of Stephanas were the first converts in Achaia, and that they have devoted themselves to the service of the saints, be subject to such as these, and to every fellow worker and laborer" (16:15–16). Paul himself baptized the household of Stephanas (1:16). They are honored not for eloquence or status, but because they "devoted themselves to the service of the saints." To "be subject to" such people means respecting and following those who serve faithfully; it is not blind obedience, still less authority for anyone to dominate others. Where a leader abuses spiritual authority or harms people, believers owe no such submission; the church must deal with it, and crimes should be reported to the police (Rom 13:4; cf. the note in chapter 11).

**4. They refreshed my spirit.** "For they refreshed my spirit as well as yours. Give recognition to such men" (16:18). Those in the church who serve quietly and refresh others' spirits should be noticed and thanked.

> The point: Standing firm in the faith and doing everything in love are one calling. The church should honor those who quietly serve the saints.

## Going Deeper: 16:19–24 Our Lord, Come!

**1. The church in their house.** "Aquila and Prisca, together with the church in their house, send you hearty greetings in the Lord" (16:19). Aquila and Priscilla had made tents with Paul in Corinth (Acts 18:2–3); now in Ephesus their home is where the church meets. Many Chinese churches today also began as a Bible study in someone's home.

**2. A holy kiss.** "Greet one another with a holy kiss" (16:20). This was how family and close friends greeted one another then; in Chinese churches today it may be a handshake, a hug or a heartfelt word. The point is that believers are family.

**3. In his own hand.** "I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand" (16:21). Paul often dictated his letters and added a few lines in his own hand at the end to show the letter was his (cf. Rom 16:22; Gal 6:11; 2 Thess 3:17).

**4. "Our Lord, come!"** "If anyone has no love for the Lord, let him be accursed. Our Lord, come!" (16:22). "Accursed" translates *anathema*, a very strong word: whoever refuses to love the Lord stands under God's judgment. This is a solemn warning, not permission for believers to curse people, and it leads straight into "Our Lord, come!" and grace (16:23). The Aramaic can be divided *maran atha*, "our Lord has come / is coming" (the CUV's reading), or *marana tha*, "Our Lord, come!" (the ESV and many modern versions). Both are possible. The Didache (10:6) shows some early churches using it as a prayer at the Lord's Supper, and Rev 22:20 prays "Come, Lord Jesus!" Either way, that Paul keeps the Aramaic in a Greek-speaking church shows the earliest Aramaic-speaking Jewish believers already called Jesus "our Lord"; on the prayer reading, they were praying to him.

**5. Grace and love.** "The grace of the Lord Jesus be with you. My love be with you all in Christ Jesus. Amen" (16:23–24). This letter has rebuked divisions, sexual immorality, lawsuits, pride and selfishness at the Lord's Supper; yet its last sentence is: my love be with you "all." Paul loves this church that has given him so much grief, because Christ loved them first.

> The point: The letter ends with grace and love, and with hope: "Our Lord, come!" Today we wait for him in love.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Our Lord, come!" Having read 1 Corinthians, I seem to see a church much like my own: divisions, pride, sexual confusion, lawsuits over money, people looking after themselves at the gathering, even doubts about the resurrection. Paul did not give up on them; his last sentence is, "My love be with you all in Christ Jesus." I can look back over these chapters too: which one sounds most like me? The "I follow Paul" of chapter 1, the pride of "knowledge" in chapter 8, the brother eating alone whom I never noticed in chapter 11, or the love in chapter 13 that I could hardly bring myself to read? None of these is meant to push me away; they are meant to bring me back to the cross, to the Lord who "died for our sins... was buried... was raised on the third day" (15:3–4). Today I end this book not with a plan but with the early church's prayer:

Come, Lord Jesus. Until you come, make me stand firm in the faith, and let all that I do be done in love. May your grace be with us all. Amen.
