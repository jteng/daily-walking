## 本章脉络

约翰一书第一章只有十节，可以分成两段。

**序言：生命之道显现了**（1-4节）：约翰没有问安，一开口就讲那「从起初原有的生命之道」。使徒们听见过他，看见过他，亲手摸过他。他们把所看见、所听见的传给读者，使读者与使徒相交，也与父并他儿子耶稣基督相交，使喜乐充足。

**神就是光**（5-10节）：使徒从主所听见的信息是：「神就是光，在他毫无黑暗。」接着约翰用三次「我们若说」（6、8、10节），揭穿三种虚假的说法，每一次后面都跟着一条真实的路（7、9节；2:1）：
- 说与神相交，却仍在黑暗里行；真实的路是在光明中行，耶稣的血洗净我们一切的罪。
- 说自己无罪；真实的路是认自己的罪，神必赦免。
- 说自己没有犯过罪；回答在2:1-2：我们在父那里有一位中保。

## 深读 1:1-4 我们所听见、所看见、亲手摸过的

**1. 「从起初原有的」。** 「论到从起初原有的生命之道，就是我们所听见、所看见、亲眼看过、亲手摸过的。」（1:1）这句话让人立刻想起约翰福音的开头：「太初有道，道与神同在，道就是神。」（约1:1）不过两处的重点不同。约翰福音的「太初」，是指创世以前；这里的「从起初」，有人认为也指永远，有人认为是指耶稣开始传道、福音从起初所传的样子（参2:7、24）。两种读法其实相连：从起初所传的，正是那从永远就与父同在的一位（1:2）。

**2. 「生命之道」是一个信息，还是一位？** 原文开头连说了四个「所……的」，用的是中性的代名词，好像在讲一件事、一个信息；可是后面又说这生命「原与父同在」，「显现与我们」（1:2），又像在讲一位。许多释经者认为，约翰是故意这样写的：福音的信息不能和那一位分开，传「生命之道」，就是传那位道成肉身的耶稣基督。

**3. 「亲手摸过的」。** 约翰一步一步加重：听见，看见，「亲眼看过」，最后是「亲手摸过」。这个「摸」字，路加也用在复活的主对门徒说的话里：「摸我看看！魂无骨无肉，你们看，我是有的。」（路24:39）约翰这样说，很可能是在回应那些否认「耶稣基督是成了肉身来的」人（4:2-3）。永远的生命不是一种思想，也不是只在灵里显现的幻影，而是那位原与父同在的永远的儿子，他成了有血有肉、可以被人摸到的人。
- 这对我们的信仰很要紧。基督若没有真的成了肉身，就没有真的死，他的血也不能「洗净我们一切的罪」（1:7）。
- 这也提醒我们：基督教不是建立在人的感受或个人的神秘经验上，而是建立在历史上真实发生的事，和亲眼见过的人的见证上。

**4. 「我们」是谁？** 这一段反复说「我们」，又说「传给你们」。多数释经者认为，这里的「我们」是指使徒和亲眼见过主的见证人，和读者「你们」分开。也有人认为，「我们」是指一同站在使徒传统里的教会。按前一种读法，今天的信徒没有亲眼见过主，却可以藉着使徒的见证，与使徒有同样的相交（1:3）。耶稣对多马说过：「你因看见了我才信；那没有看见就信的有福了。」（约20:29）

**5. 「使你们与我们相交」。** 「相交」这个词的意思是共有、同享。信徒彼此相交，不只是一同聚会、吃饭，而是一同有分于同一位主。约翰把两种相交放在一起：「使你们与我们相交。我们乃是与父并他儿子耶稣基督相交的。」（1:3）与神相交，必然带出与神的儿女相交；离开了与神的相交，人与人的相交也只剩下外表。

> 要点：福音不是一套可以自己去体会的道理，而是那位永远的生命真实地来到我们中间；我们藉着使徒的见证认识他，就与父、与子、也与一切信他的人相交。

## 深读 1:5 神就是光，在他毫无黑暗

**1. 「这是我们从主所听见……的信息」。** 「神就是光，在他毫无黑暗。这是我们从主所听见、又报给你们的信息。」（1:5）约翰没有说这是他自己的领悟，而是「从主所听见」的。耶稣说过：「我是世界的光。跟从我的，就不在黑暗里走，必要得着生命的光。」（约8:12）

**2. 光是什么意思？** 在圣经里，「光」至少有两层意思：
- **真理和启示：** 神是光，他让人认识他，人也在他的光中看见一切（诗36:9）。
- **圣洁和公义：** 神是光，他完全圣洁，在他里面没有一点罪恶。

约翰在这里特别强调后一层，因为接下来讲的都是罪的问题。他还加上一句：「在他毫无黑暗。」原文用的是很强的否定，意思是一点也没有。神不是大部分光明、偶尔有一点阴影；他的圣洁没有掺杂。

**3. 这对我们的意义。** 有些人以为，只要有属灵的知识，生活怎样都没有关系。约翰却说：既然神就是光，认识神的人就不能满不在乎地留在黑暗里。认识这位神，必然改变人怎样活。另一方面，神是光，也表示在他面前什么都藏不住：「黑暗也不能遮蔽我，使你不见，黑夜却如白昼发亮。」（诗139:12）这对罪人是可怕的消息，可是接下来约翰要告诉我们，这光也是好消息。

> 要点：神是完全圣洁、毫无黑暗的光；认识他，就不能和黑暗妥协，也不必再在他面前躲藏。

## 深读 1:6-7 在光明中行

**1. 第一个「我们若说」。** 「我们若说是与神相交，却仍在黑暗里行，就是说谎话，不行真理了。」（1:6）「行」在圣经里常指人的生活方式。这里的问题不是偶然跌倒，而是一面说与神相交，一面照样活在黑暗中，好像两者没有冲突。约翰的判断很直接：这是说谎话，而且「不行真理」。在约翰的用法里，真理不只是要相信的，也是要去行的（参约3:21）。

**2. 「在光明中行」是什么意思？** 「我们若在光明中行，如同神在光明中，就彼此相交，他儿子耶稣的血也洗净我们一切的罪。」（1:7）在光明中行，不是说已经毫无罪过，因为同一节就说我们还需要血的洗净。许多释经者认为，在光明中行，是指活在神面前，不躲藏，不假装，让神的光照出我们真实的样子，并且照着所看见的去回应；也包括离开被光照出来的罪，照主所行的去行（2:6）。
- 「在黑暗里行」的人，是躲开光，怕被看见（约3:20）。
- 「在光明中行」的人，不是没有罪，而是让罪被照出来，带到神面前。

**3. 「就彼此相交」。** 我们也许以为约翰会说，这样就与神相交了，他却说「就彼此相交」。在光明中行的人，不只是和神之间没有遮掩，在弟兄姊妹面前也不再需要戴面具。教会里的相交若只停留在客气和面子上，往往是因为我们还不敢一同站在光中。

**4. 「他儿子耶稣的血」。** 「血」在圣经里指的是舍命的死，特别是献祭的死：「因为活物的生命是在血中。我把这血赐给你们，可以在坛上为你们的生命赎罪。」（利17:11）约翰说的是「他儿子耶稣的血」：那位与父同在的永远的生命（1:2），真的流了血。「洗净」这个动词在这里是现在式，许多人认为它表示持续的作用：信徒在光中行，就不断地经历这血的洗净。「一切的罪」也很要紧：没有一样罪太大，是这血不能洗净的。

> 要点：在光明中行，不是装作没有罪，而是不再躲藏，并且离开光所照出的罪；我们敢站在光中，是因为耶稣的血一直在洗净我们一切的罪。

## 深读 1:8-10 我们若认自己的罪

**1. 第二个「我们若说」。** 「我们若说自己无罪，便是自欺，真理不在我们心里了。」（1:8）这里的「无罪」，原文是「没有罪」，许多人认为是指否认自己里面还有罪性，或否认自己有罪责。这样的人骗不了神，也骗不了别人，只是骗了自己。
- 约翰所反驳的人，可能已有后来诺斯底派那样的想法：人的灵是纯洁的，身体所做的不会玷污灵，所以说不上有罪。
- 今天的说法也许不一样，意思却相近：「我不过是有些缺点」「我本性不坏」「那是环境造成的」。

**2. 「我们若认自己的罪」。** 「我们若认自己的罪，神是信实的，是公义的，必要赦免我们的罪，洗净我们一切的不义。」（1:9）「认」（*homologeō*）的意思是公开承认。这个词由「同样」和「说」组成，所以许多传道人这样解释：对自己的罪说神所说的话，不再找借口，也不把罪说小。约翰说的是「认自己的罪」，罪是复数，可见不只是笼统地说「我是罪人」，而是具体地把罪带到神面前（参诗32:5）。
- 这里的认罪首先是向神认罪。雅各也吩咐信徒「要彼此认罪，互相代求」（雅5:16）。
- 天主教认为告解（和好）是基督设立的圣事，信徒要向神父逐一认罪，由神父宣告赦免。信义宗保留私下向牧者认罪、领受赦罪宣告的做法，但不要求列举每一件罪。改革宗和多数福音派教会强调，信徒可以直接到神面前认罪，也可以在合宜的时候向可信的肢体或牧者认罪，求他们代祷；牧者可以宣讲神赦罪的应许，但不要求人向神职人员认罪。三个传统都承认，赦罪终究是神的作为。

**3. 「神是信实的，是公义的」。** 神赦免认罪的人，第一是因为他「信实」：他必守自己的应许（参耶31:34）。第二是因为他「公义」。这一点很奇妙：我们以为公义的神应当惩罚罪，怎么会因为公义而赦免呢？答案在下一章：耶稣基督「为我们的罪作了挽回祭」（2:2）。罪的刑罚已经由基督担当了，所以神赦免信靠基督的人，正是公义的。保罗也说，神这样做，「使人知道他自己为义，也称信耶稣的人为义」（罗3:26）。

**4. 已经称义的人，为什么还要认罪？** 有人会问：信徒的罪不是已经一次永远蒙赦免了吗？为什么还要求赦免？改革宗的回答是：在神审判台前，信徒已经称义，这地位永不改变；可是在父与儿女的关系里，信徒犯了罪，仍会失去与神相交的喜乐，需要回来认罪。《威斯敏斯德信条》说，神继续赦免已称义之人的罪；他们永不能从称义的地位上堕落，但可能因罪落在神父亲般的不悦之下，不再享有神脸上的光，直到他们谦卑、认罪、求赦免，并重新操练信心和悔改（第十一章第五节）。耶稣也教门徒天天祷告：「免我们的债。」（太6:12）

**5. 第三个「我们若说」。** 「我们若说自己没有犯过罪，便是以神为说谎的，他的道也不在我们心里了。」（1:10）1:8说的是否认自己里面有罪，这里说的是否认自己做过的罪。这比自欺更严重：神的话清楚地说人人都犯了罪（罗3:23），否认这一点，就是说神在说谎。

**6. 这一段和后面说信徒不犯罪的经文。** 约翰在这里清楚地说，信徒若说自己无罪，就是自欺。后面3:6、9又说从神生的「不犯罪」。两处要一起读：约翰一面不许人轻看罪，一面也不许人假装已经无罪。第三章会再讲。

> 要点：认罪不是换取赦免的功劳，而是不再替自己辩护；神赦免我们，是信实的，也是公义的，因为基督已经替我们担当了刑罚。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我们若在光明中行，如同神在光明中，就彼此相交，他儿子耶稣的血也洗净我们一切的罪。」（1:7）我在教会里有许多朋友，小组里一起吃饭、一起祷告。可是我们彼此分享的代祷事项，多半是别人的病、老人家的身体、工作的调动，很少是自己的罪。我不敢说我对配偶的冷淡，在网上看的东西，心里对某位同工的嫉妒。我怕说出来，别人会怎么看我；我也怕这事传出去，自己在教会里抬不起头。于是我们彼此相交，却是隔着一层的相交，每个人都把最暗的那一块留在自己手里。

约翰把「在光明中行」和「彼此相交」连在一起。我们不敢彼此敞开，往往不只是因为别人不可靠，也是因为我们心里还以为，被人看见真实的自己，就会失去被接纳的地位。可是在神面前，我的一切本来就是敞开的。他的光早已照透了我，他却没有把我推开，反而差他的儿子来，用自己的血洗净我「一切的罪」。我最怕被人知道的那件事，也在「一切」里面。我在人面前不敢站在光中，是不是因为我还没有真正相信，神已经看见了全部，仍然接纳我？

这不是说要把每一件罪都在众人面前说出来。认罪首先是向神。至于向人敞开，可以先找一位可信、成熟、会保守秘密的弟兄或姊妹，或者找牧者，请他为我代祷。若我是被人伤害的一方，没有人可以强迫我在众人面前讲出自己的伤痛；若有人犯了伤害他人的罪，尤其是性侵、家暴或虐待，那就不只是私下认罪的问题；听的人也不能因为答应过保密就隐瞒，要向有关部门报告，教会更不可以为了面子把事情压下去。

在叙加的井旁，耶稣对一个正午出来打水的妇人（许多人认为她是在躲着人群），说出了她素来所行的事。她没有因为被看透而逃走，反而跑回城里，对众人说：「你们来看！有一个人将我素来所行的一切事都给我说出来了，莫非这就是基督吗？」（约4:29）被他完全看见，又被他完全接纳，这是她的见证。那城里有好些人信了耶稣，就是因为那妇人作见证说：「他将我素来所行的一切事都给我说出来了。」（约4:39）那光照透了她，却没有灭了她；那光带她回到了人群中间。

## The Shape of the Chapter

1 John 1 has only ten verses and falls into two parts.

**Prologue: the word of life made manifest** (vv. 1–4): John gives no greeting. He begins at once with "that which was from the beginning... concerning the word of life." The apostles heard him, saw him, and touched him with their hands. They proclaim what they have seen and heard so that the readers may have fellowship with them, and with the Father and his Son Jesus Christ, and so that joy may be complete.

**God is light** (vv. 5–10): The message the apostles heard from the Lord is that "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all." Then, with three "if we say" statements (vv. 6, 8, 10), John exposes three false claims, and each is followed by a true way (vv. 7, 9; 2:1):
- Claiming fellowship with God while walking in darkness; the true way is to walk in the light, and the blood of Jesus cleanses us from all sin.
- Claiming to have no sin; the true way is to confess our sins, and God will forgive.
- Claiming not to have sinned; the answer comes in 2:1–2: we have an advocate with the Father.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–4 What We Have Heard, Seen and Touched

**1. "That which was from the beginning."** "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we looked upon and have touched with our hands, concerning the word of life" (1:1). This at once recalls the opening of John's Gospel: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (John 1:1). But the stress differs. The Gospel's "in the beginning" reaches back before creation. Here, some take "from the beginning" to mean from eternity as well; others, the beginning of Jesus' ministry or of the gospel as it was proclaimed (see 2:7, 24). The two readings belong together: what was proclaimed from the beginning is the one who was with the Father from eternity (1:2).

**2. Is "the word of life" a message or a person?** The verse opens with four "that which" clauses using a neuter pronoun, as though speaking of a thing or a message. But then this life "was with the Father" and "was made manifest to us" (1:2), as though speaking of a person. Many commentators think John writes this way on purpose: the message of the gospel cannot be separated from the person. To proclaim "the word of life" is to proclaim the incarnate Jesus Christ.

**3. "Touched with our hands."** John builds up step by step: heard, seen, "looked upon," and finally "touched with our hands." Luke uses this same verb for "touch" in the risen Lord's words to his disciples: "Touch me, and see. For a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see that I have" (Luke 24:39). John is probably answering those who would not confess "that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh" (4:2–3). Eternal life is not an idea, nor a phantom that only appeared in spirit; it is the eternal Son, who was with the Father and became a real man of flesh and blood whom people could touch.
- This matters for our faith. If Christ did not truly become flesh, he did not truly die, and his blood could not "cleanse us from all sin" (1:7).
- It also reminds us that Christianity does not rest on human feelings or private spiritual experiences. It rests on things that really happened in history, and on the testimony of those who saw them.

**4. Who are "we"?** This passage keeps saying "we," and "proclaim to you." Most commentators take "we" to mean the apostles and eyewitnesses, as distinct from the readers, "you." Others take "we" to mean the church that stands together in the apostolic tradition. On the first reading, believers today have not seen the Lord, yet through the apostles' testimony they can share the same fellowship with them (1:3). Jesus told Thomas, "Have you believed because you have seen me? Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed" (John 20:29).

**5. "So that you too may have fellowship with us."** The word "fellowship" means having something in common, sharing in it. Believers' fellowship is not only meeting and eating together; it is sharing together in the same Lord. John puts two fellowships side by side: "so that you too may have fellowship with us; and indeed our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ" (1:3). Fellowship with God always leads to fellowship with God's children; cut off from fellowship with God, fellowship between people is only outward.

> The point: The gospel is not a set of ideas to work out for ourselves. It is the eternal life truly come among us. Knowing him through the apostles' testimony, we have fellowship with the Father, with the Son, and with all who believe in him.

## Going Deeper: 1:5 God Is Light, and in Him Is No Darkness at All

**1. "This is the message we have heard from him."** "This is the message we have heard from him and proclaim to you, that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" (1:5). John does not call this his own insight; it is what they "heard from him." Jesus said, "I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life" (John 8:12).

**2. What does light mean?** In Scripture "light" has at least two senses:
- **Truth and revelation:** God is light; he makes himself known, and in his light people see everything (Ps 36:9).
- **Holiness and righteousness:** God is light; he is perfectly holy, with no evil in him at all.

John stresses the second here, because all that follows is about sin. He adds, "in him is no darkness at all." The Greek uses a strong negative: not any at all. God is not mostly bright with an occasional shadow; his holiness is unmixed.

**3. What this means for us.** Some think that as long as they have spiritual knowledge, it does not matter how they live. John says that since God is light, those who know him cannot stay in darkness without caring. Knowing this God always changes how a person lives. And because God is light, nothing can be hidden from him: "even the darkness is not dark to you; the night is bright as the day" (Ps 139:12). For sinners this is frightening news, but John is about to show that this light is good news too.

> The point: God is light, perfectly holy, with no darkness at all. To know him is to stop making peace with darkness, and to stop hiding from him.

## Going Deeper: 1:6–7 Walking in the Light

**1. The first "if we say."** "If we say we have fellowship with him while we walk in darkness, we lie and do not practice the truth" (1:6). In Scripture "walk" often means a way of life. The problem here is not an occasional stumble. It is claiming fellowship with God while living on in darkness, as though the two did not conflict. John's verdict is blunt: this is a lie, and we "do not practice the truth." For John, truth is not only to be believed but to be done (see John 3:21).

**2. What is "walking in the light"?** "But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin" (1:7). Walking in the light does not mean being free of sin, for the same verse says we still need the cleansing blood. Many commentators take it to mean living before God without hiding or pretending, letting his light show us as we really are, and responding to what we see. It also means turning from what the light exposes and walking as he walked (2:6).
- Those who "walk in darkness" avoid the light, afraid of being seen (John 3:20).
- Those who "walk in the light" are not sinless; they let their sin be exposed and bring it to God.

**3. "We have fellowship with one another."** We might expect John to say "we have fellowship with God," but he says "with one another." Those who walk in the light have nothing to hide from God, and they no longer need masks before their brothers and sisters either. When fellowship in a church stays at the level of politeness and face, it is often because we do not yet dare to stand in the light together.

**4. "The blood of Jesus his Son."** In Scripture "blood" means a life laid down in death, especially in sacrifice: "For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls" (Lev 17:11). John says "the blood of Jesus his Son": the eternal life who was with the Father (1:2) truly shed his blood. The verb "cleanses" is present tense here, and many take it to describe an ongoing work: as believers walk in the light, they keep on being cleansed by this blood. "All sin" matters too: no sin is too great for this blood to cleanse.

> The point: Walking in the light is not pretending to have no sin; it is no longer hiding, and turning from what the light shows. We dare to stand in the light because the blood of Jesus keeps cleansing us from all sin.

## Going Deeper: 1:8–10 If We Confess Our Sins

**1. The second "if we say."** "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us" (1:8). Many take "have no sin" to mean denying that sin still dwells in us, or denying our guilt. People who say this do not fool God or others; they fool only themselves.
- John's opponents may have held ideas like those of later Gnostics: that the human spirit is pure and what the body does cannot defile it, so there is no sin to speak of.
- Today the words may differ but the idea is close: "I just have a few flaws," "I'm not a bad person at heart," "It was my circumstances."

**2. "If we confess our sins."** "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1:9). The word "confess" (*homologeō*) means to acknowledge or admit openly. It is built from "same" and "say," so many preachers put it this way: to say about my sin what God says, with no more excuses and without making it small. John says "confess our sins," plural; this is not only saying in general "I am a sinner," but bringing particular sins to God (see Ps 32:5).
- This confession is first of all to God. James also tells believers, "confess your sins to one another and pray for one another" (Jas 5:16).
- The Catholic Church holds that penance (reconciliation) is a sacrament instituted by Christ, in which the faithful confess their sins to a priest, who pronounces absolution. Lutheran churches kept private confession to a pastor and the word of absolution, without requiring every sin to be listed. Reformed and most evangelical churches stress that believers may come straight to God to confess, and may also, when it is fitting, confess to a trusted fellow believer or pastor and ask for prayer; a minister may declare God's promise of pardon, but confession to clergy is not required. All three agree that forgiveness is finally God's own act.

**3. "He is faithful and just."** God forgives those who confess, first because he is "faithful": he keeps his promise (see Jer 31:34). Second, because he is "just." This is remarkable. We would expect a just God to punish sin; how can he forgive because he is just? The answer comes in the next chapter: Jesus Christ "is the propitiation for our sins" (2:2). Christ has borne the penalty of sin, so when God forgives those who trust in Christ, he is acting justly. Paul says God did this "so that he might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (Rom 3:26).

**4. Why do the justified still confess?** Some ask: haven't a believer's sins already been forgiven once for all? Why ask for forgiveness again? The Reformed answer is this: before God's judgment seat the believer is already justified, and that standing never changes; but in the relationship of a father and his children, a believer who sins still loses the joy of fellowship with God and needs to come back and confess. The Westminster Confession says that "God doth continue to forgive the sins of those that are justified"; they "can never fall from the state of justification," yet they "may, by their sins, fall under God's fatherly displeasure, and not have the light of His countenance restored unto them, until they humble themselves, confess their sins, beg pardon, and renew their faith and repentance" (11.5). Jesus also taught his disciples to pray daily, "forgive us our debts" (Matt 6:12).

**5. The third "if we say."** "If we say we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us" (1:10). Verse 8 denies the sin within us; this verse denies the sins we have done. This is worse than self-deception: God's word says plainly that all have sinned (Rom 3:23), and to deny it is to say God is lying.

**6. This passage and the later verses on believers not sinning.** John says plainly here that believers who claim to have no sin deceive themselves. Later, in 3:6 and 3:9, he says that whoever abides in him and is born of God does not sin (the ESV renders it "keeps on sinning," "makes a practice of sinning"). The two must be read together: John will not let us make light of sin, nor let us pretend we are already free of it. Chapter 3 returns to this.

> The point: To confess is not to earn forgiveness; it is to stop defending ourselves. God is faithful and just to forgive us, because Christ has borne the penalty in our place.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin" (1:7). I have many friends at church; in our small group we eat and pray together. But the prayer requests we share are mostly about other people's illnesses, aging parents, job changes, and seldom about our own sins. I do not dare mention my coldness toward my spouse, what I look at online, or my envy of a fellow worker. I am afraid of what others would think of me, and afraid it would get around and I could not hold my head up at church. So we have fellowship, but through a screen; each of us keeps the darkest part in our own hands.

John ties "walking in the light" to "fellowship with one another." When we do not dare to open up to each other, it is often not only because others are untrustworthy, but because deep down we still think that if people saw us as we are, we would lose our place among them. Yet before God everything about me is already open. His light has long since shone right through me, and he has not pushed me away; he sent his Son to cleanse me with his own blood from "all sin." The thing I most fear anyone knowing is included in that "all." When I do not dare to stand in the light before people, is it because I have not really believed that God has seen it all and still accepts me?

This does not mean telling every sin to everyone. Confession is first to God. As for opening up to others, I can begin with one trustworthy, mature brother or sister who will keep a confidence, or with a pastor, and ask them to pray for me. If I am the one who was harmed, no one may force me to tell my pain in front of others. And if someone has committed a sin that harms others, especially sexual abuse, domestic violence or other abuse, it is not only a matter of private confession; a promise of confidence cannot cover it. It must be reported to the proper authorities, and the church must never suppress it to save face.

At the well in Sychar, Jesus told a woman who came for water at noon (many think she was avoiding the crowds) all that she had done. She did not run from being seen through; she ran back to town and told everyone, "Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did. Can this be the Christ?" (John 4:29). To be fully seen by him and fully received by him: that was her testimony. Many in that town believed in Jesus "because of the woman's testimony, 'He told me all that I ever did'" (John 4:39). The light shone right through her, and it did not destroy her; it led her back among her people.
