## 本章脉络

约翰一书第二章接着第一章讲「在光明中行」，可以分成五段。

**中保和挽回祭**（1-2节）：约翰写信是要叫信徒不犯罪；若有人犯罪，在父那里有一位中保，就是那义者耶稣基督，他为我们的罪作了挽回祭。

**认识他的人遵守他的命令**（3-11节）：说「我认识他」却不遵守他命令的，是说谎的；住在主里面的，就该照主所行的去行。彼此相爱是一条旧命令，也是一条新命令；恨弟兄的，仍在黑暗里。

**写给小子、父老、少年人；不要爱世界**（12-17节）：约翰向不同的读者说明他写信的理由：他们的罪已经赦免了，他们认识父，胜了那恶者。所以不要爱世界，因为世界和其上的情欲都要过去。

**敌基督的和恩膏**（18-27节）：如今是末时了，已经有好些敌基督的出来，他们从教会中出去，不认父与子。信徒却从那圣者受了恩膏，要将从起初所听见的常存在心里。

**住在主里面**（28-29节）：要住在主里面，好叫他显现的时候，我们可以坦然无惧。行公义的人，都是他所生的。

## 深读 2:1-2 中保和挽回祭

**1. 「是要叫你们不犯罪」。** 「我小子们哪，我将这些话写给你们，是要叫你们不犯罪。若有人犯罪，在父那里我们有一位中保，就是那义者耶稣基督。」（2:1）约翰在第一章说，人若说自己无罪，就是自欺（1:8）。可是他不愿意读者因此觉得犯罪是理所当然的。他写信的目的，是要信徒不犯罪。同时他也知道信徒仍会跌倒，所以紧接着说：「若有人犯罪……」既不轻看罪，也不叫跌倒的人绝望，这是约翰一贯的平衡。

**2. 「中保」。** 这个词（*paraklētos*）原意是被请来站在旁边的人，在法庭上常指替人辩护的人。约翰福音用同一个词称圣灵为「保惠师」（约14:16、26）；耶稣说，父要「另外赐给你们一位保惠师」，可见耶稣自己是第一位。在地上，圣灵与我们同在；在父面前，耶稣基督为我们说话（参罗8:34；来7:25）。
- 他是「那义者」。我们的中保不是靠我们的义替我们辩护，而是靠他自己的义。他不是去求父对罪睁一只眼闭一只眼；他站在父面前，凭的是自己已经为我们的罪付上了代价。

**3. 「挽回祭」。** 「他为我们的罪作了挽回祭。」（2:2）这个词（*hilasmos*）和同字根的词，在七十士译本里与赎罪的祭有关。学者对它的意思有过争论：英国学者多德认为它只是除去罪污的意思；莱昂·莫里斯等人则论证，这组词在一般希腊文里带有平息忿怒的意思，圣经也清楚地讲到神向罪发的忿怒（罗1:18；约3:36）。多数福音派学者认为，两层意思都在其中：基督的死除去了罪，也平息了神公义的忿怒。不过，这不是说一位慈爱的儿子去安抚一位不情愿的父，因为差儿子作挽回祭的，正是父自己的爱（4:10）。

**4. 「也是为普天下人的罪」。** 这句话怎样理解，各传统有不同的读法：
- **阿民念派和卫斯理宗（如阿民念、约翰·卫斯理）：** 基督的死是为每一个人预备的，凡信的人都可以得着；人不得救，不是因为基督没有为他死，而是因为他不肯信。
- **假设性普世救赎论（如法国的亚目拉督、英国的达文南）：** 基督的死在神的心意中是为所有人的，只要他们信；同时神又特别拣选了一些人，使他们必定信。
- **特定救赎论（又称限定救赎论，如约翰·欧文）：** 「普天下」是说不只是为犹太人或这些读者，也是为全世界各国、各族中属神的人（参约11:51-52「将神四散的子民都聚集归一」；启5:9）。加尔文在这节的注释中也这样解释。后两种读法都出自改革宗教会内部。

各方都同意几件事：基督的死足够赦免一切的罪；福音要白白地传给每一个人；没有人因为罪太大而在这挽回祭之外。约翰在4:14也说：「父差子作世人的救主。」

> 要点：和合本译作「作了」挽回祭，原文却是现在式：他现在就是我们的挽回祭。所以跌倒的人回到神面前，靠的不是自己的悔恨有多深，而是这位义者耶稣基督。

## 深读 2:3-11 认识他的人遵守他的命令

**1. 「我们若遵守他的诫命，就晓得是认识他」。** 「我们若遵守他的诫命，就晓得是认识他。人若说『我认识他』，却不遵守他的诫命，便是说谎话的，真理也不在他心里了。」（2:3-4）这是全书第一次说「我们……就晓得」。认识神不只是知道关于神的事，而是和他有关系；这关系必定显在生活中。约翰没有说遵守诫命使人得以认识神，而是说遵守诫命显明人已经认识他。顺服是认识神的果子，不是认识神的代价。

**2. 「照主所行的去行」。** 「人若说他住在主里面，就该自己照主所行的去行。」（2:6）基督首先是我们的救主（2:2），然后才是我们的榜样；被他拯救的人，就要跟着他走。耶稣在洗门徒的脚以后说：「我给你们作了榜样，叫你们照着我向你们所做的去做。」（约13:15）

**3. 旧命令，也是新命令。** 「我写给你们的，不是一条新命令，乃是你们从起初所受的旧命令……再者，我写给你们的，是一条新命令，在主是真的，在你们也是真的；因为黑暗渐渐过去，真光已经照耀。」（2:7-8）这条命令就是彼此相爱（参3:11；约13:34）。说它旧，是因为读者从信主的时候就听见了；说它新，是因为基督来了，用他的爱给这命令带来了新的标准和新的能力。「黑暗渐渐过去」：新的时代已经开始，虽然还没有完全来到。

**4. 「恨弟兄的，是在黑暗里」。** 「人若说自己在光明中，却恨他的弟兄，他到如今还是在黑暗里。」（2:9）约翰用词很直接，没有中间地带：不是爱，就是恨。他说的「恨」，不一定是激烈的仇视，也包括冷漠、不理、看不起（参3:17）。恨弟兄的结果是：「也不知道往哪里去，因为黑暗叫他眼睛瞎了。」（2:11）苦毒会叫人看不清自己，也看不清别人。

> 要点：认识神的人会走在光中，照主所行的去行，彼此相爱；这不是我们赚取认识神的方法，而是那真光已经照进我们生命的记号。

## 深读 2:12-17 你们的罪得了赦免；不要爱世界

**1. 三组读者。** 约翰两次分别对「小子们」「父老」「少年人」说话（2:12-14）。和合本和英文标准版这里分节不同：和合本2:14开头的「小子们哪，我曾写信给你们，因为你们认识父」，在英文标准版是2:13的最后一句。这三组人是谁？
- 有人认为是按年龄分的；
- 有人认为是按属灵的成熟分的：初信的、刚强的、成熟的；
- 也有人认为「小子们」是指全体信徒（约翰在全书都这样称呼读者，2:1、28），然后再分成年长的和年轻的两组。

原文从2:14起，「我写」变成了「我曾写」，和合本用「曾」字表明，英文标准版则仍译作现在式。无论怎样读，约翰写信的理由都是先讲神已经做成的事：「因为你们的罪藉着主名得了赦免。」（2:12）不是因为读者的表现，而是「藉着主名」。

**2. 「不要爱世界」。** 「不要爱世界和世界上的事。人若爱世界，爱父的心就不在他里面了。」（2:15）约翰福音说「神爱世人」（约3:16），这里却说「不要爱世界」，这不矛盾。约翰用「世界」有不同的意思：有时指神所爱的人类；这里是指与神为敌、「卧在那恶者手下」的世界，就是离开神的人所形成的价值和风气（5:19）。不要爱世界，不是说要逃离人群或轻看神所造的万物，而是不让那抵挡神的价值观占据我们的心。

**3. 三样情欲。** 「因为，凡世界上的事，就像肉体的情欲、眼目的情欲，并今生的骄傲，都不是从父来的，乃是从世界来的。」（2:16）
- **肉体的情欲：** 堕落的人性里那些违背神的欲望。
- **眼目的情欲：** 看见了就想要，想占为己有。
- **今生的骄傲：** 「今生」这个词，原文也可以指生计、财物（3:17「世上财物」的「财物」是同一个词）。就是因自己拥有什么、有什么地位而夸口，看重面子、和人比较。

许多人注意到，这三样和夏娃在伊甸园受的引诱相近：那树的果子「好作食物，也悦人的眼目，且是可喜爱的，能使人有智慧」（创3:6）。

**4. 「惟独遵行神旨意的，是永远常存」。** 「这世界和其上的情欲都要过去，惟独遵行神旨意的，是永远常存。」（2:17）世界的问题不只是它不好，也是它留不住。人把心放在会过去的东西上，最后什么都抓不住；那遵行神旨意的人，却「永远常存」，因为他和永远的神连在一起。

> 要点：我们不爱世界，不是靠自己咬紧牙关，而是因为我们的罪已经「藉着主名」得了赦免，父先爱了我们，我们心里就有了爱父的心，这爱比世界更大，也比世界更长久。

## 深读 2:18-27 敌基督的和恩膏

**1. 「如今是末时了」。** 「小子们哪，如今是末时了。你们曾听见说，那敌基督的要来；现在已经有好些敌基督的出来了。」（2:18）约翰说「如今是末时了」，有人据此说，约翰以为世界很快就要结束，结果他错了。不过新约常把基督第一次来到第二次来之间的整个时期称为「末世」（来1:2；徒2:17）。约翰的意思是：神最后的工作已经开始，我们正活在这个时期里。
- 「那敌基督的」：读者「曾听见」将来有一位敌基督的要来。许多人把他和保罗说的「那大罪人」（帖后2:3-4）连在一起。
- 「好些敌基督的」：约翰说，那敌基督的灵现在已经在许多人身上工作（参4:3）。在约翰一书里，敌基督的不一定是一个政治人物，而是任何「不认父与子」的人（2:22）。

**2. 「他们从我们中间出去」。** 「他们从我们中间出去，却不是属我们的；若是属我们的，就必仍旧与我们同在。」（2:19）改革宗传统用这节经文讲圣徒的坚忍：真正属基督的人，最后必定持守到底；离开的人显明他们本来就不属于基督。阿民念派和卫斯理宗通常认为，这节只是说这些离开的人本来就不属于基督，并没有回答真信徒会不会离弃信仰；他们认为真信徒可能离弃信仰，根据的是别的经文（如来6:4-6）。不过两个传统都同意，约翰在这里是安慰留下的人：那些人离开了，不表示真理落空了，也不表示留下的人信错了。

**3. 「谁是说谎话的呢？」** 「谁是说谎话的呢？不是那不认耶稣为基督的吗？不认父与子的，这就是敌基督的。凡不认子的，就没有父；认子的，连父也有了。」（2:22-23）约翰把认识子和认识父连在一起。人不能一面说自己认识神，一面否认耶稣是基督。今天也有人说：「耶稣是一位好老师，和孔子、释迦牟尼一样，各宗教殊途同归。」约翰会说：不认子的，就没有父。

**4. 「从那圣者受了恩膏」。** 「你们从那圣者受了恩膏，并且知道这一切的事。」（2:20）
- 「那圣者」多数人认为是指基督（参2:27-28）。「恩膏」这个词（*chrisma*）和「基督」（*Christos*，受膏者）是同一个字根。那些人是敌基督的，信徒却有从基督来的恩膏。
- 多数释经者认为，这恩膏是指圣灵（参林后1:21-22；约14:26）；也有人认为是指信徒所领受的神的道。其实圣灵和道分不开：圣灵藉着从起初所听见的道教导信徒（2:24）。
- 「知道这一切的事」：这里的抄本有出入。和合本跟随的读法是知道一切的事；几份最早的抄本（西乃抄本、梵蒂冈抄本）写的是你们众人都有知识，英文标准版就是这样译的。若是后一种读法，约翰可能是在回应那些认为只有少数人才有属灵知识的人：每一个信徒都有恩膏，都有知识。

**5. 「并不用人教训你们」。** 「你们从主所受的恩膏常存在你们心里，并不用人教训你们，自有主的恩膏在凡事上教训你们。」（2:27）这不是说信徒不需要任何教师，因为约翰自己正在教导他们，基督也把「牧师和教师」赐给教会（弗4:11）。约翰是说：你们不需要那些带着新道理来的人，好像没有他们，你们就不认识真理。信徒要「将那从起初所听见的，常存在心里」（2:24）。

> 要点：在许多声音中，信徒不必惊慌，因为每一个信徒都从基督领受了圣灵的恩膏；要住在从起初所听见的道里，也就是住在子和父里面。

## 深读 2:28-29 住在主里面

**1. 「他若显现，我们就可以坦然无惧」。** 「小子们哪，你们要住在主里面。这样，他若显现，我们就可以坦然无惧；当他来的时候，在他面前也不至于惭愧。」（2:28）「坦然无惧」这个词，原意是可以放胆说话。约翰在全书四次用这个词：在主来的时候（2:28），在审判的日子（4:17），在祷告的时候（3:21；5:14）。信徒可以在主面前抬起头来，不是因为自己完全，而是因为住在他里面。

**2. 「凡行公义之人都是他所生的」。** 「你们若知道他是公义的，就知道凡行公义之人都是他所生的。」（2:29）这是约翰一书第一次讲「从神生」。孩子像父亲；从公义的神生的人，生命里也会显出公义。约翰没有说人行公义就成为神的儿女，而是说行公义显明他「是他所生的」。第三章会进一步展开。

> 要点：我们能在主显现时坦然无惧，不在于我们有多完全，而在于我们住在那位义者里面。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们从主所受的恩膏常存在你们心里，并不用人教训你们，自有主的恩膏在凡事上教训你们。」（2:27）我听讲道，多半是在网上听几位有名的传道人。他们讲得清楚、有力，我很受益。可是不知不觉，我自己翻开圣经的时候越来越少了；遇到不懂的经文，第一个反应是去找哪位老师讲过，而不是自己去读、去求问主。慢慢地，我习惯了听别人把经文嚼碎了再喂给我。这样很省力，也很安全：别人讲出来的道理，我可以点头，也可以放下；可是我自己安静读经的时候，那话会直接对着我说。我信主多年，心里却一直觉得，自己不配直接到神的话面前来。

约翰的收信人，正是一群面对各种新道理的普通信徒。他没有说：「去找一位更有名的老师。」他说：「你们从那圣者受了恩膏。」（2:20）这恩膏不是另外加给某些人的，而是每一个信基督的人都已经有了。约翰也不是叫他们关起门来，各人凭感觉解经；他叫他们回到「从起初所听见的」（2:24），在教会中一同持守。神赐下教师，是要帮助我们自己认识主，不是代替我们认识主；目标是「直等到我们众人在真道上同归于一，认识神的儿子」（弗4:13）。约翰给的试金石也很简单：所讲的是不是那「从起初所听见的」（2:24）？是不是承认耶稣是基督，是成了肉身来的（2:22；4:2）？这试金石不需要神学学位，每一个认识子的人都拿得起。我是不是因为怕自己读不懂，或者觉得自己不够格，就不敢自己到神的话面前来？还是我其实更愿意听人讲，因为那样神的话就碰不到我？

这恩膏是从谁来的？约翰说，是从「那圣者」，从「主」来的。「基督」的意思就是受膏者。他在拿撒勒的会堂里，打开以赛亚书，念道：「主的灵在我身上，因为他用膏膏我，叫我传福音给贫穷的人；差遣我报告：被掳的得释放，瞎眼的得看见。」（路4:18）那位受膏的主，把自己所受的那灵浇灌给他的百姓（参徒2:33）。我不必无助地站在一群专家和一本难懂的书中间；那位开瞎子眼睛的主，也用他的灵亲自教导我。他打开我的眼睛，不是要我成为懂很多的人，而是要我看见他自己。那位受膏者，本来就是为贫穷的人来的。

## The Shape of the Chapter

1 John 2 carries on from chapter 1's "walking in the light" and falls into five parts.

**Advocate and propitiation** (vv. 1–2): John writes so that believers may not sin; but if anyone does sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous, who is the propitiation for our sins.

**Those who know him keep his commandments** (vv. 3–11): whoever says "I know him" but does not keep his commandments is a liar; whoever abides in him ought to walk as he walked. Loving one another is an old commandment and also a new one; whoever hates his brother is still in darkness.

**To little children, fathers and young men; do not love the world** (vv. 12–17): John tells his different readers why he writes: their sins are forgiven, they know the Father, they have overcome the evil one. So do not love the world, for the world is passing away along with its desires.

**Antichrists and the anointing** (vv. 18–27): it is the last hour, and many antichrists have come; they went out from the church and deny the Father and the Son. But believers have been anointed by the Holy One, and are to let what they heard from the beginning abide in them.

**Abide in him** (vv. 28–29): abide in him, so that when he appears we may have confidence. Everyone who practices righteousness has been born of him.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–2 Advocate and Propitiation

**1. "So that you may not sin."** "My little children, I am writing these things to you so that you may not sin. But if anyone does sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" (2:1). In chapter 1 John said that whoever claims to have no sin deceives himself (1:8). But he does not want readers to conclude that sinning is to be expected. He writes so that believers will not sin. At the same time he knows they will still fall, so he goes straight on: "But if anyone does sin..." Not making light of sin, and not leaving the fallen in despair: that is John's constant balance.

**2. "Advocate."** This word (*paraklētos*) means someone called to stand alongside, and in a court it often meant a defender. John's Gospel uses the same word for the Holy Spirit, the "Helper" (John 14:16, 26). Jesus said the Father would give "another Helper," so Jesus himself is the first. On earth the Spirit is with us; before the Father, Jesus Christ speaks for us (see Rom 8:34; Heb 7:25).
- He is "the righteous." Our advocate defends us not on the strength of our righteousness but of his own. He does not ask the Father to look the other way; he stands before the Father as the very one who has paid for our sins.

**3. "Propitiation."** "He is the propitiation for our sins" (2:2). In the Septuagint this word (*hilasmos*) and its relatives are linked with atoning sacrifice. Scholars have debated its meaning. The British scholar C. H. Dodd argued that it means only "expiation," the removing of sin's defilement. Leon Morris and others argued that in ordinary Greek the word group carries the sense of turning away wrath, and Scripture speaks plainly of God's wrath against sin (Rom 1:18; John 3:36). Most evangelical scholars hold that both senses are present: Christ's death removes sin and also turns away God's just wrath. But this is not a loving Son placating an unwilling Father, for it was the Father's own love that sent the Son to be the propitiation (4:10).

**4. "Also for the sins of the whole world."** Different traditions read this phrase differently:
- **Arminian and Wesleyan (e.g. Arminius, John Wesley):** Christ's death was provided for every person, and all who believe may receive it; people are lost not because Christ did not die for them but because they will not believe.
- **Hypothetical universalism (Amyraut; in England, Davenant and others):** in God's intention Christ's death was for all people on condition that they believe; at the same time God chose some in particular so that they would surely believe.
- **Particular redemption, or definite atonement (e.g. John Owen):** "the whole world" means not only Jews or these readers, but God's people from every nation and people in the world (see John 11:51–52, "to gather into one the children of God who are scattered abroad"; Rev 5:9). Calvin's commentary on this verse reads it the same way. Both of these last two views arose within the Reformed churches.

All agree on several things: Christ's death is sufficient to forgive every sin; the gospel is to be offered freely to everyone; no one is shut out of this propitiation because his sin is too great. John says in 4:14, "the Father has sent his Son to be the Savior of the world."

> The point: John says Christ *is* the propitiation; he himself, in his person, is our atoning sacrifice. So when we fall and come back to God, we rely not on how deep our remorse is, but on him.

## Going Deeper: 2:3–11 Those Who Know Him Keep His Commandments

**1. "By this we know that we have come to know him."** "And by this we know that we have come to know him, if we keep his commandments. Whoever says 'I know him' but does not keep his commandments is a liar, and the truth is not in him" (2:3–4). This is the first of the letter's "by this we know" statements. Knowing God is not only knowing facts about him; it is a relationship with him, and that relationship always shows in how we live. John does not say that keeping the commandments makes us know God, but that keeping them shows we already know him. Obedience is the fruit of knowing God, not the price of it.

**2. "Walk in the same way in which he walked."** "Whoever says he abides in him ought to walk in the same way in which he walked" (2:6). Christ is not only our example; he is first our Savior (2:2). But those he saves follow him. After washing the disciples' feet, Jesus said, "For I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you" (John 13:15).

**3. An old commandment and a new one.** "Beloved, I am writing you no new commandment, but an old commandment that you had from the beginning... At the same time, it is a new commandment that I am writing to you, which is true in him and in you, because the darkness is passing away and the true light is already shining" (2:7–8). The commandment is to love one another (see 3:11; John 13:34). It is old because the readers heard it from the time they first believed. It is new because Christ came, and his love gave the command a new standard and a new power. "The darkness is passing away": the new age has begun, though it has not yet fully come.

**4. "Whoever hates his brother is in the darkness."** "Whoever says he is in the light and hates his brother is still in darkness" (2:9). John speaks starkly, with no middle ground: either love or hate. The "hate" he means need not be fierce hostility; it includes coldness, ignoring, contempt (see 3:17). The result of hating a brother is that he "does not know where he is going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes" (2:11). Bitterness keeps us from seeing ourselves or others clearly.

> The point: Those who know God walk in the light, walk as the Lord walked, and love one another. This is not how we earn knowing God; it is the sign that the true light has already shone into our lives.

## Going Deeper: 2:12–17 Your Sins Are Forgiven; Do Not Love the World

**1. Three groups of readers.** Twice John addresses "little children," "fathers" and "young men" (2:12–14). The Chinese Union Version and the ESV divide the verses differently here: the CUV begins 2:14 with "I write to you, children, because you know the Father," which is the last sentence of 2:13 in the ESV. Who are these three groups?
- Some take them as age groups.
- Some take them as stages of spiritual maturity: new believers, strong believers, mature believers.
- Others take "little children" as all the believers (as John calls his readers throughout, 2:1, 28), then divided into older and younger.

From CUV 2:14 (ESV 2:13c) the Greek shifts from "I write" to "I wrote"; the CUV marks this, while the ESV keeps "I write." On any reading, John's reason for writing begins with what God has already done: "because your sins are forgiven for his name's sake" (2:12). Not because of the readers' performance, but "for his name's sake."

**2. "Do not love the world."** "Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him" (2:15). John's Gospel says "God so loved the world" (John 3:16), and here John says "do not love the world." There is no contradiction. John uses "world" in different senses: sometimes the human race God loves; here the world-system that opposes God and "lies in the power of the evil one" (5:19). Not loving the world does not mean fleeing people or despising what God has made. It means not letting the values that oppose God take hold of our hearts.

**3. Three desires.** "For all that is in the world—the desires of the flesh and the desires of the eyes and pride in possessions—is not from the Father but is from the world" (2:16).
- **The desires of the flesh:** the desires of fallen human nature that go against God.
- **The desires of the eyes:** seeing and wanting, wanting to possess.
- **Pride in possessions:** the word translated "possessions" (*bios*) can mean livelihood or goods (the same word in 3:17 is "the world's goods"). It means boasting in what one has and in one's standing, caring about face and comparing ourselves with others.

Many have noticed how closely these three match Eve's temptation in Eden: the tree "was good for food, and... a delight to the eyes, and... to be desired to make one wise" (Gen 3:6).

**4. "Whoever does the will of God abides forever."** "And the world is passing away along with its desires, but whoever does the will of God abides forever" (2:17). The world's problem is not only that it is bad, but that it will not last. Those who set their hearts on what passes away are left holding nothing. Those who do God's will "abide forever," because they are joined to the eternal God.

> The point: We do not stop loving the world by gritting our teeth. We stop because our sins have been forgiven "for his name's sake," the Father has loved us first, and the love for the Father he has put in us is greater than the world and outlasts it.

## Going Deeper: 2:18–27 Antichrists and the Anointing

**1. "It is the last hour."** "Children, it is the last hour, and as you have heard that antichrist is coming, so now many antichrists have come" (2:18). Some think John expected the world to end very soon and was mistaken. But the New Testament often calls the whole period between Christ's first and second comings "the last days" (Heb 1:2; Acts 2:17). John means that God's final work has begun and we are living in it.
- "Antichrist": the readers "have heard" that an antichrist is coming. Many link him with Paul's "man of lawlessness" (2 Thess 2:3–4).
- "Many antichrists": John says the spirit of antichrist is already at work in many people (see 4:3). In 1 John, an antichrist need not be a political figure; it is anyone who "denies the Father and the Son" (2:22).

**2. "They went out from us."** "They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would have continued with us" (2:19). The Reformed tradition reads this verse in terms of the perseverance of the saints: those who truly belong to Christ will hold on to the end, and those who leave show they never belonged to him. Arminian and Wesleyan readers usually take the verse to describe these particular people, who never truly belonged, without settling whether a true believer can fall away; they draw that possibility from other passages (e.g. Heb 6:4–6). But both traditions agree that John is comforting those who stayed: the others' leaving does not mean the truth has failed, or that those who remained believed wrongly.

**3. "Who is the liar?"** "Who is the liar but he who denies that Jesus is the Christ? This is the antichrist, he who denies the Father and the Son. No one who denies the Son has the Father. Whoever confesses the Son has the Father also" (2:22–23). John ties knowing the Son to knowing the Father. No one can claim to know God while denying that Jesus is the Christ. Some today say, "Jesus was a good teacher, like Confucius or the Buddha; all religions lead to the same place." John would answer: whoever denies the Son does not have the Father.

**4. "Anointed by the Holy One."** "But you have been anointed by the Holy One, and you all have knowledge" (2:20).
- Most take "the Holy One" to be Christ (see 2:27–28). The word "anointing" (*chrisma*) shares its root with "Christ" (*Christos*, the Anointed One). Those men are "antichrists"; believers have an anointing that comes from Christ.
- Most commentators take the anointing to be the Holy Spirit (see 2 Cor 1:21–22; John 14:26); some take it to be the word of God the believers received. The two cannot really be separated: the Spirit teaches believers through the word they heard from the beginning (2:24).
- "You all have knowledge": the manuscripts differ here. The Chinese Union Version follows the reading "you know all things"; several of the earliest manuscripts (Sinaiticus, Vaticanus) read "you all have knowledge," which the ESV translates. On that reading, John may be answering those who claimed that only a few had spiritual knowledge: every believer has the anointing, every believer has knowledge.

**5. "You have no need that anyone should teach you."** "But the anointing that you received from him abides in you, and you have no need that anyone should teach you. But as his anointing teaches you about everything... abide in him" (2:27). This does not mean believers need no teachers at all, for John himself is teaching them, and Christ gave the church "pastors and teachers" (Eph 4:11). John means: you do not need those who arrive with new doctrines, as though without them you would not know the truth. Believers are to "let what you heard from the beginning abide in you" (2:24).

> The point: Among many voices believers need not panic, for every believer has received the Spirit's anointing from Christ. Abide in the word heard from the beginning, and so abide in the Son and in the Father.

## Going Deeper: 2:28–29 Abide in Him

**1. "When he appears we may have confidence."** "And now, little children, abide in him, so that when he appears we may have confidence and not shrink from him in shame at his coming" (2:28). The word "confidence" originally meant freedom to speak boldly. John uses it four times: at the Lord's coming (2:28), on the day of judgment (4:17), and in prayer (3:21; 5:14). Believers can lift up their heads before the Lord, not because they are perfect, but because they abide in him.

**2. "Everyone who practices righteousness has been born of him."** "If you know that he is righteous, you may be sure that everyone who practices righteousness has been born of him" (2:29). This is the letter's first mention of being born of God. Children resemble their father; those born of the righteous God will show righteousness in their lives. John does not say that practicing righteousness makes people God's children, but that it shows they "have been born of him." Chapter 3 develops this.

> The point: Our confidence when the Lord appears does not depend on how perfect we are, but on abiding in the Righteous One.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But the anointing that you received from him abides in you, and you have no need that anyone should teach you. But as his anointing teaches you about everything—and is true and is no lie, just as it has taught you—abide in him" (2:27). Most of the preaching I hear is online, from a few well-known preachers. They are clear and powerful, and I have gained a great deal. But without noticing, I open my own Bible less and less. When I meet a passage I do not understand, my first thought is to look for a teacher who has preached on it, not to read it myself and ask the Lord. Slowly I have grown used to having Scripture chewed for me first. It is easier, and safer: a point someone else makes I can agree with and set aside, but when I sit quietly with the text myself, the word speaks straight to me. I have believed for years, yet I still feel I have no right to come to God's word for myself.

John was writing to ordinary believers facing all kinds of new teaching. He did not tell them, "Go and find a more famous teacher." He said, "you have been anointed by the Holy One" (2:20). This anointing is not an extra given to some; everyone who believes in Christ already has it. Nor does John tell them to shut themselves away and each interpret Scripture by feel. He sends them back to "what you heard from the beginning" (2:24), held together in the church. The teachers God gives are there to help us know the Lord ourselves, "until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God" (Eph 4:13), not to know him in our place. John's touchstone is simple: is this what we "heard from the beginning" (2:24)? Does it confess that Jesus is the Christ, come in the flesh (2:22; 4:2)? This touchstone needs no degree; anyone who knows the Son can hold it. Have I stayed away from God's word myself because I am afraid I will not understand it, or feel I am not qualified? Or do I prefer hearing it explained, because then it never quite reaches me?

From whom does this anointing come? John says from "the Holy One," from "him." "Christ" means the Anointed One. In the synagogue at Nazareth he opened Isaiah and read: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim liberty to the captives and recovering of sight to the blind" (Luke 4:18). The Anointed Lord has given his people the Spirit with whom he himself was anointed (see Acts 2:33). I am not standing helpless between a crowd of experts and a book too hard for me; the Lord who opened blind eyes teaches me himself by his Spirit. He opens my eyes not to make me someone who knows a lot, but so that I may see him. The Anointed One came for the poor to begin with.
