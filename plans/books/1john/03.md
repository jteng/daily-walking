## 本章脉络

约翰一书第三章从2:29「凡行公义之人都是他所生的」接下来，讲神的儿女。可以分成四段。

**我们是神的儿女**（1-3节）：父赐给我们何等的慈爱，使我们得称为神的儿女，我们也真是他的儿女。将来主显现，我们必要像他。有这指望的人，就洁净自己。

**神的儿女不再活在罪中**（4-10节）：罪就是违背律法；主显现是要除掉人的罪，除灭魔鬼的作为。从神生的，就不犯罪；从行义不行义、爱弟兄不爱弟兄，就显出谁是神的儿女，谁是魔鬼的儿女。

**不可像该隐，要在行为上相爱**（11-18节）：该隐恨他的兄弟，杀了他；世人恨信徒，也不要希奇。主为我们舍命，我们从此知道何为爱；有世上财物的，看见弟兄穷乏，不可塞住怜恤的心。

**我们的心在神面前可以安稳**（19-24节）：我们的心若责备我们，神比我们的心大。我们若照他的命令信他儿子的名，又彼此相爱，就住在他里面；他所赐的圣灵叫我们知道他住在我们里面。

## 深读 3:1-3 我们是神的儿女

**1. 「你看父赐给我们是何等的慈爱」。** 「你看父赐给我们是何等的慈爱，使我们得称为神的儿女；我们也真是他的儿女。」（3:1）「何等的」这个词，原意带有惊奇：这是从哪里来的爱？是什么样的爱？约翰叫读者停下来看。我们成为神的儿女，不是因为我们找到了神，而是因为父把这爱「赐给」了我们。约翰福音说：「凡接待他的，就是信他名的人，他就赐他们权柄作神的儿女。」（约1:12）
- 「我们也真是他的儿女」：这不只是一个称呼：神不但称我们为儿女，我们也真是他的儿女。
- 「世人所以不认识我们，是因未曾认识他」：世人不明白信徒，并不奇怪，因为他们也不认识信徒的父。

**2. 「主若显现，我们必要像他」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，我们现在是神的儿女，将来如何，还未显明；但我们知道，主若显现，我们必要像他，因为必得见他的真体。」（3:2）约翰说了「现在」，也说了「将来」：
- **现在：** 我们已经是神的儿女，这身份已经确定了。
- **将来：** 我们会变成什么样子，现在还看不出来。可是有一件事是确定的：「我们必要像他」。保罗也说：「我们不是都要睡觉，乃是都要改变。」（林前15:51-52）
- 「显现」的主语原文没有写明，和合本加了「主」；按2:28，多数人认为是指基督再来。
- 「因为必得见他的真体」：这句话可以有两种理解。一种是：我们看见他，就被改变成像他。另一种是：我们必须先被改变，才能看见他的真体。两种理解都指向同一个盼望：有一天，我们要面对面看见基督。

**3. 「就洁净自己」。** 「凡向他有这指望的，就洁净自己，像他洁净一样。」（3:3）盼望不是让人坐着等候，而是改变人现在的生活。一个知道自己将要见主面的人，今天就会为那一天预备自己。不过约翰的次序很清楚：先是父的爱（3:1），然后是确定的盼望（3:2），最后才是洁净自己（3:3）。我们洁净自己，不是为了成为神的儿女，而是因为我们已经是了。

> 要点：我们成为神的儿女，全是父所赐的爱；将来要像基督的盼望，叫我们今天就为见他的那一天预备自己。

## 深读 3:4-10 从神生的不再活在罪中

**1. 「违背律法就是罪」。** 「凡犯罪的，就是违背律法；违背律法就是罪。」（3:4）「违背律法」这个词（*anomia*），也可以译作不法。有些释经者认为，约翰用这个词，是因为在末世的教训里，「不法」是指抵挡神的反叛（参帖后2:7-8「不法的隐意」「这不法的人」；约翰也说「如今是末时了」，2:18）。若是这样，约翰是说：罪不只是缺点或软弱，而是站到抵挡神的那一边去。

**2. 主显现的目的。** 约翰两次讲主为什么显现：
- 「你们知道主曾显现，是要除掉人的罪，在他并没有罪。」（3:5）这让人想起施洗约翰的话：「看哪，神的羔羊，除去世人罪孽的！」（约1:29）那「没有罪」的一位能除掉我们的罪，是因他为我们的罪作了挽回祭（2:2；4:10）。
- 「神的儿子显现出来，为要除灭魔鬼的作为。」（3:8）耶稣说过，没有人能进壮士家里抢夺他的家具，「必先捆住那壮士」（可3:27）。基督来，是为了战胜魔鬼、救出被掳的人。

所以信徒不能和罪和平共处：基督来，正是要除掉罪。若我们说自己属基督，却继续满不在乎地留在罪中，就是和基督来的目的背道而驰。

**3. 「凡从神生的，就不犯罪」。** 「凡从神生的，就不犯罪，因神的道存在他心里；他也不能犯罪，因为他是由神生的。」（3:9）这句话怎样和1:8「我们若说自己无罪，便是自欺」怎样并存？常见的读法有几种：
- **习惯性犯罪：** 动词是现在式，指持续地、以罪为常地活着。不过单凭时态不能定案（5:16说弟兄「犯了」罪，用的也是现在式），所以持这看法的人也看重上下文。从神生的人仍会跌倒，但不会安然活在罪中（例如斯托得；英文标准版就是这样翻译的）。
- **特定的罪：** 约翰说的「罪」，是3:4那种抵挡神的不法，或5:16「至于死的罪」，有人认为就是否认基督、离弃真道的罪。奥古斯丁认为，这罪是指违背爱，就是恨弟兄（参3:10-15）。
- **新生命的本性：** 约翰是在讲从神生的新生命本身。就这新生命来说，它不会犯罪；信徒仍会犯罪，是因为旧的本性还在（路德等人的读法）。
- **卫斯理宗：** 约翰·卫斯理认为，这是每一个从神生的人都有的特权：只要住在信心和爱里，就不会再明知故犯地违背神的律法。他也从这节经文发展出基督徒的完全（完全成圣）的教导：神在今生就能使人的心充满完全的爱。但卫斯理也承认，信徒仍有因无知和软弱而有的过失，仍需要基督的血。

几种读法各有理由，但都同意：约翰并不是和1:8、2:1矛盾，而是说从神生的人，生命的方向已经改变了。

**4. 「神的道存在他心里」。** 和合本这里译作「神的道」，原文是「种子」（*sperma*）。这「种子」指什么，有不同的看法：有人认为是指神的道（参雅1:18；彼前1:23），和合本就是这样译的；有人认为是指圣灵；也有人认为是指神所赐的新生命。无论怎样，约翰的意思是：神亲自在我们里面种下了新生命，这生命会长大，会结果子。我们能胜过罪，靠的不是自己的意志有多坚强，而是神在我们里面的工作。

**5. 「从此就显出」。** 「从此就显出谁是神的儿女，谁是魔鬼的儿女。凡不行义的就不属神，不爱弟兄的也是如此。」（3:10）约翰又回到两个试验：行义和爱弟兄。孩子会像父亲；我们的生活显出我们是谁的儿女。

> 要点：基督来是要除掉罪、除灭魔鬼的作为；从神生的人不能再安然活在罪中，因为神已经把新生命种在他里面。

## 深读 3:11-18 不可像该隐，要在行为上相爱

**1. 「不可像该隐」。** 「不可像该隐；他是属那恶者，杀了他的兄弟。为什么杀了他呢？因自己的行为是恶的，兄弟的行为是善的。」（3:12）这是约翰一书唯一直接提到的旧约人物。该隐恨亚伯，不是因为亚伯害了他，而是因为亚伯的善照出了他的恶（参创4:4-7；来11:4）。嫉妒常常是这样：别人的好，叫我们看见自己的不好，我们不肯悔改，反倒恨那个人。

**2. 「世人若恨你们，不要以为希奇」。** 「弟兄们，世人若恨你们，不要以为希奇。」（3:13）信徒被人误解、排斥，甚至被恨，并不奇怪，因为该隐的路一直延续到今天。耶稣也说过：「世人若恨你们，你们知道，恨你们以先已经恨我了。」（约15:18）

**3. 「凡恨他弟兄的，就是杀人的」。** 「我们因为爱弟兄，就晓得是已经出死入生了。没有爱心的，仍住在死中。凡恨他弟兄的，就是杀人的。」（3:14-15）这和耶稣在登山宝训中说的一样：「凡向弟兄动怒的，难免受审判。」（太5:22）神看的不只是手做的，也是心里的。我们也许从来没有伤害过人的身体，却在心里一次又一次地把某个人「除掉」了：不理他，盼他失败，当他不存在。

**4. 「主为我们舍命，我们从此就知道何为爱」。** 「主为我们舍命，我们从此就知道何为爱；我们也当为弟兄舍命。」（3:16）该隐夺去兄弟的命，基督却为弟兄舍去自己的命。这是全书对爱最清楚的定义之一：爱不是先从我们的感受开始，而是从十字架开始。这里的「舍命」，是自愿地把命放下，和约翰福音里好牧人「为羊舍命」是同一个说法（约10:11、15）。

**5. 「凡有世上财物的」。** 「凡有世上财物的，看见弟兄穷乏，却塞住怜恤的心，爱神的心怎能存在他里面呢？」（3:17）约翰从最大的（舍命）讲到最日常的（财物）。不是每个人都有机会为弟兄死，但每个人都常有机会为弟兄付出。「怜恤的心」原文是指人的心肠，就是最深的情感；「塞住」原文就是平常说「关上」的那个字，福音书用它来说关门（太6:6；25:10）。
- 「小子们哪，我们相爱，不要只在言语和舌头上，总要在行为和诚实上。」（3:18）
- 这不是说要毫无分辨地给。帮助人需要智慧（参帖后3:10），有时最好的帮助不是给钱。但智慧是叫我们爱得更好，不是给关上心门找借口。

> 要点：该隐夺去兄弟的命，基督为我们舍了自己的命；我们从十字架认识爱，也就在具体的付出中彼此相爱。

## 深读 3:19-24 我们的心在神面前可以安稳

**1. 「神比我们的心大」。** 「从此就知道我们是属真理的，并且我们的心在神面前可以安稳。我们的心若责备我们，神比我们的心大，一切事没有不知道的。」（3:19-20）「从此」是指上一节在行为和诚实上的爱。下一句话有两种读法：
- **安慰的读法：** 我们的心常常控告自己，但神比我们的心大；他知道一切，也知道我们对他的爱和信靠是真的，他的怜悯比我们的自责更大（多数现代释经者，例如斯托得）。
- **警告的读法：** 若连我们的心都责备我们，那么知道一切的神更要责备我们（加尔文等人这样读）。

从上下文看，前一种读法更合乎约翰的意思，因为他说的是「我们的心在神面前可以安稳」。不过两种读法都提醒我们：最后的判断不在于我们自己的感觉，而在于那位知道一切的神，他已经因基督的血赦免了我们（1:7；2:1-2）。

**2. 「我们一切所求的，就从他得着」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，我们的心若不责备我们，就可以向神坦然无惧了。并且我们一切所求的，就从他得着；因为我们遵守他的命令，行他所喜悦的事。」（3:21-22）这不是说我们拿顺服去换神的应允，像民间拜神许愿还愿那样；而是说：活在神旨意中的儿女，所求的也会照着父的心意（参5:14）。

**3. 神的命令：信和爱。** 「神的命令就是叫我们信他儿子耶稣基督的名，且照他所赐给我们的命令彼此相爱。」（3:23）约翰把一切命令归结为一条，其中有两面：信和爱。这是本书第一次用「信」这个动词。信是根，爱是果子；两者不能分开。

**4. 「因他所赐给我们的圣灵」。** 「遵守神命令的，就住在神里面，神也住在他里面。我们所以知道神住在我们里面是因他所赐给我们的圣灵。」（3:24）我们怎么知道神住在我们里面？不只是靠我们看自己的行为，也是因为神赐下了圣灵。这圣灵引导我们承认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的（4:2），也在我们心里作见证（5:6、10）。

> 要点：我们的心常常控告自己，但神比我们的心大；他赐下圣灵，叫我们信他的儿子、彼此相爱，也叫我们知道他住在我们里面。

## 默想

**默想：** 「凡有世上财物的，看见弟兄穷乏，却塞住怜恤的心，爱神的心怎能存在他里面呢？」（3:17）我每天都看见太多的需要：新闻里的灾难，亲友托人来求助，教会代祷单上的病人和失业的家庭，路边伸手的人。一开始我会难过，也会帮一点。可是需要太多了，我帮不过来，也怕被骗，渐渐地，我学会了把心关上。看见了，我就转过脸去；听见了，我就说「我会为你祷告」，然后忘记。我不觉得自己冷酷，只是累了，好像要保护自己，就得先把那扇门关上。

约翰说的是「塞住」，像关门一样。他没有说帮不过来的人没有爱，他说的是「塞住怜恤的心」。我不能救所有的人，这是真的；我也需要智慧，不能盲目地给；被人利用、勒索，或身处危险，就不必继续给，这也不是硬心。可是我是不是用「帮不过来」和「要有智慧」，替自己关上的那扇门找理由？约翰要的不是我一个人去满足所有的需要，而是在神放在我面前的那一位弟兄、那一位姊妹身上，「在行为和诚实上」去爱（3:18）。若是我用这些话替自己找借口，我需要的不只是休息，更是悔改。若我是真的累垮了，也不必再添一层自责：「我们的心若责备我们，神比我们的心大」（3:20）。主也对疲乏的门徒说：「你们来，同我暗暗地到旷野地方去歇一歇。」（可6:31）歇息不是关上心门；关上心门，是连那一个人也不愿再看见。

我们的心会累。主在世上也累过，他的心却从来没有关上。他「看见许多的人，就怜悯他们；因为他们困苦流离，如同羊没有牧人一般」（太9:36）。拿因城的寡妇送她独生的儿子出殡，「主看见那寡妇，就怜悯她，对她说：『不要哭！』」（路7:13）他和我一样「看见」了，却没有转过脸去。这两处译作「怜悯」的词，和约翰在这里说的「怜恤的心」是同一个字根。我心里关上门的时候，也正是我最需要他怜悯的时候。他没有因为我的冷淡而关上他的心；他看见我这个困苦流离、心肠变硬的人，也怜悯了我。

「主为我们舍命，我们从此就知道何为爱。」（3:16）十字架上，他的心为我敞开，从来没有关上。他为我把命舍了，我的心也就可以在他的爱里重新打开，哪怕只是为眼前的那一个人。

## The Shape of the Chapter

1 John 3 picks up from 2:29, "everyone who practices righteousness has been born of him," and speaks of God's children. It falls into four parts.

**We are God's children** (vv. 1–3): see what kind of love the Father has given us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are. When he appears we shall be like him. Everyone who has this hope purifies himself.

**God's children no longer live in sin** (vv. 4–10): sin is lawlessness; he appeared to take away sins and to destroy the works of the devil. No one born of God makes a practice of sinning; by practicing righteousness or not, and loving the brother or not, it is evident who are the children of God and who are the children of the devil.

**Not like Cain; love in deed** (vv. 11–18): Cain hated his brother and murdered him; do not be surprised if the world hates you. By this we know love, that he laid down his life for us. Whoever has the world's goods and sees his brother in need must not close his heart against him.

**Reassuring our heart before God** (vv. 19–24): when our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart. If we believe in the name of his Son and love one another, as he commanded, we abide in him; and the Spirit he has given us lets us know that he abides in us.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–3 We Are God's Children

**1. "See what kind of love the Father has given to us."** "See what kind of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are" (3:1). The phrase "what kind of" carries a note of wonder: where does such love come from? What sort of love is it? John tells his readers to stop and look. We become God's children not because we found God, but because the Father "has given" it. John's Gospel says, "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God" (John 1:12).
- "And so we are": this is more than a name. God does not only call us children; we really are his children.
- "The reason why the world does not know us is that it did not know him": it is no wonder the world does not understand believers, for it does not know their Father either.

**2. "When he appears we will be like him."** "Beloved, we are God's children now, and what we will be has not yet appeared; but we know that when he appears we will be like him, because we shall see him as he is" (3:2). John speaks of "now" and of the future:
- **Now:** we are already God's children; this standing is settled.
- **Later:** what we will become cannot yet be seen. But one thing is certain: "we will be like him." Paul says too, "we shall all be changed" (1 Cor 15:51–52).
- The Greek does not name who "appears"; in light of 2:28 most take it as Christ's coming.
- "Because we shall see him as he is": this can be read two ways. Either seeing him transforms us into his likeness, or we must first be transformed in order to see him as he is. Both readings point to the same hope: one day we will see Christ face to face.

**3. "Purifies himself."** "And everyone who thus hopes in him purifies himself as he is pure" (3:3). Hope does not make people sit and wait; it changes how they live now. Someone who knows he is going to see the Lord begins to live in the light of that day. But John's order is clear: first the Father's love (3:1), then the sure hope (3:2), and only then purifying ourselves (3:3). We purify ourselves not in order to become God's children, but because we already are.

> The point: That we are God's children is wholly the Father's gift of love. The hope of being like Christ makes us begin, today, to live in the light of that day.

## Going Deeper: 3:4–10 Those Born of God No Longer Live in Sin

**1. "Sin is lawlessness."** "Everyone who makes a practice of sinning also practices lawlessness; sin is lawlessness" (3:4). Some commentators think John chose the word "lawlessness" (*anomia*) because in teaching about the last days it meant rebellion against God (see 2 Thess 2:3, 7–8, "the man of lawlessness," "the mystery of lawlessness," "the lawless one"; John has already said "it is the last hour," 2:18). If so, John is saying that sin is not only a flaw or a weakness, but taking sides against God.

**2. Why he appeared.** John twice says why the Lord appeared:
- "You know that he appeared to take away sins, and in him there is no sin" (3:5). This recalls John the Baptist's words: "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29). The sinless one could take away our sins because he became the propitiation for them (2:2; 4:10).
- "The reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil" (3:8). Jesus said no one can enter a strong man's house and plunder his goods "unless he first binds the strong man" (Mark 3:27). Christ came to defeat the devil and set his captives free.

So believers cannot make peace with sin: Christ came precisely to take sin away. If we say we belong to Christ but go on living carelessly in sin, we are working against the very reason he came.

**3. "No one born of God makes a practice of sinning."** "No one born of God makes a practice of sinning, for God's seed abides in him, and he cannot keep on sinning because he has been born of God" (3:9). How does this fit with 1:8, "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves"? Several readings are common:
- **Habitual sin:** the verbs are present tense and mean living on in sin as a settled pattern. Tense alone cannot settle it (5:16 uses the same present tense of a brother sinning), so those who hold this view also lean on the context. Those born of God still fall, but they do not live at ease in sin (for example, Stott; the ESV translates this way).
- **A particular sin:** the "sin" John means is the lawlessness of 3:4, or the "sin that leads to death" of 5:16, which some identify as denying Christ and abandoning the truth. Augustine took it as the sin against love, hatred of the brother (see 3:10–15).
- **The nature of the new life:** John speaks of the new life born of God itself. As far as that new life is concerned, it does not sin; believers still sin because the old nature remains (Luther and others).
- **Wesleyan:** John Wesley held that this is the privilege of everyone born of God: while he abides in faith and love, he does not wilfully break a known law of God. From here he developed his teaching on Christian perfection (entire sanctification), that God can fill the heart with love in this life. But Wesley also granted that believers still have faults of ignorance and weakness and still need Christ's blood.

Each reading has its reasons, but all agree that John is not contradicting 1:8 and 2:1. He means that the direction of life has changed for those born of God.

**4. "God's seed abides in him."** The Chinese Union Version translates this "God's word," but the Greek says "seed" (*sperma*). Views differ on what the seed is: some take it as God's word (see Jas 1:18; 1 Pet 1:23), as the CUV translates; some as the Holy Spirit; others as the new life God gives. On any view, John means that God himself has planted new life in us, and that life will grow and bear fruit. A believer's hope of overcoming sin lies not in the strength of his own will, but in God's work within him.

**5. "By this it is evident."** "By this it is evident who are the children of God, and who are the children of the devil: whoever does not practice righteousness is not of God, nor is the one who does not love his brother" (3:10). John returns to two tests: righteousness and love for the brother. Children resemble their father; our lives show whose children we are.

> The point: Christ came to take away sin and destroy the devil's works. Those born of God can no longer live at ease in sin, because God has planted new life in them.

## Going Deeper: 3:11–18 Not Like Cain; Love in Deed

**1. "We should not be like Cain."** "We should not be like Cain, who was of the evil one and murdered his brother. And why did he murder him? Because his own deeds were evil and his brother's righteous" (3:12). This is the only Old Testament figure 1 John names directly. Cain hated Abel not because Abel had harmed him, but because Abel's goodness exposed his own evil (see Gen 4:4–7; Heb 11:4). Envy often works like this: another's good shows us our own failure, and instead of repenting we hate the other person.

**2. "Do not be surprised… that the world hates you."** "Do not be surprised, brothers, that the world hates you" (3:13). Believers being misunderstood, shut out, even hated, is no surprise, for Cain's way runs on to this day. Jesus said, "If the world hates you, know that it has hated me before it hated you" (John 15:18).

**3. "Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer."** "We know that we have passed out of death into life, because we love the brothers. Whoever does not love abides in death. Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer" (3:14–15). This is what Jesus taught in the Sermon on the Mount: "everyone who is angry with his brother will be liable to judgment" (Matt 5:22). God looks not only at what the hand does but at the heart. We may never have harmed anyone's body, yet in our hearts we have "got rid of" someone again and again: ignoring him, hoping he fails, acting as if he does not exist.

**4. "By this we know love, that he laid down his life for us."** "By this we know love, that he laid down his life for us, and we ought to lay down our lives for the brothers" (3:16). Cain took his brother's life; Christ laid down his own life for his brothers. This is one of the letter's clearest definitions of love: love does not begin with our feelings; it begins at the cross. "Laid down his life" means giving up his life willingly, the same expression used of the good shepherd who "lays down his life for the sheep" (John 10:11, 15).

**5. "If anyone has the world's goods."** "But if anyone has the world's goods and sees his brother in need, yet closes his heart against him, how does God's love abide in him?" (3:17). John moves from the greatest (laying down one's life) to the most everyday (possessions). Not everyone will have a chance to die for a brother, but everyone often has a chance to give for one. "Heart" here is literally the inner parts, the deepest feelings; "closes" is the ordinary verb for shutting, used of shutting a door (Matt 6:6; 25:10).
- "Little children, let us not love in word or talk but in deed and in truth" (3:18).
- This does not mean giving without discernment. Helping people takes wisdom (see 2 Thess 3:10), and sometimes the best help is not money. But wisdom serves better love; it is not an excuse for shutting the door.

> The point: Cain took his brother's life; Christ laid down his own life for us. We learn love from the cross, and so love one another in concrete giving.

## Going Deeper: 3:19–24 Reassuring Our Heart Before God

**1. "God is greater than our heart."** "By this we shall know that we are of the truth and reassure our heart before him; for whenever our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart, and he knows everything" (3:19–20). "By this" points back to love in deed and truth (3:18). The next clause can be read two ways:
- **As comfort:** our heart often accuses us, but God is greater than our heart; he knows everything, including that our love and trust toward him are real, and his mercy is greater than our self-reproach (most modern commentators, for example, Stott).
- **As warning:** if even our own heart condemns us, how much more will God, who knows everything (Calvin and others read it this way).

The context favors the first, since John is speaking of how we may "reassure our heart before him." But both readings remind us that the final verdict does not rest on our own feelings; it rests with the God who knows everything, who has forgiven us through the blood of Christ (1:7; 2:1–2).

**2. "Whatever we ask we receive from him."** "Beloved, if our heart does not condemn us, we have confidence before God; and whatever we ask we receive from him, because we keep his commandments and do what pleases him" (3:21–22). This does not mean obedience is a bargain struck with God for answered prayer, as in folk religion's vows and offerings. It means that children living in God's will ask in line with the Father's heart (see 5:14).

**3. God's commandment: believe and love.** "And this is his commandment, that we believe in the name of his Son Jesus Christ and love one another, just as he has commanded us" (3:23). John sums up all the commands as one, with two parts: believe and love. This is the first time the letter uses the verb "believe." Faith is the root, love the fruit; the two cannot be separated.

**4. "By the Spirit whom he has given us."** "Whoever keeps his commandments abides in him, and he in them. And by this we know that he abides in us, by the Spirit whom he has given us" (3:24). How do we know God abides in us? Not only by looking at our own conduct, but because God has given the Spirit. The Spirit leads us to confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh (4:2), and testifies in our hearts (5:6, 10).

> The point: Our heart often accuses us, but God is greater than our heart. He has given his Spirit, who leads us to believe in his Son and love one another, and lets us know that he abides in us.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But if anyone has the world's goods and sees his brother in need, yet closes his heart against him, how does God's love abide in him?" (3:17). Every day I see too many needs: disasters on the news, friends of friends asking for help, the sick and the jobless on the church prayer list, the person holding out a hand by the road. At first I was moved, and I helped a little. But the needs are endless, I cannot meet them all, and I am afraid of being cheated. Little by little I have learned to shut my heart. I see, and I look away. I hear, and I say, "I'll pray for you," and then forget. I do not feel cruel, only tired, as if to protect myself I must first shut that door.

John's word is the one for shutting a door. He does not say that those who cannot help everyone lack love; he speaks of the one who "closes his heart." It is true that I cannot save everyone, and I need wisdom rather than blind giving; and if I am being exploited, pressured or put in danger, stopping is not hardness of heart. But have I used "I can't help everyone" and "I need to be wise" as excuses for the door I have shut? John does not ask me to meet every need alone. He asks me to love "in deed and in truth" (3:18) the particular brother or sister God puts in front of me. If I have been making excuses, what I need is not only rest but repentance. And if I am truly worn out, I need not pile self-reproach on top: "whenever our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart" (3:20). The Lord said to his tired disciples, "Come away by yourselves to a desolate place and rest a while" (Mark 6:31). Rest is not a shut heart; a shut heart is refusing to see even the one person in front of me.

Our hearts grow tired. The Lord grew tired too in his days on earth, yet his heart was never shut. "When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd" (Matt 9:36). When the widow of Nain followed her only son's bier, "when the Lord saw her, he had compassion on her and said to her, 'Do not weep'" (Luke 7:13). He saw, as I see, and did not look away. The word for "had compassion" in both verses comes from the same root as the "heart" John speaks of here. The moment I shut my heart is the very moment I most need his compassion. He did not shut his heart because of my coldness; he saw me too, harassed and hardened, and had compassion.

"By this we know love, that he laid down his life for us" (3:16). At the cross his heart was opened to me, and it has never been shut. He laid down his life for me, and so my heart can open again in his love, even if only for the one person in front of me.
