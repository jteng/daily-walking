## 本章脉络

约翰一书第四章接着3:24「因他所赐给我们的圣灵」，先讲怎样分辨诸灵，再讲神的爱。可以分成四段。

**试验那些灵**（1-6节）：不可凡灵都信，因为许多假先知已经出来。凡灵认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的，就是出于神；不认耶稣的，是那敌基督者的灵。信徒已经胜了他们，因为在他们里面的，比那在世界上的更大。

**神就是爱**（7-12节）：爱是从神来的；神差他独生子到世间来，使我们藉着他得生，又差他作挽回祭，这就是爱了。神既这样爱我们，我们也当彼此相爱。从来没有人见过神，我们若彼此相爱，神就住在我们里面。

**我们知道也信神的爱**（13-16节）：神将他的灵赐给我们；父差子作世人的救主；凡认耶稣为神儿子的，神就住在他里面。神爱我们的心，我们也知道也信。

**爱里没有惧怕**（17-21节）：爱在我们里面得以完全，我们在审判的日子可以坦然无惧；完全的爱把惧怕除去。我们爱，因为神先爱我们。不爱看得见的弟兄，就不能爱看不见的神。

## 深读 4:1-6 试验那些灵

**1. 「一切的灵，你们不可都信」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，一切的灵，你们不可都信，总要试验那些灵是出于神的不是，因为世上有许多假先知已经出来了。」（4:1）这里说的「灵」，是指藉着人说话的灵：每一个自称受圣灵感动的讲道、预言或启示，背后都有一个灵。约翰不是叫信徒怀疑一切，而是叫他们分辨。耶稣也说过：「你们要防备假先知。」（太7:15）

**2. 试验的标准。** 「凡灵认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的，就是出于神的；从此你们可以认出神的灵来。凡灵不认耶稣，就不是出于神。」（4:2-3）约翰给的第一个标准，不是这人有没有能力、讲得感不感人、有没有神迹，而是他怎样讲耶稣。保罗也说，不法的人来，要「行各样的异能、神迹，和一切虚假的奇事」（帖后2:9）。
- 「是成了肉身来的」：原文是完成式，许多人认为这表示基督不只是曾经成了肉身，而且到如今仍是那位成了肉身的主。
- 今天我们也可以用同样的标准来分辨：一个教导是否承认耶稣是真神、也是真人？是否承认他真的死了、真的复活了？是否把人带回到那位历史上的耶稣基督，还是带到一种只讲感受、能力或成功的「基督」？

**3. 「那在你们里面的，比那在世界上的更大」。** 「小子们哪，你们是属神的，并且胜了他们；因为那在你们里面的，比那在世界上的更大。」（4:4）约翰说信徒已经「胜了他们」，不是因为信徒比假先知更聪明或更有能力，而是因为住在信徒里面的神，比在世界上工作的那恶者更大。信徒面对迷惑，不需要惊慌。

**4. 「认识神的就听从我们」。** 「他们是属世界的，所以论世界的事，世人也听从他们。我们是属神的，认识神的就听从我们。」（4:5-6）这里的「我们」，和1:1-3一样，多数人认为是指使徒。约翰不是说凡听他讲道的就都属神，而是说：凡认识神的人，会接受使徒所传的福音。今天，这使徒的见证就写在新约圣经里。要分辨真理的灵和谬妄的灵，就看一个教导是否「听从」使徒的话。另一方面，假先知很受欢迎，「世人也听从他们」：受欢迎不是真理的标准。

> 要点：分辨真假，不是看谁讲得最有能力，而是看谁承认那成了肉身的耶稣基督；我们不必惧怕迷惑，因为住在我们里面的那一位更大。

## 深读 4:7-12 神就是爱

**1. 「爱是从神来的」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，我们应当彼此相爱，因为爱是从神来的。凡有爱心的，都是由神而生，并且认识神。没有爱心的，就不认识神，因为神就是爱。」（4:7-8）约翰没有说「爱就是神」。这两句话差别很大。说「神就是爱」，是说神的本性是爱，他一切的作为都出于爱；说「爱就是神」，却是把人的感受当作神来拜。约翰也说过「神就是光」（1:5）：神的爱永远是圣洁的爱，不会和罪妥协。

**2. 神的爱在此就显明了。** 「神差他独生子到世间来，使我们藉着他得生，神爱我们的心在此就显明了。」（4:9）约翰不是从一个抽象的定义讲爱，而是从一件事讲爱：神差他的独生子来。这正是约翰福音最有名的一句话：「神爱世人，甚至将他的独生子赐给他们。」（约3:16）
- 「独生子」这个词（*monogenēs*）强调的是独一无二。神没有另一个像他这样的儿子可以给；他把最宝贵的给了我们。
- 「使我们藉着他得生」：我们本来是死的（参3:14），神差他的儿子来，是要使我们活。

**3. 「这就是爱了」。** 「不是我们爱神，乃是神爱我们，差他的儿子为我们的罪作了挽回祭，这就是爱了。」（4:10）爱的源头不在我们，而在神。神爱我们，不是因为我们先爱了他，也不是因为我们可爱，而是在我们还是罪人的时候（罗5:8）。他的爱也不是只说说而已，而是付上了代价：他的儿子为我们的罪作了挽回祭。不认识十字架的人，不能真正明白「神就是爱」是什么意思。

**4. 「我们也当彼此相爱」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，神既是这样爱我们，我们也当彼此相爱。」（4:11）约翰在这里的结论却不是「神爱我们，所以我们也要爱神」（这当然也是真的，4:19-21），而是「神爱我们，所以我们也当彼此相爱」。神的爱流到我们身上，不是要停在我们这里，而是要从我们流到别人那里。

**5. 「从来没有人见过神」。** 「从来没有人见过神，我们若彼此相爱，神就住在我们里面，爱他的心在我们里面得以完全了。」（4:12）约翰福音也说：「从来没有人看见神，只有在父怀里的独生子将他表明出来。」（约1:18）神是看不见的；子来到世上，将他表明出来；现在，信徒彼此相爱的时候，看不见的神在他们中间显出来。「爱他的心」原文可以理解为神的爱，也可以理解为我们爱神的心（和合本取后者）。不论哪一种，「得以完全」都不是说我们的爱已经完美无缺，而是说神的爱达到了它的目的：从神出来，经过基督，在信徒的彼此相爱里结出果子。

> 要点：「神就是爱」的意思，要到十字架那里去看；那爱是神先付出的，也要藉着我们彼此相爱，叫人看见那看不见的神。

## 深读 4:13-16 我们知道也信神的爱

**1. 「神将他的灵赐给我们」。** 「神将他的灵赐给我们，从此就知道我们是住在他里面，他也住在我们里面。」（4:13）这和3:24几乎一样。约翰讲确据的时候，不是只叫我们看自己的行为，也提醒我们：神已经把他的灵赐给我们了。

**2. 「父差子作世人的救主」。** 「父差子作世人的救主；这是我们所看见且作见证的。」（4:14）这一句说出了三位一体的神怎样一同工作：父差遣，子来作救主，圣灵使我们知道并承认（4:13；参4:2、15）。同样的称呼（「救世主」）在约翰福音里是撒玛利亚人说的（约4:42），新约中只有这两处。耶稣不只是犹太人的救主，也是全世界的救主。

**3. 「凡认耶稣为神儿子的」。** 「凡认耶稣为神儿子的，神就住在他里面，他也住在神里面。」（4:15）神住在人里面，不是因为这人有特别的属灵经历，而是因为他承认耶稣是神的儿子。这是很朴素的根基，也是很稳固的根基。

**4. 「我们也知道也信」。** 「神爱我们的心，我们也知道也信。神就是爱；住在爱里面的，就是住在神里面，神也住在他里面。」（4:16）约翰说「知道」，也说「信」。这两个动词在原文都是完成式，意思是：我们早已认定，到如今仍然这样认识、这样信靠。「住在爱里面」，就是住在神的爱里，让这爱成为我们生活的家。

> 要点：我们能确知神住在我们里面，是因为他赐下圣灵，使我们承认耶稣是神的儿子；我们已经认识、也信靠了神的爱，这爱就成了我们安身的家。

## 深读 4:17-21 爱里没有惧怕

**1. 「在审判的日子坦然无惧」。** 「这样，爱在我们里面得以完全，我们就可以在审判的日子坦然无惧。因为他如何，我们在这世上也如何。」（4:17）「他如何，我们在这世上也如何」，有几种理解：有人认为是说，基督怎样是父所爱的，我们在基督里也照样是父所爱的；有人认为是说，基督怎样爱，我们在世上也怎样爱。前一种读法说的是我们在基督里的地位，后一种说的是我们的生命像他。两种读法都叫我们在审判的日子可以坦然无惧，而根基都在基督。

**2. 「爱里没有惧怕」。** 「爱里没有惧怕；爱既完全，就把惧怕除去。因为惧怕里含着刑罚，惧怕的人在爱里未得完全。」（4:18）这里的惧怕，不是敬畏神的那种敬畏（参箴1:7），而是怕受刑罚的惧怕，像奴仆怕主人的鞭子。保罗说：「你们所受的，不是奴仆的心，仍旧害怕；所受的，乃是儿子的心，因此我们呼叫：『阿爸！父！』」（罗8:15）
- 「惧怕里含着刑罚」：有人认为，这惧怕本身已经预尝了它所怕的刑罚。
- 「爱既完全，就把惧怕除去」：不是我们的爱完全了，才不惧怕；而是神的爱在我们里面达到它的目的，叫我们知道刑罚已经由基督担当了（4:10）。
- 人若还没有投靠基督，怕审判并不多余；这惧怕正是要催他今天就到十字架前来，在那里得着赦免与平安。

**3. 「我们爱，因为神先爱我们」。** 「我们爱，因为神先爱我们。」（4:19）最早、最可靠的抄本在这里没有说我们爱的是谁（有些较晚的抄本加了「他」或「神」，英文钦定本因此译作「我们爱他」）。我们爱神，也爱人，都是因为神先爱了我们。这一个「先」字，道出了整个福音：神没有等我们变好，没有等我们先爱他。

**4. 「不爱他所看见的弟兄」。** 「人若说『我爱神』，却恨他的弟兄，就是说谎话的；不爱他所看见的弟兄，就不能爱没有看见的神。」（4:20）爱看不见的神，似乎比爱看得见的人容易：神不会惹我生气，不会让我失望。可是约翰说，爱神的真假，要从我们怎样对待那个看得见、常常让我们为难的弟兄姊妹上看出来。
- 爱弟兄不是说要对一切的恶默不作声。若有人在教会中伤害别人，特别是欺压、虐待别人，爱受害的人，就包括保护他们，必要时向警方和有关部门报告（罗13:4）；受害的人也不必留在危险里，或勉强与仍不安全的人和好。

> 要点：完全的爱除去怕受刑罚的惧怕，因为刑罚已经由基督担当了；我们能爱，是因为神先爱了我们，这爱也要在我们怎样对待看得见的弟兄姊妹上显出来。

## 默想

**默想：** 「不是我们爱神，乃是神爱我们，差他的儿子为我们的罪作了挽回祭，这就是爱了。……我们爱，因为神先爱我们。」（4:10、19）有一段时间，我很怕唱「我爱你，主」这一类的诗歌。我张开口，心里却很冷。我知道我应该爱神，可是我的爱好像很薄，三心二意：祷告的时候想着别的事，主日一过就忘了他，有时候甚至觉得，我爱的是神给我的东西，多过爱神自己。我开始怀疑：爱神爱得这样冷淡的人，真的属于神吗？

约翰写这封信，一直在讲爱的试验。可是在这一章的中心，他先把我的眼目从我对神的爱移开：「不是我们爱神，乃是神爱我们。」爱的定义不是从我开始的。我若用自己爱神有多少，来衡量神爱不爱我，我永远不会安稳，因为我的爱总是会忽冷忽热。可是神的爱已经在一件确定的事上显明了：他差他的儿子来，为我的罪作了挽回祭。我感觉冷淡的那一天，这件事没有改变。我是不是一直在看自己的心有多热，却很少去看神的心已经为我做了什么？

这不是说我的冷淡没有关系。爱神的命令是真的，我的三心二意也需要悔改。可是悔改不是逼自己产生感觉，而是回到那先爱我的爱面前，像约翰说的，去「知道也信」神爱我们的心（4:16）。我们爱，是「因为」神先爱我们；爱是回应，不是先付的订金。我担心自己爱神的恩赐多过爱神自己；可是神在这里给我的，不只是又一样恩赐，而是他的儿子。我定睛在这位为我作挽回祭的子身上，看见的就不只是神的礼物，更是神自己的心。下一次唱这样的诗歌，我可以先在心里说：主啊，是你先爱了我。

神藉着先知何西阿，说到他对以色列的爱：「我原教导以法莲行走，用膀臂抱着他们，他们却不知道是我医治他们。我用慈绳爱索牵引他们。」（何11:3-4）神扶着学步的孩子，孩子却不知道是谁在扶他；长大以后，还转身去向巴力献祭（何11:2）。以色列的心，正是三心二意的心。可是神没有说「那就算了」，而是说：「以法莲哪，我怎能舍弃你？」（何11:8）我对神的爱，也许就像那孩子，摇摇晃晃，还常常转向别处。可是在我学会爱他以前，他已经爱了我；他差他的儿子来，正是用这慈绳爱索，把我牵到他自己面前。

## The Shape of the Chapter

1 John 4 follows from 3:24, "by the Spirit whom he has given us." It first speaks of discerning spirits, then of God's love. It falls into four parts.

**Test the spirits** (vv. 1–6): do not believe every spirit, for many false prophets have gone out. Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God; every spirit that does not confess Jesus is the spirit of the antichrist. Believers have already overcome them, for he who is in them is greater than he who is in the world.

**God is love** (vv. 7–12): love is from God. God sent his only Son into the world so that we might live through him, and sent him to be the propitiation for our sins; in this is love. Since God so loved us, we ought to love one another. No one has ever seen God; if we love one another, God abides in us.

**We have come to know and believe God's love** (vv. 13–16): God has given us his Spirit; the Father sent the Son to be the Savior of the world; whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God abides in him. We have come to know and to believe the love God has for us.

**No fear in love** (vv. 17–21): love is perfected with us, so that we may have confidence for the day of judgment; perfect love casts out fear. We love because he first loved us. Whoever does not love his brother whom he has seen cannot love God whom he has not seen.

## Going Deeper: 4:1–6 Test the Spirits

**1. "Do not believe every spirit."** "Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, for many false prophets have gone out into the world" (4:1). The "spirits" here are the spirits that speak through people: behind every sermon, prophecy or revelation that claims to be inspired by the Spirit, there is a spirit. John does not tell believers to doubt everything, but to discern. Jesus too said, "Beware of false prophets" (Matt 7:15).

**2. The test.** "By this you know the Spirit of God: every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God, and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God" (4:2–3). John's first test is not whether someone has power, speaks movingly or works miracles, but how he speaks of Jesus. Paul says the lawless one will come "with all power and false signs and wonders" (2 Thess 2:9).
- "Has come in the flesh": the Greek is a perfect participle, and many take it to mean that Christ not only once became flesh but is still the incarnate Lord.
- We can use the same test today. Does a teaching confess that Jesus is truly God and truly man? That he truly died and truly rose? Does it lead people back to the Jesus Christ of history, or to a "Christ" of feelings, power or success?

**3. "He who is in you is greater than he who is in the world."** "Little children, you are from God and have overcome them, for he who is in you is greater than he who is in the world" (4:4). John says believers "have overcome them," not because they are cleverer or stronger than the false prophets, but because the God who lives in them is greater than the evil one at work in the world. Believers facing deception need not panic.

**4. "Whoever knows God listens to us."** "They are from the world; therefore they speak from the world, and the world listens to them. We are from God. Whoever knows God listens to us" (4:5–6). As in 1:1–3, most take "we" here to mean the apostles. John is not saying that everyone who listens to his preaching belongs to God, but that everyone who knows God will receive the gospel the apostles proclaimed. Today that apostolic testimony is written in the New Testament. Whether a teaching "listens" to the apostles is how we tell the Spirit of truth from the spirit of error. And false prophets are popular, for "the world listens to them": popularity is no test of truth.

> The point: We tell true from false not by who speaks with the most power, but by who confesses the incarnate Jesus Christ. We need not fear deception, because the one who lives in us is greater.

## Going Deeper: 4:7–12 God Is Love

**1. "Love is from God."** "Beloved, let us love one another, for love is from God, and whoever loves has been born of God and knows God. Anyone who does not love does not know God, because God is love" (4:7–8). John does not say "love is God." The difference is great. To say "God is love" is to say that God's nature is love and all he does flows from love; to say "love is God" is to worship human feeling as God. John also said "God is light" (1:5): God's love is always holy love; it never makes peace with sin.

**2. "In this the love of God was made manifest."** "In this the love of God was made manifest among us, that God sent his only Son into the world, so that we might live through him" (4:9). John does not explain love by an abstract definition but by an event: God sent his only Son. This is the most famous sentence of John's Gospel: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son" (John 3:16).
- The word "only" (*monogenēs*) stresses uniqueness. God had no other Son like him to give; he gave us what was most precious.
- "So that we might live through him": we were dead (see 3:14), and God sent his Son to make us live.

**3. "In this is love."** "In this is love, not that we have loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins" (4:10). Love's source is not in us but in God. God loved us not because we loved him first, nor because we were lovable, but while we were still sinners (Rom 5:8). And his love was not only words; it paid a price: his Son became the propitiation for our sins. Whoever does not know the cross cannot really understand what "God is love" means.

**4. "We also ought to love one another."** "Beloved, if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another" (4:11). Here John's conclusion is not (though it is also true, 4:19–21) "God loves us, so we must love God," but "God loves us, so we ought to love one another." God's love comes to us not to stop with us, but to flow through us to others.

**5. "No one has ever seen God."** "No one has ever seen God; if we love one another, God abides in us and his love is perfected in us" (4:12). John's Gospel says, "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (John 1:18). God is unseen; the Son came into the world and made him known; now, when believers love one another, the unseen God is shown among them. "His love" can mean God's love for us or our love for him (the CUV takes the second). Either way, "perfected" does not mean our love is flawless. It means God's love has reached its goal: coming from God, through Christ, and bearing fruit in believers' love for one another.

> The point: What "God is love" means is seen at the cross. That love was given by God first, and through our love for one another the unseen God is seen.

## Going Deeper: 4:13–16 We Have Come to Know and Believe God's Love

**1. "He has given us of his Spirit."** "By this we know that we abide in him and he in us, because he has given us of his Spirit" (4:13). This is almost the same as 3:24. When John speaks of assurance, he does not only tell us to look at our conduct; he reminds us that God has given us his Spirit.

**2. "The Father has sent his Son to be the Savior of the world."** "And we have seen and testify that the Father has sent his Son to be the Savior of the world" (4:14). This sentence shows the triune God working together: the Father sends, the Son comes as Savior, and the Spirit leads us to know and confess it (4:13; cf. 4:2, 15). In John's Gospel, "Savior of the world" is what the Samaritans called him (John 4:42), the only two places in the New Testament where the title occurs. Jesus is Savior not only of the Jews but of the whole world.

**3. "Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God."** "Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God abides in him, and he in God" (4:15). God lives in a person not because he has had special spiritual experiences, but because he confesses that Jesus is the Son of God. This is a plain foundation, and a very firm one.

**4. "We have come to know and to believe."** "So we have come to know and to believe the love that God has for us. God is love, and whoever abides in love abides in God, and God abides in him" (4:16). John says "know" and also "believe." Both are perfects in Greek: a knowing and trusting that has been settled and still stands. To "abide in love" is to live in God's love, to make that love the home of our life.

> The point: We can be sure God lives in us because he has given his Spirit, who leads us to confess that Jesus is the Son of God. Having come to know and trust God's love, we have made it our home.

## Going Deeper: 4:17–21 No Fear in Love

**1. "Confidence for the day of judgment."** "By this is love perfected with us, so that we may have confidence for the day of judgment, because as he is so also are we in this world" (4:17). "As he is so also are we in this world" is read in several ways. Some take it to mean that as Christ is loved by the Father, so are we in Christ; others, that as Christ loves, so do we love in the world. The first reading speaks of our standing in Christ, the second of our lives becoming like his. Both give us reason for confidence on the day of judgment, and both rest on Christ.

**2. "There is no fear in love."** "There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear. For fear has to do with punishment, and whoever fears has not been perfected in love" (4:18). The fear here is not the reverent fear of God (see Prov 1:7), but fear of punishment, like a slave's fear of his master's whip. Paul says, "For you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!'" (Rom 8:15).
- "Fear has to do with punishment": some take this to mean that such fear already tastes the punishment it dreads.
- "Perfect love casts out fear": it is not that once our love is perfect we stop fearing, but that God's love reaches its goal in us and lets us know that Christ has borne the punishment (4:10).
- For anyone not yet in Christ, fear of judgment is not misplaced; it should drive them to the cross today, where there is forgiveness and peace.

**3. "We love because he first loved us."** "We love because he first loved us" (4:19). The earliest and best manuscripts do not say whom we love (some later manuscripts add "him" or "God," which is why the KJV reads "We love him"). We love God, and we love people, because God first loved us. That one word "first" is the whole gospel: God did not wait for us to improve, or for us to love him first.

**4. "He who does not love his brother whom he has seen."** "If anyone says, 'I love God,' and hates his brother, he is a liar; for he who does not love his brother whom he has seen cannot love God whom he has not seen" (4:20). Loving an unseen God can seem easier than loving people we can see: God does not irritate or disappoint us. But John says that whether our love for God is real shows in how we treat the brother or sister we can see, who often makes life hard for us.
- Loving the brother does not mean keeping silent about every wrong. If someone in the church harms others, especially by oppression or abuse, love for those harmed includes protecting them and, where needed, reporting to the police and proper authorities (Rom 13:4); those harmed need not stay in danger or be pressed to reconcile with someone still unsafe.

> The point: Perfect love casts out the fear of punishment, because Christ has borne the punishment. We are able to love because God first loved us, and that love shows in how we treat the brothers and sisters we can see.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "In this is love, not that we have loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins... We love because he first loved us" (4:10, 19). For a while I dreaded singing songs like "I love you, Lord." I opened my mouth, but my heart was cold. I knew I should love God, but my love seemed thin and divided: my mind wandered when I prayed, I forgot him as soon as Sunday was over, and sometimes I suspected that I loved what God gives more than God himself. I began to wonder whether someone who loves God like this can really belong to him.

John spends this letter on the tests of love. But at the center of this chapter he first turns my eyes away from my love for God: "not that we have loved God but that he loved us." The definition of love does not begin with me. If I measure whether I am loved by how much I love God, I will never be at rest, because my love keeps running hot and cold. But God's love has been shown in one settled event: he sent his Son to be the propitiation for my sins. On the day I feel cold, that event has not changed. Have I kept looking at how warm my own heart is, and seldom looked at what God's heart has already done for me?

This does not mean my coldness does not matter. The command to love God is real, and my divided heart needs repentance. But repentance is not forcing myself to produce feelings; it is coming back to the love that loved me first, and, as John says, to "know and to believe the love that God has for us" (4:16). We love "because" he first loved us; love is a response, not a down payment. I worry that I love God's gifts more than God himself; but what he gives here is not one more gift. It is his Son. When I look at the Son who became the propitiation for me, I see not only God's gift but God's own heart. The next time I sing such a song, I can first say in my heart: Lord, you loved me first.

Through the prophet Hosea, God spoke of his love for Israel: "Yet it was I who taught Ephraim to walk; I took them up by their arms, but they did not know that I healed them. I led them with cords of kindness, with the bands of love" (Hos 11:3–4). God held up the child learning to walk, but the child did not know who was holding him; when he grew up, he turned away and sacrificed to the Baals (Hos 11:2). Israel's heart was a divided heart. Yet God did not say, "Then let him go." He said, "How can I give you up, O Ephraim?" (Hos 11:8). My love for God may be like that child's: unsteady, and often turning elsewhere. But before I learned to love him, he loved me, and in sending his Son he has drawn me to himself with those very cords of kindness.
