## 本章脉络

约翰一书第五章把全书的线索收拢起来：信、爱、顺服、见证、确据。可以分成四段。

**信的人胜过世界**（1-5节）：凡信耶稣是基督的，都是从神而生；爱神的，也必爱从神生的。我们遵守神的诫命，这就是爱他了，他的诫命不是难守的。使我们胜了世界的，就是我们的信心。

**神为他儿子作的见证**（6-12节）：耶稣基督是藉着水和血而来的；圣灵、水与血都作见证。神的见证就是：神赐给我们永生，这永生是在他儿子里面。人有了神的儿子就有生命。

**知道自己有永生，向神坦然祷告**（13-17节）：约翰写信，是要叫信的人知道自己有永生。我们照神的旨意求，他就听我们。看见弟兄犯了不至于死的罪，就当为他祈求。

**我们知道**（18-21节）：三次「我们知道」：从神生的不继续犯罪，那恶者无法害他；我们是属神的；神的儿子已经来到，使我们认识那位真实的。最后一句是：「你们要自守，远避偶像！」

## 深读 5:1-5 信的人胜过世界

**1. 信、生、爱连在一起。** 「凡信耶稣是基督的，都是从神而生，凡爱生他之神的，也必爱从神生的。」（5:1）约翰把信、重生和爱连在一起：
- **信：** 「凡信耶稣是基督的，都是从神而生」。原文「信」是现在式，「生」是完成式。许多释经者认为，这表示信是重生的结果，不是重生的原因：人能信，是因为神已经使他重生（参约1:12-13）。同样的句式也出现在2:29和4:7：行义的、爱人的，都是「从神生的」；没有人会说行义或爱人是重生的原因。也有人认为，约翰在这里只是说两者总是同时存在，并没有讲先后。
- **爱：** 爱父亲的，也会爱父亲的孩子。我们若爱神，就不能不爱神的儿女。

**2. 「他的诫命不是难守的」。** 「我们遵守神的诫命，这就是爱他了，并且他的诫命不是难守的。」（5:3）这不是说神的诫命容易做到，好像不需要付代价；而是说，它不是压在我们身上的重担。耶稣说：「我的轭是容易的，我的担子是轻省的。」（太11:30）为什么不是难守的？下一节说出了原因：因为从神生的人，已经有了新生命。

**3. 「使我们胜了世界的，就是我们的信心」。** 「因为凡从神生的，就胜过世界；使我们胜了世界的，就是我们的信心。胜过世界的是谁呢？不是那信耶稣是神儿子的吗？」（5:4-5）约翰说「胜了」，原文是不定过去时，把得胜看为一件已经成就的事。有人认为是指读者已经胜过了那些离开的假教师（4:4；参2:13-14），有人认为是指信徒信主的那一刻，也有人认为是指基督在十字架上一次永远的得胜。耶稣说：「你们可以放心，我已经胜了世界。」（约16:33）信心本身没有力量，它的力量在于它所信的那一位。

> 要点：神的诫命对我们不是重担，因为我们是从神生的；我们胜过世界，不是靠自己的刚强，而是信靠那位已经胜了世界的神的儿子。

## 深读 5:6-12 神为他儿子作的见证

**1. 「藉着水和血而来的」。** 「这藉着水和血而来的，就是耶稣基督；不是单用水，乃是用水又用血，并且有圣灵作见证，因为圣灵就是真理。」（5:6-7）「水和血」指什么，有几种读法：
- **受洗和受死：** 多数现代释经者认为，「水」指耶稣在约旦河受洗，「血」指他在十字架上受死。约翰强调「不是单用水」，可能是针对那些说「基督」在耶稣受洗时降在他身上、受难前又离开了他的人（见本书导读）。约翰说：受死的，正是那位神的儿子。
- **十字架上流出的血和水：** 有人认为是指约翰福音所记的，「有血和水流出来」（约19:34）。
- **圣礼：** 也有人认为是指洗礼和圣餐，从古代教会到后世，都有人这样读。

第一种读法最合上下文。约翰的重点是：耶稣基督的身份不是在受洗时开始、在受难前结束；他是从降生到受死，一直都是神的儿子。

**2. 三样见证。** 「作见证的原来有三：就是圣灵、水，与血，这三样也都归于一。」（5:8）旧约律法要求两三个见证人（申19:15）。水（他受洗时圣灵的降临，约1:32-34；以及天上父的声音，太3:16-17）、血（他的死）和圣灵（在信徒心里的见证），一同证明耶稣是神的儿子。英文钦定本在这里多出的一段话（约翰短句），导读已经讲过，这里不再重复。另外，和合本这几节和英文标准版分节不同：和合本5:7相当于英文标准版5:6的后半节。

**3. 「神的见证更该领受了」。** 「我们既领受人的见证，神的见证更该领受了，因神的见证是为他儿子作的。」（5:9）我们在日常生活中常常相信人的见证：法庭的证人、医生的诊断、报纸的报道。神亲自为他的儿子作了见证，我们更该相信。「不信神的，就是将神当作说谎的。」（5:10）不信不只是看法不同，而是不肯信神的话，等于把神当作说谎的；这是得罪神的事。

**4. 「人有了神的儿子就有生命」。** 「这见证就是神赐给我们永生；这永生也是在他儿子里面。人有了神的儿子就有生命，没有神的儿子就没有生命。」（5:11-12）永生不是一样可以和基督分开的东西，好像我们从神手里领了一份礼物就走；永生就在他儿子里面。有了神的儿子，就有生命；没有神的儿子，不管有多少宗教的经历和知识，行了多少善，都没有生命。

> 要点：神亲自为他的儿子作见证：那受洗的、受死的，都是神的儿子；永生就在他里面，有了他就有生命。

## 深读 5:13-17 知道自己有永生，向神坦然祷告

**1. 「要叫你们知道自己有永生」。** 「我将这些话写给你们信奉神儿子之名的人，要叫你们知道自己有永生。」（5:13）约翰在这里说明了全书的目的（见本书导读）。注意他是写给「信奉神儿子之名的人」：确据的根基不在于我们有多完全，而在于我们所信的那一位。

**2. 「我们若照他的旨意求什么，他就听我们」。** 「我们若照他的旨意求什么，他就听我们，这是我们向他所存坦然无惧的心。既然知道他听我们一切所求的，就知道我们所求于他的，无不得着。」（5:14-15）
- 「照他的旨意」：这不是限制祷告，而是给祷告带来安稳。我们不必担心求错了会害了自己，因为父只会照他美善的旨意成就。
- 「无不得着」：「得着」原文是现在式的「有」，意思是我们已经有了。许多人认为，约翰是说：神一听，事情就已经定了，即使我们还没有看见成就。

**3. 「当为他祈求」。** 「人若看见弟兄犯了不至于死的罪，就当为他祈求，神必将生命赐给他；有至于死的罪，我不说当为这罪祈求。」（5:16）祷告不只是为自己求。约翰在这里讲的，是为犯罪的弟兄代求。看见弟兄犯罪，信徒的第一个反应应当是祷告，而不是议论或疏远。

**4. 「至于死的罪」是什么？** 这节经文常常叫人害怕，历来有几种读法：
- **离弃基督的罪：** 许多释经者认为，在约翰一书的上下文里，这是指那些不认耶稣是基督、离开教会的人所犯的罪（2:19、22）。他们故意弃绝那唯一能赐生命的神的儿子（5:12）。
- **招致身体死亡的罪：** 有人认为是指神用身体的死亡来管教的罪，例如亚拿尼亚和撒非喇（徒5:5、10），或哥林多教会中有人因妄领圣餐而死（林前11:30）。
- **天主教的「死罪」和「小罪」：** 天主教用这节经文支持「死罪」（使人失去恩宠）与「小罪」的分别（《天主教教理》第1854条）。改革宗传统也承认罪有轻重（《威斯敏斯德大要理问答》第150-152问），但认为再小的罪也当受死的刑罚（参罗6:23）。

约翰没有说出是哪一种罪，也没有禁止为这样的人祷告，只说「我不说当为这罪祈求」。若有人常常担心自己是否已经犯了不可赦免的罪，这份担心本身往往就表明，他心里还渴慕神，还没有离弃基督（参可3:28-29）。

> 要点：我们能确知自己有永生，是因为我们信神的儿子；有了这确据，就可以坦然地祷告，也为跌倒的弟兄姊妹代求。

## 深读 5:18-21 我们知道

**1. 三次「我们知道」。** 全书以三句确定的话结束：
- 「我们知道凡从神生的，必不犯罪。」（5:18）原文是现在式，指不继续活在罪中，见3:9和第三章。
- 「我们知道，我们是属神的，全世界都卧在那恶者手下。」（5:19）
- 「我们也知道，神的儿子已经来到，且将智慧赐给我们，使我们认识那位真实的。」（5:20）

**2. 「从神生的，必保守自己」。** 和合本这里作「从神生的，必保守自己，那恶者也就无法害他」。这里的抄本有出入：和合本跟随的读法是「保守自己」；有些重要的早期抄本（如梵蒂冈抄本）写的是「保守他」，英文标准版就是这样译的。若照后一种读法，这节前面讲信徒「从神生的」，用的是完成式；后面的「从神生的」却换成了不定过去时。许多人因此认为，后者是指神的儿子耶稣基督：是基督保守那从神生的信徒，那恶者就无法害他。耶稣在被卖的那一夜也为门徒祷告说：「只求你保守他们脱离那恶者。」（约17:15）两种读法都是真的：信徒要警醒保守自己（5:21），但信徒最终的安全，在于基督保守他。

**3. 「这是真神，也是永生」。** 「我们也在那位真实的里面，就是在他儿子耶稣基督里面。这是真神，也是永生。」（5:20）「这是真神」的「这」指谁？
- 许多释经者认为是指最靠近的「他儿子耶稣基督」。若是这样，这节经文就像约翰福音1:1和约翰福音20:28一样，直接称耶稣为神。约翰也在本书开头称耶稣为「那永远的生命」（1:2）。
- 也有人认为是指前面「那位真实的」，就是父神（参约17:3「独一的真神」）。

无论怎样读，约翰都说：我们是「在他儿子耶稣基督里面」，才在那位真实的神里面。

**4. 「你们要自守，远避偶像」。** 「小子们哪，你们要自守，远避偶像！」（5:21）全书最后一句话，没有祝福，也没有问安，只有这一句警告。偶像是指什么？有人认为是指有形的偶像：若这封信是写给以弗所一带的教会（见导读），那里正是拜亚底米女神的地方（徒19:27-28）；也有人认为是指那些离开的人所传的假基督：任何「神」，若不是那位成了肉身、为我们死而复活的耶稣基督，都是偶像。约翰在5:20刚刚说了「这是真神」，所以一切取代真神、叫人离开他的，都是偶像。

> 要点：我们要警醒自守，但我们最终的安全，在于神的儿子保守我们，那恶者无法害我们；认识了那位真实的神，就要远避一切代替他的偶像。

## 默想

**默想：** 「人若看见弟兄犯了不至于死的罪，就当为他祈求，神必将生命赐给他。」（5:16）我们教会有一位弟兄，最近被人发现在生意上做了不诚实的事。消息传开以后，大家都在议论：有人生气，有人说「早就看出来了」，也有人说以后要离他远一点。我也参加了这些谈话。可是我想了想，自从听说这件事以来，我议论他已经很多次了，却一次也没有为他祷告过。

约翰说，看见弟兄犯罪，第一件事是「为他祈求」。这不是把罪轻描淡写，因为约翰说「凡不义的事都是罪」（5:17）；也不是叫人不去处理。若这罪伤害了别人，就要按教会的纪律处理，该赔偿的要赔偿；若涉及犯法，特别是欺诈、虐待或伤害他人，就要交给执法机关，教会不可为了面子把事情压下去（罗13:4）。被他亏负的人要先得到照顾，谁也不可为了顾全他的面子，要求他们放弃追讨。可是这一切都不能代替祷告，而且祷告会改变我们议论的口气。为一个人祷告的时候，很难同时看不起他。保罗说：「若有人偶然被过犯所胜，你们属灵的人就当用温柔的心把他挽回过来；又当自己小心，恐怕也被引诱。」（加6:1）我谈论他的时候多，还是为他向神祷告的时候多？我爱谈他的事，是不是因为这样我就显得比他干净？我那些议论也是罪，也需要有人为我祈求；我若认这罪，神是信实的，是公义的，也必赦免（1:9）。

以色列人求立王，得罪了耶和华，撒母耳却对他们说：「至于我，断不停止为你们祷告，以致得罪耶和华。」（撒上12:23）撒母耳把停止为他们祷告看作得罪耶和华。但为人代祷，并不是要我做他的救主。约翰说「神必将生命赐给他」：赐生命的是神，不是靠我的祷告有多大力量。

我能这样为他祷告，是因为我自己也是这样被保守的。和合本5:18作「从神生的，必保守自己」，另有一些早期抄本作保守他；照后一种读法，许多人认为那保守信徒的，就是神的儿子。主耶稣不只为当年的门徒，也为「那些因他们的话信我的人」求父保守他们脱离那恶者（约17:15、20）；如今他亲自成就这事。那恶者无法害我，不是因为我比那位弟兄站得稳，而是因为保守我的是他。「我们也在那位真实的里面，就是在他儿子耶稣基督里面。」（5:20）我为这位弟兄所求的生命，不是靠我的祷告替他争来的，乃是那位保守我的主本来就乐意赐的。

## The Shape of the Chapter

1 John 5 gathers up the threads of the whole letter: faith, love, obedience, testimony, assurance. It falls into four parts.

**Those who believe overcome the world** (vv. 1–5): everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God; everyone who loves the Father loves whoever has been born of him. This is the love of God, that we keep his commandments, and his commandments are not burdensome. The victory that has overcome the world is our faith.

**God's testimony concerning his Son** (vv. 6–12): Jesus Christ came by water and blood; the Spirit, the water and the blood testify. This is God's testimony: God gave us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. Whoever has the Son has life.

**Knowing we have eternal life; praying with confidence** (vv. 13–17): John writes so that believers may know they have eternal life. If we ask anything according to his will, he hears us. If anyone sees his brother committing a sin not leading to death, he shall ask.

**We know** (vv. 18–21): three times "we know": those born of God do not keep on sinning, and the evil one does not touch them; we are from God; the Son of God has come so that we may know him who is true. The last words are "Little children, keep yourselves from idols."

## Going Deeper: 5:1–5 Those Who Believe Overcome the World

**1. Believing, being born and loving belong together.** "Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God, and everyone who loves the Father loves whomever has been born of him" (5:1). John ties believing, new birth and love together:
- **Believing:** "Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God." In Greek "believes" is present tense and "has been born" is perfect. Many commentators take this to mean that faith is the result of new birth, not its cause: people can believe because God has already given them new birth (see John 1:12–13). The same pattern appears in 2:29 and 4:7 (whoever practices righteousness, whoever loves, "has been born of God"), where no one would say righteousness or love causes the new birth. Others think John is only saying that the two always go together, without speaking of order.
- **Loving:** whoever loves a father will love his children. If we love God, we cannot fail to love God's children.

**2. "His commandments are not burdensome."** "For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments. And his commandments are not burdensome" (5:3). This does not mean God's commands are easy and cost nothing; it means they are not a weight crushing us. Jesus said, "For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light" (Matt 11:30). Why are they not burdensome? The next verse gives the reason: those born of God already have new life.

**3. "The victory that has overcome the world—our faith."** "For everyone who has been born of God overcomes the world. And this is the victory that has overcome the world—our faith. Who is it that overcomes the world except the one who believes that Jesus is the Son of God?" (5:4–5). "Has overcome" is an aorist, viewing the victory as accomplished. Some take it as the readers' victory over the false teachers who left (4:4; cf. 2:13–14), some as the moment they came to faith, others as Christ's once-for-all victory at the cross. Jesus said, "But take heart; I have overcome the world" (John 16:33). Faith has no power of its own; its power lies in the One it trusts.

> The point: God's commands are not a burden to us, because we have been born of God. We overcome the world not by our own strength, but by trusting the Son of God who has already overcome it.

## Going Deeper: 5:6–12 God's Testimony Concerning His Son

**1. "He who came by water and blood."** "This is he who came by water and blood—Jesus Christ; not by the water only but by the water and the blood. And the Spirit is the one who testifies, because the Spirit is the truth" (5:6). What do "water and blood" mean? There are several readings:
- **Baptism and death:** most modern commentators take "water" as Jesus' baptism in the Jordan and "blood" as his death on the cross. John's stress, "not by the water only," may be aimed at those who said "the Christ" came upon Jesus at his baptism and left him before his suffering (see the introduction). John says that the one who died was the Son of God himself.
- **The blood and water from the cross:** some take it as what John's Gospel records: "there came out blood and water" (John 19:34).
- **The sacraments:** some take it as baptism and the Lord's Supper, a reading found in the early church and since.

The first reading fits the context best. John's point is that Jesus Christ's identity did not begin at his baptism and end before his passion; from his birth to his death he was the Son of God.

**2. Three witnesses.** "For there are three that testify: the Spirit and the water and the blood; and these three agree" (5:7–8). The Old Testament law required two or three witnesses (Deut 19:15). The water (the Spirit's descent at his baptism, John 1:32–34, and the Father's voice from heaven, Matt 3:16–17), the blood (his death) and the Spirit (his witness in believers' hearts) together testify that Jesus is the Son of God. The extra words in the King James Version here (the Johannine Comma) were covered in the introduction. Note too that the CUV and ESV number these verses differently: CUV 5:7 matches the second half of ESV 5:6.

**3. "The testimony of God is greater."** "If we receive the testimony of men, the testimony of God is greater, for this is the testimony of God that he has borne concerning his Son" (5:9). In daily life we often trust human testimony: a witness in court, a doctor's diagnosis, a news report. God himself has testified about his Son, so we should trust him all the more. "Whoever does not believe God has made him a liar" (5:10). Unbelief is not just a difference of opinion; it refuses God's word and treats him as a liar. It is sin against God.

**4. "Whoever has the Son has life."** "And this is the testimony, that God gave us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. Whoever has the Son has life; whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life" (5:11–12). Eternal life is not something that can be separated from Christ, as if we took a gift from God's hand and walked away. Eternal life is in his Son. Whoever has the Son has life; whoever does not, whatever religious experience, knowledge or good works he has, does not have life.

> The point: God himself testifies to his Son: the one baptized and the one who died is the Son of God. Eternal life is in him, and whoever has him has life.

## Going Deeper: 5:13–17 Knowing We Have Eternal Life; Praying with Confidence

**1. "That you may know that you have eternal life."** "I write these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God that you may know that you have eternal life" (5:13). Here the purpose of the whole letter is stated (see the introduction). Notice that he writes "to you who believe in the name of the Son of God": the ground of assurance is not how perfect we are, but the One we believe.

**2. "If we ask anything according to his will he hears us."** "And this is the confidence that we have toward him, that if we ask anything according to his will he hears us. And if we know that he hears us in whatever we ask, we know that we have the requests that we have asked of him" (5:14–15).
- "According to his will": this does not restrict prayer; it steadies it. We need not fear that a wrong request will harm us, because the Father will only act according to his good will.
- "We have the requests": "have" is present tense. Many take John to mean that once God hears, the matter is settled, even if we have not yet seen it done.

**3. "He shall ask."** "If anyone sees his brother committing a sin not leading to death, he shall ask, and God will give him life—to those who commit sins that do not lead to death. There is sin that leads to death; I do not say that one should pray for that" (5:16). Prayer is not only for ourselves. Here John speaks of praying for a brother who sins. When a believer sees a brother sin, his first response should be prayer, not gossip or distance.

**4. What is "sin that leads to death"?** This verse often frightens people. There have been several readings:
- **Abandoning Christ:** many commentators take it, in the context of 1 John, as the sin of those who denied that Jesus is the Christ and left the church (2:19, 22). They deliberately rejected the only Son who gives life (5:12).
- **Sin that brings physical death:** some take it as sin God disciplines with bodily death, such as Ananias and Sapphira (Acts 5:5, 10), or those in Corinth who died after taking the Lord's Supper unworthily (1 Cor 11:30).
- **Catholic mortal and venial sin:** the Catholic Church uses this verse in support of the distinction between mortal sin (which destroys grace) and venial sin (*Catechism of the Catholic Church* 1854). Reformed confessions also hold that sins differ in seriousness (Westminster Larger Catechism 150–152), but that no sin is so small it does not deserve death (cf. Rom 6:23).

John does not say which sin he means, nor does he forbid prayer for such a person; he only says, "I do not say that one should pray for that." Someone who keeps worrying whether he has already committed an unforgivable sin usually shows by that very worry that his heart still longs for God and has not abandoned Christ (see Mark 3:28–29).

> The point: We can know we have eternal life because we believe in the Son of God. With that assurance we can pray with confidence, and pray for brothers and sisters who fall.

## Going Deeper: 5:18–21 We Know

**1. Three times "we know."** The letter ends with three certain statements:
- "We know that everyone who has been born of God does not keep on sinning" (5:18).
- "We know that we are from God, and the whole world lies in the power of the evil one" (5:19).
- "And we know that the Son of God has come and has given us understanding, so that we may know him who is true" (5:20).

**2. "He who was born of God protects him."** The ESV reads, "but he who was born of God protects him, and the evil one does not touch him" (5:18). The manuscripts differ here: the Chinese Union Version follows the reading "keeps himself"; some important early manuscripts (such as Codex Vaticanus) read "protects him," as the ESV translates. On that reading, John switches from the perfect used of believers ("everyone who has been born of God") to an aorist ("he who was born of God"), and many take this second phrase to mean the Son of God, Jesus Christ: Christ keeps the believer born of God, so the evil one cannot touch him. On the night he was betrayed, Jesus prayed for his disciples: "I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from the evil one" (John 17:15). Both readings are true: believers must keep watch over themselves (5:21), but their final safety lies in Christ keeping them.

**3. "He is the true God and eternal life."** "And we are in him who is true, in his Son Jesus Christ. He is the true God and eternal life" (5:20). Who is "he"?
- Many commentators take it as the nearest antecedent, "his Son Jesus Christ." If so, this verse, like John 1:1 and 20:28, calls Jesus God directly. John also called Jesus "the eternal life" at the start of the letter (1:2).
- Others take it as "him who is true," God the Father (cf. John 17:3, "the only true God").

On either reading, John says that we are in the true God by being "in his Son Jesus Christ."

**4. "Keep yourselves from idols."** "Little children, keep yourselves from idols" (5:21). The letter's last sentence has no blessing and no greeting, only this warning. What are the idols? Some take them as literal idols: if the letter went to churches around Ephesus (see the introduction), that was the city of Artemis (Acts 19:27–28). Others take them as the false Christ preached by those who left: any "god" who is not the Jesus Christ who came in the flesh, died and rose for us is an idol. John has just said, "He is the true God," so anything that leads away from the true God is an idol.

> The point: We are to keep ourselves, yet our final safety lies in the Son of God, who keeps us so that the evil one cannot touch us. Knowing the true God, we keep ourselves from every idol that would take his place.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "If anyone sees his brother committing a sin not leading to death, he shall ask, and God will give him life" (5:16). A brother in our church was recently found to have been dishonest in his business. Once the news spread, everyone was talking. Some were angry, some said, "I always knew," and some said they would keep their distance from now on. I joined in these conversations. But when I thought about it, since I heard the news I had talked about him many times, and not once prayed for him.

John says that when we see a brother sin, the first thing is to "ask" for him. This does not make light of sin, for John says, "All wrongdoing is sin" (5:17), nor does it mean nothing should be done. If the sin has harmed others, the church must deal with it by its discipline, and whatever should be repaid must be repaid. If it involves a crime, especially fraud, abuse or harm to others, it must be handed to the authorities, and the church must not hush it up to save face (Rom 13:4). Those he wronged must be cared for first, and no one should press them to drop their claim to spare his reputation. But none of this replaces prayer, and prayer changes the tone in which we talk. It is hard to pray for someone and look down on him at the same time. Paul says, "Brothers, if anyone is caught in any transgression, you who are spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness. Keep watch on yourself, lest you too be tempted" (Gal 6:1). Do I talk about him more than I talk to God for him? Do I enjoy talking about him because it makes me look cleaner than he is? My talk is sin too, the kind for which someone could ask on my behalf; and if I confess it, God is faithful and just to forgive this as well (1:9).

When Israel sinned by demanding a king, Samuel told them, "Moreover, as for me, far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD by ceasing to pray for you" (1 Sam 12:23). Samuel counted ceasing to pray for them as sin against the LORD. But praying for someone does not make me his savior. John says "God will give him life": it is God who gives life, not the strength of my prayer.

I can pray for him like this because I am kept in the same way. "He who was born of God protects him, and the evil one does not touch him" (5:18), and many take the one who keeps the believer to be the Son of God. The Lord Jesus asked the Father to keep from the evil one not only his first disciples but "those who will believe in me through their word" (John 17:15, 20), and now he does it himself. The evil one does not touch me, not because I stand firmer than this brother, but because he is the one who keeps me. "We are in him who is true, in his Son Jesus Christ" (5:20). The life I ask for this brother is not something my prayer wins for him; it is what the Lord who keeps me is glad to give.
