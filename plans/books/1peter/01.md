## 本章脉络

第一章可以分成五段。**问安**（1-2节）：写给分散寄居的人，就是照父神的先见被拣选，藉着圣灵得成圣洁，以致顺服耶稣基督，又蒙他血所洒的人。**活泼的盼望**（3-9节）：神藉耶稣基督从死里复活重生了我们，叫我们有活泼的盼望和存留在天上的基业；我们在百般的试炼中暂时忧愁，信心却比金子更宝贵；我们没有见过主，却爱他，有说不出来、满有荣光的大喜乐。**先知和天使所渴慕的救恩**（10-12节）：先知预先考察基督的受苦和荣耀，天使也愿意详细察看。**要圣洁**（13-21节）：要约束自己的心，专心盼望；要像那召我们的一样圣洁；要存敬畏的心度寄居的日子，因为我们得赎是凭着基督的宝血。**彼此相爱**（22-25节）：要从心里彼此切实相爱，因为我们是藉着神活泼常存的道重生的。

这一章的次序，就是全书的次序：先是神为我们做了什么（重生、基业、保守、救赎），然后才是我们当怎样活（盼望、圣洁、相爱）。第13节的「所以」是转折点。

## 深读 1:1-2 分散寄居的选民

**1. 「分散……寄居的」。** 「耶稣基督的使徒彼得写信给那分散在本都、加拉太、加帕多家、亚细亚、庇推尼寄居的。」（1:1）
- **「被拣选」和「寄居」：** 原文在1:1就把这两个词并列，直译是：蒙拣选的寄居者；和合本把「被拣选」放到了第2节。在神那里，他们是被拣选的；在世人中间，他们是寄居的外人。
- **「分散」：** 这个词原本是指散居在巴勒斯坦以外的犹太人（参雅1:1）。彼得把它用在大多是外邦人的信徒身上：他们也像被掳的以色列人一样，真正的家不在这里。

**2. 三位一体的神。** 「就是照父神的先见被拣选，藉着圣灵得成圣洁，以致顺服耶稣基督，又蒙他血所洒的人。愿恩惠、平安多多地加给你们。」（1:2）
- **父、圣灵、子：** 这一节简短地说出三位一体的神在救恩中的工作：父拣选，圣灵使人成圣，子用他立约的血洒在我们身上。
- **「先见」：** 「照父神的先见被拣选」，历代信徒有不同的理解。改革宗传统（如加尔文）一般认为，神的「先见」不只是预先看见，而是预先认定、预先爱了他所拣选的人（参罗8:29；弗1:4）；拣选的根据在神自己的旨意。这一派也指出，1:20用同一个词说基督「预先被神知道」。阿米念传统（如卫斯理）一般认为，神预先看见谁会信，就照着这先见拣选他们；这一派会指出，这个词的普通意思就是预先知道，并引用提前2:4。两种看法都同意：救恩从神开始，不是从人开始。
- **「蒙他血所洒」：** 这叫人想起西乃山立约的时候，百姓说：「耶和华所吩咐的，我们都必遵行。」摩西就「将血洒在百姓身上」（出24:7-8；参来9:19-20）。彼得把「顺服」和「血所洒」放在一起，正是呼应这一幕。信徒是在基督的血所立的新约里，被分别出来归给神的。

> 要点：在世人中间，信徒是寄居的外人；在神面前，他们是父所拣选、圣灵所分别、基督宝血所洒的人。

## 深读 1:3-9 活泼的盼望

**1. 「重生了我们」。** 「愿颂赞归与我们主耶稣基督的父神！他曾照自己的大怜悯，藉耶稣基督从死里复活，重生了我们，叫我们有活泼的盼望。」（1:3）
- **先赞美：** 彼得写给受苦的人，第一句不是安慰，而是颂赞。不是因为苦难不重，而是因为神所做的更大。
- **「照自己的大怜悯」：** 重生的原因不在我们，而在神的怜悯。主耶稣说：「人若不重生，就不能见神的国。」（约3:3）
- **「藉耶稣基督从死里复活」：** 我们的盼望是「活泼的」，因为基督是活着的。死了的人不能给人盼望，复活的主却能。

**2. 存留的基业，被保守的人。** 「可以得着不能朽坏、不能玷污、不能衰残、为你们存留在天上的基业。你们这因信蒙神能力保守的人，必能得着所预备、到末世要显现的救恩。」（1:4-5）
- **三个「不能」：** 不能朽坏（不会腐烂）、不能玷污（不会被弄脏）、不能衰残（不会褪色）。地上的产业都会失去，这份基业却不会。
- **两边都有保守：** 基业「存留在天上」，我们也「蒙神能力保守」。「保守」原文是一个军事用语，像驻军守城一样。神不只为我们保存基业，也保守承受基业的人。
- **「因信」：** 神保守我们，是藉着我们的信心；而从整本圣经看，这信心本身也是神所保守的（参腓1:6；犹24）。

**3. 「暂时忧愁」。** 「因此，你们是大有喜乐；但如今，在百般的试炼中暂时忧愁，叫你们的信心既被试验，就比那被火试验仍然能坏的金子更显宝贵，可以在耶稣基督显现的时候得着称赞、荣耀、尊贵。」（1:6-7）
- **喜乐和忧愁同时存在：** 彼得没有说信徒不会忧愁。喜乐和忧愁可以同时在一个人心里。
- **「暂时」：** 和永远的基业相比，试炼是暂时的（参林后4:17）。原文在「暂时忧愁」前面还有一个短语，意思是：如果有必要（和合本没有译出）。试炼不是偶然临到的，而是在神认为需要的时候、照着他的旨意临到的（参3:17「神的旨意若是叫你们因行善受苦」）。
- **炼金：** 「神啊，你曾试验我们，熬炼我们，如熬炼银子一样。」（诗66:10）炼金的人用火，不是要毁掉金子，而是要炼去杂质。信心比金子更宝贵，因为金子终究会坏，信心却通向永远的荣耀。

**4. 「你们虽然没有见过他，却是爱他」。** 「你们虽然没有见过他，却是爱他；如今虽不得看见，却因信他就有说不出来、满有荣光的大喜乐；并且得着你们信心的果效，就是灵魂的救恩。」（1:8-9）
- **彼得见过，读者没有：** 彼得曾经和耶稣同行约三年，读者却从来没有见过他。可是彼得说，他们也爱他。主耶稣对多马说：「那没有看见就信的有福了。」（约20:29）
- **「说不出来」的喜乐：** 这喜乐不是从顺境来的，而是从认识基督本身来的。

> 要点：我们的盼望是活的，因为基督已经从死里复活。基业存留在天上，我们也被神的能力保守在地上；试炼是暂时的，却能炼出比金子更宝贵的信心。我们没有见过他，却爱他，在他里面有说不出来、满有荣光的大喜乐。

## 深读 1:10-12 先知和天使所渴慕的

**「论到这救恩，那预先说你们要得恩典的众先知早已详细地寻求考察，就是考察在他们心里基督的灵，预先证明基督受苦难，后来得荣耀，是指着什么时候，并怎样的时候。他们得了启示，知道他们所传讲的一切事，不是为自己，乃是为你们。那靠着从天上差来的圣灵传福音给你们的人，现在将这些事报给你们；天使也愿意详细察看这些事。」**（1:10-12）
- **「基督的灵」在先知心里：** 旧约的先知说预言，是基督的灵在他们里面说话。可见基督在降生以前早已存在。
- **「受苦难，后来得荣耀」：** 这是旧约预言的一个中心主题（彼得在2:22-25就引用了以赛亚书53章），也是全书的主线。主耶稣复活以后也对门徒说：「基督这样受害，又进入他的荣耀，岂不是应当的吗？」（路24:26）
- **先知的渴慕：** 但以理听见了异象，却说：「我听见这话，却不明白。」（但12:8）主耶稣说：「从前有许多先知和义人要看你们所看的，却没有看见。」（太13:17）
- **天使也愿意察看：** 「详细察看」原文是弯腰往里看（约20:5用同一个词）。天使不需要救赎，却渴想看明神向罪人所施的恩典。受苦的信徒所领受的，是连天使都羡慕的。

> 要点：先知所渴慕、天使所察看的救恩，如今已经传给了我们。天使弯腰察看的，是基督的受苦和后来的荣耀；被邻舍看不起的信徒，如今却亲耳听见了这福音。

## 深读 1:13-21 要圣洁

**1. 「所以要约束你们的心」。** 「所以要约束你们的心，谨慎自守，专心盼望耶稣基督显现的时候所带来给你们的恩。」（1:13）
- **「约束你们的心」：** 原文直译是：束上你们心思的腰。古人穿长袍，要做工或赶路，就先把衣襟束在腰间。这很可能叫人想起以色列人吃逾越节的羊羔，要「腰间束带……赶紧地吃」（出12:11；参路12:35）。
- **「专心盼望……恩」：** 圣洁的生活从盼望开始。恩典不只是过去的，也是将来要「带来」的。

**2. 「你们要圣洁，因为我是圣洁的」。** 「你们既作顺命的儿女，就不要效法从前蒙昧无知的时候那放纵私欲的样子。那召你们的既是圣洁，你们在一切所行的事上也要圣洁。因为经上记着说：『你们要圣洁，因为我是圣洁的。』」（1:14-16）
- **「顺命的儿女」：** 先有儿女的身份，后有顺命的生活。
- **引自利未记：** 「你们要成为圣洁，因为我是圣洁的。」（利11:44；19:2）圣洁的意思是被分别出来归给神，也是品格上像神。
- **「在一切所行的事上」：** 圣洁不只在主日的教会里，也在周一的会议上，在没有人查的账目里，在关起门来对家人说话的语气里。

**3. 「存敬畏的心度你们在世寄居的日子」。** 「你们既称那不偏待人、按各人行为审判人的主为父，就当存敬畏的心度你们在世寄居的日子。」（1:17）
- **父和审判者：** 我们称神为「父」，这位父也是「不偏待人」的审判者。这不是叫人活在恐惧中，而是叫人不可轻看神。「按各人行为审判」，不是说我们靠行为得救（下一节就说，我们得赎是凭着基督的宝血），而是说这位父看重儿女怎样活（参林后5:10）。我们敬畏他，正是因为「知道」他为我们付了何等的代价（1:18）。
- **「寄居的日子」：** 信徒在世上的时间是有限的，像客旅一样。

**4. 「凭着基督的宝血」。** 「知道你们得赎，脱去你们祖宗所传流虚妄的行为，不是凭着能坏的金银等物，乃是凭着基督的宝血，如同无瑕疵、无玷污的羔羊之血。」（1:18-19）
- **「得赎」：** 这个词原指付赎价，使奴隶或俘虏得自由；在旧约里，它常用来说神把以色列人「从为奴之家救赎」出来（申7:8；参出6:6）。主耶稣说：「人子来……要舍命作多人的赎价。」（可10:45）
- **「祖宗所传流虚妄的行为」：** 读者原来是外邦人，从祖宗那里承受了拜偶像的习俗和整套价值观。放在华人处境中，这可能包括把祖先当作神明祭拜、烧香求保佑、拜神求财、看风水、查黄历择日，也包括那些一代传一代、看似理所当然的人生目标和家里过日子的方式。这不是否定孝道：孝敬父母是神所吩咐的（出20:12），怀念先人也不是罪；虚妄的是把先人或鬼神当作敬拜、倚靠的对象。「虚妄」就是空洞、没有结果。
- **不是金银：** 以赛亚说：「你们是无价被卖的，也必无银被赎。」（赛52:3）赎价不是钱，而是基督的血。
- **「无瑕疵、无玷污的羔羊」：** 逾越节的羊羔要「无残疾」（出12:5；彼得也可能同时想到以赛亚书53:7那「像羊羔被牵到宰杀之地」的受苦仆人）。基督是神的羔羊，他的血代替我们流出，神的审判就越过了我们。

**5. 「创世以前……在这末世」。** 「基督在创世以前是预先被神知道的，却在这末世才为你们显现。你们也因着他，信那叫他从死里复活、又给他荣耀的神，叫你们的信心和盼望都在于神。」（1:20-21）
- **救赎不是临时的补救：** 早在创世以前，神就定意要基督作赎罪的羔羊。彼得在五旬节也说，耶稣是「按着神的定旨先见被交与人」的（徒2:23）。
- **「为你们显现」：** 神永远的计划，是为这些平凡、受苦的信徒成就的。

> 要点：圣洁的生活从盼望和敬畏开始，而敬畏的根基是救赎的代价。我们不是用金银买回来的，而是用基督的宝血，那创世以前就预备好的羔羊之血。

## 深读 1:22-25 彼此相爱

**1. 「从心里彼此切实相爱」。** 「你们既因顺从真理，洁净了自己的心，以致爱弟兄没有虚假，就当从心里彼此切实相爱。」（1:22）
- **「切实」：** 原文的意思是恳切、持续不懈。路加用同一个词说，彼得被囚的时候，「教会却为他切切地祷告神」（徒12:5）；彼得后来又说：「最要紧的是彼此切实相爱」（彼前4:8）。爱不是一种温吞的客气。
- **爱的根源：** 主耶稣说：「我怎样爱你们，你们也要怎样相爱。」（约13:34）

**2. 「不能坏的种子」。** 「你们蒙了重生，不是由于能坏的种子，乃是由于不能坏的种子，是藉着神活泼常存的道。因为凡有血气的，尽都如草；他的美荣都像草上的花。草必枯干，花必凋谢；惟有主的道是永存的。所传给你们的福音就是这道。」（1:23-25）
- **回到重生：** 第3节说神「重生了我们」，这里说是「藉着神活泼常存的道」。神用他的话，生出新的生命。
- **引自以赛亚书40章：** 被掳的以色列人以为自己被神遗忘了（赛40:27），先知却说，人的荣耀像草一样会枯干，神的话却永远立定（赛40:6-8）。
- **这段为什么紧接在「彼此相爱」后面？** 信徒是同一颗种子所生的弟兄姊妹，有同一个父，所以要彼此相爱。

> 要点：信徒是藉着神永存的道重生的，是同一位父的儿女，所以要从心里彼此切实相爱。人的荣耀像草，神的话却永远长存。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们既称那不偏待人、按各人行为审判人的主为父，就当存敬畏的心度你们在世寄居的日子，知道你们得赎，脱去你们祖宗所传流虚妄的行为，不是凭着能坏的金银等物，乃是凭着基督的宝血。」（1:17-19）每个家都会传下一些东西，不只是房子和姓氏，也有一套过日子的方式。也许在我家里，做错了事从来不认错；吵完架就冷战几天，谁也不先开口；有心事不说，报喜不报忧；钱的事要瞒着，心里的伤也要瞒着。我小时候暗暗发誓，长大了绝不这样。可是有一天，我发现自己也在冷战，也开始什么都不说。彼得说，这就是「祖宗所传流虚妄的行为」：一代传一代，看起来理所当然，到头来却是空的。我以为只要自己够有决心，就能不再像他们，可是这么多年的决心并没有做到。彼得却不是先叫我更努力，而是先告诉我一件已经成就的事：我已经「得赎」了。付代价的不是我，代价也不是金银，金银买不回一个人的心；神付上的是「基督的宝血，如同无瑕疵、无玷污的羔羊之血」。这羔羊「在创世以前是预先被神知道的」（1:20）；在我家这些旧样子开始以前，神就已经定意要把我买回来。所以我离开这些旧样子，不是为了换取他的接纳，而是因为我已经不再属于它们了。彼得所说的「敬畏」，也不是怕他把我丢掉，而是不肯轻看他为我付上的代价。如果我已经把这些旧样子传给了下一代，这宝血也是为我这一部分流的；那赎了我的主，也能赎他们。今天，我可以在主面前说出一个从家里传下来、我自己也还在活的旧样子，承认这是得罪他的，求他赦免我，照他的方法改变我。然后我可以停下来，单单仰望那羔羊：他的血比一切金银都宝贵，叫我的信心和盼望都在于神（1:21）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 falls into five parts. **Greeting** (vv. 1–2): to the exiles of the dispersion, chosen according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, in the sanctification of the Spirit, for obedience to Jesus Christ and for sprinkling with his blood. **A living hope** (vv. 3–9): God has caused us to be born again to a living hope and an inheritance kept in heaven through the resurrection of Jesus Christ; we are grieved for a little while by various trials, yet our faith is more precious than gold; we have not seen the Lord, yet we love him, and rejoice with joy that is inexpressible and filled with glory. **The salvation prophets and angels longed for** (vv. 10–12): the prophets searched out the sufferings and glories of Christ, and angels long to look into these things. **Be holy** (vv. 13–21): prepare your minds and set your hope fully on grace; be holy as he who called you is holy; conduct yourselves with fear in your exile, because you were ransomed with the precious blood of Christ. **Love one another** (vv. 22–25): love one another earnestly from a pure heart, because you were born again through the living and abiding word of God.

The order of this chapter is the order of the whole letter: first what God has done for us (new birth, inheritance, guarding, redemption), and only then how we are to live (hope, holiness, love). The "therefore" of verse 13 is the hinge.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–2 The Chosen Exiles

**1. "Elect exiles of the dispersion."** "Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, To those who are elect exiles of the dispersion in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia" (1:1).
- **Two names that seem to clash:** the Greek of 1:1 puts the two words side by side (ESV "elect exiles"); the CUV moves "chosen" into verse 2. Before God they are chosen; among people they are foreigners.
- **"The dispersion":** the word first referred to Jews scattered outside Palestine (see Jas 1:1). Peter applies it to believers who were mostly Gentiles: like Israel in exile, their true home is not here.

**2. The triune God.** "According to the foreknowledge of God the Father, in the sanctification of the Spirit, for obedience to Jesus Christ and for sprinkling with his blood: May grace and peace be multiplied to you" (1:2).
- **Father, Spirit, Son:** this verse briefly sets out the work of the triune God in salvation: the Father chooses, the Spirit sanctifies, the Son sprinkles us with his covenant blood.
- **"Foreknowledge":** Christians have understood "according to the foreknowledge of God the Father" differently. The Reformed tradition (for example, Calvin) generally holds that God's "foreknowledge" is not only seeing ahead but setting his love on people beforehand (see Rom 8:29; Eph 1:4), so that election rests on God's own purpose; they also note that 1:20 uses the same word of Christ being "foreknown." The Arminian tradition (for example, Wesley) generally holds that God foresaw who would believe and chose them according to that foresight; they point to the ordinary sense of the word, "know beforehand," and to 1 Tim 2:4. Both agree that salvation begins with God, not with us.
- **"Sprinkling with his blood":** this recalls the covenant at Sinai, when the people said, "All that the LORD has spoken we will do," and Moses "took the blood and threw it on the people" (Exod 24:7–8; see Heb 9:19–20, "sprinkled... all the people"). "Obedience" and "sprinkling" stand together just as at Sinai, where the people pledged obedience and Moses then applied the blood. Believers are set apart for God within the new covenant made in Christ's blood.

> The point: Among people, believers are foreigners in exile; before God, they are chosen by the Father, set apart by the Spirit and sprinkled with the blood of Christ.

## Going Deeper: 1:3–9 A Living Hope

**1. "Caused us to be born again."** "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! According to his great mercy, he has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead" (1:3).
- **Praise first:** writing to suffering people, Peter's first sentence is not comfort but praise. Not because the suffering is light, but because what God has done is greater.
- **"According to his great mercy":** the cause of new birth is not in us but in God's mercy. The Lord Jesus said, "unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God" (John 3:3).
- **"Through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead":** our hope is "living" because Christ is alive. A dead man cannot give anyone hope; the risen Lord can.

**2. A kept inheritance, a guarded people.** "To an inheritance that is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for you, who by God's power are being guarded through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time" (1:4–5).
- **Three "un-" words:** imperishable (it will not rot), undefiled (it will not be soiled), unfading (it will not lose its color). Every earthly inheritance can be lost; this one cannot.
- **Kept on both sides:** the inheritance is "kept in heaven," and we are "guarded by God's power." "Guarded" is a military word, like a garrison holding a city. God does not only keep the inheritance safe; he keeps the heirs.
- **"Through faith":** God guards us through our faith, and the wider witness of Scripture shows that this faith is itself kept by God (see Phil 1:6; Jude 24).

**3. "Grieved for a little while."** "In this you rejoice, though now for a little while, as was necessary, you have been grieved by various trials, so that the tested genuineness of your faith, more precious than gold that perishes though it is tested by fire, may be found to result in praise and glory and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ" (1:6–7).
- **Joy and grief together:** Peter does not say believers will not grieve. Joy and grief can live in the same heart at the same time.
- **"For a little while":** compared with an eternal inheritance, trials are brief (see 2 Cor 4:17). The Greek adds "if need be" (ESV "as was necessary"; the CUV does not translate it). Trials do not come by chance but when God judges them needed, under his will (compare 3:17, "if that should be God's will").
- **Refining gold:** "For you, O God, have tested us; you have tried us as silver is tried" (Ps 66:10). The refiner uses fire not to destroy the gold but to burn away its dross. Faith is more precious than gold, because gold finally perishes, but faith leads to eternal glory.

**4. "Though you have not seen him, you love him."** "Though you have not seen him, you love him. Though you do not now see him, you believe in him and rejoice with joy that is inexpressible and filled with glory, obtaining the outcome of your faith, the salvation of your souls" (1:8–9).
- **Peter had seen him; his readers had not:** Peter walked with Jesus for about three years; his readers had never seen him. Yet Peter says they love him. The Lord Jesus told Thomas, "Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed" (John 20:29).
- **"Inexpressible" joy:** this is not happiness produced by circumstances, but the joy of knowing Christ himself.

> The point: Our hope is alive because Christ has risen from the dead. The inheritance is kept in heaven, and we are guarded by God's power on earth; trials are brief, but they refine a faith more precious than gold. We have not seen him, yet we love him, and in him we rejoice with joy that is inexpressible and filled with glory.

## Going Deeper: 1:10–12 What Prophets and Angels Longed For

**"Concerning this salvation, the prophets who prophesied about the grace that was to be yours searched and inquired carefully, inquiring what person or time the Spirit of Christ in them was indicating when he predicted the sufferings of Christ and the subsequent glories. It was revealed to them that they were serving not themselves but you, in the things that have now been announced to you through those who preached the good news to you by the Holy Spirit sent from heaven, things into which angels long to look"** (1:10–12).
- **"The Spirit of Christ" in the prophets:** when the Old Testament prophets prophesied, the Spirit of Christ spoke through them. This also implies that Christ existed before he was born.
- **"The sufferings of Christ and the subsequent glories":** this is a central theme of Old Testament prophecy (Peter quotes Isaiah 53 in 2:22–25) and the main thread of this letter. After his resurrection the Lord Jesus told his disciples, "Was it not necessary that the Christ should suffer these things and enter into his glory?" (Luke 24:26).
- **The prophets' longing:** Daniel heard the vision and said, "I heard, but I did not understand" (Dan 12:8). The Lord Jesus said, "many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see, and did not see it" (Matt 13:17).
- **Angels long to look:** "look into" means to stoop down and peer in (the same verb as in John 20:5). Angels do not need redemption, yet they long to look into God's grace to sinners. What these suffering believers have received is something angels long to see.

> The point: The salvation prophets longed for and angels peer into has now been preached to us. What angels stoop to look into is the sufferings of Christ and the glories that follow, and despised believers have now heard this good news for themselves.

## Going Deeper: 1:13–21 Be Holy

**1. "Therefore, preparing your minds for action."** "Therefore, preparing your minds for action, and being sober-minded, set your hope fully on the grace that will be brought to you at the revelation of Jesus Christ" (1:13).
- **"Preparing your minds for action":** literally "girding up the loins of your mind." People wore long robes; before working or traveling, they tucked them up into their belts. This probably recalls how Israel ate the Passover lamb "with your belt fastened... in haste" (Exod 12:11; see Luke 12:35).
- **"Set your hope fully on the grace":** a holy life begins with hope. Grace is not only past; it will also be "brought" to us in the future.

**2. "You shall be holy, for I am holy."** "As obedient children, do not be conformed to the passions of your former ignorance, but as he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your conduct, since it is written, 'You shall be holy, for I am holy'" (1:14–16).
- **"Obedient children":** first the identity of children, then the life of obedience.
- **From Leviticus:** "be holy, for I am holy" (Lev 11:44; 19:2). Holiness means being set apart for God, and also being like God in character.
- **"In all your conduct":** holiness is not only for Sunday at church, but for Monday's meeting, for the accounts no one will check, and for the tone of voice behind our own front door.

**3. "Conduct yourselves with fear throughout the time of your exile."** "And if you call on him as Father who judges impartially according to each one's deeds, conduct yourselves with fear throughout the time of your exile" (1:17).
- **Father and Judge:** we call God "Father," and this Father is also the impartial Judge. This does not mean living in terror, but it does mean not taking God lightly. Judgment according to deeds does not mean we are saved by deeds; the very next verse says we were ransomed by Christ's blood. It means this Father cares how his children live (see 2 Cor 5:10). We fear him precisely because we "know" the price he paid for us (1:18).
- **"Your exile":** believers' time on earth is limited, like travelers passing through.

**4. "With the precious blood of Christ."** "Knowing that you were ransomed from the futile ways inherited from your forefathers, not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot" (1:18–19).
- **"Ransomed":** a word for paying a price to free a slave or captive; in the Old Testament it is used of God, who "redeemed you from the house of slavery" (Deut 7:8; see Exod 6:6). The Lord Jesus said, "the Son of Man came... to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45).
- **"The futile ways inherited from your forefathers":** the readers were Gentiles who had inherited idolatrous customs and values from their ancestors. In a Chinese setting this may include worshipping ancestors as spirits and burning incense to them for protection, praying to gods for wealth, consulting feng shui and the almanac for lucky days, and also the life goals passed from one generation to the next as if they were self-evident, and a family's whole way of living. This is not a rejection of filial piety: honoring parents is God's command (Exod 20:12), and remembering the dead is not sin; the futility lies in worshipping or relying on ancestors or spirits. "Futile" means empty, fruitless.
- **Not silver or gold:** Isaiah says, "You were sold for nothing, and you shall be redeemed without money" (Isa 52:3). The ransom was not money but the blood of Christ.
- **"A lamb without blemish or spot":** the Passover lamb had to be "without blemish" (Exod 12:5; Peter may also have in mind the Servant of Isa 53:7, "like a lamb that is led to the slaughter"). Christ is the Lamb of God; his blood was shed in our place, so that judgment passes over us.

**5. "Before the foundation of the world... in the last times."** "He was foreknown before the foundation of the world but was made manifest in the last times for your sake, who through him are believers in God, who raised him from the dead and gave him glory, so that your faith and hope are in God" (1:20–21).
- **Redemption was no last-minute repair:** before the world was made, God had purposed Christ to be the atoning Lamb. At Pentecost Peter said the same: Jesus was "delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God" (Acts 2:23).
- **"For your sake":** the eternal plan was carried out for these ordinary, suffering believers.

> The point: A holy life begins with hope and with reverent fear, and that fear rests on the price of our redemption. We were not bought back with silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, the Lamb appointed before the world began.

## Going Deeper: 1:22–25 Love One Another

**1. "Love one another earnestly from a pure heart."** "Having purified your souls by your obedience to the truth for a sincere brotherly love, love one another earnestly from a pure heart" (1:22).
- **"Earnestly":** the word means fervently, without letting up. Luke uses the same word when "earnest prayer for him was made to God by the church" while Peter was in prison (Acts 12:5), and Peter later writes, "Above all, keep loving one another earnestly" (1 Pet 4:8). Love is not a lukewarm politeness.
- **The source of love:** the Lord Jesus said, "just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another" (John 13:34).

**2. "Imperishable seed."** "Since you have been born again, not of perishable seed but of imperishable, through the living and abiding word of God; for 'All flesh is like grass and all its glory like the flower of grass. The grass withers, and the flower falls, but the word of the Lord remains forever.' And this word is the good news that was preached to you" (1:23–25).
- **Back to new birth:** verse 3 says God "caused us to be born again"; here it is "through the living and abiding word of God." God brings new life into being through his word.
- **From Isaiah 40:** the exiles in Babylon thought God had forgotten them (Isa 40:27), but the prophet said that human glory withers like grass, while God's word stands forever (Isa 40:6–8).
- **Why after "love one another"?** Believers are brothers and sisters born of the same seed, with the same Father, so they are to love one another.

> The point: Believers have been born again through God's abiding word; they are children of one Father, and so they are to love one another earnestly from the heart. Human glory is like grass, but the word of God remains forever.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And if you call on him as Father who judges impartially according to each one's deeds, conduct yourselves with fear throughout the time of your exile, knowing that you were ransomed from the futile ways inherited from your forefathers, not with perishable things such as silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ" (1:17–19). Every family hands something down: not only a house and a surname, but a way of living. Perhaps in my family no one ever admitted being wrong; a quarrel was followed by days of cold silence, and no one spoke first; worries were kept quiet and only good news was shared; money was hidden, and so were wounds. As a child I promised myself I would never be like that. Then one day I found myself going silent for days, just as they did. Peter calls this "the futile ways inherited from your forefathers": passed down, taken for granted, and in the end empty. I thought that with enough resolve I could simply stop being like them, and years of resolve have not done it. Peter does not begin by telling me to try harder. He begins with something already done: I have been ransomed. I did not pay the price, and the price was not silver or gold, which cannot buy back a single heart. God paid "the precious blood of Christ, like that of a lamb without blemish or spot." That Lamb "was foreknown before the foundation of the world" (1:20); before my family's old ways ever began, God had resolved to buy me back. So when I turn from these ways, I am not earning his welcome; I am leaving what no longer owns me. And the "fear" Peter speaks of is not dread that he will throw me away, but a refusal to treat lightly what he paid. If I have already handed these ways on to the next generation, that blood was shed for that part of me too, and the Lord who ransomed me can ransom them. Today I can name before him one inherited way I still live by, confess it as sin against him, ask his forgiveness, and ask him to change me in his own way. Then I can stop and simply look at the Lamb, whose blood is more precious than all silver and gold, so that my faith and hope are in God (1:21).
