## 本章脉络

第二章可以分成四段。**爱慕灵奶**（1-3节）：除去一切的恶毒、诡诈、假善、嫉妒和毁谤，像才生的婴孩爱慕奶一样爱慕纯净的灵奶。**活石和灵宫**（4-10节）：主是活石，被人所弃，却被神所拣选、所宝贵；我们也像活石，被建造成为灵宫，作圣洁的祭司；我们是被拣选的族类，是有君尊的祭司，是属神的子民。**寄居的人在世上**（11-17节）：要禁戒肉体的私欲；在外邦人中品行端正；为主的缘故顺服人的一切制度；作自由人，也作神的仆人；尊敬众人，亲爱弟兄，敬畏神，尊敬君王。**跟随基督的脚踪**（18-25节）：作仆人的要顺服主人，若因行善受苦而忍耐，在神看是可喜爱的；因为基督也为我们受过苦，给我们留下榜样；他被挂在木头上，亲身担当了我们的罪；我们从前像迷路的羊，如今归到灵魂的牧人那里。

第1章讲我们是神重生的儿女，第2章讲我们是神所建造的一家人，是他的祭司，也是他在世上的见证人。这一章的中心是基督：他是房角石（6节），是受苦的榜样（21节），更是担当我们罪的那一位（24节）。

## 深读 2:1-3 爱慕灵奶

**「所以，你们既除去一切的恶毒、诡诈，并假善、嫉妒，和一切毁谤的话，就要爱慕那纯净的灵奶，像才生的婴孩爱慕奶一样，叫你们因此渐长，以致得救。你们若尝过主恩的滋味，就必如此。」**（2:1-3）
- **「所以」：** 接着1:22-25。我们既是藉着神的道重生的，就要继续靠神的道长大。
- **先除去：** 恶毒、诡诈、假善、嫉妒、毁谤，这五样都是破坏彼此相爱的罪（1:22）。
- **「灵奶」：** 这里不是说初信者只能喝奶、不能吃干粮（像来5:12那样），而是说所有的信徒都要像婴孩一样，渴慕神的话。婴孩饿了不会客气，会哭着要奶。
- **「尝过主恩的滋味」：** 引自诗篇34:8：「你们要尝尝主恩的滋味，便知道他是美善」。诗篇里的「主」是耶和华，彼得紧接着说「主乃活石」，指的就是耶稣。爱慕神的话，是因为已经尝过主是美善的。「你的言语在我上膛何等甘美，在我口中比蜜更甜！」（诗119:103）

> 要点：神用他的道生了我们，也用他的道养育我们。尝过主恩滋味的人，会像婴孩渴慕奶一样渴慕神的话。

## 深读 2:4-10 活石和君尊的祭司

**1. 「主乃活石」。** 「主乃活石，固然是被人所弃的，却是被神所拣选、所宝贵的。你们来到主面前，也就像活石，被建造成为灵宫，作圣洁的祭司，藉着耶稣基督奉献神所悦纳的灵祭。」（2:4-5）
- **「活石」：** 石头本来是死的，基督却是活的石头，因为他从死里复活了。彼得自己的名字就是「石头」（约1:42），可是他把人引向那块房角石：基督。他也曾在公会面前引用诗篇118:22讲论耶稣（徒4:11），正如主自己所引用的（太21:42）。
- **被人所弃，被神所宝贵：** 人对基督的评价，和神对他的评价完全相反。读者也是这样：被邻舍看不起，却被神宝贵。
- **「灵宫」：** 神的殿不再是耶路撒冷的一座建筑，而是信徒组成的群体（参林前3:16）。每一块石头都不同，却被建造在一起。
- **「灵祭」：** 不再是牛羊的祭，而是把身体献上当作活祭（罗12:1），「以颂赞为祭」（来13:15），「行善和捐输」（来13:16）。这一切都要「藉着耶稣基督」，因为只有他的祭能使我们的献上蒙悦纳。

**2. 房角石和绊脚石。** 「因为经上说：看哪，我把所拣选、所宝贵的房角石安放在锡安；信靠他的人必不至于羞愧。所以，他在你们信的人就为宝贵，在那不信的人有话说：匠人所弃的石头已作了房角的头块石头。又说：作了绊脚的石头，跌人的磐石。他们既不顺从，就在道理上绊跌；他们这样绊跌也是预定的。」（2:6-8）
- **三处旧约经文：** 以赛亚书28:16（房角石），诗篇118:22（匠人所弃的石头），以赛亚书8:14（绊脚的石头）。同一块石头，对信的人是宝贵的根基，对不信的人是叫人跌倒的石头。和合本以赛亚书28:16作「信靠的人必不着急」，彼得是照七十士译本引用，作「必不至于羞愧」。
- **「他们这样绊跌也是预定的」：** 这句话有两种主要理解。第一种（改革宗传统，如加尔文）：神不但定了不信的人必要绊跌，也把他们这不顺从的绊跌包括在他的旨意之中；然而他们绊跌的原因仍是自己「不顺从」。第二种（阿米念传统，如卫斯理宗的解经家）：神所预定的是不顺从的人必要绊跌这个结果，而不是预定哪些人不顺从。两种看法都同意：人要为自己的不信负完全的责任，神仍然掌管一切。请留意，紧接着第9节，彼得就说信徒是「被拣选的族类」。

**3. 「惟有你们是被拣选的族类」。** 「惟有你们是被拣选的族类，是有君尊的祭司，是圣洁的国度，是属神的子民，要叫你们宣扬那召你们出黑暗入奇妙光明者的美德。你们从前算不得子民，现在却作了神的子民；从前未曾蒙怜恤，现在却蒙了怜恤。」（2:9-10）
- **以色列的称呼：** 这些称呼来自旧约两处经文：西乃山上神对以色列说「你们要归我作祭司的国度，为圣洁的国民」，又要「作属我的子民」（出19:5-6）；以赛亚书说到新的出埃及时，神称以色列为「我的选民」，是「我为自己所造的，好述说我的美德」（赛43:20-21）。彼得把它们用在大多是外邦人的教会身上。
- **「有君尊的祭司」：** 「祭司」原文是一个集体名词，指一群祭司、一个祭司的团体：我们是一同被建造、一同事奉的。旧约只有利未支派亚伦的后裔可以作祭司；如今每一个信徒都属于这祭司的群体，都可以藉着基督来到神面前（参来10:19-22），也都要为别人代求、事奉神。马丁·路德引用这节经文教导所有基督徒都是祭司，后人称之为信徒皆祭司。这不是说教会不需要牧者和长老（5:1-4），而是说没有一个信徒是「旁观者」。
- **「宣扬……美德」：** 祭司的职分不只是献祭，也是见证：宣扬那召我们出黑暗入光明的神。
- **何西阿的应许：** 「本非我民的，我必对他说：你是我的民。」（何2:23；参1:9）何西阿原本是应许神要重新收纳背约的以色列（何1:10-11）；保罗把这话用在「不但是从犹太人中，也是从外邦人中」蒙召的人身上（罗9:24-26），彼得也是这样用。
- **以色列和教会：** 这些以色列的称呼怎样用在教会身上，福音派有不同的理解：改革宗（盟约神学）传统一般认为，在基督里由犹太人和外邦人组成的教会，就是神那一个子民的延续和成全；时代论传统（包括渐进时代论）一般认为，教会在基督里同享这些称号和福分，但神对民族以色列的应许将来还要应验（参罗11:25-29）。两种看法都同意：外邦信徒如今在基督里完全是神的子民，并不是二等的。

> 要点：被人所弃的基督，是神所宝贵的房角石。我们这些从前算不得子民的人，因着神的怜恤，成了他的祭司和子民，要宣扬他的美德。

## 深读 2:11-17 寄居的人在世上

**1. 「你们是客旅，是寄居的」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，你们是客旅，是寄居的。我劝你们要禁戒肉体的私欲；这私欲是与灵魂争战的。你们在外邦人中，应当品行端正，叫那些毁谤你们是作恶的，因看见你们的好行为，便在鉴察的日子归荣耀给神。」（2:11-12）
- **客旅的生活：** 寄居的人不会把这里当作永久的家，所以不随从这里的私欲。
- **被毁谤的时候：** 早期教会常被人误会：稍后的文献记载，人说他们不拜神明，是无神论者；不参加城里的节庆，是不忠于国家；甚至误传他们在聚会中吃人肉（可能因为听说他们吃主的身体）。彼得也要他们准备好「以温柔、敬畏的心回答各人」（3:15），但他在这里强调：最有力的回应是好行为。
- **「在鉴察的日子」：** 可能是指神审判的日子（参赛10:3「降罚的日子」），那时连毁谤的人也不得不承认神；也可能是指神眷顾、拯救他们的日子，就是他们信主的时候（参路19:44「眷顾你的时候」）。主耶稣也说：「叫他们看见你们的好行为，便将荣耀归给你们在天上的父。」（太5:16）

**2. 「为主的缘故，要顺服人的一切制度」。** 「你们为主的缘故，要顺服人的一切制度，或是在上的君王，或是君王所派罚恶赏善的臣宰。因为神的旨意原是要你们行善，可以堵住那糊涂无知人的口。你们虽是自由的，却不可藉着自由遮盖恶毒，总要作神的仆人。务要尊敬众人，亲爱教中的弟兄，敬畏神，尊敬君王。」（2:13-17）
- **「为主的缘故」：** 顺服政府，不是因为政府是最高的权柄，而是因为神设立了它「罚恶赏善」（参罗13:1-4）。
- **顺服的界限：** 当政府命令人违背神的时候，彼得自己就说过：「听从你们，不听从神，这在神面前合理不合理，你们自己酌量吧！」（徒4:19）按照教会的传统，彼得写这封信时在位的君王是尼禄，彼得后来也是在尼禄手下殉道的。所以「顺服」不是盲从，也不是不能用合法的方式为自己和别人争取公义（保罗就曾以罗马公民的身份抗议不公平的对待，徒16:37；22:25）。
- **「自由」和「仆人」：** 信徒是自由的，因为不属于任何人，只属于神；正因为是神的仆人，所以甘心顺服、行善。
- **四句话：** 「尊敬众人，亲爱教中的弟兄，敬畏神，尊敬君王。」注意对象的不同：对众人和君王是「尊敬」，对弟兄是「亲爱」，只有对神是「敬畏」。君王值得尊敬，却不配得敬畏，因为他不是神。箴言说「你要敬畏耶和华与君王」（箴24:21），彼得却把两者分开：敬畏只归给神。所以下一节「存敬畏的心顺服主人」，一般解经家认为是指存着敬畏神的心（参19节「为叫良心对得住神」），不是把主人当作神来惧怕。

> 要点：信徒在世上是寄居的，却不是逃避的。我们为主的缘故顺服人间的制度，用好行为回应毁谤；但我们只敬畏神，当人的命令和神的命令冲突的时候，要听从神。

## 深读 2:18-25 跟随基督的脚踪

**1. 「就是那乖僻的也要顺服」。** 「你们作仆人的，凡事要存敬畏的心顺服主人；不但顺服那善良温和的，就是那乖僻的也要顺服。倘若人为叫良心对得住神，就忍受冤屈的苦楚，这是可喜爱的。你们若因犯罪受责打，能忍耐，有什么可夸的呢？但你们若因行善受苦，能忍耐，这在神看是可喜爱的。」（2:18-20）
- **当时的处境：** 这里的「仆人」原文是指家奴。在罗马帝国，奴隶几乎没有法律保障，常遭主人打骂。彼得不是在为奴隶制度辩护，而是在对一群无法改变处境的人说话：在这样的处境中，你们怎样跟随基督？按通常的理解，保罗也说：「若能以自由，就求自由更好。」（林前7:21）新约也吩咐作主人的「不要威吓他们」（弗6:9），要「公公平平地待仆人」（西4:1），并且把「抢人口」的列在罪中（提前1:10）。历史上虽然也有人误用这些经文为奴隶制度辩护，但许多基督徒正是从这些真理出发，起来反对奴隶制度。
- **今天怎样应用？** 在工作中受到不公平的对待，基督徒仍要尽忠职守，不以恶报恶。可是这段经文不是叫人留在虐待中：遭受家暴、性侵、人身威胁或违法剥削的人，应当先确保安全，可以离开，可以向警察、法院或劳工部门求助（罗13:4）；这不是不忍耐，而是寻求神所设立的保护。教会同工知道这样的事，应当协助受害者报警或求助，不可私下调解。教会绝不可用这段经文劝受害的人回到危险中，也不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩。
- **「可喜爱的」：** 原文就是「恩典」这个词。因行善受苦而忍耐的人，是在恩典中活着，神看见了。

**2. 「给你们留下榜样」。** 「你们蒙召原是为此；因基督也为你们受过苦，给你们留下榜样，叫你们跟随他的脚踪行。他并没有犯罪，口里也没有诡诈。他被骂不还口；受害不说威吓的话，只将自己交托那按公义审判人的主。」（2:21-23）
- **「榜样」：** 原文是小孩子学写字时照着描的字帖。我们一笔一笔地照着基督的样子写。
- **引自以赛亚书53章：** 「口中也没有诡诈」（赛53:9）。耶稣在大祭司面前受审的时候，「却不言语」（太26:63），那时彼得就在大祭司的院子里（可14:54；路22:61）。他后来称自己是「作基督受苦的见证」的（彼前5:1）。
- **不还口，却不是不说话：** 耶稣被大祭司的差役打的时候，曾经说：「我若说的不是，你可以指证那不是；我若说的是，你为什么打我呢？」（约18:23）他不报复，却也指出不公义。
- **「交托那按公义审判人的主」：** 基督不报复，不是因为不公义不要紧，而是因为他把审判交给了神。

**3. 「亲身担当了我们的罪」。** 「他被挂在木头上，亲身担当了我们的罪，使我们既然在罪上死，就得以在义上活。因他受的鞭伤，你们便得了医治。你们从前好像迷路的羊，如今却归到你们灵魂的牧人监督了。」（2:24-25）
- **不只是榜样：** 如果基督只是榜样，我们永远学不像。可是彼得紧接着说，他「亲身担当了我们的罪」。他不只给我们看怎样受苦，更替我们受了我们该受的刑罚。「哪知他为我们的过犯受害，为我们的罪孽压伤……因他受的鞭伤，我们得医治。」（赛53:5）以赛亚书也说：「他却担当多人的罪」（赛53:12），彼得用的就是这个「担当」。这里的「医治」，主要是指从罪中得医治，不是应许身体的病一定得医治。
- **「被挂在木头上」：** 这叫人想起申命记21:23，被挂在木头上的人是被咒诅的；彼得讲道时也说耶稣是被「挂在木头上」杀的（徒5:30；10:39）。基督替我们成了咒诅（参加3:13）。
- **对奴隶说的话：** 彼得对那些常常被鞭打的奴隶说：「因他受的鞭伤，你们便得了医治。」他们身上的伤痕，基督也受过；而基督的伤，医治了他们最深的伤：罪。
- **「灵魂的牧人监督」：** 「我们都如羊走迷；各人偏行己路」（赛53:6），如今却回到了好牧人那里。

> 要点：基督在受苦中给我们留下榜样，但他首先是我们的代替者：他被挂在木头上，亲身担当了我们的罪。我们能跟随他的脚踪，是因为他先把我们这迷路的羊带了回来。

## 默想

**默想：** 「惟有你们是被拣选的族类，是有君尊的祭司，是圣洁的国度，是属神的子民，要叫你们宣扬那召你们出黑暗入奇妙光明者的美德。」（2:9）我常常觉得，事奉神是牧师和长老的事。他们讲道、探访、带查经；我只是坐在下面的会友，每个主日来，听完道回家。周一到周五，我在公司开会、算账、教书、开车、带孩子，这些好像和神没什么关系，只是「世上的事」。彼得却对一群奴隶、妻子和普通人说：你们都是「有君尊的祭司」。在旧约，只有少数人可以进到神面前；如今，因为那位大祭司（参来4:14）「亲身担当了我们的罪」（2:24），连我也可以藉着他来到神面前。我的祭司职分，不是在讲台上，而是在我所在的地方：在饭桌上为家人祷告，在办公室里诚实做事，在病床边陪伴一个人，在公车上为身边的陌生人默默代求。「无论做什么，都要从心里做，像是给主做的。」（西3:23）这些「灵祭」，单靠我自己是不够格献上的，可是彼得说，它们是「藉着耶稣基督」蒙神悦纳的（2:5）。我从前在黑暗里，算不得子民，也未曾蒙怜恤；如今却被召进入他奇妙的光明。我不是一个旁观的会友，而是一个祭司，在这个星期的每一天，宣扬他的美德。主啊，你召我出黑暗入奇妙光明，愿我这一生都宣扬你的美德。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 falls into four parts. **Longing for spiritual milk** (vv. 1–3): put away all malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy and slander, and like newborn infants long for pure spiritual milk. **Living stones and a spiritual house** (vv. 4–10): the Lord is a living stone, rejected by men but chosen and precious to God; we too, like living stones, are being built up as a spiritual house and a holy priesthood; we are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a people for God's own possession. **Exiles in the world** (vv. 11–17): abstain from the passions of the flesh; keep your conduct honorable among the Gentiles; be subject for the Lord's sake to every human institution; live as free people and as servants of God; honor everyone, love the brotherhood, fear God, honor the emperor. **Following in Christ's steps** (vv. 18–25): servants are to be subject to their masters, and if they endure when they suffer for doing good, this is a gracious thing in God's sight; for Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example; he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree; we were straying like sheep, but have now returned to the Shepherd of our souls.

Chapter 1 spoke of us as God's children born again; chapter 2 speaks of us as the household God is building, his priests and his witnesses in the world. At the center of the chapter is Christ: he is the cornerstone (v. 6), the example in suffering (v. 21), and above all the one who bore our sins (v. 24).

## Going Deeper: 2:1–3 Longing for Spiritual Milk

**"So put away all malice and all deceit and hypocrisy and envy and all slander. Like newborn infants, long for the pure spiritual milk, that by it you may grow up to salvation, if indeed you have tasted that the Lord is good"** (2:1–3).
- **"So":** this follows from 1:22–25. Since we have been born again through God's word, we are to keep growing through God's word.
- **First put away:** malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy and slander are all sins that destroy love for one another (1:22).
- **"Spiritual milk":** this is not saying that new believers can only drink milk and not eat solid food (as in Heb 5:12), but that all believers should long for God's word like infants. A hungry baby is not polite about it; he cries for milk.
- **"Tasted that the Lord is good":** from Psalm 34:8. In the psalm "the LORD" is Yahweh; Peter goes straight on to speak of Jesus as the living stone. We long for God's word because we have already tasted that the Lord is good. "How sweet are your words to my taste, sweeter than honey to my mouth!" (Ps 119:103).

> The point: God brought us to birth by his word, and he feeds us by his word. Those who have tasted that the Lord is good will long for his word as infants long for milk.

## Going Deeper: 2:4–10 Living Stones and a Royal Priesthood

**1. "A living stone."** "As you come to him, a living stone rejected by men but in the sight of God chosen and precious, you yourselves like living stones are being built up as a spiritual house, to be a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ" (2:4–5).
- **"A living stone":** stones are dead things, but Christ is a living stone, because he has risen from the dead. Peter's own name means "rock" (John 1:42), yet he points his readers to the cornerstone, Christ. He had preached Psalm 118:22 about Jesus before the council (Acts 4:11), as the Lord himself had quoted it (Matt 21:42).
- **Rejected by men, precious to God:** people's verdict on Christ is the very opposite of God's. So too for the readers: despised by their neighbors, precious to God.
- **"A spiritual house":** God's temple is no longer a building in Jerusalem but the community of believers (see 1 Cor 3:16). Every stone is different, yet they are built together.
- **"Spiritual sacrifices":** no longer the sacrifice of cattle and sheep, but presenting our bodies as a living sacrifice (Rom 12:1), "a sacrifice of praise" (Heb 13:15), "to do good and to share what you have" (Heb 13:16). All this is "through Jesus Christ," because only his sacrifice makes our offerings acceptable.

**2. Cornerstone and stumbling stone.** "For it stands in Scripture: 'Behold, I am laying in Zion a stone, a cornerstone chosen and precious, and whoever believes in him will not be put to shame.' So the honor is for you who believe, but for those who do not believe, 'The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone,' and 'A stone of stumbling, and a rock of offense.' They stumble because they disobey the word, as they were destined to do" (2:6–8).
- **Three Old Testament texts:** Isaiah 28:16 (the cornerstone), Psalm 118:22 (the stone the builders rejected), Isaiah 8:14 (the stone of stumbling). The same stone is a precious foundation for those who believe and a stone that trips those who do not. Peter follows the Greek Old Testament of Isaiah 28:16 ("will not be put to shame"); the Hebrew has "will not be in haste."
- **"As they were destined to do":** there are two main readings. First (the Reformed tradition, e.g., Calvin): God appointed not only that unbelievers would stumble but included their disobedient stumbling within his purpose, while the cause of their stumbling remains their own "disobedience." Second (the Arminian tradition, e.g., Wesleyan interpreters): what God appointed was the outcome, that the disobedient would stumble, not which people would disobey. Both agree that people are fully responsible for their unbelief and that God still rules over all. Notice that in the very next verse Peter calls believers "a chosen race."

**3. "But you are a chosen race."** "But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light. Once you were not a people, but now you are God's people; once you had not received mercy, but now you have received mercy" (2:9–10).
- **Israel's names:** these titles come from two Old Testament passages: at Sinai God told Israel, "you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" and "my treasured possession" (Exod 19:5–6); and in Isaiah's promise of a new exodus God calls Israel "my chosen people... that they might declare my praise" (Isa 43:20–21). Peter applies them to a church made up mostly of Gentiles.
- **"A royal priesthood":** "priesthood" (*hierateuma*) is a collective noun, a body of priests: we are built and serve together. In the Old Testament only Aaron's descendants from the tribe of Levi could serve as priests; now every believer belongs to this priesthood and may come to God through Christ (see Heb 10:19–22), and is called to pray for others and serve God. Martin Luther cited this verse to teach that all Christians are priests, a teaching later summed up as "the priesthood of all believers." This does not mean the church needs no pastors or elders (5:1–4); it means no believer is a spectator.
- **"Proclaim the excellencies":** a priest's office is not only to offer sacrifices but to bear witness, proclaiming the God who called us out of darkness into light.
- **Hosea's promise:** "And I will say to Not My People, 'You are my people'" (Hos 2:23; see 1:9). Hosea first promised that God would take back covenant-breaking Israel (Hos 1:10–11); Paul applies the words to those called "not from the Jews only but also from the Gentiles" (Rom 9:24–26), and Peter does the same.
- **Israel and the church:** evangelicals understand differently how Israel's titles apply to the church. The Reformed (covenantal) tradition generally sees the church of Jew and Gentile in Christ as the continuation and fulfillment of the one people of God; the dispensational tradition (including progressive dispensationalism) generally holds that the church shares these titles and blessings in Christ while God's promises to ethnic Israel still await future fulfillment (see Rom 11:25–29). Both agree that Gentile believers are now fully God's people in Christ, not second-class members.

> The point: Christ, rejected by men, is the cornerstone precious to God. We who once were not a people have, by God's mercy, become his priests and his people, to proclaim his excellencies.

## Going Deeper: 2:11–17 Exiles in the World

**1. "Sojourners and exiles."** "Beloved, I urge you as sojourners and exiles to abstain from the passions of the flesh, which wage war against your soul. Keep your conduct among the Gentiles honorable, so that when they speak against you as evildoers, they may see your good deeds and glorify God on the day of visitation" (2:11–12).
- **The sojourner's life:** someone passing through does not treat this place as a permanent home, and so does not follow its passions.
- **When slandered:** the early church was often misunderstood: somewhat later sources record that Christians were called atheists for not worshiping the gods, disloyal for not joining civic festivals, even cannibals (perhaps from hearing that they "ate the Lord's body"). Peter does want them ready to "make a defense" with gentleness and respect (3:15), but here he stresses that the strongest answer is good deeds.
- **"The day of visitation":** this may be the day of God's judgment (see Isa 10:3, "the day of punishment"), when even slanderers will have to acknowledge God; or it may be the day God visits them in mercy, when they come to faith (see Luke 19:44, "the time of your visitation"). The Lord Jesus also said, "let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father who is in heaven" (Matt 5:16).

**2. "Be subject for the Lord's sake to every human institution."** "Be subject for the Lord's sake to every human institution, whether it be to the emperor as supreme, or to governors as sent by him to punish those who do evil and to praise those who do good. For this is the will of God, that by doing good you should put to silence the ignorance of foolish people. Live as people who are free, not using your freedom as a cover-up for evil, but living as servants of God. Honor everyone. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the emperor" (2:13–17).
- **"For the Lord's sake":** we submit to government not because it is the highest authority, but because God has set it in place "to punish those who do evil and to praise those who do good" (see Rom 13:1–4).
- **The limits of submission:** when the authorities command what God forbids, Peter himself said, "Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, you must judge" (Acts 4:19). According to church tradition, the emperor when Peter wrote was Nero, under whom Peter was later martyred. So "be subject" is not blind obedience, nor does it rule out seeking justice for oneself and others by lawful means (Paul protested unjust treatment as a Roman citizen, Acts 16:37; 22:25).
- **"Free" and "servants":** believers are free because they belong to no one but God; precisely as God's servants, they willingly submit and do good.
- **Four commands:** "Honor everyone. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the emperor." Notice the differences: everyone and the emperor are to be "honored," the brothers "loved," and only God "feared." The emperor deserves honor, but not fear, for he is not God. Proverbs says, "fear the LORD and the king" (Prov 24:21), but Peter separates the two: fear belongs to God alone. So in the next verse, "with all respect" (Greek "in all fear") is generally taken to mean fear of God (compare "mindful of God," v. 19), not fearing the master as if he were God.

> The point: Believers are exiles in the world, but not escapists. For the Lord's sake we submit to human institutions and answer slander with good deeds; but we fear God alone, and when human commands clash with God's, we obey God.

## Going Deeper: 2:18–25 Following in Christ's Steps

**1. "Also to the unjust."** "Servants, be subject to your masters with all respect, not only to the good and gentle but also to the unjust. For this is a gracious thing, when, mindful of God, one endures sorrows while suffering unjustly. For what credit is it if, when you sin and are beaten for it, you endure? But if when you do good and suffer for it you endure, this is a gracious thing in the sight of God" (2:18–20).
- **The setting:** "servants" here means household slaves. In the Roman Empire slaves had almost no legal protection and were often beaten by their masters. Peter is not defending slavery; he is speaking to people who could not change their situation: in this situation, how will you follow Christ? On the usual reading, Paul also says, "if you can gain your freedom, avail yourself of the opportunity" (1 Cor 7:21). The New Testament also commands masters to "stop your threatening" (Eph 6:9) and to "treat your slaves justly and fairly" (Col 4:1), and it lists "enslavers" among sinners (1 Tim 1:10). Although these texts were also misused to defend slavery, many Christians, starting from these very truths, rose up to oppose it.
- **How does it apply today?** When treated unfairly at work, Christians are still to do their work faithfully and not repay evil for evil. But this passage does not tell anyone to stay in abuse. Those who suffer domestic violence, sexual assault, threats to their safety or unlawful exploitation should first get to safety and may leave, and may seek help from the police, the courts or labor authorities (Rom 13:4). This is not a failure to endure; it is seeking the protection God has provided. Church leaders who learn of such things should help the victim report it and seek help, not settle it privately. The church must never use this passage to send victims back into danger, nor cover things up because "family shame should stay in the family."
- **"A gracious thing":** the Greek word is "grace." Whoever endures suffering for doing good is living in grace, and God sees it.

**2. "Leaving you an example."** "For to this you have been called, because Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example, so that you might follow in his steps. He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth. When he was reviled, he did not revile in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but continued entrusting himself to him who judges justly" (2:21–23).
- **"Example":** the word means the model letters a child traces when learning to write. We copy the shape of Christ, stroke by stroke.
- **From Isaiah 53:** "there was no deceit in his mouth" (Isa 53:9). When Jesus was tried before the high priest, he "remained silent" (Matt 26:63), and Peter was there in the high priest's courtyard (Mark 14:54; Luke 22:61). He later calls himself "a witness of the sufferings of Christ" (1 Pet 5:1).
- **No retaliation, but not silence:** when an officer of the high priest struck him, Jesus said, "If what I said is wrong, bear witness about the wrong; but if what I said is right, why do you strike me?" (John 18:23). He did not retaliate, but he did name injustice.
- **"Entrusting himself to him who judges justly":** Christ did not retaliate, not because injustice did not matter, but because he left the judgment to God.

**3. "He himself bore our sins."** "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, that we might die to sin and live to righteousness. By his wounds you have been healed. For you were straying like sheep, but have now returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls" (2:24–25).
- **Not only an example:** if Christ were only an example, we could never copy him well enough. But Peter goes straight on: he "himself bore our sins." He did not only show us how to suffer; he suffered in our place the penalty we deserved. "But he was wounded for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities... and with his stripes we are healed" (Isa 53:5). Isaiah also says, "yet he bore the sin of many" (Isa 53:12); Peter uses the same word, "bore." The "healing" here is mainly healing from sin, not a promise that every bodily illness will be healed.
- **"On the tree":** this recalls Deuteronomy 21:23: one hanged on a tree is cursed; Peter's own preaching spoke of Jesus killed "by hanging him on a tree" (Acts 5:30; 10:39). Christ became a curse for us (see Gal 3:13).
- **Words to slaves:** to slaves who were often beaten, Peter says, "By his wounds you have been healed." Christ bore the same marks they bore, and his wounds healed their deepest wound: sin.
- **"The Shepherd and Overseer of your souls":** "All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own way" (Isa 53:6), but now we have come back to the good Shepherd.

> The point: In his suffering Christ left us an example, but first he is our substitute: he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree. We can follow in his steps because he first brought us, straying sheep, back home.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light" (2:9). I often feel that serving God is for pastors and elders. They preach, visit and lead Bible study; I am only a church member sitting in the pew, coming every Sunday and going home after the sermon. Monday to Friday I sit in meetings, do accounts, teach, drive, look after the children, and none of it seems to have much to do with God; it is just "worldly business." But Peter says to a group of slaves, wives and ordinary people: you are all "a royal priesthood." In the Old Testament only a few could come near to God; now, because the great High Priest (see Heb 4:14) "himself bore our sins" (2:24), even I can come to God through him. My priesthood is not in the pulpit but wherever I am: praying for my family at the dinner table, working honestly at the office, sitting beside someone's hospital bed, quietly praying for the stranger next to me on the bus. "Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord" (Col 3:23). On my own I am not fit to offer any of these "spiritual sacrifices," but Peter says they are acceptable to God "through Jesus Christ" (2:5). Once I was in darkness, not a people, without mercy; now I have been called into his marvelous light. I am not a spectator in the pew but a priest, proclaiming his excellencies every day of this week. Lord, you called me out of darkness into your marvelous light; may my whole life proclaim your excellencies.
