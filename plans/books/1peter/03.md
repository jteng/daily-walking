## 本章脉络

第三章可以分成四段。**妻子和丈夫**（1-7节）：作妻子的要顺服自己的丈夫，好叫不信的丈夫因妻子的品行被感化；不要只以外面的妆饰为美，要以里面温柔、安静的心为妆饰；作丈夫的要按情理和妻子同住，敬重她，因为她与你一同承受生命之恩。**彼此相待**（8-12节）：都要同心，彼此体恤，相爱如弟兄；不以恶报恶，倒要祝福，正如诗篇34篇所说。**为义受苦**（13-17节）：为义受苦也是有福的；不要怕人的威吓，只要心里尊主基督为圣；有人问你们心中盼望的缘由，就要以温柔、敬畏的心回答。**基督受苦，又得胜**（18-22节）：基督也曾一次为罪受苦，义的代替不义的，为要引我们到神面前；他藉着灵向监狱里的灵宣告；挪亚时代的八个人藉着水得救，这水所表明的洗礼，现在藉着耶稣基督复活也拯救我们；他已经进入天堂，在神的右边，众天使和有权柄的都服从了他。

第2章末了讲仆人怎样跟随基督的脚踪，第3章接着讲家里和教会里的关系，然后又回到受苦。全章的高峰是第18节：信徒能在不公平的处境中行善、祝福人，是因为基督已经「义的代替不义的」受了苦，而且他已经得胜，坐在神的右边。

## 深读 3:1-7 妻子和丈夫

**1. 「不听道，也可以因妻子的品行被感化过来」。** 「你们作妻子的要顺服自己的丈夫；这样，若有不信从道理的丈夫，他们虽然不听道，也可以因妻子的品行被感化过来；这正是因看见你们有贞洁的品行和敬畏的心。」（3:1-2）
- **当时的处境：** 在罗马社会，妻子通常要跟从丈夫的宗教。一个妻子信了耶稣，丈夫却不信，这在当时是很不寻常的，也常常带来家庭冲突。彼得没有叫她放弃信仰，而是叫她在家里活出信仰来。
- **「不听道」：** 有些丈夫听不进妻子讲的道理。彼得的意思是：那就不必天天讲，让生活说话。许多华人教会里信主的姊妹，都在这样的家庭中多年祷告、默默见证。「可以」不是保证。丈夫信不信，不在于妻子做得够不够好；叫人回转的是神。她的见证在神面前从来不是白费的。
- **「顺服」的界限：** 妻子在信仰上已经没有跟从丈夫，可见顺服不是在一切事上听丈夫的。顺服从来不是叫妻子跟着丈夫去犯罪（徒5:29），也绝不是叫她忍受虐待。如果婚姻中有家暴、性暴力、威胁或控制，向警察或法院求助是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人可以离开，到安全的地方去，不必为了「顺服」或为了感化丈夫而留在危险中。对方若没有真实悔改，安全也没有保障，她不必与他和好。教会的责任是保护受害者，绝不可为了面子、「家丑不可外扬」而劝她忍耐。

**2. 「里面……温柔、安静的心」。** 「你们不要以外面的辫头发，戴金饰，穿美衣为妆饰，只要以里面存着长久温柔、安静的心为妆饰；这在神面前是极宝贵的。」（3:3-4）
- **不是禁止打扮：** 彼得不是说女人不可以梳头、戴首饰、穿好衣服，而是说不要把这些当作自己真正的美。
- **「长久」：** 原文是「不能朽坏」，和1:4的基业、1:23的种子同一个词。外表的美会衰残，里面的美却能存到永远。
- **「温柔、安静」：** 不是说要软弱、没有主见，而是心里安稳，因为她把自己交托给神。

**3. 「撒拉的女儿」。** 「因为古时仰赖神的圣洁妇人正是以此为妆饰，顺服自己的丈夫，就如撒拉听从亚伯拉罕，称他为主。你们若行善，不因恐吓而害怕，便是撒拉的女儿了。」（3:5-6）
- **「称他为主」：** 撒拉在创世记18:12心里称亚伯拉罕为「我主」，这在当时是对丈夫的尊称。
- **「不因恐吓而害怕」：** 这句话很重要。彼得知道，这些妻子可能面对丈夫或社会的恐吓。他没有叫她们在恐惧中屈服，而是叫她们不要害怕，继续行善，因为她们是「仰赖神」的人。

**4. 「与你一同承受生命之恩」。** 「你们作丈夫的也要按情理和妻子同住；因她比你软弱，与你一同承受生命之恩的，所以要敬重她。这样，便叫你们的祷告没有阻碍。」（3:7）
- **对丈夫的要求：** 在当时的社会，丈夫对妻子几乎有完全的权力。彼得却要丈夫「按情理」（原文直译是：按着知识，就是了解她、体贴她）和妻子同住，又要「敬重她」。
- **「比你软弱」：** 原文直译是：较软弱的器皿。多数解经家认为，这主要是指身体的力量，也包括妻子在当时社会和法律上的弱势地位，不是说女人在灵性、智力或价值上比男人低。「较」字是比较的说法，表示丈夫同样是器皿，同样是瓦器。正因为她较容易受伤害，丈夫更要保护她、尊重她。
- **「一同承受生命之恩」：** 在神面前，妻子和丈夫完全平等，同为后嗣（参加3:28）。
- **「祷告没有阻碍」：** 一个苦待妻子的丈夫，在神面前的祷告是有阻碍的。神看重丈夫怎样待妻子。
- **不同的理解：** 历代忠心的信徒对夫妻角色有不同的看法。一种看法认为家庭中夫妻角色男女有别（常称为互补派，如韦恩·古德恩、托马斯·施莱纳）：彼得在这里所讲的顺服和敬重，是神为婚姻所定的次序，适用于各个时代。另一种看法认为家庭中夫妻角色不以性别区分（常称为平等派，如克雷格·基纳、斯科特·麦克奈特）：彼得是在当时的家庭结构中，教导信徒怎样作见证、不叫福音被毁谤，今天应用时要看处境。两种看法都同意：丈夫和妻子同样尊贵，同承生命之恩；丈夫不可辖制、苦待妻子；任何虐待都不能用这段经文来辩护。

> 要点：信主的妻子仰望神，就可以在不信的家庭里用生活作见证，不必在恐吓中害怕；丈夫要了解、敬重妻子，因为他们二人同是靠着生命之恩活着的。神看重夫妻怎样彼此对待，任何虐待都不是顺服的一部分。

## 深读 3:8-12 不以恶报恶

**1. 「总而言之」。** 「总而言之，你们都要同心，彼此体恤，相爱如弟兄，存慈怜谦卑的心。」（3:8）
- **对所有人说：** 前面对仆人、妻子、丈夫分别说话，现在对全教会说。五个词描写一个健康的教会：同心、体恤、相爱、慈怜、谦卑。

**2. 「倒要祝福」。** 「不以恶报恶，以辱骂还辱骂，倒要祝福；因你们是为此蒙召，好叫你们承受福气。」（3:9）
- **主耶稣的教训：** 「要爱你们的仇敌，为那逼迫你们的祷告。」（太5:44）
- **「为此蒙召」：** 和2:21一样。我们蒙召，是为了像基督那样，被骂不还口，反而祝福人。
- **祝福不是假装没事：** 祝福人不是说伤害不存在，也不是说不能寻求公义（参2:13-14）；而是不让仇恨在心里扎根，把报应交给神。

**3. 引诗篇34篇。** 「因为经上说：人若爱生命，愿享美福，须要禁止舌头不出恶言，嘴唇不说诡诈的话；也要离恶行善，寻求和睦，一心追赶。因为，主的眼看顾义人；主的耳听他们的祈祷。惟有行恶的人，主向他们变脸。」（3:10-12）
- **大卫在难中写的诗：** 诗篇34篇的标题说，这是大卫在亚比米勒面前装疯、被赶出去以后作的（撒上21:10-15称这王为迦特王亚吉；亚比米勒很可能是非利士王的称号，像埃及王称法老一样）。彼得在2:3已经暗引过这篇诗（「你们若尝过主恩的滋味」，参诗34:8）。大卫在逃难中仍然说：要离恶行善，寻求和睦。
- **神的眼和耳：** 受苦的人觉得没有人看见、没有人听见。彼得说，神的眼看顾义人，神的耳听他们的祷告。

> 要点：信徒蒙召，是要像基督那样，不以恶报恶，倒要祝福。我们能这样做，是因为神的眼看顾我们，神的耳听我们的祷告。

## 深读 3:13-17 为义受苦也是有福的

**1. 「不要怕人的威吓」。** 「你们若是热心行善，有谁害你们呢？你们就是为义受苦，也是有福的。不要怕人的威吓，也不要惊慌；只要心里尊主基督为圣。」（3:13-15上）
- **「有谁害你们呢？」：** 这不是说行善的人一定不会受苦，因为下一句就说「就是为义受苦」。意思是：人可以伤害我们的身体和名声，却不能夺走神赐的福。
- **「为义受苦……有福的」：** 这几乎就是主耶稣的话：「为义受逼迫的人有福了！」（太5:10）
- **引以赛亚书8章：** 「他们所怕的，你们不要怕……但要尊万军之耶和华为圣，以他为你们所当怕的。」（赛8:12-13）彼得把「万军之耶和华」换成「主基督」：可见在彼得心中，旧约的「万军之耶和华」就是主基督。这是新约清楚表明基督是神的经文之一。敬畏基督的人，就不必惧怕人。

**2. 「要常作准备」。** 「有人问你们心中盼望的缘由，就要常作准备，以温柔、敬畏的心回答各人；存着无亏的良心，叫你们在何事上被毁谤，就在何事上可以叫那诬赖你们在基督里有好品行的人自觉羞愧。」（3:15下-16）
- **「回答」：** 原文 *apologia* 常用在法庭上，指答辩；「护教学」（apologetics）一词就是从这里来的。不过这里不只是在法庭上，也是在日常生活中，任何人问起的时候。
- **「盼望的缘由」：** 别人问的不是「你为什么这么保守」，而是「你为什么在苦难中还有盼望」。信徒的盼望本身，就会引起人的好奇。
- **「以温柔、敬畏的心」：** 回答的态度和内容一样重要。在网上和人争论信仰，赢了道理却失了温柔，就不是彼得所说的回答。
- **「无亏的良心」：** 生活和言语一致，别人的毁谤就站不住。

**3. 「总强如因行恶受苦」。** 「神的旨意若是叫你们因行善受苦，总强如因行恶受苦。」（3:17）
- **「神的旨意若是」：** 受苦不是偶然的，也在神的旨意之中；但彼得用「若是」，表示这样的受苦可能会临到，却不是时时都有。
- **两种受苦：** 因行恶受苦是罪有应得；因行善受苦，却是跟随基督。

> 要点：为义受苦的人是有福的。我们不必惧怕人，因为我们心里尊主基督为圣；有人问起我们盼望的缘由，我们要以温柔、敬畏的心回答。

## 深读 3:18-22 基督受苦，又得胜

**1. 「义的代替不义的」。** 「因基督也曾一次为罪受苦，就是义的代替不义的，为要引我们到神面前。按着肉体说，他被治死；按着灵性说，他复活了。」（3:18）
- **「一次」：** 基督受苦只有一次，却永远有效，不需要重复（参来9:28）。
- **「义的代替不义的」：** 这是新约中最清楚说明代赎（基督代替我们受刑罚）的经文之一（参2:24「他被挂在木头上，亲身担当了我们的罪」）。我们是不义的，基督是义的；他替我们受了我们该受的刑罚。
- **「为要引我们到神面前」：** 救恩的目的不只是免去刑罚，而是把我们带到神面前，与神和好。救恩最大的礼物，就是神自己。我们本来因罪与神隔绝（赛59:2）；今天还在远处的人，可以凭信心来到这位代替我们的基督面前。
- **「按着灵性说，他复活了」：** 原文直译是：在灵里被叫活。多数解经家认为，这不是说只有基督的灵活过来，而是说他复活以后，进入了圣灵所赐、属于新时代的生命（参罗1:4）。他的身体也真实地复活了（路24:39）。

**2. 「去传道给那些在监狱里的灵听」。** 「他藉这灵曾去传道给那些在监狱里的灵听，就是那从前在挪亚预备方舟、神容忍等待的时候，不信从的人。当时进入方舟，藉着水得救的不多，只有八个人。」（3:19-20）
- **新约中最难解的经文之一：** 马丁·路德说，他不确定彼得在这里的意思。这里的「传道」原文是宣告（如传令官宣告），彼得没有用4:6「传福音」那个词；宣告的内容可以是福音，也可以是审判或得胜。历代主要有三种理解：
- **基督藉着挪亚传道：** 基督的灵曾藉着挪亚，向挪亚时代不信从的人传悔改的道；那些人如今被拘禁在「监狱」中（如韦恩·古德恩；奥古斯丁则认为「监狱」是指他们当时在愚昧黑暗中的光景）。支持的理由是：彼得说「基督的灵」也曾在旧约先知里面（1:11），彼得后书也称挪亚是「传义道的」（彼后2:5）。
- **基督向堕落的天使宣告得胜：** 「灵」在新约中多半是指天使或邪灵这类灵界的活物，而犹太传统（如《以诺一书》）把创世记6:1-4和挪亚时代堕落的天使连在一起；彼得后书2:4和犹大书6也讲到被拘留的天使。按这种看法，基督在复活升天的时候，向这些邪灵宣告了他的得胜（许多现代解经家持这个看法，如威廉·道尔顿、托马斯·施莱纳）；他们也指出，第19节的「去」和第22节「进入天堂」的「进入」是同一个词。
- **基督在死后降到阴间：** 早期教会和天主教、东正教的传统多认为，基督在死与复活之间，降到死人之处，向旧约的死人宣告救恩（他们引4:6；弗4:9）。《使徒信经》中「降在阴间」一句（许多华人教会通用的中文译本没有译出这一句），各宗派的解释也不同。天主教和东正教多照这个意思理解；路德宗认为是基督向阴间宣告得胜。改革宗有两种解释：加尔文和《海德堡要理问答》第44问认为，这是指基督在客西马尼园和十字架上，承受了地狱般的痛苦；《威斯敏斯德大要理问答》第50问认为，这是指他在死人的状态中，处在死亡的权势之下，直到第三天。
- **无论哪一种：** 彼得的重点是清楚的：那位为我们受苦的基督已经得胜，一切灵界的权势都在他以下（3:22）。受苦的信徒不必惧怕。这段经文不是说人死后还有第二次悔改的机会（参来9:27）。

**3. 「这水所表明的洗礼」。** 「这水所表明的洗礼，现在藉着耶稣基督复活也拯救你们；这洗礼本不在乎除掉肉体的污秽，只求在神面前有无亏的良心。耶稣已经进入天堂，在神的右边；众天使和有权柄的，并有能力的，都服从了他。」（3:21-22）
- **挪亚和洗礼：** 挪亚一家藉着水从审判中被保全；信徒受洗，是与那位替我们承受审判、又从死里复活的基督联合（参罗6:3-4）。
- **「不在乎除掉肉体的污秽」：** 拯救人的不是水本身，不是外面的洗净。
- **「只求在神面前有无亏的良心」：** 这句话的原文不容易译。有人理解为向神求一个无亏的良心，也有人理解为向神立约，要存无亏的良心。无论哪一种，这半句所强调的是受洗的人向神的回应。
- **「藉着耶稣基督复活」：** 洗礼所以能拯救，全在于耶稣基督的复活；能力不在水，而在复活的主。
- **不同的理解：** 历代教会对洗礼「拯救」的意思有不同的理解。天主教认为，基督藉着洗礼使人重生，赦免原罪和一切本罪。路德宗认为，洗礼是神藉着道使人重生的施恩媒介，要藉着信心领受。改革宗认为，洗礼是恩典之约的记号和印证，圣灵藉着它把所表明的恩典赐给属他的人，要藉着信心领受。浸信会传统认为，洗礼是已经重生的信徒顺服主的命令、公开承认信仰的行动。各宗派都同意：救恩的根基是基督的死和复活，不是水本身，而洗礼是基督所吩咐的（太28:19）。
- **「在神的右边」：** 那位在地上受苦的基督，如今坐在宝座上，一切天使和权势都服从了他。

> 要点：基督一次为罪受苦，义的代替不义的，为要引我们到神面前。他已经复活、升天，一切权势都服在他以下；信徒的洗礼，表明我们已经与这位得胜的主联合。

## 默想

**默想：** 「这水所表明的洗礼，现在藉着耶稣基督复活也拯救你们……耶稣已经进入天堂，在神的右边；众天使和有权柄的，并有能力的，都服从了他。」（3:21-22）我信主好几年了，却一直没有受洗。我心里有许多理由：父母会很伤心，他们说「信教可以，受洗就是不要祖宗了」；亲戚会议论；我也怕受了洗，别人就会盯着我看，万一我跌倒了，岂不是更羞辱主的名？我想，等父母老了、想通了，等我自己更有把握了，再说吧。我告诉自己，心里信就够了，洗不洗只是一个形式。可是彼得写信给的这些人，正是因为公开承认基督，才被邻舍毁谤、被家人误会的。他没有对他们说「那就悄悄地信吧」，而是说：「不要怕人的威吓，也不要惊慌；只要心里尊主基督为圣。」（3:14-15）我怕的到底是什么？我怕失去家人的认可，怕丢面子，怕付代价。可是我怕付的代价，他早已用更深的方式付上了：「义的代替不义的」（3:18）。他没有在羞辱面前退缩，在众人面前被挂在木头上。他的亲属也曾出来要拉住他，说他「癫狂了」（可3:21；参约7:5）。他这样做，不是要我欠他一份勇气，而是「为要引我们到神面前」。家人的认可是真实而宝贵的，可是带我回家的从来不是它，而是他。如今他已经复活，「在神的右边」，连天上地下的一切权势都服从了他；我所怕的那些议论和眼光，没有一样在他之上。受洗不是叫我离开父母，而是叫我在父母面前，也在众人面前，承认我是属于这位主的。如果我在等自己更有把握、更像样一点，彼得说，洗礼拯救人，不在乎除掉肉体的污秽，而是「藉着耶稣基督复活」；它靠的是他已经成就的，不是我觉得自己准备得够不够。若我还没有受洗，我可以去找牧师谈一谈，求主给我智慧和勇气，也为父母祷告，用尊敬和温柔向他们说明。如果受洗会叫我面对真实的危险，比如我还未成年、住在家里，或家人可能动手，我可以和牧师商量时间和保护的办法；为智慧而等候，和因惧怕而躲藏，不是一回事。若我已经受洗了，我可以想起那一天：洗净我罪的不是水本身，而是基督的血；那一天所表明的，是我已经与一位得胜的主联合。若我是在小时候受的洗，记不得那一天，我仍可以记得那一天所指向的：是神先寻找了我。主耶稣，你义的代替不义的，为要引我到神面前；你已经坐在神的右边，一切权势都服了你。我心里还有惧怕；求你叫我心里尊你为圣，不怕人的威吓，因为我是你的。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 falls into four parts. **Wives and husbands** (vv. 1–7): wives are to be subject to their own husbands, so that unbelieving husbands may be won by their conduct; their adorning should not be merely outward, but the inner beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit; husbands are to live with their wives in an understanding way, showing them honor as heirs with them of the grace of life. **How to treat one another** (vv. 8–12): have unity of mind, sympathy, brotherly love; do not repay evil for evil, but bless, as Psalm 34 says. **Suffering for righteousness** (vv. 13–17): even if you suffer for righteousness' sake, you are blessed; have no fear of them, but in your hearts honor Christ the Lord as holy; be ready to give a reason for your hope with gentleness and respect. **Christ suffered and triumphed** (vv. 18–22): Christ suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring us to God; in the spirit he proclaimed to the spirits in prison; eight persons were brought safely through water in Noah's day, and baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you through the resurrection of Jesus Christ; he has gone into heaven and is at God's right hand, with angels and powers subject to him.

Chapter 2 ended with servants following in Christ's steps; chapter 3 goes on to relationships at home and in the church, then returns to suffering. The summit of the chapter is verse 18: believers can do good and bless others in unjust circumstances because Christ has already suffered, "the righteous for the unrighteous," and because he has triumphed and sits at God's right hand.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–7 Wives and Husbands

**1. "They may be won without a word by the conduct of their wives."** "Likewise, wives, be subject to your own husbands, so that even if some do not obey the word, they may be won without a word by the conduct of their wives, when they see your respectful and pure conduct" (3:1–2).
- **The setting:** in Roman society a wife was expected to follow her husband's religion. A wife who believed in Jesus while her husband did not was unusual, and it often brought conflict at home. Peter does not tell her to give up her faith, but to live it out at home.
- **"Without a word":** some husbands will not listen to their wives' arguments. Peter says: then you need not argue every day; let your life speak. Many believing sisters in Chinese churches have prayed for years in homes like this, bearing quiet witness. "May" is not a guarantee. Whether a husband believes does not depend on whether his wife has done well enough; it is God who turns hearts, and her witness is never wasted in his sight.
- **The limits of submission:** this wife has already not followed her husband in religion, so submission plainly does not mean obeying him in everything. Submission never means following a husband into sin (Acts 5:29), and it never means enduring abuse. If there is domestic violence, sexual violence, threats or coercive control in a marriage, seeking help from the police or the courts is right (Rom 13:4). The victim may leave for a safe place; she need not stay in danger for the sake of "submission" or of "winning her husband," nor reconcile with him until there is real repentance and safety is assured. The church's duty is to protect the victim, and it must never urge her to endure in order to save face ("family shame should stay in the family").

**2. "The hidden person of the heart... a gentle and quiet spirit."** "Do not let your adorning be external, the braiding of hair, the wearing of gold, or the putting on of clothing, but let your adorning be the hidden person of the heart with the imperishable beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit, which in God's sight is very precious" (3:3–4).
- **Not a ban on grooming:** Peter is not saying women may not braid their hair, wear jewelry or dress well, but that these should not be treated as their true beauty.
- **"Imperishable":** the same word as the inheritance of 1:4 and the seed of 1:23. Outward beauty fades; inner beauty lasts forever.
- **"Gentle and quiet":** this does not mean weak or without a mind of one's own, but settled in heart, because she has entrusted herself to God.

**3. "Sarah's children."** "For this is how the holy women who hoped in God used to adorn themselves, by submitting to their husbands, as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord. And you are her children, if you do good and do not fear anything that is frightening" (3:5–6).
- **"Calling him lord":** in Genesis 18:12 Sarah thinks of Abraham as "my lord," a respectful way of referring to a husband at the time.
- **"Do not fear anything that is frightening":** this sentence matters. Peter knows these wives may face intimidation from their husbands or from society. He does not tell them to give way in fear, but not to be afraid, and to go on doing good, because they are women who "hoped in God."

**4. "Heirs with you of the grace of life."** "Likewise, husbands, live with your wives in an understanding way, showing honor to the woman as the weaker vessel, since they are heirs with you of the grace of life, so that your prayers may not be hindered" (3:7).
- **What is asked of husbands:** in that society husbands held almost complete power over their wives. Yet Peter tells husbands to live with their wives "in an understanding way" (literally "according to knowledge": knowing her, considering her) and to "show honor" to her.
- **"The weaker vessel":** most interpreters take this mainly as physical strength, and also as the wife's weaker social and legal position at the time, not as meaning women are lower in spirit, mind or worth. "Weaker" is comparative: the husband is a vessel too. Precisely because she is more easily harmed, the husband must protect and honor her all the more.
- **"Heirs with you of the grace of life":** before God, wife and husband are fully equal, heirs together (see Gal 3:28).
- **"So that your prayers may not be hindered":** a husband who mistreats his wife finds his prayers hindered before God. God cares how a husband treats his wife.
- **Different readings:** faithful believers have understood the roles of husband and wife differently. The view that the roles of husband and wife in the home are distinguished by sex (often called complementarian; for example, Wayne Grudem and Thomas Schreiner) holds that the submission and honor Peter describes here are the order God established for marriage, a principle for every age. The view that the roles of husband and wife in the home are not distinguished by sex (often called egalitarian; for example, Craig Keener and Scot McKnight) holds that Peter is teaching believers within the family structure of their time how to bear witness and keep the gospel from being reviled, and that today's application depends on the setting. Both views agree that husband and wife are equally honored and heirs together of the grace of life, that a husband must not dominate or mistreat his wife, and that no abuse can be defended from this passage.

> The point: A believing wife whose hope is in God can bear witness in an unbelieving home and need not give way to fear; a husband must understand and honor his wife, because both of them live by the same grace of life. God cares how husband and wife treat each other, and no abuse is part of submission.

## Going Deeper: 3:8–12 Do Not Repay Evil for Evil

**1. "Finally, all of you."** "Finally, all of you, have unity of mind, sympathy, brotherly love, a tender heart, and a humble mind" (3:8).
- **To everyone:** after speaking to servants, wives and husbands, Peter now addresses the whole church. Five words describe a healthy church: unity, sympathy, love, tenderness, humility.

**2. "But on the contrary, bless."** "Do not repay evil for evil or reviling for reviling, but on the contrary, bless, for to this you were called, that you may obtain a blessing" (3:9).
- **The teaching of Jesus:** "Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you" (Matt 5:44).
- **"To this you were called":** as in 2:21. We are called to be like Christ, who did not revile in return but blessed.
- **Blessing is not pretending:** blessing someone does not mean the harm is not real, nor that we may not seek justice (see 2:13–14). It means not letting hatred take root in the heart, and leaving vengeance to God.

**3. Psalm 34.** "For 'Whoever desires to love life and see good days, let him keep his tongue from evil and his lips from speaking deceit; let him turn away from evil and do good; let him seek peace and pursue it. For the eyes of the Lord are on the righteous, and his ears are open to their prayer. But the face of the Lord is against those who do evil'" (3:10–12).
- **A psalm written in trouble:** the title of Psalm 34 says David wrote it after he pretended to be mad before Abimelech and was driven away (1 Sam 21:10–15 names the king Achish of Gath; "Abimelech" was probably a Philistine royal title, as "Pharaoh" was in Egypt). Peter has already alluded to this psalm at 2:3. Even as a fugitive he said: turn away from evil and do good; seek peace.
- **God's eyes and ears:** sufferers feel that no one sees and no one hears. Peter says God's eyes are on the righteous, and his ears are open to their prayer.

> The point: Believers are called, like Christ, not to repay evil for evil but to bless. We can do this because God's eyes are on us and his ears are open to our prayer.

## Going Deeper: 3:13–17 Blessed in Suffering for Righteousness

**1. "Have no fear of them."** "Now who is there to harm you if you are zealous for what is good? But even if you should suffer for righteousness' sake, you will be blessed. Have no fear of them, nor be troubled, but in your hearts regard Christ the Lord as holy" (3:13–15a).
- **"Who is there to harm you?":** this does not mean those who do good will never suffer, for the next sentence says "even if you should suffer." It means: people can harm our bodies and our reputations, but they cannot take away the blessing God gives.
- **"Suffer for righteousness' sake... blessed":** these are almost the words of the Lord Jesus: "Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake" (Matt 5:10).
- **From Isaiah 8:** "do not fear what they fear, nor be in dread. But the LORD of hosts, him you shall regard as holy" (Isa 8:12–13). Peter replaces "the LORD of hosts" with "Christ the Lord," one of the New Testament's direct confessions that Christ is God. Whoever fears Christ need not fear people.

**2. "Always being prepared."** "Always being prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect, having a good conscience, so that, when you are slandered, those who revile your good behavior in Christ may be put to shame" (3:15b–16).
- **"A defense":** the word is often used of a defense in court (English "apologetics" comes from it). But here it is not only in court; it is in everyday life, whenever anyone asks.
- **"A reason for the hope":** people do not ask, "Why are you so conservative?" but, "Why do you still have hope in suffering?" A believer's hope itself makes people curious.
- **"With gentleness and respect":** the manner of the answer matters as much as its content. Winning an online argument about faith while losing gentleness is not the answer Peter means.
- **"A good conscience":** when life and words agree, slander cannot stand.

**3. "Better to suffer for doing good."** "For it is better to suffer for doing good, if that should be God's will, than for doing evil" (3:17).
- **"If that should be God's will":** suffering is not chance; it too is within God's will. But Peter's "if" presents such suffering as possible, not constant.
- **Two kinds of suffering:** suffering for doing evil is deserved; suffering for doing good is following Christ.

> The point: Those who suffer for righteousness are blessed. We need not fear people, because in our hearts we honor Christ as Lord; and when someone asks the reason for our hope, we answer with gentleness and respect.

## Going Deeper: 3:18–22 Christ Suffered and Triumphed

**1. "The righteous for the unrighteous."** "For Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit" (3:18).
- **"Once":** Christ's suffering was once for all, effective forever, never to be repeated (see Heb 9:28).
- **"The righteous for the unrighteous":** one of the clearest statements of substitutionary atonement in the New Testament (see 2:24, "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree"). We are unrighteous; Christ is righteous; he suffered in our place the penalty we deserved.
- **"That he might bring us to God":** the goal of salvation is not only to remove the penalty, but to bring us to God, reconciled to him. The greatest gift of salvation is God himself. We were cut off from God by sin (Isa 59:2); anyone still far off may come to God today through the Christ who stood in our place.
- **"Made alive in the spirit":** most interpreters take this not to mean that only Christ's spirit came back to life, but that in his resurrection he entered a new mode of life belonging to the Holy Spirit (see Rom 1:4). His body was truly raised as well (Luke 24:39).

**2. "He went and proclaimed to the spirits in prison."** "In which he went and proclaimed to the spirits in prison, because they formerly did not obey, when God's patience waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared, in which a few, that is, eight persons, were brought safely through water" (3:19–20).
- **One of the hardest passages in the New Testament:** Martin Luther said he was not sure what Peter meant here. The verb here means "proclaim" (as a herald does); Peter does not use the word "preach the gospel" that he uses in 4:6, so the content could be gospel, judgment or victory. There are three main readings:
- **Christ preached through Noah:** the Spirit of Christ preached repentance through Noah to the disobedient people of Noah's day, who are now held "in prison" (so Wayne Grudem; Augustine took the "prison" as the darkness of ignorance they were in at the time). In support: Peter says "the Spirit of Christ" was also in the Old Testament prophets (1:11), and 2 Peter calls Noah "a herald of righteousness" (2 Pet 2:5).
- **Christ proclaimed victory to fallen angels:** in the New Testament "spirits" usually means spiritual beings, and Jewish tradition (for example, *1 Enoch*) linked Genesis 6:1–4 and Noah's day with fallen angels; 2 Peter 2:4 and Jude 6 also speak of imprisoned angels. On this view, in his resurrection and ascension Christ announced his victory to these evil spirits (so many modern interpreters, including William Dalton and Thomas Schreiner); they also note that the "went" of verse 19 is the same word as "has gone into heaven" in verse 22.
- **Christ descended to the dead after his death:** the early church and the Catholic and Orthodox traditions often held that between his death and resurrection Christ went down to the realm of the dead and proclaimed salvation to the Old Testament dead (they cite 4:6; Eph 4:9). The Apostles' Creed's "he descended into hell" (a line many Chinese versions of the Creed in common use omit) is also read differently: Catholic and Orthodox teaching largely in this sense; the Lutheran tradition as Christ proclaiming his triumph in hell; Reformed teaching in two ways: Calvin and the Heidelberg Catechism (Q44) take it as Christ enduring hell's torments in his sufferings in Gethsemane and on the cross; the Westminster Larger Catechism (Q50) takes it as his remaining in the state of the dead, under the power of death, until the third day.
- **On any reading:** Peter's point is clear: the Christ who suffered for us has triumphed, and every spiritual power is beneath him (3:22). Suffering believers need not be afraid. This passage does not teach a second chance to repent after death (see Heb 9:27).

**3. "Baptism, which corresponds to this."** "Baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you, not as a removal of dirt from the body but as an appeal to God for a good conscience, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ, who has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God, with angels, authorities, and powers having been subjected to him" (3:21–22).
- **Noah and baptism:** Noah's family was brought safely through the water of judgment; in baptism believers are joined to the Christ who bore judgment for us and rose again (see Rom 6:3–4).
- **"Not as a removal of dirt from the body":** what saves is not the water itself, nor an outward washing.
- **"An appeal to God for a good conscience":** the Greek phrase is hard to translate. Some take it as "an appeal to God for a good conscience" (ESV); others as "a pledge to God from a good conscience." Either way, this clause stresses the response of the one baptized toward God.
- **"Through the resurrection of Jesus Christ":** baptism saves only through the resurrection of Jesus Christ.
- **Different readings:** the church has understood how baptism "saves" in different ways. The Catholic Church teaches that through baptism Christ regenerates and forgives original sin and all personal sins. The Lutheran tradition holds that baptism is a means of grace by which God regenerates through his word, received by faith. The Reformed tradition holds that baptism is a sign and seal of the covenant of grace, through which the Spirit confers the grace it signifies on those to whom it belongs, received by faith. The Baptist tradition holds that baptism is an act of obedience in which those already born again publicly confess their faith. All agree that the ground of salvation is Christ's death and resurrection, not the water itself, and that Christ commanded baptism (Matt 28:19).
- **"At the right hand of God":** the Christ who suffered on earth now sits on the throne, and every angel and power is subject to him.

> The point: Christ suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring us to God. He has risen and ascended, and every power is beneath him; a believer's baptism points to our union with this triumphant Lord.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you... through the resurrection of Jesus Christ, who has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God, with angels, authorities, and powers having been subjected to him" (3:21–22). I have believed for several years, yet I have never been baptized. I have plenty of reasons: my parents would be heartbroken; they say, "Believing in a religion is fine, but getting baptized means abandoning your ancestors." Relatives would talk. I am also afraid that once I am baptized people will watch me more closely, and if I fall, I will bring more shame on the Lord's name. I tell myself I will wait until my parents are older and come round, until I am more sure myself. I tell myself that believing in my heart is enough, and baptism is only a formality. But the people Peter wrote to were slandered by their neighbors and misunderstood by their families precisely because they had publicly confessed Christ. He did not tell them, "Then believe quietly." He said, "Have no fear of them, nor be troubled, but in your hearts regard Christ the Lord as holy" (3:14–15). What am I really afraid of? Of losing my family's approval, of losing face, of paying a price. Yet the price I am afraid of paying, he has already paid in a far deeper way: "the righteous for the unrighteous" (3:18). He did not turn back from the shame; he was hung on a tree before everyone. His own family came out to seize him, saying he was "out of his mind" (Mark 3:21; see John 7:5). He did this not so that I would owe him an act of courage, but "that he might bring us to God." My family's approval is real and precious, but it was never what brought me home; he did. Now he has risen and is "at the right hand of God," and every power in heaven and on earth is subject to him; none of the talk and the looks I fear stands above him. Baptism does not ask me to leave my parents; it asks me to confess, before my parents and before everyone, that I belong to this Lord. And if I am waiting until I am more sure, or more presentable, Peter says baptism saves not as a removal of dirt from the body but "through the resurrection of Jesus Christ"; it rests on what he has done, not on how ready I feel. If I have not been baptized, I can go and talk with my pastor, ask the Lord for wisdom and courage, pray for my parents, and explain it to them with respect and gentleness. If baptism would put me in real danger, say I am still a minor living at home, or my family might turn violent, I can talk with my pastor about timing and protection; waiting for wisdom is not the same as hiding out of fear. If I have been baptized, I can remember that day: the water itself did not wash away my sins; Christ's blood did. What that day showed is that I have been joined to a Lord who has triumphed. If I was baptized as a child and cannot remember the day, I can still remember what it pointed to: God sought me first. Lord Jesus, you suffered, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring me to God; you sit at God's right hand, and every power is subject to you. I am still afraid; but help me honor you as holy in my heart, and not fear what people threaten, because I am yours.
