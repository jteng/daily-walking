## 本章脉络

第四章可以分成三段。**与罪断绝**（1-6节）：基督既在肉身受苦，我们也当存同样的心志作为兵器，不再从人的情欲，只从神的旨意度余下的光阴；往日随从外邦人行邪淫醉酒的时候已经够了；旧日的朋友以为怪，毁谤我们，他们必在审判活人死人的主面前交账。**万物的结局近了**（7-11节）：要谨慎自守，警醒祷告；最要紧的是彼此切实相爱，因为爱能遮掩许多的罪；要互相款待，不发怨言；各人要照所得的恩赐彼此服事，作神百般恩赐的好管家，叫神在凡事上因耶稣基督得荣耀。**火炼的试验**（12-19节）：火炼的试验临到，不要以为奇怪，倒要欢喜，因为我们是与基督一同受苦；为基督的名受辱骂是有福的，因为神荣耀的灵常住在我们身上；不可因作恶受苦，若为作基督徒受苦，却不要羞耻；审判要从神的家起首；照神旨意受苦的人，要一心为善，将自己的灵魂交与那信实的造化之主。

这一章接着3:18-22。基督已经受苦、复活、得胜，所以我们也要用同样的心志面对苦难。第11节的颂赞是全章的中心：无论讲道、服事还是受苦，目的都是「叫神在凡事上因耶稣基督得荣耀」。

## 深读 4:1-6 与罪断绝

**1. 「将这样的心志作为兵器」。** 「基督既在肉身受苦，你们也当将这样的心志作为兵器，因为在肉身受过苦的，就已经与罪断绝了。你们存这样的心，从今以后就可以不从人的情欲，只从神的旨意在世度余下的光阴。」（4:1-2）
- **「兵器」：** 原文是武装自己。基督徒的生活是一场争战（参2:11「这私欲是与灵魂争战的」）。我们的兵器，就是基督受苦时的那个心志：宁可受苦，也不犯罪。
- **「在肉身受过苦的，就已经与罪断绝了」：** 这句话很难解。有人认为是指基督：他受苦受死，就永远与罪的权势了断了（参罗6:10；基督本身从未犯罪，2:22）。也有人认为是指信徒：一个愿意为基督受苦的人，显明他已经和罪决裂了；或者说，信徒在洗礼中与基督同死（3:21；罗6:7），已经与罪的辖制断绝。这不是说受苦的人就不再犯罪，而是说他生命的方向已经改变了。
- **「余下的光阴」：** 不管我们还剩多少年，都可以不再为自己的情欲活，而为神的旨意活。

**2. 「时候已经够了」。** 「因为往日随从外邦人的心意行邪淫、恶欲、醉酒、荒宴、群饮，并可恶拜偶像的事，时候已经够了。他们在这些事上，见你们不与他们同奔那放荡无度的路，就以为怪，毁谤你们。他们必在那将要审判活人死人的主面前交帐。」（4:3-5）
- **旧日的朋友：** 读者信主以前，和朋友一起参加城里的宴乐和拜偶像的节庆。信主以后，他们不去了，朋友就觉得奇怪，甚至说他们的坏话。
- **今天的样子：** 有些人信主以后，不再参加应酬中的灌酒和乱来的娱乐，不再一起说同事的坏话，就被人说「装清高」「不合群」。彼得说：这不奇怪。
- **「交账」：** 毁谤人的人，有一天要向主交账。所以信徒不必自己去辩个明白，也不必报复。

**3. 「就是死人也曾有福音传给他们」。** 「为此，就是死人也曾有福音传给他们，要叫他们的肉体按着人受审判，他们的灵性却靠神活着。」（4:6）
- **两种主要的理解：** 多数解经家（如施莱纳、古德恩）认为，这是指那些在世时听了福音、信了主，后来已经死去的信徒：在世人看来，他们和别人一样死了（「肉体按着人受审判」），但在神面前，他们的灵却活着。读者可能担心，信了主的人死了，是不是白信了？彼得说：不是。也有人把这节和3:19连起来，认为是基督在死后向死人传福音；但这种读法在这里较难成立：第5节的「死人」是指在审判时已经死了的人；这里的动词是「传福音」，和3:19「传道」（宣告）不是同一个词，对象也是「死人」，不是「灵」（参3:19的注释）；而且彼得的用意是安慰读者：已经死去的信徒并没有白信。
- **不是第二次机会：** 这节经文不是说人死后还可以听福音、再悔改（来9:27）。

> 要点：基督既在肉身受了苦，我们也要用同样的心志武装自己：宁可受苦，也不犯罪。旧日的朋友会觉得奇怪，但我们余下的光阴，是为神的旨意而活。

## 深读 4:7-11 万物的结局近了

**1. 「谨慎自守，警醒祷告」。** 「万物的结局近了。所以，你们要谨慎自守，警醒祷告。」（4:7）
- **结局近了：** 新约的作者都活在主随时会来的盼望中（参雅5:8）。这不是叫人放下一切去等，而是叫人清醒地活着。
- **为了祷告：** 原文直译是：为了祷告。清醒、自守的目的，是能好好地祷告。一个被焦虑、忙碌、娱乐弄得昏昏沉沉的人，很难祷告。

**2. 「爱能遮掩许多的罪」。** 「最要紧的是彼此切实相爱，因为爱能遮掩许多的罪。」（4:8）
- **「最要紧的」：** 在万物结局近了的时候，最要紧的不是计算日期，而是相爱。
- **引自箴言：** 「恨能挑启争端；爱能遮掩一切过错。」（箴10:12）在一个群体中，爱心让人不揪住别人的小过错不放，不到处宣扬，愿意饶恕。
- **遮掩不是隐瞒：** 这里说的是饶恕彼此之间的过犯，不是掩盖违法的罪行。这也不是说我们的爱可以抵消自己的罪；赦罪只在基督（2:24）。凡涉及虐待、性侵或其他伤害他人的罪，爱心正是要保护受害的人，向警察或有关部门举报（罗13:4），绝不是为了「家丑不可外扬」而替人隐瞒。

**3. 「互相款待，不发怨言」。** 「你们要互相款待，不发怨言。」（4:9）
- **当时的处境：** 早期教会多在信徒家里聚会，传道的人到处走，也要住在信徒家中。款待是很实际、也很累人的服事（参来13:2；罗12:13）。
- **「不发怨言」：** 彼得知道，款待久了，人会抱怨：又要煮饭，又要收拾，又是我。他没有叫人停止款待，也没有说累了是罪，而是叫人留意心里的怨言。

**4. 「作神百般恩赐的好管家」。** 「各人要照所得的恩赐彼此服事，作神百般恩赐的好管家。若有讲道的，要按着神的圣言讲；若有服事人的，要按着神所赐的力量服事，叫神在凡事上因耶稣基督得荣耀。原来荣耀、权能都是他的，直到永永远远。阿们！」（4:10-11）
- **「各人」：** 每一个信徒都领受了恩赐（参罗12:6-8；林前12:7）。
- **「管家」：** 管家不是主人。恩赐不是我们的，是神交托的，要用来服事别人。
- **「百般恩赐」：** 「百般」和1:6「百般的试炼」同一个词。神的恩典，像我们的试炼一样多种多样。
- **讲道和服事：** 彼得把恩赐分成两大类：说话的和做事的。讲道的人要像传讲神的圣言那样讲：敬畏、忠于神已经说的话，不是讲自己的意见；服事的人要记得，力量是「神所赐的」，不是靠自己硬撑。
- **「荣耀、权能都是他的」：** 「他」可能指神，也可能指耶稣基督（参启1:5-6）；两种读法都合乎圣经。
- **「因耶稣基督得荣耀」：** 一切服事的目的，不是叫人看见我们，而是叫神得荣耀；而这荣耀是「因耶稣基督」，因为我们一切的服事，都是藉着他才蒙神悦纳（2:5）。

> 要点：在万物结局近了的时候，最要紧的是彼此切实相爱，用神所赐的恩赐和力量彼此服事。服事的力量是神给的，荣耀也归给神。

## 深读 4:12-19 火炼的试验

**1. 「不要以为奇怪」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，有火炼的试验临到你们，不要以为奇怪（似乎是遭遇非常的事），倒要欢喜；因为你们是与基督一同受苦，使你们在他荣耀显现的时候，也可以欢喜快乐。」（4:12-13）
- **「火炼的试验」：** 和1:7的炼金相连。火是要炼净，不是要毁灭。
- **「不要以为奇怪」：** 4:4说外邦人对信徒「以为怪」；这里说信徒不要对苦难「以为奇怪」。跟随一位被钉十字架的主，受苦并不是意外。
- **「与基督一同受苦」：** 这不是说我们的受苦能为人赎罪，那是基督独一的工作（3:18）。而是说，我们因属于他，就和他一同受世人的排斥（参罗8:17；腓3:10）。

**2. 「神荣耀的灵常住在你们身上」。** 「你们若为基督的名受辱骂，便是有福的；因为神荣耀的灵常住在你们身上。」（4:14）
- **主耶稣的话：** 「人为人子恨恶你们，拒绝你们，辱骂你们……你们就有福了！」（路6:22）使徒们被鞭打以后，「心里欢喜，因被算是配为这名受辱」（徒5:41）。
- **「常住在你们身上」：** 这叫人想起以赛亚书11:2：「耶和华的灵必住在他身上。」那住在弥赛亚身上的灵，如今也住在受辱的信徒身上。人看见的是羞辱，神看见的是荣耀。

**3. 「若为作基督徒受苦，却不要羞耻」。** 「你们中间却不可有人因为杀人、偷窃、作恶、好管闲事而受苦。若为作基督徒受苦，却不要羞耻，倒要因这名归荣耀给神。」（4:15-16）
- **分辨两种受苦：** 不是一切的苦难都是为主受苦。因为自己做错事、说话刻薄、多管闲事而招来的麻烦，不能算是为义受苦，要悔改。
- **「好管闲事」：** 这个词在新约中只出现在这里，可能是指插手、干涉别人的事（也有人认为是指侵占别人的东西）。
- **「基督徒」：** 这个称呼在新约中只出现三次（徒11:26；26:28；这里），起初多半是外人对信徒带着轻蔑的称呼。彼得说，被人这样叫，不必羞耻，反而要「因这名」荣耀神。

**4. 「审判要从神的家起首」。** 「因为时候到了，审判要从神的家起首。若是先从我们起首，那不信从神福音的人将有何等的结局呢？若是义人仅仅得救，那不虔敬和犯罪的人将有何地可站呢？」（4:17-18）
- **旧约的背景：** 以西结看见神的审判「从圣所起」（结9:6）；玛拉基说主要像炼金之人的火，先洁净利未人（玛3:2-3）。
- **对信徒是炼净：** 对神的家来说，这「审判」是炼净的火，从来不是定罪（罗8:1），就是他们现在所受的苦；对不信的人，却是最后的定罪。
- **「义人仅仅得救」：** 引自箴言11:31（彼得是照七十士译本引用；希伯来原文〔和合本照此翻译〕作「义人在世尚且受报」）。这不是说信徒的得救不稳，而是说得救的路要经过艰难，并且全是神的恩典把人带到底（1:5）。

**5. 「将自己灵魂交与那信实的造化之主」。** 「所以，那照神旨意受苦的人要一心为善，将自己灵魂交与那信实的造化之主。」（4:19）
- **「交与」：** 这和主耶稣在十字架上（按路加的记载）最后一句话用的是同一个词：「父啊！我将我的灵魂交在你手里。」（路23:46；诗31:5）
- **「信实的造化之主」：** 「造化之主」这个名词在新约中只出现在这里（罗1:25「造物的主」原文是另一种说法）；新约也只有这里把「信实」和「造化之主」连在一起。造我们的神，必不撇下他所造的人。
- **「一心为善」：** 交托不是消极地等，而是继续行善。

> 要点：火炼的试验不是奇怪的事，而是与基督一同受苦。为基督的名受辱的人是有福的，神荣耀的灵住在他们身上。受苦的人可以继续行善，把自己的灵魂交托给那信实的造物主。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们要互相款待，不发怨言。……若有服事人的，要按着神所赐的力量服事，叫神在凡事上因耶稣基督得荣耀。」（4:9、11）我在教会服事很多年了：主日学、诗班、小组在我家，节期的爱宴，搬椅子、关灯、最后一个走。起初是甘心的，后来就有了怨言，只是不说出口：为什么总是我？那些人只会来吃，从不帮忙收拾。有时候我一边洗碗，一边在心里算：我做了多少，他们做了多少。我很累，却不敢停，怕一停，别人就说我不够爱主；我也不敢说，怕人觉得我不属灵。彼得说「不发怨言」，我读了心里一沉，因为我正是在发怨言。可是彼得在下一节说的话，叫我看见问题在哪里：服事要「按着神所赐的力量」。我这些年，多半是靠自己的力量撑着，也多半是想被人看见。靠自己力量的服事，迟早会变成怨言；为了被人看见的服事，没有人看见的时候就会变苦。主耶稣也服事过人。他「拿一条手巾束腰」，跪下洗门徒的脚（约13:4-5），连那个要卖他的人的脚也洗了。他这样做，不是为了被看见；他在服事中，也常常退到旷野去祷告（可1:35；路5:16），从父那里得力。我服事得再多，也比不上他在十字架上为我舍命、担当我的罪（2:24）。所以我的怨言，可以先带到他面前认罪；我的疲累，也可以老老实实地告诉他，也告诉教会的同工。摩西的岳父对他说：「这事太重，你独自一人办理不了。」（出18:18）承认自己办不了，请别人分担，不是不爱主。主啊，我承认我常常靠自己的力量服事，心里又积满了怨言；求你赦免我，也赐我你所赐的力量。原来荣耀、权能都是你的，直到永永远远，不是我的。阿们！

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 falls into three parts. **Done with sin** (vv. 1–6): since Christ suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves with the same way of thinking, and live the rest of your time no longer for human passions but for the will of God; the time past is enough for living as the Gentiles do; old friends are surprised and malign you, but they will give account to him who judges the living and the dead. **The end of all things is at hand** (vv. 7–11): be self-controlled and sober-minded for the sake of your prayers; above all, keep loving one another earnestly, since love covers a multitude of sins; show hospitality without grumbling; use whatever gift you have received to serve one another as good stewards of God's varied grace, so that in everything God may be glorified through Jesus Christ. **The fiery trial** (vv. 12–19): do not be surprised at the fiery trial, but rejoice, because you share Christ's sufferings; if you are insulted for the name of Christ you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory rests on you; let none suffer as an evildoer, but if anyone suffers as a Christian, let him not be ashamed; judgment begins at the household of God; let those who suffer according to God's will entrust their souls to a faithful Creator while doing good.

This chapter follows 3:18–22. Christ has suffered, risen and triumphed, so we are to face suffering with the same mind. The doxology of verse 11 is the center of the chapter: whether we speak, serve or suffer, the goal is "that in everything God may be glorified through Jesus Christ."

## Going Deeper: 4:1–6 Done with Sin

**1. "Arm yourselves with the same way of thinking."** "Since therefore Christ suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves with the same way of thinking, for whoever has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin, so as to live for the rest of the time in the flesh no longer for human passions but for the will of God" (4:1–2).
- **"Arm yourselves":** the Christian life is a battle (see 2:11, "the passions of the flesh, which wage war against your soul"). Our weapon is the mind Christ had when he suffered: better to suffer than to sin.
- **"Whoever has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin":** a hard sentence. Some take it of Christ: by suffering and dying he was done with sin's power once for all (see Rom 6:10; Christ himself never sinned, 2:22). Others take it of believers: someone willing to suffer for Christ shows he has broken with sin; or believers, who died with Christ in baptism (3:21; Rom 6:7), have been set free from sin's rule. It does not mean that those who suffer no longer sin, but that the direction of their life has changed.
- **"The rest of the time":** however many years we have left, we can stop living for our own passions and live for God's will.

**2. "The time that is past suffices."** "The time that is past suffices for doing what the Gentiles want to do, living in sensuality, passions, drunkenness, orgies, drinking parties, and lawless idolatry. With respect to this they are surprised when you do not join them in the same flood of debauchery, and they malign you; but they will give account to him who is ready to judge the living and the dead" (4:3–5).
- **Old friends:** before coming to faith, the readers joined their friends in the city's parties and idolatrous festivals. Afterwards they stopped going, and their friends found it strange and even spoke against them.
- **Today:** some people, after coming to faith, stop joining the forced drinking and crude entertainment of business dinners, stop running down colleagues together, and are called "stuck-up" or "antisocial." Peter says: this is not strange.
- **"Give account":** those who slander will one day give account to the Lord. So believers need not prove themselves in every argument, nor take revenge.

**3. "The gospel was preached even to those who are dead."** "For this is why the gospel was preached even to those who are dead, that though judged in the flesh the way people are, they might live in the spirit the way God does" (4:6).
- **Two main readings:** most interpreters (for example, Schreiner and Grudem) take this of believers who heard the gospel and believed while alive, and have since died: in the world's eyes they died like everyone else ("judged in the flesh the way people are"), but before God their spirits live. The readers may have worried that believers who died had believed in vain. Peter says: no. Others link this verse with 3:19 and take it as Christ preaching to the dead after his death; but that reading is harder here: "the dead" in verse 5 are those who have died by the time of judgment; the verb here is "preach the gospel," not the "proclaim" of 3:19, and its hearers are "the dead," not "spirits" (see on 3:19); and Peter's aim is to reassure readers that believers who have died did not believe in vain.
- **Not a second chance:** this verse does not teach that people can hear the gospel and repent after death (Heb 9:27).

> The point: Since Christ suffered in the flesh, we arm ourselves with the same mind: better to suffer than to sin. Old friends may find it strange, but the rest of our time is lived for the will of God.

## Going Deeper: 4:7–11 The End of All Things Is at Hand

**1. "Self-controlled and sober-minded for the sake of your prayers."** "The end of all things is at hand; therefore be self-controlled and sober-minded for the sake of your prayers" (4:7).
- **The end is near:** the New Testament writers all lived in the hope that the Lord could come at any time (see Jas 5:8). This does not mean dropping everything to wait, but living clear-headed.
- **For prayer:** the purpose of sober self-control is to be able to pray. Someone dazed by anxiety, busyness or entertainment finds it hard to pray.

**2. "Love covers a multitude of sins."** "Above all, keep loving one another earnestly, since love covers a multitude of sins" (4:8).
- **"Above all":** when the end of all things is near, what matters most is not calculating dates but loving one another.
- **From Proverbs:** "Hatred stirs up strife, but love covers all offenses" (Prov 10:12). In a community, love does not hold on to other people's small faults or broadcast them, and is willing to forgive.
- **Covering is not concealing:** this is about forgiving offenses between one another, not covering up crime. Nor does it mean our love atones for our own sins; forgiveness is through Christ alone (2:24). Where abuse, sexual assault or other harm to others is involved, love means protecting those harmed and reporting to the police or proper authorities (Rom 13:4), never hiding it to keep "family shame" quiet.

**3. "Show hospitality to one another without grumbling."** "Show hospitality to one another without grumbling" (4:9).
- **The setting:** the early church met mostly in believers' homes, and traveling preachers stayed with believers. Hospitality was a very practical and tiring ministry (see Heb 13:2; Rom 12:13).
- **"Without grumbling":** Peter knows that after a while people complain: cooking again, cleaning up again, me again. He does not tell them to stop showing hospitality, nor does he say tiredness is sin; he tells them to watch for the grumbling in their hearts.

**4. "Good stewards of God's varied grace."** "As each has received a gift, use it to serve one another, as good stewards of God's varied grace: whoever speaks, as one who speaks oracles of God; whoever serves, as one who serves by the strength that God supplies, in order that in everything God may be glorified through Jesus Christ. To him belong glory and dominion forever and ever. Amen" (4:10–11).
- **"Each":** every believer has received a gift (see Rom 12:6–8; 1 Cor 12:7).
- **"Stewards":** a steward is not the owner. Gifts are not ours; God has entrusted them to us to serve others.
- **"Varied grace":** "varied" is the same word as "various trials" in 1:6. God's grace is as varied as our trials.
- **Speaking and serving:** Peter divides gifts into two broad kinds: speaking and doing. Those who speak must speak *as* those handling the oracles of God: with reverence, and faithful to what God has said, not offering their own opinions; those who serve must remember that the strength is "supplied by God," not squeezed out of themselves.
- **"To him belong glory and dominion":** "to him" may refer to God or to Jesus Christ (compare Rev 1:5–6); both readings are faithful to Scripture.
- **"Glorified through Jesus Christ":** the aim of all service is not that people see us, but that God is glorified, and that "through Jesus Christ," because all our service is acceptable to God only through him (2:5).

> The point: As the end of all things draws near, what matters most is to love one another earnestly and serve one another with the gifts and strength God supplies. The strength comes from God, and the glory goes to God.

## Going Deeper: 4:12–19 The Fiery Trial

**1. "Do not be surprised."** "Beloved, do not be surprised at the fiery trial when it comes upon you to test you, as though something strange were happening to you. But rejoice insofar as you share Christ's sufferings, that you may also rejoice and be glad when his glory is revealed" (4:12–13).
- **"The fiery trial":** this links with the refining of gold in 1:7. Fire refines; it does not destroy.
- **"Do not be surprised":** in 4:4 the Gentiles are "surprised" at believers; here believers are not to be "surprised" at suffering. For followers of a crucified Lord, suffering is no accident.
- **"Share Christ's sufferings":** this does not mean our suffering atones for anyone; that is Christ's unique work (3:18). It means that because we belong to him, we share in the world's rejection of him (see Rom 8:17; Phil 3:10).

**2. "The Spirit of glory and of God rests upon you."** "If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory and of God rests upon you" (4:14).
- **The words of Jesus:** "Blessed are you when people hate you and when they exclude you and revile you... on account of the Son of Man!" (Luke 6:22). After the apostles were beaten, they left "rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the name" (Acts 5:41).
- **"Rests upon you":** this recalls Isaiah 11:2: "And the Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him." The Spirit who rests on the Messiah now rests on insulted believers. People see shame; God sees glory.

**3. "If anyone suffers as a Christian, let him not be ashamed."** "But let none of you suffer as a murderer or a thief or an evildoer or as a meddler. Yet if anyone suffers as a Christian, let him not be ashamed, but let him glorify God in that name" (4:15–16).
- **Two kinds of suffering:** not all trouble is "suffering for the Lord." Trouble we bring on ourselves by wrongdoing, harsh words or meddling is not suffering for righteousness; it calls for repentance.
- **"A meddler":** this word appears only here in the New Testament; it probably means meddling in others' affairs (some suggest misappropriating what belongs to others).
- **"Christian":** the name appears only three times in the New Testament (Acts 11:26; 26:28; here), and at first it was probably a contemptuous name outsiders used. Peter says there is no shame in being called this; rather, glorify God "in that name."

**4. "Judgment to begin at the household of God."** "For it is time for judgment to begin at the household of God; and if it begins with us, what will be the outcome for those who do not obey the gospel of God? And 'If the righteous is scarcely saved, what will become of the ungodly and the sinner?'" (4:17–18).
- **Old Testament background:** Ezekiel saw God's judgment "begin at my sanctuary" (Ezek 9:6); Malachi says the Lord will be like a refiner's fire, purifying the sons of Levi first (Mal 3:2–3).
- **For believers, refining:** for God's household this "judgment" is the refining fire of their present suffering, never condemnation (Rom 8:1); for unbelievers it is final condemnation.
- **"If the righteous is scarcely saved":** from Proverbs 11:31 (Peter follows the Greek Old Testament; the Hebrew reads "If the righteous is repaid on earth"). This does not mean believers' salvation is uncertain, but that the way of salvation passes through hardship, and that it is wholly God's grace that brings people through to the end (1:5).

**5. "Entrust their souls to a faithful Creator."** "Therefore let those who suffer according to God's will entrust their souls to a faithful Creator while doing good" (4:19).
- **"Entrust":** this is the same verb Jesus used in his last words on the cross in Luke's account: "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit!" (Luke 23:46; Ps 31:5).
- **"A faithful Creator":** the noun "Creator" occurs only here in the New Testament (Rom 1:25 uses a different construction), and only here is God called a *faithful* Creator. The God who made us will not abandon what he has made.
- **"While doing good":** entrusting is not passive waiting; it goes on doing good.

> The point: The fiery trial is not strange; it is sharing Christ's sufferings. Those insulted for the name of Christ are blessed, for the Spirit of glory rests on them. Those who suffer can go on doing good, and entrust their souls to their faithful Creator.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Show hospitality to one another without grumbling... whoever serves, as one who serves by the strength that God supplies, in order that in everything God may be glorified through Jesus Christ" (4:9, 11). I have served at church for many years: Sunday school, the choir, small group in my home, the festival meals, setting out chairs, switching off the lights, last to leave. At first I did it gladly; later the grumbling came, though I never said it aloud: why is it always me? Those people only come to eat and never help clear up. Sometimes I wash the dishes and keep score in my head: how much I have done, how much they have done. I am tired, but I dare not stop, in case people say I don't love the Lord enough; and I dare not say anything, in case people think I am unspiritual. When Peter says "without grumbling," my heart sinks, because grumbling is exactly what I am doing. But what Peter says next shows me where the problem lies: serve "by the strength that God supplies." For years I have mostly been holding on by my own strength, and mostly wanting to be seen. Service done in my own strength sooner or later turns into grumbling; service done to be seen turns bitter when no one is watching. The Lord Jesus served too. He "took a towel, tied it around his waist," and knelt to wash his disciples' feet (John 13:4–5), even the feet of the one who would betray him. He did it not to be seen; and in the middle of his ministry he often withdrew to desolate places to pray (Mark 1:35; Luke 5:16), drawing strength from the Father. However much I serve, it is nothing next to his giving his life on the cross and bearing my sins (2:24). So I can bring my grumbling to him first and confess it; and I can tell him honestly that I am tired, and tell my fellow workers at church too. Moses' father-in-law told him, "the thing is too heavy for you. You are not able to do it alone" (Exod 18:18). Admitting I cannot do it alone and asking others to share the load is not a failure to love the Lord. Lord, I confess that I have often served in my own strength, with a heart full of grumbling; forgive me, and give me the strength you supply. To you belong glory and dominion forever and ever, not to me. Amen.
