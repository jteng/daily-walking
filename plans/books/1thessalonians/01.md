## 本章脉络

第一章是一段很长的感谢，可以分成三部分。**问安**（1节）：保罗、西拉、提摩太写信给「在父神和主耶稣基督里的教会」。**感谢神的工作**（2-5节）：保罗为他们的信心、爱心和盼望感谢神，并且知道他们是蒙拣选的，因为福音传到他们那里，「不独在乎言语，也在乎权能和圣灵」。**他们成了榜样**（6-10节）：他们在大难之中仍有圣灵所赐的喜乐，信心传遍各处；各地的人都在讲，他们怎样「离弃偶像，归向神，要服事那又真又活的神，等候他儿子从天降临」。

整章的焦点不在帖撒罗尼迦人有多好，而在神在他们身上做了什么：神爱他们，拣选他们，用福音的大能改变他们。保罗感谢的对象是神。

## 深读 1:1 在父神和主耶稣基督里的教会

**1. 三个写信的人。** 「保罗、西拉、提摩太写信给……」（1:1）西拉就是使徒行传里与保罗同行的那一位（徒15:40；17:4），原文这里用的是他的拉丁名字。提摩太是保罗的年轻同工，刚刚被打发回帖撒罗尼迦（3:2）。保罗在这里没有称自己为「使徒」（比较加1:1；林前1:1）。一个可能的原因是，他和这个教会的关系很亲密，不需要先确立自己的权柄。

**2. 「在父神和主耶稣基督里」。** 保罗说这个教会是「在父神和主耶稣基督里」的。这封信写在耶稣复活以后大约二十年，保罗已经把「主耶稣基督」和「父神」并列，用同一个介词连在一起，说教会同时活在父和子里面。一个严守独一神信仰的犹太人这样说话，许多学者认为这表明，在最早的教会里，耶稣已经被尊为与父同享神的尊荣的主（参3:11，「神－我们的父和我们的主耶稣」一同引领，原文那里的动词是单数）。
- **教会是谁的？** 帖撒罗尼迦城里有许多团体：行会、宗教社团、城里的公民大会。「教会」这个词在希腊城市里指公民大会；在希腊文旧约里，它常指以色列的会众，就是神召聚到他面前的百姓（参申4:10）。保罗说，这群人的身份不在于他们属于哪一个城、哪一个行会，而在于他们在父神和主耶稣基督里。

**3. 「愿恩惠、平安归与你们」。** 这是保罗书信中常见的问安，把希腊人的问候（恩惠）和犹太人的问候（平安）合在一起。但在保罗笔下，这不只是客套话：恩惠是神白白的爱，平安是因这爱与神和好。整封信都建立在这两样上面。

## 深读 1:2-5 信心、爱心、盼望和神的拣选

**1. 三样恩典，三种表现。** 「不住地记念你们因信心所做的工夫，因爱心所受的劳苦，因盼望我们主耶稣基督所存的忍耐」（1:3）。这很可能是信心、爱心、盼望在新约中最早连在一起出现的地方之一（参加5:5-6；林前13:13；西1:4-5）。保罗注意的不只是这三样本身，而是它们在生活中结出的果子：
- **信心做工：** 真信心不是闲着的，它会动起来（参加5:6）。这不是靠行为得救，而是得救的信心必然会活出来。
- **爱心受劳苦：** 「劳苦」这个词指做到筋疲力尽的辛劳。爱不只是一种感觉，而是愿意为人累着。
- **盼望存忍耐：** 「忍耐」不是消极地忍受，而是在压力下站立得稳、不肯放弃。他们能这样，是因为盼望的是「我们主耶稣基督」，盼望有一个确实的对象。

**2. 「我知道你们是蒙拣选的」。** 「被神所爱的弟兄啊，我知道你们是蒙拣选的」（1:4）。保罗怎么知道？他没有看见神的册子，而是看见了神的工作：「因为我们的福音传到你们那里，不独在乎言语，也在乎权能和圣灵，并充足的信心」（1:5）。他们的改变，就是神拣选的记号。
- **拣选是出于爱：** 保罗先说「被神所爱的」，再说「蒙拣选的」。拣选不是冷冰冰的命运，而是神的爱在时间以先就定意要得着他们（参申7:7-8）。
- **不同的理解：** 神的拣选怎样与人的回应相连，教会历史上有不同的看法。改革宗传统（如加尔文，今天如巴刻）认为，神在创世以前，在基督里无条件地拣选了一些人（弗1:4），他们必然会信。阿民念和卫斯理传统（如霍华德·马歇尔）认为，神预先知道谁会信，就拣选了他们；也有学者（如威廉·克莱因）强调，神拣选的首先是在基督里的群体，人藉着信加入这群体。这几种看法都同意：人得救全是出于神的恩典；福音要传给所有人；没有人能拿拣选作借口不信或不传福音。
- **这节经文的用处：** 保罗讲拣选，不是为了叫人猜测自己是不是在名单上，而是为了叫受苦的信徒有把握：你们信主不是偶然的，是神的手在做工。

**3. 「不独在乎言语」。** 保罗不是说言语不重要；福音本身就是要用言语讲明的。他是说，言语以外还有「权能和圣灵」。圣灵叫人听见的不只是一番道理，而是神亲自的呼召。
- **「充足的信心」：** 这可能指传福音的人满有把握地讲，也可能指听的人被说服，深信不疑。从上下文看，两种都说得通；因为紧接着讲的是传道人「是怎样为人」，许多解经家（如杰弗里·韦玛）比较倾向前一种。
- **「我们……是怎样为人」：** 紧接着，保罗提到自己的生活（1:5下）。福音的能力也显在传福音的人身上。第二章会详细讲这一点。

> 要点：信徒能确知自己蒙神拣选，不是靠窥探神隐秘的旨意，而是看见神的福音在自己身上真实地做了工。拣选的根源是神的爱，不是我们的好。

## 深读 1:6-8 在大难之中有圣灵的喜乐

**1. 患难和喜乐同时存在。** 「你们在大难之中，蒙了圣灵所赐的喜乐，领受真道」（1:6）。「大难」原文指压力、逼迫。他们一信主就受苦：家人的反对、邻舍的排斥，可能还有失去生计（参2:14；徒17:5-9）。可是他们同时有喜乐。这喜乐不是从环境来的，是「圣灵所赐的」。
- **不是假装不痛：** 圣经里的喜乐并不要求人否认痛苦。保罗自己在受苦的时候也忧伤（3:7「在一切困苦患难之中」）。圣灵的喜乐，是在痛苦里面仍然知道：神是我的，我是神的。

**2. 效法我们，也效法了主。** 「领受真道就效法我们，也效法了主」（1:6）。他们效法的，不是保罗的口才，而是他在患难中仍然传福音的样式（2:2）；而这样式又是从主耶稣来的，他「因那摆在前面的喜乐，就轻看羞辱，忍受了十字架的苦难」（来12:2）。
- **一条效法的链子：** 主→保罗→帖撒罗尼迦人→马其顿和亚该亚的信徒（1:7）。福音常常不是靠书本传下去，而是靠一个人的生命影响另一个人。

**3. 主的道从你们那里传扬出来。** 「因为主的道从你们那里已经传扬出来」（1:8）。「传扬出来」这个词在新约里只出现在这里，许多人认为它带着声音响亮、四处回荡的意思，像号角或雷声一样。帖撒罗尼迦在大道上，又是港口，来往的人很多，这群信徒的故事就这样传开了。
- **不是靠宣传：** 保罗说「所以不用我们说什么话」（1:8）。一群普通人在受苦中仍然喜乐地信靠神，这本身就是最响亮的见证。

> 要点：福音最有力的见证，常常不是一场精彩的布道会，而是一群受苦的普通人，因圣灵所赐的喜乐仍然站立得住。

## 深读 1:9-10 离弃偶像，等候他儿子

**1. 掉转方向。** 「你们是怎样离弃偶像，归向神」（1:9）。「归向」原文就是转身、回头的意思，旧约先知常用它呼召以色列回转（参耶3:12-14）。信主不只是在原有的生活上加一点宗教，而是转过身来：脸向着神，背向着偶像。
- **重点在神：** 原文先说「归向神」，再说「离弃偶像」。这是同一个转身，不是先后两步，但重点在神身上。人不是先靠自己丢掉偶像，才配来到神面前；而是看见了神，偶像就不再有那么大的吸引力。

**2. 「又真又活的神」。** 偶像是死的、假的：「有口却不能言，有眼却不能看」（诗115:5）。神是「又真又活的」。他是真的，不是人手所造的；他是活的，会说话，会行事，会救人。
- **今天的偶像：** 对许多华人家庭来说，偶像是很具体的：家里的神台、祖先牌位、观音像、财神，还有逢年过节的拜祭。离弃这些，往往会带来家人的误会和压力。信徒不可焚香、跪拜祖先牌位或神明（林前10:20-21），但仍要用敬重和温柔对待家人，可以用别的方式表达孝敬和怀念（参本研读林前第八章、第十章关于祭祀和祭物的讨论）。不过，偶像也可以是看不见的：任何我们指望它带来安全、意义和价值，却不是神的东西，都可能成为偶像，例如金钱、事业、别人的认可，甚至一段关系。

**3. 服事和等候。** 「要服事那又真又活的神，等候他儿子从天降临」（1:9-10）。离开偶像不是留下一片空白，而是有了新的主人和新的盼望。
- **服事：** 原文是作奴仆的意思。从前是偶像的奴仆，如今是神的仆人。但这位主人不像偶像那样要人用祭物去讨好他，他自己先爱了我们。
- **等候：** 信徒的生活有一个方向：主要来。这等候不是坐着看天，而是在盼望中忠心地生活（参4:11-12；5:6）。

**4. 「救我们脱离将来忿怒的耶稣」。** 这一节浓缩了福音的要点：他是神的儿子；神叫他从死里复活；他要从天降临；他救我们脱离将来的忿怒。
- **将来的忿怒：** 神的忿怒不是喜怒无常的情绪，而是他对罪公义、圣洁的审判。保罗毫不避讳地说，这忿怒是「将来」必有的。
- **他怎样救：** 保罗在5:9-10说：「神不是预定我们受刑，乃是预定我们藉着我们主耶稣基督得救。他替我们死」。耶稣代替我们承受了罪的刑罚，所以信他的人不必再承受。
- **「救我们」：** 原文用的是现在分词。有人认为这是一个称号，就是那位拯救我们的，正像以赛亚书说「必有一位救赎主来到锡安」（赛59:20；参罗11:26）；也有人认为这里的现在式指向将来那日的拯救。无论哪一种，他都是我们的拯救者。

> 要点：福音叫人从偶像转向又真又活的神，现在服事他，将来等候他的儿子。我们敢等候那审判的日子，是因为来的那一位，正是替我们死、救我们脱离忿怒的耶稣。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们是怎样离弃偶像，归向神，要服事那又真又活的神，等候他儿子从天降临」（1:9-10）。有人信主的时候，真的把家里的神像搬了出去；有人从小在教会长大，从来没有拜过什么偶像，就以为这句话和自己没有关系。可是偶像不一定摆在神台上。我最怕失去的是什么？当一切都不顺的时候，我第一个想抓住的是什么？是存款，是孩子的前途，是一个人的认可，还是我的健康和能干？也许我早已归向了神，却在一些日子里又悄悄转过身去，向这些东西要平安。帖撒罗尼迦人的转身并不轻松，他们为此受了苦；我也不必假装自己的心已经完全转过来了。但保罗说，他们等候的那一位，是「救我们脱离将来忿怒的耶稣」。他不是等我把偶像全清干净了才来救我；他在我还背着脸的时候，就已经为我死了。今天，我可以把那个最想抓住的东西说出来，告诉他：「主啊，这不是我的神，你才是。」然后转过身来，不是因为我有力量转，而是因为他一直在那里，又真又活，并且要来接我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 is one long thanksgiving, in three parts. **Greeting** (v. 1): Paul, Silvanus and Timothy write "to the church of the Thessalonians in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ." **Thanks for God's work** (vv. 2–5): Paul thanks God for their faith, love and hope, and knows they are chosen, because the gospel came to them "not only in word, but also in power and in the Holy Spirit." **They became an example** (vv. 6–10): in much affliction they still had the joy of the Holy Spirit, and their faith became known everywhere; people all over are telling how they "turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God, and to wait for his Son from heaven."

The focus of the whole chapter is not how good the Thessalonians were but what God did in them: God loved them, chose them, and changed them by the power of the gospel. The one Paul thanks is God.

## Going Deeper: 1:1 The Church in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ

**1. Three writers.** "Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy, To the church..." (1:1). Silvanus is the Silas who travels with Paul in Acts (Acts 15:40; 17:4); Silvanus is his Roman name. Timothy is Paul's young co-worker, recently sent back to Thessalonica (3:2). Here Paul does not call himself an "apostle" (compare Gal 1:1; 1 Cor 1:1). One likely reason is that his relationship with this church is close, and he has no need to establish his authority first.

**2. "In God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ."** Paul says this church is "in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ." Writing about twenty years after Jesus' resurrection, Paul already sets "the Lord Jesus Christ" alongside "God the Father," joined by a single preposition, saying the church lives in both the Father and the Son. When a strictly monotheistic Jew speaks this way, many take this to show that in the earliest church Jesus was already honored as Lord, sharing the honor that belongs to God (see 3:11, where "our God and Father himself, and our Lord Jesus" together direct Paul's way, and the verb there is singular).
- **Whose church?** Thessalonica had many groups: trade guilds, religious associations, the citizens' assembly. In Greek cities the word for "church" meant the citizens' assembly; in the Greek Old Testament it often translates the "assembly" of Israel gathered before God (see Deut 4:10). Paul says this group's identity lies not in which city or guild they belong to but in being in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

**3. "Grace to you and peace."** This is Paul's usual greeting, joining a Greek greeting (grace) with a Jewish one (peace). But in Paul's hands it is not mere politeness: grace is God's free love, and peace is being reconciled to God through that love. The whole letter rests on these two.

## Going Deeper: 1:2–5 Faith, Love, Hope and God's Choosing

**1. Three graces, three outworkings.** "Remembering before our God and Father your work of faith and labor of love and steadfastness of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ" (1:3). This is probably among the earliest places where faith, love and hope appear together (see Gal 5:5–6; 1 Cor 13:13; Col 1:4–5). Paul notices not only the three themselves but the fruit they bear in life:
- **Faith works:** true faith is not idle; it moves (see Gal 5:6). This is not salvation by works; saving faith will always show itself.
- **Love labors:** the word "labor" means toil to the point of exhaustion. Love is not only a feeling but a willingness to be worn out for others.
- **Hope endures:** "steadfastness" is not passive putting up with things but standing firm under pressure and refusing to give up. They could do this because their hope was "in our Lord Jesus Christ"; their hope had a sure object.

**2. "We know... that he has chosen you."** "For we know, brothers loved by God, that he has chosen you" (1:4). How did Paul know? He had not seen God's book; he had seen God's work: "because our gospel came to you not only in word, but also in power and in the Holy Spirit and with full conviction" (1:5). Their transformation was the mark of God's choosing.
- **Choosing comes from love:** Paul first says "loved by God," then "chosen." Election is not cold fate but God's love resolving before time to have them as his own (see Deut 7:7–8).
- **Different understandings:** Church history holds different views of how God's choosing relates to human response. The Reformed tradition (Calvin, and today J. I. Packer) holds that before creation God chose some in Christ unconditionally (Eph 1:4), and they will surely believe. The Arminian and Wesleyan traditions (such as I. Howard Marshall) hold that God chose those he foreknew would believe; others (such as William W. Klein) stress that God chose first a people in Christ, which individuals join by faith. All these views agree that salvation is wholly by God's grace, that the gospel is to be preached to all, and that no one may use election as an excuse not to believe or not to evangelize.
- **What this verse is for:** Paul speaks of election not to make people guess whether they are on the list but to give suffering believers assurance: your faith is no accident; God's hand has been at work.

**3. "Not only in word."** Paul is not saying words do not matter; the gospel must be put into words. He is saying that beyond the words it came "in power and in the Holy Spirit." The Spirit makes people hear not just an argument but God's own call.
- **"Full conviction":** this may mean that the preachers spoke with full assurance, or that the hearers were persuaded and convinced. In context both fit; since 1:5b goes on to "what kind of men we proved to be," many (such as Jeffrey Weima) lean toward the preachers' assurance.
- **"What kind of men we proved to be":** Paul at once mentions his own conduct (1:5b). The power of the gospel also showed in those who preached it. Chapter 2 develops this.

> The point: Believers can know they are chosen by God, not by prying into his hidden will, but by seeing that his gospel has truly done its work in them. The root of election is God's love, not our goodness.

## Going Deeper: 1:6–8 The Joy of the Spirit in Much Affliction

**1. Affliction and joy together.** "You received the word in much affliction, with the joy of the Holy Spirit" (1:6). "Affliction" means pressure, persecution. They suffered as soon as they believed: opposition from family, rejection by neighbors, perhaps the loss of a livelihood (see 2:14; Acts 17:5–9). Yet at the same time they had joy. This joy did not come from their circumstances; it was the joy "of the Holy Spirit."
- **Not pretending it doesn't hurt:** joy in Scripture does not require denying pain. Paul himself was distressed in his own suffering (3:7, "in all our distress and affliction"). The Spirit's joy is knowing, even in pain, that God is mine and I am his.

**2. Imitators of us and of the Lord.** "You became imitators of us and of the Lord" (1:6). What they imitated was not Paul's eloquence but his way of preaching the gospel even in affliction (2:2); and that way came from the Lord Jesus, "who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame" (Heb 12:2).
- **A chain of imitation:** the Lord → Paul → the Thessalonians → the believers in Macedonia and Achaia (1:7). The gospel is often passed on not by books but by one life shaping another.

**3. The word sounded forth from you.** "For not only has the word of the Lord sounded forth from you" (1:8). The Greek verb behind "sounded forth" appears only here in the New Testament, and many take it to suggest a loud, echoing sound, like a trumpet or thunder. Thessalonica sat on the highway and was a port, with many travelers, and the story of these believers spread that way.
- **Not by publicity:** Paul says, "so that we need not say anything" (1:8). A group of ordinary people still trusting God with joy in suffering is itself the loudest testimony.

> The point: The most powerful witness to the gospel is often not a brilliant evangelistic rally but a group of ordinary people who suffer and still stand, held up by the joy the Holy Spirit gives.

## Going Deeper: 1:9–10 Turned from Idols, Waiting for His Son

**1. Turning around.** "How you turned to God from idols" (1:9). "Turned" means to turn around, to come back; the Old Testament prophets often used it to call Israel to return (see Jer 3:12–14). Coming to faith is not adding a little religion to the life one already has; it is turning around: face toward God, back toward the idols.
- **The weight on God:** the Greek names God first, "to God from idols." This is one turning, not two steps, but the weight falls on God. People do not first get rid of their idols by their own strength and so become fit to come to God; they see God, and the idols lose much of their pull.

**2. "The living and true God."** Idols are dead and false: "They have mouths, but do not speak; eyes, but do not see" (Ps 115:5). God is "living and true." He is true, not made by human hands; he is living: he speaks, acts and saves.
- **Idols today:** for many Chinese families, idols are very concrete: the family altar and ancestor tablets, a statue of Guanyin or the god of wealth, and offerings at festivals. Leaving these often brings misunderstanding and pressure from family. Believers must not burn incense or bow to ancestor tablets or deities (1 Cor 10:20–21), yet they should still treat their families with respect and gentleness, and may honor and remember them in other ways (see this study's discussion of offerings and sacrificial food in 1 Corinthians chapters 8 and 10). But idols can also be invisible: anything we look to for security, meaning and worth that is not God can become an idol, such as money, career, others' approval, even a relationship.

**3. Serving and waiting.** "To serve the living and true God, and to wait for his Son from heaven" (1:9–10). Leaving idols does not leave an empty space; it brings a new master and a new hope.
- **Serving:** the word means to serve as a slave. Once slaves of idols, they are now God's servants. But this master is not like the idols, who must be appeased with offerings; he loved us first.
- **Waiting:** the believer's life has a direction: the Lord is coming. This waiting is not sitting and staring at the sky but living faithfully in hope (see 4:11–12; 5:6).

**4. "Jesus who delivers us from the wrath to come."** This verse condenses the heart of the gospel: he is God's Son; God raised him from the dead; he will come from heaven; he delivers us from the wrath to come.
- **The wrath to come:** God's wrath is not a moody outburst but his righteous and holy judgment on sin. Paul says plainly that this wrath is surely "to come."
- **How he delivers:** in 5:9–10 Paul says, "God has not destined us for wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ, who died for us." Jesus bore sin's penalty in our place, so those who trust him need not bear it.
- **"Who delivers us":** the Greek is a present participle. Some read it as a title, our Deliverer, like the Redeemer who "will come to Zion" (Isa 59:20; see Rom 11:26, "the Deliverer"); others read the present as pointing to the rescue on that coming day. Either way, he is our rescuer.

> The point: The gospel turns people from idols to the living and true God, to serve him now and wait for his Son. We dare to await the day of judgment because the one who is coming is Jesus, who died for us and delivers us from wrath.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "How you turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God, and to wait for his Son from heaven" (1:9–10). Some people, when they came to faith, really did carry the idols out of their homes. Others grew up in church, never bowed to anything, and assume this verse has nothing to do with them. But idols are not always on an altar. What am I most afraid of losing? When everything goes wrong, what is the first thing I reach for? Savings, my children's future, someone's approval, my health and competence? Perhaps I turned to God long ago, yet on some days I quietly turn back around and ask these things for peace. The Thessalonians' turning was not easy; they suffered for it. I need not pretend my heart has fully turned either. But Paul says the one they were waiting for is "Jesus who delivers us from the wrath to come." He did not wait until I had cleared out every idol before he came to save me; while my back was still turned, he had already died for me. Today I can name the thing I most want to cling to and tell him, "Lord, this is not my God; you are." Then turn around, not because I have the strength to turn, but because he has been there all along, living and true, and he is coming for me.
