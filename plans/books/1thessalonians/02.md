## 本章脉络

第二章有四段。**保罗怎样传福音**（1-6节）：他在腓立比受了害，仍放胆传讲；他不是要讨人喜欢，没有用谄媚的话，也没有藏着贪心。**像母亲，又像父亲**（7-12节）：他在他们中间存心温柔，「如同母亲乳养自己的孩子」；又昼夜做工，免得叫人受累；他劝勉他们，「好像父亲待自己的儿女一样」。**领受神的道，也为它受苦**（13-16节）：他们领受神的道，就像犹太的教会一样，受了同胞的苦害。**被迫离别的思念**（17-20节）：保罗很想回去，却被撒但阻挡；他们是保罗在主来的时候的盼望、喜乐和冠冕。

保罗为什么要花这么长的篇幅讲自己当初的为人？许多解经家（如杰弗里·韦玛）认为，他走得匆忙，城里有人趁机说他的坏话：这些人不过是到处骗钱、骗人追随的江湖客，一遇到麻烦就溜了。当时的希腊城市里确实有不少这样的游方讲师。也有学者（如亚伯拉罕·马勒布）认为，保罗未必是在回应具体的指控，而是用当时的人熟悉的对比，把自己的为人摆出来作榜样。无论哪一种，保罗都是为福音说话：如果传福音的人是骗子，福音也就站不住了。

## 深读 2:1-6 不是要讨人喜欢

**1. 受了害仍放胆。** 「我们从前在腓立比被害受辱……然而还是靠我们的神放开胆量，在大争战中把神的福音传给你们」（2:2）。在腓立比，保罗和西拉被剥了衣服，用棍打，下在监里（徒16:22-24）。一个只为名利的人，不会刚被打完就到下一个城去讲同样的话。
- **胆量从哪里来：** 保罗说是「靠我们的神」。勇气不是天生的性格，而是信靠神的结果。

**2. 三个「不是」。** 「我们的劝勉不是出于错误，不是出于污秽，也不是用诡诈」（2:3）。「错误」是指所传的内容是假的；「污秽」可能指不纯的动机；保罗在4:7用这个词指性方面的不洁，所以也有人认为他在否认借传道满足私欲；「诡诈」是指用手段骗人上钩。保罗说，这三样都没有。

**3. 讨神喜欢，不是讨人喜欢。** 「神既然验中了我们，把福音托付我们，我们就照样讲，不是要讨人喜欢，乃是要讨那察验我们心的神喜欢」（2:4）。「验中」和「察验」是同一个词根，原指试验金属，看它是不是真的。神先察验了保罗，把福音托付他；神也一直在察验他的心。
- **福音是托付的：** 保罗不是福音的主人，只是受托的管家（参林前4:1-2）。管家不能按客人的口味改动主人的东西。
- **今天的试探：** 传道人和信徒都会面对这个试探：讲人爱听的，避开人不爱听的；或者为了被接纳，把福音说得软一点。保罗的答案是：我要向那察验人心的神交账。

**4. 没有谄媚，没有贪心，不求荣耀。** 「我们从来没有用过谄媚的话……也没有藏着贪心，这是神可以作见证的。我们作基督的使徒，虽然可以叫人尊重，却没有向你们或向别人求荣耀」（2:5-6）。
- **谄媚：** 说好听的话，为的是得到好处。
- **贪心的幌子：** 原文是用一个好听的理由来掩盖贪心。保罗说这件事人看不见，所以他请神作见证。
- **「可以叫人尊重」：** 原文直译是可以成为重担，可以理解为使徒有权要求供养（参林前9:4-6），也可以理解为可以摆出使徒的威严。两种意思都通：保罗本可以要，却选择不要。保罗在2:9说「免得叫你们一人受累」，用的是相关的词，这比较支持前一种理解。

> 要点：传福音的人要向那察验人心的神交账，不是向听众的掌声交账。福音是神托付的，我们没有权柄按自己的方便去改它。

## 深读 2:7-12 像母亲，又像父亲

**1. 「如同母亲乳养自己的孩子」。** 「只在你们中间存心温柔，如同母亲乳养自己的孩子」（2:7）。这里的「母亲」原文是乳母，但保罗特别加上「自己的」孩子：不是受雇的乳母照顾别人的孩子，而是母亲亲自喂养自己的骨肉。在一个男性领袖常以威严为美的文化里，一位使徒说自己像喂奶的母亲，是很惊人的。
- **一个抄本问题：** 「温柔」和「婴孩」在原文只差一个字母，而前一个词正好以这个字母结尾，抄写时很容易多抄或漏抄。较早、较多的抄本作「婴孩」，意思是保罗在他们中间像婴孩一样，没有心机、不摆架子；现在通行的希腊文新约校勘本也采用这个读法，许多学者（如杰弗里·韦玛、戈登·费依）认为这是原文。但和合本和多数英文译本照另一些抄本译作「温柔」，也有学者认为「婴孩」和紧接着的母亲比喻不太协调。无论哪一种，意思都相近：保罗不用权势压人。

**2. 连自己的性命也给你们。** 「我们既是这样爱你们，不但愿意将神的福音给你们，连自己的性命也愿意给你们，因你们是我们所疼爱的」（2:8）。「性命」原文也可以译作「自己」：保罗给他们的不只是一篇讲道，而是他自己，他的时间、他的心、他的生活。
- **福音和生命连在一起：** 有些人传福音像推销员发传单；保罗却像父母一样，把自己的生活向他们敞开。这正是基督的样式：他「为我舍己」（加2:20）。

**3. 昼夜做工。** 「你们记念我们的辛苦劳碌，昼夜做工，传神的福音给你们，免得叫你们一人受累」（2:9）。保罗是制造帐棚的（徒18:3），可能是做皮革活。这种手艺人每天从天亮做到天黑，社会地位不高。许多学者认为，保罗白天在作坊里做工，边做工边和来往的人谈福音，晚上很可能还要教导他们。
- **为什么不收钱：** 保罗不是认为传道人不该受供养（林前9:14；加6:6），而是在这样一个新的教会里，他不愿意给人任何借口说他是为钱来的。

**4. 「好像父亲待自己的儿女一样」。** 「我们怎样劝勉你们，安慰你们，嘱咐你们各人，好像父亲待自己的儿女一样，要叫你们行事对得起那召你们进他国、得他荣耀的神」（2:11-12）。母亲的形象说的是温柔和舍己，父亲的形象说的是教导和引导。在古代，父亲有责任教导儿女怎样做人。
- **「各人」：** 保罗不是只对着人群讲道，而是一个一个地关心。
- **三个动词：** 劝勉、安慰、嘱咐。有时要推一把，有时要扶一把，有时要郑重地提醒。
- **目标：** 「行事对得起……神」。不是为了讨保罗喜欢，而是因为神已经召他们进入他的国和荣耀。先有神的呼召，然后才有这样的生活。

**5. 属灵的父母不是控制人的理由。** 保罗用母亲和父亲的比喻，是为了说明他怎样温柔、舍己地爱他们，不是为了说明他有权控制他们。今天有些教会领袖也喜欢自称「属灵父亲」「属灵母亲」，却用这个称呼要求别人绝对顺服、不许质疑，甚至在金钱、婚姻或身体上利用别人。这正是保罗在2:3-6所拒绝的：「污秽」「诡诈」「谄媚」「贪心」。如果有人以属灵权柄为名伤害了你，那不是你的错；你可以离开，可以向可靠的人求助；如果涉及侵犯和犯罪，应当报警，交给执法和司法机关处理（罗13:4）。受害的人不必和不安全的人和好；就算施害的人认罪、得了饶恕，也不表示他可以恢复职分，或再接触受害人和孩子。教会不可以为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩。

> 要点：保罗像母亲一样温柔，像父亲一样引导，把自己的生命和福音一起给了人。这样的爱是从基督来的：他不但把真理告诉我们，更把自己给了我们。

## 深读 2:13-16 领受神的道，也为它受苦

**1. 不是人的道，乃是神的道。** 「因你们听见我们所传神的道就领受了；不以为是人的道，乃以为是神的道。这道实在是神的，并且运行在你们信主的人心中」（2:13）。原文有两个动词：一个是领受传下来的道，这是保罗用来指领受福音传统的词（参林前15:1-3）；一个是心里接纳，像欢迎一位客人进门一样。他们听的是保罗的声音，却认出了神的话。
- **神的道在做工：** 「运行」这个词在保罗书信中常用来指神的大能在做工（参腓2:13）。神的道不是写在纸上就停在那里，而是活的，会改变人（参来4:12）。

**2. 受同胞的苦害。** 「你们也受了本地人的苦害，像他们受了犹太人的苦害一样」（2:14）。帖撒罗尼迦的信徒多半是外邦人，逼迫他们的也是他们自己城里的人，包括亲戚、邻舍、同行。犹太地的基督徒也受过自己同胞的逼迫（参徒8:1-3；保罗自己从前就是逼迫的人）。
- **今天的「本地人」：** 许多华人信徒最深的压力，不是来自陌生人，而是来自家里：父母说你背祖忘宗，亲戚说你不孝，同事说你古怪。保罗告诉帖撒罗尼迦人：你们不是第一批这样受苦的人，在你们以前，神的众教会都走过这条路。

**3. 这几句严厉的话怎样理解？** 「这犹太人杀了主耶稣和先知，又把我们赶出去。他们不得神的喜悦，且与众人为敌，不许我们传道给外邦人使外邦人得救，常常充满自己的罪恶。」（2:15-16）这几句话在历史上曾被人用来仇视犹太人，所以要仔细读。许多解经家认为，这里指的是犹太地那些反对福音的人；使徒行传也记载，帖撒罗尼迦的风波是会堂里不信的人鼓动起来的（徒17:5）。
- **保罗自己是犹太人：** 他说的不是犹太民族本身，而是那些拒绝耶稣、并且阻挡福音传给外邦人的人。他在罗马书中为自己的骨肉之亲「大有忧愁，心里时常伤痛」（罗9:2），又说神并没有弃绝他的百姓（罗11:1-2）。
- **先知式的责备：** 这样的话是以色列内部的先知传统，正像耶稣自己责备耶路撒冷「杀害先知」（太23:37），司提反说「哪一个先知不是你们祖宗逼迫呢？」（徒7:52）。这是家里人在说家里的事，不是外人在辱骂一个民族。
- **「充满自己的罪恶」：** 这是旧约的说法，参创世记15:16「亚摩利人的罪孽还没有满盈」；耶稣也说「你们去充满你们祖宗的恶贯吧！」（太23:32）。罪积满了，审判就要来到。
- **谁杀了耶稣？** 新约说，希律、彼拉多、外邦人和以色列民都参与了（徒4:27）。但耶稣是「按着神的定旨先见被交与人」（徒2:23）；他「被交给人，是为我们的过犯」（罗4:25）：是我们的罪把他钉上十字架。
- **教会的悔改：** 历史上，一些自称基督徒的人曾拿这样的经文逼害犹太人，这是极大的罪，与福音完全相反。

**4. 「神的忿怒临在他们身上已经到了极处」。** 这句话（2:16）怎样理解，解经家有几种看法：
- **指已经发生的事：** 有人认为指当时临到犹太人的灾难，例如一般认为在主后49年罗马皇帝驱逐犹太人（参徒18:2），或者同一时期在耶路撒冷圣殿发生的屠杀和饥荒。也有人认为这句话是在主后70年以后加进去的（参导读）。
- **指必然的结局：** 有人认为保罗用过去式，是要说明神的审判已经确定，像先知讲将来的事常用过去式一样。
- **「到了极处」：** 这个短语也可以译作「终于」或「直到末了」。

无论哪一种，保罗都没有说以色列被永远弃绝；他在罗马书11章盼望「以色列全家都要得救」（罗11:26）。

> 要点：神的道在领受它的人心里有能力，这能力也常常带来苦难。但受苦的信徒并不孤单，在他们以前，神的众教会都走过这条路；而那位被杀的主耶稣，正是为我们受死的。

## 深读 2:17-20 我们的盼望和喜乐

**1. 被硬生生拆开。** 「弟兄们，我们暂时与你们离别，是面目离别，心里却不离别」（2:17）。「离别」原文的意思是像孤儿一样被拆开。我们通常说孩子失去父母是孤儿，许多解经家认为保罗在这里却用这个词形容父母失去孩子。他走得太突然，信徒又太年轻，他心里非常牵挂。
- **「极力地想法子」：** 保罗不是说说而已，他真的一再想办法回去。

**2. 「只是撒但阻挡了我们」。** 「我－保罗有一两次要去，只是撒但阻挡了我们」（2:18）。保罗没有说是怎样阻挡的。有人推测可能和耶孙交的保状有关（徒17:9），城里的官员也许不许保罗再回来；也有人推测是疾病或其他逼迫。
- **属灵的争战是真实的：** 保罗不把一切都看成单纯的环境。在神主权的掌管下，撒但确实在抵挡福音。但撒但不是最后的赢家：保罗去不成，就写了这封信，今天全世界的教会都在读它。
- **不必凡事都怪撒但：** 使徒行传记载，圣灵（也就是耶稣的灵）曾禁止他们去某些地方（徒16:6-7）。他没有教我们把每一次不顺都归给魔鬼；分辨的时候需要谦卑和祷告。

**3. 「所夸的冠冕」。** 「我们的盼望和喜乐，并所夸的冠冕是什么呢？岂不是我们主耶稣来的时候、你们在他面前站立得住吗？因为你们就是我们的荣耀，我们的喜乐」（2:19-20）。「冠冕」原文是花冠，可以指运动会上得胜者的花冠，也可以指节庆或尊荣的花冠；「所夸的冠冕」这个说法，在希腊文旧约中也出现过（参箴16:31）。
- **「来的时候」：** 这个词原文常用来指君王或大官正式到访一座城。耶稣自己也用这个词指他的降临（太24:27），保罗用它来指主耶稣的再来。
- **人就是冠冕：** 保罗在主面前最想夸的，不是自己建了多少教会、讲了多少道，而是这些人自己。传福音的人最大的赏赐，是看见他所爱的人站在主面前。

> 要点：保罗心里最深的盼望和喜乐，不是他自己的成就，而是他所爱的人在主来的时候站在主面前。他爱他们，因为基督先爱了他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我们既是这样爱你们，不但愿意将神的福音给你们，连自己的性命也愿意给你们，因你们是我们所疼爱的。」（2:8）有时我很愿意给人一些东西：一本书、一段经文、一句劝勉，甚至一次很用心的查经。可是我不太愿意给出我自己。我不想让别人看见我的软弱，不想让他们走进我的家、我的时间、我的生活，因为那样太累，也太危险，我可能会被看穿，也可能会被辜负。也许我曾经把自己给出去，结果受了伤，从此就学会了保持距离。保罗不是不知道这代价，他「辛苦劳碌，昼夜做工」，后来还为这些人被硬生生拆开，心里痛了很久。可是他仍然说：你们是我们所疼爱的。他这样爱，是因为他自己先被这样爱过。耶稣没有只从天上把真理丢下来给我，他亲自来了，住在我们中间，「为我舍己」（加2:20）。在我还不可爱的时候，他就把自己给了我。今天，我不必一下子向所有人敞开；但我可以想一想，有没有一个人，神放在我身边，需要的不只是我的话，而是我这个人？我可以带着基督给我的爱，向那个人走近一步。就算我走得很笨拙，就算我仍有惧怕，他已经把他自己给了我，这就够了。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 has four parts. **How Paul preached the gospel** (vv. 1–6): after suffering at Philippi he still spoke boldly; he did not seek to please people, used no flattery and hid no greed. **Like a mother, and like a father** (vv. 7–12): he was gentle among them, "like a nursing mother taking care of her own children"; he worked night and day so as not to be a burden; he exhorted them "like a father with his children." **Receiving God's word and suffering for it** (vv. 13–16): they received the word of God and, like the churches in Judea, suffered from their own countrymen. **Longing after being torn away** (vv. 17–20): Paul longed to come back but Satan hindered him; they are his hope, joy and crown at the Lord's coming.

Why does Paul spend so long on how he lived among them? Many commentators (such as Jeffrey Weima) think that he left in a hurry, and some in the city used it to slander him: these men are just wandering con artists after money and followers, who slip away at the first sign of trouble. Greek cities of the day did have many such traveling teachers. Others (such as Abraham Malherbe) think Paul is not answering specific charges but presenting his conduct as a model, using a contrast his hearers knew well. Either way, Paul is speaking for the gospel: if those who preached it were frauds, the gospel itself would fall.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–6 Not to Please People

**1. Bold after suffering.** "Though we had already suffered and been shamefully treated at Philippi... we had boldness in our God to declare to you the gospel of God in the midst of much conflict" (2:2). At Philippi Paul and Silas were stripped, beaten with rods and thrown into prison (Acts 16:22–24). A man seeking only fame and money would not go straight on to the next city to say the same thing.
- **Where the boldness came from:** Paul says it was "in our God." Courage is not an inborn temperament but the fruit of trusting God.

**2. Three "nots."** "For our appeal does not spring from error or impurity or any attempt to deceive" (2:3). "Error" means the message was false; "impurity" may mean impure motives; Paul uses the word for sexual impurity in 4:7, so some think he is also denying any sexual motive; "deceive" means using tricks to hook people. Paul says none of these was true of him.

**3. Pleasing God, not people.** "But just as we have been approved by God to be entrusted with the gospel, so we speak, not to please man, but to please God who tests our hearts" (2:4). "Approved" and "tests" share a root that originally meant testing metal to see whether it was genuine. God first tested Paul and entrusted him with the gospel, and God keeps testing his heart.
- **The gospel is a trust:** Paul is not the owner of the gospel, only a steward entrusted with it (see 1 Cor 4:1–2). A steward may not alter the master's goods to suit the guests' taste.
- **Today's temptation:** preachers and ordinary believers alike face this temptation: say what people like to hear and avoid what they don't, or soften the gospel to be accepted. Paul's answer is: I must give account to God, who tests the heart.

**4. No flattery, no greed, no seeking glory.** "For we never came with words of flattery, as you know, nor with a pretext for greed—God is witness. Nor did we seek glory from people, whether from you or from others, though we could have made demands as apostles of Christ" (2:5–6).
- **Flattery:** saying pleasant things in order to get something.
- **A pretext for greed:** a fine-sounding reason used to cover greed. Paul says people cannot see this, so he calls God as witness.
- **"Could have made demands":** the Greek is literally that they could have been a weight. It may mean apostles had a right to be supported (see 1 Cor 9:4–6), or that they could have stood on apostolic dignity. Both fit: Paul could have claimed it and chose not to. In 2:9 he uses a related word ("that we might not be a burden"), which leans toward the first reading.

> The point: Those who preach the gospel answer to God who tests the heart, not to the applause of the audience. The gospel is entrusted to us by God; we have no right to alter it for our own convenience.

## Going Deeper: 2:7–12 Like a Mother, and Like a Father

**1. "Like a nursing mother taking care of her own children."** "But we were gentle among you, like a nursing mother taking care of her own children" (2:7). The word can mean a wet nurse, but Paul adds "her own" children: not a hired nurse caring for someone else's child, but a mother nursing her own. In a culture where male leaders prized dignity and severity, an apostle comparing himself to a nursing mother was startling.
- **A manuscript question:** in Greek, "gentle" and "infants" differ by a single letter, and the word before ends in that letter, so a scribe could easily add or drop it. The earlier and broader manuscript evidence reads "infants," meaning Paul was like a little child among them, without guile or pretension; the standard critical Greek text prints it, and many scholars (such as Jeffrey Weima and Gordon Fee) accept it. The CUV and most English versions follow other manuscripts in reading "gentle," and some scholars think "infants" sits awkwardly beside the nursing-mother image that follows. Either way the sense is close: Paul did not throw his weight around.

**2. Our own selves as well.** "So, being affectionately desirous of you, we were ready to share with you not only the gospel of God but also our own selves, because you had become very dear to us" (2:8). Paul gave them not just a sermon but himself: his time, his heart, his life.
- **The gospel and a life together:** some people share the gospel like a salesman handing out flyers; Paul, like a parent, opened his life to them. This is Christ's own way: he "gave himself for me" (Gal 2:20).

**3. Working night and day.** "For you remember, brothers, our labor and toil: we worked night and day, that we might not be a burden to any of you, while we proclaimed to you the gospel of God" (2:9). Paul was a tentmaker (Acts 18:3), likely a leatherworker. Such craftsmen worked from dawn to dusk and had low social standing. Many scholars think Paul worked in the shop by day, talking about the gospel with whoever came by, and probably taught them in the evenings as well.
- **Why he took no money:** Paul did not think preachers should not be supported (1 Cor 9:14; Gal 6:6), but in a brand-new church like this he would not give anyone an excuse to say he had come for money.

**4. "Like a father with his children."** "For you know how, like a father with his children, we exhorted each one of you and encouraged you and charged you to walk in a manner worthy of God, who calls you into his own kingdom and glory" (2:11–12). The mother image speaks of gentleness and self-giving; the father image speaks of teaching and guiding. In the ancient world a father was responsible for teaching his children how to live.
- **"Each one of you":** Paul did not only preach to a crowd; he cared for them one by one.
- **Three verbs:** exhorted, encouraged, charged. Sometimes a push, sometimes a hand to steady, sometimes a solemn reminder.
- **The goal:** "to walk in a manner worthy of God." Not to please Paul, but because God has already called them into his kingdom and glory. God's call comes first; this life follows.

**5. Spiritual parents are no license for control.** Paul uses mother and father images to show how gently and self-givingly he loved them, not to claim a right to control them. Today some church leaders like to call themselves "spiritual fathers" or "spiritual mothers," yet use the title to demand absolute obedience, forbid questions, or even exploit others financially, in marriage, or sexually. This is exactly what Paul rejects in 2:3–6: "impurity," "deceive," "flattery," "greed." If someone has harmed you in the name of spiritual authority, it is not your fault. You may leave, and you may seek help from someone trustworthy; where there is abuse or crime, it should be reported to the police and dealt with by the authorities and courts (Rom 13:4). Victims need not reconcile with someone unsafe; even if the offender confesses and is forgiven, that does not restore his office or give him access to victims or children. The church must not cover it up to keep "family shame" from getting out.

> The point: Paul was gentle like a mother and guiding like a father, giving people his own life together with the gospel. Such love comes from Christ: he did not only tell us the truth; he gave us himself.

## Going Deeper: 2:13–16 Receiving God's Word and Suffering for It

**1. Not the word of men but the word of God.** "When you received the word of God, which you heard from us, you accepted it not as the word of men but as what it really is, the word of God, which is at work in you believers" (2:13). Paul uses two verbs here. The first ("received") is the word for receiving a handed-on message (see 1 Cor 15:1–3); the second ("accepted") means welcoming it in the heart, as one welcomes a guest in at the door. They heard Paul's voice, yet recognized God's word.
- **God's word at work:** the verb "at work" is often used in Paul for God's power working (see Phil 2:13). God's word does not stay put once written on paper; it is living and changes people (see Heb 4:12).

**2. Suffering from their own countrymen.** "For you suffered the same things from your own countrymen as they did from the Jews" (2:14). The Thessalonian believers were mostly Gentiles, and those persecuting them were people of their own city: relatives, neighbors, people in the same trade. The Christians in Judea had also been persecuted by their own people (see Acts 8:1–3; Paul himself had been one of the persecutors).
- **Today's "countrymen":** for many Chinese believers, the deepest pressure comes not from strangers but from home: parents who say you have abandoned your ancestors, relatives who call you unfilial, coworkers who find you strange. Paul tells the Thessalonians: you are not the first to suffer like this; God's churches have walked this road before you.

**3. How to read these harsh words?** "The Jews, who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets, and drove us out, and displease God and oppose all mankind by hindering us from speaking to the Gentiles that they might be saved—so as always to fill up the measure of their sins" (2:15–16). These words have been used in history to fuel hatred of Jews, so they must be read carefully. Many take Paul to mean the Judean opponents of the gospel; Acts also records that the trouble in Thessalonica was stirred up by unbelievers from the synagogue (Acts 17:5).
- **Paul himself was a Jew:** he is speaking not of the Jewish people as such but of those who rejected Jesus and blocked the gospel from going to the Gentiles. In Romans he has "great sorrow and unceasing anguish" for his kinsmen (Rom 9:2), and says God has not rejected his people (Rom 11:1–2).
- **Prophetic rebuke:** such words belong to Israel's own prophetic tradition, just as Jesus rebuked Jerusalem as the city "that kills the prophets" (Matt 23:37), and Stephen asked, "Which of the prophets did not your fathers persecute?" (Acts 7:52). It is one of the family speaking about the family, not an outsider abusing a people.
- **"To fill up the measure of their sins":** this is Old Testament language; see Genesis 15:16, "the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet complete," and Jesus' words, "Fill up, then, the measure of your fathers" (Matt 23:32). When sin is full, judgment comes.
- **Who killed Jesus?** The New Testament says Herod, Pilate, the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel were all involved (Acts 4:27). Yet Jesus was "delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God" (Acts 2:23); he "was delivered up for our trespasses" (Rom 4:25): our sins nailed him to the cross.
- **The church's repentance:** in history, some who called themselves Christians used passages like this to persecute Jews. This was a grievous sin, utterly contrary to the gospel.

**4. "God's wrath has come upon them at last."** Commentators read this line (2:16) in several ways:
- **An event already past:** some think it refers to a disaster then befalling the Jews, such as the emperor's expulsion of Jews from Rome, usually dated AD 49 (see Acts 18:2), or the massacre in the temple courts and the famine of the same period. Some think the line was added after AD 70 (see the introduction).
- **A certain outcome:** some think Paul uses the past tense to show that God's judgment is settled, as the prophets often spoke of future things in the past tense.
- **"At last":** the phrase can also be rendered "fully" or "to the end."

Whichever is right, Paul does not say Israel is rejected forever; in Romans 11 he hopes that "all Israel will be saved" (Rom 11:26).

> The point: God's word has power in those who receive it, and that power often brings suffering. But suffering believers are not alone; God's churches have walked this road before them, and the Lord Jesus who was killed is the one who died for us.

## Going Deeper: 2:17–20 Our Hope and Joy

**1. Torn apart.** "But since we were torn away from you, brothers, for a short time, in person not in heart" (2:17). Paul's word means to be torn apart like an orphan. We usually call a child who loses its parents an orphan, but many take Paul to use the word here for parents who lose their children. He left too suddenly, the believers were too young, and his heart was full of worry.
- **"Endeavored the more eagerly":** this was not mere talk; he really tried again and again to return.

**2. "But Satan hindered us."** "Because we wanted to come to you—I, Paul, again and again—but Satan hindered us" (2:18). Paul does not say how. Some guess it was connected with the security Jason had posted (Acts 17:9), so that the city officials would not let Paul return; others guess illness or other persecution.
- **Spiritual warfare is real:** Paul does not treat everything as mere circumstance. Under God's sovereign rule, Satan really does oppose the gospel. But Satan does not have the last word: Paul could not go, so he wrote this letter, and churches around the world read it today.
- **Not blaming Satan for everything:** Acts records that the Holy Spirit (the Spirit of Jesus) kept them from going to certain places (Acts 16:6–7). He does not teach us to blame the devil for every setback; discerning takes humility and prayer.

**3. "Crown of boasting."** "For what is our hope or joy or crown of boasting before our Lord Jesus at his coming? Is it not you? For you are our glory and joy" (2:19–20). The Greek word for "crown" is a wreath, given to victors at the games or as a festal or civic honor; the phrase "crown of boasting" also appears in the Greek Old Testament (see Prov 16:31).
- **"At his coming":** the Greek word was often used for the official visit of a king or high official to a city. Jesus used the same word for his own coming (Matt 24:27), and Paul uses it for the Lord Jesus' return.
- **People are the crown:** what Paul most wants to boast of before the Lord is not how many churches he planted or sermons he preached, but these people themselves. The greatest reward of one who shares the gospel is to see those he loves standing before the Lord.

> The point: Paul's deepest hope and joy was not his own achievement but that those he loved would be there before the Lord at his coming. He loved them because Christ had first loved him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "So, being affectionately desirous of you, we were ready to share with you not only the gospel of God but also our own selves, because you had become very dear to us" (2:8). Sometimes I am glad to give people things: a book, a verse, a word of encouragement, even a carefully prepared Bible study. But I am less willing to give myself. I don't want others to see my weakness; I don't want them coming into my home, my time, my life, because it is tiring and risky: I might be seen through, or let down. Perhaps I once gave myself and was hurt, and since then I have learned to keep my distance. Paul knew the cost. He "worked night and day," and later, torn away from these people, his heart ached for a long time. Yet he still said, you had become very dear to us. He loved like this because he had first been loved like this. Jesus did not just drop truth down to me from heaven; he came himself, lived among us, and "gave himself for me" (Gal 2:20). While I was still unlovely, he gave himself to me. Today I need not open my life to everyone at once. But I can think: is there one person God has placed near me who needs not only my words but me? I can take one step toward that person, carrying the love Christ has given me. Even if I step clumsily, even if I am still afraid, he has already given himself to me, and that is enough.
