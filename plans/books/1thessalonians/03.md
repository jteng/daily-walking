## 本章脉络

第三章接着第二章末了的思念，分成三段。**打发提摩太**（1-5节）：保罗「既不能再忍」，宁可自己独自留在雅典，也要打发提摩太回去坚固他们，免得他们被患难摇动。**好消息来了**（6-10节）：提摩太带回他们信心和爱心的好消息，保罗说：「你们若靠主站立得稳，我们就活了。」**保罗的祷告**（11-13节）：愿神开路让他回去，愿主叫他们的爱心增长，并在主耶稣同他众圣徒来的时候，心里坚固，成为圣洁。

这一章让我们看见一位牧者的心。保罗担心的不是自己的事工做得好不好，而是这群年轻的信徒会不会在患难中倒下。他的忧虑最后化成了祷告，而祷告的终点，又是主的再来。

## 深读 3:1-5 患难原是命定的

**1. 宁可独自留下。** 「我们既不能再忍，就愿意独自等在雅典，打发我们的兄弟在基督福音上作神执事的提摩太前去」（3:1-2）。在雅典，保罗是一个外地人（参徒17:16-34）；把提摩太打发回去以后，他身边就没有同工了。他「愿意」孤单，也要把最得力的帮手送回去。
- **提摩太的称呼：** 有些抄本称提摩太为神的同工，有些抄本作神的执事，和合本照后者翻译。称一个人为神的同工，说法很大胆，有些抄写的人也许因此改了字；多数经文鉴别学者认为，同工是原来的读法（参林前3:9「我们是与神同工的」）。无论哪一种，保罗都是在告诉帖撒罗尼迦人：我差来的不是一个小助手，而是在福音上为神做工的人。

**2. 去做什么？** 「坚固你们，并在你们所信的道上劝慰你们，免得有人被诸般患难摇动」（3:2-3）。「坚固」原指用支柱把东西撑稳；「摇动」这个词在古希腊文中可以形容狗摇尾巴讨好人，所以有人认为这里带着被花言巧语引离的意思。不过多数解经家认为，这里只是被摇动、不安。患难可以把人吓走，也可以用温和的方式把人慢慢拉走。
- **劝慰：** 这个词兼有劝勉和安慰两层意思。软弱的人需要人在身边，不只是听一篇道理。

**3. 「我们受患难原是命定的」。** 「因为你们自己知道，我们受患难原是命定的。我们在你们那里的时候，预先告诉你们，我们必受患难，以后果然应验了」（3:3-4）。「命定」是说这是神所定的道路，不是意外，也不是神失去了掌管。
- **保罗早就说过：** 他在传福音的时候，并没有应许他们信主以后一帆风顺。他「预先告诉」他们会受苦（参徒14:22「我们进入神的国，必须经历许多艰难」）。
- **对今天的意义：** 有些传福音的方式会让人以为，信了耶稣就会平安、健康、发达。当苦难来了，这样的人就会以为神不在了，或者自己信错了。保罗说：患难不是神离开的记号，而是跟随一位被钉十字架的主必走的路（参彼前4:12-13）。这不是说每一样苦难都是逼迫，也不是说要去找苦吃；而是说，苦难临到的时候，不必以为奇怪。

**4. 「恐怕那诱惑人的」。** 「我既不能再忍，就打发人去，要晓得你们的信心如何，恐怕那诱惑人的到底诱惑了你们，叫我们的劳苦归于徒然」（3:5）。「那诱惑人的」就是撒但（参2:18）。他利用患难，引诱人怀疑神、离开神。
- **保罗也会担心：** 一个伟大的使徒，也会担心自己的劳苦是不是白费了。他没有假装刚强，坦白承认「不能再忍」；但他也没有被忧虑压垮，而是采取行动：差人去，然后祷告。

> 要点：苦难不是神离开的证据；跟随被钉十字架的主，本来就要走这条路，主早已说明了。神没有应许我们不受苦，却应许在苦难中坚固我们。

## 深读 3:6-10 好消息：我们就活了

**1. 「好消息」。** 「但提摩太刚才从你们那里回来，将你们信心和爱心的好消息报给我们」（3:6）。「报好消息」这个动词在保罗书信别处都是指传福音，这是唯一一处用来说别的事。保罗用这个词，也许是要表明：他们站立得稳的消息，本身就是福音结出的果子，叫他大大喜乐。
- **他们也想念保罗：** 「又说你们常常记念我们，切切地想见我们，如同我们想见你们一样」（3:6）。城里那些说保罗坏话的人（参第二章）没有离间他们。

**2. 「你们若靠主站立得稳，我们就活了」。** 「所以，弟兄们，我们在一切困苦患难之中，因着你们的信心就得了安慰。你们若靠主站立得稳，我们就活了」（3:7-8）。保罗自己也在患难中（参徒18:5-6；林前2:3），而他得到的安慰，是从这群年轻信徒来的。
- **安慰是双向的：** 保罗打发提摩太去安慰他们，结果是他们的信心安慰了保罗。在神的家里，没有人永远只是给的人，或永远只是受的人。
- **「靠主」：** 原文是在主里。他们能站稳，不是靠自己的毅力，而是因为他们在主里。

**3. 感谢和喜乐。** 「我们在神面前，因着你们甚是喜乐；为这一切喜乐，可用何等的感谢为你们报答神呢？」（3:9）保罗没有把他们站稳的功劳归给自己或提摩太，也没有归给他们自己，而是感谢神。他们站稳，是神的作为。

**4. 「补满你们信心的不足」。** 「我们昼夜切切地祈求，要见你们的面，补满你们信心的不足」（3:10）。保罗刚说完他们信心的好消息，紧接着就说他们的信心还有「不足」。
- **不是矛盾：** 他们的信心是真的，却还年轻、还不完整。他们需要更多的教导（例如第四、五章讲的圣洁、睡了的人和主的日子），需要在认识上、生活上更成熟。
- **「补满」和「不足」：** 「补满」这个动词有修补、使之完全的意思，马太福音4:21「补网」用的就是这个动词；「不足」这个名词，也用在别处指缺少的部分（参腓2:30「不及之处」）。信心有缺口并不可耻，这正是教会彼此教导、彼此扶持的原因。

> 要点：真实的信心也可能还有缺欠。神用患难试验信心，用弟兄姊妹的扶持坚固信心，用他的话补满信心的缺口。我们若能站稳，是因为我们在主里站立。

## 深读 3:11-13 保罗的祷告

**1. 父和子一同引领。** 「愿神－我们的父和我们的主耶稣一直引领我们到你们那里去」（3:11）。这里有两个主语：「神我们的父」和「我们的主耶稣」，但「引领」这个动词在原文是单数。许多解经家认为，保罗很自然地让父与子共用一个单数动词，把他们看作一同行事。单数动词本身不足以证明什么，但帖撒罗尼迦后书2:16-17先提主耶稣，动词仍是单数，这个看法就更有根据了。这不是说父和子是同一个位格，而是说，保罗把耶稣放在只属于神的位置上：在祷告中把他与父并列呼求，求他引领。
- **祷告蒙了应允，只是来得晚一些：** 保罗后来确实回到了马其顿（徒20:1-2）。神的引领有他的时候。

**2. 爱心增长、充足。** 「又愿主叫你们彼此相爱的心，并爱众人的心都能增长，充足，如同我们爱你们一样」（3:12）。保罗没有求主除掉他们的患难，而是求主叫他们的爱心增长。
- **「彼此」和「众人」：** 爱不只在教会里面，也要临到「众人」，包括那些正在逼迫他们的同胞（参5:15）。
- **爱是主赐的：** 「愿主叫你们……增长」。爱心的增长不是我们自己拼命挤出来的，而是主的工作。我们可以求他。

**3. 心里坚固，成为圣洁。** 「好使你们当我们主耶稣同他众圣徒来的时候，在我们父神面前，心里坚固，成为圣洁，无可责备」（3:13）。这是保罗祷告的目标：在主再来的那一天，他们能站在神面前，无可责备。
- **从爱到圣洁：** 保罗先求爱心增长，然后说「好使」他们心里坚固、成为圣洁。圣洁不是冷冰冰地守规条，而是在爱中长大。
- **「无可责备」：** 不是说信徒在今生已经完全无罪，而是说神要坚固他们，叫他们在那日能站立得住。这是神的工作：「愿主……好使你们」。保罗在5:23-24也这样说：「愿赐平安的神亲自使你们全然成圣……那召你们的本是信实的，他必成就这事。」
- **「众圣徒」是谁？** 「圣徒」原文也可以译作「圣者」。有人认为指天使，因为撒迦利亚书说「耶和华－我的神必降临，有一切圣者同来」（亚14:5），耶稣也说人子要「同圣天使降临」（可8:38）；有人认为指已经离世的信徒（参4:14「神也必将他们与耶稣一同带来」）；也有人认为天使和信徒都包括在内。

> 要点：保罗在忧虑中祷告，求的不是患难快快过去，而是主叫他们的爱心增长，并且坚固他们，在主来的时候能站立得住。这是主自己的工作。

## 默想

**默想：** 「免得有人被诸般患难摇动。因为你们自己知道，我们受患难原是命定的。」（3:3）也许当初带我信主的人，没有告诉我会受苦；也许有人告诉过我，我却没有放在心上。后来苦难真的来了：一场病、一次失业、一段关系的破裂，或者因为信主在家里受了冷落。我的信心被摇动了。我心里问过：神是不是不在了？我是不是信错了？我甚至不敢告诉别人，因为我觉得一个好基督徒不应该这样。保罗没有因为帖撒罗尼迦人的信心有「不足」就看不起他们；他昼夜为他们祈求，要来「补满」那些缺口。他也没有假装自己从不担心；他说他「不能再忍」。耶稣对快要跌倒的彼得说：「我已经为你祈求，叫你不至于失了信心。」（路22:32）彼得后来还是跌倒了，但他的信心没有完全失去，因为有一位为他祈求的主。今天，我可以把那个被摇动的地方老老实实地告诉主，也可以告诉一位可靠的弟兄姊妹，让他们为我祷告。我能站稳，不是靠我的信心有多坚强，而是因为主「坚固」我的心。保罗的祷告不是求他们自己坚固起来，而是「愿主……好使你们……心里坚固」。那位曾为彼得祈求的主耶稣，现今在神的右边，也替我祈求（罗8:34）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 continues the longing at the end of chapter 2, in three parts. **Sending Timothy** (vv. 1–5): "when we could bear it no longer," Paul chose to be left alone in Athens so that he could send Timothy back to strengthen them, so that no one would be moved by afflictions. **Good news arrives** (vv. 6–10): Timothy brings good news of their faith and love, and Paul says, "For now we live, if you are standing fast in the Lord." **Paul's prayer** (vv. 11–13): may God open the way for him to return, may the Lord make their love increase, and may their hearts be established blameless in holiness at the coming of our Lord Jesus with all his saints.

This chapter shows us a pastor's heart. Paul's worry is not whether his ministry is going well, but whether these young believers will fall in affliction. His anxiety becomes prayer in the end, and the prayer ends, again, at the Lord's coming.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–5 Destined for Affliction

**1. Willing to be left alone.** "Therefore when we could bear it no longer, we were willing to be left behind at Athens alone, and we sent Timothy, our brother and God's coworker in the gospel of Christ" (3:1–2). In Athens Paul was a stranger (see Acts 17:16–34); once he had sent Timothy back, he had no co-worker beside him. He was "willing" to be alone in order to send back his best helper.
- **What Timothy is called:** some manuscripts call Timothy God's coworker, others God's minister; the CUV follows the latter. Calling someone God's coworker is a bold phrase, and some copyists may have found it too bold and changed it; most textual critics judge "coworker" original (cf. 1 Cor 3:9). Either way, Paul is telling the Thessalonians: the one I sent is not a minor assistant but someone working for God in the gospel.

**2. Sent to do what?** "To establish and exhort you in your faith, that no one be moved by these afflictions" (3:2–3). "Establish" originally meant to prop something up with a support; the word for "moved" could describe a dog wagging its tail to fawn on someone, so some think it carries the idea of being coaxed away, though most take it here simply as "shaken" or "unsettled." Affliction can frighten people off, or it can draw them away gently, little by little.
- **Exhort:** this word carries both urging and comforting. Weak believers need someone beside them, not just a lecture.

**3. "We are destined for this."** "For you yourselves know that we are destined for this. For when we were with you, we kept telling you beforehand that we were to suffer affliction, just as it has come to pass" (3:3–4). "Destined" means this is the road God has appointed, not an accident, nor a sign that God has lost control.
- **Paul had said so already:** when he preached the gospel, he did not promise them smooth sailing after conversion. He "kept telling you beforehand" that they would suffer (see Acts 14:22, "through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God").
- **For today:** some ways of preaching the gospel leave people thinking that believing in Jesus will bring safety, health and prosperity. When suffering comes, such people think God has gone, or that they believed wrongly. Paul says: affliction is not a sign that God has left; it is the road that following a crucified Lord must take (see 1 Pet 4:12–13). This does not mean every hardship is persecution, nor that we should go looking for suffering; it means that when suffering comes, we need not be surprised.

**4. "For fear that somehow the tempter had tempted you."** "For this reason, when I could bear it no longer, I sent to learn about your faith, for fear that somehow the tempter had tempted you and our labor would be in vain" (3:5). "The tempter" is Satan (see 2:18). He uses affliction to tempt people to doubt God and leave him.
- **Paul worried too:** a great apostle could worry that his labor had been wasted. He did not pretend to be strong; he admitted he "could bear it no longer." But he was not crushed by worry; he acted: he sent someone, and he prayed.

> The point: Suffering is not evidence that God has left but the road that following a crucified Lord was always said to involve. God has not promised that we will not suffer, but he has promised to strengthen us in suffering.

## Going Deeper: 3:6–10 Good News: Now We Live

**1. "Good news."** "But now that Timothy has come to us from you, and has brought us the good news of your faith and love" (3:6). Elsewhere in Paul's letters the verb "bring good news" always refers to preaching the gospel; this is the only place he uses it for something else. His choice may suggest that news of their standing firm was itself the fruit of the gospel, and a great joy to him.
- **They missed Paul too:** "and reported that you always remember us kindly and long to see us, as we long to see you" (3:6). The people slandering Paul in the city (see chapter 2) had not turned them against him.

**2. "For now we live, if you are standing fast in the Lord."** "For this reason, brothers, in all our distress and affliction we have been comforted about you through your faith. For now we live, if you are standing fast in the Lord" (3:7–8). Paul himself was in affliction (see Acts 18:5–6; cf. 1 Cor 2:3), and his comfort came from these young believers.
- **Comfort flows both ways:** Paul sent Timothy to comfort them, and their faith ended up comforting Paul. In God's household no one is always only the giver or always only the receiver.
- **"In the Lord":** they stood firm not by their own willpower but "in the Lord."

**3. Thanks and joy.** "For what thanksgiving can we return to God for you, for all the joy that we feel for your sake before our God" (3:9). Paul gives the credit for their standing firm not to himself or Timothy, nor even to them, but thanks God. That they stood was God's doing.

**4. "Supply what is lacking in your faith."** "As we pray most earnestly night and day that we may see you face to face and supply what is lacking in your faith" (3:10). Right after the good news about their faith, Paul says their faith still "lacks" something.
- **No contradiction:** their faith was real, but still young and incomplete. They needed more teaching (such as chapters 4–5 give on holiness, those who have fallen asleep, and the day of the Lord), and they needed to mature in understanding and in life.
- **The word "supply":** the Greek verb can mean to mend or restore, and is used of mending nets (Matt 4:21); the noun "what is lacking" is used elsewhere for what is missing (see Phil 2:30). A gap in faith is nothing to be ashamed of; it is the very reason the church teaches and supports one another.

> The point: Real faith can still have gaps. God tests it through affliction, strengthens it through others' support, and fills it out with his word. If we stand firm at all, it is because we stand in the Lord.

## Going Deeper: 3:11–13 Paul's Prayer

**1. Father and Son direct together.** "Now may our God and Father himself, and our Lord Jesus, direct our way to you" (3:11). There are two subjects here, "our God and Father" and "our Lord Jesus," but the verb "direct" is singular in Greek. Many commentators think Paul, without stopping to explain, treats the Father and the Son as acting together as one. A singular verb alone proves little, but 2 Thess 2:16–17, which names Jesus first and again uses singular verbs, strengthens the point. This does not mean the Father and the Son are the same person; it means Paul places Jesus where only God belongs: invoking him alongside the Father in prayer and asking him to guide.
- **A prayer answered, though later:** Paul did eventually return to Macedonia (Acts 20:1–2). God's guidance has its own timing.

**2. Love increasing and abounding.** "And may the Lord make you increase and abound in love for one another and for all, as we do for you" (3:12). Paul does not ask the Lord to remove their afflictions, but to make their love grow.
- **"One another" and "all":** love is not only for those inside the church; it reaches "all," including the countrymen who were persecuting them (see 5:15).
- **Love is the Lord's gift:** "May the Lord make you increase." Growth in love is not squeezed out by our own effort; it is the Lord's work, and we may ask him for it.

**3. Hearts established in holiness.** "So that he may establish your hearts blameless in holiness before our God and Father, at the coming of our Lord Jesus with all his saints" (3:13). This is the aim of Paul's prayer: that on the day the Lord returns they may stand before God blameless.
- **From love to holiness:** Paul first asks for love to grow, then says "so that" their hearts may be established in holiness. Holiness is not cold rule-keeping; it is growing up in love.
- **"Blameless":** not that believers are already sinless in this life, but that God will establish them so they can stand on that day. This is God's work: "may the Lord... so that he may establish." Paul says the same in 5:23–24: "Now may the God of peace himself sanctify you completely... He who calls you is faithful; he will surely do it."
- **Who are "all his saints"?** "Saints" can also be rendered "holy ones." Some think it means angels, since Zechariah says "the LORD my God will come, and all the holy ones with him" (Zech 14:5), and Jesus says the Son of Man will come "with the holy angels" (Mark 8:38); some think it means believers who have died (see 4:14, "God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep"); others think both are included.

> The point: In his anxiety Paul prays, not that the affliction pass quickly, but that the Lord make their love grow and establish them to stand at his coming. This is the Lord's own work.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "That no one be moved by these afflictions. For you yourselves know that we are destined for this" (3:3). Perhaps the person who led me to faith never told me I would suffer; or perhaps someone did, and I did not take it to heart. Then suffering really came: an illness, a lost job, a broken relationship, or coldness at home because of my faith. My faith was shaken. I asked in my heart: has God gone? Did I believe wrongly? I did not even dare tell anyone, because I felt a good Christian should not be like this. Paul did not look down on the Thessalonians because their faith had gaps; he prayed night and day to come and "supply what is lacking." Nor did he pretend he never worried; he said he "could bear it no longer." Jesus said to Peter, who was about to fall, "I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail" (Luke 22:32). Peter still fell, but his faith did not fail completely, because he had a Lord who prayed for him. Today I can tell the Lord honestly where I have been shaken, and I can tell a trustworthy brother or sister and let them pray for me. I stand firm not because my faith is strong, but because the Lord "establishes" my heart. Paul's prayer is not that they would establish themselves, but "may the Lord... establish your hearts." The Lord Jesus who prayed for Peter is now at the right hand of God, interceding for me too (Rom 8:34).
