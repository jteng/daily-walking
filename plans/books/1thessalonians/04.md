## 本章脉络

从第四章开始，保罗由回顾转向劝勉。本章分成四段。**更加勉励**（1-2节）：他们已经知道怎样讨神喜悦，现在要「更加勉励」。**成为圣洁**（3-8节）：「神的旨意就是要你们成为圣洁，远避淫行」，用圣洁、尊贵守着自己的身体。**相爱和做工**（9-12节）：他们蒙了神的教训彼此相爱，要更加相爱，并且立志作安静人，亲手做工。**睡了的人**（13-18节）：信主的人死了，活着的人不要像没有指望的人那样忧伤；主必降临，在基督里死了的人必先复活，我们要和主永远同在。

这几个题目看起来各不相同，其实都和提摩太带回来的消息有关（3:6、10）。他们的信心是真的，但还有「不足」：在性的事上，外邦的旧习惯还拉扯着他们；有些人可能不肯安分做工；还有人为死去的弟兄姊妹忧伤，不知道他们在主来的时候会怎样。保罗一样一样地回答，最后都回到同一个盼望：「我们就要和主永远同在。」

## 深读 4:1-2 更加勉励

**1. 不是从头开始，而是继续向前。** 「你们既然受了我们的教训，知道该怎样行可以讨神的喜悦，就要照你们现在所行的更加勉励」（4:1）。保罗先肯定他们已经在走（「照你们现在所行的」），然后才鼓励他们「更加」。
- **「讨神的喜悦」：** 这不是靠行为赚得神的爱。他们已经是「被神所爱」的（1:4）。就像儿女已经被父母爱着，仍然愿意叫父母欢喜一样，信徒是因为蒙了爱，才愿意讨神喜悦。

**2. 「凭主耶稣」。** 「你们原晓得，我们凭主耶稣传给你们什么命令」（4:2）。保罗的教导不是他个人的意见，而是凭主耶稣的权柄。这为下面关于性的教导定了调：这不是保守的文化习惯，而是主的命令。

## 深读 4:3-8 神的旨意就是要你们成为圣洁

**1. 「神的旨意」。** 「神的旨意就是要你们成为圣洁，远避淫行」（4:3）。许多信徒常常为「神的旨意是什么」烦恼：该读哪个专业、做哪份工作、和谁结婚。保罗在这里很直接地说出神的一个旨意：你们要成为圣洁。
- **「成为圣洁」：** 这个词指一个过程，是神使人越来越像他自己的工作（参5:23）。它在本段出现了三次（4:3、4、7）。
- **「淫行」：** 原文是一个很广的词，包括婚姻以外的一切性行为：婚前的、婚外的、嫖妓等等。

**2. 外邦的习惯。** 「不放纵私欲的邪情，像那不认识神的外邦人」（4:5）。在希腊罗马的城市里，男人在婚外和奴隶、妓女发生关系，常常被视为平常的事，只要不碰别人的妻子就可以了。许多神庙和节庆也和放纵连在一起。帖撒罗尼迦的信徒是从这样的文化里出来的。
- **今天的处境：** 今天的文化也有它的「平常」：婚前同居、色情影片、网上的暧昧关系、应酬场所里的种种安排。有些华人信徒在生意或工作上，会被要求陪客户去一些地方；也有人出差在外，没有人看见。保罗的话今天同样清楚：这不是「不认识神的」人的问题，而是我们自己的问题。

**3. 「守着自己的身体」。** 「要你们各人晓得怎样用圣洁、尊贵守着自己的身体」（4:4）。「身体」原文是「器皿」，这个词怎样理解，解经家有不同的看法：
- **指自己的身体：** 和合本和许多英文译本这样理解，意思是要学会管住自己的身体（参林前6:18-20）。
- **指妻子：** 有些解经家认为意思是「娶妻」，也就是在婚姻里，以圣洁、尊重的态度对待妻子（参彼前3:7，原文称妻子为较软弱的器皿）。
- **委婉的说法：** 也有人认为这个词在这里委婉地指性器官，意思是要控制自己的性欲。

这几种看法的重点都一样：性是神所赐的好东西，要在圣洁和尊重中，在一男一女的婚姻里享受（创2:24；来13:4），而不是任凭私欲放纵。

**4. 不要欺负弟兄。** 「不要一个人在这事上越分，欺负他的弟兄；因为这一类的事，主必报应」（4:6）。大多数解经家认为「这事」仍然指性的事：淫乱不只是个人的私事，它会伤害别人，例如破坏另一个人的婚姻，或者占另一个人的便宜。也有少数人认为这里转到了生意上的欺骗。
- **「主必报应」：** 神看见一切，也会为受害的人伸冤。这句话对受害的人是安慰：你所受的伤害，神没有忽略。
- **关于性侵犯：** 如果有人在教会、家里或工作中受到性侵犯或性骚扰，那不是受害人的错，也不是受害人的「不洁」。神的审判是对着施害的人说的。这样的事应当报警，交给执法和司法机关处理（罗13:4）；受害人不必和不安全的人和好，也不必留在危险中。饶恕不等于恢复施害者的职分，也不等于让他再接近受害人或孩子。教会绝不可以为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩。

**5. 神召我们，是要我们圣洁。** 「神召我们，本不是要我们沾染污秽，乃是要我们成为圣洁。所以，那弃绝的，不是弃绝人，乃是弃绝那赐圣灵给你们的神」（4:7-8）。
- **呼召在先：** 圣洁不是我们为了被神接纳而努力达到的门槛；而是因为神已经召了我们，我们才被呼召过圣洁的生活。
- **神赐圣灵：** 「赐」在原文是现在式，意思是神一直在把他的圣灵赐给我们。旧约先知应许，神要把他的灵放在他百姓里面，使他们能遵行他的律例（结36:27）。神没有只给我们一条命令，就让我们自己去拼；他把能力也赐给了我们。

**6. 给已经跌倒的人。** 这段话很清楚，也很严肃。读到这里，有些人心里会很沉重，因为他们在这件事上已经失败过，也许不止一次。保罗写信给哥林多教会时，列出了淫乱的人，接着说：「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今你们奉主耶稣基督的名，并藉着我们神的灵，已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」（林前6:11）基督的血足以洗净这样的罪。圣洁的道路，是从十字架开始的。耶稣对那个被抓到的妇人说：「我也不定你的罪。去吧，从此不要再犯罪了！」（约8:11）先有不定罪，然后才有不再犯罪。
- **不必独自挣扎：** 如果你正在色情或其他性的罪中挣扎，可以找一位可靠的、同性的弟兄或姊妹坦白，请他们为你祷告，也陪你走。这不是羞耻的事，而是光明中的行走。

> 要点：神的旨意是要我们成为圣洁，这包括我们的身体和性。这不是冷冰冰的禁令，而是那召我们、并把圣灵赐给我们的神，要我们活出他的样式。对已经跌倒的人，十字架是起点，不是终点。

## 深读 4:9-12 彼此相爱，安静做工

**1. 「蒙了神的教训」。** 「论到弟兄们相爱，不用人写信给你们；因为你们自己蒙了神的教训，叫你们彼此相爱」（4:9）。「蒙了神的教训」原文是一个词，很可能是保罗自己造的。它让人想起以赛亚书的应许：「你的儿女都要受耶和华的教训」（赛54:13；参约6:45）。新约的信徒有圣灵在心里，神亲自教他们怎样爱。
- **「弟兄相爱」：** 原文这个词本来是指亲兄弟姊妹之间的爱。在家人可能已经和他们断绝来往的时候，教会就成了他们的新家。

**2. 更加勉励。** 「你们向马其顿全地的众弟兄固然是这样行，但我劝弟兄们要更加勉励」（4:10）。他们的爱已经越过自己的城，临到整个马其顿的信徒，可能包括接待来往的弟兄、彼此帮助。保罗还是说：「更加」。爱没有「够了」的时候。

**3. 立志作安静人。** 「又要立志作安静人，办自己的事，亲手做工」（4:11）。「立志」这个词原指追求名声、争取荣誉。保罗把它和「安静」放在一起，几乎是一种幽默：你们要拼命追求的，是安安静静地过日子。
- **为什么要这样说？** 可能有几个原因：有些人以为主快要来了，就不再做工（参帖后3:10-12）；也有学者认为，当时有些穷人依靠有钱的「恩主」生活，要为恩主四处奔走，替他争面子；也可能有人因为热心，到处插手别人的事。保罗说：好好做自己的工，不要成为别人的负担。
- **亲手做工：** 在希腊罗马文化里，有地位的人看不起用手做工的人。保罗自己就是用手做工的（2:9），他要信徒看见：平凡的劳动是尊贵的（参弗4:28）。

**4. 「向外人行事端正」。** 「叫你们可以向外人行事端正，自己也就没有什么缺乏了」（4:12）。信徒在城里本来就被人议论，如果再有人游手好闲，靠人接济，就会让福音受羞辱。
- **不是看不起不能工作的人：** 保罗在这里批评的是「不肯」做工的人（参帖后3:10），不是因为疾病、残障、年老或失业而不能工作的人。对这些人，教会要「扶助软弱的人」（5:14）。

> 要点：神亲自教我们彼此相爱。爱有时表现为大大的牺牲，有时只是安静、忠心地做好自己每天的工作，不成为别人的负担。

## 深读 4:13-14 不像那些没有指望的人

**1. 为什么有这个问题？** 「论到睡了的人，我们不愿意弟兄们不知道，恐怕你们忧伤，像那些没有指望的人一样」（4:13）。保罗离开以后，教会里有人去世了，也许是因为逼迫，也许是生病。活着的人很困惑：他们会不会错过主的再来？保罗在那里的时间很短，大概还没有详细教导身体复活的事。

**2. 忧伤是可以的。** 保罗没有说不要忧伤，而是说不要「像那些没有指望的人一样」忧伤。耶稣自己在拉撒路的坟前也「哭了」（约11:35）。信徒失去亲人，同样会心痛、会流泪。这不是信心不够。
- **没有指望的忧伤：** 古代希腊罗马的墓碑上，常常刻着「我曾经不存在，后来存在，如今不再存在」这类的话，表达对死亡的无奈。古希腊诗人也写过：「活人才有盼望，死人没有盼望。」保罗说，信徒的忧伤不一样，因为有指望。

**3. 「睡了」。** 保罗把信徒的死称为「睡了」（4:13、14、15）。这不是说死后灵魂就失去知觉；保罗在别处说，离世是「与基督同在」，「这是好得无比的」（腓1:23；参林后5:8）。「睡」是一个充满盼望的说法：睡觉的人还要醒来。他们的身体在等候复活。
- **耶稣是「死」了：** 值得留意的是，保罗说「耶稣死而复活了」（4:14），说到耶稣，他没有用「睡」这个字。耶稣承受了死亡全部的重量，所以属他的人的死，就变成了「睡」。

**4. 「神也必将他们与耶稣一同带来」。** 「我们若信耶稣死而复活了，那已经在耶稣里睡了的人，神也必将他们与耶稣一同带来」（4:14）。信徒的盼望不是建立在一种模糊的乐观上，而是建立在一件历史事实上：耶稣死了，又复活了。他的复活是保证，属他的人也必复活（参林前15:20）。
- **与华人的丧礼：** 信徒在丧礼上，可以哀哭，可以怀念，可以讲述逝者的生平，也可以唱诗、读经、祷告，把盼望讲出来。这里所说的盼望，是给「在耶稣里睡了的人」的；对于没有信主就去世的亲人，圣经没有给我们更多的应许，我们也不必猜测，可以把他们交在公义又慈爱的神手中。（另参本研读林前第十五章。）

## 深读 4:15-18 主必亲自从天降临

**1. 「照主的话」。** 「我们现在照主的话告诉你们一件事」（4:15）。这「主的话」是什么，有不同看法：可能是耶稣在世时的教训，只是福音书没有一字一句地记下来（参太24:30-31）；也可能是复活的主藉着先知启示给教会的话。无论哪一种，保罗都表明：这不是他自己的推测。

**2. 主再来的情景。** 「因为主必亲自从天降临，有呼叫的声音和天使长的声音，又有神的号吹响；那在基督里死了的人必先复活」（4:16）。
- **「亲自」：** 不是差一位天使，而是主自己来。
- **呼叫、天使长的声音、号：** 这些都是旧约里神显现、召聚他百姓时常用的意象（参出19:16；亚9:14），耶稣也说过，「他要差遣使者，用号筒的大声，将他的选民……都招聚了来」（太24:31）。
- **死了的人必先复活：** 这正是帖撒罗尼迦人担心的问题的答案。死去的信徒不但不会错过，反而要「先」复活。

**3. 「被提」和「相遇」。** 「以后我们这活着还存留的人必和他们一同被提到云里，在空中与主相遇」（4:17）。「被提」原文的意思是被突然、有力地抓去。拉丁文译本把这个词译成一个字，后来西方神学中「被提」这个术语，就是从这个拉丁字来的。
- **「相遇」这个词：** 在当时，这个词常常用来指城里的人出城去迎接来访的君王或大官，然后陪着他一同进城（参徒28:15，罗马的弟兄「出来」迎接保罗；太25:6「新郎来了，你们出来迎接他」）。许多解经家（如弗雷德里克·布鲁斯、吉恩·格林）因此认为，信徒在空中与主相遇以后，会陪着主一同回到地上；但也有人认为这个词在这里不一定带着这样的含义。

**4. 不同的看法。** 关于被提和主再来的次序，福音派内部有几种主要看法，都是认真读经的弟兄姊妹所持守的：
- **灾前被提：** 时代论传统（如约翰·沃尔沃德）认为，主会先在空中接走教会，然后地上有一段大灾难，最后主再与圣徒一同降临地上。
- **灾后被提：** 另一些学者（如乔治·赖德、道格拉斯·穆尔）认为，教会要经过大灾难，被提和主降临是同一件事：信徒被提到空中迎接主，随即与他一同降临。
- **一次再来：** 改革宗、路德宗以及许多历史悠久的传统（如安东尼·胡克玛所代表的无千禧年派），还有天主教和东正教，一般也认为被提和主的再来是同一件事，并不把它们分成两个阶段。
- **千禧年的问题：** 至于主再来和启示录20章的「一千年」怎样连在一起（前千禧年派、无千禧年派、后千禧年派），这段经文没有讨论。

这些看法在细节上不同，但都同意最重要的几件事：主耶稣必亲自、有形有体地再来；在基督里死了的人必复活；活着的信徒也必改变（林前15:51-52）；我们要和主永远同在。

**5. 「这样，我们就要和主永远同在」。** 这是整段的高峰。保罗没有详细描写天上的样子，也没有排时间表。他只说：我们要和主在一起，而且是永远。基督徒的盼望，中心不是一个地方，而是一个人。

**6. 「你们当用这些话彼此劝慰」。** 「所以，你们当用这些话彼此劝慰」（4:18）。保罗讲末世的事，目的不是叫人争论、害怕，而是叫人彼此安慰。如果我们讲主的再来，结果只是叫人惧怕，或者在教会里分党，那就偏离了保罗的用意。
- **「我们这活着还存留的人」：** 保罗把自己放在「活着」的人中间，有人据此说保罗以为主一定会在他有生之年再来，结果错了。但保罗在下一段马上说，那日子来到「好像夜间的贼一样」（5:2），没有人知道时候。他是以每一代信徒都应有的盼望说话：主随时可能来。

> 要点：信徒的忧伤是有指望的忧伤，因为耶稣死而复活了。主必亲自降临，死了的人必先复活，我们要和主永远同在。关于时间和次序，信徒有不同的看法；但这盼望的目的，是叫我们彼此安慰。

## 默想

**默想：** 「所以，你们当用这些话彼此劝慰。」（4:18）当身边有人失去亲人的时候，我常常不知道该说什么。有时我说「节哀顺变」，有时说「他去了更好的地方」，有时什么也说不出来，只好站得远一点，怕说错话。保罗给了我们「这些话」，但他并没有叫我们一见面就讲一篇末世论。耶稣在拉撒路的坟前，先是「哭了」（约11:35），然后才说「复活在我，生命也在我」（约11:25）。他先和人一同流泪，再把盼望说出来。今天，也许有一位弟兄姊妹正在为离世的父母、配偶，甚至孩子忧伤。我可以先去坐在他旁边，不急着解释，不急着叫他刚强。等到合适的时候，我可以轻轻地说：「主必亲自从天降临……我们就要和主永远同在。」也许我自己就是那个正在忧伤的人。那么，我不必假装不痛；我可以哭，也可以让别人用这些话来安慰我。那位为拉撒路流泪的主，也会为我流泪；那位从死里复活的主，也必亲自来接我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

From chapter 4 Paul turns from looking back to exhortation. The chapter has four parts. **More and more** (vv. 1–2): they already know how to please God, and now they are to do so "more and more." **Holiness** (vv. 3–8): "this is the will of God, your sanctification: that you abstain from sexual immorality," controlling one's own body in holiness and honor. **Love and work** (vv. 9–12): they have been taught by God to love one another, and are to love more, and to aspire to live quietly and work with their hands. **Those who have fallen asleep** (vv. 13–18): when believers die, the living are not to grieve like those without hope; the Lord will descend, the dead in Christ will rise first, and we will always be with the Lord.

These topics look unrelated, but all of them connect with the news Timothy brought (3:6, 10). Their faith was real but still "lacking": in sexual matters, old pagan habits still pulled at them; some may have been unwilling to work steadily; others grieved for brothers and sisters who had died and did not know what would happen to them at the Lord's coming. Paul answers each in turn, and all of them come back to the same hope: "we will always be with the Lord."

## Going Deeper: 4:1–2 More and More

**1. Not starting over, but going further.** "As you received from us how you ought to live and to please God, just as you are doing, that you do so more and more" (4:1). Paul first affirms that they are already on the road ("just as you are doing"), and only then urges them on "more and more."
- **"To please God":** this is not earning God's love by works. They are already "loved by God" (1:4). Just as children who are already loved still want to make their parents glad, believers want to please God because they are loved.

**2. "Through the Lord Jesus."** "For you know what instructions we gave you through the Lord Jesus" (4:2). Paul's teaching is not his personal opinion but comes with the authority of the Lord Jesus. This sets the tone for what follows on sex: it is not conservative cultural habit but the Lord's command.

## Going Deeper: 4:3–8 This Is the Will of God, Your Sanctification

**1. "The will of God."** "For this is the will of God, your sanctification: that you abstain from sexual immorality" (4:3). Many believers agonize over "what God's will is": which subject to study, which job to take, whom to marry. Here Paul states one part of God's will directly: that you be holy.
- **"Sanctification":** this word describes a process, God's work of making people more and more like himself (see 5:23). It appears three times in this passage (4:3, 4, 7).
- **"Sexual immorality":** the Greek word is broad, covering all sexual activity outside marriage: before marriage, outside marriage, with prostitutes, and so on.

**2. Pagan habits.** "Not in the passion of lust like the Gentiles who do not know God" (4:5). In Greek and Roman cities, it was often considered normal for men to have sex outside marriage with slaves or prostitutes, so long as they did not touch another man's wife. Many temples and festivals were also tied to sexual license. The Thessalonian believers had come out of such a culture.
- **Today:** today's culture has its own "normal": living together before marriage, pornography, flirtatious relationships online, and the arrangements of certain entertainment venues. Some Chinese believers in business or work are expected to take clients to such places; others travel for work where no one sees. Paul's words are just as clear today: this is not only a problem for "those who do not know God," but our own.

**3. "Control his own body."** "That each one of you know how to control his own body in holiness and honor" (4:4). The Greek word for "body" is literally "vessel," and commentators understand it in different ways:
- **One's own body:** the CUV and many English versions take it this way, meaning learning to master one's own body (see 1 Cor 6:18–20).
- **A wife:** some commentators think it means "take a wife," that is, treating one's wife within marriage in holiness and honor (see 1 Pet 3:7, which calls the wife "the weaker vessel").
- **A euphemism:** others think the word here politely refers to the sexual organ, meaning controlling one's sexual desire.

All these views make the same point: sex is God's good gift, to be enjoyed in holiness and honor within the marriage of one man and one woman (Gen 2:24; Heb 13:4), not handed over to lust.

**4. Do not wrong your brother.** "That no one transgress and wrong his brother in this matter, because the Lord is an avenger in all these things" (4:6). Most commentators think "this matter" still refers to sex: sexual immorality is not a private matter; it harms others, for example by breaking up another person's marriage or taking advantage of someone. A few think Paul has turned to cheating in business.
- **"The Lord is an avenger":** God sees everything and will vindicate those who have been wronged. For victims this is comfort: God has not overlooked the harm done to you.
- **On sexual abuse:** if someone has suffered sexual assault or harassment in church, at home or at work, it is not the victim's fault, nor the victim's "impurity." God's judgment is spoken against the one who did the harm. Such things should be reported to the police and dealt with by the authorities and courts (Rom 13:4). Victims need not reconcile with someone unsafe or remain in danger. Forgiveness does not restore an abuser's office or give him access to victims or children. The church must never cover it up to keep "family shame" from getting out.

**5. God called us to holiness.** "For God has not called us for impurity, but in holiness. Therefore whoever disregards this, disregards not man but God, who gives his Holy Spirit to you" (4:7–8).
- **The call comes first:** holiness is not a threshold we must reach to be accepted by God; we are called to a holy life because God has already called us.
- **God gives the Spirit:** "gives" is present tense in Greek: God keeps giving us his Holy Spirit. The Old Testament prophets promised that God would put his Spirit within his people and cause them to walk in his statutes (Ezek 36:27). God has not just handed us a command and left us to struggle alone; he gives us the power as well.

**6. For those who have already fallen.** This passage is clear and serious. Reading it, some will feel heavy, because they have already failed in this area, perhaps more than once. When Paul wrote to the Corinthians, he listed the sexually immoral and then said, "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God" (1 Cor 6:11). The blood of Christ is enough to wash such sin. The road of holiness begins at the cross. Jesus said to the woman who had been caught, "Neither do I condemn you; go, and from now on sin no more" (John 8:11). First comes "no condemnation," then "sin no more."
- **No need to struggle alone:** if you are struggling with pornography or other sexual sin, you can confess to a trustworthy brother or sister of the same sex and ask them to pray for you and walk with you. This is not shameful; it is walking in the light.

> The point: God's will is that we be holy, and that includes our bodies and our sexuality. This is not a cold prohibition but the God who called us and gives us his Spirit, wanting us to live out his likeness. For those who have fallen, the cross is the starting point, not the end.

## Going Deeper: 4:9–12 Love One Another, Live Quietly

**1. "Taught by God."** "Now concerning brotherly love you have no need for anyone to write to you, for you yourselves have been taught by God to love one another" (4:9). "Taught by God" is a single word in Greek, very likely coined by Paul. It recalls Isaiah's promise, "All your children shall be taught by the LORD" (Isa 54:13; see John 6:45). New covenant believers have the Spirit in their hearts, and God himself teaches them how to love.
- **"Brotherly love":** the Greek word originally meant the love between siblings. At a time when their own families may have cut them off, the church became their new family.

**2. More and more.** "For that indeed is what you are doing to all the brothers throughout Macedonia. But we urge you, brothers, to do this more and more" (4:10). Their love already reached beyond their own city to believers throughout Macedonia, perhaps by hosting traveling brothers and helping one another. Still Paul says, "more and more." Love never reaches "enough."

**3. Aspire to live quietly.** "And to aspire to live quietly, and to mind your own affairs, and to work with your hands" (4:11). The word "aspire" originally meant to seek honor and pursue a reputation. Putting it next to "quietly" is almost humorous: what you should strive for with all your might is a quiet life.
- **Why say this?** There may be several reasons. Some may have thought the Lord was coming so soon that they stopped working (see 2 Thess 3:10–12). Some scholars think that poorer people at the time lived off wealthy patrons and had to run around on their errands and enhance their standing. Others may simply, out of zeal, have been meddling everywhere. Paul says: do your own work well, and do not become a burden on others.
- **Working with your hands:** in Greek and Roman culture, people of status looked down on manual labor. Paul himself worked with his hands (2:9), and wanted believers to see that ordinary labor is honorable (see Eph 4:28).

**4. "Live properly before outsiders."** "So that you may live properly before outsiders and be dependent on no one" (4:12). The believers were already talked about in the city; if some were idle and living off others, the gospel would be put to shame.
- **Not looking down on those who cannot work:** Paul is criticizing those who are "not willing" to work (see 2 Thess 3:10), not those who cannot work because of illness, disability, age or unemployment. For them the church is to "help the weak" (5:14).

> The point: God himself teaches us to love one another. Sometimes love takes the form of great sacrifice; sometimes it is simply doing our daily work quietly and faithfully, not becoming a burden on others.

## Going Deeper: 4:13–14 Not Like Those Who Have No Hope

**1. Why the question?** "But we do not want you to be uninformed, brothers, about those who are asleep, that you may not grieve as others do who have no hope" (4:13). After Paul left, some in the church had died, perhaps through persecution, perhaps through illness. The living were confused: would the dead miss the Lord's coming? Paul's time with them had been short, and he had probably not yet taught them in detail about bodily resurrection.

**2. Grief is allowed.** Paul does not say "do not grieve," but do not grieve "as others do who have no hope." Jesus himself "wept" at Lazarus's tomb (John 11:35). Believers who lose loved ones also ache and weep. This is not a lack of faith.
- **Grief without hope:** ancient Greek and Roman gravestones often bore lines like "I was not, I was, I am not," expressing resignation before death. A Greek poet wrote, "There is hope for the living, but the dead have none." Paul says the believer's grief is different, because it has hope.

**3. "Asleep."** Paul calls the death of believers "sleep" (4:13, 14, 15). This does not mean the soul loses consciousness after death; elsewhere Paul says that to depart is to "be with Christ, for that is far better" (Phil 1:23; see 2 Cor 5:8). "Sleep" is a word full of hope: those who sleep will wake. Their bodies await the resurrection.
- **Jesus "died":** notice that Paul says "Jesus died and rose again" (4:14); for Jesus he does not use the word "sleep." Jesus bore the full weight of death, so that for those who belong to him death becomes "sleep."

**4. "God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep."** "For since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so, through Jesus, God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep" (4:14). The believer's hope does not rest on vague optimism but on a fact of history: Jesus died and rose again. His resurrection is the guarantee that those who are his will also rise (see 1 Cor 15:20).
- **Chinese funerals:** at a funeral, believers may weep, remember, tell the story of the one who has died, sing, read Scripture and pray, and speak the hope aloud. The hope spoken of here is for "those who have fallen asleep" through Jesus. For family members who died without believing, Scripture gives us no further promise, and we need not speculate; we can entrust them to a God who is both just and merciful. (See also this study's chapter on 1 Corinthians 15.)

## Going Deeper: 4:15–18 The Lord Himself Will Descend from Heaven

**1. "By a word from the Lord."** "For this we declare to you by a word from the Lord" (4:15). Views differ on what this "word from the Lord" is: it may be teaching Jesus gave during his earthly life, though not recorded word for word in the Gospels (see Matt 24:30–31); or it may be a word the risen Lord revealed to the church through a prophet. Either way, Paul makes clear that this is not his own speculation.

**2. The scene of the Lord's coming.** "For the Lord himself will descend from heaven with a cry of command, with the voice of an archangel, and with the sound of the trumpet of God. And the dead in Christ will rise first" (4:16).
- **"Himself":** not sending an angel, but the Lord coming in person.
- **Cry, archangel's voice, trumpet:** these are all images the Old Testament uses when God appears and gathers his people (see Exod 19:16; Zech 9:14), and Jesus said, "he will send out his angels with a loud trumpet call, and they will gather his elect" (Matt 24:31).
- **The dead will rise first:** this is precisely the answer to the Thessalonians' worry. The believers who have died will not miss out; they will rise "first."

**3. "Caught up" and "meet."** "Then we who are alive, who are left, will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air" (4:17). "Caught up" means to be seized suddenly and forcefully. The Latin translation rendered this with a single word, from which the English word "rapture" comes.
- **The word "meet":** at the time this word was often used for the citizens of a city going out to meet a visiting king or official and then escorting him back into the city (see Acts 28:15, where the brothers from Rome "came... to meet" Paul; Matt 25:6, "Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him"). So many commentators (such as F. F. Bruce and Gene Green) think that believers, after meeting the Lord in the air, will accompany him back to earth; others think the word need not carry that sense here.

**4. Different views.** On the rapture and the order of events at the Lord's return, evangelicals hold several main views, each held by brothers and sisters who read Scripture carefully:
- **Pretribulational rapture:** the dispensational tradition (such as John Walvoord) holds that the Lord will first take the church away in the air, then there will be a period of great tribulation on earth, and finally the Lord will descend to earth with his saints.
- **Posttribulational rapture:** other scholars (such as George Ladd and Douglas Moo) hold that the church will go through the great tribulation, and that the rapture and the Lord's descent are one event: believers are caught up to meet the Lord and at once come down with him.
- **A single coming:** the Reformed and Lutheran traditions and many older traditions (such as the amillennialism represented by Anthony Hoekema), as well as Catholic and Orthodox teaching, generally hold that the rapture and the Lord's return are one event, not two stages.
- **The millennium:** how the Lord's return relates to the "thousand years" of Revelation 20 (premillennial, amillennial, postmillennial) is not discussed in this passage.

These views differ in detail but agree on what matters most: the Lord Jesus will return personally and bodily; the dead in Christ will rise; living believers will be changed (1 Cor 15:51–52); and we will always be with the Lord.

**5. "And so we will always be with the Lord."** This is the summit of the passage. Paul does not describe heaven in detail or give a timetable. He says only that we will be with the Lord, and forever. The center of Christian hope is not a place but a person.

**6. "Encourage one another with these words."** "Therefore encourage one another with these words" (4:18). Paul speaks of the last things not to start arguments or spread fear, but so that people may comfort one another. If our talk about the Lord's return only makes people afraid, or divides the church into parties, we have missed Paul's purpose.
- **"We who are alive, who are left":** Paul places himself among "the living," and some say this shows he was sure the Lord would come in his lifetime, and was wrong. But in the very next passage Paul says the day will come "like a thief in the night" (5:2), and no one knows the time. He speaks with the hope every generation of believers should have: the Lord may come at any time.

> The point: The believer's grief is grief with hope, because Jesus died and rose again. The Lord will descend in person, the dead will rise first, and we will always be with the Lord. Believers hold different views on timing and order, but the purpose of this hope is that we comfort one another.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Therefore encourage one another with these words" (4:18). When someone near me loses a loved one, I often do not know what to say. Sometimes I say "be strong and accept it," sometimes "they've gone to a better place," and sometimes I can't say anything, so I keep my distance for fear of saying the wrong thing. Paul has given us "these words," but he does not tell us to deliver a lecture on the end times the moment we meet. At Lazarus's tomb Jesus first "wept" (John 11:35), and then said, "I am the resurrection and the life" (John 11:25). He wept with people first, and then spoke the hope. Today perhaps a brother or sister is grieving a parent, a spouse, even a child. I can go and sit beside them first, without hurrying to explain, without hurrying to tell them to be strong. When the time is right, I can say gently, "The Lord himself will descend from heaven... and so we will always be with the Lord." Perhaps I am the one who is grieving. Then I need not pretend it does not hurt; I can weep, and I can let others comfort me with these words. The Lord who wept for Lazarus weeps with me too; the Lord who rose from the dead will come for me himself.
