## 本章脉络

第五章是全信的结尾，分成三大段。**主的日子**（1-11节）：主的日子要像夜间的贼一样来到，但信徒是光明之子，要警醒谨守；「神不是预定我们受刑，乃是预定我们藉着我们主耶稣基督得救」。**教会的生活**（12-22节）：敬重劳苦的人，彼此和睦；警戒、勉励、扶助、忍耐；常常喜乐，不住地祷告，凡事谢恩；不要消灭圣灵的感动，但要凡事察验。**祝福和结语**（23-28节）：愿神亲自使他们全然成圣；「那召你们的本是信实的，他必成就这事」；请他们为保罗祷告，把信念给众人听，愿主的恩常与他们同在。

第四章回答的是「死了的人怎么办」，第五章回答的是「活着的人怎么办」。保罗不给时间表，而是告诉他们怎样在等候中生活：警醒、谨守，彼此建立。全信的最后，焦点又回到神身上：叫人成圣的是神，召人的是神，成就这事的也是神。

## 深读 5:1-3 好像夜间的贼

**1. 「时候、日期」。** 「弟兄们，论到时候、日期，不用写信给你们」（5:1）。这个说法也见于但以理书（但2:21），耶稣复活以后也对门徒说：「父凭着自己的权柄所定的时候、日期，不是你们可以知道的。」（徒1:7）帖撒罗尼迦人很可能想知道主到底什么时候来。保罗说，这件事你们已经知道了，就是：没有人知道。

**2. 「好像夜间的贼一样」。** 「因为你们自己明明晓得，主的日子来到，好像夜间的贼一样」（5:2）。这个比喻是耶稣自己用过的：「家主若知道几更天有贼来，就必警醒」（太24:43）。贼不会预先通知，所以重点不是猜什么时候，而是随时预备好。
- **「主的日子」：** 这是旧约先知常用的说法，指神介入历史、施行审判和拯救的日子（参摩5:18；珥2:31）。新约把这一天和主耶稣的再来连在一起。
- **关于预言日期：** 教会历史上，不断有人宣称算出了主再来的确切日子，吸引许多人变卖家产等候，结果一次又一次落空，也叫许多人跌倒。保罗和耶稣的教导都很清楚：任何人宣称知道日子，都不必相信（参太24:36）。

**3. 「平安稳妥」。** 「人正说『平安稳妥』的时候，灾祸忽然临到他们，如同产难临到怀胎的妇人一样，他们绝不能逃脱」（5:3）。许多学者指出，「平安稳妥」很像罗马帝国宣传自己的口号：罗马带来了和平与安全。也有学者认为这里主要是呼应旧约假先知的话：「平安了！平安了！其实没有平安。」（耶6:14）耶利米书接着也说「疼痛仿佛产难的妇人」（耶6:24）。两种背景可以同时存在：人以为靠自己的制度和力量就可以安全，却不知道审判近了。这也是对还没有信主的人的警告：那日子来到，人的制度、财富和功德都靠不住。「现在正是拯救的日子」（林后6:2），要悔改，投靠那位替我们死的主。
- **产难的比喻：** 产难有两个特点：来得突然，却也一定会来。怀孕的妇人不知道确切的时刻，却知道那一天一定会到。

## 深读 5:4-11 光明之子，警醒谨守

**1. 「你们却不在黑暗里」。** 「弟兄们，你们却不在黑暗里，叫那日子临到你们像贼一样。你们都是光明之子，都是白昼之子」（5:4-5）。保罗不是要吓唬信徒，而是要提醒他们自己是谁。那日子临到不信的人，像贼一样；对信徒，却是他们所盼望的日子。
- **「之子」：** 这是希伯来式的说法，「光明之子」就是属于光明、带着光明特征的人。信徒有这样的身份，不是自己挣来的，而是因为神把他们从黑暗中迁了出来（参西1:13）。

**2. 不要睡觉。** 「所以，我们不要睡觉像别人一样，总要警醒谨守」（5:6）。这里的「睡觉」和4:13的「睡了」意思不同：那里是指死亡，这里是指属灵上的麻木、不在意。
- **「谨守」：** 原文的意思是清醒、不醉。保罗紧接着说「醉了的人是在夜间醉」（5:7）。这不只是说不要喝醉酒，而是说不要让任何东西麻醉我们的心，叫我们忘记主要来。

**3. 信、爱、望的军装。** 「但我们既然属乎白昼，就应当谨守，把信和爱当作护心镜遮胸，把得救的盼望当作头盔戴上」（5:8）。这个意象来自以赛亚书：神自己「以公义为铠甲，以拯救为头盔」（赛59:17），出来为他的百姓争战。保罗后来在以弗所书6章把这个意象展开得更完整。
- **又是信、爱、望：** 保罗在1:3提过这三样，现在又把它们当作军装。在等候主来的时候，保护我们的不是自己的聪明，而是信靠神、彼此相爱，以及得救的确实盼望。
- **得救的盼望：** 「盼望」在圣经里不是一厢情愿地希望会这样，而是对神所应许之事的确实期待。

**4. 「他替我们死」。** 「因为神不是预定我们受刑，乃是预定我们藉着我们主耶稣基督得救。他替我们死，叫我们无论醒着，睡着，都与他同活」（5:9-10）。这是全信最清楚的福音宣告。
- **「受刑」：** 原文就是「忿怒」，和1:10的「将来忿怒」是同一个词。神的忿怒是真实的，但神对信徒的定意，不是忿怒，而是拯救。（持灾前被提看法的人也常引用这一节，认为信徒不必经历大灾难。）
- **「替我们」：** 耶稣的死是为了我们、代替我们。他承受了我们当受的忿怒，好叫我们得着他的生命（参林后5:21）。我们能够不惧怕主的日子，根基不在于我们警醒得够不够，而在于他已经替我们死了。
- **「无论醒着，睡着」：** 保罗在这里又回到了第四章的问题。「醒着」指活着的人，「睡着」指已经死去的人。无论主来的时候我们是活着还是已经死了，都要「与他同活」。这是对帖撒罗尼迦人最后的安慰。

**5. 彼此劝慰，互相建立。** 「所以，你们该彼此劝慰，互相建立，正如你们素常所行的」（5:11）。这句话和4:18几乎一样。末世的教导，最后落在教会彼此之间的关系上。
- **「建立」：** 原文是盖房子的意思。信徒彼此的话语和关心，就像一块一块的砖，把对方建造起来。

> 要点：主的日子会突然来到，但信徒不必惧怕，因为神预定我们得救，不是受刑；耶稣已经替我们死了。我们等候他的方式，是清醒地活着，穿戴信、爱、望，彼此建立。

## 深读 5:12-15 在教会里彼此相待

**1. 敬重劳苦的人。** 「弟兄们，我们劝你们敬重那在你们中间劳苦的人，就是在主里面治理你们、劝戒你们的；又因他们所做的工，用爱心格外尊重他们」（5:12-13）。这个教会刚成立不久，大概还没有正式的「长老」称号，但已经有一些人在带领、照顾和劝戒其他人。
- **三件事：** 他们「劳苦」（做得筋疲力尽）、「治理」（原文有带领和照顾的意思）、「劝戒」（警告、纠正）。
- **「因他们所做的工」：** 尊重的理由不是头衔或地位，而是他们的工作。保罗要信徒「用爱心」尊重，不是因为惧怕。
- **尊重不等于不受监督：** 华人教会尊重传道人是好的；但尊重带领的人，不等于他们说什么都不能质疑，更不等于他们犯了罪也要掩盖。新约同样要求，对长老的指控要有见证人，犯罪的要在众人面前受责备（提前5:19-20）。如果带领的人有欺压、性侵犯或其他违法的事，应当按程序处理，涉及犯罪的应当报警（罗13:4）。受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必和不安全的人和好；施害的人即使认罪悔改，也不能因此恢复职分，或再接近受害人和孩子。教会绝不可以为了「家丑不可外扬」而替带领的人遮掩。

**2. 「你们也要彼此和睦」。** 「你们也要彼此和睦」（5:13）。一个受外面逼迫的教会，最怕的就是里面不和。和睦不是不讲真话、把问题压下去，而是在真理和爱中一同生活。

**3. 不同的人，不同的帮助。** 「我们又劝弟兄们，要警戒不守规矩的人，勉励灰心的人，扶助软弱的人，也要向众人忍耐」（5:14）。这句话不是只对领袖说的，而是对「弟兄们」，也就是全教会说的。
- **不守规矩的人：** 原文指像士兵离开队伍一样，不守秩序；在这封信里很可能是指不肯做工的人（参4:11-12；帖后3:6-12）。这样的人需要「警戒」。
- **灰心的人：** 原文直译是心小的人，也就是胆怯、容易沮丧的人。这样的人需要「勉励」，而不是责备。
- **软弱的人：** 可能指信心软弱的，也可能指身体或生活上软弱的。这样的人需要有人「扶助」，就是在旁边扶着他。
- **向众人忍耐：** 不同的人需要不同的对待，但对所有的人，都要有耐心。神对我们也是这样忍耐（参彼后3:9）。

**4. 不可以恶报恶。** 「你们要谨慎，无论是谁都不可以恶报恶；或是彼此相待，或是待众人，常要追求良善」（5:15）。这个教会正受着「本地人的苦害」，要做到这句话很不容易。这是耶稣自己的教导（太5:44），也是他自己的样式：「他被骂不还口」（彼前2:23）。彼得接着说：「他被挂在木头上，亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24）。他不但没有以恶报恶，反而亲身承受了我们的恶；我们能不以恶报恶，是因为我们自己先蒙了这样的恩。
- **不报复不等于不求公义：** 不以恶报恶，不是说受害的人不可以报警、不可以寻求法律的保护，也不是说他们必须继续留在危险之中。把事情交给神和他所设立的权柄（罗12:19；13:4），正是不自己报复的方式。

> 要点：教会是一个家：带领的人劳苦服事，其他人用爱尊重他们；软弱的、灰心的、走偏的，各人都得到合适的帮助。这一切都源于神先向我们忍耐。

## 深读 5:16-18 常常喜乐，不住地祷告，凡事谢恩

**1. 三个很短的命令。** 「要常常喜乐，不住地祷告，凡事谢恩；因为这是神在基督耶稣里向你们所定的旨意」（5:16-18）。这三句话在原文都很短，很容易背，也很难做到。
- **常常喜乐：** 这不是要人永远面带笑容。保罗自己也有眼泪（参林后2:4）。这喜乐的根基是神，不是环境；所以在患难中也可能有喜乐（参1:6）。
- **不住地祷告：** 不是说一天二十四小时都跪着，而是像呼吸一样，生活中随时转向神。保罗自己就是这样，他说自己「昼夜切切地祈求」（3:10）。
- **凡事谢恩：** 原文的意思是在一切的事上谢恩，不是为一切的事谢恩。我们不必为罪恶、虐待或灾难本身感谢神；但在任何处境中，我们都能找到可以感谢神的地方，因为他仍然与我们同在。（以弗所书5:20说「凡事要……常常感谢父神」，那是为神一切的恩赐和他美好的旨意感谢，不是为罪恶本身感谢。）

**2. 「神……所定的旨意」。** 第四章已经说过，神清楚的旨意是叫我们成圣（4:3）；这里保罗又加上喜乐、祷告、谢恩。
- **「在基督耶稣里」：** 这是神「在基督耶稣里」向我们所定的。我们能喜乐、祷告、谢恩，是因为在基督里，神已经成为我们的父。

**3. 不是不许哀伤。** 诗篇里有许多哀歌，耶稣在客西马尼园也「忧愁起来，极其难过」（太26:37）。「常常喜乐」不是要我们压抑痛苦，而是在痛苦中仍然向神说话。保罗和西拉在腓立比的监里，背上还带着伤，「约在半夜……祷告，唱诗赞美神」（徒16:25）。

## 深读 5:19-22 不要消灭圣灵的感动

**1. 「不要消灭」。** 「不要消灭圣灵的感动；不要藐视先知的讲论」（5:19-20）。「消灭」原文是用来指扑灭火的。圣灵在圣经里常常和火连在一起（参徒2:3）。信徒可以用冷淡、怀疑或刻意的压制，扑灭圣灵在教会中的工作。
- **先知的讲论：** 早期教会里，有人会说出圣灵感动的话，用来劝勉、安慰、造就人（林前14:3）。帖撒罗尼迦教会可能因为有人滥用，就开始看不起这样的讲论。
- **不同的看法：** 今天福音派对「先知的讲论」有不同的理解。终止论（如加芬）认为，带着启示性质的先知恩赐在使徒时代以后已经停止，今天相应的是根据圣经的讲道和劝勉；延续论（如古德恩）认为，这种恩赐今天仍然存在，但它的权威低于圣经，必须经过察验。（另参本研读林前第十二至十四章。）

**2. 「但要凡事察验」。** 「但要凡事察验；善美的要持守，各样的恶事要禁戒不做」（5:21-22）。保罗没有叫人全盘接受，也没有叫人全盘拒绝，而是要察验（参约一4:1；林前14:29）。
- **用什么察验：** 用圣经，用福音的核心（基督是谁、他做了什么），也看说话的人的生命和果子。
- **「各样的恶事」：** 有些旧译本（如英文钦定本）把这句译成各样恶的外表，有人就理解为凡事都要避嫌、怕别人误会。「瓜田李下」式的避嫌自有它的智慧，但这节经文讲的不是这个。和合本译作「各样的恶事」，更接近原文。保罗是说，察验之后发现是恶的，就要远离，不管它以什么面貌出现。

> 要点：圣灵在教会里做工，我们不可以扑灭他；但不是每一句自称出于圣灵的话都是真的，所以要用神的话凡事察验，持守善美的，远离一切的恶。

## 深读 5:23-28 他必成就这事

**1. 「赐平安的神亲自」。** 「愿赐平安的神亲自使你们全然成圣！」（5:23）保罗在全信讲了许多劝勉，最后却不是说你们要努力成圣，而是祷告：愿神亲自使你们成圣。成圣是神的工作。
- **「全然」：** 原文的意思是完全、彻底，不留下任何一部分。神不是只要我们的一部分，而是要使我们整个人都圣洁。

**2. 灵、魂、身子。** 「又愿你们的灵与魂与身子得蒙保守，在我们主耶稣基督降临的时候，完全无可指摘！」（5:23）人常用这句话来讨论人的构成：
- **三分说：** 有些人认为人由灵、魂、体三部分组成（如十九世纪的德里慈，以及《司可福串注圣经》），这种看法对华人教会影响很深（例如倪柝声的《属灵人》）。
- **二分说：** 更多的改革宗和福音派神学家（如加尔文、伯克富、古德恩）认为，人由物质的身体和非物质的灵魂组成，「灵」和「魂」是从不同角度描述同一个内在的人（参太10:28只提「身体」和「灵魂」；可12:30也连用心、性、意、力来说整个人）。

无论哪一种，保罗在这里的重点都不是给人的构成画图，而是说：神要保守整个人，里里外外，直到主来。

**3. 「那召你们的本是信实的」。** 「那召你们的本是信实的，他必成就这事。」（5:24）这是全信最安慰人的一句话。
- **我们的成圣，靠的是神的信实：** 帖撒罗尼迦人的信心还有「不足」（3:10），在性的事上还在挣扎（4:3-8），有人灰心，有人软弱（5:14）。可是保罗的盼望不是建立在他们身上，而是建立在神身上。神开始了这工作，他也必完成（参腓1:6）。
- **「召」：** 神的呼召贯穿全信：他召他们进他的国、得他的荣耀（2:12），召他们成为圣洁（4:7），现在又说，那召他们的是信实的。

**4. 结语。** 「请弟兄们为我们祷告。与众弟兄亲嘴问安，务要圣洁。我指着主嘱咐你们，要把这信念给众弟兄听。愿我主耶稣基督的恩常与你们同在！」（5:25-28）
- **使徒也需要祷告：** 保罗不是高高在上的属灵伟人，他也请这些年轻的信徒为他祷告。
- **圣洁的亲嘴：** 在当时，亲吻是家人和朋友之间常见的问安方式。保罗说「务要圣洁」，是要他们像神家里的人那样，用圣洁的心彼此问安。华人教会今天多用握手、点头，或一声「平安」，表达的是同样的意思。
- **「念给众弟兄听」：** 如果这是保罗现存最早的书信（参导读），这也是新约里最早明文吩咐把书信念给全教会听的地方（参西4:16）。保罗为什么要起誓嘱咐，经文没有说；也许是要确保每一个人，包括不识字的人，都听到这些话。
- **恩典开始，恩典结束：** 这封信以「愿恩惠、平安归与你们」开始（1:1），以「愿我主耶稣基督的恩常与你们同在」结束。

> 要点：保罗把所有的劝勉都交在神手中：叫人成圣的是神，召人的是神，成就这事的也是神。我们的盼望不在于自己有多坚强，而在于那召我们的本是信实的。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因为神不是预定我们受刑，乃是预定我们藉着我们主耶稣基督得救。他替我们死，叫我们无论醒着，睡着，都与他同活。」（5:9-10）有些人一想到主的日子，心里就紧张。也许是小时候听过的讲道，讲到末日的灾难，讲到有人被提、有人被留下；也许是看了一部电影，或者听人说主再来的日子已经被算出来了。我曾经害怕：如果主今天来，我是不是准备好了？我是不是够警醒？那种害怕，有时让我更努力地读经祷告，有时却让我干脆不去想。保罗写这一段，不是为了叫帖撒罗尼迦人害怕，而是叫他们「彼此劝慰」。他说，那日子对不信的人像贼，对信的人却不是这样，因为神对我们的定意不是忿怒，而是拯救；而这拯救的根据，是耶稣「替我们死」。到那日我能站立得住，不是因为我警醒得够，而是因为他替我承受了忿怒。如果我连自己是不是属他的人都不确定，这句话也是向我发出的邀请：他是「替我们死」的，替罪人死的；今天我就可以投靠他，不必等到自己觉得够好。今天，我可以把心里对那日子的恐惧说出来，然后读这一句：「那召你们的本是信实的，他必成就这事。」（5:24）他召了我，他必保守我，直到他来；那一天，将是我见他面的日子。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 closes the letter in three main parts. **The day of the Lord** (vv. 1–11): the day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night, but believers are children of light and must stay awake and sober; "God has not destined us for wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ." **Church life** (vv. 12–22): respect those who labor among you and be at peace; admonish, encourage, help, be patient; rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances; do not quench the Spirit, but test everything. **Blessing and closing** (vv. 23–28): may God himself sanctify them completely; "He who calls you is faithful; he will surely do it"; pray for us, read this letter to all, and the grace of the Lord be with you.

Chapter 4 answered "what about the dead?"; chapter 5 answers "what about the living?" Paul gives no timetable; instead he tells them how to live while they wait: awake, sober, building one another up. At the very end the focus returns to God: it is God who sanctifies, God who calls, and God who will do it.

## Going Deeper: 5:1–3 Like a Thief in the Night

**1. "The times and the seasons."** "Now concerning the times and the seasons, brothers, you have no need to have anything written to you" (5:1). The pairing appears in Daniel (Dan 2:21), and after his resurrection Jesus told the disciples, "It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father has fixed by his own authority" (Acts 1:7). The Thessalonians very likely wanted to know when the Lord would come. Paul says: you already know this, namely, that no one knows.

**2. "Like a thief in the night."** "For you yourselves are fully aware that the day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night" (5:2). Jesus himself used this picture: "if the master of the house had known in what part of the night the thief was coming, he would have stayed awake" (Matt 24:43). A thief gives no notice, so the point is not to guess the time but to be ready at any time.
- **"The day of the Lord":** a phrase the Old Testament prophets often used for the day God steps into history to judge and to save (see Amos 5:18; Joel 2:31). The New Testament links that day with the return of the Lord Jesus.
- **On predicting dates:** throughout church history, people have claimed to have calculated the exact day of the Lord's return, drawing many to sell their possessions and wait, only to be disappointed again and again, and causing many to stumble. Paul's teaching and Jesus' are clear: no one who claims to know the day is to be believed (see Matt 24:36).

**3. "Peace and security."** "While people are saying, 'There is peace and security,' then sudden destruction will come upon them as labor pains come upon a pregnant woman, and they will not escape" (5:3). Many scholars note that "peace and security" sounds very like the Roman Empire's own propaganda: Rome had brought peace and safety. Others think the main echo is the Old Testament false prophets, "saying, 'Peace, peace,' when there is no peace" (Jer 6:14). Jeremiah goes on to speak of "pain as of a woman in labor" (Jer 6:24). Both backgrounds can stand together: people think their own systems and strength make them safe, unaware that judgment is near. This is also a warning to those who have not yet believed: when that day comes, no system, wealth or merit will hold. "Now is the day of salvation" (2 Cor 6:2); repent and take refuge in the Lord who died for us.
- **The picture of labor pains:** labor has two features: it comes suddenly, and it surely comes. A pregnant woman does not know the exact moment, but she knows the day will come.

## Going Deeper: 5:4–11 Children of Light, Awake and Sober

**1. "But you are not in darkness."** "But you are not in darkness, brothers, for that day to surprise you like a thief. For you are all children of light, children of the day" (5:4–5). Paul is not trying to frighten believers but to remind them who they are. To unbelievers that day comes like a thief; to believers it is the day they long for.
- **"Children of":** this is a Hebrew way of speaking; "children of light" are people who belong to the light and bear its marks. Believers did not earn this identity; God brought them out of darkness (see Col 1:13).

**2. Do not sleep.** "So then let us not sleep, as others do, but let us keep awake and be sober" (5:6). "Sleep" here differs from "asleep" in 4:13: there it meant death; here it means spiritual numbness and indifference.
- **"Be sober":** the word means clear-headed, not drunk. Paul goes on, "those who get drunk, are drunk at night" (5:7). This is not only about not getting drunk on wine; it means not letting anything dull our hearts until we forget that the Lord is coming.

**3. The armor of faith, love and hope.** "But since we belong to the day, let us be sober, having put on the breastplate of faith and love, and for a helmet the hope of salvation" (5:8). The image comes from Isaiah, where God himself "put on righteousness as a breastplate, and a helmet of salvation on his head" (Isa 59:17) and went out to fight for his people. Paul later develops the image more fully in Ephesians 6.
- **Faith, love and hope again:** Paul mentioned these three in 1:3, and now makes them armor. While we wait for the Lord, what protects us is not our own cleverness but trust in God, love for one another, and a sure hope of salvation.
- **The hope of salvation:** "hope" in Scripture is not wishful thinking but confident expectation of what God has promised.

**4. "Who died for us."** "For God has not destined us for wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ, who died for us so that whether we are awake or asleep we might live with him" (5:9–10). This is the letter's clearest statement of the gospel.
- **"Wrath":** the same word as "the wrath to come" in 1:10. God's wrath is real, but God's purpose for believers is not wrath but salvation. (Pretribulationists often cite this verse for the church's exemption from the tribulation.)
- **"For us":** Jesus died for us and in our place. He bore the wrath we deserved so that we might have his life (see 2 Cor 5:21). We need not fear the day of the Lord, not because we are watchful enough, but because he has already died for us.
- **"Whether we are awake or asleep":** here Paul returns to the question of chapter 4. "Awake" means those still alive; "asleep" means those who have died. Whether we are alive or already dead when the Lord comes, we will "live with him." This is Paul's final comfort to the Thessalonians.

**5. Encourage and build up one another.** "Therefore encourage one another and build one another up, just as you are doing" (5:11). This is almost the same as 4:18. Teaching about the end lands, in the end, on how the church treats one another.
- **"Build up":** the word means to build a house. Believers' words and care for one another are like bricks, building each other up.

> The point: The day of the Lord will come suddenly, but believers need not fear, because God has destined us for salvation, not wrath; Jesus has already died for us. We wait for him by living awake, wearing faith, love and hope, and building one another up.

## Going Deeper: 5:12–15 How to Treat One Another in the Church

**1. Respect those who labor.** "We ask you, brothers, to respect those who labor among you and are over you in the Lord and admonish you, and to esteem them very highly in love because of their work" (5:12–13). This young church probably had no formal title of "elder" yet, but some were already leading, caring for and admonishing others.
- **Three things:** they "labor" (to the point of exhaustion), are "over you" (the word carries the sense of leading and caring for), and "admonish" (warn and correct).
- **"Because of their work":** the reason for respect is not title or status but their work. Paul wants believers to esteem them "in love," not out of fear.
- **Respect is not freedom from accountability:** Respecting pastors, as Chinese churches do, is good; but respecting leaders does not mean they can never be questioned, still less that their sin should be covered up. The New Testament also requires that charges against an elder be supported by witnesses and that those who persist in sin be rebuked publicly (1 Tim 5:19–20). If a leader is guilty of oppression, abuse or crime, it should be dealt with through proper process, and crimes should be reported to the police (Rom 13:4). Victims need not remain in danger or reconcile with someone unsafe; even genuine repentance does not restore an abuser's office or give him access to victims or children. The church must never cover up a leader's abuse to save face.

**2. "Be at peace among yourselves."** "Be at peace among yourselves" (5:13). A church under persecution from outside has most to fear from discord within. Peace does not mean avoiding the truth and pushing problems down; it means living together in truth and love.

**3. Different people, different help.** "And we urge you, brothers, admonish the idle, encourage the fainthearted, help the weak, be patient with them all" (5:14). These words are addressed not only to leaders but to "brothers," the whole church.
- **The idle:** the word describes a soldier out of rank, disorderly; in this letter it very likely means those who will not work (see 4:11–12; 2 Thess 3:6–12). Such people need to be "admonished."
- **The fainthearted:** literally "small-souled," that is, timid and easily discouraged. Such people need encouragement, not rebuke.
- **The weak:** this may mean weak in faith, or weak in body or circumstances. Such people need someone to "help," to come alongside and hold them up.
- **Patient with all:** different people need different treatment, but all need patience. God is patient with us like this (see 2 Pet 3:9).

**4. Do not repay evil for evil.** "See that no one repays anyone evil for evil, but always seek to do good to one another and to everyone" (5:15). For a church suffering "from your own countrymen," this was hard to do. It is Jesus' own teaching (Matt 5:44), and his own way: "When he was reviled, he did not revile in return" (1 Pet 2:23). Peter goes on, "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" (1 Pet 2:24). Far from repaying evil, he bore ours in his own body; we can refuse to repay evil because we ourselves have first received such grace.
- **Not taking revenge is not giving up on justice:** not repaying evil for evil does not mean victims may not report to the police or seek the protection of the law, nor that they must remain in danger. Handing the matter to God and to the authorities he has appointed (Rom 12:19; 13:4) is the very way not to take revenge oneself.

> The point: The church is a family: leaders labor to serve and others esteem them in love; the weak, the fainthearted and those who stray each receive the help they need. All of this flows from God's patience with us first.

## Going Deeper: 5:16–18 Rejoice Always, Pray Without Ceasing, Give Thanks

**1. Three short commands.** "Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you" (5:16–18). In Greek these three lines are very short, easy to memorize and hard to live.
- **Rejoice always:** this does not mean wearing a permanent smile. Paul himself had tears (see 2 Cor 2:4). This joy rests on God, not circumstances, so it is possible even in affliction (see 1:6).
- **Pray without ceasing:** not kneeling twenty-four hours a day, but turning to God throughout life, like breathing. Paul himself did this; he says he prayed "most earnestly night and day" (3:10).
- **Give thanks in all circumstances:** the Greek means giving thanks "in everything," not "for everything." We need not thank God for sin, abuse or disaster themselves; but in any situation we can find something to thank God for, because he is still with us. (Eph 5:20 does say "giving thanks always and for everything"; that is thanks for all God's gifts and his good purposes, not thanks for evil itself.)

**2. "The will of God."** Chapter 4 already showed that God's clear will is our sanctification (4:3); here Paul adds joy, prayer and thanksgiving.
- **"In Christ Jesus":** this is God's will for us "in Christ Jesus." We can rejoice, pray and give thanks because in Christ God has become our Father.

**3. Not a ban on lament.** The Psalms are full of laments, and in Gethsemane Jesus "began to be sorrowful and troubled" (Matt 26:37). "Rejoice always" does not ask us to suppress pain but to keep speaking to God in it. Paul and Silas, in prison at Philippi still wounded from the beating, were "about midnight... praying and singing hymns to God" (Acts 16:25).

## Going Deeper: 5:19–22 Do Not Quench the Spirit

**1. "Do not quench."** "Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies" (5:19–20). "Quench" is the word for putting out a fire. In Scripture the Holy Spirit is often linked with fire (see Acts 2:3). Believers can put out the Spirit's work in the church through coldness, suspicion or deliberate suppression.
- **Prophecies:** in the early church some would speak words prompted by the Spirit, for upbuilding, encouragement and consolation (1 Cor 14:3). The Thessalonian church may have begun to despise such words because some had misused them.
- **Different views:** evangelicals today understand "prophecy" differently. Cessationists (such as Richard Gaffin) hold that the revelatory gift of prophecy ceased after the apostolic age, and that its counterpart today is preaching and exhortation grounded in Scripture. Continuationists (such as Wayne Grudem) hold that the gift continues today, but with authority lower than Scripture, and must always be tested. (See also this study's chapters on 1 Corinthians 12–14.)

**2. "But test everything."** "But test everything; hold fast what is good. Abstain from every form of evil" (5:21–22). Paul tells them neither to accept everything nor to reject everything, but to test (see 1 John 4:1; 1 Cor 14:29).
- **Test by what:** by Scripture, by the heart of the gospel (who Christ is and what he has done), and by the life and fruit of the one speaking.
- **"Every form of evil":** an old English translation rendered this "all appearance of evil," and some took it to mean always avoiding anything that might look bad to others. But the Greek means "every kind of evil": once testing shows something to be evil, stay away from it, whatever form it takes. Care about appearances has its own wisdom, but this verse is not about that; the CUV's "every form of evil" is closer to the Greek.

> The point: The Holy Spirit works in the church, and we must not quench him; but not every word claiming to come from the Spirit is true, so we test everything by God's word, hold fast what is good, and keep away from every evil.

## Going Deeper: 5:23–28 He Will Surely Do It

**1. "The God of peace himself."** "Now may the God of peace himself sanctify you completely" (5:23). After all his exhortations, Paul does not end by telling them to work hard at being holy, but with a prayer that God himself would sanctify them. Sanctification is God's work.
- **"Completely":** the word means wholly, through and through, leaving no part out. God does not want only part of us; he means to make the whole person holy.

**2. Spirit, soul and body.** "And may your whole spirit and soul and body be kept blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ" (5:23). This verse is often used in discussions of human nature:
- **Trichotomy:** some hold that humans have three parts, spirit, soul and body (such as Franz Delitzsch in the nineteenth century, and the Scofield Reference Bible); this view has also been very influential in Chinese churches (for example, Watchman Nee's *The Spiritual Man*).
- **Dichotomy:** more Reformed and evangelical theologians (such as Calvin, Louis Berkhof and Wayne Grudem) hold that humans consist of a material body and an immaterial soul, and that "spirit" and "soul" describe the same inner person from different angles (see Matt 10:28, which mentions only "body" and "soul"; Mark 12:30 likewise piles up heart, soul, mind and strength for the whole person).

Either way, Paul's point here is not to draw a diagram of human nature, but to say that God will keep the whole person, inside and out, until the Lord comes.

**3. "He who calls you is faithful."** "He who calls you is faithful; he will surely do it" (5:24). This is the most comforting sentence in the letter.
- **Our sanctification rests on God's faithfulness:** the Thessalonians' faith still had gaps (3:10); they were still struggling in sexual matters (4:3–8); some were fainthearted, some weak (5:14). But Paul's hope did not rest on them; it rested on God. God began this work, and he will complete it (see Phil 1:6).
- **"Calls":** God's call runs through the whole letter: he calls them into his own kingdom and glory (2:12), calls them to holiness (4:7), and now Paul says the one who calls them is faithful.

**4. Closing.** "Brothers, pray for us. Greet all the brothers with a holy kiss. I put you under oath before the Lord to have this letter read to all the brothers. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you" (5:25–28).
- **Apostles need prayer too:** Paul is no spiritual giant on a pedestal; he asks these young believers to pray for him.
- **A holy kiss:** a kiss was a common greeting among family and friends at the time. Paul says "holy": greet one another as members of God's household. In many cultures today a handshake, an embrace or a greeting expresses the same thing; Chinese churches often use a handshake, a nod or a word of "peace."
- **"Read to all the brothers":** if this is Paul's earliest surviving letter (see the introduction), this is the earliest written instruction in the New Testament for a letter to be read to the whole church (cf. Col 4:16). The text does not say why Paul puts them under oath; perhaps to make sure everyone, including those who could not read, heard it.
- **Beginning and ending in grace:** the letter begins with "Grace to you and peace" (1:1) and ends with "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you."

> The point: Paul places all his exhortations in God's hands: it is God who sanctifies, God who calls, and God who will do it. Our hope does not lie in how strong we are, but in the faithfulness of the one who calls us.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For God has not destined us for wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ, who died for us so that whether we are awake or asleep we might live with him" (5:9–10). Some people tense up whenever they think about the day of the Lord. Perhaps it was sermons heard in childhood about end-time disasters, about some taken and some left behind; perhaps a film, or someone claiming the date of the Lord's return had been calculated. I have been afraid: if the Lord came today, would I be ready? Am I watchful enough? That fear has sometimes made me read and pray harder, and sometimes made me stop thinking about it at all. Paul did not write this passage to frighten the Thessalonians but so that they could "encourage one another." He says that to unbelievers that day comes like a thief, but not so for believers, because God's purpose for us is not wrath but salvation; and the ground of that salvation is that Jesus "died for us." I can stand before that day not because I have been watchful enough, but because he bore the wrath in my place. And if I am not even sure that I belong to him, these words are an invitation to me as well: he "died for us," for sinners; I can take refuge in him today, without waiting until I feel good enough. Today I can name my fear of that day before him, and then read this line: "He who calls you is faithful; he will surely do it" (5:24). He called me, he will keep me until he comes; and that day will be the day I see his face.
