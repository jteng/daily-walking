## 这卷书在讲什么

提摩太前书是保罗写给一位同工的信。保罗往马其顿去，把提摩太留在以弗所，「好嘱咐那几个人不可传异教」（1:3）。以弗所的教会已经有长老（参徒20:17），却出了问题：有人讲「荒渺无凭的话语和无穷的家谱」（1:4），有人「想要作教法师，却不明白自己所讲说的」（1:7），有人禁止嫁娶、禁戒食物（4:3），有人以敬虔为得利的门路（6:5）。提摩太要在这样的处境中坚守真道，也要整顿神的家。

保罗自己说明了写信的目的：「倘若我耽延日久，你也可以知道在神的家中当怎样行。这家就是永生神的教会，真理的柱石和根基。」（3:15）

所以这封信讲了很多教会生活的实际问题：
- **教导：** 要抵挡异教和一切「敌正道的事」（1:10），持守「纯正的话」（6:3）。
- **祷告和敬拜：** 要为万人祷告，为君王和一切在位的祷告（2:1-2）；男人、女人在聚会中当怎样行（2:8-15）。
- **带领的人：** 监督和执事要有怎样的品格（3:1-13）；怎样对待长老，包括对长老的控告（5:17-25）。
- **教会里的各种人：** 老年人和少年人（5:1-2）、寡妇（5:3-16）、作仆人的（6:1-2）、富足的人（6:17-19）。
- **提摩太自己：** 要操练敬虔（4:7）、不可叫人小看他年轻（4:12）、要谨慎自己和自己的教训（4:16）、要逃避贪财（6:10-11）、要为真道打那美好的仗（6:12）。

但这封信不只是一本教会手册。在讲规矩以前，保罗先讲福音：「『基督耶稣降世，为要拯救罪人。』这话是可信的，是十分可佩服的。在罪人中我是个罪魁。」（1:15）在讲完监督和执事以后，他又唱出一首颂赞基督的诗：「大哉，敬虔的奥秘……」（3:16）。教会的秩序，是从福音来的；神的家，是建立在基督身上的。

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 提摩太是谁。** 提摩太很可能是路司得人，母亲是「信主之犹太妇人」，父亲是希腊人（徒16:1）。他从小就从外祖母罗以和母亲友妮基那里认识圣经（提后1:5；3:15）。保罗第二次旅行布道时带他同去（徒16:3），此后他一直是保罗最亲近的同工之一。保罗称他为「我所亲爱、有忠心的儿子」（林前4:17），又说：「我没有别人与我同心」（腓2:20）。保罗也常常派他去处理棘手的教会问题（帖前3:2；林前4:17；腓2:19）。
- **他的为人：** 从保罗的话看，提摩太可能比较年轻（4:12），身体也不太好（5:23），有时显得胆怯（参林前16:10-11；提后1:7）。保罗却把这样一个人留在一个艰难的地方。

**2. 以弗所。** 以弗所是罗马亚细亚省最重要的城市之一，有著名的亚底米神庙（参徒19:27、35）。保罗在那里住了将近三年（徒19:10；20:31），福音从那里传遍亚细亚。保罗与以弗所长老告别时曾警告说：「就是你们中间，也必有人起来说悖谬的话，要引诱门徒跟从他们。」（徒20:30）提摩太前书显示，这话已经应验了。

**3. 作者问题。** 提摩太前书、提摩太后书和提多书常被合称为教牧书信。从爱任纽（约主后180年）起，教会普遍接受这三封信是保罗写的；只有马吉安的正典没有收入这三封信，据说他提安也不接受提摩太前后书。
- **怀疑的看法：** 十九世纪初，施莱尔马赫在1807年撰文质疑提摩太前书，后来艾希霍恩把质疑扩大到三封信。此后许多学者（如哈里森、马丁·狄比留斯）认为，教牧书信是保罗死后有人托名写的。他们的理由主要是：用词和文风与保罗其他书信不同（如「敬虔」、「纯正的道理」、「可信的话」这些词很常见）；教会的组织似乎比保罗在世时更完备；信中提到的保罗行踪，很难放进使徒行传的记载里。
- **折中的看法：** 也有福音派学者（如霍华德·马歇尔）认为，可能是保罗的门生在他死后不久，根据他的教导和材料写成的，而不是刻意冒名。
- **支持保罗为作者的看法：** 许多福音派学者（如乔治·奈特、威廉·孟恩斯、菲利普·汤纳），以及一些非福音派学者（如路加·提摩太·约翰逊）认为是保罗写的。他们回答说：主题不同，用词自然不同；保罗可能用了代笔的人（参罗16:22），文风也会受影响；长老（监督）和执事在保罗早期的书信和使徒行传中已经出现（徒14:23；20:17；腓1:1）；至于行踪：使徒行传写到保罗在罗马被软禁两年就结束了（徒28:30-31）。许多人认为保罗后来获释，再次出外旅行，这几封信就写在那段时间。
- **本研读的立场：** 照信本身所说的（1:1），把它当作保罗的书信来读。

**4. 写作时间。** 如果保罗在罗马第一次被囚后获释，提摩太前书大约写于主后62-64年，在他再次被捕、殉道（提后4:6）以前。也有少数学者（如约翰·罗宾逊）认为可以放在使徒行传的时期之内，例如保罗离开以弗所往马其顿去的时候（徒20:1）。本研读采用前一种看法，这也是主张保罗为作者的学者中较普遍的看法。罗马的革利免在主后一世纪末说，保罗到了西方的尽头（《革利免一书》5.7）；许多人认为，这是保罗获释后再出外旅行的线索。

**5. 异教是什么。** 保罗没有系统地描述以弗所的错误教训，我们只能从信中的零星线索来推想：
- 有犹太的色彩：「无穷的家谱」、「想要作教法师」（1:4、7）。
- 有禁欲的色彩：「禁止嫁娶，又禁戒食物」（4:3）。
- 自以为有特别的知识：保罗称之为「似是而非的学问」（6:20），原文就是「知识」这个词。
- 和争论、钱财有关：「专好问难，争辩言词」，「以敬虔为得利的门路」（6:4-5）。

许多学者认为，这种教训混合了犹太传统和禁欲主义，也带有一些后来发展成诺斯底主义的早期倾向；提摩太后书还提到许米乃等人说「复活的事已过」（提后2:17-18）。保罗的回应不是只辩倒对方，而是把人带回福音：命令的总归就是爱（1:5），律法要用得合宜（1:8），神所造的物都是好的（4:4），敬虔加上知足的心便是大利（6:6）。

## 钥匙经文：1:15；3:15-16

**1. 「基督耶稣降世，为要拯救罪人」。** 这是教牧书信五句「可信的话」中的第一句（1:15；3:1；4:9；提后2:11；多3:8）。保罗在整封信的开头，先说出自己是怎样一个人：「在罪人中我是个罪魁。」提摩太要整顿教会，但教会不是一群好人的俱乐部，而是蒙了怜悯的罪人组成的家。

**2. 「在神的家中当怎样行」。** 保罗用「家」来形容教会。所以他教导提摩太，对老年人要「劝他如同父亲；劝少年人如同弟兄；劝老年妇女如同母亲；劝少年妇女如同姊妹」（5:1-2）。教会的秩序不是公司的规章，也不是论资排辈的宗族，而是一个家的秩序；家里有父亲，就是永生的神。

**3. 「真理的柱石和根基」。** 教会不是真理的来源，而是支撑、高举真理的柱子和根基。有学者认为，这可能会叫以弗所人想起亚底米神庙那许多高大的柱子。神的教会也是一座殿，但它所高举的，是关于基督的真理。

**4. 「大哉，敬虔的奥秘」。** 3:16很可能是初期教会的一首诗歌，或一段认信的话。敬虔的奥秘不是一套方法，而是一个人：「神在肉身显现……被接在荣耀里。」和合本这里作「神」，跟随的是较晚的抄本；较早的抄本作那一位，英文标准译本就是这样译的。无论哪一种读法，所说的都是道成肉身的基督（详见第三章）。教会要怎样行，最终在于教会认识谁。

## 全书结构

提摩太前书是一封书信，大致可以这样分：
- **问安**（1:1-2）。
- **抵挡异教，持守福音**（1:3-20）：命令的总归就是爱；律法要用得合宜；保罗自己是蒙怜悯的罪魁；要打那美好的仗。
- **敬拜和祷告**（2:1-15）：为万人祷告，因为神愿意万人得救，只有一位中保；男人、女人在聚会中当怎样行。
- **监督和执事**（3:1-13）。
- **神的家与敬虔的奥秘**（3:14-16）。
- **作基督耶稣的好执事**（4:1-16）：后来必有人离弃真道；神所造的物都是好的；操练敬虔；不可叫人小看你年轻。
- **怎样对待教会中的各种人**（5:1-6:2）：老年人和少年人、寡妇、长老、作仆人的。
- **敬虔与钱财，最后的嘱咐**（6:3-21）：敬虔加上知足的心便是大利；贪财是万恶之根；要打那美好的仗；嘱咐富足的人；要保守所托付的。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 先看福音，再看规矩。** 这封信有许多关于教会秩序的教导，但保罗每讲一段规矩，就会回到福音（1:15-16；2:5-6；3:16；6:13-16）。读的时候，要问：这条教导和基督所做的有什么关系？

**2. 留意「敬虔」。** 「敬虔」在这封信中出现了很多次（2:2；3:16；4:7-8；6:3、5-6、11）。保罗所说的敬虔，不只是宗教的外表，而是在生活中敬畏神、顺服神。敬虔的根，是「敬虔的奥秘」，就是基督自己。这不是靠自己修身养性修出来的，而是从认识基督生出来的。

**3. 读难解的经文时要谦卑。** 这封信有几处经文，历代忠心的信徒有不同的理解：女人在聚会中的角色（2:11-15）、「只作一个妇人的丈夫」（3:2）、3:11说的是执事的妻子还是女执事、寡妇的名册（5:9）。我们要认真查考经文，把不同的看法公平地摆出来，也要在意见不同时彼此尊重。

**4. 留意保罗对一个人的爱。** 这是一位年长的使徒写给「因信主作我真儿子」（1:2）的信。保罗叮嘱他，也关心他的身体（5:23），为他打气（4:12），把重担交给他，又把他交托给神。读这封信，也可以留意神怎样用一个人去坚固另一个人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我往马其顿去的时候，曾劝你仍住在以弗所。」（1:3）保罗离开了，提摩太却要留下来。留在一个有人反对、有人背后说话、问题又多的地方，不是一件容易的事。我也可能正在一个想离开的地方：带我信主的牧者调走了，同工一个个离开，只剩下我；或者是一个艰难的教会，一个总是有难处的家。我要先分辨：如果那里有人在伤害我，有人用权柄压制、欺负人，主没有要我留在危险里，我可以寻求帮助、向警方或相关机构举报（罗13:4），也可以离开。可是如果只是辛苦，只是看不到成果，只是没有人感谢，我就要在主面前安静下来，问他是否要我留下来。留下或离开，本身都不是属灵高低的记号；保罗自己也离开了以弗所，往马其顿去作主的工。如果我已经离开了，也不必一直背着这件事；若有亏欠，可以带到主面前，他是赦免人的主；他在我现在所在的地方与我同在。我想起耶稣。他「定意向耶路撒冷去」（路9:51），因为他知道那是父差他去的地方。后来他被钉在十字架上，人在底下讥诮他说：「你如果是神的儿子，就从十字架上下来吧！」（太27:40）他本来可以下来，却没有下来。他留在那里，是为了担当我的罪。我留下来，不是要靠自己咬紧牙关撑下去；乃是因为那位为我留在十字架上的主，也与我同在这里。今天我可以对他说：主啊，我在这里，求你在这里坚固我。

## What This Book Is About

1 Timothy is a letter Paul wrote to a coworker. Paul had gone on to Macedonia and left Timothy in Ephesus "that you may charge certain persons not to teach any different doctrine" (1:3). The church in Ephesus already had elders (see Acts 20:17), but trouble had come: some devoted themselves "to myths and endless genealogies" (1:4); some desired "to be teachers of the law, without understanding either what they are saying" (1:7); some forbade marriage and required abstinence from foods (4:3); some imagined "that godliness is a means of gain" (6:5). In this setting Timothy was to hold fast to the truth and set God's household in order.

Paul himself states why he is writing: "if I delay, you may know how one ought to behave in the household of God, which is the church of the living God, a pillar and buttress of truth" (3:15).

So the letter deals with many practical questions of church life:
- **Teaching:** resisting false teaching and holding to "sound words" (6:3) and resisting "whatever else is contrary to sound doctrine" (1:10).
- **Prayer and worship:** praying for all people, for kings and all in high positions (2:1–2); how men and women are to conduct themselves in the gathering (2:8–15).
- **Leaders:** the character of overseers and deacons (3:1–13); how to treat elders, including accusations against them (5:17–25).
- **Different people in the church:** older and younger people (5:1–2), widows (5:3–16), slaves (6:1–2), the rich (6:17–19).
- **Timothy himself:** train yourself for godliness (4:7); let no one despise you for your youth (4:12); keep a close watch on yourself and on the teaching (4:16); flee the love of money (6:10–11); fight the good fight of the faith (6:12).

But the letter is more than a church manual. Before giving any instructions, Paul speaks of the gospel: "The saying is trustworthy and deserving of full acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the foremost" (1:15). After describing overseers and deacons, he breaks into a hymn in praise of Christ: "Great indeed, we confess, is the mystery of godliness..." (3:16). The order of the church comes from the gospel; God's household is built on Christ.

## Author and Setting

**1. Who Timothy was.** Timothy was probably from Lystra; his mother was "a Jewish woman who was a believer, but his father was a Greek" (Acts 16:1). From childhood he had known the Scriptures through his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice (2 Tim 1:5; 3:15). Paul took him along on his second missionary journey (Acts 16:3), and from then on he was one of Paul's closest coworkers. Paul calls him "my beloved and faithful child in the Lord" (1 Cor 4:17) and says, "I have no one like him" (Phil 2:20). Paul often sent him to deal with hard church situations (1 Thess 3:2; 1 Cor 4:17; Phil 2:19).
- **His temperament:** from Paul's words, Timothy may have been relatively young (4:12) and not in good health (5:23), and at times he seems to have been timid (see 1 Cor 16:10–11; 2 Tim 1:7). Paul left someone like this in a hard place.

**2. Ephesus.** Ephesus was one of the most important cities in the Roman province of Asia, home to the famous temple of Artemis (see Acts 19:27, 35). Paul stayed there nearly three years (Acts 19:10; 20:31), and from there the gospel spread through Asia. When Paul said farewell to the Ephesian elders, he warned them: "from among your own selves will arise men speaking twisted things, to draw away the disciples after them" (Acts 20:30). 1 Timothy shows that this had come true.

**3. Authorship.** 1 Timothy, 2 Timothy and Titus are often called the "Pastoral Letters." From Irenaeus (c. AD 180) on, the church broadly received all three as Paul's; only Marcion's canon left them out, and Tatian is reported to have rejected 1 and 2 Timothy.
- **The doubting view:** since the early nineteenth century (Schleiermacher's 1807 study questioned 1 Timothy, and Eichhorn soon extended the doubt to all three), many scholars (such as P. N. Harrison and Martin Dibelius) have held that the Pastoral Letters were written in Paul's name after his death. Their main reasons: the vocabulary and style differ from Paul's other letters (words such as "godliness," "sound doctrine" and "trustworthy saying" are common here); church organization seems more developed than in Paul's day; and the movements of Paul mentioned in the letters are hard to fit into the account in Acts.
- **A middle view:** some evangelical scholars (such as I. Howard Marshall) suggest a disciple of Paul wrote them soon after his death, from his teaching and materials, without intent to deceive.
- **The view that Paul wrote it:** many evangelical scholars (such as George Knight, William Mounce, Philip Towner), and some scholars outside evangelicalism (such as Luke Timothy Johnson), hold that Paul wrote it. They answer that different subjects naturally call for different vocabulary; Paul may have used a secretary (see Rom 16:22), which would affect the style; elders/overseers and deacons already appear in Paul's earlier letters and in Acts (Acts 14:23; 20:17; Phil 1:1); and as for Paul's movements, Acts ends with Paul under house arrest in Rome for two years (Acts 28:30–31). Many believe Paul was later released and traveled again, and that these letters were written in that period.
- **This study's position:** following the letter's own claim (1:1), we read it as Paul's letter.

**4. Date.** If Paul was released after his first Roman imprisonment, 1 Timothy was written around AD 62–64, before he was arrested again and martyred (2 Tim 4:6). A few scholars (such as John A. T. Robinson) place it within the period covered by Acts, for example when Paul left Ephesus for Macedonia (Acts 20:1). This study follows the first view, the usual one among those who hold Pauline authorship (late in the first century Clement of Rome says Paul reached "the limits of the west," which many take as a hint of travels after his release; 1 Clement 5.7).

**5. What the false teaching was.** Paul does not describe the Ephesian error systematically; we can only infer it from scattered clues in the letter:
- It had a Jewish coloring: "endless genealogies," "desiring to be teachers of the law" (1:4, 7).
- It had an ascetic coloring: those who "forbid marriage and require abstinence from foods" (4:3).
- It claimed special knowledge: Paul calls it "what is falsely called 'knowledge'" (6:20).
- It was tied to quarrels and money: "an unhealthy craving for controversy and for quarrels about words," "imagining that godliness is a means of gain" (6:4–5).

Many scholars think this was a mixture of Jewish tradition, asceticism and some early tendencies of what later became Gnosticism; 2 Timothy also mentions Hymenaeus and others "saying that the resurrection has already happened" (2 Tim 2:17–18). Paul's response is not only to refute them but to bring people back to the gospel: the aim of our charge is love (1:5); the law is good if used lawfully (1:8); everything created by God is good (4:4); godliness with contentment is great gain (6:6).

## Key Verses: 1:15; 3:15–16

**1. "Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners."** This is the first of the five "trustworthy sayings" in the Pastoral Letters (1:15; 3:1; 4:9; 2 Tim 2:11; Titus 3:8). At the very start of the letter Paul says what kind of man he is: "of whom I am the foremost." Timothy is to set the church in order, but the church is not a club of good people; it is the household of sinners who have received mercy.

**2. "How one ought to behave in the household of God."** Paul calls the church a household. So he tells Timothy to "encourage him as you would a father. Treat younger men like brothers, older women like mothers, younger women like sisters" (5:1–2). The order of the church is not a company's regulations, nor a clan ranked by seniority, but the order of a family, and the Father of this family is the living God.

**3. "A pillar and buttress of truth."** The church is not the source of truth; it is the pillar and foundation that holds the truth up. Some scholars suggest this may have reminded the Ephesians of the many tall columns of the temple of Artemis. God's church is a temple too, but what it holds up is the truth about Christ.

**4. "Great indeed... is the mystery of godliness."** Verse 3:16 is very likely an early Christian hymn or confession. The mystery of godliness is not a method but a person: "He was manifested in the flesh... taken up in glory." The CUV reads "God" here, following later manuscripts; the earliest manuscripts read "who" (ESV "He"). Either way the subject is the incarnate Christ (see chapter 3). How the church is to behave depends, in the end, on whom the church knows.

## Structure of the Book

1 Timothy is a letter and can be outlined roughly as follows:
- **Greeting** (1:1–2).
- **Resisting false teaching, holding to the gospel** (1:3–20): the aim of our charge is love; the law is good if used lawfully; Paul himself is the foremost of sinners who received mercy; wage the good warfare.
- **Worship and prayer** (2:1–15): pray for all people, because God desires all people to be saved and there is one mediator; how men and women are to conduct themselves in the gathering.
- **Overseers and deacons** (3:1–13).
- **The household of God and the mystery of godliness** (3:14–16).

## How to Read This Book

**1. Look first at the gospel, then at the instructions.** The letter has much teaching on church order, but each time Paul gives instructions he returns to the gospel (1:15–16; 2:5–6; 3:16; 6:13–16). As you read, ask: how does this instruction relate to what Christ has done?

**2. Notice "godliness."** The word "godliness" appears many times in this letter (2:2; 3:16; 4:7–8; 6:3, 5–6, 11). As Paul uses it, it is not merely a religious exterior but reverence for God and obedience to him in daily life, rooted in "the mystery of godliness," Christ himself. It is not self-cultivation but the fruit of knowing Christ.

**3. Read the hard passages with humility.** In several passages faithful believers through the centuries have understood the text differently: women's role in the gathering (2:11–15); "the husband of one wife" (3:2); whether 3:11 speaks of deacons' wives or women deacons; the list of widows (5:9). We should study the text carefully, set out the different views fairly, and respect one another where we differ.

**4. Notice Paul's love for one man.** This is a letter from an aging apostle to "my true child in the faith" (1:2). Paul charges him, cares about his health (5:23), encourages him (4:12), lays a burden on him and commits him to God. As you read, notice how God uses one person to strengthen another.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "As I urged you when I was going to Macedonia, remain at Ephesus" (1:3). Paul left; Timothy had to stay. Staying in a place where people oppose you, talk behind your back and the problems never end is not easy. I may be in a place I want to leave: the pastor who led me to faith has moved on, coworkers have left one by one, and I am the one still here; or a difficult church, a home where there is always trouble. First I need to discern: if someone there is harming me, if someone is using authority to crush or bully others, the Lord has not asked me to stay in danger; I can seek help, report it to the police or the proper authorities (Rom 13:4), and I can leave. But if it is only hard, only fruitless to my eyes, only unthanked, then I need to grow quiet before the Lord and ask whether he wants me to stay. Staying or leaving is not in itself a measure of spirituality; Paul himself left Ephesus for the Lord's work in Macedonia. If I have already left, I need not keep carrying it; if I failed in leaving, I can bring that to the Lord, who forgives; he is with me where I am now. I think of Jesus. He "set his face to go to Jerusalem" (Luke 9:51), because he knew that was where the Father had sent him. Later, when he hung on the cross, people below mocked him: "If you are the Son of God, come down from the cross" (Matt 27:40). He could have come down, and he did not. He stayed there to bear my sin. If I stay, it is not by gritting my teeth and holding on in my own strength; it is because the Lord who stayed on the cross for me is here with me too. Today I can say to him: Lord, here I am; strengthen me here.
