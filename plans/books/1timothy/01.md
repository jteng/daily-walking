## 本章脉络

第一章分成五段。**问安**（1-2节）：保罗称提摩太为「因信主作我真儿子」，愿恩惠、怜悯、平安归与他。**命令的总归就是爱**（3-7节）：提摩太要嘱咐那几个人不可传异教，不可听从荒渺的话语和无穷的家谱；这些人偏离了爱，就是从清洁的心、无亏的良心、无伪的信心生出来的爱。**律法要用得合宜**（8-11节）：律法是好的，是为不法和不服的人设立的，与那荣耀的福音相合。**我是罪人中的罪魁**（12-17节）：保罗从前是亵渎神、逼迫人的，却蒙了怜悯；基督耶稣降世，为要拯救罪人；基督在他身上显明一切的忍耐，给后来信他的人作榜样。**打那美好的仗**（18-20节）：提摩太要常存信心和无亏的良心；有人丢弃良心，在真道上如同船破坏了一般。

这一章的中心是第15节。保罗要提摩太去纠正别人的错误，却也要他记住：我自己是罪魁，我是蒙了怜悯的人。这是一切服事的起点。

## 深读 1:3-7 命令的总归就是爱

**1. 「荒渺无凭的话语和无穷的家谱」。** 「我往马其顿去的时候，曾劝你仍住在以弗所，好嘱咐那几个人不可传异教，也不可听从荒渺无凭的话语和无穷的家谱」（1:3-4）。
- **「异教」：** 原文的意思是传别的道理。这里不是指教会外面的其他宗教，而是在教会里面传与使徒教训不同的道理（参6:3；加1:6-7）。
- **「荒渺无凭的话语和无穷的家谱」：** 保罗没有细说内容。许多解经家认为，这可能是根据旧约人物的家谱，编出许多传说和猜测，这在当时一些犹太著作中很常见（参多1:14「犹太人荒谬的言语」）。
- **「只生辩论」：** 这些事叫人争辩不休，「并不发明神在信上所立的章程」。这里的「发明」是阐明、彰显的意思。「章程」这个词原文与管家有关，指管家治理一个家的安排；在这里是指神安排救恩、治理他家的计划。这计划是在信心里领受的，不是靠猜测找出来的。

**2. 「命令的总归就是爱」。** 「但命令的总归就是爱；这爱是从清洁的心和无亏的良心，无伪的信心生出来的。」（1:5）
- **「命令」：** 这里指保罗交给提摩太的嘱咐（1:3、18），不是指摩西的律法。提摩太去纠正错误的目的，不是要赢得辩论，而是要叫人生出爱来。
- **爱的三个源头：** 清洁的心（内心不被污秽搅乱，参太5:8）、无亏的良心（在神面前没有隐藏的罪）、无伪的信心（不是表演给人看的信心）。这三样是内里的，爱是从里面流出来的。
- **对照：** 那些传异教的人追求知识和辩论，结果是争竞；福音的教导追求的是爱。

**3. 「想要作教法师」。** 「有人偏离这些，反去讲虚浮的话，想要作教法师，却不明白自己所讲说的所论定的。」（1:6-7）这些人很有把握，「所论定的」原文是很肯定地断言；可是保罗说，他们其实不明白。在教会里，讲得最肯定的人，不一定是最明白的人。

> 要点：教导真理的目的不是赢得辩论，而是生出爱来；这爱从一颗被福音洁净的心、一个在神面前无亏的良心、一份不虚假的信心中流出来。

## 深读 1:8-11 律法原是好的

**1. 「律法原是好的，只要人用得合宜」。** 「我们知道律法原是好的，只要人用得合宜」（1:8；参罗7:12）。保罗不反对律法，他反对的是错用律法：在以弗所，是把律法当作猜测和争论的材料（1:4、7）；更普遍的危险，是把律法当作叫人自以为义的阶梯。

**2. 「律法不是为义人设立的」。** 「因为律法不是为义人设立的，乃是为不法和不服的，不虔诚和犯罪的……设立的」（1:9）。
- **这是什么意思？** 保罗不是说义人可以不顾律法，而是说律法的定罪功用是对着罪人的。律法像一面镜子，叫人看见自己的罪（参罗3:20）。
- **清单的次序：** 许多解经家注意到，从「弑父母」开始，这清单大致跟着十诫的后半部：孝敬父母（第五诫，参出21:15）、不可杀人（第六诫）、不可奸淫（第七诫）、不可偷盗（第八诫）、不可作假见证（第九诫，「说谎话的，并起假誓的」）。前面的「不虔诚」、「不圣洁」等，大致可以对应十诫前半部得罪神的罪。
- **「抢人口」：** 就是拐卖人口，对应第八诫。摩西律法把这罪定为死罪（出21:16；申24:7），一些犹太解经者（如斐洛）称之为最严重的偷窃。今天拐卖妇女儿童的事仍然存在，神看这是极重的罪。
- **「亲男色的」：** 保罗在这里和林前6:9用的是同一个词。这个词很可能是从七十士译本利未记18:22、20:13「与男人苟合」的用语组合而成的，在清单中也正放在第七诫的位置上。历代教会普遍认为，这是指男人之间的性行为；近代有学者（如博斯韦尔）提出不同的理解，也有学者（如赖特、盖格农）作了回应，但多数解经家（包括许多非福音派学者）仍然这样理解。保罗在这里说的是行为，不是一个人所经历的试探。我们也要留意：保罗把这罪和说谎、起假誓放在同一张清单里，清单上的每一种罪都需要福音。保罗在林前6:11也说：「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今……已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」福音呼召每一个罪人悔改，包括我自己；也应许每一个悔改信靠的人得着完全的洗净。在同性吸引中挣扎的人，教会不可推开，也不可轻看；我们每一个人都是靠同一位救主得救的。

**3. 「照着可称颂之神交托我荣耀福音」。** 律法所定罪的，是一切「敌正道的事」（1:10）；而这正道是「照着可称颂之神交托我荣耀福音说的」（1:11）。律法叫人看见罪，福音叫人看见救主。律法用得合宜，就会把人带到福音面前。

> 要点：律法是好的，能照出我们的罪，却不是给我们夸口的阶梯；律法用得合宜，就会把罪人带到那荣耀的福音面前。

## 深读 1:12-17 在罪人中我是个罪魁

**1. 「我感谢那给我力量的」。** 「我感谢那给我力量的我们主基督耶稣，因他以我有忠心，派我服事他。」（1:12）保罗服事主，不是因为他先有力量，而是基督「给我力量」。「以我有忠心」不是说保罗先证明了自己可靠，然后才被选上；下一节立刻说明，他被派的时候是怎样的一个人。

**2. 「我从前是亵渎神的，逼迫人的，侮慢人的」。** 「我从前是亵渎神的，逼迫人的，侮慢人的；然而我还蒙了怜悯」（1:13）。
- **三个词：** 「亵渎神的」是用言语攻击神；「逼迫人的」是用行动迫害教会（参徒26:9-11）；「侮慢人的」原文指傲慢、施暴的人。保罗没有把自己的过去说得轻描淡写。
- **「因我是不信不明白的时候而做的」：** 这不是说保罗因为不明白就不算有罪，也不是说他配得怜悯。旧约区分误犯的罪和擅敢行的罪（民15:27-31）；彼得也对耶路撒冷的人说：「你们做这事是出于不知」（徒3:17；参路23:34，有些古抄本没有这句）。许多解经家认为，保罗的意思是：他的罪虽然可怕，却不是民数记所说「擅敢行」、明知故犯地抵挡真理的罪（民15:30；对照本章20节）。他蒙怜悯的真正原因在第16节：基督要显明他的忍耐。所以保罗仍然说「我还蒙了怜悯」，因为怜悯从来都是不配得的。

**3. 「我主的恩是格外丰盛」。** 「并且我主的恩是格外丰盛，使我在基督耶稣里有信心和爱心。」（1:14）「格外丰盛」原文是满溢出来的意思（参罗5:20「恩典就更显多了」）。保罗从前满了亵渎和暴力，现在恩典满溢，带来信心和爱心，正是第5节所说的：从无伪的信心生出来的爱。

**4. 「基督耶稣降世，为要拯救罪人」。** 「『基督耶稣降世，为要拯救罪人。』这话是可信的，是十分可佩服的。在罪人中我是个罪魁。」（1:15）
- **「这话是可信的」：** 这是教牧书信中五句「可信的话」的第一句（参3:1；4:9；提后2:11；多3:8），很可能是当时教会中流传的一句话，保罗加以认可。和合本「十分可佩服的」是旧时的说法，意思是值得人完全接受、诚心信服。
- **「降世」：** 这句话包含了基督先存的意思：他本来在父那里，然后来到世上（参约1:9；3:17）。
- **「为要拯救罪人」：** 许多华人认为宗教都是劝人为善；但基督来，不是为了帮助好人变得更好，而是为了拯救罪人（参路5:32；19:10）。他拯救罪人，是藉着舍自己，代替罪人受死：「他舍自己作万人的赎价」（2:6）。罪人得救，不是靠把自己改好，乃是靠信他得永生（1:16）。
- **「我是个罪魁」：** 注意保罗用的是现在式：「我是」，不是「我从前是」。他得救多年以后，仍然这样看自己。这不是假谦虚，也不是华人习惯的客套自谦。一个人越认识神，就越看见自己的罪，也越看见恩典之大；保罗说的，正是这样的人的话（参林前15:9-10）。

**5. 「显明他一切的忍耐」。** 「然而，我蒙了怜悯，是因耶稣基督要在我这罪魁身上显明他一切的忍耐，给后来信他得永生的人作榜样。」（1:16）
- **「一切的忍耐」：** 基督对保罗的忍耐是完全的。在大马士革的路上，主没有对扫罗说要惩罚他，而是说：「扫罗！扫罗！你为什么逼迫我？」（徒9:4）
- **「作榜样」：** 原文可以指样本、草图，就是给后来的人看的样式。神救了保罗，好像是要告诉后来每一个人：如果连他都能得救，你也能，只要你信靠他。没有人罪大到基督的忍耐够不着。

**6. 「但愿尊贵、荣耀归与……独一的神」。** 「但愿尊贵、荣耀归与那不能朽坏、不能看见、永世的君王、独一的神，直到永永远远。阿们！」（1:17）保罗讲到自己的得救，就忍不住赞美。这颂赞的话出自旧约：「惟耶和华是真神，是活神，是永远的王」（耶10:10；参申6:4）。在拜亚底米、也尊奉罗马皇帝的以弗所，保罗宣告只有一位「永世的君王、独一的神」。真正认识恩典的人，最后不是夸自己，而是敬拜神。

> 要点：基督耶稣降世，为要拯救罪人，这是可信的话。没有人好到不需要他，也没有人坏到他救不了；他在罪魁身上显明的忍耐，今天仍在等候每一个人回转归向他。

## 深读 1:18-20 打那美好的仗

**1. 「照从前指着你的预言」。** 「我儿提摩太啊，我照从前指着你的预言，将这命令交托你，叫你因此可以打那美好的仗」（1:18）。提摩太被按立的时候，曾有指着他的预言（参4:14）。保罗提醒他：你的服事不是你自己选的，是神呼召的。在困难的时候，这呼召可以坚固他。

**2. 「常存信心和无亏的良心」。** 「常存信心和无亏的良心。有人丢弃良心，就在真道上如同船破坏了一般。」（1:19）
- **良心和真道连在一起：** 一个人若长期违背良心，在生活中容让罪，最后往往会改变自己的信仰来配合自己的生活。错误的道理常常不是从头脑开始的，而是从被丢弃的良心开始的。这不是说每一个有疑问的人都在隐藏罪；诚实的疑问可以带到神面前。
- **「如同船破坏了一般」：** 以弗所是港口城市，读信的人都知道船破是什么样子。船破常常是先偏离了航道（参1:6「偏离」）。

**3. 「把他们交给撒但」。** 「其中有许米乃和亚历山大；我已经把他们交给撒但，使他们受责罚就不再谤渎了。」（1:20）
- **这是什么意思？** 多数解经家认为，这是指把他们赶出教会的团契（参林前5:5、13），使他们回到撒但掌权的世界里。
- **目的是挽回：** 「使他们受责罚」原文是受管教、受训练的意思。教会施行纪律，目的不是报复，而是叫人悔改。华人教会有时为了顾全情面不肯管教，有时又把管教变成整人；这两样都不合圣经。
- **这两个人：** 许米乃后来又出现在提后2:17-18，说「复活的事已过」；亚历山大可能就是提后4:14的「铜匠亚历山大」，但不能确定。

> 要点：服事是一场美好的仗；打这仗，是出于神的呼召（1:18），力量是基督所赐的（1:12），我们靠着他常存信心和无亏的良心。良心被丢弃，信仰的船就会偏离航道；但教会的管教仍以挽回为目的。

## 默想

**默想：** 「然而，我蒙了怜悯，是因耶稣基督要在我这罪魁身上显明他一切的忍耐，给后来信他得永生的人作榜样。」（1:16）如果有人请我讲讲自己信主的见证，我会怎样讲？我可能会讲我从前怎样，后来怎样改变，现在生活怎样好起来。这些都可能是真的。可是保罗讲自己的见证，重点不在他变得多好，而在基督多么忍耐。也许我觉得自己的见证太平淡：从小在教会长大，没有做过什么大坏事。可是保罗说的「罪魁」，不是要我比较谁的罪更大，而是要我看见：我和保罗一样，都需要基督的怜悯，也都是靠他的忍耐活到今天的。也许正相反，我觉得自己的过去太黑暗，不敢说出来，怕别人知道了会看不起我。可是保罗把自己最不堪的过去写在信里，因为那正是基督忍耐的证据。我不必把每一件事都告诉每一个人，有些事只适合对一两位可信的人说；如果那黑暗是别人加在我身上的，那不是我的罪。可是在主面前，我什么都不必再藏，因为他早已知道，也没有放弃我。今天我可以这样回想：主在哪些时候忍耐了我？在我还不信的时候，在我信了却又跌倒的时候，在我一次又一次犯同样的罪的时候，他都没有放弃我。我的见证，最后要讲的不是我，而是他。然后，我也可以像保罗一样，用这句话结束：「但愿尊贵、荣耀归与那不能朽坏、不能看见、永世的君王、独一的神，直到永永远远。阿们！」（1:17）

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 falls into five parts. **Greeting** (vv. 1–2): Paul calls Timothy "my true child in the faith" and wishes him grace, mercy and peace. **The aim of our charge is love** (vv. 3–7): Timothy is to charge certain persons not to teach a different doctrine or devote themselves to myths and endless genealogies; these people have swerved from the love that comes from a pure heart, a good conscience and a sincere faith. **Using the law lawfully** (vv. 8–11): the law is good; it is laid down for the lawless and disobedient, in keeping with the glorious gospel. **I am the foremost of sinners** (vv. 12–17): Paul was once a blasphemer and persecutor, yet he received mercy; Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; in Paul Christ displayed his perfect patience as an example to those who would believe. **Wage the good warfare** (vv. 18–20): Timothy is to hold faith and a good conscience; some have rejected conscience and made shipwreck of their faith.

The center of the chapter is verse 15. Paul is sending Timothy to correct others' errors, but he also reminds him: I myself am the foremost of sinners; I am a man who has received mercy. That is where all ministry begins.

## Going Deeper: 1:3–7 The Aim of Our Charge Is Love

**1. "Myths and endless genealogies."** "As I urged you when I was going to Macedonia, remain at Ephesus that you may charge certain persons not to teach any different doctrine, nor to devote themselves to myths and endless genealogies" (1:3–4).
- **"A different doctrine":** the Greek means "to teach otherwise." It does not refer to other religions outside, but to teaching inside the church that differs from the apostles' teaching (see 6:3; Gal 1:6–7).
- **"Myths and endless genealogies":** Paul does not describe their content. Many commentators think these may have been legends and speculations built on the genealogies of Old Testament figures, something common in some Jewish writings of the time (see Titus 1:14, "Jewish myths").
- **"Which promote speculations":** these things lead to endless argument, "rather than the stewardship from God that is by faith." "Stewardship" is the word for a household manager's office: God's plan for arranging salvation and ordering his household. That plan is received by faith, not discovered by speculation.

**2. "The aim of our charge is love."** "The aim of our charge is love that issues from a pure heart and a good conscience and a sincere faith" (1:5).
- **"Our charge":** this refers to the instruction Paul gave Timothy (1:3, 18), not to the law of Moses. The purpose of Timothy's correcting error is not to win an argument but to produce love.
- **Three sources of love:** a pure heart (an inner life not muddied by defilement, see Matt 5:8), a good conscience (no hidden sin before God), and a sincere faith (not a faith performed for others). All three are inward; love flows out from within.
- **The contrast:** the false teachers pursue knowledge and debate, and the result is quarreling; the gospel's teaching aims at love.

**3. "Desiring to be teachers of the law."** "Certain persons, by swerving from these, have wandered away into vain discussion, desiring to be teachers of the law, without understanding either what they are saying or the things about which they make confident assertions" (1:6–7). These people are very sure of themselves; "make confident assertions" means to state something emphatically. Yet Paul says they do not actually understand. In the church, the one who speaks most confidently is not necessarily the one who understands most.

> The point: The aim of teaching the truth is not to win arguments but to produce love, love that flows from a heart cleansed by the gospel, a conscience clear before God and a faith that is not pretended.

## Going Deeper: 1:8–11 The Law Is Good

**1. "The law is good, if one uses it lawfully."** "Now we know that the law is good, if one uses it lawfully" (1:8; see Rom 7:12). Paul is not against the law; he is against its misuse: in Ephesus, as material for speculation and argument (1:4, 7); and, more generally, as a ladder for self-righteousness.

**2. "The law is not laid down for the just."** "Understanding this, that the law is not laid down for the just but for the lawless and disobedient, for the ungodly and sinners..." (1:9).
- **What does this mean?** Paul is not saying the righteous may ignore the law, but that the law's condemning work is aimed at sinners. The law is like a mirror that shows people their sin (see Rom 3:20).
- **The order of the list:** many commentators notice that from "those who strike their fathers and mothers" onward, the list roughly follows the second half of the Ten Commandments: honor your father and mother (fifth; see Exod 21:15), you shall not murder (sixth), you shall not commit adultery (seventh), you shall not steal (eighth; "enslavers" are kidnappers and traffickers, a capital crime in the law of Moses [Exod 21:16; Deut 24:7], which some Jewish interpreters [such as Philo] called the worst form of theft), you shall not bear false witness (ninth; "liars, perjurers"). The earlier words, "ungodly," "unholy" and so on, may correspond broadly to sins against God in the first half. Trafficking of women and children still happens today; God counts it a grave sin.
- **"Men who practice homosexuality":** Paul uses the same word here as in 1 Cor 6:9. The word appears to be formed from the Septuagint wording of Lev 18:22; 20:13 ("lie with a male"), and in the list it stands in the place of the seventh commandment. The church through the centuries has generally understood it to refer to sexual relations between men; some modern scholars (such as John Boswell) have proposed other readings, others (such as David F. Wright and Robert Gagnon) have replied, but most commentators (including many who are not evangelical) still hold that this is its meaning. Notice that Paul puts this sin in the same list as lying and perjury; every sin on the list needs the gospel. In 1 Cor 6:11 Paul also says, "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified." Paul names a practice, not the experience of temptation; the gospel calls every sinner, me included, to repentance, and promises complete washing to everyone who repents and trusts in Christ. The church must not push away or look down on those who struggle with same-sex attraction; every one of us is saved by the same Savior.

**3. "In accordance with the glorious gospel of the blessed God with which I have been entrusted."** The law is laid down against "whatever else is contrary to sound doctrine" (1:10), and that sound doctrine is "in accordance with the glorious gospel of the blessed God with which I have been entrusted" (1:11). The law shows people their sin; the gospel shows them their Savior. Used lawfully, the law brings people to the gospel.

> The point: The law is good. It exposes our sin; it is not a ladder for boasting. Used lawfully, it brings sinners to the glorious gospel.

## Going Deeper: 1:12–17 Of Whom I Am the Foremost

**1. "I thank him who has given me strength."** "I thank him who has given me strength, Christ Jesus our Lord, because he judged me faithful, appointing me to his service" (1:12). Paul serves the Lord not because he first had strength, but because Christ "has given me strength." "Judged me faithful" does not mean Paul first proved himself reliable and was then chosen; the next verse immediately says what kind of man he was when he was appointed.

**2. "Formerly I was a blasphemer, persecutor, and insolent opponent."** "Though formerly I was a blasphemer, persecutor, and insolent opponent. But I received mercy" (1:13).
- **Three words:** a "blasphemer" attacks God with words; a "persecutor" attacks the church with deeds (see Acts 26:9–11); an "insolent opponent" is an arrogant, violent man. Paul does not soften his past.
- **"Because I had acted ignorantly in unbelief":** this does not mean that Paul's ignorance made him guiltless, or that he deserved mercy. The Old Testament distinguishes unintentional sins from sins done "with a high hand" (Num 15:27–31); Peter too told the people of Jerusalem, "you acted in ignorance" (Acts 3:17; see Luke 23:34, a line some early manuscripts lack). Many take the clause to explain not why Paul deserved mercy but why his sin was not the defiant, "high-handed" sin of Num 15:30, the knowing rejection of the truth (contrast v. 20). The real reason for the mercy is given in verse 16: Christ meant to display his patience. He still says "I received mercy"; mercy is never deserved.

**3. "The grace of our Lord overflowed for me."** "And the grace of our Lord overflowed for me with the faith and love that are in Christ Jesus" (1:14). "Overflowed" means to abound beyond measure (see Rom 5:20, "grace abounded all the more"). Paul was once full of blasphemy and violence; now grace overflows, bringing faith and love, the very faith and love of verse 5.

**4. "Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners."** "The saying is trustworthy and deserving of full acceptance, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the foremost" (1:15).
- **"The saying is trustworthy":** this is the first of the five "trustworthy sayings" in the Pastoral Letters (see 3:1; 4:9; 2 Tim 2:11; Titus 3:8), probably a saying that circulated in the churches, which Paul endorses.
- **"Came into the world":** the phrase implies Christ's preexistence: he was with the Father and then came into the world (see John 1:9; 3:17).
- **"To save sinners":** Many assume all religions just teach people to be good; but Christ did not come to help good people become better but to save sinners (see Luke 5:32; 19:10). He saves sinners by giving himself in their place: he "gave himself as a ransom for all" (2:6). Sinners are saved not by improving themselves but by believing in him for eternal life (1:16).
- **"Of whom I am the foremost":** notice that Paul uses the present tense: "I am," not "I was." Many years after his conversion he still sees himself this way. This is not false modesty, nor polite self-deprecation; it is the voice of a man who, the more he knows God, sees more of his own sin and more of the greatness of grace (see 1 Cor 15:9–10).

**5. "To display his perfect patience."** "But I received mercy for this reason, that in me, as the foremost, Jesus Christ might display his perfect patience as an example to those who were to believe in him for eternal life" (1:16).
- **"Perfect patience":** Christ's patience toward Paul was complete. On the Damascus road the Lord did not say, "Saul, I will punish you," but, "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?" (Acts 9:4).
- **"As an example":** the word can mean a sample or an outline sketch, a pattern for those who come later. In saving Paul, God seems to say to everyone after him: if even he could be saved, so can you, if you trust in him. No one's sin is so great that Christ's patience cannot reach it.

**6. "To the King of ages... the only God, be honor and glory."** "To the King of ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, be honor and glory forever and ever. Amen" (1:17). When Paul speaks of his own salvation, he cannot help praising God. The doxology comes from the Old Testament: "the LORD is the true God; he is the living God and the everlasting King" (Jer 10:10; see Deut 6:4). In an Ephesus that worshiped Artemis and honored the emperors, Paul declares there is only one "King of ages... the only God." Those who truly know grace end not by boasting in themselves but by worshiping God.

> The point: Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; the saying is trustworthy. No one is good enough not to need him, and no one is too far gone for him to save. The patience he displayed in the foremost of sinners still waits today for everyone to turn to him.

## Going Deeper: 1:18–20 Wage the Good Warfare

**1. "In accordance with the prophecies previously made about you."** "This charge I entrust to you, Timothy, my child, in accordance with the prophecies previously made about you, that by them you may wage the good warfare" (1:18). When Timothy was set apart for ministry, there had been prophecies about him (see 4:14). Paul reminds him: your ministry is not something you chose for yourself; God called you to it. In hard times that call can steady him.

**2. "Holding faith and a good conscience."** "Holding faith and a good conscience. By rejecting this, some have made shipwreck of their faith" (1:19).
- **Conscience and faith go together:** if someone goes on violating conscience and tolerating sin in daily life, he will often end by changing his beliefs to fit his life. False teaching often begins not in the head but in a conscience that has been thrown overboard. This does not mean every honest question hides a sin; honest questions can be brought to God.
- **"Made shipwreck":** Ephesus was a port city; its readers knew what a shipwreck looked like. A wreck often begins with drifting off course (see 1:6, "swerving").

**3. "Whom I have handed over to Satan."** "Among whom are Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom I have handed over to Satan that they may learn not to blaspheme" (1:20).
- **What does this mean?** Most commentators take it to mean putting them out of the church's fellowship (see 1 Cor 5:5, 13), returning them to the world where Satan rules.
- **The aim is restoration:** "that they may learn" means to be disciplined or trained. Church discipline aims not at revenge but at repentance. Churches sometimes avoid discipline to save face, and sometimes turn discipline into a way of punishing a person; neither is biblical.
- **These two men:** Hymenaeus appears again in 2 Tim 2:17–18, "saying that the resurrection has already happened"; Alexander may be "Alexander the coppersmith" of 2 Tim 4:14, but this is not certain.

> The point: Ministry is a good warfare, fought under God's call (1:18) and in the strength Christ gives (1:12); leaning on him, we hold faith and a good conscience. When conscience is thrown away, the ship of faith drifts off course; yet the church's discipline still aims to restore.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But I received mercy for this reason, that in me, as the foremost, Jesus Christ might display his perfect patience as an example to those who were to believe in him for eternal life" (1:16). If someone asked me to share my testimony, how would I tell it? I might talk about what I used to be like, how I changed, and how much better my life is now. All of that may be true. But when Paul tells his testimony, the focus is not on how good he became but on how patient Christ was. Perhaps I feel my testimony is too ordinary: I grew up in church and never did anything very bad. But when Paul says "the foremost," he is not asking me to compare whose sins are bigger; he wants me to see that I, like Paul, need Christ's mercy, and that I too have lived to this day by his patience. Or perhaps it is the opposite: my past feels too dark to tell, and I am afraid that if people knew they would look down on me. Yet Paul wrote the worst of his past into his letter, because that was exactly the evidence of Christ's patience. I do not have to tell everyone everything; some things are for one or two trusted people. And if the darkness was done to me, it is not my sin. But before the Lord I need hide nothing, for he already knows, and he has not given up on me. Today I can look back and ask: when has the Lord been patient with me? When I did not yet believe, when I believed and then fell, when I committed the same sin again and again, he did not give up on me. In the end my testimony is not about me but about him. And then, like Paul, I can end with these words: "To the King of ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, be honor and glory forever and ever. Amen" (1:17).
