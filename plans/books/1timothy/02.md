## 本章脉络

第二章分成三段。**为万人祷告**（1-7节）：要为万人恳求、祷告、代求、祝谢，也为君王和一切在位的祷告；因为神愿意万人得救，只有一位神，在神和人中间只有一位中保，就是降世为人的基督耶稣，他舍自己作万人的赎价。**男人在祷告中当怎样**（8节）：男人要无忿怒，无争论，举起圣洁的手，随处祷告。**女人在聚会中当怎样**（9-15节）：女人要以善行为妆饰，不以炫耀的打扮为妆饰；要沉静学道；保罗不许女人讲道，也不许她辖管男人，并举出创造的次序和始祖堕落的经过为理由；女人若常存信心、爱心，又圣洁自守，就必在生产上得救。

这一章从祷告讲起，整章都围绕着敬拜。保罗关心的是：神的百姓聚集的时候，应当是一个怎样的群体？他的答案从福音开始：神愿意万人得救，所以教会要为万人祷告。

## 深读 2:1-4 为万人祷告

**1. 「第一要为万人……」。** 「我劝你，第一要为万人恳求、祷告、代求、祝谢」（2:1）。「第一」不只是次序上的第一，也是最重要的事。整顿教会，保罗从祷告开始。
- **四个词：** 「恳求」是为具体的需要向神祈求；「祷告」泛指向神说话；「代求」是为别人向神请求；「祝谢」是感谢。这四个词不必严格区分，合起来是说：要为万人献上各样的祷告。
- **「为万人」：** 那些传异教的人可能把得救的范围缩小到少数有特别知识的人。保罗说：要为所有的人祷告，没有一个人在我们的祷告之外。

**2. 「为君王和一切在位的」。** 「为君王和一切在位的，也该如此，使我们可以敬虔、端正、平安无事地度日。」（2:2）
- **当时的君王是谁？** 写这封信的时候，罗马皇帝很可能是尼禄。后来正是他在罗马逼迫基督徒。保罗没有说只为好的君王祷告，而是为「一切在位的」祷告。这延续了旧约的教导：被掳的人要为巴比伦城「求平安，为那城祷告耶和华」（耶29:7）。
- **为什么要为他们祷告？** 「使我们可以敬虔、端正、平安无事地度日」。保罗不是求教会得到特权，而是求一个可以安静地敬畏神、过正直生活的环境；在这样的环境里，福音也更容易传开（参2:4）。
- **为他们祷告，不等于赞同他们的一切作为：** 我们为在位的人祷告，因为他们也是需要救恩的人，也因为神掌管万有（参罗13:1）。为他们祷告，是把他们带到神面前，不是把他们当作神。初期教会为皇帝祷告，却不肯向皇帝的像献香。

**3. 「他愿意万人得救，明白真道」。** 「这是好的，在神我们救主面前可蒙悦纳。他愿意万人得救，明白真道。」（2:3-4）
- **神的心意：** 神不喜悦恶人死亡（结18:23、32），「不愿有一人沉沦，乃愿人人都悔改」（彼后3:9）。神对失丧的人有真实的怜悯，这是我们为万人祷告的根据。
- **「万人」是什么意思？** 这一节在历史上有不同的理解。改革宗传统（如加尔文）常认为，「万人」在这里指各种各样的人，包括第2节所说的君王和在位的，就是不分身份、种族。也有改革宗神学家（如约翰·派博）说，神有他启示出来的心意（他真实地愿意人得救），也有他隐秘的旨意（他按着主权拣选人），这两方面在神里面并不矛盾。阿民念和卫斯理传统（如约翰·卫斯理；解经家如霍华德·马歇尔）则认为，神真实地愿意每一个人得救，并藉着先行的恩典使人都能回应，但人仍可能拒绝。两种传统都同意：并不是每一个人最后都会得救（参太7:13-14），也都同意：教会要为所有人祷告、向所有人传福音。
- **「明白真道」：** 得救和明白真理是连在一起的。那些人传的是「似是而非的学问」（6:20）；神要人认识的真理，却是下面第5-6节所讲的福音。

> 要点：神愿意万人得救，所以教会要为万人祷告，包括那些我们不喜欢、甚至敌对我们的掌权者。没有一个人在神的怜悯之外，也不应该有一个人在我们的祷告之外。

## 深读 2:5-7 只有一位中保

**1. 「只有一位神」。** 「因为只有一位神」（2:5）。这是以色列人每天背诵的认信：「以色列啊，你要听！耶和华－我们神是独一的主。」（申6:4）因为只有一位神，他就是万人的神，不只是某一个民族、某一群人的神（参罗3:29-30）。这正是要为万人祷告的理由。

**2. 「在神和人中间，只有一位中保」。** 「在神和人中间，只有一位中保，乃是降世为人的基督耶稣」（2:5）。
- **「中保」：** 就是站在两方中间、使两方和好的人。约伯曾经叹息：「我们中间没有听讼的人可以向我们两造按手。」（伯9:33）现在，这样一位中保来了。
- **「只有一位」：** 没有别的中间人能把我们带到神面前：不是天使，不是圣徒，不是有特别属灵知识的人，也不是神职人员，也不是祖先，或庙里任何的菩萨神明（参约14:6；徒4:12）。宗教改革强调这一点：信徒可以藉着基督直接来到神面前（来4:16）。天主教传统也认信基督是唯一的中保，但认为马利亚和圣徒的代求，是在基督中保职分之下的一种参与（《天主教教理》第956、969-970条）。改教家特别反对向马利亚和已故圣徒祈求，认为这模糊了基督的独一性；请弟兄姊妹为我们代祷则是另一回事（2:1）。
- **「降世为人的」：** 原文直译是：人基督耶稣。中保必须是人，才能代表人；他也必须是神，才能把神带到人面前（参来2:17；论他的神性，参约1:1、14；来1:3）。保罗在这里特别强调他的人性：他是真正的人，懂得我们的软弱（来4:15），今天，这位成为人的基督仍在父面前为我们代求（罗8:34；来7:25）。

**3. 「他舍自己作万人的赎价」。** 「他舍自己作万人的赎价，到了时候，这事必证明出来。」（2:6）
- **「赎价」：** 这话很接近耶稣自己说的：「人子来……要舍命作多人的赎价。」（可10:45）赎价是赎回奴隶或俘虏所付的代价。保罗用的词，比耶稣所用的多了一个表示代替的前缀。许多人认为，这是强调替代：基督舍自己，代替我们，付了我们付不起的代价。
- **「舍自己」：** 不是神从基督身上夺去生命，而是基督自己甘心舍命（参约10:18；加2:20）。
- **「万人」：** 这里和第4节一样，在历史上有不同的理解。改革宗传统（如加尔文和《多特信经》）认为，基督的死足够为所有人赎罪，但它的目的和实际的果效是救赎所有属他的人，「万人」指各样的人，不分种族和身份（参启5:9）；也有一些改革宗神学家（如达文南特）认为，基督为每一个人死，条件是人要信，同时神又特别定意要拯救选民；阿民念和卫斯理传统（如约翰·卫斯理；解经家如霍华德·马歇尔）认为，基督为每一个人死，为所有人预备了救恩，要藉着信才能领受。这几种看法都相信：基督的死是真实的代赎，凡信的人都必得救，教会要向所有人传这个好消息。

**4. 「我为此奉派作传道的」。** 「我为此奉派作传道的，作使徒，作外邦人的师傅，教导他们相信，学习真道。」（2:7）保罗的整个人生，都是为了这一位中保和这一个赎价。他从前逼迫信徒，现在被派去把这个好消息传给外邦人。

> 要点：在神和人中间只有一位中保，就是降世为人的基督耶稣；他舍自己作了赎价。我们来到神面前，不需要别的关系，也不需要先献上什么，只需要他。

## 深读 2:8-10 举起圣洁的手，只要有善行

**1. 「我愿男人……随处祷告」。** 「我愿男人无忿怒，无争论，举起圣洁的手，随处祷告」（2:8）。
- **「举起圣洁的手」：** 举手祷告是旧约和初期教会常见的姿势（参诗141:2；28:2）。重点不在姿势，而在「圣洁的手」，也就是祷告的人生活要清洁。
- **「无忿怒，无争论」：** 以弗所的教会有争论（1:4；6:4-5），男人很可能也卷入其中。保罗是说：一边和弟兄争吵、怀恨，一边举手祷告，是自相矛盾的（参太5:23-24；彼前3:7）。这不是说心里有怒气的人就不能来到神面前；诗篇里满了把怒气带到神面前的祷告。我们可以带着怒气来，只是不能抱着它不放，拿它去攻击弟兄。
- **「随处」：** 可能指以弗所各处的家庭教会。无论在哪里聚会，都要这样祷告。

**2. 「又愿女人……以正派衣裳为妆饰」。** 「又愿女人廉耻、自守，以正派衣裳为妆饰，不以编发、黄金、珍珠，和贵价的衣裳为妆饰，只要有善行，这才与自称是敬神的女人相宜。」（2:9-10）
- **保罗反对的是什么？** 在当时，精致的编发配上黄金和珍珠，是富有妇女显示身份和财富的方式，有时也带有性的诱惑。保罗不是禁止女人编头发，而是反对在敬拜中炫耀自己、吸引人注意自己（参彼前3:3-4）。
- **「只要有善行」：** 保罗把重点从外面转到里面，从炫耀转到服事。这不只是对女人的要求：男人也不应该在教会里炫耀财富和地位（参雅2:1-4）。

> 要点：敬拜的时候，男人要放下怒气和争论，女人要放下炫耀。我们放得下，是因为来到神面前只靠那一位中保，不必再靠争赢或打扮来证明自己。

## 深读 2:11-15 沉静学道

这一段是新约中争议最多的经文之一。历代忠心的信徒对它有不同的理解，下面把主要的看法放在一起，请读者自己查考。

**1. 「女人要沉静学道」。** 「女人要沉静学道，一味地顺服。」（2:11）
- **「学道」：** 在保罗的时代，一些拉比的话反对教导女人律法（如《密西拿·疑妻篇》3.4），希腊罗马社会也很少让女人受教育。保罗却说，女人要学道。这在当时是一个积极的教导（参路10:39，马利亚坐在耶稣脚前听道）。
- **「沉静」：** 原文和第2节的「平安无事」是同一个词根，意思是安静、不吵闹，不一定是完全不说话。

**2. 「我不许女人讲道，也不许她辖管男人」。** 「我不许女人讲道，也不许她辖管男人，只要沉静。」（2:12）这是争议的中心。
- **「辖管」：** 这个词在新约中只出现在这里，在当时的希腊文中也不常见。有学者认为这是个中性的词，就是行使权柄；也有学者认为带有负面的意思，如专横、操控。有学者（如寇斯坦伯格）从「不许……也不许……」的句法认为，讲道和辖管应同属正面或同属负面；另一些学者（如佩恩、贝尔维尔）不同意。这个问题至今仍有讨论。
- **认为教会某些职分男女有别的看法（常称为互补派）：** 许多学者（如乔治·奈特、威廉·孟恩斯、托马斯·施莱纳、道格拉斯·穆尔，以及古德恩、派博）认为，保罗在这里不许女人在教会聚集中担任教导男人、带领全教会的职分，就是长老或监督的职分（参3:2「善于教导」）。他们认为第13节诉诸神创造的次序，所以这教导不只适用于以弗所，而是适用于各时代的教会。他们也强调：这不是说女人的价值比较低，男女都照神的形象被造（创1:27），在基督里都是一（加3:28）；女人在教会中有许多教导和服事的机会，例如教导其他妇女（多2:3-5）和孩子（参提后1:5；3:15）、在私下讲解神的道（徒18:26）、祷告和说预言（林前11:5，和合本译作「讲道」，原文是说预言）。
- **认为教会职分不分男女的看法（常称为平等派）：** 另一些学者（如戈登·费依、克雷格·基纳、菲利普·佩恩）认为，保罗是针对以弗所当时的问题：一些女人被异教迷惑（参5:13；提后3:6-7），又缺少受教育的机会，所以保罗暂时不许她们教导，先叫她们「学道」。他们认为第13-14节是举例说明受迷惑的后果，不是一条永久的原则；他们也指出，保罗在别处称赞女同工，例如女执事非比（罗16:1）、百基拉（罗16:3），以及在聚会中祷告、说预言的女人（林前11:5）。
- **双方都同意的：** 男女都是照神形象造的，都同样蒙恩；女人在神的国中有尊贵、不可缺少的服事；任何人都不可藉着这段经文轻看、压制或伤害女人。

**3. 「因为先造的是亚当，后造的是夏娃」。** 「因为先造的是亚当，后造的是夏娃，且不是亚当被引诱，乃是女人被引诱，陷在罪里。」（2:13-14）
- **第13节：** 保罗引用创世记第2章的次序（创2:7、22）。互补派认为，这表明神在创造中设立的次序；平等派认为，这可能是针对以弗所传异教的人对创世记的误用。
- **第14节：** 保罗暗指创3:13「那蛇引诱我，我就吃了」。对这一节主要有两种理解：许多互补派学者（如施莱纳、穆尔）认为，这是支持第12节的第二个理由：在堕落的事上，创造的次序被颠倒了，夏娃领头，亚当跟从，结果带来了祸患；许多平等派学者和另一些人认为，保罗提到夏娃，是因为以弗所的一些女人正在被假教师引诱，就像夏娃从前一样。
- **不是说女人天生容易受骗：** 历史上有些解经家认为，这表示女人天生比较容易受骗；今天两派的多数学者都不接受这种理解。保罗在罗马书说罪是从「一人」亚当入了世界（罗5:12），亚当虽然没有被引诱，罪责却不因此减轻；他是明知故犯。

**4. 「就必在生产上得救」。** 「然而，女人若常存信心、爱心，又圣洁自守，就必在生产上得救。」（2:15）这节也很难解，主要有三种理解：
- **因那「生产」得救：** 有学者（如乔治·奈特）认为，「生产」指的是那一次独特的生产，就是「女人的后裔」（创3:15）基督的降生。女人虽然先被引诱，救主却是藉着一个女人降生的（参加4:4）。
- **在生产中蒙保守：** 有人认为「得救」在这里是身体上的保守，就是女人在生产时蒙神保护。
- **在神所给的呼召中活出救恩：** 许多学者（如施莱纳、穆尔、汤纳）认为，以弗所的假教师「禁止嫁娶」（4:3），保罗在这里说：女人不必放弃婚姻、不必拒绝作母亲，才能得救；她们是在神所给的生活中，常存信心、爱心、圣洁，活出她们的救恩。
- **这节绝不是说：** 保罗绝不是说女人要靠生孩子才能得救。这句话本身就说要「常存信心」；整封信也说救恩是因基督的赎价（2:6），也是因信（1:16）。没有结婚的女人、不能生育的女人、失去孩子的母亲（在看重传宗接代的家庭里，她们常常背着羞耻），和其他人一样，都是藉着信基督得救的。

**5. 一个必要的提醒。** 无论我们采取哪一种看法，谁都不可用这段经文为家庭暴力或教会中的欺压辩护。「顺服」从来不是要人忍受虐待；遭受家暴或性侵的人，可以而且应当寻求帮助，向警察和法院报案也是正当的（罗13:4）；受害者不必留在危险之中，也不必与不安全的人和好；饶恕不等于恢复施暴者的职分，也不等于让他再接近受害者或孩子；教会不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而劝受害者沉默。保罗在这封信中也吩咐提摩太，要「劝少年妇女如同姊妹；总要清清洁洁的」（5:2）。

> 要点：这段经文有真实的难处，忠心的信徒有不同的理解；但无论哪一种看法，男女都是照神形象造的，都是因信基督得救，都要在神的家中彼此尊重，一同事奉那位舍己作赎价的主。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因为只有一位神，在神和人中间，只有一位中保，乃是降世为人的基督耶稣；他舍自己作万人的赎价。」（2:5-6）在我们的社会里，很多事都要「找关系」：看病要托人认识医生，孩子上学要找人说情，办事要先送礼。久而久之，我面对神的时候，可能也不知不觉用同样的方法：觉得自己的祷告分量不够，要找一个「更属灵」的人替我祷告，神才会听；或者觉得要先送上点什么，神才肯接纳我。请别人为我代祷是好的，圣经也这样教导我们（2:1）；但他们不是我的中保。保罗说，在神和人中间，只有一位中保。他不是一个需要我先打点、先送礼的人；他自己就是那份礼物，「他舍自己作万人的赎价」。他也不是一个高高在上、不认识我的人；他是「降世为人的基督耶稣」，懂得我的软弱，现在就在父面前为我祈求（罗8:34）。而且，门那边坐着的，也不是一位要人打点才肯开门的官。保罗称他为「神我们救主」，是他自己「愿意万人得救」（2:3-4），是他差了这位中保来。今天，我不需要先找到对的人，也不需要先准备好什么，就可以藉着他来到神面前。我可以直接对父说：我是藉着耶稣来的，他已经替我付清了。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 falls into three parts. **Prayer for all people** (vv. 1–7): supplications, prayers, intercessions and thanksgivings are to be made for all people, including kings and all in high positions, because God desires all people to be saved; there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom for all. **How men are to pray** (v. 8): men are to pray in every place, lifting holy hands without anger or quarreling. **How women are to conduct themselves in the gathering** (vv. 9–15): women are to adorn themselves with good works, not with showy display; they are to learn quietly; Paul does not permit a woman to teach or exercise authority over a man, giving the order of creation and the fall as his reason; she will be saved through childbearing if they continue in faith, love and holiness, with self-control.

The chapter begins with prayer and centers on worship. Paul's concern is: when God's people gather, what kind of community should they be? His answer begins with the gospel: God desires all people to be saved, so the church prays for all people.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–4 Prayer for All People

**1. "First of all... for all people."** "First of all, then, I urge that supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be made for all people" (2:1). "First of all" means not only first in sequence but first in importance. To set the church in order, Paul begins with prayer.
- **Four words:** "supplications" are requests for specific needs; "prayers" is the general word for speaking to God; "intercessions" are requests on behalf of others; "thanksgivings" are expressions of gratitude. The four need not be sharply distinguished; together they mean that every kind of prayer is to be offered for all people.
- **"For all people":** the false teachers may have narrowed salvation to a few people with special knowledge. Paul says: pray for everyone; no one is outside our prayers.

**2. "For kings and all who are in high positions."** "For kings and all who are in high positions, that we may lead a peaceful and quiet life, godly and dignified in every way" (2:2).
- **Who was the emperor?** When this letter was written, the Roman emperor was most likely Nero, the very man who later persecuted Christians in Rome. Paul does not say "pray for good kings," but for "all who are in high positions." This continues the Old Testament teaching that the exiles were to "seek the welfare of the city... and pray to the LORD on its behalf" (Jer 29:7).
- **Why pray for them?** "That we may lead a peaceful and quiet life, godly and dignified in every way." Paul is not asking privileges for the church but a setting in which people can quietly fear God and live upright lives; in such a setting the gospel also spreads more freely (see 2:4).
- **Praying for them is not approving all they do:** we pray for those in authority because they too need salvation, and because God rules over all (see Rom 13:1). But such prayer does not treat them as gods. The early church prayed for the emperor but refused to offer incense to his image.

**3. "Who desires all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth."** "This is good, and it is pleasing in the sight of God our Savior, who desires all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth" (2:3–4).
- **God's heart:** God has no pleasure in the death of the wicked (Ezek 18:23, 32); he is "not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance" (2 Pet 3:9). God has real compassion for the lost, and this is the ground of our praying for all people.
- **What does "all people" mean?** This verse has been understood in different ways. The Reformed tradition (for example Calvin) has often taken "all people" here to mean all kinds of people, including the kings and officials of verse 2, regardless of status or race. Some Reformed theologians (such as John Piper) also say that God has a revealed will (he truly desires people to be saved) and a hidden will (he elects in sovereign freedom), and that the two do not contradict each other in God. The Arminian and Wesleyan traditions (for example John Wesley; among commentators, I. Howard Marshall) hold that God truly desires every single person to be saved and by his prevenient grace enables all to respond, though people may refuse. Both traditions agree that not everyone will finally be saved (see Matt 7:13–14), and both agree that the church must pray for all people and preach the gospel to all.
- **"The knowledge of the truth":** salvation and knowing the truth go together. Unlike those who teach "what is falsely called 'knowledge,'" the truth God wants people to know is what verses 5–6 go on to say.

> The point: God desires all people to be saved, so the church prays for all people, including rulers we dislike or who oppose us. No one is outside God's compassion, and no one should be outside our prayers.

## Going Deeper: 2:5–7 One Mediator

**1. "There is one God."** "For there is one God" (2:5). This is the confession Israel recited every day: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one" (Deut 6:4). Because there is only one God, he is the God of all people, not of one nation or group alone (see Rom 3:29–30). This is exactly why we pray for all people.

**2. "One mediator between God and men."** "And there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (2:5).
- **"Mediator":** one who stands between two parties to reconcile them. Job once lamented, "There is no arbiter between us, who might lay his hand on us both" (Job 9:33). Now such a mediator has come.
- **"One":** there is no other go-between who can bring us to God: not angels, not saints, not people with special spiritual knowledge, not clergy, nor ancestors, nor any deity or bodhisattva in a temple (see John 14:6; Acts 4:12). The Reformation stressed this: believers may come directly to God through Christ (Heb 4:16). Catholic teaching also confesses Christ as the one mediator, but holds that the intercession of Mary and the saints shares in Christ's mediation under him (Catechism of the Catholic Church 956, 969–970). The Reformers objected especially to calling on Mary and the departed saints, which they held blurs Christ's uniqueness; asking fellow believers to pray for us is another matter (2:1).
- **"The man":** the Greek reads literally "the man Christ Jesus." A mediator must be human to represent humans; he must also be God to bring God to us (see Heb 2:17; for his deity, John 1:1, 14; Heb 1:3). Here Paul stresses his humanity: he is truly human, sympathizes with our weaknesses (Heb 4:15), and even now, as a man, intercedes for us before the Father (Rom 8:34; Heb 7:25).

**3. "Who gave himself as a ransom for all."** "Who gave himself as a ransom for all, which is the testimony given at the proper time" (2:6).
- **"Ransom":** this is very close to Jesus' own words: "the Son of Man came... to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45). A ransom is the price paid to free a slave or captive. Paul's word, antilytron, adds the prefix anti ("in place of") to Jesus' word; many take it to stress substitution: Christ gave himself in our place and paid the price we could not pay.
- **"Gave himself":** God did not take Christ's life from him against his will; Christ laid down his life willingly (see John 10:18; Gal 2:20).
- **"For all":** as with verse 4, this has been understood in different ways. The Reformed tradition (for example Calvin and the Canons of Dort) holds that Christ's death is sufficient for all but was intended to redeem, and actually redeems, all who belong to him, "all" meaning all kinds of people regardless of race or status (see Rev 5:9). Some Reformed theologians (the hypothetical universalists, such as John Davenant) hold that Christ died for every person on condition of faith, while God specially purposed to save the elect. The Arminian and Wesleyan traditions (for example John Wesley; among commentators, I. Howard Marshall) hold that Christ died for every person and made salvation available to all, received through faith. All three views hold that Christ's death was a real substitution, that all who believe will be saved, and that the church must announce this good news to everyone.

**4. "For this I was appointed a preacher."** "For this I was appointed a preacher and an apostle (I am telling the truth, I am not lying), a teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth" (2:7). Paul's whole life is given to this one mediator and this one ransom. Once he persecuted believers; now he is sent to bring this good news to the Gentiles.

> The point: There is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom. To come to God we need no other connection and nothing to offer first; we need only him.

## Going Deeper: 2:8–10 Holy Hands and Good Works

**1. "I desire then that in every place the men should pray."** "I desire then that in every place the men should pray, lifting holy hands without anger or quarreling" (2:8).
- **"Lifting holy hands":** lifting the hands in prayer was a common posture in the Old Testament and the early church (see Ps 141:2; 28:2). The point is not the posture but "holy hands," a clean life.
- **"Without anger or quarreling":** the church in Ephesus had quarrels (1:4; 6:4–5), and the men were likely in the middle of them. Paul is saying that lifting hands in prayer while quarreling with and resenting a brother is a contradiction (see Matt 5:23–24; 1 Pet 3:7). This does not mean an angry heart cannot come to God; the Psalms are full of prayers that bring anger to him. We may come with our anger; we may not hold on to it and turn it on one another.
- **"In every place":** probably the various house churches around Ephesus. Wherever they gather, they are to pray this way.

**2. "Likewise also that women should adorn themselves in respectable apparel."** "Likewise also that women should adorn themselves in respectable apparel, with modesty and self-control, not with braided hair and gold or pearls or costly attire, but with what is proper for women who profess godliness, with good works" (2:9–10).
- **What is Paul opposing?** In that culture, elaborate braided hairstyles with gold and pearls were a way for wealthy women to display status and wealth, sometimes with sexual allure. Paul is not forbidding braids; he is opposing self-display, drawing attention to oneself in worship (see 1 Pet 3:3–4).
- **"With good works":** Paul moves the focus from outside to inside, from display to service. This is not a demand on women only: men too should not display wealth and status in the church (see Jas 2:1–4).

> The point: In worship, men are to lay down anger and quarrels, women to lay down display. We can let them go because we come to God through the one Mediator alone; we no longer need to prove ourselves by winning arguments or by how we look.

## Going Deeper: 2:11–15 Learning Quietly

This is one of the most debated passages in the New Testament. Faithful believers through the centuries have understood it differently; the main views are set out side by side below for readers to weigh.

**1. "Let a woman learn quietly."** "Let a woman learn quietly with all submissiveness" (2:11).
- **"Learn":** in Paul's day some rabbinic sayings discouraged teaching women the law (e.g. Mishnah Sotah 3.4), and Greco-Roman society seldom educated women. Paul says women are to learn. In that setting this was a positive instruction (see Luke 10:39, Mary sitting at Jesus' feet listening to his teaching).
- **"Quietly":** the Greek word shares a root with "quiet" in verse 2 ("a peaceful and quiet life"). It means calm, not disruptive, not necessarily total silence.

**2. "I do not permit a woman to teach or to exercise authority over a man."** "I do not permit a woman to teach or to exercise authority over a man; rather, she is to remain quiet" (2:12). This is the heart of the debate.
- **"Exercise authority":** this word occurs only here in the New Testament and is uncommon in Greek of the period. Some scholars think it is neutral, "to exercise authority"; others think it is negative, such as "to domineer" or "to usurp." Some (such as Andreas Köstenberger) argue from the "neither... nor" construction that "teach" and "exercise authority" must both be positive or both negative; others (such as Philip Payne and Linda Belleville) dispute this. Study of the word is still debated.
- **The view that certain church offices are distinguished by sex (often called complementarian):** many scholars (such as George Knight, William Mounce, Thomas Schreiner and Douglas Moo, as well as Wayne Grudem and John Piper) hold that Paul here does not permit women, in the church's gathering, to hold the office of teaching men and leading the whole church, that is, the office of elder or overseer (see 3:2, "able to teach"). Because verse 13 appeals to the order of creation, they hold that this teaching applies not only to Ephesus but to the church in every age. They also stress that this does not mean women are of lesser worth: men and women alike are made in God's image (Gen 1:27) and are one in Christ (Gal 3:28); women have many opportunities to teach and serve in the church, such as teaching other women (Titus 2:3–5) and children (see 2 Tim 1:5; 3:15), explaining God's word privately (Acts 18:26), and praying and prophesying (1 Cor 11:5).
- **The view that church offices are open to both sexes (often called egalitarian):** other scholars (such as Gordon Fee, Craig Keener and Philip Payne) hold that Paul is addressing a specific problem in Ephesus: some women had been led astray by false teaching (see 5:13; 2 Tim 3:6–7) and lacked education, so Paul temporarily forbade them to teach and first told them to "learn." They see verses 13–14 as an illustration of the consequences of deception, not a permanent principle, and they point out that Paul elsewhere commends women coworkers, such as Phoebe, a deacon (Rom 16:1), Prisca (Rom 16:3), and women who pray and prophesy in the gathering (1 Cor 11:5).
- **What both sides agree on:** men and women are both made in God's image and receive the same grace; women have honored and indispensable service in God's kingdom; no one may use this passage to belittle, suppress or harm women.

**3. "For Adam was formed first, then Eve."** "For Adam was formed first, then Eve; and Adam was not deceived, but the woman was deceived and became a transgressor" (2:13–14).
- **Verse 13:** Paul cites the order of Genesis 2 (Gen 2:7, 22). Complementarians see here an order God established in creation; egalitarians think Paul may be answering the Ephesian false teachers' misuse of Genesis.
- **Verse 14:** Paul alludes to Gen 3:13, "The serpent deceived me, and I ate." Interpreters read this verse in two main ways. Many complementarians (such as Schreiner and Moo) take it as a second reason supporting verse 12: at the fall the order of creation was reversed, with Eve taking the lead and Adam following, and harm followed. Many egalitarians, and some others, think Paul mentions Eve because some women in Ephesus were being deceived by false teachers, as Eve once was.
- **Not that women are by nature easily deceived:** many older interpreters took the verse to mean women are by nature more easily deceived; most modern interpreters on both sides reject that reading. In Romans Paul says sin entered the world through "one man," Adam (Rom 5:12); Adam's guilt was not lessened because he was not deceived; he sinned knowingly.

**4. "She will be saved through childbearing."** "Yet she will be saved through childbearing, if they continue in faith and love and holiness, with self-control" (2:15). This verse is also hard; there are three main readings:
- **Saved through "the childbearing":** some scholars (such as George Knight) think "childbearing" refers to the birth of the Child, the "offspring" of the woman (Gen 3:15), Christ. Though the woman was deceived first, it was through a woman that the Savior was born (see Gal 4:4).
- **Kept safe in childbirth:** some take "saved" here as physical preservation, God protecting women through childbirth.
- **Living out salvation in God's calling:** many scholars (such as Schreiner, Moo and Towner) note that the Ephesian false teachers "forbid marriage" (4:3). Paul is saying that women do not need to abandon marriage and motherhood in order to be saved; in the life God has given them, continuing in faith, love and holiness, they live out their salvation.
- **What it certainly does not mean:** Paul is not saying women are saved by having children. The verse itself says "if they continue in faith"; the whole letter says salvation comes through Christ's ransom (2:6) and through faith (1:16). Unmarried women, women who cannot have children, and mothers who have lost children (who in families that prize carrying on the family line often bear shame) are saved, like everyone else, through faith in Christ.

**5. A necessary word.** Whichever view we take, this passage can never be used to justify domestic violence or abuse in the church. "Submission" never means enduring abuse; those who suffer domestic violence or sexual abuse can and should seek help, and reporting to the police and courts is right (Rom 13:4); victims need not remain in danger or reconcile with someone unsafe; forgiveness does not restore an abuser's office or give him access to victims or children; the church must not urge victims to keep silent to protect its reputation (the Chinese saying 家丑不可外扬, "don't air the family's shame outside"). In this same letter Paul tells Timothy to treat "younger women as sisters, in all purity" (5:2).

> The point: This passage has real difficulties, and faithful believers understand it differently; but on every view, men and women are made in God's image, are saved through faith in Christ, and are to honor one another in God's household as they serve together the Lord who gave himself as a ransom.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, who gave himself as a ransom for all" (2:5–6). In our society many things depend on "finding a connection" (找关系): to see a good doctor you need someone who knows him; to get a child into a school you need someone to put in a word; to get something done you first bring a gift. Over time I may come to God the same way without noticing: feeling that my own prayers do not carry enough weight, so I need someone "more spiritual" to pray for me before God will listen; or feeling that I must bring something first before God will accept me. Asking others to pray for me is good; Scripture teaches it (2:1). But they are not my mediator. Paul says there is only one mediator between God and men. He is not someone I must first win over with a gift; he himself is the gift: "who gave himself as a ransom for all." Nor is he someone high above who does not know me; he is "the man Christ Jesus," who understands my weakness and is even now interceding for me before the Father (Rom 8:34). And the one on the other side of the door is not an official who must be won over before he opens it. Paul calls him "God our Savior," who himself "desires all people to be saved" (2:3–4); he is the one who sent this Mediator. Today I do not need to find the right person or prepare anything first; I can come to God through him. I can say to the Father directly: I have come through Jesus; he has already paid in full for me.
