## 本章脉络

第三章分成三段。**监督**（1-7节）：「人若想要得监督的职分，就是羡慕善工」；作监督的必须无可指责，有节制，善于教导，好好管理自己的家，不可是初入教的，在教外也要有好名声。**执事**（8-13节）：执事要端庄、不一口两舌、存清洁的良心，要先受试验；女执事（或执事的妻子）也要端庄、不说谗言、凡事忠心；善作执事的，在真道上大有胆量。**神的家与敬虔的奥秘**（14-16节）：保罗写这些，是要提摩太知道在神的家中当怎样行；这家是永生神的教会，真理的柱石和根基；敬虔的奥秘就是基督：「神在肉身显现……被接在荣耀里」。

这一章前半部分像一张品格清单，后半部分是一首赞美诗。两者放在一起，是要告诉我们：教会带领人的品格，不是从一套规条来的，而是从认识那位「在肉身显现」的主来的。

## 深读 3:1-7 作监督的，必须无可指责

**1. 「人若想要得监督的职分，就是羡慕善工」。** 「『人若想要得监督的职分，就是羡慕善工。』这话是可信的。」（3:1）
- **「监督」：** 原文是看守、照管的人。在新约中，「监督」和「长老」常指同一个职分（参徒20:17、28；多1:5、7）：「长老」着重这人的成熟，「监督」着重他的工作。彼得也称基督为我们「灵魂的牧人监督」（彼前2:25）。
- **「善工」：** 保罗说想作监督是好的，但他强调这是「工」，不是地位。在一个看重面子和头衔的文化中，这话提醒我们：教会的职分是服事，不是荣誉（参太20:26-28）。
- **「想要」：** 这不是说人只要想当就可以当；接下来的清单就是要看他是否合适。但保罗也不认为愿意承担这份工作是骄傲。教会需要有人愿意为群羊摆上自己。

**2. 品格的清单。** 「作监督的，必须无可指责，只作一个妇人的丈夫，有节制，自守，端正，乐意接待远人，善于教导；不因酒滋事，不打人，只要温和，不争竞，不贪财」（3:2-3）。
- **「无可指责」：** 这是总纲，下面的各项都是它的说明。不是说他没有罪，而是说他的生活没有明显、公开、使人可以合理指控的过错。
- **几乎全是品格：** 这份清单中只有「善于教导」是一种能力，其余都是品格。神看重的首先不是一个人的口才、学历、财富或在社会上的地位，而是他的为人。
- **「不打人，只要温和」：** 一个用暴力、用怒气、用权柄压制别人的人，不可以作监督（参彼前5:3「也不是辖制所托付你们的」）。教会如果发现带领人有欺压、暴力或性方面的不当行为，不可为了保全面子而掩盖，要按真理处理；涉及犯罪的，应当向有关当局报告。
- **「不贪财」：** 以弗所的假教师「以敬虔为得利的门路」（6:5）。带领人不可以把服事当作赚钱的途径（参彼前5:2）。

**3. 「只作一个妇人的丈夫」。** 这句话有几种理解：
- **对婚姻忠诚：** 原文直译是一个女人的男人。多数现代解经家（如奈特、孟恩斯、汤纳）认为，重点是在婚姻中对妻子忠诚，不可多妻，也不可有婚外的关系。
- **一生只结一次婚：** 一些初期教父认为，作监督的人即使丧偶，也不可再婚。可是保罗在5:14和罗7:2-3并没有反对丧偶的人再婚。
- **没有离婚再婚：** 有人认为这是排除离婚再婚的人。各教会对此有不同的做法。
- **一定要结婚：** 很少人这样理解。保罗自己没有结婚（林前7:7-8），他在这里关心的是已婚者的忠诚，不是要求每一个监督都必须结婚。

**4. 「好好管理自己的家」。** 「好好管理自己的家，使儿女凡事端庄顺服。人若不知道管理自己的家，焉能照管神的教会呢？」（3:4-5）
- **家是一个试验场：** 一个人在家中怎样对待妻子和儿女，最能显出他的真实品格。教会也是一个「家」（3:15），在自己家里不能用爱心和忠心带领的人，在神的家中也难以照管人。
- **不是要求完美的孩子：** 「端庄顺服」是说儿女在他的管教下有秩序，不是说孩子永远不会犯错，或者孩子长大后的每一个选择都要由父母负责。
- **「照管」：** 这个词也用在好撒玛利亚人的故事中：「照应他」（路10:34-35）。照管教会，是带着怜悯去照顾，不是发号施令。

**5. 「初入教的不可作监督」。** 「初入教的不可作监督，恐怕他自高自大，就落在魔鬼所受的刑罚里。」（3:6）「初入教的」原文是新栽种的。一个人信主不久就被放在高位上，很容易骄傲。许多人认为「魔鬼所受的刑罚」指的是魔鬼因骄傲而受的审判。教会不可因为一个人有能力、有钱或有名望，就急着让他作带领人（参5:22）。

**6. 「在教外有好名声」。** 「监督也必须在教外有好名声，恐怕被人毁谤，落在魔鬼的网罗里。」（3:7）不信的人也在看教会的带领人。如果一个带领人在工作上不诚实，在邻舍中名声很坏，福音就会被人轻看。

> 要点：神为他的家选带领人，看的首先不是才干、财富或地位，而是品格。教会的职分是一份「善工」，是像基督那样为羊摆上自己的服事。

## 深读 3:8-13 作执事的，也是如此

**1. 「作执事的」。** 「作执事的，也是如此：必须端庄，不一口两舌，不好喝酒，不贪不义之财；要存清洁的良心，固守真道的奥秘。」（3:8-9）
- **「执事」：** 原文就是服事的人。许多人认为，执事的起源可以追溯到使徒行传第6章，那七个人被选出来管理供给寡妇的事，使使徒可以「专心以祈祷、传道为事」（徒6:3-4）。不过，使徒行传第6章没有称他们为执事。腓1:1也提到「诸位监督，诸位执事」。
- **和监督的不同：** 执事的清单没有「善于教导」，所以多数人认为，执事主要负责实际的服事和管理，不是教导和带领全教会。但品格的要求几乎是一样的。
- **「不一口两舌」：** 执事常常要在不同的人中间走动，处理实际的需要，很容易对这个人说一套，对那个人说另一套。
- **「固守真道的奥秘」：** 执事做的虽然是实际的事，但他也必须持守真道。实际的服事和纯正的信仰不可分开。

**2. 「要先受试验」。** 「这等人也要先受试验，若没有可责之处，然后叫他们作执事。」（3:10）保罗没有说怎样试验，可能是让教会观察一段时间。教会选立同工，要有耐心，不要因为急需人手就随便安排人。

**3. 「女执事也是如此」。** 「女执事也是如此：必须端庄，不说谗言，有节制，凡事忠心。」（3:11）原文只是女人也是如此，可以有两种理解：
- **执事的妻子：** 有学者（如加尔文）认为，这是指执事（或监督）的妻子。英文标准译本（ESV）译作「他们的妻子」。
- **女执事：** 另一些学者认为是指女执事。理由是：「也是如此」和第8节的结构一样，像是在介绍另一组服事的人；原文没有「他们的」；保罗在别处称非比为坚革哩教会的执事（罗16:1，原文与「执事」是同一个词）。初期教父金口约翰也这样理解。和合本译作「女执事」，就是采取这种理解。
- **两种理解的人都同意：** 女人在教会中有重要的服事，她们的品格和男人一样受到看重。

**4. 「执事只要作一个妇人的丈夫」。** 「执事只要作一个妇人的丈夫，好好管理儿女和自己的家。」（3:12）这和对监督的要求一样（参3:2、4）。

**5. 「美好的地步……大有胆量」。** 「因为善作执事的，自己就得到美好的地步，并且在基督耶稣里的真道上大有胆量。」（3:13）
- **「美好的地步」：** 可能指在教会中受人尊重，也可能指在神面前的好名声。无论哪一种，都不是世人所看重的地位。耶稣说：「我在你们中间如同服事人的。」（路22:27）
- **「大有胆量」：** 忠心服事的人，在信仰上会越来越坚定，也越来越能坦然地面对神、面对人。

> 要点：执事的服事看起来很实际，却和真道连在一起。在神的家中，摆桌子、探访、管账、照顾有需要的人，都是尊贵的服事；忠心的人在其中会越来越认识基督。

## 深读 3:14-15 在神的家中当怎样行

**1. 「倘若我耽延日久」。** 「我指望快到你那里去，所以先将这些事写给你。倘若我耽延日久，你也可以知道在神的家中当怎样行。」（3:14-15）保罗希望能快去以弗所，但不确定。他把教导写下来，提摩太和以弗所教会就可以照着行。今天我们读这封信，也是在读保罗留给教会的话。

**2. 「神的家」。** 「这家就是永生神的教会」（3:15）。
- **「家」：** 原文可以指房屋，也可以指家庭。保罗两层意思都用：教会是神的家人（5:1-2），也是神居住的殿（参林前3:16；弗2:21-22）。
- **「永生神」：** 以弗所有亚底米的神像，那是不能说话、没有生命的偶像（参徒19:26）。教会的神是活的神。

**3. 「真理的柱石和根基」。** 「真理的柱石和根基」（3:15）。
- **「柱石」：** 柱子把屋顶撑起来，叫人从远处就能看见。教会要高举真理，让世人看见。
- **「根基」：** 原文指地基或支撑物。教会要稳稳地守住真理，不让它被异教摇动。
- **教会和真理的关系：** 教会不是真理的来源，真理是从神来的，记在圣经里（提后3:16）。教会是支撑和保守真理的群体。这也提醒我们：真理不是只靠个人保守的，而是在教会的团契中一同持守的。

> 要点：教会是永生神的家，是真理的柱石和根基。我们在教会中怎样行，不只关乎人际关系，更关乎神的荣耀和真理的见证。

## 深读 3:16 大哉，敬虔的奥秘

**1. 「大哉，敬虔的奥秘，无人不以为然」。** 「无人不以为然」原文是我们一同承认的意思，很可能是在介绍初期教会大家一同诵念的认信或诗歌。
- **「奥秘」：** 在保罗的用法里，「奥秘」不是神秘难懂的事，而是从前隐藏、现在被神显明出来的事（参罗16:25-26；西1:26-27）。
- **「敬虔的奥秘」：** 敬虔不是从我们的努力开始的，而是从神所显明的基督开始的。前面第9节说「真道的奥秘」，这里说「敬虔的奥秘」，两者都指向基督。

**2. 「神在肉身显现」。** 这里有一个抄本上的问题。和合本根据较晚的抄本，译作「神在肉身显现」；较早的抄本（多数学者认为更接近原文）作他在肉身显现，原文是那一位的意思，指的是基督，英文标准译本（ESV）就是这样译的。两种读法的意思其实相近：这「在肉身显现」的一位，本来就已经存在，然后取了人的身体（参约1:14；腓2:6-7）。这正是道成肉身的信仰。

**3. 六句诗。** 这首诗很可能分成三组，每组两句，彼此对照：
- **「在肉身显现，被圣灵称义」：** 他在肉身中显现，被人轻看，被钉十字架；但圣灵使他从死里复活，证明他是义的，「以大能显明是神的儿子」（罗1:4；参彼前3:18）。「称义」在这里是被证明为义的意思。
- **「被天使看见，被传于外邦」：** 天上的天使看见他的得胜（参彼前3:22），地上的外邦人听见他的福音。天上和地上都知道他。
- **「被世人信服，被接在荣耀里」：** 世上的人信了他，他自己被接到天上的荣耀里（徒1:9）。
- **次序：** 「被接在荣耀里」在时间上早于福音传遍外邦，诗歌却把它放在最后。这首诗不是要排列时间表，而是用对照的方式，赞美基督从卑微到荣耀的全部工作。

**4. 为什么放在这里？** 保罗刚讲完监督和执事的品格，就唱出这首诗。他是要告诉提摩太：敬虔的生活不是从一张清单开始的，而是从这位基督开始的。我们能够活出敬虔，是因为他在肉身显现，为我们死，又复活、升天；我们是靠着他，也是为了他。下一章，保罗就要讲到那些把敬虔变成禁戒规条的人（4:1-5）。

> 要点：敬虔的奥秘不是一套方法，而是一个人：在肉身显现、被圣灵称义、被接在荣耀里的基督。教会的一切秩序和品格，都要从认识他、敬拜他开始。

## 默想

**默想：** 「大哉，敬虔的奥秘，无人不以为然！就是神在肉身显现，被圣灵称义，被天使看见，被传于外邦，被世人信服，被接在荣耀里。」（3:16）一听到「敬虔」这个词，我可能会想到一些习惯：每天读经祷告、主日不缺席、不抽烟、不喝酒、说话有礼貌。这些都可能是好的。可是保罗说，敬虔的奥秘不是一套习惯，而是一个人。当我读到前面那张监督和执事的清单，也许心里会想：我离这些还差得很远。其实，真正完全合乎这张清单的，只有一位，就是基督自己。他无可指责，他温和，他不争竞，他好好照管神的家，他为羊舍命（约10:11）。今天，我可以慢慢地读这六句诗，每读一句就停一下。我最需要的是哪一句？是「在肉身显现」，知道他来到我所在的地方？是「被圣灵称义」，知道那被人定罪的，神已经证明他为义，而我在他里面也蒙了接纳？是「被传于外邦」，知道这福音也传给了像我这样的人？还是「被接在荣耀里」，知道他现在就在父的右边，我的将来也在他手里？我可以选一句，带着它过今天；然后和历代的教会一同说：大哉，敬虔的奥秘！

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 falls into three parts. **Overseers** (vv. 1–7): "If anyone aspires to the office of overseer, he desires a noble task"; an overseer must be above reproach, self-controlled, able to teach, managing his own household well, not a recent convert, and well thought of by outsiders. **Deacons** (vv. 8–13): deacons must be dignified, not double-tongued, holding the mystery of the faith with a clear conscience, and tested first; women deacons (or deacons' wives) likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, faithful in all things; those who serve well gain great confidence in the faith. **The household of God and the mystery of godliness** (vv. 14–16): Paul writes so that Timothy may know how to behave in the household of God, the church of the living God, a pillar and buttress of truth; the mystery of godliness is Christ: "He was manifested in the flesh... taken up in glory."

The first half of the chapter reads like a checklist of character; the second half is a hymn. Placed side by side, they tell us that the character of the church's leaders does not come from a set of rules but from knowing the one who was "manifested in the flesh."

## Going Deeper: 3:1–7 An Overseer Must Be Above Reproach

**1. "If anyone aspires to the office of overseer, he desires a noble task."** "The saying is trustworthy: If anyone aspires to the office of overseer, he desires a noble task" (3:1).
- **"Overseer":** the word means one who watches over and cares for others. In the New Testament "overseer" and "elder" often refer to the same office (see Acts 20:17, 28; Titus 1:5, 7): "elder" stresses the person's maturity, "overseer" his work. Peter calls Christ "the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls" (1 Pet 2:25).
- **"A noble task":** Paul says aspiring to be an overseer is good, but he stresses that it is a "task," a work, not a status. In a culture that prizes face and titles, this reminds us that office in the church is service, not honor (see Matt 20:26–28).
- **"Aspires":** this does not mean that anyone who wants the office may have it; the list that follows tests whether he is fit. But Paul does not regard willingness to take on this work as pride. The church needs people willing to give themselves for the flock.

**2. The list of character.** "Therefore an overseer must be above reproach, the husband of one wife, sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, able to teach, not a drunkard, not violent but gentle, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money" (3:2–3).
- **"Above reproach":** this is the heading; the rest of the list fills it out. It does not mean he is sinless, but that his life has no obvious, public fault that could fairly be charged against him.
- **Almost all character:** only "able to teach" on this list is a skill; everything else is character. What God values first is not eloquence, education, wealth or social standing, but what kind of person someone is.
- **"Not violent but gentle":** someone who uses violence, anger or authority to crush others may not be an overseer (see 1 Pet 5:3, "not domineering over those in your charge"). If a church finds that a leader has been abusive, violent or sexually inappropriate, it must not cover it up to save face but deal with it truthfully; where a crime is involved, it should be reported to the proper authorities.
- **"Not a lover of money":** the false teachers in Ephesus imagined "that godliness is a means of gain" (6:5). Leaders must not treat ministry as a way to make money (see 1 Pet 5:2).

**3. "The husband of one wife."** This phrase has been understood in several ways:
- **Faithful in marriage:** the Greek reads literally "a one-woman man." Most modern commentators (such as Knight, Mounce and Towner) think the point is faithfulness to one's wife in marriage: no polygamy and no relationships outside marriage.
- **Married only once in a lifetime:** some early church fathers held that an overseer may not remarry even after his wife's death. But in 5:14 and Rom 7:2–3 Paul does not oppose the remarriage of the widowed.
- **Not divorced and remarried:** some take it to exclude those who have divorced and remarried. Churches handle this in different ways.
- **Must be married:** few read it this way. Paul himself was unmarried (1 Cor 7:7–8); his concern here is the faithfulness of married men, not a requirement that every overseer marry.

**4. "He must manage his own household well."** "He must manage his own household well, with all dignity keeping his children submissive, for if someone does not know how to manage his own household, how will he care for God's church?" (3:4–5).
- **The home as a testing ground:** how a man treats his wife and children at home shows his true character most clearly. The church is also a "household" (3:15); someone who cannot lead his own home with love and faithfulness will find it hard to care for people in God's household.
- **Not a demand for perfect children:** "submissive" means his children are orderly under his care, not that children never misbehave, or that parents are answerable for every choice their grown children make.
- **"Care for":** the same word is used in the story of the Good Samaritan: "took care of him" (Luke 10:34–35). Caring for the church means looking after people with compassion, not issuing orders.

**5. "He must not be a recent convert."** "He must not be a recent convert, or he may become puffed up with conceit and fall into the condemnation of the devil" (3:6). "Recent convert" means literally "newly planted." Someone placed in high office soon after coming to faith can easily become proud. Many take "the condemnation of the devil" to mean the judgment the devil incurred through pride. A church must not rush someone into leadership because he is capable, wealthy or prominent (see 5:22).

**6. "Well thought of by outsiders."** "Moreover, he must be well thought of by outsiders, so that he may not fall into disgrace, into a snare of the devil" (3:7). Unbelievers are watching the church's leaders too. If a leader is dishonest at work or has a bad name among his neighbors, the gospel will be held in contempt.

> The point: When God chooses leaders for his household, he looks first not at talent, wealth or status but at character. Office in the church is a "noble task," service that gives itself for the sheep as Christ did.

## Going Deeper: 3:8–13 Deacons Likewise

**1. "Deacons likewise."** "Deacons likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not addicted to much wine, not greedy for dishonest gain. They must hold the mystery of the faith with a clear conscience" (3:8–9).
- **"Deacons":** the word simply means "servants." Many trace the origin of deacons to Acts 6, where seven men were chosen to oversee the distribution to widows so that the apostles could "devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word" (Acts 6:3–4). Acts 6, however, does not call them deacons. Phil 1:1 also mentions "the overseers and deacons."
- **How they differ from overseers:** the deacons' list lacks "able to teach," so most think deacons served mainly in practical service and administration rather than in teaching and leading the whole church. But the character required is nearly the same.
- **"Not double-tongued":** deacons often move among many people handling practical needs, and it is easy to tell one person one thing and another person something else.
- **"Hold the mystery of the faith":** though deacons do practical work, they must also hold to the truth. Practical service and sound faith cannot be separated.

**2. "Let them also be tested first."** "And let them also be tested first; then let them serve as deacons if they prove themselves blameless" (3:10). Paul does not say how they are tested; perhaps the church observes them over time. A church choosing workers needs patience and should not appoint people carelessly because it urgently needs hands.

**3. "Their wives likewise" or "women likewise."** "Their wives likewise must be dignified, not slanderers, but sober-minded, faithful in all things" (3:11). The Greek says only "women likewise," which can be read two ways:
- **Deacons' wives:** some scholars (such as Calvin) take it to mean the wives of deacons (or of overseers). The ESV translates "their wives."
- **Women deacons:** other scholars take it to mean women deacons. Their reasons: "likewise" follows the same pattern as verse 8, as if introducing another group of servants; the Greek has no "their"; and Paul elsewhere calls Phoebe a deacon of the church at Cenchreae (Rom 16:1, the same Greek word as "deacon"). The early church father John Chrysostom read it this way. The CUV translates "women deacons," taking this view.
- **Both views agree:** women have important service in the church, and their character is valued just as men's is.

**4. "Let deacons each be the husband of one wife."** "Let deacons each be the husband of one wife, managing their children and their own households well" (3:12). This matches what is required of overseers (see 3:2, 4).

**5. "A good standing... great confidence."** "For those who serve well as deacons gain a good standing for themselves and also great confidence in the faith that is in Christ Jesus" (3:13).
- **"A good standing":** this may mean respect in the church or a good name before God. Either way, it is not the kind of status the world prizes. Jesus said, "I am among you as the one who serves" (Luke 22:27).
- **"Great confidence":** those who serve faithfully grow steadier in the faith and freer to face God and people openly.

> The point: Deacons' work looks practical, yet it is bound up with the truth. In God's household, setting up tables, visiting, keeping the accounts and caring for people in need are all honorable service; the faithful come to know Christ more deeply in it.

## Going Deeper: 3:14–15 How to Behave in the Household of God

**1. "If I delay."** "I hope to come to you soon, but I am writing these things to you so that, if I delay, you may know how one ought to behave in the household of God" (3:14–15). Paul hoped to come to Ephesus soon but was not sure. He wrote his teaching down so that Timothy and the Ephesian church could follow it. When we read this letter today, we too are reading the words Paul left for the church.

**2. "The household of God."** "Which is the church of the living God" (3:15).
- **"Household":** the Greek can mean a house or a family. Paul uses both senses: the church is God's family (5:1–2) and the temple where God dwells (see 1 Cor 3:16; Eph 2:21–22).
- **"The living God":** Ephesus had images of Artemis, idols that could not speak and had no life (see Acts 19:26). The church's God is the living God.

**3. "A pillar and buttress of truth."** (3:15).
- **"Pillar":** a pillar holds up the roof and can be seen from far away. The church is to hold up the truth for the world to see.
- **"Buttress":** the word means a foundation or support. The church is to hold the truth firmly so that false teaching cannot shake it.
- **The church and the truth:** the church is not the source of truth; the truth comes from God and is written in Scripture (2 Tim 3:16). The church is the community that holds up and guards the truth. This also reminds us that the truth is not kept by individuals alone but held together in the church's fellowship.

> The point: The church is the household of the living God, a pillar and buttress of truth. How we behave in the church concerns not only our relationships with one another but God's glory and the witness of the truth.

## Going Deeper: 3:16 Great Indeed Is the Mystery of Godliness

**1. "Great indeed, we confess, is the mystery of godliness."** "We confess" means "by common confession," and very likely introduces a confession or hymn the early church recited together.
- **"Mystery":** as Paul uses the word, a "mystery" is not something obscure but something once hidden and now revealed by God (see Rom 16:25–26; Col 1:26–27).
- **"The mystery of godliness":** godliness does not begin with our effort but with Christ, whom God has revealed. Verse 9 spoke of "the mystery of the faith"; here Paul speaks of "the mystery of godliness"; both point to Christ.

**2. "He was manifested in the flesh."** There is a manuscript question here. The CUV follows later manuscripts and reads "God was manifested in the flesh"; the earlier manuscripts (which most scholars think are closer to the original) read "He who," referring to Christ, and the ESV translates it that way. The two readings mean much the same thing: the one "manifested in the flesh" already existed and then took a human body (see John 1:14; Phil 2:6–7). This is the faith of the incarnation.

**3. Six lines.** The hymn very likely falls into three pairs, each pair a contrast:
- **"Manifested in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit":** he appeared in the flesh, was despised and crucified; but the Spirit raised him from the dead, showing him to be righteous, "declared to be the Son of God in power" (Rom 1:4; see 1 Pet 3:18). "Vindicated" here means shown to be in the right.
- **"Seen by angels, proclaimed among the nations":** angels in heaven saw his victory (see 1 Pet 3:22); Gentiles on earth heard his gospel. Heaven and earth both know him.
- **"Believed on in the world, taken up in glory":** people in the world believed in him, and he himself was taken up into heavenly glory (Acts 1:9).
- **The order:** being "taken up in glory" came before the gospel spread among the nations, yet the hymn puts it last. The hymn is not laying out a timetable; it uses contrasts to praise the whole of Christ's work, from humiliation to glory.

**4. Why here?** Paul has just finished describing the character of overseers and deacons, and then he breaks into this hymn. He wants Timothy to know that a godly life does not begin with a checklist but with this Christ. We can live godly lives because he was manifested in the flesh, died for us, rose and ascended; we live by him and for him. In the next chapter Paul turns to people who have turned godliness into rules of abstinence (4:1–5).

> The point: The mystery of godliness is not a method but a person: Christ, manifested in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit, taken up in glory. All the church's order and character must begin with knowing and worshiping him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Great indeed, we confess, is the mystery of godliness: He was manifested in the flesh, vindicated by the Spirit, seen by angels, proclaimed among the nations, believed on in the world, taken up in glory" (3:16). When I hear the word "godliness," I may think of habits: reading the Bible and praying every day, never missing Sunday, not smoking or drinking, speaking politely. These may all be good. But Paul says the mystery of godliness is not a set of habits; it is a person. When I read the list for overseers and deacons, I may think: I am still far from all this. In truth, only one person fully fits that list: Christ himself. He is above reproach; he is gentle; he is not quarrelsome; he cares well for God's household; he lays down his life for the sheep (John 10:11). Today I can read these six lines slowly, pausing after each one. Which line do I need most? "Manifested in the flesh," knowing he came into the place where I am? "Vindicated by the Spirit," knowing that the one people condemned, God has shown to be righteous, and that in him I too am accepted? "Proclaimed among the nations," knowing this gospel has reached people like me? Or "taken up in glory," knowing he is now at the Father's right hand and my future is in his hands? I can choose one line and carry it through the day, and then say with the church of every age: Great indeed is the mystery of godliness!
