## 本章脉络

第四章分成三段。**后来必有人离弃真道**（1-5节）：圣灵明说，有人要听从邪灵和鬼魔的道理，禁止嫁娶，禁戒食物；但凡神所造的物都是好的，若感谢着领受，就没有一样可弃的，都因神的道和人的祈求成为圣洁了。**作基督耶稣的好执事**（6-10节）：提摩太要把这些事提醒弟兄们，弃绝荒谬的话，在敬虔上操练自己；操练身体益处还少，惟独敬虔凡事都有益处；我们的指望在乎永生的神，他是万人的救主，更是信徒的救主。**不可叫人小看你年轻**（11-16节）：提摩太要在言语、行为、爱心、信心、清洁上作信徒的榜样；要以宣读、劝勉、教导为念；不要轻忽所得的恩赐；要谨慎自己和自己的教训。

第三章以「敬虔的奥秘」作结，这奥秘就是基督；第四章就讲到假的敬虔和真的敬虔。假的敬虔是一套禁戒的规条，真的敬虔是在基督里，感谢着领受神所赐的一切，并且天天操练自己，越来越像他。

## 深读 4:1-5 凡神所造的物都是好的

**1. 「在后来的时候，必有人离弃真道」。** 「圣灵明说，在后来的时候，必有人离弃真道，听从那引诱人的邪灵和鬼魔的道理。」（4:1）
- **「圣灵明说」：** 可能指旧约或耶稣的预言（参可13:22；徒20:29-30），也可能指教会中的先知所说的话。无论哪一种，保罗都说：这是圣灵预先告诉我们的。
- **「后来的时候」：** 新约常说，从基督第一次来到他再来之间的整个时期，就是「末后的日子」（参来1:2；约一2:18）。所以保罗说的不只是遥远的将来，以弗所的教会已经在经历这样的事。
- **「鬼魔的道理」：** 这些教训看起来很属灵，甚至很严格，但背后有属灵的欺骗。错误的教训不只是想法错了，也是属灵的争战。

**2. 「良心如同被热铁烙惯了一般」。** 「这是因为说谎之人的假冒；这等人的良心如同被热铁烙惯了一般。」（4:2）
- **「烙惯了」：** 这个词可以指被烙铁烫过、失去感觉的皮肤；也可以指奴隶或罪犯身上被烙的印记。两种意思都很严肃：一个人一次又一次违背良心，最后良心就麻木了，或者他就被打上了另一个主人的印记。
- **和第一章相连：** 保罗在1:19已经说过，有人丢弃良心，在真道上如同船破坏了一般。

**3. 「禁止嫁娶，又禁戒食物」。** 「他们禁止嫁娶，又禁戒食物，就是神所造、叫那信而明白真道的人感谢着领受的。」（4:3）
- **为什么禁止？** 这些人可能认为身体和物质是低等的，越远离它们就越属灵；也可能认为复活已经过去了（参提后2:18），信徒应该像天使一样不嫁不娶（参路20:35-36）。也可能和犹太人的洁净条例有关：保罗在提多书提到「犹太人荒谬的言语」之后，就说「在洁净的人，凡物都洁净」（多1:14-15；参西2:16）。这几种因素可能混在一起（参本书导读）。
- **保罗的回答：** 婚姻和食物都是「神所造」的（创1:31；2:24）。拒绝神所造的好东西，看起来很敬虔，其实是在否认创造主的美善（参西2:20-23）。

**4. 「若感谢着领受，就没有一样可弃的」。** 「凡神所造的物都是好的，若感谢着领受，就没有一样可弃的，都因神的道和人的祈求成为圣洁了。」（4:4-5）
- **「凡神所造的物都是好的」：** 这句话呼应创世记第1章：「神看着一切所造的都甚好。」（创1:31）马可记述耶稣的教导时，也加上一句：「各样的食物都是洁净的」（可7:19）。
- **「因神的道……成为圣洁」：** 「神的道」可能指神在创造时宣告万物都好的话，也可能指饭前祷告中引用的圣经，或者指福音宣告食物洁净的话（参可7:19；徒10:15）。
- **「和人的祈求」：** 就是感谢的祷告。饭前谢恩不是一种迷信的仪式，而是承认：这一切都是从神来的。
- **这不是说什么都可以：** 保罗在别处也说，若吃某样东西会叫软弱的弟兄跌倒，就宁可不吃（林前8:13）。保罗在这里所反对的，是把禁戒当作得救或更属灵的条件。
- **祭过的食物：** 按林前8-10章的原则，参与祭拜（上供、烧香、跪拜、在供桌前同吃）是「与鬼相交」，不可有分（林前10:20-21）；平常买来或别人请吃、没有宗教意义的食物，可以感谢着吃（林前10:25-27）；若有人特别说明这是祭过的，为了那人的良心就不吃（林前10:28）。
- **在华人处境中：** 有些人信主以前许过吃素的愿，或者觉得某些食物不洁净；有些教会把不喝酒、不吃某种东西当作属灵的记号。信主以前向菩萨或神明许的素愿，应当在主面前认罪、弃绝，它不再约束在基督里的人；他可以感谢着吃。若良心一时还不安，也可以暂时不吃，但不是为了还愿（罗14:6）。教会为了见证或保护软弱的人（例如在劝酒成风的场合，或为了帮助受酒瘾困扰的人），提倡不喝酒，是出于爱心的智慧；但若把它当作衡量属灵与否、甚至得救的标准，就落入了保罗所警告的错误。

> 要点：神所造的都是好的。真正的敬虔不是远离神的恩赐，而是在感谢中领受它，在每一顿饭、在家庭、在工作中，承认那位赐恩的主。

## 深读 4:6-10 在敬虔上操练自己

**1. 「基督耶稣的好执事」。** 「你若将这些事提醒弟兄们，便是基督耶稣的好执事，在真道的话语和你向来所服从的善道上得了教育。」（4:6）
- **「好执事」：** 这里的「执事」是一般的意思，就是服事的人，不是第三章的职分。
- **「得了教育」：** 原文是现在式，许多人认为这是强调要不断从真道得着喂养。一个人要喂养别人，自己先要天天吃神的话（参太4:4）。

**2. 「在敬虔上操练自己」。** 「只是要弃绝那世俗的言语和老妇荒谬的话，在敬虔上操练自己。」（4:7）
- **「老妇荒谬的话」：** 这是当时的一句俗语，指没有根据的无稽之谈，不是要轻看年长的妇女（参5:2）。
- **「操练」：** 原文是体育场上锻炼身体的那个词。运动员要每天练习，有计划，有节制（林前9:25）。敬虔也是这样，不是一时的热心，而是长期的操练：读经、祷告、敬拜、团契、服事、认罪悔改。
- **操练不是赚取恩典：** 我们不是靠操练得救，而是因为已经蒙恩，就操练自己，在恩典中长进（参提后2:1；彼后3:18）。操练的人不是在争取神的接纳，而是在学习以那位已经接纳他的神为乐，并且顺服他。

**3. 「操练身体，益处还少」。** 「『操练身体，益处还少；惟独敬虔，凡事都有益处，因有今生和来生的应许。』这话是可信的，是十分可佩服的。」（4:8-9）
- **「益处还少」：** 保罗没有说操练身体没有益处，而是说它的益处有限，只在今生。今天许多人很看重养生、健身、饮食，这些都有它的价值；但如果我们花在身体上的心思远远多过花在灵魂上的，就本末倒置了。
- **「今生和来生的应许」：** 敬虔不只是为了将来，也带来今生的益处：平安、知足、与神同行的喜乐（参6:6）。
- **哪一句是「可信的话」？** 解经家对「这话是可信的」指的是第8节还是第10节，看法不同。和合本把第8节放在引号里，采取前一种理解。主张指第8节的人指出，第10节开头有「因为」（和合本作「正是为此」），像是解释，而不像格言本身；第8节的对句形式也像一句格言。

**4. 「他是万人的救主，更是信徒的救主」。** 「我们劳苦努力，正是为此，因我们的指望在乎永生的神；他是万人的救主，更是信徒的救主。」（4:10）
- **「劳苦努力」：** 操练敬虔是辛苦的，但我们的盼望不在自己的努力上，而「在乎永生的神」。
- **「万人的救主，更是信徒的救主」：** 这句话有几种理解。
- **「更是」的意思：** 有些改革宗学者（如乔治·奈特）认为，「更是」在这里可以理解为进一步说明前一句：神是万人的救主，也就是说，他是信徒的救主。
- **普遍恩典：** 加尔文等改革宗解经家认为，神以普遍的恩典保守、供应所有的人（参太5:45；徒14:17），在这个意义上是万人的「救主」；但只有信的人才真正得着永远的救恩。
- **为万人预备的救恩：** 阿民念和卫斯理传统（如约翰·卫斯理；解经家如霍华德·马歇尔）认为，神为所有的人预备了救恩，愿意所有的人得救（参2:4），但只有信的人才真正领受。
- **共同的地方：** 这几种看法都不认为所有的人最后都必得救；保罗在同一章就说「有人离弃真道」（4:1），又说要「救自己，又能救听你的人」（4:16）。（参第二章对2:4、6的讨论。）

> 要点：敬虔需要操练，像运动员一样天天练习；但我们操练，不是为了赚取神的爱，而是因为我们的盼望在乎那位永生的神，他是信徒的救主。

## 深读 4:11-16 不可叫人小看你年轻

**1. 「不可叫人小看你年轻」。** 「这些事，你要吩咐人，也要教导人。不可叫人小看你年轻，总要在言语、行为、爱心、信心、清洁上，都作信徒的榜样。」（4:11-12）
- **提摩太有多年轻？** 他跟随保罗已有十几年，所以这时可能是三十多岁。在当时的社会，四十岁以下仍常被看作「年轻」，而以弗所的长老中很可能有比他年长的人。
- **怎样不叫人小看？** 保罗没有叫他用权柄压人，也没有叫他为自己辩护，而是叫他作榜样。这里有五个方面：言语、行为、爱心、信心、清洁。最好的回应不是争辩，而是生命。
- **华人教会的处境：** 我们的文化很看重辈分和年资。年轻的传道人和同工常常被说「你还年轻，不懂」。这段经文对年轻人说：不要因为年轻就退缩，也不要用骄傲来回应，要用生命作榜样（参耶1:6-7）。它也对年长的人说：不要因为一个人年轻就小看他，要看神在他身上所做的工。

**2. 「以宣读、劝勉、教导为念」。** 「你要以宣读、劝勉、教导为念，直等到我来。」（4:13）
- **「宣读」：** 指在聚会中公开诵读圣经。这是从犹太会堂继承下来的（参路4:16-21；尼8:8）。在许多人没有自己的圣经、也不识字的时代，公开读经非常重要。今天我们也不应该轻看在聚会中好好地读经。
- **「劝勉、教导」：** 读了经，还要讲解，使人明白，并劝人照着去行。

**3. 「不要轻忽所得的恩赐」。** 「你不要轻忽所得的恩赐，就是从前藉着预言、在众长老按手的时候赐给你的。」（4:14）
- **恩赐是神给的：** 提摩太的服事不是从他自己的能力开始的，而是神所赐的（参提后1:6）。
- **「轻忽」：** 恩赐不会自动长大，需要使用、挑旺。一个胆怯的人很容易因为别人的批评，就把神给的恩赐放在一边。
- **「众长老按手」：** 教会公开地承认、差派他（「藉着预言」，参1:18）。保罗在提后1:6说是藉着他自己按手；两处可能指同一件事，保罗和众长老一同按手。

**4. 「使众人看出你的长进来」。** 「这些事你要殷勤去做，并要在此专心，使众人看出你的长进来。」（4:15）神不要求提摩太一开始就完美，而是要他「长进」。人们看见的，不是一个没有缺点的人，而是一个在恩典中不断成长的人。

**5. 「又能救自己，又能救听你的人」。** 「你要谨慎自己和自己的教训，要在这些事上恒心；因为这样行，又能救自己，又能救听你的人。」（4:16）
- **先谨慎自己：** 保罗先说「自己」，再说「自己的教训」。一个传道人如果只顾教训，不顾自己的生命，最后两样都会失去（参林前9:27）。
- **「救自己」是什么意思？** 这不是说我们靠自己的努力得救。救恩完全是神的恩典（1:15-16；提后1:9）。这里的「救」，是指在信心中坚持到底，最后完全得救；神使用我们的恒心和他所托付的道，来保守我们和听道的人（参腓2:12-13）。我们得救的根据，是基督舍自己作万人的赎价（2:6）；恒心是得救之人的路，不是得救的代价。
- **听的人：** 传道人的生命和教导，关系到别人的灵魂。这是一个严肃的责任，也是一个荣耀的托付。

> 要点：神不要提摩太先变得年长，才开始服事；他要提摩太在恩典中长进，用生命作榜样，谨慎自己和所教导的道。神的恩赐和神的话，就足以使用一个年轻又胆怯的人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「不可叫人小看你年轻，总要在言语、行为、爱心、信心、清洁上，都作信徒的榜样。」（4:12）也许我正是那个被人小看的人：在教会里，因为年轻，我的意见常常没有人听；我刚开始服事，别人一句「你还年轻，不懂」，就让我想放弃。也许正相反，我是那个小看别人的人：看见一个年轻的弟兄姊妹上台，心里就先打了折扣，也许是因为我把自己的价值放在了资历上，一个年轻人做得好，好像就显得我不那么重要了；或者在家里，从来不认真听儿女说他们从神那里学到了什么。无论我是哪一种，我都可以想到耶稣。他开始传道的时候「年纪约有三十岁」（路3:23），在他长大的地方，人们说：「这不是那木匠吗？」「他们就厌弃他」（可6:3）。他被人小看，「我们也不尊重他」（赛53:3）。可是他没有用争辩来证明自己，而是一直走下去，走到十字架，为那些小看他的人舍命，也为我舍命。我被人小看的时候，可以记得：他比我更明白这种滋味，而父对他的喜悦从来没有因为人的轻看而减少。我小看别人的时候，要先向主认罪：我自己也曾是「不尊重他」的人，他却没有小看我。今天，我可以求主帮助我在一件小事上作个小小的榜样，不是为了证明自己，而是因为他的恩典；也可以去听一个比我年轻的人说话，听他讲神在他身上做了什么。那位曾被人厌弃的主，父已经叫他从死里复活；我和那个年轻人身上的「长进」（4:15），都是他亲自在做的工。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 falls into three parts. **Some will depart from the faith** (vv. 1–5): the Spirit expressly says that some will devote themselves to deceitful spirits and teachings of demons, forbidding marriage and requiring abstinence from foods; but everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving, for it is made holy by the word of God and prayer. **A good servant of Christ Jesus** (vv. 6–10): Timothy is to put these things before the brothers, have nothing to do with silly myths, and train himself for godliness; bodily training is of some value, but godliness is of value in every way; our hope is set on the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of those who believe. **Let no one despise you for your youth** (vv. 11–16): Timothy is to set the believers an example in speech, conduct, love, faith and purity; to devote himself to the public reading of Scripture, to exhortation and to teaching; not to neglect the gift he has; and to keep a close watch on himself and on the teaching.

Chapter 3 ended with "the mystery of godliness," which is Christ; chapter 4 turns to false godliness and true godliness. False godliness is a set of rules of abstinence; true godliness is, in Christ, receiving all that God gives with thanksgiving and training ourselves day by day to become more like him.

## Going Deeper: 4:1–5 Everything Created by God Is Good

**1. "In later times some will depart from the faith."** "Now the Spirit expressly says that in later times some will depart from the faith by devoting themselves to deceitful spirits and teachings of demons" (4:1).
- **"The Spirit expressly says":** this may refer to prophecies in the Old Testament or from Jesus (see Mark 13:22; Acts 20:29–30), or to words spoken by prophets in the church. Either way Paul says: the Spirit told us this in advance.
- **"In later times":** the New Testament often calls the whole period between Christ's first coming and his return "the last days" (see Heb 1:2; 1 John 2:18). So Paul is not speaking only of a distant future; the church in Ephesus was already living it.
- **"Teachings of demons":** these teachings look very spiritual, even very strict, but behind them lies spiritual deception. False teaching is not only an intellectual error; it is also spiritual warfare.

**2. "Whose consciences are seared."** "Through the insincerity of liars whose consciences are seared" (4:2).
- **"Seared":** the word can mean skin burned by a hot iron until it loses feeling; it can also mean the brand burned onto a slave or criminal. Both senses are sobering: someone who violates conscience again and again ends with a conscience gone numb, or bears the mark of another master.
- **The link with chapter 1:** Paul has already said in 1:19 that some have rejected conscience and made shipwreck of their faith.

**3. "Who forbid marriage and require abstinence from foods."** "Who forbid marriage and require abstinence from foods that God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and know the truth" (4:3).
- **Why forbid them?** These people may have thought the body and material things inferior, so that the further one kept from them the more spiritual one became; or they may have believed the resurrection had already happened (see 2 Tim 2:18), so that believers should be like the angels, who neither marry nor are given in marriage (see Luke 20:35–36). They may also have drawn on Jewish purity rules: right after "Jewish myths" Paul says "To the pure, all things are pure" (Titus 1:14–15; see Col 2:16). These strands may have been mixed together (see the introduction).
- **Paul's answer:** marriage and food are both things "God created" (Gen 1:31; 2:24). Rejecting the good things God made looks godly, but it actually denies the goodness of the Creator (see Col 2:20–23).

**4. "Nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving."** "For everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving, for it is made holy by the word of God and prayer" (4:4–5).
- **"Everything created by God is good":** this echoes Genesis 1: "God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good" (Gen 1:31). Mark notes that Jesus "declared all foods clean" (Mark 7:19).
- **"Made holy by the word of God":** "the word of God" may mean God's word in creation declaring all things good, or the Scripture quoted in a prayer before meals, or the gospel word that declares foods clean (Mark 7:19; Acts 10:15).
- **"And prayer":** that is, the prayer of thanksgiving. Giving thanks before a meal is not a superstitious ritual; it acknowledges that everything comes from God.
- **This does not mean anything goes:** Paul says elsewhere that if eating something will make a weak brother stumble, he would rather not eat it (1 Cor 8:13). What Paul opposes here is making abstinence a condition of salvation or of being more spiritual.
- **Food offered in worship:** in a Chinese setting, 1 Corinthians 8–10 gives the lines: taking part in the offering (setting out offerings, burning incense, bowing, eating at the altar table) is fellowship with demons and must be refused (1 Cor 10:20–21); ordinary food bought or served without religious meaning may be eaten with thanksgiving (10:25–27); if someone points out that it was offered, abstain for that person's conscience (10:28).
- **In a Chinese setting:** some people took a vegetarian vow before coming to faith, or feel certain foods are "unclean"; some churches treat not drinking or not eating certain things as a mark of spirituality. A vegetarian vow made to a bodhisattva or deity before conversion should be confessed and renounced before the Lord; it does not bind someone in Christ; he may eat with thanksgiving. If his conscience is not yet at rest he may abstain for a time, but not in order to keep the vow (Rom 14:6). A church that encourages abstaining from alcohol for the sake of witness or to protect the weak (for example amid pressure to drink at banquets, or where someone struggles with addiction) is exercising loving wisdom; but making it the measure of spirituality, or even of salvation, falls into the error Paul warns against.

> The point: Everything God made is good. True godliness does not keep away from God's gifts but receives them with thanksgiving, acknowledging the gracious Lord in every meal, every home and every job.

## Going Deeper: 4:6–10 Train Yourself for Godliness

**1. "A good servant of Christ Jesus."** "If you put these things before the brothers, you will be a good servant of Christ Jesus, being trained in the words of the faith and of the good doctrine that you have followed" (4:6).
- **"Servant":** the word is the general one for someone who serves, not the office of deacon in chapter 3.
- **"Being trained":** the Greek is a present participle, often taken to stress ongoing nourishment. Anyone who would feed others must first keep being fed by God's word (see Matt 4:4).

**2. "Train yourself for godliness."** "Have nothing to do with irreverent, silly myths. Rather train yourself for godliness" (4:7).
- **"Silly myths":** the Greek is literally "old wives' tales," a common expression of the time for baseless nonsense; it is not meant to belittle older women (see 5:2).
- **"Train":** the Greek is the word for exercising the body in the gymnasium. An athlete trains every day, with a plan and with self-control (1 Cor 9:25). So too with godliness: not a moment of zeal but long training: reading Scripture, prayer, worship, fellowship, service, confession and repentance.
- **Training does not earn grace:** we are not saved by training; because we have already received grace, we train ourselves to grow in it (see 2 Tim 2:1; 2 Pet 3:18). Someone in training is not striving for God's acceptance but learning to enjoy and obey the God who has already accepted him.

**3. "Bodily training is of some value."** "For while bodily training is of some value, godliness is of value in every way, as it holds promise for the present life and also for the life to come. The saying is trustworthy and deserving of full acceptance" (4:8–9).
- **"Of some value":** Paul does not say bodily training is worthless, only that its value is limited to this life. Many people today care a great deal about health, fitness and diet, and these have their value; but if we give far more thought to the body than to the soul, we have our priorities upside down.
- **"Promise for the present life and also for the life to come":** godliness is not only for the future; it brings benefits in this life as well: peace, contentment, the joy of walking with God (see 6:6).
- **Which is the "trustworthy saying"?** Commentators differ on whether "the saying is trustworthy" refers to verse 8 or verse 10. The CUV puts verse 8 in quotation marks, taking the first view. Those who take it as verse 8 note that verse 10 begins with "for," which reads like an explanation rather than the saying itself, and that verse 8 has the balanced form of a proverb. The ESV leaves the question open.

**4. "The Savior of all people, especially of those who believe."** "For to this end we toil and strive, because we have our hope set on the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of those who believe" (4:10).
- **"Toil and strive":** training in godliness is hard work, but our hope is not in our own effort; it is "set on the living God."
- **"The Savior of all people, especially of those who believe":** this phrase is read in several ways.
- **"Especially" as "that is":** some Reformed scholars (such as George Knight) think "especially" can here mean "that is": God is the Savior of all people, namely, of those who believe.
- **Common grace:** Calvin and other Reformed interpreters hold that God preserves and provides for all people by common grace (see Matt 5:45; Acts 14:17), and in that sense is the "Savior" of all, but that only those who believe actually receive eternal salvation.
- **Salvation provided for all:** the Arminian and Wesleyan traditions (for example John Wesley; among commentators, I. Howard Marshall) hold that God has provided salvation for all and desires all to be saved (see 2:4), but that only those who believe actually receive it.
- **Common ground:** none of these views holds that all people will finally be saved; in this same chapter Paul says "some will depart from the faith" (4:1) and speaks of saving "both yourself and your hearers" (4:16). (See the discussion of 2:4, 6.)

> The point: Godliness takes training, daily practice like an athlete's. But we train not to earn God's love; we train because our hope is set on the living God, who is the Savior of those who believe.

## Going Deeper: 4:11–16 Let No One Despise You for Your Youth

**1. "Let no one despise you for your youth."** "Command and teach these things. Let no one despise you for your youth, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity" (4:11–12).
- **How young was Timothy?** He had been with Paul for over a decade, so he may now have been in his thirties. In that society men under forty were often still considered "young," and the Ephesian elders likely included men older than he was.
- **How does one avoid being despised?** Paul does not tell him to use authority to pressure people or to defend himself, but to be an example in five areas: speech, conduct, love, faith, purity. The best answer is not argument but a life.
- **In Chinese churches:** our culture values seniority and years of service. Young pastors and coworkers often hear, "You're still young; you don't understand." This passage tells the young: do not shrink back because you are young, and do not answer with pride; be an example with your life (see Jer 1:6–7). It also tells the older: do not despise someone for being young; look at what God is doing in him.

**2. "Devote yourself to the public reading of Scripture, to exhortation, to teaching."** "Until I come, devote yourself to the public reading of Scripture, to exhortation, to teaching" (4:13).
- **"Public reading":** reading Scripture aloud in the gathering, a practice inherited from the synagogue (see Luke 4:16–21; Neh 8:8). In an age when many had no Bible of their own and could not read, public reading was vital. Today too we should not neglect reading Scripture well when we gather.
- **"Exhortation, teaching":** after the reading comes explanation, so that people understand, and exhortation, so that they act on it.

**3. "Do not neglect the gift you have."** "Do not neglect the gift you have, which was given you by prophecy when the council of elders laid their hands on you" (4:14).
- **The gift is God's:** Timothy's ministry did not begin with his own ability; God gave it (see 2 Tim 1:6).
- **"Neglect":** a gift does not grow by itself; it must be used and fanned into flame. A timid person can easily set aside God's gift because of others' criticism.
- **"The council of elders laid their hands on you":** the church publicly recognized and sent him ("by prophecy," see 1:18). In 2 Tim 1:6 Paul speaks of his own laying on of hands; the two may describe the same event, with Paul and the elders laying hands on him together.

**4. "So that all may see your progress."** "Practice these things, devote yourself to them, so that all may see your progress" (4:15). God did not require Timothy to be perfect from the start, but to "progress." What people see is not someone without flaws but someone growing steadily in grace.

**5. "You will save both yourself and your hearers."** "Keep a close watch on yourself and on the teaching. Persist in this, for by so doing you will save both yourself and your hearers" (4:16).
- **Watch yourself first:** Paul says "yourself" before "the teaching." A minister who attends only to his teaching and not to his own life will in the end lose both (see 1 Cor 9:27).
- **What does "save yourself" mean?** It does not mean we are saved by our own effort. Salvation is wholly God's grace (1:15–16; 2 Tim 1:9). "Save" here means persevering in faith to the end and being finally saved; God uses our perseverance and the word he has entrusted to us to keep us and our hearers (see Phil 2:12–13). The ground of our salvation is Christ, who gave himself as a ransom (2:6); perseverance is the road the saved walk, not the price of being saved.
- **The hearers:** a minister's life and teaching bear on other people's souls. This is a solemn responsibility and a glorious trust.

> The point: God did not make Timothy wait until he was older to begin serving; he called him to grow in grace, to be an example with his life, and to keep watch over himself and the word he taught. God's gift and God's word are enough to use someone young and timid.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Let no one despise you for your youth, but set the believers an example in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity" (4:12). Perhaps I am the one being despised: in church my opinions are seldom heard because I am young; I have just begun to serve, and one remark, "You're still young; you don't understand," makes me want to quit. Or perhaps it is the other way around, and I am the one who despises others: when a young brother or sister stands up front, I have already marked them down in my heart, perhaps because I have put my worth in my seniority, and a young person doing well seems to make me matter less; or at home I never really listen when my children tell me what they have learned from God. Whichever I am, I can think of Jesus. When he began his ministry he "was about thirty years of age" (Luke 3:23), and in the town where he grew up people said, "Is not this the carpenter?" "And they took offense at him" (Mark 6:3). He was despised, "and we esteemed him not" (Isa 53:3). Yet he did not argue to prove himself; he kept going, all the way to the cross, and laid down his life for those who despised him, and for me. When I am despised, I can remember that he knows the taste of it better than I do, and that the Father's delight in him never lessened because people looked down on him. When I look down on others, I confess it to him: I too was among those who "esteemed him not," yet he did not look down on me. Today I can ask the Lord to let me, in one small thing, be a small example, not to prove myself but because of his grace; and I can go and listen to someone younger than I am, and hear what God has been doing in their life. The Lord who was once despised, the Father has raised from the dead; the "progress" (4:15) in me and in that younger person is his own work.
