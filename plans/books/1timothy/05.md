## 本章脉络

第五章分成四段。**在神的家中彼此对待**（1-2节）：要劝老年人如同父亲，劝少年人如同弟兄，劝老年妇女如同母亲，劝少年妇女如同姊妹，总要清清洁洁的。**寡妇**（3-16节）：要尊敬那真为寡妇的；寡妇若有儿女或孙子孙女，他们要先在自己家中行孝；那独居无靠的寡妇是仰赖神，昼夜祈求；记在册子上的寡妇要年满六十岁，有行善的名声；年轻的寡妇最好再嫁；信主的妇女家中若有寡妇，要自己救济她们。**长老**（17-25节）：善于管理的长老配受加倍的敬奉；控告长老要有两三个见证；犯罪的要在众人面前责备；不可有偏心；按手不可急促；有些人的罪和善行是明显的，有些是随后显出来的。中间还插进一句保罗对提摩太个人的关心：可以稍微用点酒，因为他屡次患病（23节）。

这一章看起来是一些实际的安排：怎样照顾寡妇，怎样对待长老。但每一条都建立在3:15的真理上：教会是神的家。一个家，要照顾软弱的，也要尊重带领的；要有爱心，也要有公义。

## 深读 5:1-2 如同父亲，如同弟兄

**1. 「不可严责老年人，只要劝他如同父亲」。** 「不可严责老年人，只要劝他如同父亲；劝少年人如同弟兄；劝老年妇女如同母亲；劝少年妇女如同姊妹；总要清清洁洁的。」（5:1-2）
- **「严责」：** 原文是用很重的话斥责。提摩太是教会的带领人，但对年长的人，即使需要劝诫，也要带着尊重，像儿子劝父亲一样。
- **这是一个家：** 保罗用了四个家人的称呼：父亲、弟兄、母亲、姊妹。在教会里，我们不是同事，也不是顾客，而是一家人（参可3:34-35）。
- **「总要清清洁洁的」：** 对少年妇女，要像对亲姊妹一样，在言语、行为和心思上都清洁。教会的带领人尤其要谨慎，不可利用牧养的关系，在情感或身体上越过界线。

> 要点：教会是神的家。我们成为一家人，是因为基督把我们带到同一位父面前；所以就连劝诫和纠正，也要带着家人的尊重和清洁的爱。

## 深读 5:3-8 尊敬那真为寡妇的

**1. 「要尊敬那真为寡妇的」。** 「要尊敬那真为寡妇的。」（5:3）
- **「尊敬」：** 这里不只是心里的尊重，也包括实际的供养（参5:17-18「敬奉」）。在当时，寡妇如果没有家人照顾，往往陷入贫穷。旧约一再吩咐以色列人照顾孤儿寡妇（申24:19-21），神自己也是「作寡妇的伸冤者」（诗68:5）。初期教会一开始就照顾寡妇（徒6:1；9:39）。
- **「真为寡妇的」：** 保罗接下来解释，就是没有家人可以依靠的寡妇。

**2. 「先在自己家中学着行孝」。** 「若寡妇有儿女，或有孙子孙女，便叫他们先在自己家中学着行孝，报答亲恩，因为这在神面前是可悦纳的。」（5:4）
- **「行孝」：** 原文是「敬虔」的动词形式。保罗把孝敬父母看作在家里活出敬虔。敬虔不只在聚会中，也在家里。
- **「报答亲恩」：** 父母曾经养育我们，现在我们照顾年老的父母，这是神的心意（出20:12）。耶稣曾经责备那些以「各耳板」为借口、不奉养父母的人（可7:10-13）。
- **和华人文化的关系：** 华人文化很重视孝道，这一点和圣经的吩咐相近，但根基不同。儒家的孝以人伦为本，也包括祭祖；圣经的孝道是「在神面前」的：我们孝敬父母，是因为敬畏神、遵行他的诫命（出20:12）。孝道不能高过神（太10:37），也不包括祭祖、烧香跪拜这些违背信仰的事；信徒可以用追思礼拜、扫墓献花来纪念先人、感谢神；而尽孝最要紧的，是好好照顾在世的长辈。另一方面，信主的人也不可以「属灵」为借口，忽略对父母的实际照顾。

**3. 「那独居无靠、真为寡妇的，是仰赖神」。** 「那独居无靠、真为寡妇的，是仰赖神，昼夜不住地祈求祷告。但那好宴乐的寡妇正活着的时候也是死的。」（5:5-6）
- **「仰赖神」：** 一个没有人可以依靠的人，把她的盼望放在神身上。路加福音中的亚拿就是这样一位寡妇：她「并不离开圣殿，禁食祈求，昼夜事奉神」（路2:37）。
- **「好宴乐的」：** 指放纵享乐、只为自己活的人。保罗说她「正活着的时候也是死的」，就是外表活着，灵里却已经死了（参启3:1）。

**4. 「比不信的人还不好」。** 「人若不看顾亲属，就是背了真道，比不信的人还不好，不看顾自己家里的人，更是如此。」（5:8）这是全章最重的一句话。连不信的人都知道要照顾自己的家人；一个自称信主的人若不照顾，就是在行为上否认了信仰。照顾家人不只是文化的要求，也是信仰的见证。若父母曾经虐待儿女，儿女仍可以用安全的方式、保持适当的距离来尽奉养的责任，不必回到危险之中。有些人身在海外，有些人自己身体不好，有些人经济实在困难，没有办法亲自照顾父母，心里常常内疚。保罗责备的是不肯看顾的人，不是有心无力的人；看顾可以有许多方式：常常打电话、尽量回去探望、在经济上支持、托可靠的人就近照顾。真有亏欠的地方，就向主认罪，他必赦免；力有不逮的地方，就交托给主，求他指引一条走得通的路。

> 要点：照顾年老和孤单的人，是神家的标记。孝敬父母是在神面前的敬虔；无依无靠的寡妇，有神自己作她们的依靠，教会也要成为她们的家。

## 深读 5:9-16 寡妇的名册

**1. 「寡妇记在册子上」。** 「寡妇记在册子上，必须年纪到六十岁，从来只作一个丈夫的妻子，又有行善的名声，就如养育儿女，接待远人，洗圣徒的脚，救济遭难的人，竭力行各样善事。」（5:9-10）
- **这名册是什么？** 学者们有不同的看法。有人认为，这是教会长期供养的寡妇的名单；也有人认为，这是一群在教会中专门服事（如祷告、探访、接待）的寡妇，她们由教会供养，也承诺委身于这样的服事。后来的初期教会中，确实有一个专门服事的「寡妇」群体。
- **「从来只作一个丈夫的妻子」：** 和3:2对监督的要求相对应（参第三章的几种看法）。有人认为是指一生只结过一次婚；许多人认为是指在婚姻中忠诚：保罗在第14节劝年轻的寡妇再嫁，他不会因为她们听从了这个劝告，日后就把她们排除在名册之外。
- **「洗圣徒的脚」：** 指谦卑地服事别人，可能也包括实际地接待旅行中的信徒。耶稣自己为门徒洗脚（约13:14-15）。

**2. 「至于年轻的寡妇」。** 「至于年轻的寡妇，就可以辞她；因为她们的情欲发动，违背基督的时候就想要嫁人。她们被定罪，是因废弃了当初所许的愿」（5:11-12）。
- **「当初所许的愿」：** 原文这个词一般译作「信」。许多解经家认为这里指一种誓约：记在名册上的寡妇承诺专心服事基督、不再嫁人，后来若想再嫁，就违背了这个誓约；也有解经家认为是指离弃起初对基督的信，例如嫁给不信的人，或跟从假教师（参5:15「已经有转去随从撒但的」）。
- **保罗不是反对再嫁：** 他在第14节说：「我愿意年轻的寡妇嫁人，生养儿女，治理家务。」问题不在于再婚，而在于许了愿又背弃，以及在第13节所说的「挨家闲游……说长道短」。这可能也和假教师有关，他们在各家中传异教（参提后3:6）。
- **今天的意义：** 这不是说年轻的寡妇在属灵上比较软弱，而是保罗出于牧者的智慧，不让她们过早许下一个难以持守的誓愿。

**3. 「信主的妇女，若家中有寡妇」。** 「信主的妇女，若家中有寡妇，自己就当救济她们，不可累着教会，好使教会能救济那真无倚靠的寡妇。」（5:16）家人能照顾的，先由家人照顾；这样，教会有限的资源就能用在最需要的人身上。

> 要点：教会对寡妇的照顾既有爱心，也有智慧：家人先尽本分，教会照顾真正无依无靠的人，也尊重那些一生忠心服事的人。

## 深读 5:17-25 对待长老

**1. 「配受加倍的敬奉」。** 「那善于管理教会的长老，当以为配受加倍的敬奉；那劳苦传道教导人的，更当如此。因为经上说：『牛在场上踹谷的时候，不可笼住它的嘴』；又说：『工人得工价是应当的。』」（5:17-18）
- **「加倍的敬奉」：** 「敬奉」和第3节的「尊敬」在原文是同一个词根，包括尊重和经济上的供给。从第18节引用的经文看，这里显然包括工价。「加倍」可能是指尊重加上供给，也可能只是说丰厚的敬奉，不一定是精确的两倍工资。
- **「劳苦传道教导人的」：** 这可能表示，在长老中有一些人特别专注于讲道和教导。
- **两处引用：** 第一句出自申25:4（参林前9:9）。第二句和路10:7耶稣的话在原文中几乎一字不差。对「经上说」的范围有两种看法：许多学者认为，「经上说」同时引出这两句。若是这样，保罗已经把耶稣的话当作与旧约同等的圣经。这句话可能出自已经写成的路加福音（路加正是保罗的同工，参提后4:11），这也和路加福音较早成书的看法相合（参彼后3:16）。另一些学者认为「经上说」只引出第一句，「又说」引的是口传的主的话。无论哪一种，保罗都把主的话当作有权威的吩咐（林前9:14）。
- **华人教会的处境：** 有些教会觉得传道人清贫才属灵，给的生活费很少，甚至不够养家。保罗说，善于带领、劳苦教导的人，配受加倍的敬奉。尊重传道人，也包括让他们一家能够安心生活。

**2. 「控告长老的呈子，非有两三个见证就不要收」。** 「控告长老的呈子，非有两三个见证就不要收。犯罪的人，当在众人面前责备他，叫其余的人也可以惧怕。」（5:19-20）
- **保护长老：** 「两三个见证」出自申19:15（参太18:16）。带领人常常受人批评，教会不可以凭一个人的话，就随便定一位长老的罪。
- **但不可成为掩护：** 这条原则是要防止诬告，不是要保护犯罪的人。性侵和虐待常常发生在没有旁人的地方；但几位受害者各自的陈述、书面记录和其他证据，都可以成为「见证」。教会不可以用「没有两三个见证」为理由，拒绝认真调查对带领人的指控。「两三个见证」是教会处理控告的原则，不是报警的前提：涉及犯罪的事，应当向警方报案，不可只在教会内部处理；涉及儿童或性侵的事，不必等教会查清楚才报案（参罗13:4）。受害者也不必勉强与不安全的人修复关系，或继续留在危险中。即使犯罪的带领人真心悔改、受害者也饶恕了他，饶恕也不等于恢复他的职分，更不等于让他再接触受害者或儿童。
- **「当在众人面前责备他」：** 「犯罪的人」原文是现在分词，许多解经家认为是指那些继续犯罪、不肯悔改的长老。带领人的罪经过查证属实，教会就要在众人面前处理，不可因为事情原本隐秘，或为了顾全面子，就把它掩盖（「家丑不可外扬」不是圣经的原则）。这样，「其余的人也可以惧怕」，就是存敬畏的心，不敢轻看罪。

**3. 「不可存成见，行事也不可有偏心」。** 「我在神和基督耶稣并蒙拣选的天使面前嘱咐你：要遵守这些话，不可存成见，行事也不可有偏心。」（5:21）保罗用非常严肃的话嘱咐提摩太：处理这些事的时候，不可因为一个人有名、有钱、有恩赐，或者是自己的朋友，就偏袒他（参雅2:1、9）；也不可因为对一个人有成见，就先定了他的罪。

**4. 「给人行按手的礼，不可急促」。** 「给人行按手的礼，不可急促；不要在别人的罪上有分，要保守自己清洁。」（5:22）多数人认为，这是指按立长老。如果教会急着把一个还没有经过考验的人放在带领的位置上，他后来犯了罪，按立他的人也有责任（参3:6、10）。这也适用于犯过罪的带领人：不可急着恢复他的职分。（也有少数人认为，这里的按手是指接纳悔改的人重回教会。）

**5. 「可以稍微用点酒」。** 「因你胃口不清，屡次患病，再不要照常喝水，可以稍微用点酒。」（5:23）
- **一句个人的关心：** 在这些严肃的嘱咐中间，保罗突然关心起提摩太的胃。他不只是发出嘱咐的使徒，也是一位属灵的父亲（参1:2）。
- **为什么要说这话？** 有人认为，提摩太完全不喝酒，可能有几个原因：受了当时禁戒风气的影响；不想让主张禁戒的假教师抓到把柄，说他放纵；也要在「不因酒滋事」（3:3）上作榜样。保罗说：为了身体的缘故，可以稍微用点酒。当时的水常常不干净，掺酒的水可能比较安全。
- **这不是鼓励多喝酒：** 「稍微」这个词很重要。保罗在别处也说「不要醉酒」（弗5:18）。他在这里所反对的，是把禁戒当作敬虔的标准，而忽略了身体的需要。

**6. 「有些人的罪是明显的」。** 「有些人的罪是明显的，如同先到审判案前；有些人的罪是随后跟了去的。这样，善行也有明显的，那不明显的也不能隐藏。」（5:24-25）选立带领人的时候，有些人的问题一眼就看得出来，有些人的问题要过一段时间才显出来。所以「不可急促」（5:22）。同样，有些人的善行很明显，有些人默默地服事，没有人注意；但神都看见，最后也都要显明出来（参太6:4）。

> 要点：神的家要尊重忠心的带领人，也要按公义处理带领人的罪；不可诬告，不可掩盖，不可偏心。在这一切背后，是那位在暗中察看、最后要显明一切的神。

## 默想

**默想：** 「那独居无靠、真为寡妇的，是仰赖神，昼夜不住地祈求祷告。」（5:5）有些人的生活走到一个地步，好像只剩下祷告了：丈夫或妻子走了，儿女在远方，身体也不如从前，能做的事越来越少。也许我就是这样的人：一天里大部分的时间，屋子里都很安静，我常常觉得孤单，有时连祷告都觉得空空的。路加福音记载了一位寡妇亚拿，她「现在已经八十四岁，并不离开圣殿，禁食祈求，昼夜事奉神」（路2:37）。几十年的祷告，看起来没有什么结果；可是有一天，马利亚和约瑟抱着婴孩耶稣进了圣殿，「正当那时，她进前来称谢神，将孩子的事对一切盼望耶路撒冷得救赎的人讲说」（路2:38）。这些年日的祷告，并不是她做不了别的事、才剩下来的一件事；那些年日，是她与神同在的日子。她最后得着的，不是别人重新觉得她有用，而是亲眼看见了她所盼望的那一位。那个婴孩后来长大，在十字架上替我们担当罪；临死的时候，他还看见站在十字架下的母亲，把她交托给所爱的门徒照顾（约19:26-27）。他自己最痛的时候，还记挂着母亲往后有人看顾。「神在他的圣所作孤儿的父，作寡妇的伸冤者。神叫孤独的有家」（诗68:5-6）。今天，我可以老实告诉主：我常常觉得孤单，有时连祷告都觉得空空的；然后像亚拿一样，在祷告中等候他，因为我所等候的，不是自己重新变得有用，而是主自己。如果我认识这样一位年长的弟兄姊妹，我也可以去问他：我能为你祷告什么？你也愿意为我祷告吗？说不定，主正要藉着我，叫一个孤独的人有家。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 falls into four parts. **How to treat one another in God's household** (vv. 1–2): encourage an older man as a father, younger men as brothers, older women as mothers, younger women as sisters, in all purity. **Widows** (vv. 3–16): honor widows who are truly widows; if a widow has children or grandchildren, they are first to show godliness to their own household; the widow left all alone has set her hope on God and prays night and day; a widow to be enrolled must be at least sixty, with a reputation for good works; younger widows had better remarry; a believing woman with widows among her relatives is to care for them herself. **Elders** (vv. 17–25): elders who rule well are worthy of double honor; a charge against an elder needs two or three witnesses; those who persist in sin are to be rebuked in the presence of all; there must be no partiality; do not be hasty in laying on hands; the sins and good works of some are conspicuous, while others appear later. In the middle comes a personal word of care from Paul to Timothy: use a little wine, because of his frequent ailments (v. 23).

This chapter looks like a set of practical arrangements: how to care for widows, how to treat elders. But each instruction rests on the truth of 3:15: the church is God's household. A household cares for the weak and honors those who lead; it has both love and justice.

## Going Deeper: 5:1–2 As Fathers, as Brothers

**1. "Do not rebuke an older man but encourage him as you would a father."** "Do not rebuke an older man but encourage him as you would a father. Treat younger men like brothers, older women like mothers, younger women like sisters, in all purity" (5:1–2).
- **"Rebuke":** the Greek means to reprimand harshly. Timothy is the church's leader, but even when an older man needs correcting, Timothy is to speak with respect, as a son would appeal to his father.
- **This is a family:** Paul uses four family words: father, brothers, mothers, sisters. In the church we are not colleagues or customers but family (see Mark 3:34–35).
- **"In all purity":** toward younger women he is to act as toward his own sisters, pure in word, deed and thought. Church leaders especially must take care never to use a pastoral relationship to cross emotional or physical boundaries.

> The point: The church is God's household. We are family because Christ has brought us to the same Father; so even correction and rebuke are to carry the respect of family and the purity of love.

## Going Deeper: 5:3–8 Honor Widows Who Are Truly Widows

**1. "Honor widows who are truly widows."** "Honor widows who are truly widows" (5:3).
- **"Honor":** not only inward respect but practical support (see 5:17–18, "double honor"). In that world a widow without family to care for her often fell into poverty. The Old Testament repeatedly commanded Israel to care for the fatherless and the widow (Deut 24:19–21), and God himself is the "protector of widows" (Ps 68:5). The early church cared for widows from the very beginning (Acts 6:1; 9:39).
- **"Truly widows":** Paul goes on to explain: widows with no family to depend on.

**2. "Let them first learn to show godliness to their own household."** "But if a widow has children or grandchildren, let them first learn to show godliness to their own household and to make some return to their parents, for this is pleasing in the sight of God" (5:4).
- **"Show godliness":** the Greek is the verb form of "godliness." Paul calls caring for one's parents the practice of godliness at home. Godliness is not only in the gathering but in the home.
- **"Make some return to their parents":** our parents raised us; now caring for them in old age is God's will (Exod 20:12). Jesus rebuked those who used "Corban" as an excuse not to support their parents (Mark 7:10–13).
- **In relation to Chinese culture:** Chinese filial piety resembles the biblical command but rests on a different foundation: Confucian human relations, which include ancestral rites. Biblical filial piety is "in the sight of God": we honor our parents because we fear God and keep his command (Exod 20:12); filial duty cannot rank above God (Matt 10:37), and it does not include ancestor rites, burning incense or bowing, or other things contrary to the faith; believers can remember the departed through memorial services and flowers at the grave, giving thanks to God; but filial duty is above all caring for living elders. On the other hand, believers must not use "spiritual" reasons as an excuse to neglect the practical care of their parents.

**3. "She who is truly a widow, left all alone, has set her hope on God."** "She who is truly a widow, left all alone, has set her hope on God and continues in supplications and prayers night and day, but she who is self-indulgent is dead even while she lives" (5:5–6).
- **"Set her hope on God":** someone with no one else to lean on puts her hope in God. Anna in Luke's Gospel was such a widow: "She did not depart from the temple, worshiping with fasting and prayer night and day" (Luke 2:37).
- **"Self-indulgent":** someone given to pleasure and living for herself. Paul says she "is dead even while she lives," alive outwardly but dead in spirit (see Rev 3:1).

**4. "Worse than an unbeliever."** "But if anyone does not provide for his relatives, and especially for members of his household, he has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever" (5:8). This is the weightiest sentence in the chapter. Even unbelievers know to care for their own families; someone who claims to believe and does not is denying the faith by his conduct. Caring for one's family is not only a cultural expectation but a witness of faith. Where a parent has been abusive, provision can be made safely and at a distance; it does not require returning to danger. Some, living abroad, ill themselves or in real financial hardship, cannot care for their parents in person and carry constant guilt. Paul rebukes those who will not provide, not those who cannot do all they wish. Provision can take many forms: regular calls, visits when possible, financial support, entrusting care to someone reliable. Where we have truly failed, we can confess it to the Lord, and he forgives; where we simply cannot do more, we can entrust it to him and ask him to show us a way forward.

> The point: Caring for the old and the lonely is a mark of God's household. Honoring parents is godliness before God; and for those with no one to lean on, God himself is their support, and the church is to become their family.

## Going Deeper: 5:9–16 The List of Widows

**1. "Let a widow be enrolled."** "Let a widow be enrolled if she is not less than sixty years of age, having been the wife of one husband, and having a reputation for good works: if she has brought up children, has shown hospitality, has washed the feet of the saints, has cared for the afflicted, and has devoted herself to every good work" (5:9–10).
- **What was this list?** Scholars differ. Some think it was a list of widows the church supported long term; others think it was a group of widows devoted to particular service in the church (such as prayer, visiting and hospitality), supported by the church and committed to that service. The early church did later have "widows" as a recognized group of servants.
- **"The wife of one husband":** this parallels 3:2 (see the views set out in chapter 3). Some take it to mean married only once; many take it to mean faithfulness in marriage, since in verse 14 Paul urges younger widows to remarry and would hardly bar them from the list later for doing so.
- **"Washed the feet of the saints":** humble service of others, perhaps including practical hospitality to traveling believers. Jesus himself washed his disciples' feet (John 13:14–15).

**2. "But refuse to enroll younger widows."** "But refuse to enroll younger widows, for when their passions draw them away from Christ, they desire to marry and so incur condemnation for having abandoned their former faith" (5:11–12).
- **"Their former faith":** the CUV reads "the vow they first made." The Greek word is usually "faith." Many commentators take it here as a pledge: enrolled widows promised to devote themselves to Christ's service and not remarry, so a widow who later wished to remarry would break that pledge. Others take it as abandoning their first faith in Christ, for example by marrying an unbeliever or following the false teachers (see 5:15, "some have already strayed after Satan").
- **Paul is not against remarriage:** in verse 14 he says, "I would have younger widows marry, bear children, manage their households." The problem is not remarriage but making a pledge and then abandoning it, along with what verse 13 describes: "going about from house to house... gossips and busybodies." This may also be connected with the false teachers, who spread their teaching from house to house (see 2 Tim 3:6).
- **What it means today:** this does not mean younger widows are spiritually weaker; it is Paul's pastoral wisdom, not wanting them to take on too early a vow that would be hard to keep.

**3. "If any believing woman has relatives who are widows."** "If any believing woman has relatives who are widows, let her care for them. Let the church not be burdened, so that it may care for those who are really widows" (5:16). What the family can do, the family does first; then the church's limited resources can go to those in greatest need.

> The point: The church's care for widows combines love with wisdom: the family does its part first, the church cares for those with no one to lean on, and it honors those who have served faithfully all their lives.

## Going Deeper: 5:17–25 How to Treat Elders

**1. "Worthy of double honor."** "Let the elders who rule well be considered worthy of double honor, especially those who labor in preaching and teaching. For the Scripture says, 'You shall not muzzle an ox when it treads out the grain,' and, 'The laborer deserves his wages'" (5:17–18).
- **"Double honor":** "honor" is from the same root as "honor" in verse 3, including both respect and financial support. The verses quoted in verse 18 show that wages are clearly included. "Double" may mean respect plus material support, or simply generous honor; it need not mean exactly twice the pay.
- **"Those who labor in preaching and teaching":** this may indicate that among the elders some gave themselves especially to preaching and teaching.
- **Two quotations:** the first is from Deut 25:4 (see 1 Cor 9:9). The second agrees almost word for word in Greek with Jesus' saying in Luke 10:7. There are two views of how far "the Scripture says" reaches. Many scholars think it introduces both quotations: Paul already counts Jesus' words, perhaps the written Gospel of Luke (Luke was Paul's companion, 2 Tim 4:11), as Scripture alongside the Old Testament; this fits an early date for Luke (see also 2 Pet 3:16). Others think "the Scripture says" introduces only the first quotation, and "and" introduces a saying of the Lord known orally. Either way, Paul treats the Lord's word as authoritative command (1 Cor 9:14).
- **In Chinese churches:** some churches feel that a pastor's poverty is a sign of spirituality and pay so little that it cannot support a family. Paul says that those who lead well and labor in teaching are worthy of double honor. Honoring ministers includes enabling them and their families to live without anxiety.

**2. "Do not admit a charge against an elder except on the evidence of two or three witnesses."** "Do not admit a charge against an elder except on the evidence of two or three witnesses. As for those who persist in sin, rebuke them in the presence of all, so that the rest may stand in fear" (5:19–20).
- **Protecting elders:** "two or three witnesses" comes from Deut 19:15 (see Matt 18:16). Leaders often face criticism, and the church must not condemn an elder on one person's word.
- **But never a shield:** this principle exists to prevent false accusation, not to protect wrongdoers. In many cases of sexual abuse and other abuse, things happen where no one else is present; yet the separate testimonies of several victims, written records and other evidence can all serve as "witnesses." A church must not use "there are not two or three witnesses" as a reason to refuse a serious investigation of an accusation against a leader. Where a crime is involved, it should be reported to the police, not handled only inside the church; and victims do not need to reconcile with someone unsafe or stay in danger. "Two or three witnesses" is a rule for how the church receives a charge; it is not a precondition for going to the police. Where children or sexual abuse are involved, do not wait for the church to finish its own inquiry before reporting (see Rom 13:4). Even if an offending leader truly repents and the victim forgives him, forgiveness does not restore his office, nor does it give him access to victims or children again.
- **"Rebuke them in the presence of all":** "those who persist in sin" translates a present participle, and many commentators take it to mean elders who go on sinning and will not repent. When a charge against a leader has been established, the church must deal with it before all, and must not cover it up because it happened in private or to save face ("don't air the family's shame outside," 家丑不可外扬, is not a biblical principle). Then "the rest may stand in fear," that is, hold God in awe and not take sin lightly.

**3. "Without prejudging, doing nothing from partiality."** "In the presence of God and of Christ Jesus and of the elect angels I charge you to keep these rules without prejudging, doing nothing from partiality" (5:21). Paul charges Timothy in the most solemn terms: in handling such matters, he must not favor someone because he is famous, wealthy, gifted or a friend (see Jas 2:1, 9); nor may he condemn someone in advance because of a prejudice against him.

**4. "Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands."** "Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands, nor take part in the sins of others; keep yourself pure" (5:22). Most take this to refer to ordaining elders. If a church rushes someone untested into leadership and he later falls into sin, those who appointed him share responsibility (see 3:6, 10). This applies also to a leader who has fallen: do not be hasty to restore him to office. (A minority take the laying on of hands here as restoring penitent sinners to fellowship.)

**5. "Use a little wine."** "(No longer drink only water, but use a little wine for the sake of your stomach and your frequent ailments)" (5:23).
- **A personal word of care:** in the middle of these solemn instructions, Paul suddenly shows concern for Timothy's stomach. He is not only an apostle giving orders but a spiritual father (see 1:2).
- **Why say this?** Some think Timothy may have been influenced by the ascetic climate, or wanted to give the ascetic false teachers no ground to accuse him of laxity, and to be an example of "not a drunkard" (3:3), and so abstained from wine entirely. Paul says: for your health, use a little wine. Water at the time was often unsafe, and water mixed with wine was probably safer.
- **This is no license for heavy drinking:** the word "little" matters. Elsewhere Paul says, "do not get drunk with wine" (Eph 5:18). What he opposes here is making abstinence the standard of godliness while neglecting the body's needs.

**6. "The sins of some men are conspicuous."** "The sins of some men are conspicuous, going before them to judgment, but the sins of others appear later. So also good works are conspicuous, and even those that are not cannot remain hidden" (5:24–25). In choosing leaders, some people's problems are obvious at a glance; others' appear only with time. That is why one must not be "hasty" (5:22). Likewise, some people's good works are plain to see, while others serve quietly and no one notices; but God sees it all, and in the end it will all come to light (see Matt 6:4).

> The point: God's household honors faithful leaders and deals justly with leaders' sins: no false accusation, no cover-up, no partiality. Behind all of it stands the God who sees in secret and will at last bring everything to light.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "She who is truly a widow, left all alone, has set her hope on God and continues in supplications and prayers night and day" (5:5). Some people reach a point in life where prayer seems to be all that is left: a husband or wife has died, the children live far away, the body is not what it was, and there is less and less one can do. Perhaps I am such a person: for most of the day the house is quiet; I am often lonely, and sometimes even my prayers feel empty. Luke tells of Anna, who lived "as a widow until she was eighty-four. She did not depart from the temple, worshiping with fasting and prayer night and day" (Luke 2:37). Decades of prayer seemed to produce nothing; then one day Mary and Joseph carried the infant Jesus into the temple, "and coming up at that very hour she began to give thanks to God and to speak of him to all who were waiting for the redemption of Jerusalem" (Luke 2:38). Anna's decades of prayer were not a leftover task, the one thing she could still manage; they were her days with God. What she received in the end was not the sense of being useful again, but the sight of the One she had waited for. That child grew up and on the cross bore our sins in our place; even as he was dying he saw his mother standing below and entrusted her to the care of the disciple he loved (John 19:26–27). In his own worst hour he made sure she would be cared for. "Father of the fatherless and protector of widows is God in his holy habitation. God settles the solitary in a home" (Ps 68:5–6). Today I can tell him honestly that I am often lonely and that sometimes even my prayers feel empty; and then, like Anna, wait for him in prayer, because what I am waiting for is not a more useful version of myself but him. If I know an older brother or sister like this, I can also go and ask: What can I pray for you? And would you pray for me? Perhaps the Lord means to use me to give one solitary person a home.
