## 本章脉络

第六章分成五段。**作仆人的**（1-2节）：在轭下作仆人的，要尊敬自己的主人，免得神的名和道理被人亵渎；有信主的主人，更要加意服事。**敬虔与钱财**（3-10节）：传异教的人自高自大，专好争辩，以敬虔为得利的门路；然而，敬虔加上知足的心便是大利；想要发财的人陷在网罗里，贪财是万恶之根。**打那美好的仗**（11-16节）：属神的人要逃避这些事，追求公义、敬虔、信心、爱心、忍耐、温柔；要为真道打那美好的仗，持定永生；保罗在那向本丢‧彼拉多作过美好见证的基督耶稣面前嘱咐他，又颂赞那可称颂、独有权能的万王之王。**嘱咐富足的人**（17-19节）：不要自高，不要倚靠无定的钱财，只要倚靠那厚赐百物给我们享受的神；要行善，甘心施舍。**保守所托付的**（20-21节）：要躲避那似是而非的学问；愿恩惠常与你们同在。

这一章又回到书信开头的问题：假教师。他们的问题不只是道理错了，还有爱钱的心。保罗的回答是：真正的「大利」不是钱财，而是敬虔加上知足；真正的富足，是倚靠那厚赐百物的神，持定那真正的生命。

## 深读 6:1-2 凡在轭下作仆人的

**1. 「当以自己主人配受十分的恭敬」。** 「凡在轭下作仆人的，当以自己主人配受十分的恭敬，免得神的名和道理被人亵渎。」（6:1）
- **当时的处境：** 在罗马帝国，奴隶占人口的相当一部分（估计各地不同，大约一成到两成，城市里更多），早期教会中有许多信徒是奴隶。罗马的奴隶制度和近代跨大西洋的黑奴贸易有些不同：罗马的奴隶不以种族划分；许多奴隶，尤其是城里的家奴，后来可以得自由。但这仍然是把人当作财产、世代相传的制度，常常充满暴力和屈辱。
- **保罗为什么这样说？** 他关心的是「神的名和道理」不被亵渎。如果信主的奴隶因为得了福音的自由，就轻看主人、不肯做工，外人就会说：这个新宗教叫人造反。保罗要信徒在一个不公义的处境中，仍然用好的品格为福音作见证（参多2:9-10）。

**2. 「不可因为与他是弟兄就轻看他」。** 「仆人有信道的主人，不可因为与他是弟兄就轻看他；更要加意服事他；因为得服事之益处的，是信道蒙爱的。」（6:2）
- **在基督里是弟兄：** 主人和奴隶在教会里同是弟兄，这在当时的社会是破天荒的事（参加3:28；门16「不再是奴仆，乃是高过奴仆，是亲爱的兄弟」）。
- **但不可以此为借口：** 有些信主的奴隶可能想：既然我们是弟兄，我就不必那么尊重他了。保罗说，正因为他是「信道蒙爱的」弟兄，更要好好服事他。

**3. 保罗赞成奴隶制度吗？**
- **他没有号召革命：** 保罗没有叫奴隶起来反抗，这在当时只会带来血腥的镇压，也会叫福音被看作政治运动。
- **但福音埋下了种子：** 保罗在1:10把「抢人口」（贩卖人口）的人列为违背律法的罪人；他劝作奴隶的「若能以自由，就求自由更好」（林前7:21）；他要腓利门把阿尼西母当作「亲爱的兄弟」（门16）；他也吩咐主人「不要威吓他们」，因为主人和奴隶「同有一位主在天上」（弗6:9）。历史上，许多信徒正是根据圣经反对奴隶制度：四世纪的教父尼撒的贵格利公开谴责蓄奴，英国的威伯福斯推动了废奴。
- **不可误用：** 历史上有人曾用这类经文为奴隶制度辩护，这是误用圣经，教会应当为此认罪。
- **今天怎样应用？** 今天的雇员不是奴隶，但这段经文的原则仍然适用：信徒在工作中要尽心、诚实，不要因为老板或上司是信徒，就随便对待工作。但这不是要人忍受违法的对待。遭受剥削、欠薪、虐待或性骚扰的人，可以寻求法律保护；向警察或有关部门举报是正当的（罗13:4）；也可以离开不安全的工作环境（参林前7:21）。反过来，信主的老板也不可以拿「我们是弟兄」作借口，要信徒员工无偿加班或忍受不公平的待遇；保罗吩咐作主人的「要公公平平地待仆人」（西4:1）。如果欺压人的是教会里的弟兄，教会也不可为了面子、「家丑不可外扬」而包庇。

> 要点：福音没有用暴力推翻社会制度，却从根本上改变了人与人的关系：主人和奴隶在基督里成了弟兄。信徒在不公义的处境中仍忠心做事，是因为他们真正事奉的是主基督（西3:23-24），为要叫神的名得荣耀。

## 深读 6:3-10 敬虔加上知足的心便是大利

**1. 「以敬虔为得利的门路」。** 「若有人传异教，不服从我们主耶稣基督纯正的话与那合乎敬虔的道理，他是自高自大，一无所知，专好问难，争辩言词，从此就生出嫉妒、纷争、毁谤、妄疑，并那坏了心术、失丧真理之人的争竞。他们以敬虔为得利的门路。」（6:3-5）
- **「专好问难」：** 原文有病态的意思，指对争论上了瘾，像得了病一样。这正好和「纯正的话」相对（「纯正」原文是健康的意思）：纯正的道理叫人健康，好争论却叫人生病。
- **「以敬虔为得利的门路」：** 这些假教师把宗教当作赚钱的生意。今天也有人传成功神学（富足福音），说只要信得够、奉献得够，神就会让你发财。保罗说，这样的人是「失丧真理」的。
- **在华人处境中：** 有些人把信仰当作求平安、求发财的另一种方法，和从前拜财神、求签其实没有两样。保罗要我们分辨：我信主，是为了得着神自己，还是只为了得着神给的好处？

**2. 「敬虔加上知足的心便是大利了」。** 「然而，敬虔加上知足的心便是大利了；因为我们没有带什么到世上来，也不能带什么去。只要有衣有食，就当知足。」（6:6-8）
- **保罗把话反过来说：** 假教师以为敬虔可以带来钱财。保罗说，敬虔确实是大利，却不是他们想的那种利。
- **「知足」：** 这个词在当时的斯多亚哲学中很常见，指不依靠外在环境的自足。但保罗的知足不是靠自己的意志，而是靠基督：「我无论在什么景况都可以知足……我靠着那加给我力量的，凡事都能做。」（腓4:11-13）保罗在别处用同一个词说：「神能将各样的恩惠多多地加给你们，使你们凡事常常充足」（林后9:8）。信徒的知足，来自神的供应。
- **「没有带什么到世上来」：** 这句话叫人想起约伯说的：「我赤身出于母胎，也必赤身归回」（伯1:21；参传5:15）。人走的时候，银行存款、房子、头衔都带不走。
- **「有衣有食，就当知足」：** 这不是说信徒不可以工作赚钱、改善生活，也不是说越穷越属灵。箴言的祷告很有智慧：「使我也不贫穷也不富足；赐给我需用的饮食」（箴30:8）。

**3. 「贪财是万恶之根」。** 「但那些想要发财的人，就陷在迷惑、落在网罗和许多无知有害的私欲里，叫人沉在败坏和灭亡中。贪财是万恶之根。有人贪恋钱财，就被引诱离了真道，用许多愁苦把自己刺透了。」（6:9-10）
- **问题不是钱，而是「贪」：** 保罗说的不是「钱是万恶之根」，而是「贪财」，就是爱钱的心。一个穷人也可能贪财，一个富人也可能不贪财。
- **「万恶之根」：** 许多解经家认为，这句话像当时一句常见的格言，希腊的道德家也有类似的话，说贪财是众恶的根源。意思是各样的罪恶都可能从贪财长出来，并不是说一切罪都出于贪财；有些英文译本就译作各样罪恶的一个根。单看原文的文法（有没有冠词），不能决定这一点；但整段的语气支持这个理解：欺骗、偷窃、嫉妒、背叛，甚至离弃信仰，都可以从这个根长出来。
- **「用许多愁苦把自己刺透了」：** 想要发财的人以为钱会带来快乐，结果却是焦虑、失眠、家庭破裂、良心不安。耶稣说：「人的生命不在乎家道丰富。」（路12:15）

> 要点：真正的大利不是钱财，而是敬虔加上知足的心。我们赤身而来，也要赤身而去；到了永恒里，我们仍然拥有的，只有神自己，和我们在基督里的生命。

## 深读 6:11-16 打那美好的仗

**1. 「但你这属神的人」。** 「但你这属神的人要逃避这些事，追求公义、敬虔、信心、爱心、忍耐、温柔。」（6:11）
- **「属神的人」：** 这是旧约对摩西、以利亚等先知的称呼（申33:1；王上17:24）。保罗用这个尊贵的称呼来叫提摩太（提后3:17也用这个称呼，指一切因圣经「得以完全」的信徒）。
- **「逃避」和「追求」：** 敬虔的生活有两方面：逃避贪财和争论，也追求美好的品格。光是逃避不够，还要有所追求。

**2. 「为真道打那美好的仗」。** 「你要为真道打那美好的仗，持定永生。你为此被召，也在许多见证人面前，已经作了那美好的见证。」（6:12）
- **「打那美好的仗」：** 许多解经家认为这是竞技场上的用语，指运动员全力以赴的竞赛（参林前9:25）；这个词也可以指争战（参1:18）。信仰的路，需要拼上全部的力气。
- **「持定永生」：** 永生是神所赐的，但我们要紧紧抓住它。
- **「那美好的见证」：** 可能指提摩太受洗或被按立时，在众人面前公开承认信仰；也有人认为是指他曾在审问他的官长面前承认信仰，正和基督在彼拉多面前的见证相对应。

**3. 「向本丢‧彼拉多作过那美好见证的基督耶稣」。** 「我在叫万物生活的神面前，并在向本丢‧彼拉多作过那美好见证的基督耶稣面前嘱咐你：要守这命令，毫不玷污，无可指责，直到我们的主耶稣基督显现。」（6:13-14）
- **基督的见证：** 耶稣在彼拉多面前承认自己是王，又说：「我为此而生，也为此来到世间，特为给真理作见证。」（约18:37）他明知这见证会带他上十字架，仍然没有退缩。（原文也可以译作在本丢‧彼拉多的时候，指他一生直到受死的见证。）
- **提摩太的见证和基督的见证：** 保罗把提摩太的见证和基督的见证放在一起。提摩太的见证是跟随基督的见证，我们也是这样。我们能站稳，不是因为自己勇敢，而是因为基督已经先为我们作了见证，走完了那条路。他在彼拉多面前没有退缩，一直走上十字架，替我们这些常常退缩、甚至否认他的人，担当了罪的刑罚（2:6「舍自己作万人的赎价」）。他替我们站在了审判台前，所以我们站得住。
- **「叫万物生活的神」：** 提摩太面对的也许是危险甚至死亡，但他所事奉的神是叫万物生活的，也是叫死人复活的。

**4. 「那可称颂、独有权能的万王之王、万主之主」。** 「到了日期，那可称颂、独有权能的万王之王、万主之主，就是那独一不死、住在人不能靠近的光里，是人未曾看见、也是不能看见的，要将他显明出来。但愿尊贵和永远的权能都归给他。阿们！」（6:15-16）
- **一首颂歌：** 保罗在1:17讲到自己蒙怜悯，就忍不住赞美；这里他讲到基督的显现，又一次发出颂赞。两处都称神为不能看见、独一的君王。
- **「万王之王、万主之主」：** 这个称呼出自旧约：「耶和华－你们的神－他是万神之神，万主之主」（申10:17；参诗136:3；但2:47）。当时的亚细亚尊崇罗马皇帝，也有人称皇帝为「主」。在这样的环境里，这首颂歌自然形成一种对照：真正掌权的只有一位。启示录把同样的称号归给羔羊（启17:14；19:16），可见子与父同享这尊荣。
- **「住在人不能靠近的光里」：** 神是圣洁的，人不能看见他而存活（出33:20）。可是这位不能看见的神，已经藉着他的儿子把自己表明出来（约1:18），也要到了日期，使他的儿子显现出来（14-15节）。

> 要点：信仰的生活是一场美好的仗。我们能打这仗，是因为基督已经在彼拉多面前作了美好的见证，走上了十字架；那位万王之王、万主之主，必在他的日期使基督显现。

## 深读 6:17-19 嘱咐那些今世富足的人

**1. 「不要自高，也不要倚靠无定的钱财」。** 「你要嘱咐那些今世富足的人，不要自高，也不要倚靠无定的钱财；只要倚靠那厚赐百物给我们享受的神。」（6:17）
- **「今世富足的人」：** 保罗没有说富人不能信主，也没有叫他们把财产全部变卖。但他提醒：他们的富足只是「今世」的。
- **两个危险：** 一是骄傲（「自高」），以为有钱就比别人高；二是安全感放错了地方（「倚靠无定的钱财」）。钱财是「无定的」：股市会跌，公司会倒闭，房子会贬值。
- **「厚赐百物给我们享受的神」：** 这句话很重要。保罗不是禁欲主义者（参4:3-4）。神「厚赐百物」，是要「给我们享受」的。问题不在于享受神所赐的，而在于倚靠恩赐而不倚靠赐恩的神。

**2. 「在好事上富足」。** 「又要嘱咐他们行善，在好事上富足，甘心施舍，乐意供给人，为自己积成美好的根基，预备将来，叫他们持定那真正的生命。」（6:18-19）
- **另一种富足：** 保罗巧妙地重复了「富足」这个词：今世富足的人，要「在好事上富足」。
- **「积成美好的根基」：** 这和耶稣的教导一样：「只要积攒财宝在天上」（太6:20）。这不是说用钱可以买到永生，而是说：一个真正认识神恩典的人，会用钱财去爱人；这就显明他的心在哪里（太6:21）。
- **「那真正的生命」：** 钱财所能买的生活，不是「真正的生命」。真正的生命是与神同在的生命，在今生就开始，到永远都不会失去。

> 要点：钱财是无定的，神是可靠的。富足的人要倚靠那厚赐百物给我们享受的神，并用今世的钱财在好事上富足，持定那真正的生命。

## 深读 6:20-21 保守所托付你的

**1. 「保守所托付你的」。** 「提摩太啊，你要保守所托付你的，躲避世俗的虚谈和那敌真道、似是而非的学问。」（6:20）
- **「所托付你的」：** 原文是一个法律用语，指托人保管的财物。福音不是提摩太发明的，是神托付给他的，他要原原本本地保守，再交给后来的人（参提后1:14；2:2）。
- **「似是而非的学问」：** 原文直译是冒称的知识，就是假冒的知识。假教师自以为有高深的知识，其实是虚谈。

**2. 「愿恩惠常与你们同在」。** 「已经有人自称有这学问，就偏离了真道。愿恩惠常与你们同在！」（6:21）
- **「你们」：** 在较早、较可靠的抄本里，这里是复数。这封信虽然是写给提摩太的，却是要在以弗所教会中宣读的。
- **最后一句话：** 这封信讲了许多规矩、品格和责任，但最后一句是恩惠。提摩太能保守所托付的，不是靠他自己的力量，而是靠神的恩惠。保罗在另一封信中说：我「知道我所信的是谁，也深信他能保全我所交付他的，直到那日」（提后1:12）。

> 要点：福音是神托付给教会的宝贵财物，我们要保守它，再传给下一代；但最终保守我们的，是那位赐恩惠的主。

## 默想

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 falls into five parts. **Slaves** (vv. 1–2): those under a yoke as slaves are to regard their masters as worthy of all honor, so that the name of God and the teaching may not be reviled; those with believing masters are to serve them all the better. **Godliness and money** (vv. 3–10): the false teacher is puffed up, craves controversy, and imagines that godliness is a means of gain; but there is great gain in godliness with contentment; those who desire to be rich fall into a snare, for the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. **Fight the good fight** (vv. 11–16): the man of God is to flee these things and pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, steadfastness and gentleness; to fight the good fight of the faith and take hold of eternal life; Paul charges him before Christ Jesus, who made the good confession before Pontius Pilate, and praises the blessed and only Sovereign, the King of kings. **A charge to the rich** (vv. 17–19): they are not to be haughty or set their hopes on uncertain riches, but on God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy; they are to do good and be generous. **Guard the deposit** (vv. 20–21): avoid what is falsely called "knowledge"; grace be with you.

This chapter returns to the problem with which the letter began: the false teachers. Their problem was not only wrong doctrine but a love of money. Paul's answer is that the real "great gain" is not money but godliness with contentment, and that true wealth is to hope in God, who richly provides us with everything, and to take hold of that which is truly life.

## Going Deeper: 6:1–2 Those Under a Yoke as Slaves

**1. "Regard their own masters as worthy of all honor."** "Let all who are under a yoke as slaves regard their own masters as worthy of all honor, so that the name of God and the teaching may not be reviled" (6:1).
- **The setting:** in the Roman Empire slaves made up a substantial part of the population (estimates vary, roughly 10–20 percent, more in the cities), and many in the early church were slaves. Roman slavery differed in some ways from the later Atlantic slave trade (for example, it was not based on race, and many slaves, especially urban household slaves, were eventually freed), but it was still a hereditary system that treated people as property and was often full of violence and humiliation.
- **Why does Paul say this?** His concern is that "the name of God and the teaching may not be reviled." If believing slaves, having found freedom in the gospel, began to despise their masters and refuse to work, outsiders would say that this new religion incites revolt. Paul wants believers, even in an unjust situation, to bear witness to the gospel with good character (see Titus 2:9–10).

**2. "Must not be disrespectful on the ground that they are brothers."** "Those who have believing masters must not be disrespectful on the ground that they are brothers; rather they must serve all the better since those who benefit by their good service are believers and beloved" (6:2).
- **Brothers in Christ:** masters and slaves were brothers in the church, something revolutionary in that society (see Gal 3:28; Phlm 16, "no longer as a slave but more than a slave, as a beloved brother").
- **But not an excuse:** some believing slaves may have thought: since we are brothers, I need not respect him so much. Paul says that precisely because he is a brother, "believing and beloved," they should serve him all the better.

**3. Did Paul approve of slavery?**
- **He did not call for revolution:** Paul did not tell slaves to rise up; in that world it would only have brought bloody suppression and made the gospel look like a political movement.
- **But the gospel planted seeds:** in this very letter Paul lists "enslavers" (traffickers in people) among the lawbreakers (1:10); he tells slaves, "if you can gain your freedom, avail yourself of the opportunity" (1 Cor 7:21); he asks Philemon to receive Onesimus "as a beloved brother" (Phlm 16); and he tells masters to "stop your threatening," knowing that master and slave have the same Master in heaven (Eph 6:9). Many believers opposed slavery on the basis of Scripture: Gregory of Nyssa in the fourth century openly condemned slaveholding, and William Wilberforce in Britain worked for abolition.
- **Not to be misused:** in history some used passages like this to defend slavery. That is a misuse of Scripture, and the church should confess it.
- **How does it apply today?** Employees today are not slaves, but the principle still applies: believers are to work wholeheartedly and honestly, and not take their work lightly because the boss or manager is a believer. This does not mean enduring unlawful treatment: those who suffer exploitation, unpaid wages, abuse or sexual harassment may seek the protection of the law; reporting to the police or proper authorities is right (Rom 13:4), and one may leave an unsafe workplace (see 1 Cor 7:21). Conversely, a believing employer must not use "we are brothers" to demand unpaid overtime or unfair treatment; Paul tells masters to "treat your slaves justly and fairly" (Col 4:1). If the one doing wrong is a fellow church member, the church must not cover it up to save face.

> The point: The gospel did not overthrow the social order by force, but it changed relationships at the root, from within: master and slave became brothers in Christ. Believers who serve faithfully even in unjust situations do so because the One they really serve is the Lord Christ (Col 3:23–24), for the glory of God's name.

## Going Deeper: 6:3–10 Great Gain in Godliness with Contentment

**1. "Imagining that godliness is a means of gain."** "If anyone teaches a different doctrine and does not agree with the sound words of our Lord Jesus Christ and the teaching that accords with godliness, he is puffed up with conceit and understands nothing. He has an unhealthy craving for controversy and for quarrels about words, which produce envy, dissension, slander, evil suspicions, and constant friction among people who are depraved in mind and deprived of the truth, imagining that godliness is a means of gain" (6:3–5).
- **"Unhealthy craving":** the Greek suggests sickness, an unhealthy appetite for argument. It is the opposite of "sound words" (literally, "healthy words"): sound doctrine makes people healthy; controversy makes them sick.
- **"Godliness is a means of gain":** these false teachers treated religion as a business. Today too some preach a "prosperity gospel," saying that if you believe enough and give enough, God will make you rich. Paul says this is to be deprived of the truth.
- **In a Chinese setting:** some people treat faith as another way to seek safety and wealth, not essentially different from praying to the god of wealth or drawing fortune sticks in the past. Paul wants us to discern: do we believe in order to have God, or in order to have God's things?

**2. "There is great gain in godliness with contentment."** "Now there is great gain in godliness with contentment, for we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out of the world. But if we have food and clothing, with these we will be content" (6:6–8).
- **Paul's reversal:** the false teachers say godliness brings gain; Paul says: yes, godliness is great gain, but not the kind of gain they mean.
- **"Contentment":** this word was common in Stoic philosophy at the time, meaning self-sufficiency independent of outward circumstances. But Paul's contentment does not rest on his own willpower; it rests on Christ: "I have learned in whatever situation I am to be content... I can do all things through him who strengthens me" (Phil 4:11–13). Elsewhere Paul uses the same word for a sufficiency God supplies: "God is able to make all grace abound to you, so that having all sufficiency in all things..." (2 Cor 9:8).
- **"We brought nothing into the world":** this echoes Job: "Naked I came from my mother's womb, and naked shall I return" (Job 1:21; see Eccl 5:15). When we go, we take no bank balance, house or title with us.
- **"If we have food and clothing, with these we will be content":** this does not mean believers may not work to earn money and improve their lives, nor that poverty in itself is spiritual. The prayer in Proverbs is wise: "give me neither poverty nor riches; feed me with the food that is needful for me" (Prov 30:8).

**3. "The love of money is a root of all kinds of evils."** "But those who desire to be rich fall into temptation, into a snare, into many senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin and destruction. For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. It is through this craving that some have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pangs" (6:9–10).
- **The problem is not money but love of it:** Paul does not say "money is the root of all evil," but "the love of money." A poor person can love money, and a rich person can be free of that love.
- **"A root of all kinds of evils":** many commentators take this as a proverb-like saying (similar maxims about love of money circulated among Greek moralists), meaning that every kind of evil can grow from it, not that every sin comes from it; the ESV renders it this way. The lack of an article does not settle the question by itself, but the flow of the passage supports this reading: deceit, theft, envy, betrayal, even abandoning the faith can all grow from this root.
- **"Pierced themselves with many pangs":** those who desire to be rich think money will bring happiness; instead it brings anxiety, sleeplessness, broken families and a troubled conscience. Jesus said, "one's life does not consist in the abundance of his possessions" (Luke 12:15).

> The point: The real great gain is not money but godliness with contentment. We came with nothing and will leave with nothing; what we will still have in eternity is God himself and our life in Christ.

## Going Deeper: 6:11–16 Fight the Good Fight

**1. "But as for you, O man of God."** "But as for you, O man of God, flee these things. Pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, steadfastness, gentleness" (6:11).
- **"Man of God":** this is the Old Testament title for Moses, Elijah and other prophets (Deut 33:1; 1 Kgs 17:24). Paul gives Timothy this honored title (2 Tim 3:17 uses it of any believer equipped by Scripture).
- **"Flee" and "pursue":** a godly life has two sides: fleeing the love of money and quarrels, and pursuing good character. Fleeing alone is not enough; there must also be something pursued.

**2. "Fight the good fight of the faith."** "Fight the good fight of the faith. Take hold of the eternal life to which you were called and about which you made the good confession in the presence of many witnesses" (6:12).
- **"Fight the good fight":** many take this as the language of the athletic contest, an athlete's all-out effort (see 1 Cor 9:25); the word can also mean combat (see 1:18). The life of faith calls for everything we have.
- **"Take hold of the eternal life":** eternal life is God's gift, but we are to grasp it firmly.
- **"The good confession":** this may refer to Timothy's public confession of faith before many at his baptism or ordination; some think of a confession made before hostile authorities, paralleling Christ's before Pilate.

**3. "Christ Jesus, who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate made the good confession."** "I charge you in the presence of God, who gives life to all things, and of Christ Jesus, who in his testimony before Pontius Pilate made the good confession, to keep the commandment unstained and free from reproach until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ" (6:13–14).
- **Christ's confession:** before Pilate Jesus acknowledged that he was a king and said, "For this purpose I was born and for this purpose I have come into the world, to bear witness to the truth" (John 18:37). He knew that this testimony would take him to the cross, and he did not draw back. (The phrase can also mean "in the time of Pontius Pilate," pointing to his whole witness up to his death.)
- **Timothy's confession and Christ's:** Paul sets the two side by side. Timothy's confession follows Christ's; so does ours. We can stand firm not because we are brave but because Christ has already borne witness for us and walked that road to the end. He did not draw back before Pilate because he was going to the cross to bear, in our place, the penalty for the sins of people like us who often draw back and deny him (2:6, "who gave himself as a ransom for all"). We can stand because he stood in the dock in our place.
- **"God, who gives life to all things":** Timothy may face danger, even death, but the God he serves gives life to all things and raises the dead.

**4. "The blessed and only Sovereign, the King of kings and Lord of lords."** "Which he will display at the proper time, he who is the blessed and only Sovereign, the King of kings and Lord of lords, who alone has immortality, who dwells in unapproachable light, whom no one has ever seen or can see. To him be honor and eternal dominion. Amen" (6:15–16).
- **A doxology:** in 1:17 Paul broke into praise as he spoke of the mercy shown to him; here he does so again as he speaks of Christ's appearing. Both times God is the invisible, only King.
- **"King of kings and Lord of lords":** the title comes from the Old Testament: "the LORD your God is God of gods and Lord of lords" (Deut 10:17; see Ps 136:3; Dan 2:47). In a province where emperors were honored with cult and sometimes called "lord," such praise also implied a contrast: there is only one true Sovereign. Revelation gives the same title to the Lamb (Rev 17:14; 19:16): the Son shares the Father's honor.
- **"Who dwells in unapproachable light":** God is holy; no one can see him and live (Exod 33:20). Yet this invisible God has made himself known through his Son (John 1:18), and at the proper time he will display the appearing of his Son.

> The point: The life of faith is a good fight. We can fight it because Christ has already made the good confession before Pilate and gone to the cross; the King of kings and Lord of lords will bring about Christ's appearing at the proper time.

## Going Deeper: 6:17–19 A Charge to the Rich in This Present Age

**1. "Not to be haughty, nor to set their hopes on the uncertainty of riches."** "As for the rich in this present age, charge them not to be haughty, nor to set their hopes on the uncertainty of riches, but on God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy" (6:17).
- **"The rich in this present age":** Paul does not say the rich cannot believe, nor does he tell them to sell everything. But he reminds them that their wealth belongs only to "this present age."
- **Two dangers:** first, pride ("haughty"), thinking that money makes one better than others; second, security placed in the wrong thing ("the uncertainty of riches"). Riches are uncertain: markets fall, companies fail, houses lose value.
- **"God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy":** this phrase matters. Paul is no ascetic (see 4:3–4). God "richly provides" everything "to enjoy." The problem is not enjoying what God gives but trusting the gifts instead of the Giver.

**2. "Rich in good works."** "They are to do good, to be rich in good works, to be generous and ready to share, thus storing up treasure for themselves as a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of that which is truly life" (6:18–19).
- **Another kind of wealth:** Paul plays on words: those who are rich in this age are to be "rich in good works."
- **"A good foundation for the future":** this matches Jesus' teaching: "lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven" (Matt 6:20). It does not mean money can buy eternal life, but that someone who truly knows God's grace will use money to love others, and this shows where his heart is (Matt 6:21).
- **"That which is truly life":** the life money can buy is not "truly life." True life is life with God, beginning now and never lost.

> The point: Riches are uncertain; God is dependable. The rich are to hope in God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy, and to use this age's wealth to be rich in good works, taking hold of that which is truly life.

## Going Deeper: 6:20–21 Guard the Deposit

**1. "Guard the deposit entrusted to you."** "O Timothy, guard the deposit entrusted to you. Avoid the irreverent babble and contradictions of what is falsely called 'knowledge'" (6:20).
- **"The deposit":** the Greek is a legal term for property entrusted to someone for safekeeping. Timothy did not invent the gospel; God entrusted it to him, and he is to guard it intact and pass it on to those who come after (see 2 Tim 1:14; 2:2).
- **"What is falsely called 'knowledge'":** the false teachers thought they had deep knowledge, but it was empty talk.

**2. "Grace be with you."** "For by professing it some have swerved from the faith. Grace be with you" (6:21).
- **"You":** the better manuscripts have the plural. Though the letter was written to Timothy, it was meant to be read in the church at Ephesus.
- **The last word:** this letter has spoken of many instructions, qualities and responsibilities, but its last word is grace. Timothy will guard the deposit not by his own strength but by God's grace. In another letter Paul says, "I know whom I have believed, and I am convinced that he is able to guard until that Day what has been entrusted to me" (2 Tim 1:12).

> The point: The gospel is a treasure God has entrusted to the church; we are to guard it and pass it on to the next generation. But in the end the one who guards us is the Lord who gives grace.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Now there is great gain in godliness with contentment, for we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out of the world" (6:6–7). Is there a number in my head: a salary, a savings balance, a size of flat, and then I will be content? Yet each time I come close to it, the number quietly moves up a little. I start thinking about switching to a better-paid job, or pushing hard for a few more years and working more overtime. Paul does not say that wanting to improve one's life is sin; he speaks of the "desire to be rich," the heart that always feels it is not enough and always wants a little more. That desire is a snare that tightens the more I struggle, until in the end I have "pierced [myself] with many pangs." We came with nothing and will leave with nothing; that ever-rising number cannot be taken along. The psalmist once envied the prosperity of the wicked; then he went into God's sanctuary and said, "Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is nothing on earth that I desire besides you... God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever" (Ps 73:25–26). My portion is not in how much I have; it is God himself. When the Lord Jesus left this world, the soldiers cast lots even for his clothing (John 19:23–24); at the cross he was stripped of everything. He willingly gave up everything and gave himself for my covetousness and unbelief, so that God himself might be my portion. Today I can name that number honestly before the Lord, confessing the covetousness in it and the unbelief in his provision: Lord, I have been telling myself that only at that number would it be enough. Then I can remember: "Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have, for he has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you'" (Heb 13:5). The next time the number moves up again, I can ask a different question: not how much I am still short, but whether this Lord who gave himself for me is still my portion today.
