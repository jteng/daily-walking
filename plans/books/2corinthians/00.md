## 这卷书在讲什么

哥林多后书是保罗书信中最个人、最坦白的一封。在这封信里，保罗不只是教导，也把自己的心掏出来给哥林多人看：他在亚细亚「甚至连活命的指望都绝了」（1:8）；他「多多地流泪」写信（2:4）；他在特罗亚因为找不到提多，「心里不安」（2:13）；他到了马其顿，「外有争战，内有惧怕」（7:5）；他身上还有「一根刺」，三次求主叫它离开，主却没有挪去（12:7-8）。

为什么一位使徒要这样讲自己的软弱？因为哥林多教会里来了一些人，他们看不起保罗：说他「气貌不扬，言语粗俗」（10:10），说他反复不定（1:17），说他没有像样的荐信（3:1）。这些人自称是使徒，夸口自己的出身、口才和属灵经历。保罗的回答出人意料：他不去证明自己比他们更强，反而夸自己的软弱，因为主对他说：「我的恩典够你用的，因为我的能力是在人的软弱上显得完全。」（12:9）

所以，这卷书的中心不是保罗，而是钉十字架又复活的基督：「他因软弱被钉在十字架上，却因神的大能仍然活着。」（13:4）基督为我们的罪受苦，然后进入荣耀；我们与他联合，跟从他、服事他，也走同样先受苦、后得荣耀的路。

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 作者。** 「奉神旨意作基督耶稣使徒的保罗和兄弟提摩太」（1:1）。保罗是这封信的作者，这一点几乎没有人怀疑。

**2. 保罗和哥林多教会的往来。** 根据两封哥林多书信和使徒行传，许多学者把事情的经过大致整理如下：
- 保罗建立哥林多教会，在那里住了一年半（徒18:1-18）。
- 他写了一封信，现在已经失传（林前5:9）。
- 他从以弗所写了哥林多前书（林前16:8）。
- 他又去了一趟哥林多，那是一次痛苦的探访（2:1；参12:14；13:1-2「第三次」）。很可能就在那次，有人当众顶撞、伤害了他（2:5；7:12）。
- 回到以弗所后，他「多多地流泪」写了一封严厉的信（2:3-4；7:8），并且差提多送去。
- 他离开以弗所，到特罗亚等提多，没有等到，就往马其顿去（2:12-13；参徒20:1）。
- 在马其顿，提多终于来了，带来好消息：多数哥林多人已经悔改，重新接纳保罗（7:6-16）。
- 保罗于是写了哥林多后书，大约在主后55-56年。

那封流泪写的信是哪一封，学者有不同的看法。较早的解经家多认为就是哥林多前书；今天多数学者认为是另一封已经失传的信；也有人认为它保存在哥林多后书10–13章里。

**3. 「最大的使徒」。** 保罗在信中两次讽刺地称对手为「那些最大的使徒」（11:5；12:11），又直接说他们是「假使徒」（11:13）。他们是犹太人出身、自称是基督仆人的教师（11:22-23），却「另传一个耶稣」（11:4）。他们带着荐信而来（3:1），以口才和异象自夸（10:10；11:6；12:1），并且向教会要钱，甚至欺压人（11:20）。有学者（如恩斯特·凯斯曼、查尔斯·巴瑞特）认为「最大的使徒」是指耶路撒冷的使徒，与11:13的「假使徒」不同；但多数学者（如维克多·弗尼什、默里·哈里斯）认为，这两个称呼指的是同一群人：11:5紧接在11:4讲「另传一个耶稣」的人之后，11:13-15也还在同一段论证里。

**4. 这卷书是一封信，还是几封信合成的？** 1–9章的语气大致是和好与安慰，10–13章却忽然变得严厉。有些学者（如巴瑞特、弗尼什）认为10–13章是保罗后来另写的一封信；也有学者（如较早的阿道夫·豪斯拉特、詹姆斯·肯尼迪）认为10–13章就是那封流泪写的信。还有更复杂的分割理论，例如把2:14–7:4或8、9两章看作另外的书信。另一些学者（如保罗·巴尼特、大卫·加兰、哈里斯）认为全书是一封信：保罗可能分几次写成，中间又听到新的消息；或者1–9章主要是对已经悔改的多数人说的，10–13章是对仍被假使徒影响的人说的。现存的所有古抄本，都把哥林多后书当作一封完整的信。无论采取哪一种看法，这卷书都是神所默示的话，信息是一致的。

## 钥匙经文：4:7

**1. 「这宝贝」。** 上一节说：「那吩咐光从黑暗里照出来的神，已经照在我们心里，叫我们得知神荣耀的光显在耶稣基督的面上。」（4:6）宝贝就是福音，就是神显在基督面上的荣耀。

**2. 「放在瓦器里」。** 瓦器是当时最便宜、最容易破的器皿，常用来装油、装水，甚至用来收藏贵重的东西（参耶32:14，地契就是放在瓦器里保存的）。保罗说，神把最宝贵的福音，放在最普通、最脆弱的人身上。

**3. 「要显明这莫大的能力是出于神，不是出于我们」。** 神这样做是有意的。如果器皿本身华丽坚固，人就会去欣赏器皿；正因为器皿只是粗陋易碎的瓦器，人才看得出能力不是出于器皿，而是出于神。

## 全书结构

哥林多后书可以大致分成五个部分：
- **患难中的安慰与行程的改变**（1:1–2:13）：「愿颂赞归与……赐各样安慰的神」；保罗解释他为什么没有按原来的计划去哥林多。
- **新约职事的荣耀与软弱**（2:14–7:4）：这是全书最长、神学最丰富的一段，讲新约的荣耀（3章）、瓦器里的宝贝（4章）、复活的盼望（5:1-10）、劝人与神和好的职分（5:11-21），以及保罗向哥林多人敞开的心（6:1–7:4）。
- **提多带来的好消息**（7:5-16）：哥林多人「依着神的意思忧愁」，生出了悔改。
- **为耶路撒冷圣徒的捐献**（8–9章）：「他本来富足，却为你们成了贫穷」（8:9）。
- **对假使徒的回应**（10–13章）：保罗「像愚妄人」自夸（11:17），夸的却是他的软弱；第三次去哥林多之前的最后警告；以三一神的祝福结束（13:14）。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 留意「软弱」和「能力」。** 这一对词贯穿全书。神的能力不是绕过软弱，而是在软弱中显明出来（4:7；12:9-10；13:4）。

**2. 留意「安慰」和「患难」。** 在1:3-7这五节经文里，「安慰」这个词（包括名词和动词）在原文中出现了十次。保罗不是在顺境中学会安慰别人，而是在患难中先被神安慰。

**3. 留意保罗怎样衡量一个人的服事。** 假使徒看重荐信、口才、外表和成就；保罗看重的是心中有没有基督、生命有没有被改变、是不是像基督一样为人舍己。今天许多教会用人数、名气、规模和网络影响力来衡量服事，保罗的话是很有分量的提醒。这也问到我们自己：我暗暗用什么来衡量自己和别人？

## 默想

**默想：** 「我们有这宝贝放在瓦器里，要显明这莫大的能力是出于神，不是出于我们。」（4:7）我们很容易只把自己好的一面给人看：在教会里总是说「感谢主，一切都好」，在朋友圈里只放笑脸、成绩和旅行的照片，在家人面前不敢承认自己失败了。这背后常常是怕丢面子，也是想靠自己的形象站立得住。保罗却把自己的眼泪、惧怕和那根拿不掉的刺，都写在信里给人看。他不是在诉苦，而是要人看见：撑着他的，不是他自己。我们的主耶稣也没有保全自己的体面，他「因软弱被钉在十字架上」，为我这个常常要面子的人赤身受了羞辱；他「却因神的大能仍然活着」（13:4）。因为他已经替我担当了我的罪和我的羞辱，我就不必再靠一个完美的形象活着。在开始读哥林多后书的时候，我能不能把一件一直不敢让人知道的软弱带到神面前，不是求他先替我把它藏好、补好，而是求他就在这裂缝里显出他的能力，叫人看见的是他，不是我？我这瓦器有裂缝，主的恩典却够我用。

## What This Book Is About

2 Corinthians is the most personal and candid of Paul's letters. Here Paul does more than teach. He opens his heart to the Corinthians: in Asia "we despaired of life itself" (1:8); he wrote to them "with many tears" (2:4); in Troas "my spirit was not at rest" because he could not find Titus (2:13); in Macedonia there was "fighting without and fear within" (7:5); and he carried "a thorn... in the flesh," which he asked the Lord three times to remove, and the Lord did not take it away (12:7–8).

Why would an apostle speak this way about his weakness? Because some people had come into the Corinthian church who looked down on Paul. They said his "bodily presence is weak, and his speech of no account" (10:10), that he wavered (1:17), that he had no proper letters of recommendation (3:1). They called themselves apostles and boasted of their background, eloquence and spiritual experiences. Paul's answer is surprising. He does not try to prove he is stronger than they are. He boasts of his weaknesses, because the Lord said to him, "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness" (12:9).

So the center of this book is not Paul but Christ crucified and risen: "he was crucified in weakness, but lives by the power of God" (13:4). Christ suffered for our sins and then entered glory; united to him, those who follow and serve him walk the same road, suffering first and glory after.

## Author and Setting

**1. Author.** "Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by the will of God, and Timothy our brother" (1:1). Paul's authorship is almost universally accepted.

**2. Paul's dealings with the Corinthian church.** From the two Corinthian letters and Acts, many scholars piece the story together roughly like this:
- Paul founded the church and stayed a year and a half (Acts 18:1–18).
- He wrote a letter that has not survived (1 Cor 5:9).
- He wrote 1 Corinthians from Ephesus (1 Cor 16:8).
- He made a visit to Corinth that was painful (2:1; cf. 12:14; 13:1–2, "the third time"). Probably on that visit someone publicly opposed and wounded him (2:5; 7:12).
- Back in Ephesus, he wrote a severe letter "with many tears" (2:3–4; 7:8) and sent Titus with it.
- He left Ephesus and waited for Titus at Troas. When Titus did not come, he went on to Macedonia (2:12–13; cf. Acts 20:1).
- In Macedonia Titus finally arrived with good news: most of the Corinthians had repented and welcomed Paul again (7:6–16).
- Paul then wrote 2 Corinthians, around AD 55–56.

Scholars differ on which letter was the "tearful letter." Older interpreters mostly took it to be 1 Corinthians. Most scholars today think it was another letter that has been lost. Some think it is preserved in 2 Corinthians 10–13.

**3. The "super-apostles."** Twice Paul ironically calls his opponents "these super-apostles" (11:5; 12:11), and he calls them plainly "false apostles" (11:13). They were Jewish teachers (11:22) who claimed to be servants of Christ (11:23) but preached "another Jesus" (11:4). They came with letters of recommendation (3:1), boasted of eloquence and visions (10:10; 11:6; 12:1), and took money from the church, even mistreating it (11:20). Some scholars (such as Ernst Käsemann and C. K. Barrett) think the "super-apostles" were the Jerusalem apostles, distinct from the "false apostles" of 11:13. Most (such as Victor Furnish and Murray Harris) think the two names refer to the same group, since 11:5 follows directly on those who preach "another Jesus" in 11:4, and 11:13–15 continues the same argument.

**4. One letter, or several combined?** Chapters 1–9 are mostly about reconciliation and comfort. Chapters 10–13 suddenly turn severe. Some scholars (such as C. K. Barrett and Victor Furnish) think chapters 10–13 are a later letter Paul wrote separately. Others (earlier, Adolf Hausrath and J. H. Kennedy) think chapters 10–13 are the "tearful letter" itself. There are more complex partition theories too, for example treating 2:14–7:4, or chapters 8 and 9, as separate letters. Still others (such as Paul Barnett, David Garland and Murray Harris) think the book is one letter. Paul may have written it in stages and received fresh news along the way, or he may address chapters 1–9 mainly to the repentant majority and chapters 10–13 to those still swayed by the false apostles. Every surviving ancient manuscript presents 2 Corinthians as a single letter. Whichever view one takes, the book is God's inspired word, and its message is one.

## Key Verse: 4:7

**1. "This treasure."** The previous verse says, "For God, who said, 'Let light shine out of darkness,' has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (4:6). The treasure is the gospel, the glory of God shown in the face of Christ.

**2. "In jars of clay."** Clay jars were the cheapest and most breakable vessels of the day, used for oil and water and even for keeping valuables (cf. Jer 32:14, where deeds of purchase are stored in an earthenware vessel). Paul says God puts his most precious gospel in the most ordinary, fragile people.

**3. "To show that the surpassing power belongs to God and not to us."** God does this on purpose. If the vessel were splendid and strong, people would admire the vessel. Because it is a battered clay jar, people can see that the power comes from God, not from the jar.

## Structure

2 Corinthians falls broadly into five parts:
- **Comfort in affliction and a change of plans** (1:1–2:13): "Blessed be... God of all comfort"; Paul explains why he did not come to Corinth as first planned.
- **The glory and weakness of new covenant ministry** (2:14–7:4): the longest and richest section of the letter, on the glory of the new covenant (ch. 3), treasure in jars of clay (ch. 4), the hope of resurrection (5:1–10), the ministry of reconciliation (5:11–21), and Paul's heart opened to the Corinthians (6:1–7:4).
- **Good news from Titus** (7:5–16): the Corinthians "felt a godly grief" that led to repentance.
- **The collection for the saints in Jerusalem** (chs. 8–9): "though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor" (8:9).
- **Answering the false apostles** (chs. 10–13): Paul boasts "as a fool" (11:17), but of his weaknesses; final warnings before his third visit; and the closing blessing of the triune God (13:14).

## How to Read This Book

**1. Watch for "weakness" and "power."** This pair runs through the whole letter. God's power does not bypass weakness. It is shown in weakness (4:7; 12:9–10; 13:4).

**2. Watch for "comfort" and "affliction."** In the five verses of 1:3–7, the Greek word for "comfort" (noun and verb) appears ten times. Paul did not learn to comfort others in easy times. He was first comforted by God in affliction.

**3. Watch how Paul measures ministry.** The false apostles prized letters of recommendation, eloquence, appearance and achievement. Paul asks whether Christ is in the heart, whether lives have been changed, and whether the servant gives himself for others as Christ did. For churches today that measure ministry by numbers, fame, size and online reach, this is a weighty reminder. It also asks us: what do I quietly use to measure myself and others?

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But we have this treasure in jars of clay, to show that the surpassing power belongs to God and not to us" (4:7). It is easy to show people only our good side: at church we always say, "Thank God, everything is fine"; online we post only smiles, achievements and holiday photos; with family we dare not admit we have failed. Behind this is often the fear of losing face, and the hope of standing on our own image. Paul, though, wrote his tears, his fears and the thorn that would not go away into a letter for others to read. He was not complaining. He wanted people to see that what held him up was not himself. Our Lord Jesus did not protect his own dignity either. He "was crucified in weakness," stripped and shamed for me, who so often cares about face; and he "lives by the power of God" (13:4). Because he has already borne my sin and my shame in my place, I no longer have to live by a flawless image. As I begin 2 Corinthians, can I bring to God one weakness I have never dared let anyone know, not asking him first to hide it or patch it, but to show his power through that very crack, so that people see him and not me? This jar of clay is cracked, but the Lord's grace is sufficient for me.
