## 本章脉络

第一章有三段。**问安**（1-2节）：保罗和提摩太写信给在哥林多神的教会「并亚该亚遍处的众圣徒」。**患难中的安慰**（3-11节）：保罗一开头就颂赞「发慈悲的父，赐各样安慰的神」，然后讲到他在亚细亚几乎丧命的经历：「叫我们不靠自己，只靠叫死人复活的神。」**行程的改变**（12-24节）：有人说保罗改了行程，是「反复不定」；保罗回答说，神是信实的，「神的应许，不论有多少，在基督都是是的」；他没有去哥林多，是「为要宽容你们」。

保罗在别的书信里，开头多半先为读者感谢神；这里他却先颂赞神，因为神在他自己的患难中安慰了他。他要哥林多人先认识这位神，再来明白他这个人。

## 深读 1:3-7 安慰我们，叫我们能安慰人

**1. 发慈悲的父。** 「愿颂赞归与我们的主耶稣基督的父神，就是发慈悲的父，赐各样安慰的神。」（1:3）「愿颂赞归与……神」是犹太人祷告中常用的格式。保罗用这个格式，却加上了「我们的主耶稣基督的父」：以色列的神，就是耶稣基督的父。「发慈悲的父」的意思是，慈悲从他而出；他本身就是一切怜悯的源头（参哀3:22-23）。

**2. 「安慰」这个词。** 原文这个词不只是叫人心里舒服一点，也有在旁边扶持、鼓励、使人刚强起来的意思。主耶稣称圣灵为「保惠师」（约14:16），用的是同一个词根的名词。神的安慰，不是叫患难消失，而是在患难中扶住我们。

**3. 安慰的流动。** 「我们在一切患难中，他就安慰我们，叫我们能用神所赐的安慰去安慰那遭各样患难的人。」（1:4）神安慰我们，不是停在我们身上，而是要藉着我们流到别人身上。一个经历过丧亲、疾病、失业或被出卖的人，常常最能明白正在同样处境中的人；不是因为他有一套答案，而是因为他自己也曾被神扶住。

**4. 基督的苦楚。** 「我们既多受基督的苦楚，就靠基督多得安慰。」（1:5）这不是说信徒的受苦能为罪补上什么；基督替我们受的刑罚，在十字架上已经一次完成了。保罗的意思是，信徒既与基督联合，就会经历和他一样的苦难，而且是因他的缘故而受的苦（参腓3:10）。也有解经家认为，这里带有犹太人所说弥赛亚降临前那段灾难时期的意味。无论哪一种理解，都没有在基督赎罪的工作上加添什么。主曾问逼迫教会的扫罗：「你为什么逼迫我？」（徒9:4）教会受苦，基督与她同在；苦楚多，从基督来的安慰也多。

> 要点：神是一切慈悲和安慰的源头。他在患难中安慰我们，也藉着我们安慰别人；我们与基督一同受苦，也靠基督多得安慰。

## 深读 1:8-11 只靠叫死人复活的神

**1. 力不能胜。** 「弟兄们，我们不要你们不晓得，我们从前在亚细亚遭遇苦难，被压太重，力不能胜，甚至连活命的指望都绝了。」（1:8）这是什么苦难，保罗没有说。有人认为是以弗所的暴动（徒19:23-41）；有人联想到他「在以弗所同野兽战斗」的话（林前15:32），也有人认为是一场重病，或是一次监禁。我们不知道细节，但知道这次苦难重到连保罗都以为自己必死。

**2. 为什么神让他走到这一步？** 「自己心里也断定是必死的，叫我们不靠自己，只靠叫死人复活的神。」（1:9）「断定是必死的」原文的意思，像是已经宣判的死刑。神让保罗走到自己的尽头，好叫他不再靠自己。亚伯拉罕所信的，也是这位「叫死人复活、使无变为有的神」（罗4:17）。人往往到了无路可走的时候，才发现自己真正能倚靠的，只有那位叫死人复活的神。他常常在今生为我们开路；即使没有，复活也是他最后的话。

**3. 过去、现在、将来。** 「他曾救我们脱离那极大的死亡，现在仍要救我们，并且我们指望他将来还要救我们。」（1:10）保罗的盼望有过去的根据，也有对将来的仰望。神过去的信实，是我们现在信靠他的理由。

**4. 请你们为我们祷告。** 「你们以祈祷帮助我们，好叫许多人为我们谢恩，就是为我们因许多人所得的恩。」（1:11）这位大使徒并不觉得请人代祷是丢脸的事。许多人为他祷告，神回应了，结果是许多人一同感谢神。代祷把教会连在一起，也叫神得着更多的感谢。

> 要点：神有时让我们走到自己力量的尽头，好叫我们不靠自己，只靠叫死人复活的神。我们也不必独自承担，可以请弟兄姊妹为我们祷告。

## 深读 1:12-20 在基督只有一是

**1. 良心的见证。** 「我们所夸的是自己的良心，见证我们凭着神的圣洁和诚实；在世为人不靠人的聪明，乃靠神的恩惠，向你们更是这样。」（1:12）这里的「圣洁」，古抄本有不同的写法。最古老的抄本（包括蒲草纸抄本P46、西奈抄本、亚历山大抄本、梵蒂冈抄本）作圣洁，通行的希腊文本也采用这个读法，和合本跟从它；后来的许多抄本作单纯，英文ESV译本就是按这个读法翻译的。无论哪一种，保罗的意思都是：他待人没有心机，没有两面手法。但他并不是靠自己的良心称义（参林前4:4）；他说这一切都是「靠神的恩惠」。

**2. 行程为什么改变？** 保罗原来在哥林多前书说，他要先经过马其顿，再到哥林多（林前16:5-7）。后来他改了计划：先到哥林多，再往马其顿，然后回到哥林多，好叫他们「再得益处」（1:15-16）。许多学者认为，他按新的计划先到了哥林多，那次就是痛苦的探访（2:1），所以他没有按计划再回去。于是有人说：「保罗说话不算数。」

**3. 是而又非吗？** 「我有此意，岂是反复不定吗？我所起的意，岂是从情欲起的，叫我忽是忽非吗？」（1:17）「从情欲起的」原文直译是照着肉体，就是照着人自私的打算：对自己有利就说「是」，不利就说「不是」。保罗说，他改变计划不是出于这样的心。

**4. 神的「是」。** 「因为我和西拉并提摩太，在你们中间所传神的儿子耶稣基督，总没有是而又非的，在他只有一是。神的应许，不论有多少，在基督都是是的。所以藉着他也都是实在的，叫神因我们得荣耀。」（1:19-20）保罗在这里从自己的行程，一下子讲到神的信实。西拉就是使徒行传中与保罗同工的那一位（徒15:40；18:5）。神在旧约所应许的一切，包括赦罪、新心、圣灵、与神同在和永远的产业，在基督里都实现了。「也都是实在的」原文就是阿们。教会在敬拜中说「阿们」，就是对神在基督里的「是」说「是」。

> 要点：保罗的计划会改变，神的应许却永不改变。神一切的应许在基督里都是「是」；我们藉着他，在敬拜中向神说「阿们」。

## 深读 1:21-24 坚固、膏抹、印记、凭据

**1. 是神在做工。** 「那在基督里坚固我们和你们，并且膏我们的就是神。他又用印印了我们，并赐圣灵在我们心里作凭据。」（1:21-22）这两节里有父神，有基督，也有圣灵：三一神一同保守信徒。

**2. 四个词。**
- **坚固：** 许多学者根据当时的文献指出，这是商业和法律上的用词，指一项交易被确认、生效。
- **膏：** 原文和「基督」（受膏者）是同一个词根，保罗有意让这两个词互相呼应：神在基督（受膏者）里坚固我们，又膏了我们。有人认为这里的「我们」主要指保罗和他的同工，神膏他们作使徒；也有人认为是指所有信徒，因为信徒都「从那圣者受了恩膏」（约一2:20）。
- **印：** 古时盖印，表示所有权，也证明物件是真的。信徒「受了所应许的圣灵为印记」（弗1:13）。
- **凭据：** 这个词源自希伯来文；希腊文旧约译本的创世记38章，犹大给她玛的「当头」用的就是这个词。在保罗的时代，它是商业用语，指先付的一部分款项，保证全款将来一定付清。现代希腊文还用这个词指订婚。圣灵就是神给我们的定金，保证将来完全的救赎（参弗1:14；罗8:23）。

**3. 宽容你们。** 「我呼吁神给我的心作见证，我没有往哥林多去是为要宽容你们。」（1:23）保罗没有去，不是因为他反复不定，而是因为如果他当时去，就必须严厉地处理他们的问题。他宁愿等一等，给他们悔改的时间。

**4. 不是辖管你们的信心。** 「我们并不是辖管你们的信心，乃是帮助你们的快乐，因为你们凭信才站立得住。」（1:24）保罗是使徒，有从主来的权柄，可是他不把自己当作信徒信心的主人。信徒是凭信靠基督站立的，不是靠哪一位领袖。今天有些教会领袖用属灵的权威控制人：要人凡事请示、不许人提问、用「你不顺服就是悖逆神」来压人。这不是保罗的样式。如果你曾在这样的教会里受伤，那不是基督的样式；他自己说：「我心里柔和谦卑」（太11:29）。真正的牧者是「帮助你们的快乐」的同工（参彼前5:3）。

> 要点：是神自己在基督里坚固我们，用圣灵作印记和凭据，保证他必完成救恩。真正的属灵领袖不辖管人的信心，而是帮助人在基督里喜乐。

## 默想

**默想：** 「自己心里也断定是必死的，叫我们不靠自己，只靠叫死人复活的神。」（1:9）我是否正处在一个「力不能胜」的时候：身体的病痛、工作的压力、家里的难处、心里长期的低落，甚至有时觉得撑不下去？我是否因为怕麻烦别人、怕人觉得我信心不够，就一直自己扛着？这位大使徒也会绝望，他也请人为他祷告（1:11）。我们的主耶稣在客西马尼园「心里甚是忧伤，几乎要死」，也对门徒说：「你们在这里等候，和我一同警醒」（太26:38）。他明白一个人被压到极处的滋味。他为我的罪死了，又被那「叫死人复活的神」从死里复活；神一切的应许，在他里面都是「是」（1:20）。所以，我的软弱不是我被神丢弃的证据，反而可能正是神要我不再靠自己的地方。如果我心里的低落已经持续很久，或者想过结束自己的生命，那么去看医生、找专业辅导，并且告诉一位可以信任的人，并不表示我信心软弱，这正是神所赐的帮助。如果我此刻就有伤害自己的念头，请立刻拨打当地的急救电话或心理危机热线，或者马上请身边的人来陪着我。今天，我能不能先向「赐各样安慰的神」说出我的重担，再告诉一两位弟兄姊妹，请他们为我祷告？就算我今天还说不出口，那位曾救我们、现在仍要救我们的神（1:10），也不会放下我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 has three parts. **Greeting** (vv. 1–2): Paul and Timothy write to the church at Corinth, "with all the saints who are in the whole of Achaia." **Comfort in affliction** (vv. 3–11): Paul opens by blessing "the Father of mercies and God of all comfort," then tells of nearly dying in Asia: "that was to make us rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead." **A change of plans** (vv. 12–24): some said Paul's change of travel plans showed he was "vacillating." Paul answers that God is faithful, "all the promises of God find their Yes in him," and that he stayed away "to spare you."

In his other letters Paul usually begins by thanking God for his readers. Here he begins by blessing God for comfort in his own affliction. He wants the Corinthians to know this God first, and then to understand Paul.

## Going Deeper 1:3–7 Comforted So That We Can Comfort

**1. The Father of mercies.** "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort" (1:3). "Blessed be... God" was a common form in Jewish prayer. Paul uses it but adds "Father of our Lord Jesus Christ": the God of Israel is the Father of Jesus Christ. "Father of mercies" means that mercy comes from him; he is the source of all compassion (cf. Lam 3:22–23).

**2. The word "comfort."** The Greek word means more than making someone feel a little better. It carries the sense of standing alongside, encouraging and strengthening. Jesus called the Holy Spirit the "Helper" (John 14:16), a noun from the same root. God's comfort does not make affliction vanish. It holds us up in it.

**3. Comfort that flows on.** "...who comforts us in all our affliction, so that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction, with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God" (1:4). God's comfort does not stop with us. He means it to flow through us to others. Someone who has known bereavement, illness, job loss or betrayal often understands others in the same place best, not because he has a set of answers, but because he too has been held up by God.

**4. The sufferings of Christ.** "For as we share abundantly in Christ's sufferings, so through Christ we share abundantly in comfort too" (1:5). This does not mean that believers' suffering adds anything to the payment for sin. The penalty Christ bore in our place was finished once for all on the cross. Paul means that believers, united to Christ, share in sufferings that follow the pattern of his and come to them because they belong to him (cf. Phil 3:10). Some commentators also hear an echo of the sufferings expected before the end, the "messianic woes." None of these readings adds anything to his atoning work. The Lord asked Saul, who was persecuting the church, "why are you persecuting me?" (Acts 9:4). When the church suffers, Christ is with her; where suffering abounds, comfort from Christ abounds too.

> The point: God is the source of all mercy and comfort. He comforts us in affliction and comforts others through us; as we share Christ's sufferings, we share abundantly in comfort through Christ.

## Going Deeper 1:8–11 Relying on God Who Raises the Dead

**1. Beyond our strength.** "For we do not want you to be ignorant, brothers, of the affliction we experienced in Asia. For we were so utterly burdened beyond our strength that we despaired of life itself" (1:8). Paul does not say what this was. Some think of the riot in Ephesus (Acts 19:23–41); some connect it with his words "I fought with beasts at Ephesus" (1 Cor 15:32); others suggest a severe illness or an imprisonment. We do not know the details, but we know it was heavy enough that even Paul thought he would die.

**2. Why did God let him come to this?** "Indeed, we felt that we had received the sentence of death. But that was to make us rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead" (1:9). "Sentence of death" pictures a verdict already passed. God let Paul reach the end of himself so that he would stop relying on himself. Abraham too believed in the God "who gives life to the dead and calls into existence the things that do not exist" (Rom 4:17). Often only when we have no way out do we find that the one we can truly rely on is the God who raises the dead. Often he opens a way in this life; even when he does not, resurrection is his last word.

**3. Past, present, future.** "He delivered us from such a deadly peril, and he will deliver us. On him we have set our hope that he will deliver us again" (1:10). Paul's hope rests on the past and looks to the future. God's faithfulness in the past is our reason to trust him now.

**4. Pray for us.** "You also must help us by prayer, so that many will give thanks on our behalf for the blessing granted us through the prayers of many" (1:11). This great apostle did not think it shameful to ask for prayer. Many prayed, God answered, and many gave thanks together. Prayer for one another binds the church together and brings God more thanks.

> The point: God sometimes brings us to the end of our strength so that we rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead. And we need not carry it alone; we can ask brothers and sisters to pray for us.

## Going Deeper 1:12–20 In Him It Is Always Yes

**1. The testimony of conscience.** "For our boast is this: the testimony of our conscience that we behaved in the world with simplicity and godly sincerity, not by earthly wisdom but by the grace of God, and supremely so toward you" (1:12). The manuscripts differ here. The oldest (including P46, Sinaiticus, Alexandrinus and Vaticanus) read "holiness," which the standard Greek text prints and the CUV follows; many later manuscripts read "simplicity," which the ESV follows. Either way, Paul means he dealt with them without scheming or double-dealing. But he is not justified by his own conscience (cf. 1 Cor 4:4); he says all this was "by the grace of God."

**2. Why did the plans change?** In 1 Corinthians Paul said he would pass through Macedonia first and then come to Corinth (1 Cor 16:5–7). Later he changed the plan: he would come to Corinth first, go on to Macedonia, and return to Corinth, so that they "might have a second experience of grace" (1:15–16). Many think he made the first leg of that plan, and that this was the painful visit (2:1), so he did not return as planned. So some said, "Paul doesn't keep his word."

**3. Yes and No?** "Was I vacillating when I wanted to do this? Do I make my plans according to the flesh, ready to say 'Yes, yes' and 'No, no' at the same time?" (1:17). "According to the flesh" means by selfish human calculation: saying "yes" when it suits me and "no" when it doesn't. Paul says his change of plans did not come from such a heart.

**4. God's Yes.** "For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, whom we proclaimed among you, Silvanus and Timothy and I, was not Yes and No, but in him it is always Yes. For all the promises of God find their Yes in him. That is why it is through him that we utter our Amen to God for his glory" (1:19–20). From his travel plans Paul turns at once to the faithfulness of God. Silvanus is the Silas who worked with Paul in Acts (Acts 15:40; 18:5). Everything God promised in the Old Testament (forgiveness, a new heart, the Spirit, God's presence, an eternal inheritance) is fulfilled in Christ. When the church says "Amen" in worship, it says "yes" to God's "Yes" in Christ.

> The point: Paul's plans could change, but God's promises never change. All God's promises find their Yes in Christ, and through him we say our Amen to God in worship.

## Going Deeper 1:21–24 Established, Anointed, Sealed, Guaranteed

**1. God is the one at work.** "And it is God who establishes us with you in Christ, and has anointed us, and who has also put his seal on us and given us his Spirit in our hearts as a guarantee" (1:21–22). In these two verses are God the Father, Christ and the Holy Spirit: the triune God together keeps his people.

**2. Four words.**
- **Establishes:** many note from the papyri that this is a commercial and legal term for a transaction confirmed and made binding.
- **Anointed:** from the same root as "Christ" (the Anointed One); Paul deliberately plays on it: God establishes us into "Christ" and has "anointed" us. Some take "us" here mainly as Paul and his co-workers, anointed by God as apostles; others take it as all believers, who "have been anointed by the Holy One" (1 John 2:20).
- **Seal:** in the ancient world a seal marked ownership and proved a thing genuine. Believers "were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit" (Eph 1:13).
- **Guarantee:** a word borrowed from Hebrew (it is Judah's "pledge" to Tamar in the Greek of Gen 38:17–20); in Paul's day it was a commercial term for a first installment that guarantees the full payment will follow. In modern Greek the word is still used for an engagement. The Spirit is God's down payment, guaranteeing our full redemption to come (cf. Eph 1:14; Rom 8:23).

**3. To spare you.** "But I call God to witness against me- it was to spare you that I refrained from coming again to Corinth" (1:23). Paul stayed away not because he wavered, but because if he had come then he would have had to deal severely with their problems. He chose to wait and give them time to repent.

**4. Not lords over your faith.** "Not that we lord it over your faith, but we work with you for your joy, for you stand firm in your faith" (1:24). Paul was an apostle with authority from the Lord, yet he did not treat himself as master of believers' faith. Believers stand by faith in Christ, not by leaning on any leader. Some church leaders today use spiritual authority to control people: requiring permission for everything, forbidding questions, pressing people with "if you don't obey me you are rebelling against God." That is not Paul's way. If you have been hurt in a church like that, that was not Christ's way; he said, "I am gentle and lowly in heart" (Matt 11:29). A true shepherd is a co-worker "for your joy" (cf. 1 Pet 5:3).

> The point: God himself establishes us in Christ and gives the Spirit as seal and guarantee that he will finish our salvation. A true spiritual leader does not lord it over people's faith but works with them for their joy in Christ.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Indeed, we felt that we had received the sentence of death. But that was to make us rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead" (1:9). Am I in a time "beyond my strength": illness, pressure at work, trouble at home, a long heaviness of heart, perhaps even feeling I cannot go on? Have I carried it alone because I don't want to trouble others, or fear they will think my faith is weak? This great apostle despaired too, and he asked others to pray for him (1:11). In Gethsemane our Lord Jesus said, "My soul is very sorrowful, even to death," and asked his disciples, "remain here, and watch with me" (Matt 26:38). He knows what it is to be pressed to the limit. He died for my sins and was raised from the dead by the God "who raises the dead"; all God's promises find their Yes in him (1:20). So my weakness is not proof that God has abandoned me. It may be the very place where God is teaching me to stop relying on myself. If the heaviness has lasted a long time, or I have thought of ending my life, seeing a doctor or a trained counselor, and telling someone I trust, is not weak faith; it is help God provides. If I am thinking of harming myself right now, I should call local emergency services or a crisis line straight away, or ask someone near me to stay with me. Today, can I first tell the "God of all comfort" what I am carrying, and then tell one or two brothers or sisters and ask them to pray for me? Even if I cannot yet put it into words, the God who delivered us, and will deliver us again (1:10), will not let go of me.
