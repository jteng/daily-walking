## 本章脉络

第三章有三段。**活的荐信**（1-3节）：保罗不需要别人的荐信，因为哥林多信徒自己「明显是基督的信」，「不是用墨写的，乃是用永生神的灵写的」。**新约的执事**（4-11节）：「我们所能承担的，乃是出于神。」保罗比较两种职事：刻在石头上的「属死的职事」尚且有荣光，那「属灵的职事」和「称义的职事」的荣光就越发大了。**揭去的帕子**（12-18节）：摩西用帕子蒙脸，今天读旧约的人心里也常有帕子；但人「几时归向主，帕子就几时除去了」。于是「我们众人既然敞着脸得以看见主的荣光」，就「变成主的形状，荣上加荣」。

这一章是保罗对「谁能当得起呢？」（2:16）的回答。他的资格不在荐信，而在神藉着圣灵所做的新约之工。

## 深读 3:1-3 你们就是我们的荐信

**1. 荐信。** 「我们岂是又举荐自己吗？岂像别人用人的荐信给你们或用你们的荐信给人吗？」（3:1）在初期教会，荐信很常见：亚波罗往亚该亚去，弟兄们就「写信请门徒接待他」（徒18:27）；保罗也在罗马书中举荐非比（罗16:1）。荐信本身没有错。问题是那些来到哥林多的人拿着荐信，以此来证明自己比保罗更有资格。

**2. 写在心上的信。** 「你们就是我们的荐信，写在我们的心里，被众人所知道所念诵的。」（3:2）保罗说，你们这些人，就是我的荐信。一个服事的人最好的证明，不是一张纸，而是被福音改变的生命。

**3. 基督的信。** 「你们明显是基督的信，藉着我们修成的。不是用墨写的，乃是用永生神的灵写的；不是写在石版上，乃是写在心版上。」（3:3）写信的是基督，保罗是替他经手这封信的人；用来写的「墨」是圣灵。「石版」让人想起西奈山上的两块法版，那是「神用指头写的石版」（出31:18）。「心版」则让人想起先知的应许：神要把律法「写在他们心上」（耶31:33），要「除掉石心，赐给他们肉心」（结11:19；36:26）。保罗说，这个新约的应许，正在哥林多信徒身上成就。

> 要点：服事最好的证明不是荐信、头衔或名气，而是基督藉着圣灵写在人心上的信，就是被福音改变的生命。

## 深读 3:4-6 我们所能承担的，乃是出于神

**1. 不是凭自己。** 「我们因基督，所以在神面前才有这样的信心。并不是我们凭自己能承担什么事；我们所能承担的，乃是出于神。」（3:4-5）这是对2:16「这事谁能当得起呢？」的回答。保罗有信心，但他的信心不在自己，而在神。

**2. 新约的执事。** 「他叫我们能承当这新约的执事，不是凭着字句，乃是凭着精意。」（3:6）「新约」就是耶利米所应许的那个约（耶31:31-34），也是主耶稣在最后的晚餐所说「用我血所立的新约」（路22:20）。「精意」原文就是「灵」，这里指圣灵。这里的「执事」是指服事的人，不是教会中的执事职分。

**3. 字句叫人死，精意叫人活。** 「因为那字句是叫人死，精意是叫人活。」（3:6）这句话常被误解。
- 它不是说读经只要看属灵的意思，不必按字面理解经文。
- 它也不是说规则不重要，只要心意好就可以。
- 保罗的意思是：刻在石版上的律法本身是「圣洁、公义、良善的」（罗7:12），但它写在外面，不能改变人的心。律法向罪人显明罪，宣告罪的刑罚（罗3:20；7:10），却不能赐人生命。圣灵却把神的律法写在人心里，赐人新的生命。

问题不在律法，而在人的心。新约的恩典不是废掉神的旨意，而是叫人从心里爱神的旨意。

> 要点：没有人凭自己能承担服事神的事；我们所能承担的，乃是出于神。律法写在石版上，只能定罪；圣灵把神的话写在心上，叫人活。

## 深读 3:7-11 更大的荣光

**1. 摩西脸上的荣光。** 「那用字刻在石头上属死的职事尚且有荣光，甚至以色列人因摩西面上的荣光，不能定睛看他的脸。」（3:7）保罗在这里讲的是出埃及记34章：摩西从西奈山下来，「不知道自己的面皮因耶和华和他说话就发了光」，以色列人「就怕挨近他」（出34:29-30）。旧约也有荣光，因为那是神自己所立的约。

**2. 「渐渐退去的」。** 「这荣光原是渐渐退去的。」（3:7）和合本译作「渐渐退去」，出埃及记却没有说摩西脸上的光会消退。对这个词有两种主要的理解：
- 一种认为保罗是说那光确实渐渐消退，象征旧约是暂时的；
- 另一种认为这个词的意思是被废掉、被取代（3:11和合本译作「废掉的」，用的就是同一个词）：旧约的荣光并不假，但在基督来了以后，它就像月亮在太阳升起时一样，不再显得明亮。

两种理解的结论相同：旧约有荣光，但它是指向基督的，是暂时的。

**3. 三组对比。** 保罗用三组对比说明新约的荣光更大：
- 「属死的职事」与「属灵的职事」（3:7-8）；
- 「定罪的职事」与「称义的职事」（3:9）；
- 「那废掉的」与「这长存的」（3:11）。

称旧约为「属死的」「定罪的」职事，不是说律法是坏的，而是说罪人站在神圣洁的律法面前，只能被定罪。新约之所以是「称义的职事」，是因为它传扬神白白赐给人的义：「神使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义。」（5:21）

**4. 算不得有荣光。** 「那从前有荣光的，因这极大的荣光就算不得有荣光了。」（3:10）点着的灯在黑夜里很亮，太阳一出来，就显不出它的亮光了。这不是贬低旧约，而是高举基督。

> 要点：旧约有从神来的荣光，新约的荣光却大得多：它是赐生命的、称义的、长存的。我们在基督里所领受的，不是定罪，而是神白白赐给的义。

## 深读 3:12-18 揭去帕子，荣上加荣

**1. 大胆讲说。** 「我们既有这样的盼望，就大胆讲说，不像摩西将帕子蒙在脸上，叫以色列人不能定睛看到那将废者的结局。」（3:12-13）「结局」可能指那荣光的终止，也可能指旧约所指向的目标，就是基督（参罗10:4）。也有学者（如斯科特·哈夫曼）认为，帕子是为了保护心地刚硬的百姓，免得神的荣光在他们身上带来审判。保罗并没有说摩西欺骗百姓；他是说，在旧约之下，神的荣光还是有遮盖的，在新约之下，福音却可以公开、坦然地传讲。

**2. 心上的帕子。** 「但他们的心地刚硬，直到今日诵读旧约的时候，这帕子还没有揭去。这帕子在基督里已经废去了。」（3:14）整本新约只有这里用「旧约」这个说法（希伯来书称为「前约」，来8:13；9:15），指的是摩西之约，也就是在会堂中诵读的经卷。保罗说这话，不是轻看自己的同胞；他为他们「大有忧愁，心里时常伤痛」（罗9:2）。问题不在经文，而在人心；这帕子只有在基督里才能除去。主耶稣自己说：「给我作见证的就是这经。」（约5:39）

**3. 归向主。** 「但他们的心几时归向主，帕子就几时除去了。」（3:16）这句话呼应出埃及记34:34：摩西「进到耶和华面前与他说话就揭去帕子」。任何人，无论是犹太人还是外邦人，只要转向主，帕子就被除去。这也给我们盼望：一个人无论多久看不明白福音，神都能揭去他心上的帕子（参罗11:23），包括我们为之祷告多年、仍未信主的父母家人。许多华人心里也有一层帕子：以为信耶稣只是修身养性、劝人为善，和儒家的修身、佛家的行善差不多。只有转向主，才看见基督是救主，不只是道德的榜样。

**4. 主就是那灵。** 「主就是那灵；主的灵在哪里，那里就得以自由。」（3:17）这句话不是说圣子和圣灵是同一位，因为同一节里又说「主的灵」，把主和圣灵分开；保罗在13:14也清楚地分别了圣父、圣子、圣灵。许多解经家认为，保罗是在解释他刚引用的出埃及记那句话：出埃及记中摩西所归向的那位「主」，今天藉着圣灵与我们相遇。圣灵所在之处，人就得着自由：脱离定罪，脱离帕子，可以坦然来到神面前（来10:19-20）。这自由不是放纵的自由（加5:13），而是作神儿女的自由。

**5. 荣上加荣。** 「我们众人既然敞着脸得以看见主的荣光，好像从镜子里返照，就变成主的形状，荣上加荣，如同从主的灵变成的。」（3:18）
- **「我们众人」：** 在旧约，只有摩西一个人可以揭开帕子见神；在新约，所有的信徒都可以。
- **「好像从镜子里返照」：** 这个词可以理解为在镜子里观看（英文ESV译本这样译），也可以理解为像镜子一样反照（和合本译作「返照」）。无论哪一种，我们都是在看主的荣光，而且这荣光显在「耶稣基督的面上」（4:6）。
- **「变成」：** 这个词就是福音书中主耶稣在山上「变了形象」（太17:2）和罗马书12:2「变化」所用的词。原文的时态表示持续进行，语态是被动的：是圣灵在我们身上不断工作，不是我们自己改变自己。但这不是叫我们什么都不做；罗马书12:2用同一个词吩咐我们「心意更新而变化」，我们要常常仰望主。
- **「主的形状」：** 基督「本是神的像」（4:4）；神预定我们「效法他儿子的模样」（罗8:29）。这工作今生是逐渐的，到主再来的时候才完全：「主若显现，我们必要像他，因为必得见他的真体。」（约一3:2）

> 要点：在基督里，心上的帕子被除去了。所有信徒都可以敞着脸看见主的荣光；我们越看见基督，圣灵就越把我们改变成他的样式，荣上加荣。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我们众人既然敞着脸得以看见主的荣光，好像从镜子里返照，就变成主的形状，荣上加荣，如同从主的灵变成的。」（3:18）我花在审视自己的时间，是不是比仰望主的时间多得多？也许我信主已经好几年，同样的脾气、忧虑、贪心或情欲仍然缠着我；我一次又一次检查自己有没有进步，越查越灰心。保罗说，改变我们的是「主的灵」，而改变的方法是「看见主的荣光」。我不是先变好了才能来到神面前；在基督里，我已经得着「称义的职事」所传的义（3:9）。所以，我可以带着失败，向他认罪，敞着脸来到他面前。今天，我能不能不再只盯着自己的失败，而是花一点时间，在福音书里慢慢地看主耶稣：看他怎样接待罪人，怎样走向十字架，怎样从死里复活？我看他，不是把这当作改掉毛病的新方法，而是因为他本身配得我观看、值得我喜爱。那位「在你们心里动了善工的，必成全这工」（腓1:6）。我改变得很慢，但他从不放弃他所开始的工作。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 has three parts. **A living letter of recommendation** (vv. 1–3): Paul needs no one else's letter, because the Corinthian believers themselves are "a letter from Christ," "written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God." **Ministers of a new covenant** (vv. 4–11): "our sufficiency is from God." Paul compares two ministries. If the "ministry of death, carved in letters on stone" came with glory, the "ministry of the Spirit" and "ministry of righteousness" have far more. **The veil removed** (vv. 12–18): Moses veiled his face, and those who read the old covenant today often have a veil over their hearts; but "when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed." So "we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another."

This chapter is Paul's answer to "Who is sufficient for these things?" (2:16). His qualification is not in letters of recommendation but in God's new covenant work through the Spirit.

## Going Deeper 3:1–3 You Are Our Letter

**1. Letters of recommendation.** "Are we beginning to commend ourselves again? Or do we need, as some do, letters of recommendation to you, or from you?" (3:1). In the early church such letters were common. When Apollos went to Achaia, "the brothers... wrote to the disciples to welcome him" (Acts 18:27), and Paul commends Phoebe in Romans (Rom 16:1). Letters of recommendation were not wrong in themselves. The trouble was that those who came to Corinth used their letters to show they were better qualified than Paul.

**2. A letter written on hearts.** "You yourselves are our letter of recommendation, written on our hearts, to be known and read by all" (3:2). Paul says: you yourselves are my letter. The best evidence for a servant's ministry is not a piece of paper but lives changed by the gospel.

**3. A letter from Christ.** "And you show that you are a letter from Christ delivered by us, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts" (3:3). Christ is the writer; Paul is the one through whom it was written and delivered; the "ink" is the Holy Spirit. "Tablets of stone" recall the two tablets at Sinai, "written with the finger of God" (Exod 31:18). "Tablets of human hearts" recall the prophets' promise that God would write his law "on their hearts" (Jer 31:33) and "remove the heart of stone from their flesh and give them a heart of flesh" (Ezek 11:19; 36:26). Paul says this new covenant promise is being fulfilled in the Corinthian believers.

> The point: The best evidence of ministry is not letters, titles or fame, but the letter Christ writes on human hearts by the Spirit: lives changed by the gospel.

## Going Deeper 3:4–6 Our Sufficiency Is from God

**1. Not in ourselves.** "Such is the confidence that we have through Christ toward God. Not that we are sufficient in ourselves to claim anything as coming from us, but our sufficiency is from God" (3:4–5). This answers 2:16, "Who is sufficient for these things?" Paul is confident, but his confidence is in God, not in himself.

**2. Ministers of a new covenant.** "...who has made us competent to be ministers of a new covenant, not of the letter but of the Spirit" (3:6). The "new covenant" is the covenant Jeremiah promised (Jer 31:31–34), and the one Jesus spoke of at the Last Supper: "This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20). "Spirit" here means the Holy Spirit.

**3. The letter kills, the Spirit gives life.** "For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life" (3:6). This sentence is often misunderstood.
- It does not mean we should read Scripture only for a "spiritual sense" and ignore its plain meaning.
- Nor does it mean rules don't matter as long as our intentions are good.
- Paul means that the law carved on stone is itself "holy and righteous and good" (Rom 7:12), but it is written outside us and cannot change the heart. The law shows sinners their sin and pronounces its penalty (Rom 3:20; 7:10), but it cannot give life. The Spirit writes God's law within and gives new life.

The problem is not the law but the human heart. New covenant grace does not abolish God's will. It makes people love God's will from the heart.

> The point: No one is sufficient in himself to serve God; our sufficiency is from God. The law written on stone can only condemn; the Spirit writes God's word on the heart and gives life.

## Going Deeper 3:7–11 A Greater Glory

**1. The glory on Moses' face.** "Now if the ministry of death, carved in letters on stone, came with such glory that the Israelites could not gaze at Moses' face because of its glory" (3:7). Paul is speaking of Exodus 34. When Moses came down from Sinai he "did not know that the skin of his face shone because he had been talking with God," and the people "were afraid to come near him" (Exod 34:29–30). The old covenant had glory, because God himself made it.

**2. "Being brought to an end."** "...which was being brought to an end" (3:7). The CUV translates this "gradually fading," though Exodus does not say the glow on Moses' face faded. There are two main readings of the word:
- some think Paul means the glow really did fade, a sign that the old covenant was temporary;
- others think the word means "rendered inoperative, set aside" (the same word is translated "what was being brought to an end" in 3:11). On this reading the old covenant's glory was real, but once Christ came it no longer looked bright, like the moon when the sun rises.

Both readings reach the same conclusion: the old covenant had glory, but it pointed to Christ and was temporary.

**3. Three contrasts.** Paul uses three contrasts to show the greater glory of the new covenant:
- "the ministry of death" and "the ministry of the Spirit" (3:7–8);
- "the ministry of condemnation" and "the ministry of righteousness" (3:9);
- "what was being brought to an end" and "what is permanent" (3:11).

Calling the old covenant a ministry "of death" and "of condemnation" does not mean the law is bad. It means sinners standing before God's holy law can only be condemned. The new covenant is a "ministry of righteousness" because it proclaims the righteousness God freely gives: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (5:21).

**4. No glory at all.** "Indeed, in this case, what once had glory has come to have no glory at all, because of the glory that surpasses it" (3:10). A lamp shines brightly at night, but when the sun rises its light can no longer be seen. This does not belittle the old covenant. It exalts Christ.

> The point: The old covenant had glory from God, but the new covenant's glory is far greater: it gives life, it justifies, it lasts. What we receive in Christ is not condemnation but righteousness freely given by God.

## Going Deeper 3:12–18 Unveiled, from Glory to Glory

**1. Bold speech.** "Since we have such a hope, we are very bold, not like Moses, who would put a veil over his face so that the Israelites might not gaze at the outcome of what was being brought to an end" (3:12–13). "Outcome" may mean the end of that glory, or the goal the old covenant pointed to, that is, Christ (cf. Rom 10:4). Some (such as Scott Hafemann) think the veil protected a hardened people from the judgment that God's glory would otherwise bring on them. Paul does not say Moses deceived the people. He means that under the old covenant God's glory was still veiled, while under the new covenant the gospel can be preached openly and boldly.

**2. A veil over the heart.** "But their minds were hardened. For to this day, when they read the old covenant, that same veil remains unlifted, because only through Christ is it taken away" (3:14). This is the only place in the New Testament that uses the phrase "old covenant" (Hebrews speaks of the "first" covenant, Heb 8:13; 9:15), meaning the covenant of Moses, the books read aloud in the synagogue. Paul does not say this in contempt of his own people; for them he has "great sorrow and unceasing anguish" in his heart (Rom 9:2). The veil is not on the text but on the heart, and it is removed only in Christ. Jesus himself said of the Scriptures, "it is they that bear witness about me" (John 5:39).

**3. Turning to the Lord.** "But when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed" (3:16). This echoes Exodus 34:34: "Whenever Moses went in before the LORD to speak with him, he would remove the veil." Anyone, Jew or Gentile, who turns to the Lord has the veil taken away. This gives us hope too: however long someone has failed to see the gospel, God can lift the veil from his heart (cf. Rom 11:23), including parents and relatives we have prayed for over many years. Many Chinese hearts carry a veil of their own: the idea that believing in Jesus is only moral self-cultivation and encouraging goodness, much like Confucian self-cultivation or Buddhist merit. Only by turning to the Lord do we see that Christ is Savior, not merely a moral example.

**4. The Lord is the Spirit.** "Now the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom" (3:17). This does not mean the Son and the Spirit are the same person, for the same verse speaks of "the Spirit of the Lord," distinguishing them, and in 13:14 Paul clearly distinguishes Father, Son and Spirit. Many interpreters think Paul is explaining the Exodus words he has just echoed: "the Lord" to whom Moses turned in that text meets us today through the Spirit. Where the Spirit is, there is freedom: from condemnation, from the veil, freedom to come boldly before God (Heb 10:19–20). It is not freedom for the flesh (Gal 5:13) but the freedom of God's children.

**5. From glory to glory.** "And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit" (3:18).
- **"We all":** under the old covenant only Moses could remove the veil and meet God; under the new covenant every believer can.
- **"Beholding":** the word can mean "beholding as in a mirror" (ESV) or "reflecting like a mirror" (CUV). Either way, we are looking at the Lord's glory, and that glory is seen "in the face of Jesus Christ" (4:6).
- **"Transformed":** this is the word used when Jesus "was transfigured" on the mountain (Matt 17:2) and in Romans 12:2, "be transformed." It is present and passive: the Spirit's continuing work in us. We do not change ourselves, though we are called to keep beholding (and Romans 12:2 makes the same passive a command).
- **"The same image":** Christ "is the image of God" (4:4), and God predestined us "to be conformed to the image of his Son" (Rom 8:29). In this life the work is gradual; it will be complete when the Lord returns: "when he appears we will be like him, because we shall see him as he is" (1 John 3:2).

> The point: In Christ the veil over the heart is removed. Every believer may behold the Lord's glory with unveiled face, and the more we behold Christ, the more the Spirit transforms us into his image, from one degree of glory to another.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the Spirit" (3:18). Do I spend far more time inspecting myself than beholding the Lord? Perhaps I have believed for some years, and the same temper, anxiety, greed or lust still clings to me. I keep checking my progress, and the more I check, the more discouraged I get. Paul says it is "the Lord who is the Spirit" who changes us, and the way he changes us is by our "beholding the glory of the Lord." I do not have to become good before I can come to God. In Christ I already have the righteousness the "ministry of righteousness" proclaims (3:9). So I can come before him with unveiled face, confessing my failures. Today, instead of staring only at my failures, can I spend some time slowly looking at the Lord Jesus in the Gospels: how he welcomes sinners, how he walks toward the cross, how he rises from the dead? I look at him not as a new technique for fixing my faults, but because he himself is worth beholding and worth loving. "He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion" (Phil 1:6). I change slowly, but he never gives up on the work he has begun.
