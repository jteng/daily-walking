## 本章脉络

第四章有三段。**不丧胆的职事**（1-6节）：保罗「蒙怜悯，受了这职分，就不丧胆」；他不用诡诈，只把真理表明出来。人看不见福音的光，是因为「这世界的神弄瞎了心眼」；但那位说「要有光」的神，「已经照在我们心里」。**瓦器里的宝贝**（7-15节）：神把福音放在软弱的人身上，叫人看出「这莫大的能力是出于神」；保罗「四面受敌，却不被困住」，「身上常带着耶稣的死，使耶稣的生也显明在我们身上」。**看不见的永远**（16-18节）：「外体虽然毁坏，内心却一天新似一天」；「至暂至轻的苦楚，要为我们成就极重无比、永远的荣耀」。

这一章开头和结尾都说「我们不丧胆」（4:1、16）。保罗不丧胆，不是因为处境容易，而是因为他知道宝贝是谁的，能力是谁的，结局又在谁手中。

## 深读 4:1-6 神的光照在我们心里

**1. 蒙怜悯，就不丧胆。** 「我们既然蒙怜悯，受了这职分，就不丧胆。」（4:1）保罗得着这职分，不是因为他配得，而是因为「蒙怜悯」。他曾经逼迫教会（林前15:9）。正因为职分是出于怜悯，他就不必靠自己的成绩来证明自己，也不会因为别人的批评而放弃。

**2. 不谬讲神的道理。** 「乃将那些暗昧可耻的事弃绝了；不行诡诈，不谬讲神的道理，只将真理表明出来，好在神面前把自己荐与各人的良心。」（4:2）「谬讲」原文有掺杂、造假的意思，和2:17「为利混乱」相呼应。保罗不靠手段、不靠包装，只把真理公开讲出来，让人的良心在神面前判断。

**3. 福音为什么被蒙蔽？** 「如果我们的福音蒙蔽，就是蒙蔽在灭亡的人身上。此等不信之人被这世界的神弄瞎了心眼，不叫基督荣耀福音的光照着他们。」（4:3-4）「这世界的神」是指撒但，主耶稣称他为「这世界的王」（约12:31；参弗2:2）。这不是说有两个同样大的神；撒但的权势是有限的，而且在神的掌管之下，主耶稣已经宣告他「要被赶出去」。人不信，不只是因为证据不够或道理讲得不好，还有属灵的蒙蔽。所以传福音不只需要讲清楚，也需要祷告。

**4. 传基督耶稣为主。** 「我们原不是传自己，乃是传基督耶稣为主，并且自己因耶稣作你们的仆人。」（4:5）传道人很容易不知不觉地传自己：自己的经历、自己的成就、自己的名声。保罗说，信息的中心是「基督耶稣为主」；传信息的人，只是「仆人」，原文就是奴仆。

**5. 新的创造之光。** 「那吩咐光从黑暗里照出来的神，已经照在我们心里，叫我们得知神荣耀的光显在耶稣基督的面上。」（4:6）这句话让人想起创世记的第一句话：「神说：『要有光』，就有了光。」（创1:3；也有人认为这里同时想到赛9:2「有光照耀他们」）神在起初用话语创造了光；今天，他也用同样的能力，在人黑暗的心里创造光。保罗写这句话的时候，也许想起了他自己在往大马士革的路上，「忽然从天上发光，四面照着他」（徒9:3）。人能信主，是神在人心里做了一件创造的工作。

> 要点：我们能看见基督的荣光，本身就是神怜悯的神迹。人看不见福音，是因为撒但弄瞎了他们的心眼；人能看见，是因为那创造光的神照在他们心里。所以我们不传自己，只传基督耶稣为主，并且为人祷告。如果你还看不见基督的荣耀，今天就求那位说「要有光」的神照亮你的心。

## 深读 4:7-12 带着耶稣的死，显出耶稣的生

**1. 瓦器。** 「我们有这宝贝放在瓦器里，要显明这莫大的能力是出于神，不是出于我们。」（4:7）瓦器便宜、平凡、容易破。神偏偏把最宝贵的福音交给这样的人。这不是神的疏忽，而是他的设计：这样，人看见的就不是器皿，而是神的能力。

**2. 四个「却不」。** 「我们四面受敌，却不被困住；心里作难，却不至失望；遭逼迫，却不被丢弃；打倒了，却不至死亡。」（4:8-9）保罗没有说他不受压、不困惑、不被打倒。他说的是：压力是真实的，困惑是真实的，倒下也是真实的；但神不让他被压碎，不让他绝望，不丢弃他，不让他灭亡。「遭逼迫，却不被丢弃」尤其宝贵：人可以离弃我们，神却不离弃我们。

**3. 耶稣的死与耶稣的生。** 「身上常带着耶稣的死，使耶稣的生也显明在我们身上。」（4:10）这里的「死」，原文不是一般表示死的那个词；许多解经家认为它强调的是正在死去的过程（这个词在罗4:19指撒拉的「生育已经断绝」）。保罗在服事中天天经历软弱、受苦、被消耗，好像身上一直带着耶稣受死的印记；但正是在这样的身体上，复活之主的生命显明出来。这和1:9「叫我们不靠自己，只靠叫死人复活的神」是同一个道理。

**4. 死在我们身上发动，生在你们身上发动。** 「这样看来，死是在我们身上发动，生却在你们身上发动。」（4:12）保罗为哥林多人付上的代价，结出了他们属灵的生命。这是基督的样式：他死了，我们就活了。我们为人受的苦，当然不能赎任何人的罪，却可以成为别人得着生命的管道。

> 要点：神把福音放在瓦器里，好叫人看出能力是出于神。我们会被压、会困惑、会倒下，但不被压碎、不绝望、不被丢弃；在我们软弱、必死的身上，复活之主的生命正在显明。

## 深读 4:13-18 不顾念所见的，乃顾念所不见的

**1. 因信，所以说话。** 「但我们既有信心，正如经上记着说：『我因信，所以如此说话。』我们也信，所以也说话。」（4:13）这句话引自诗篇116:10，保罗用的是希腊文旧约译本的读法，和合本诗篇116:10也是这样翻译的（希伯来文这一节较难翻译，有些译本译法不同）。这是一篇感恩诗，诗人曾在死亡的威胁中蒙神拯救。保罗和那位诗人有同样的信心：在困苦中仍然信，所以仍然说话。

**2. 复活的确据。** 「自己知道那叫主耶稣复活的，也必叫我们与耶稣一同复活，并且叫我们与你们一同站在他面前。」（4:14）保罗能在压力和死亡的威胁中继续传道，是因为他知道结局：神既叫耶稣复活，也必叫我们复活。

**3. 外体毁坏，内心更新。** 「所以，我们不丧胆。外体虽然毁坏，内心却一天新似一天。」（4:16）「外体」是我们会疲倦、会生病、会老去的身体和今生的生命；「内心」是在基督里蒙圣灵更新的里面的人（参罗7:22；弗3:16）。外体在走下坡，内心却在往上走。这不是叫人轻看身体，因为身体将来要复活（4:14）；而是说，身体的衰残不能阻止神在我们里面的工作。

**4. 至暂至轻，极重无比。** 「我们这至暂至轻的苦楚，要为我们成就极重无比、永远的荣耀。」（4:17）保罗受过鞭打、监禁、船坏、饥寒（11:23-27），他不是轻看苦难的人。他说「至暂至轻」，是和将来的荣耀相比：一边是暂时的，一边是永远的；一边是轻的，一边是「极重无比」的。希伯来文的「荣耀」和「沉重」是同一个字根；许多解经家认为，保罗把荣耀说成「极重」，可能是想到了这层字义。这不是说苦难能赚得荣耀；荣耀是神白白的赏赐。但苦难也不只是被荣耀抵消，而是「要为我们成就」荣耀：神使用它来预备我们。

**5. 所见的是暂时的。** 「原来我们不是顾念所见的，乃是顾念所不见的；因为所见的是暂时的，所不见的是永远的。」（4:18）「顾念」原文有定睛、专注的意思。人很自然地被看得见的东西牵着走：病情、账单、别人的眼光。保罗不是叫我们假装看不见，而是叫我们定睛在更真实、更长久的事上。

> 要点：我们的外体会衰残，内心却在基督里一天新似一天。今生的苦难是真实的，但和永远的荣耀相比，是至暂至轻的；神使用它来为我们成就那极重无比的荣耀。

## 默想

**默想：** 「所以，我们不丧胆。外体虽然毁坏，内心却一天新似一天。」（4:16）我是否开始感觉到身体的衰退：精力不如从前，记性变差，体检报告上的问题越来越多，或者一场病叫我再也回不到从前的样子？我是否暗暗害怕，有一天自己会成为儿女的拖累？如果我正在照顾年老的父母，我是否既心疼又疲惫，有时甚至不耐烦，事后又被内疚压着？神没有说外体不会毁坏，他却应许：「直到你们年老，我仍这样；直到你们发白，我仍怀搋。」（赛46:4）主耶稣自己的身体也曾被压伤、被钉、被埋葬，为我的罪受了刑罚；可是神叫他复活了，「那叫主耶稣复活的，也必叫我们与耶稣一同复活」（4:14）。神在这个越来越软弱的瓦器里，仍然在更新我的内心。即使有一天，我（或我的父母）连祷告的话都记不起来，他也不会忘记我们；「我仍怀搋」，是他抱着我们，不是我们抓住他。所见的终要衰残；照在耶稣基督面上的神的荣光，却永不衰残（4:6）。今天，我可以这样祷告：「主啊，我老实告诉你，我怕自己衰老、生病、成为别人的负担；我照顾家人的时候，也常常力不从心、没有耐心。求你赦免我，也抱着我们。求你叫我的眼睛从所见的移开，看见你复活的荣耀。阿们。」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 has three parts. **A ministry that does not lose heart** (vv. 1–6): Paul has "this ministry by the mercy of God" and so does "not lose heart." He uses no cunning; he simply sets out the truth. People cannot see the light of the gospel because "the god of this world has blinded the minds of the unbelievers," but the God who said "Let light shine out of darkness" "has shone in our hearts." **Treasure in jars of clay** (vv. 7–15): God puts the gospel in weak people "to show that the surpassing power belongs to God." Paul is "afflicted in every way, but not crushed," "always carrying in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be manifested in our bodies." **The unseen and eternal** (vv. 16–18): "though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day"; "this slight momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison."

The chapter begins and ends with "we do not lose heart" (4:1, 16). Paul does not lose heart, not because things are easy, but because he knows whose treasure it is, whose power it is, and in whose hands the outcome lies.

## Going Deeper 4:1–6 God Has Shone in Our Hearts

**1. Mercy, and so no losing heart.** "Therefore, having this ministry by the mercy of God, we do not lose heart" (4:1). Paul received this ministry not because he deserved it but "by the mercy of God." He had persecuted the church (1 Cor 15:9). Because the ministry comes from mercy, he does not have to prove himself by results, and he will not give up because of criticism.

**2. Not tampering with God's word.** "But we have renounced disgraceful, underhanded ways. We refuse to practice cunning or to tamper with God's word, but by the open statement of the truth we would commend ourselves to everyone's conscience in the sight of God" (4:2). "Tamper" carries the sense of adulterating or falsifying, echoing the "peddlers" of 2:17. Paul relies on no tricks and no packaging. He sets out the truth openly and lets people's consciences judge before God.

**3. Why is the gospel veiled?** "And even if our gospel is veiled, it is veiled only to those who are perishing. In their case the god of this world has blinded the minds of the unbelievers, to keep them from seeing the light of the gospel of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God" (4:3–4). "The god of this world" is Satan, whom Jesus called "the ruler of this world" (John 12:31; cf. Eph 2:2). This does not mean there are two equal gods. Satan's power is limited and under God's rule, and Jesus has declared that he "will be cast out." People do not believe only because the evidence is thin or the explanation poor; there is spiritual blindness too. So evangelism needs not only clear explanation but prayer.

**4. Proclaiming Jesus Christ as Lord.** "For what we proclaim is not ourselves, but Jesus Christ as Lord, with ourselves as your servants for Jesus' sake" (4:5). Preachers can easily, without noticing, proclaim themselves: their experiences, their achievements, their brand. Paul says the center of the message is "Jesus Christ as Lord," and the messengers are only "servants," literally slaves.

**5. The light of a new creation.** "For God, who said, 'Let light shine out of darkness,' has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (4:6). This recalls the first words of Genesis: "And God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light" (Gen 1:3; some also hear Isa 9:2, "on them has light shined"). In the beginning God created light by his word; today he uses the same power to create light in dark hearts. As Paul wrote this, he may have remembered the road to Damascus, when "suddenly a light from heaven flashed around him" (Acts 9:3). When someone comes to faith, God has done a work of creation in his heart.

> The point: That we can see Christ's glory at all is a miracle of God's mercy. People cannot see the gospel because Satan has blinded their minds; they can see because the God who created light has shone in their hearts. So we do not proclaim ourselves but Jesus Christ as Lord, and we pray for people. If you cannot yet see the glory of Christ, ask the God who said "Let light shine" to shine in your heart today.

## Going Deeper 4:7–12 Carrying the Death of Jesus, Showing the Life of Jesus

**1. Jars of clay.** "But we have this treasure in jars of clay, to show that the surpassing power belongs to God and not to us" (4:7). Clay jars were cheap, ordinary and easily broken. God chose to entrust his most precious gospel to such people. This is not carelessness on God's part but his design: people see not the vessel but God's power.

**2. Four "but not"s.** "We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed" (4:8–9). Paul does not say he is never pressed, never perplexed, never struck down. The pressure is real, the perplexity is real, the falling is real; but God does not let him be crushed, does not let him despair, does not forsake him, does not let him be destroyed. "Persecuted, but not forsaken" is especially precious: people may abandon us, but God does not.

**3. The death of Jesus and the life of Jesus.** "...always carrying in the body the death of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be manifested in our bodies" (4:10). The word for "death" here (*nekrōsis*) is not Paul's usual word. Many take it to stress the process of dying (in Rom 4:19 it describes the "barrenness" of Sarah's womb). In his ministry Paul daily knew weakness, suffering and being worn down, as though carrying in his body the suffering death of Jesus. Yet it was in such a body that the life of the risen Lord was shown. This is the same truth as 1:9, "to make us rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead."

**4. Death at work in us, life in you.** "So death is at work in us, but life in you" (4:12). The cost Paul paid for the Corinthians bore fruit in their spiritual life. This is the pattern of Christ: he died, and we live. Our suffering for others cannot atone for anyone's sin, but it can become a channel through which others receive life.

> The point: God puts the gospel in jars of clay so that people see the power is his. We are pressed, perplexed and struck down, but not crushed, not in despair, not forsaken; in our weak and mortal bodies the life of the risen Lord is being shown.

## Going Deeper 4:13–18 Looking to the Things That Are Unseen

**1. Believing, and so speaking.** "Since we have the same spirit of faith according to what has been written, 'I believed, and so I spoke,' we also believe, and so we also speak" (4:13). Paul quotes Psalm 116:10 in its Greek form (Ps 115:1 in the Greek numbering); the Hebrew is difficult, which is why the ESV of the Psalm differs from Paul's quotation. The psalm is a song of thanks by someone God rescued from the threat of death. Paul shares the psalmist's faith: still believing in affliction, and so still speaking.

**2. The certainty of resurrection.** "...knowing that he who raised the Lord Jesus will raise us also with Jesus and bring us with you into his presence" (4:14). Paul could go on preaching under pressure and the threat of death because he knew the outcome: the God who raised Jesus will raise us too.

**3. Wasting away, renewed.** "So we do not lose heart. Though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day" (4:16). The "outer nature" is our body and earthly life that tire, fall ill and grow old. The "inner nature" is the person being renewed in Christ by the Spirit. The outer is going downhill while the inner is going up. This does not make the body unimportant, for the body will be raised (4:14). It means the body's decline cannot stop God's work in us.

**4. Light and momentary, an eternal weight.** "For this slight momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison" (4:17). Paul had been beaten, imprisoned, shipwrecked, hungry and cold (11:23–27). He did not make light of suffering. He calls it "slight" and "momentary" in comparison with the coming glory: momentary against eternal, slight against a weight "beyond all comparison." The Hebrew word for "glory" (*kābôd*) comes from the same root as "to be heavy," and many interpreters think Paul's "weight of glory" may play on this. This does not mean suffering earns glory; glory is God's free gift. But suffering is not merely outweighed by glory; it "is preparing" glory for us. God uses it to prepare us.

**5. What is seen is transient.** "...as we look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient, but the things that are unseen are eternal" (4:18). "Look" means to fix one's gaze or attention. We are easily led by what we can see: a diagnosis, the bills, other people's opinions. Paul does not tell us to pretend we cannot see them. He tells us to fix our eyes on what is more real and more lasting.

> The point: Our outer nature wastes away, but in Christ our inner nature is renewed day by day. Present suffering is real, but compared with eternal glory it is slight and momentary, and God uses it to prepare for us that weight of glory beyond all comparison.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "So we do not lose heart. Though our outer nature is wasting away, our inner nature is being renewed day by day" (4:16). Am I beginning to feel my body decline: less energy than before, a weaker memory, more problems on each medical report, or an illness after which I will never be quite the same? Do I secretly dread becoming a burden to my children? If I am caring for my parents, am I both heartbroken and worn out, sometimes impatient, and then weighed down by guilt? God has not said our outer nature will not waste away, but he has promised: "even to your old age I am he, and to gray hairs I will carry you" (Isa 46:4). The Lord Jesus' own body was crushed, nailed and buried, bearing the punishment for my sins; but God raised him, and "he who raised the Lord Jesus will raise us also with Jesus" (4:14). In this ever weaker jar of clay, God is still renewing my inner self. Even if one day I (or my parents) can no longer remember the words of a prayer, he will not forget us; "I will carry you" means he holds us, not that we hold on to him. What is seen will fade; the glory of God shining in the face of Jesus Christ never will (4:6). Today I can pray: "Lord, I tell you honestly that I am afraid of growing old, of illness, of becoming a burden to others; and when I care for my family I often have no strength and no patience. Forgive me, and carry us. Turn my eyes from what is seen, to see the glory of your resurrection. Amen."
