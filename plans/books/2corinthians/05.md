## 本章脉络

第五章有三段。**帐棚与房屋**（1-10节）：「我们这地上的帐棚若拆毁了，必得神所造……在天上永存的房屋」；我们「行事为人是凭着信心，不是凭着眼见」，「更愿意离开身体与主同住」；但「我们众人必要在基督台前显露出来」。**基督的爱激励我们**（11-17节）：「一人既替众人死，众人就都死了」；所以我们「不再为自己活」，也「不凭着外貌认人」；「若有人在基督里，他就是新造的人」。**与神和好**（18-21节）：神「藉着基督使我们与他和好」，又把和好的职分交给我们；「神使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义」。

这一章从将来的盼望讲起，再讲到今天的生活和使命，最后落在全本圣经中最精简的福音宣告之一：5:21。

## 深读 5:1-10 帐棚、房屋与基督的审判台

**1. 帐棚与房屋。** 「我们原知道，我们这地上的帐棚若拆毁了，必得神所造，不是人手所造，在天上永存的房屋。」（5:1）保罗自己是织帐棚的（徒18:3）。帐棚是暂时的，会旧、会破、会被拆下；神所造的房屋却是永存的。多数解经家认为，「房屋」是指信徒将来复活的身体（参林前15:42-44）。「必得」原文直译是我们现在就有；许多人认为，这表示这事确定到好像已经拿在手里。也有学者（如默里·哈里斯）认为，这表示信徒死的时候就得着这房屋；还有人认为「房屋」是指天上的居所。无论哪种理解，保罗都确信：帐棚拆毁不是结局。

**2. 不愿赤身，愿意穿上。** 「我们在这帐棚里叹息劳苦，并非愿意脱下这个，乃是愿意穿上那个，好叫这必死的被生命吞灭了。」（5:4）有些希腊哲学认为身体是灵魂的监牢，死亡就是灵魂得释放。保罗却不是这样盼望。他叹息，不是因为想摆脱身体，而是渴望得着复活的身体；「赤身」（5:3）很可能是指死后、复活前灵魂离开身体的状态。保罗最大的盼望是复活，不只是灵魂得释放。

**3. 圣灵作凭据。** 「为此，培植我们的就是神，他又赐给我们圣灵作凭据。」（5:5）这里又用了1:22的「凭据」一词。今天住在我们里面的圣灵，是神给我们的定金，保证将来复活的荣耀一定会来。

**4. 离开身体与主同住。** 「我们坦然无惧，是更愿意离开身体与主同住。」（5:8）在复活以前，信徒死了，就「与主同住」（参腓1:23；路23:43）。这是安慰，因为信徒死后不是进入虚无，也不是成为无处可归、要靠后人祭拜供养的亡魂，而是与主同在。但这还不是最后的结局；最后的结局是身体的复活（4:14）。

**5. 基督台前。** 「因为我们众人必要在基督台前显露出来，叫各人按着本身所行的，或善或恶受报。」（5:10）「台」原文是审判官的座位。哥林多城的广场上就有这样一个台，保罗曾经被人拉到那里，站在迦流面前（徒18:12，「公堂」）。这节经文是对信徒说的。它不是说信徒要在那里重新被审，看能不能得救：「在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了」（罗8:1）。它是说，信徒的一生要在主面前显露出来，按着所行的得赏赐，或者受亏损（林前3:13-15）。改革宗传统（参《威斯敏斯德信条》第33章）也强调，按行为审判会显明一个人的信心是否真实：行为是真信心的果子和凭据，却不是我们被神接纳的根据；我们被接纳的唯一根据，是基督替我们成为罪，我们在他里面成为神的义（5:21）。因信称义不会叫人轻看今生怎样活，反而叫人「立了志向，要得主的喜悦」（5:9）。

> 要点：我们的盼望不是脱离身体，而是复活的身体；圣灵就是这盼望的凭据。在基督里，我们不再被定罪，我们仍要在主面前交账；但因为已经在基督里蒙悦纳，我们乐意立志要得主的喜悦。

## 深读 5:11-17 基督的爱激励我们

**1. 劝人。** 「我们既知道主是可畏的，所以劝人。」（5:11）保罗知道人人都要站在基督台前，所以他劝人。敬畏神和爱人，在他身上是连在一起的。

**2. 颠狂与谨守。** 「我们若果颠狂，是为神；若果谨守，是为你们。」（5:13）可能有人说保罗疯了，或者说他的属灵经历太狂热（「颠狂」也可能指他的异象和启示，参12:1-4）；也有人嫌他太平凡，不够属灵。保罗说，无论别人怎么看，他做的一切，不是为自己，而是为神、为人。

**3. 基督的爱。** 「原来基督的爱激励我们；因我们想，一人既替众人死，众人就都死了。」（5:14）「基督的爱」主要是指基督对我们的爱。「激励」原文有紧紧抓住、催逼的意思：基督的爱像一股力量，抓住保罗，推动他的一生。

**4. 「替众人死」。** 「一人既替众人死」说的是代替：基督替我们死。「众人就都死了」说的是联合：我们在他里面也死了（罗6:6）。「众人」指的是谁？福音派有不同的理解：
- 改革宗传统（如约翰·欧文）认为，这是指所有在基督里的人：凡基督替他死的，也都在他里面死了，这些人就是15节「活着的人」、17节「在基督里」的人；
- 卫斯理宗传统（如约翰·卫斯理）和路德宗认为，这是指世上所有的人：「众人」与19节的「世人」相应，基督为所有人死，但只有信的人得着救恩。

两方都同意：基督的死是代替的死，人必须信靠他才能得救，而福音要向所有人传扬（5:20）。

**5. 不再为自己活。** 「并且他替众人死，是叫那些活着的人不再为自己活，乃为替他们死而复活的主活。」（5:15）基督的死不只是要免去我们的刑罚，也要改变我们活着的方向：从「为自己活」转向「为主活」。

**6. 不凭着外貌认人。** 「所以，我们从今以后，不凭着外貌认人了。虽然凭着外貌认过基督，如今却不再这样认他了。」（5:16）「凭着外貌」原文直译是照着肉体，就是照着世人的标准：出身、学历、财富、口才、外表。保罗信主以前，也是这样看基督的：一个被钉十字架的人，怎么可能是弥赛亚？现在他不再这样看基督，也不再这样看人。

**7. 新造的人。** 「若有人在基督里，他就是新造的人，旧事已过，都变成新的了。」（5:17）原文很简短，可以直译为：若有人在基督里，就有新的创造！这句话让人想起以赛亚的应许：「看哪，我要做一件新事」（赛43:19）；「看哪！我造新天新地」（赛65:17）。神在末日要更新万有，这新创造已经在基督里开始了，每一个在基督里的人，都是这新创造的一部分。

> 要点：基督替我们死，我们也在他里面死了，所以我们不再为自己活。在基督里的人是新造的人：神要更新万有的工作，已经在他身上开始了；我们也不再按世人的标准看人。

## 深读 5:18-21 与神和好

**1. 一切都是出于神。** 「一切都是出于神；他藉着基督使我们与他和好，又将劝人与他和好的职分赐给我们。」（5:18）是人得罪了神，却是神主动寻求和好。在人间，通常是犯错的一方要先去道歉；在福音里，却是被得罪的神先来找我们（参罗5:10）。

**2. 不将过犯归到他们身上。** 「这就是神在基督里，叫世人与自己和好，不将他们的过犯归到他们身上，并且将这和好的道理托付了我们。」（5:19）神怎能不把罪算在人身上？不是因为他不在乎罪，而是因为他把罪归到了基督身上（5:21）。和好是神在十字架上成就的：不只除去了我们对神的敌意，也平息了神因罪而发的公义忿怒。但这不等于人不信也已经得救。人要藉着信才得着这和好，拒绝的人仍在神的震怒之下（约3:36）；所以保罗恳求人「与神和好」（5:20）。

**3. 基督的使者。** 「所以，我们作基督的使者，就好像神藉我们劝你们一般。我们替基督求你们与神和好。」（5:20）使者代表君王说话，自己没有权力改动信息。保罗用的词却很温柔：「劝」「求」。和好的工作在基督里已经完成，人却必须接受；所以福音的使者恳求人：「与神和好！」如果你还没有与神和好，神今天就藉着他的话恳求你。

**4. 伟大的交换。** 「神使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义。」（5:21）
- **「那无罪的」：** 基督完全没有罪（来4:15）。他不是为自己的罪死。
- **「替我们成为罪」：** 不是说基督自己变成了罪人，而是说神把我们的罪归在他身上，使他承受罪的刑罚（赛53:6；加3:13）。也有解经家认为，「罪」在这里也带有赎罪祭的意思，因为希腊文旧约常用同一个词指赎罪祭（参罗8:3；赛53:10）。两种理解都说明：基督代替我们受了罪的审判。
- **「在他里面成为神的义」：** 宗教改革以来，路德宗和改革宗的解经家多认为，这是指信的人与基督联合，神就把那从神而来的义算为他们的（参腓3:9）；改教家称之为基督的义归算给信徒。也有学者（如汤姆·赖特）认为，「神的义」在这里是指神守约的信实；因为上下文讲的是和好的职分（5:18–6:2），他认为「我们」主要指使徒，意思是他们在服事中成了神守约信实的活见证。前一种理解与上文「不将他们的过犯归到他们身上」（5:19）的对比最为吻合，也是本书采取的看法。

> 要点：是被得罪的神主动与我们和好。他把我们的罪归在无罪的基督身上，又把基督的义归给我们。教会的使命，就是作基督的使者，恳求人与神和好。

## 默想

**默想：** 「若有人在基督里，他就是新造的人，旧事已过，都变成新的了。」（5:17）我是否一直活在别人给我贴的标签底下：从小被说「没出息」，被拿来和兄弟姊妹比较，被说「你就是这种人」；或者因为坐过牢、得过某种病、做过某件错事，就觉得自己一辈子都只能是那样的人？我是否也这样看别人，凭着他的过去、学历、收入或口音，就给他下了定论？有些标签是别人强加在我身上的谎言，有些却说中了我真实的罪。前一种，我不必再背。后一种，神没有假装看不见：在神面前，我真实的光景是一个被定罪的罪人。但神把我的罪归在无罪的基督身上：「神使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义。」（5:21）这个最重的判决，主耶稣已经替我承担了（西2:14）。凡悔改、信靠基督的人，神现在看他，是看他在基督里，是一个「新造的人」。如果我还不在基督里，今天就可以悔改，信靠这位替我成为罪的主（6:2）。这不是说过去的伤痛和后果都会立刻消失，而是说，过去不再能定义我是谁。我能不能把一个压在我身上多年的标签告诉神，然后读出这句话：「旧事已过，都变成新的了」？我能不能也求神叫我不再凭着外貌认人，用新的眼光看一位我早已给他定了论的人？我能这样看别人，是因为神先这样看了我：他在基督里给我的新名字，比任何人贴给我的标签都更真。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 has three parts. **Tent and building** (vv. 1–10): "if the tent, which is our earthly home, is destroyed, we have a building from God... eternal in the heavens"; "we walk by faith, not by sight," and "would rather be away from the body and at home with the Lord"; yet "we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ." **The love of Christ controls us** (vv. 11–17): "one has died for all, therefore all have died"; so we "no longer live for ourselves," and "regard no one according to the flesh"; "if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation." **Reconciled to God** (vv. 18–21): God "through Christ reconciled us to himself" and gave us the ministry of reconciliation; "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God."

The chapter moves from future hope to present life and mission, and ends on one of the most compact statements of the gospel in all Scripture: 5:21.

## Going Deeper 5:1–10 Tent, Building and the Judgment Seat of Christ

**1. Tent and building.** "For we know that if the tent, which is our earthly home, is destroyed, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens" (5:1). Paul was himself a tentmaker (Acts 18:3). A tent is temporary; it wears out, tears and is taken down. The building God makes lasts forever. Most interpreters take the "building" to be the believer's future resurrection body (cf. 1 Cor 15:42–44). Paul says "we have," which many take to mean it is as good as in hand, so certain is it. Some (e.g. Murray J. Harris) read "we have" as receiving this building at death; others take the "building" as a heavenly dwelling. On any reading, Paul is sure that the tent's destruction is not the end.

**2. Not unclothed, but further clothed.** "For while we are still in this tent, we groan, being burdened--not that we would be unclothed, but that we would be further clothed, so that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life" (5:4). Some Greek philosophy saw the body as the soul's prison and death as the soul's release. Paul's hope is different. He groans not to be rid of the body but for the resurrection body. "Unclothed" (and "naked," 5:3) probably refers to the state after death and before the resurrection, when the soul is apart from the body. Paul's highest hope is resurrection, not merely the soul set free.

**3. The Spirit as guarantee.** "He who has prepared us for this very thing is God, who has given us the Spirit as a guarantee" (5:5). Here again is the "guarantee" of 1:22. The Spirit who lives in us now is God's down payment, guaranteeing that the glory of the resurrection will surely come.

**4. At home with the Lord.** "Yes, we are of good courage, and we would rather be away from the body and at home with the Lord" (5:8). Before the resurrection, believers who die are "at home with the Lord" (cf. Phil 1:23; Luke 23:43). This is comfort: at death believers do not pass into nothingness, nor become wandering spirits dependent on their descendants' offerings, but are with the Lord. Yet this is not the final end; the final end is the resurrection of the body (4:14).

**5. The judgment seat of Christ.** "For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, so that each one may receive what is due for what he has done in the body, whether good or evil" (5:10). The "judgment seat" (*bēma*) was a judge's tribunal. The forum at Corinth had one, and Paul had once been dragged there before Gallio (Acts 18:12, "the tribunal"). This verse is addressed to believers. It does not mean believers will be tried again to see whether they are saved: "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Rom 8:1). It means a believer's life will be laid open before the Lord, to receive reward or suffer loss according to what he has done (1 Cor 3:13–15). The Reformed tradition (cf. Westminster Confession 33) also stresses that judgment according to works reveals whether faith was genuine: works are the fruit and evidence of true faith, never the ground of our acceptance. That ground is Christ alone, who was made sin for us so that in him we might become the righteousness of God (5:21). Justification by faith does not make us careless about how we live; it makes us "aim to please him" (5:9).

> The point: Our hope is not escape from the body but the resurrection body, and the Spirit is its guarantee. In Christ we are no longer condemned, we will still give account before the Lord; but because we are already accepted in Christ, we gladly make it our aim to please him.

## Going Deeper 5:11–17 The Love of Christ Controls Us

**1. Persuading others.** "Therefore, knowing the fear of the Lord, we persuade others" (5:11). Paul knew that everyone will stand before Christ's judgment seat, so he persuaded people. In him, fearing God and loving people went together.

**2. Beside ourselves, or in our right mind.** "For if we are beside ourselves, it is for God; if we are in our right mind, it is for you" (5:13). Perhaps some said Paul was mad, or that his spiritual experiences were extreme ("beside ourselves" may refer to his visions and revelations, cf. 12:1–4); others found him too ordinary, not spiritual enough. Paul says that whatever others think, all he does is not for himself but for God and for others.

**3. The love of Christ.** "For the love of Christ controls us, because we have concluded this: that one has died for all, therefore all have died" (5:14). "The love of Christ" here is mainly Christ's love for us. "Controls" carries the sense of gripping and pressing on: Christ's love is a force that has hold of Paul and drives his whole life.

**4. "Died for all."** "One has died for all" speaks of substitution: Christ died in our place. "Therefore all have died" speaks of union: in him we died too (Rom 6:6). Evangelicals differ over whom "all" means:
- the Reformed tradition (e.g. John Owen) takes it as all who are in Christ: those for whom he died also died in him, and they are "those who live" (v. 15), those "in Christ" (v. 17);
- the Wesleyan tradition (e.g. John Wesley) and Lutherans take it as everyone in the world: "all" matches "the world" in v. 19; Christ died for all, but only those who believe receive salvation.

Both agree that Christ's death was substitutionary, that people must trust him to be saved, and that the gospel is to be preached to all (5:20).

**5. No longer living for ourselves.** "...and he died for all, that those who live might no longer live for themselves but for him who for their sake died and was raised" (5:15). Christ's death does not only remove our penalty. It changes the direction of our lives, from living for ourselves to living for the Lord.

**6. Regarding no one according to the flesh.** "From now on, therefore, we regard no one according to the flesh. Even though we once regarded Christ according to the flesh, we regard him thus no longer" (5:16). "According to the flesh" means by worldly standards: family background, education, wealth, eloquence, looks. Before his conversion Paul saw Christ that way: how could a crucified man be the Messiah? Now he no longer sees Christ that way, nor anyone else.

**7. A new creation.** "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come" (5:17). The Greek is very terse and can be rendered, "If anyone is in Christ: new creation!" It recalls Isaiah's promises: "Behold, I am doing a new thing" (Isa 43:19); "For behold, I create new heavens and a new earth" (Isa 65:17). The renewal of all things that God will bring at the end has already begun in Christ, and everyone in Christ is part of that new creation.

> The point: Christ died for us, and we died in him, so we no longer live for ourselves. Whoever is in Christ is a new creation: God's work of making all things new has already begun in him, and we no longer judge people by worldly standards.

## Going Deeper 5:18–21 Reconciled to God

**1. All this is from God.** "All this is from God, who through Christ reconciled us to himself and gave us the ministry of reconciliation" (5:18). It was we who offended God, yet God took the initiative to make peace. Among people, the one in the wrong usually has to apologize first; in the gospel, the God who was wronged comes to us first (cf. Rom 5:10).

**2. Not counting their trespasses.** "...that is, in Christ God was reconciling the world to himself, not counting their trespasses against them, and entrusting to us the message of reconciliation" (5:19). How can God not count sins against people? Not because he does not care about sin, but because he counted it against Christ (5:21). Reconciliation was accomplished by God at the cross. It removed not only our hostility to God but the just wrath of God against sin. Yet this does not mean people are saved without believing; that is exactly why Paul pleads, "be reconciled to God" (5:20). It becomes ours through faith, and those who refuse it remain under God's wrath (John 3:36).

**3. Ambassadors for Christ.** "Therefore, we are ambassadors for Christ, God making his appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of Christ, be reconciled to God" (5:20). An ambassador speaks for the king and has no authority to change the message. Yet Paul's words are tender: "appeal," "implore." The work of reconciliation is finished in Christ, but people must receive it, so the gospel's ambassadors plead: "be reconciled to God!" If you are not yet reconciled to God, he is pleading with you today through his word.

**4. The great exchange.** "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (5:21).
- **"Who knew no sin":** Christ was entirely without sin (Heb 4:15). He did not die for sins of his own.
- **"Made him to be sin":** not that Christ himself became a sinner, but that God laid our sin on him, so that he bore its penalty (Isa 53:6; Gal 3:13). Some interpreters think "sin" here also carries the sense of "sin offering," since the Greek Old Testament often uses the same word for the sin offering (cf. Rom 8:3; Isa 53:10). Either way, Christ bore the judgment of sin in our place.
- **"Become the righteousness of God in him":** since the Reformation, Lutheran and Reformed interpreters have mostly taken this to mean that believers, united to Christ, are counted righteous with the righteousness that comes from God (cf. Phil 3:9), what the Reformers called Christ's righteousness credited to us. Some scholars (such as N. T. Wright) take "the righteousness of God" here as God's covenant faithfulness. Because the context is the ministry of reconciliation (5:18–6:2), they read "we" as chiefly the apostles, who in their ministry become the embodiment of God's covenant faithfulness. The first reading fits best with the contrast to "not counting their trespasses against them" (5:19), and it is the view taken here.

> The point: The God we wronged took the initiative to reconcile us to himself. He laid our sin on the sinless Christ and credits Christ's righteousness to us. The church's mission is to be Christ's ambassadors, pleading with people to be reconciled to God.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come" (5:17). Have I lived for years under labels others put on me: told from childhood that I would never amount to anything, compared with my brothers and sisters, told "you're just that kind of person"? Or because I went to prison, or have a certain illness, or did a certain wrong, do I feel I can only ever be that person? Do I label others the same way, judging someone by his past, his education, his income or his accent? Some labels are lies others have put on me; some name sins I really committed. The first kind I no longer have to carry. The second kind God does not pretend not to see: before God, my true condition is that of a condemned sinner. But God laid my sin on the sinless Christ: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (5:21). That heaviest verdict the Lord Jesus has borne for me (Col 2:14). Whoever repents and trusts Christ, God now sees in Christ, as "a new creation." If I am not yet in Christ, I can repent today and trust the Lord who was made sin for me (6:2). This does not mean past wounds and consequences vanish at once. It means the past no longer defines who I am. Can I tell God about one label that has weighed on me for years, and then read aloud, "The old has passed away; behold, the new has come"? And can I ask God to stop me regarding people according to the flesh, and to give me new eyes for someone I settled my verdict on long ago? I can see others this way only because God first saw me this way: the new name he gives me in Christ is truer than any label anyone has put on me.
