## 本章脉络

第六章有三段。**不可徒受神的恩典**（1-2节）：「现在正是悦纳的时候！现在正是拯救的日子。」**神的用人**（3-13节）：保罗列出他在「许多的忍耐、患难、穷乏、困苦」中怎样表明自己是神的用人；他「似乎一无所有，却是样样都有的」。然后他向哥林多人敞开心：「我们向你们，口是张开的，心是宽宏的」，也求他们「照样用宽宏的心」回报。**不要同负一轭**（6:14–7:1）：「你们和不信的原不相配，不要同负一轭」，「因为我们是永生神的殿」。

这一章一方面叫教会把心打开，接纳保罗；另一方面叫教会在拜偶像的事上分别出来。宽宏和分别，看起来方向相反，其实都出于同一位神：他接纳我们作他的儿女，也要我们单单属于他。

## 深读 6:1-2 现在正是拯救的日子

**1. 不可徒受恩典。** 「我们与神同工的，也劝你们不可徒受他的恩典。」（6:1）保罗刚刚说完「你们与神和好」（5:20），现在接着劝：不要让神的恩典在你们身上落空。这不是说恩典要靠人的努力才有效，而是说，领受恩典的人若不让恩典改变他的生活，反而去追随另一个福音（11:4），这恩典在他身上就落空了，这就是「徒受」了恩典。

**2. 悦纳的时候。** 「因为他说：『在悦纳的时候，我应允了你；在拯救的日子，我搭救了你。』看哪，现在正是悦纳的时候！现在正是拯救的日子。」（6:2）这句话引自以赛亚书49:8，原来是神对他的仆人所说的话；这位仆人要作「外邦人的光」（赛49:6）。保罗说，以赛亚所应许的那个日子，现在已经来到了：基督已经来了，死了，又复活了。

**3. 「现在」。** 保罗连说了两次「现在」。福音的邀请不是「等你准备好了再来」，也不是「等退休了再说」「等家里人同意了再说」。神的恩典今天就在这里。对还没有信主的人，这是呼召：不要再拖延。对已经信主的人，这是提醒：不要以为恩典是以后的事，今天就活在其中。

> 要点：以赛亚所应许的拯救之日，已经在基督里来到了。神的恩典今天就向人敞开，不要把它推到明天，也不要让它在我们身上落空。

## 深读 6:3-10 似乎一无所有，却是样样都有

**1. 不叫人有妨碍。** 「我们凡事都不叫人有妨碍，免得这职分被人毁谤。」（6:3）保罗知道，传道人的生活可以帮助人信福音，也可以成为人信福音的绊脚石。

**2. 一份不寻常的履历。** 「反倒在各样的事上表明自己是神的用人，就如在许多的忍耐、患难、穷乏、困苦、鞭打、监禁、扰乱、勤劳、警醒、不食。」（6:4-5）假使徒用荐信、口才和成功来证明自己；保罗用来证明自己的，却是他受的苦。这份履历在世人看来是失败的记录，在神看来却是忠心的记号。

**3. 内在的品格。** 「廉洁、知识、恒忍、恩慈、圣灵的感化、无伪的爱心、真实的道理、神的大能。」（6:6-7）保罗表明自己是神的用人，不只靠忍受苦难，也靠圣灵结出的品格。「圣灵的感化」原文只是「圣灵」一词，多数解经家认为这里指的就是圣灵自己：在一连串的美德中间，保罗提到圣灵自己，因为这一切都是从他而来的。

**4. 左右手的兵器。** 「仁义的兵器在左在右。」（6:7）古时的士兵右手拿刀，左手拿盾。保罗的意思可能是，他无论进攻还是防守，都只用公义的方法，不用诡诈和手段（参4:2）。

**5. 七个「似乎……却是」。** 「似乎是诱惑人的，却是诚实的；似乎不为人所知，却是人所共知的；似乎要死，却是活着的；似乎受责罚，却是不至丧命的；似乎忧愁，却是常常快乐的；似乎贫穷，却是叫许多人富足的；似乎一无所有，却是样样都有的。」（6:8-10）别人怎么看保罗是一回事，在神面前的真实又是另一回事。他「似乎贫穷，却是叫许多人富足」，这正是基督的样式：「他本来富足，却为你们成了贫穷，叫你们因他的贫穷，可以成为富足」（8:9）。

> 要点：神的用人在基督里样样都有，所以不必靠成功的履历证明自己，可以在苦难中忍耐，在圣灵里活出真实的品格。

## 深读 6:11-13 心是宽宏的

**1. 张开的口，宽宏的心。** 「哥林多人哪，我们向你们，口是张开的，心是宽宏的。」（6:11）保罗在书信中很少这样直呼收信人（另见加3:1「无知的加拉太人哪」；腓4:15「腓立比人哪」）。他说他已经把话都说出来了，心也向他们完全打开。

**2. 你们的心肠狭窄。** 「你们狭窄，原不在乎我们，是在乎自己的心肠狭窄。」（6:12）「心肠」原文指人的内脏，古人认为那是感情所在的地方。保罗的意思是：我们之间的距离，不是因为我关上了门，而是因为你们的心冷了、窄了。也许是因为那次痛苦的探访，也许是因为假使徒的挑拨，哥林多人对保罗有了戒心。

**3. 像父亲对孩子说话。** 「你们也要照样用宽宏的心报答我。我这话正像对自己的孩子说的。」（6:13）保罗是哥林多教会属灵的父亲（林前4:15）。父母被儿女误解的时候，心里的痛常常比被外人误解更深。保罗没有用权柄压他们，而是像父亲一样恳求：请把你们的心也向我打开。

> 要点：保罗向一个曾经伤害他的教会敞开自己的心，并且恳求他们也敞开心；他能这样，是因为基督先向他敞开了心。关系冷淡的时候，我们可以诚实地问：是不是有时候，是我自己的心肠变窄了？

## 深读 6:14–7:1 不要同负一轭

**1. 不相配的轭。** 「你们和不信的原不相配，不要同负一轭。」（6:14）「同负一轭」这个词，在希腊文旧约中出现在利19:19（不可叫牲畜与异类配合）；画面则让人想起「不可并用牛、驴耕地」（申22:10）。牛和驴力气不同、步伐不同，套在同一个轭下，就无法同心耕地。

**2. 五个问题。** 「义和不义有什么相交呢？光明和黑暗有什么相通呢？基督和彼列有什么相和呢？信主的和不信主的有什么相干呢？神的殿和偶像有什么相同呢？」（6:14-16）「彼列」在希伯来文旧约中是无用、邪恶的意思（撒上2:12的「恶人」，原文就是彼列的儿子），后来在犹太文献（如死海古卷）中成了撒但的一个名字。这五个问题的答案都是：没有。

**3. 这里指的是什么？** 从上下文看，保罗最直接关心的，是哥林多人与拜偶像的事（6:16；参林前10:14-22），也可能包括那些叫人「另得一个福音」的假使徒（11:4、13-15）。但这个原则也适用于别的关系：凡会把信徒和不信的人在属灵方向上绑在一起的，都要留意。教会很早就把它用在婚姻上，因为保罗在别处也说信徒「只是要嫁这在主里面的人」（林前7:39）。同时要留意三件事：
- 这不是叫信徒离开世界，不与不信的人来往（林前5:10）；
- 这也不是叫已经和不信主的配偶结婚的信徒离婚（林前7:12-13），因为「不信的丈夫就因着妻子成了圣洁」（林前7:14）；当然，这也不是要受虐待的一方留在危险中；
- 这是关于属灵方向上的捆绑，例如在婚姻、宗教活动（像家族祭拜祖先、庙会、生意开张拜神），或要违背良心的合伙生意中，和不信的人绑在同一条路上。

**4. 永生神的殿。** 「因为我们是永生神的殿，就如神曾说：我要在他们中间居住，在他们中间来往；我要作他们的神；他们要作我的子民。」（6:16）保罗把几处旧约经文合在一起（利26:11-12；结37:27；赛52:11；结20:34；撒下7:8、14）。神在旧约应许要住在他百姓中间，这应许今天在教会身上成就了：教会就是神的殿。

**5. 作神的儿女。** 「我要作你们的父；你们要作我的儿女。这是全能的主说的。」（6:18）神原本对大卫的后裔说「我要作他的父，他要作我的子」（撒下7:14），保罗在这里把它扩大为「儿女」（参赛43:6「我的众子」「我的众女」）。分别出来，不是为了失去什么，而是因为我们已经属于一位父亲。

**6. 洁净自己。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，我们既有这等应许，就当洁净自己，除去身体、灵魂一切的污秽，敬畏神，得以成圣。」（7:1）次序很重要：先有应许，后有洁净。我们不是为了成为神的儿女才洁净自己；我们已经是他的儿女，所以要活得像他的儿女。

有些学者认为6:14–7:1这一段和上下文接得不顺，可能是后来放进来的。不过，现存所有希腊文抄本都有这一段，而且都在这个位置。今天多数解经家，包括福音派解经家，都认为它与上下文是连在一起的：保罗要哥林多人向他敞开心（6:13；7:2），同时也要他们向偶像和假使徒关上门。

> 要点：信徒是永生神的殿、全能者的儿女，所以不可与拜偶像和不信的事同负一轭。这不是叫我们离开世界，而是因为我们已经属于神，要单单属于他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「哥林多人哪，我们向你们，口是张开的，心是宽宏的。你们狭窄，原不在乎我们，是在乎自己的心肠狭窄。你们也要照样用宽宏的心报答我。」（6:11-13）我的心是否因为过去的伤害，对教会、对某位牧者、对某些弟兄姊妹关上了？也许我曾经被误会、被冷落，或者在一次教会纷争中受了伤；从那以后，我虽然还来聚会，却总是站在边上，不再信任任何人，不再投入。或者，我是否也曾因为几句闲话，就对一位忠心的牧者起了戒心？保罗被这个教会伤得很深，他的心却仍然向他们敞开。他能这样，是因为基督先向他敞开了心：主耶稣被他自己的人拒绝，被门徒离弃，在十字架上仍然为钉他的人祷告（路23:34）；他张开双臂，接纳了我这个曾经与他为敌的人（5:18；罗5:10）。当然，如果我受的伤害是性侵、暴力、属灵操控或其他不安全的事，宽宏不等于要我回到危险里，不等于要我重新信任那个伤害我的人，更不等于要我为了教会的面子保持沉默。向警方或有关部门报案是正当的（罗13:4），教会也不可以用「家丑不可外扬」为理由把事情压下去。我可以寻求帮助、讲出真相，在一个安全的地方（也可以是另一间教会）重新学习信任。如果伤害是真实的，只是不涉及安全，主也不要我假装没事；我可以先在他面前哀哭，宽宏的心常常是慢慢打开的。但如果我发现，我的心变窄，更多是因为失望、骄傲，或者听信了别人背后的话，我能不能今天向神承认，求他用基督宽宏的爱，把我的心重新打开一点？在我的心还没有打开以前，他的心早已向我敞开；他不会因为我打开得慢，就向我关上门。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 has three parts. **Do not receive God's grace in vain** (vv. 1–2): "Behold, now is the favorable time; behold, now is the day of salvation." **Servants of God** (vv. 3–13): Paul lists how he commends himself as God's servant "by great endurance, in afflictions, hardships, calamities," "as having nothing, yet possessing everything." Then he opens his heart to the Corinthians: "We have spoken freely to you, Corinthians; our heart is wide open," and asks them in return to "widen your hearts also." **Do not be unequally yoked** (6:14–7:1): "Do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers... For we are the temple of the living God."

This chapter calls the church to open its heart and welcome Paul, and also to come out and be separate from idolatry. Widening and separating seem to pull in opposite directions, but both come from the same God: he welcomes us as his children, and he wants us to belong to him alone.

## Going Deeper 6:1–2 Now Is the Day of Salvation

**1. Not in vain.** "Working together with him, then, we appeal to you not to receive the grace of God in vain" (6:1). Paul has just said "be reconciled to God" (5:20), and now he urges: do not let God's grace come to nothing in you. This does not mean grace depends on human effort to work. It means that someone who receives grace but does not let it change his life, and instead follows "a different gospel" (11:4), has in effect received it for nothing.

**2. The favorable time.** "For he says, 'In a favorable time I listened to you, and in a day of salvation I have helped you.' Behold, now is the favorable time; behold, now is the day of salvation" (6:2). Paul quotes Isaiah 49:8, where God speaks to his Servant, who will be "a light for the nations" (Isa 49:6). Paul says the day Isaiah promised has now come: Christ has come, died and risen.

**3. "Now."** Paul says "now" twice. The gospel's invitation is not "come when you're ready," nor "wait until you retire" or "wait until your family agrees." God's grace is here today. For those who do not yet believe, this is a call: don't put it off any longer. For believers, it is a reminder: grace is not for later; live in it today.

> The point: The day of salvation Isaiah promised has come in Christ. God's grace is open to us today; do not put it off until tomorrow, and do not let it come to nothing in us.

## Going Deeper 6:3–10 Having Nothing, Yet Possessing Everything

**1. No obstacle.** "We put no obstacle in anyone's way, so that no fault may be found with our ministry" (6:3). Paul knew that a preacher's life can help people believe the gospel, or become a stumbling block to it.

**2. An unusual résumé.** "...but as servants of God we commend ourselves in every way: by great endurance, in afflictions, hardships, calamities, beatings, imprisonments, riots, labors, sleepless nights, hunger" (6:4–5). The false apostles proved themselves by letters, eloquence and success. Paul proves himself by what he has suffered. To the world this résumé looks like a record of failure; to God it is the mark of faithfulness.

**3. Character within.** "...by purity, knowledge, patience, kindness, the Holy Spirit, genuine love, by truthful speech, and the power of God" (6:6–7). Paul relies not only on enduring hardship but on the character the Spirit produces. In the middle of a list of virtues the Greek simply says "holy spirit"; most take it as the Holy Spirit himself, because all of them come from him.

**4. Weapons for right hand and left.** "...with the weapons of righteousness for the right hand and for the left" (6:7). An ancient soldier held a sword in his right hand and a shield in his left. Paul may mean that in attack and defense alike he uses only righteous means, never cunning or manipulation (cf. 4:2).

**5. Seven "as... yet"s.** "We are treated as impostors, and yet are true; as unknown, and yet well known; as dying, and behold, we live; as punished, and yet not killed; as sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, yet possessing everything" (6:8–10). How others see Paul is one thing; the truth before God is another. "As poor, yet making many rich" is the very pattern of Christ: "though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you by his poverty might become rich" (8:9).

> The point: Because God's servants possess everything in Christ, they need no résumé of success; they can endure hardship and live out the genuine character the Spirit forms.

## Going Deeper 6:11–13 Our Heart Is Wide Open

**1. An open mouth, a wide heart.** "We have spoken freely to you, Corinthians; our heart is wide open" (6:11). Paul rarely addresses a church directly by name like this (cf. Gal 3:1; Phil 4:15). He has said everything, and his heart is fully open to them.

**2. Restricted affections.** "You are not restricted by us, but you are restricted in your own affections" (6:12). "Affections" is literally the inner organs, which the ancients saw as the seat of feeling. Paul means: the distance between us is not because I have shut the door, but because your affection has shrunk. Perhaps because of the painful visit, perhaps because the false apostles stirred them up, the Corinthians had grown guarded toward Paul.

**3. As a father to his children.** "In return (I speak as to children) widen your hearts also" (6:13). Paul is the Corinthian church's spiritual father (1 Cor 4:15). When parents are misunderstood by their children, the pain is often deeper than being misunderstood by strangers. Paul does not press them with his authority; like a father he pleads: open your hearts to me too.

> The point: Paul opens his heart to a church that had hurt him, and pleads with them to open theirs. He could, because Christ had first opened his heart to him. When a relationship grows cold, we can honestly ask whether, at times, it is our own heart that has narrowed.

## Going Deeper 6:14–7:1 Do Not Be Unequally Yoked

**1. A mismatched yoke.** "Do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers" (6:14). The related word appears in the Greek Old Testament at Lev 19:19 (not letting cattle breed with a different kind), and the image recalls "You shall not plow with an ox and a donkey together" (Deut 22:10). An ox and a donkey differ in strength and stride; yoked together they cannot plow as one.

**2. Five questions.** "For what partnership has righteousness with lawlessness? Or what fellowship has light with darkness? What accord has Christ with Belial? Or what portion does a believer share with an unbeliever? What agreement has the temple of God with idols?" (6:14–16). "Belial" in the Hebrew Old Testament means "worthlessness" (e.g. 1 Sam 2:12, "worthless men," literally "sons of Belial"), and in later Jewish writings such as the Dead Sea Scrolls it became a name for Satan. The answer to all five questions is: none.

**3. What is in view?** From the context, Paul's most immediate concern is the Corinthians' involvement with idolatry (6:16; cf. 1 Cor 10:14–22), and possibly the false apostles who preached "a different gospel" (11:4, 13–15). But the principle also applies to other relationships that bind a believer to an unbeliever in spiritual direction. The church has long applied it to marriage, since Paul elsewhere says a believer may marry "only in the Lord" (1 Cor 7:39). At the same time, note three things:
- this does not tell believers to leave the world and avoid unbelievers (1 Cor 5:10);
- it does not tell believers already married to an unbelieving spouse to divorce (1 Cor 7:12–13), for "the unbelieving husband is made holy because of his wife" (1 Cor 7:14); nor does it require anyone to remain in danger from an abusive spouse;
- it concerns being bound in spiritual direction, for example in marriage, in religious practice (such as ancestral rites, temple festivals, or worshiping idols at a business opening), or in a partnership that would require going against conscience, binding one to an unbeliever's road.

**4. The temple of the living God.** "For we are the temple of the living God; as God said, 'I will make my dwelling among them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people'" (6:16). Paul weaves together several Old Testament texts (Lev 26:11–12; Ezek 37:27; Isa 52:11; Ezek 20:34; 2 Sam 7:8, 14). God promised in the Old Testament to dwell among his people; that promise is fulfilled today in the church, which is God's temple.

**5. Sons and daughters.** "...and I will be a father to you, and you shall be sons and daughters to me, says the Lord Almighty" (6:18). God originally said of David's offspring, "I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son" (2 Sam 7:14). Paul widens it to "sons and daughters" (cf. Isa 43:6, "my sons... my daughters"). Being separate is not about losing something; it is because we already belong to a Father.

**6. Cleansing ourselves.** "Since we have these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit, bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God" (7:1). The order matters: the promises first, then the cleansing. We do not cleanse ourselves to become God's children; we are already his children, so we live like his children.

Some scholars think 6:14–7:1 fits awkwardly in its context and may have been inserted later. Yet every surviving Greek manuscript contains the passage, and in this place. Most commentators today, including evangelical ones, think it belongs where it is: Paul wants the Corinthians to open their hearts to him (6:13; 7:2) and at the same time to shut the door on idols and false apostles.

> The point: Believers are the temple of the living God and children of the Almighty, so they must not be yoked with idolatry and unbelief. This is not a call to leave the world; it is because we already belong to God and are to belong to him alone.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "We have spoken freely to you, Corinthians; our heart is wide open. You are not restricted by us, but you are restricted in your own affections. In return (I speak as to children) widen your hearts also" (6:11–13). Has past hurt closed my heart toward the church, toward a pastor, toward certain brothers and sisters? Perhaps I was misunderstood or ignored, or wounded in a church conflict. Since then I still come to services, but I stay on the edge, trust no one and hold back. Or have I grown guarded toward a faithful pastor because of a few rumors? Paul had been deeply hurt by this church, yet his heart stayed open to them. He could do this because Christ first opened his heart to him. The Lord Jesus was rejected by his own people and deserted by his disciples, and on the cross he still prayed for those who crucified him (Luke 23:34). He opened his arms and welcomed me, who was once his enemy (5:18; Rom 5:10). Of course, if what I suffered was sexual abuse, violence, spiritual manipulation or anything unsafe, widening my heart does not mean going back into danger, or trusting again the person who harmed me, or keeping quiet to protect the church's reputation. Reporting it to the police or the proper authorities is right (Rom 13:4), and a church must never hush it up to save face. I can seek help, tell the truth, and learn to trust again in a safe place, which may be a different church. If the hurt was real but not a matter of safety, the Lord does not ask me to pretend it did not happen; I can first lament before him, and a heart usually widens slowly. But if I see that my heart has narrowed more through disappointment, pride, or listening to talk behind someone's back, can I confess it to God today, and ask him to open my heart a little again with the wide love of Christ? Before my heart is open, his is already open to me, and he will not shut his door on me because mine opens slowly.
