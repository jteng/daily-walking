## 本章脉络

第八、九两章讲的是同一件事：为耶路撒冷穷困的圣徒捐钱。第八章有三段。**马其顿众教会的榜样**（1-7节）：他们「在极穷之间还格外显出他们乐捐的厚恩」，因为他们「先把自己献给主」。**基督的恩典与均平的原则**（8-15节）：「他本来富足，却为你们成了贫穷」；捐献要照「所有的」，不是照「所无的」；「多收的也没有余；少收的也没有缺」。**诚实地处理钱财**（16-24节）：保罗派提多和两位弟兄一同去办这事，「免得有人因我们收的捐银很多，就挑我们的不是」。

保罗在这一章里很少直接提「钱」，却一再用「恩」这个词（8:1、4、6、7、9、16、19，和合本译作「恩」「恩情」「慈惠的事」「恩典」「多谢」「捐资」；第九章8、14、15节又出现，连最后一句「感谢神」也是这个词）。在保罗看来，捐献不是筹款，而是恩典在人身上流动的结果。

## 深读 8:1-7 先把自己献给主

**1. 这次捐献的背景。** 耶路撒冷的信徒很穷，原因可能有几个：早年的饥荒带来长期的困难（徒11:28-30，那时安提阿教会已经送过一次救济）；信徒受逼迫；许多人被家人和社会排斥。耶路撒冷的使徒曾嘱咐保罗「记念穷人」（加2:10）。保罗多年来在外邦教会中为这事募捐（林前16:1-4；罗15:25-27；参徒24:17）。这不只是救济，也是外邦教会与犹太信徒彼此相连的记号：「外邦人既然在他们属灵的好处上有分，就当把养身之物供给他们。」（罗15:27）

**2. 神赐的恩。** 「弟兄们，我把神赐给马其顿众教会的恩告诉你们。」（8:1）马其顿的教会包括腓立比、帖撒罗尼迦和庇哩亚。保罗没有说「马其顿人很慷慨」，而是说这是「神赐给」他们的恩。慷慨本身就是恩典的果子。

**3. 极穷之中的厚恩。** 「就是他们在患难中受大试炼的时候，仍有满足的快乐，在极穷之间还格外显出他们乐捐的厚恩。」（8:2）这些教会自己正在受逼迫（帖前1:6；腓1:29-30），而且非常穷。按常理，患难加上贫穷，只会叫人抓紧手里的东西；可是再加上喜乐，结果竟是慷慨。

**4. 再三地求我们。** 「我可以证明，他们是按着力量，而且也过了力量，自己甘心乐意地捐助，再三地求我们，准他们在这供给圣徒的恩情上有分。」（8:3-4）保罗原本大概不好意思向这么穷的教会募捐，反倒是他们「再三地求」保罗，让他们有份。

**5. 先把自己献给主。** 「并且他们所做的，不但照我们所想望的，更照神的旨意先把自己献给主，又归附了我们。」（8:5）这是一切慷慨的根源：先把自己献给主。人若先把自己交给主，钱财就不再是他的主人。

> 要点：慷慨是神赐的恩典，不是人天生的美德。马其顿的信徒在患难和极穷之中仍然乐意捐献，因为他们先把自己献给了主。

## 深读 8:8-15 基督的恩典与均平

**1. 不是吩咐。** 「我说这话，不是吩咐你们，乃是藉着别人的热心试验你们爱心的实在。」（8:8）保罗是使徒，本可以下命令，但他不这样做。被强迫的捐献不是爱。

**2. 基督的恩典。** 「你们知道我们主耶稣基督的恩典：他本来富足，却为你们成了贫穷，叫你们因他的贫穷，可以成为富足。」（8:9）这是全章的中心，也是保罗劝人捐献的真正理由。
- **「本来富足」：** 指基督在成为人以前与父同享的荣耀（约17:5；腓2:6）。
- **「成了贫穷」：** 指他道成肉身（他成了人，却没有停止作神），生在马槽，一生「没有枕头的地方」（太8:20），最后在十字架上替我们的罪受死（5:21；腓2:7-8）。
- **「可以成为富足」：** 这里的富足不是指钱财，而是指罪得赦免、与神和好、作神的儿女、得着永远的产业。有人用这节经文说信耶稣一定会发财，这完全误解了保罗（参9:11）：保罗自己「似乎贫穷，却是叫许多人富足」（6:10）。

保罗不是用罪疚感，也不是用别人的眼光来推动捐献，而是用基督的恩典：你们已经从他的贫穷中得着了这么大的富足，现在也可以让别人从你们的富余中得着帮助。

**3. 照你们所有的。** 「如今就当办成这事。既有愿做的心，也当照你们所有的去办成。因为人若有愿做的心，必蒙悦纳，乃是照他所有的，并不是照他所无的。」（8:11-12）哥林多人一年前就开始了，后来停了下来，大概是因为与保罗的关系出了问题。保罗劝他们完成。同时他说得很清楚：神看的是愿意的心，不是数目。教会不可以要人借钱奉献，不可以叫穷人觉得自己捐得少就低人一等，也不可以用「奉献越多，神越祝福」的话给人压力，也不宜在周报上公布奉献芳名和数目，叫人彼此比较。

**4. 均平。** 「我原不是要别人轻省，你们受累，乃要均平，就是要你们的富余，现在可以补他们的不足，使他们的富余，将来也可以补你们的不足，这就均平了。」（8:13-14）「均平」不是要所有人的财产都一样，也不是强制的平均分配，而是彼此补足：今天你有余，就补别人的不足；明天别人有余，也会补你的不足。「他们的富余，将来也可以补你们的不足」，有人理解为将来物质上的回报，也有人照罗马书15:27理解为外邦人从耶路撒冷得着的属灵好处；无论哪一种，原则都是彼此补足。

**5. 吗哪。** 「如经上所记：多收的也没有余；少收的也没有缺。」（8:15）这句话引自出埃及记16:18。以色列人在旷野收吗哪，神叫每个人都够用，多收的没有余，少收的也不缺；想留到第二天的，就生虫变臭了（出16:16-20）。神是供应的神；他的百姓也要彼此分享，像同一家人一样。

> 要点：捐献的真正动力是基督的恩典：他本来富足，却为我们成了贫穷。神看的是愿意的心，不是数目；信徒彼此补足，就像旷野中人人都有吗哪。

## 深读 8:16-24 在人面前也行光明的事

**1. 提多。** 「多谢神，感动提多的心，叫他待你们殷勤，像我一样。」（8:16）提多刚从哥林多回来，现在又自愿回去办这事。

**2. 一位有名的弟兄。** 「我们还打发一位兄弟和他同去，这人在福音上得了众教会的称赞。不但这样，他也被众教会挑选，和我们同行。」（8:18-19）这位弟兄是谁，保罗没有说。有些早期教父猜测是路加（如俄利根、耶柔米），屈梭多模提到路加或巴拿巴，但都只是推测。重要的是，他是「被众教会挑选」的，不是保罗自己指定的。

**3. 免得有人挑我们的不是。** 「这就免得有人因我们收的捐银很多，就挑我们的不是。我们留心行光明的事，不但在主面前，就在人面前也是这样。」（8:20-21）保罗知道，钱是最容易引起怀疑的地方，所以他刻意让几个人一起经手，而且其中有教会选出来的代表。保罗这句话几乎逐字取自箴言3:4的七十士译本（旧约希腊文译本），那里说要在主和人面前留心行美善的事（和合本照希伯来文译作「在神和世人眼前蒙恩宠」）。他在罗马书12:17也说：「众人以为美的事要留心去做。」对神诚实是根本，但在人面前也要做到清清楚楚。

**4. 今天的提醒。** 教会处理钱财，也应当：
- 由多人共同经手，不由一个人独自管理；
- 账目公开、定期查核；
- 让会众知道钱用在哪里。

华人教会常碍于人情和面子，不好意思查账、不好意思过问。其实这样做不是不信任同工，而是保护同工，也免得神的名受羞辱。这也适用于小组、团契的经费，或者替人代收的钱：再小的数目，也找另一个人一同点算、留下记录，叫人在钱上看见的是基督的诚实，不是我们的精明。

> 要点：保罗处理钱财时，不但在主面前，也在人面前行光明的事。教会今天也要用透明、多人经手和公开查账来保护同工，并且荣耀主的名。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们知道我们主耶稣基督的恩典：他本来富足，却为你们成了贫穷，叫你们因他的贫穷，可以成为富足。」（8:9）我是否常常觉得「等我有余了再说」：等房贷还完、孩子读完书、生意稳定、退休金存够了，才谈得上慷慨？我心底是不是总怕钱不够用，好像只要一松手，生活就会垮掉？马其顿的信徒在极穷之间仍然乐意捐献，不是因为他们不怕穷，而是因为他们「先把自己献给主」，相信神是那位叫旷野中「少收的也没有缺」的神。主耶稣也不是从他的富余里分一点给我；他为我放下天上的荣耀，成了贫穷，甚至舍了自己的性命。神不是看数目，而是看「愿做的心」（8:12）。今天，我能不能把我对钱的惧怕带到神面前，向他承认我常常靠钱、不靠他，然后先把自己重新献给主？然后，我可以安静地问他：我所有的，有哪些是他要我拿出来，与有需要的人分享的？如果我现在真的入不敷出、背着债，神并不要我照「所无的」去给（8:12）；我可以先把这份窘迫老实告诉他。无论我能给多少，我都已经因主的贫穷成了富足：罪得赦免，作了神的儿女。这份富足，没有人能从我手里拿走。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapters 8 and 9 deal with one subject: the collection for the poor among the saints in Jerusalem. Chapter 8 has three parts. **The example of the Macedonian churches** (vv. 1–7): "their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity," because "they gave themselves first to the Lord." **The grace of Christ and the principle of fairness** (vv. 8–15): "though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor"; giving is "according to what a person has, not according to what he does not have"; "Whoever gathered much had nothing left over, and whoever gathered little had no lack." **Handling money with integrity** (vv. 16–24): Paul sends Titus and two brothers to handle it, "so that no one should blame us about this generous gift."

In this chapter Paul hardly uses the word "money," but again and again uses the word "grace" (*charis*, 8:1, 4, 6, 7, 9, 16, 19; the ESV renders it "grace," "favor," "act of grace," "thanks"; it returns in 9:8, 14, 15, where even "Thanks be to God" is *charis*). For him giving is not fundraising but the result of grace flowing through people.

## Going Deeper 8:1–7 They Gave Themselves First to the Lord

**1. The background of the collection.** The believers in Jerusalem were poor, probably because of lasting hardship after the earlier famine (Acts 11:28–30, when Antioch had already sent relief), persecution, and the rejection many faced from family and society. The Jerusalem apostles had asked Paul to "remember the poor" (Gal 2:10). For years Paul gathered this collection in the Gentile churches (1 Cor 16:1–4; Rom 15:25–27; cf. Acts 24:17). It was more than relief; it was a sign binding the Gentile churches to Jewish believers: "For if the Gentiles have come to share in their spiritual blessings, they ought also to be of service to them in material blessings" (Rom 15:27).

**2. The grace of God given.** "We want you to know, brothers, about the grace of God that has been given among the churches of Macedonia" (8:1). The Macedonian churches included Philippi, Thessalonica and Berea. Paul does not say "the Macedonians are generous." He says it is "the grace of God... given" to them. Generosity itself is the fruit of grace.

**3. Generosity in extreme poverty.** "...for in a severe test of affliction, their abundance of joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity on their part" (8:2). These churches were themselves being persecuted (1 Thess 1:6; Phil 1:29–30), and they were very poor. Yet affliction plus poverty, with joy, overflowed in generosity.

**4. Begging us earnestly.** "For they gave according to their means, as I can testify, and beyond their means, of their own free will, begging us earnestly for the favor of taking part in the relief of the saints" (8:3–4). Paul was probably reluctant to ask such poor churches. It was they who begged him to let them take part.

**5. Themselves first to the Lord.** "...and this, not as we expected, but they gave themselves first to the Lord and then by the will of God to us" (8:5). This is the root of all generosity: giving ourselves first to the Lord. When people give themselves to the Lord first, money is no longer their master.

> The point: Generosity is a gift of God's grace, not a natural virtue. The Macedonian believers gave gladly in affliction and extreme poverty because they had first given themselves to the Lord.

## Going Deeper 8:8–15 The Grace of Christ and Fairness

**1. Not a command.** "I say this not as a command, but to prove by the earnestness of others that your love also is genuine" (8:8). Paul was an apostle and could have commanded, but he did not. Forced giving is not love.

**2. The grace of Christ.** "For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you by his poverty might become rich" (8:9). This is the center of the chapter and Paul's real reason for urging generosity.
- **"Though he was rich":** the glory Christ shared with the Father before he became man (John 17:5; Phil 2:6).
- **"He became poor":** his incarnation (he became man without ceasing to be God), his birth in a manger, a life with "nowhere to lay his head" (Matt 8:20), and finally his death on the cross in our place (5:21; Phil 2:7–8).
- **"That you... might become rich":** not riches of money, but forgiveness of sins, reconciliation with God, adoption as God's children and an eternal inheritance. Some use this verse to claim that believing in Jesus will make you wealthy, which completely misreads Paul. Paul himself was "poor, yet making many rich" (6:10).

Paul does not motivate giving by guilt or by what others will think, but by the grace of Christ: you have received such wealth from his poverty; now others can receive help from your abundance.

**3. According to what you have.** "So now finish doing it as well, so that your readiness in desiring it may be matched by your completing it out of what you have. For if the readiness is there, it is acceptable according to what a person has, not according to what he does not have" (8:11–12). The Corinthians had begun a year earlier, then stopped, probably because of the trouble with Paul. Paul urges them to finish. At the same time he is clear: God looks at a willing heart, not at the amount. A church must not ask people to borrow in order to give, must not make the poor feel inferior because they give little, must not pressure people with "the more you give, the more God will bless you," and should not publish donors' names and amounts in the bulletin for people to compare.

**4. Fairness.** "I do not mean that others should be eased and you burdened, but that as a matter of fairness your abundance at the present time should supply their need, so that their abundance may supply your need, that there may be fairness" (8:13–14). "Fairness" does not mean everyone must own the same, nor enforced redistribution. It means mutual supply: today you have more and supply another's need; tomorrow others may have more and supply yours. "Their abundance may supply your need" is read by some as future material help, by others (cf. Rom 15:27) as the spiritual riches Jerusalem has already given the Gentiles; either way the principle is mutual supply.

**5. Manna.** "As it is written, 'Whoever gathered much had nothing left over, and whoever gathered little had no lack'" (8:15). This is from Exodus 16:18. When Israel gathered manna in the wilderness, God made sure everyone had enough: those who gathered much had nothing left over, those who gathered little did not go short, and what was kept till morning bred worms (Exod 16:16–20). God is the God who provides, and his people are to share with one another like one family.

> The point: The real motive for giving is the grace of Christ: though he was rich, he became poor for us. God looks at a willing heart, not the amount; believers supply one another's needs, as everyone in the wilderness had manna.

## Going Deeper 8:16–24 Honorable in the Sight of Man Too

**1. Titus.** "But thanks be to God, who put into the heart of Titus the same earnest care I have for you" (8:16). Titus had just returned from Corinth and now volunteered to go back for this.

**2. A famous brother.** "With him we are sending the brother who is famous among all the churches for his preaching of the gospel. And not only that, but he has been appointed by the churches to travel with us" (8:18–19). Paul does not say who this brother was. Some early church fathers guessed Luke (Origen, Jerome); Chrysostom mentions Luke or Barnabas; but these are only guesses. What matters is that he was "appointed by the churches," not chosen by Paul himself.

**3. So that no one should blame us.** "We take this course so that no one should blame us about this generous gift that is being administered by us, for we aim at what is honorable not only in the Lord's sight but also in the sight of man" (8:20–21). Paul knew that money is where suspicion arises most easily, so he deliberately had several people handle it together, including representatives chosen by the churches. Paul's wording is almost exactly the Greek version of Proverbs 3:4: "take thought for what is honorable before the Lord and before men" (the Hebrew, followed by the ESV, reads "find favor... in the sight of God and man"). He uses the same phrase in Rom 12:17. Honesty before God is the root, but before people too things must be plain.

**4. A word for today.** A church handling money should also:
- have several people handle it together, not one person alone;
- keep accounts open and audit them regularly;
- let the congregation know where the money goes.

Chinese churches often hesitate to audit or ask questions out of relational obligation and face; but this is not distrust of the workers. It protects them, and it protects God's name from dishonor. The same applies to a small-group or fellowship fund, or money collected on someone's behalf: however small the sum, count it with someone else and keep a record, so that what people see in our handling of money is Christ's honesty, not our cleverness.

> The point: In handling money Paul did what was honorable not only before the Lord but before people. The church today, too, should protect its workers and honor the Lord's name by transparency, shared handling and open accounts.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor, so that you by his poverty might become rich" (8:9). Do I often think, "I'll be generous once I have more": once the mortgage is paid off, the children have finished school, the business is stable, the retirement fund is big enough? Is there, deep down, a fear of not having enough, as though if I loosen my grip my life will fall apart? The Macedonian believers gave gladly in their extreme poverty, not because they had no fear of being poor, but because "they gave themselves first to the Lord," trusting the God by whom, in the wilderness, "whoever gathered little had no lack." The Lord Jesus did not give me a little out of his surplus. He laid aside the glory of heaven, became poor and gave up his very life. God does not look at the amount but at "the readiness" (8:12). Today, can I bring my fear about money to God, confess that I often trust money rather than him, and give myself afresh to the Lord first? After that, I can quietly ask him what, out of what I have, he wants me to share with someone in need. If I am in fact not making ends meet, or am in debt, God accepts what is given "according to what a person has, not according to what he does not have" (8:12); I can tell him honestly how tight things are. However much or little I can give, I have already become rich through the Lord's poverty: forgiven, and made a child of God. No one can take that wealth out of my hands.
