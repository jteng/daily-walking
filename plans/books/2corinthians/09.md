## 本章脉络

第九章继续讲为耶路撒冷圣徒的捐献，有三段。**预备妥当**（1-5节）：保罗曾向马其顿人夸奖哥林多人「预备好了」，现在他先派弟兄们去，好叫他们所捐的「是出于乐意，不是出于勉强」。**撒种与收割**（6-11节）：「少种的少收，多种的多收」；「捐得乐意的人是神所喜爱的」；「神能将各样的恩惠多多地加给你们」。**感谢归于神**（12-15节）：这捐献不但补足圣徒的缺乏，更叫许多人感谢神、为他们祷告。全章以一句赞美结束：「感谢神，因他有说不尽的恩赐！」

第八章的中心是基督的恩典（8:9），第九章的结尾是神说不尽的恩赐（9:15）。保罗讲钱，从头到尾都在讲恩典。

## 深读 9:1-5 出于乐意，不是出于勉强

**1. 不必写信。** 「论到供给圣徒的事，我不必写信给你们；因为我知道你们乐意的心，常对马其顿人夸奖你们，说亚该亚人预备好了，已经有一年了。」（9:1-2）哥林多人一开始很热心，他们的热心「激动了许多人」，这里指的就是马其顿人。可是后来他们停了下来。保罗一面用哥林多人激励马其顿人（9:2），一面又用马其顿人激励哥林多人（8:1-5）。

**2. 免得夸奖落空。** 「万一有马其顿人与我同去，见你们没有预备，就叫我们所确信的，反成了羞愧；你们羞愧，更不用说了。」（9:4）保罗说得很坦白，还带点幽默。他提到羞愧，却点到为止，只是提醒他们兑现当初的承诺；他先派弟兄去，正是不想让捐献变成当众的压力。

**3. 不是勒索。** 「因此，我想不得不求那几位弟兄先到你们那里去，把从前所应许的捐资预备妥当，就显出你们所捐的是出于乐意，不是出于勉强。」（9:5）这里的「勉强」，原文是「贪心」这个词（和9:7的「勉强」不是同一个词），与7:2、12:17-18的「占便宜」同一个字根。有人理解为被人硬要、勒索出来的捐款，也有人理解为捐的人因为贪心舍不得而勉强给的。与它相对的「乐意」，原文是「祝福」。保罗先派人去，是为了让他们有时间安静地预备，免得他到了，大家才被迫当众凑钱。

> 要点：保罗要捐献出于乐意，不是出于勉强；这份礼物原文叫作祝福，是恩典的流露，不是被挤出来的钱。所以他提醒哥林多人兑现承诺，提到羞愧也只是点到为止，并且刻意避免用当众的场面去逼人。

## 深读 9:6-11 撒种与收割

**1. 少种少收，多种多收。** 「「少种的少收，多种的多收」，这话是真的。」（9:6）这是农夫都懂的道理（参箴11:24-25）。但保罗不是在说「奉献是一种投资，捐得越多，赚得越多」。他接下来说明，收成是什么，又是为了什么。

**2. 随本心所酌定的。** 「各人要随本心所酌定的，不要作难，不要勉强，因为捐得乐意的人是神所喜爱的。」（9:7）
- **「随本心所酌定的」：** 是在神面前想清楚、自己决定的，不是被情绪煽动，不是看别人捐多少，也不是像包红包、随礼那样，按人情和场面定一个体面的数目。
- **「不要作难」：** 不是心里难过、舍不得地捐。
- **「不要勉强」：** 不是因为压力、面子或罪疚感。
- **「捐得乐意的人」：** 这句话来自箴言22:8。七十士译本（旧约希腊文译本）在这一节多了一句（希伯来原文和和合本都没有），说神赐福给乐意施舍的人；保罗把这里换成了「喜爱」。神喜爱的不是数目，而是一颗因恩典而喜乐的心。这不是说神只爱乐意捐的人；在我们还舍不得、还是罪人的时候，他已经爱了我们。「我们爱，因为神先爱我们」（约一4:19）；乐意是他的爱在我们心里结的果子，不是换取他的爱的条件。

**3. 神能。** 「神能将各样的恩惠多多地加给你们，使你们凡事常常充足，能多行各样善事。」（9:8）这一节里有一连串的「各样」「凡事」「常常」。神不是吝啬的神。「充足」原文是哲学家常用的词，意思是自给自足、不需要依靠别人；保罗却说，真正的充足是从神来的，而且是为了「多行各样善事」，不是为了囤积。

**4. 他的仁义存到永远。** 「如经上所记：他施舍钱财，周济贫穷；他的仁义存到永远。」（9:9）这是诗篇112:9。那篇诗描写一个敬畏神的人：他慷慨施舍，他的义行会长存。多数解经家认为这里的「他」是慷慨的信徒；也有人认为保罗指的是神自己（9:8、10的主语都是神，参诗111:3）。这里的「仁义」是指义的行为，不是叫人称义的义；我们因信基督白白称义，慷慨是这恩典结出的果子。

**5. 那赐种给撒种的。** 「那赐种给撒种的，赐粮给人吃的，必多多加给你们种地的种子，又增添你们仁义的果子。」（9:10）这句话来自以赛亚书55:10（「使撒种的有种，使要吃的有粮」），「仁义的果子」也让人想起何西阿书10:12（七十士译本）。是神给种子，也是神叫它生长。我们所施舍的，原本就是从神来的。

**6. 富足是为了施舍。** 「叫你们凡事富足，可以多多施舍，就藉着我们使感谢归于神。」（9:11）神叫人富足的目的，是「可以多多施舍」，最后「使感谢归于神」。今天有些教导说，你捐钱给教会（或给某位传道人），神就会叫你发大财，这就是所谓的成功神学：把神当作投资的工具，和庙里烧香许愿、求财还愿的心态没有两样，完全违背了保罗的意思。神所应许的收成，首先是「仁义的果子」，是使人有能力继续行善，而不是让人满足贪心。

> 要点：神喜爱捐得乐意的人。他先爱了我们；我们所给的，原本就是神所赐的；神叫我们充足，是为了让我们多行善事，使感谢归于神，而不是为了让我们囤积或发财。

## 深读 9:12-15 感谢归于神

**1. 不只是补足缺乏。** 「因为办这供给的事，不但补圣徒的缺乏，而且叫许多人越发感谢神。」（9:12）捐献有两个果效：一个是实际的帮助，一个是属灵的果子，叫许多人感谢神。

**2. 顺服福音的凭据。** 「他们从这供给的事上得了凭据，知道你们承认基督顺服他的福音，多多地捐钱给他们和众人，便将荣耀归与神。」（9:13）耶路撒冷的犹太信徒中，有些人可能怀疑外邦信徒是否真的信了主；保罗也请罗马的信徒为他祷告，叫这捐项「可蒙圣徒悦纳」（罗15:31）。这份捐献会成为一个凭据：这些外邦人真是「承认基督」的人。慷慨是福音真实的见证。

**3. 彼此想念，彼此代祷。** 「他们也因神极大的恩赐显在你们心里，就切切地想念你们，为你们祈祷。」（9:14）犹太信徒和外邦信徒，过去彼此隔阂，保罗盼望他们因为这份捐献而彼此想念、彼此代祷。钱财在这里成了合一的桥梁。基督「拆毁了中间隔断的墙」（弗2:14），这份捐献，就是这道墙被拆毁的一个具体见证。

**4. 说不尽的恩赐。** 「感谢神，因他有说不尽的恩赐！」（9:15）保罗讲了两章的捐献，最后却不是讲人的慷慨，而是讲神的恩赐。这「说不尽的恩赐」，最自然的理解是神赐下他的儿子（参8:9；约3:16；罗8:32）。我们一切的施舍，都只是对这恩赐小小的回应。

> 要点：捐献不只补足人的缺乏，也叫人感谢神，使不同背景的信徒在基督里合一。一切的起点和终点，都是神那说不尽的恩赐：他的儿子。

## 默想

**默想：** 「各人要随本心所酌定的，不要作难，不要勉强，因为捐得乐意的人是神所喜爱的。」（9:7）当我奉献的时候，推动我的是什么？是怕别人看见我捐得少而没有面子？是怕神不祝福我，好像在跟神做交易？是一种「我欠了」的罪疚感？还是因为我又想起，神为我所做的，说也说不尽？也许我曾在教会里感到奉献的压力，或者曾经因为面子捐了超过自己能力的钱，后来心里很不平安；也许我一直捐得很少，因为心里舍不得。无论是哪一种，背后常常是把面子或钱财看得比神更重，这需要在神面前承认。神的心意不是要我被逼，而是要我被恩典打动。神把他的独生子赐给了我，「岂不也把万物和他一同白白地赐给我们吗？」（罗8:32）今天，我能不能先不想数目，在神面前问他，我当怎样回应，再「随本心所酌定的」去行：也许是通过可靠、账目清楚的渠道，为一个远方、贫穷或受逼迫的教会献上一份心意，也为他们祷告？如果我曾经为了面子捐过了头，神并没有因此记我的账，我也不必再用钱向人证明什么。我给得多，他的恩赐不会因此变大；我给得少，也不会因此变小。保罗讲了两章的钱，最后一句却不是讲我们给了什么，而是讲神给了什么：「感谢神，因他有说不尽的恩赐！」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 9 continues the subject of the collection for the saints in Jerusalem, in three parts. **Be ready** (vv. 1–5): Paul had boasted to the Macedonians that the Corinthians were ready; now he sends the brothers ahead "so that it may be ready as a willing gift, not as an exaction." **Sowing and reaping** (vv. 6–11): "whoever sows sparingly will also reap sparingly"; "God loves a cheerful giver"; "God is able to make all grace abound to you." **Thanksgiving to God** (vv. 12–15): the gift not only supplies the saints' needs but makes many give thanks to God and pray for the givers. The chapter ends with praise: "Thanks be to God for his inexpressible gift!"

The center of chapter 8 is the grace of Christ (8:9); the end of chapter 9 is God's inexpressible gift (9:15). When Paul talks about money, he is talking about grace from beginning to end.

## Going Deeper 9:1–5 A Willing Gift, Not an Exaction

**1. No need to write.** "Now it is superfluous for me to write to you about the ministry for the saints, for I know your readiness, of which I boast about you to the people of Macedonia, saying that Achaia has been ready since last year" (9:1–2). The Corinthians had begun with great zeal, and their zeal "stirred up most of them," that is, the Macedonians. But then they stopped. Paul spurs the Macedonians with the Corinthians' example (9:2) and the Corinthians with the Macedonians' (8:1–5).

**2. So that our boasting is not in vain.** "Otherwise, if some Macedonians come with me and find that you are not ready, we would be humiliated- to say nothing of you- for being so confident" (9:4). Paul is frank here, with a touch of humor. Paul does name the prospect of shame, but lightly, reminding them to keep the commitment they made; and he sends the brothers ahead precisely so that the gift will not be wrung out under public pressure.

**3. Not an exaction.** "So I thought it necessary to urge the brothers to go on ahead to you and arrange in advance for the gift you have promised, so that it may be ready as a willing gift, not as an exaction" (9:5). The word behind "exaction" is the noun for greed, related to the verb "take advantage" in 7:2 and 12:17–18. Some read it as a gift squeezed out of the givers (ESV, NRSV); others as a gift given grudgingly out of the givers' own covetousness (KJV, NIV). Its opposite, "willing gift," is literally "blessing." Paul sends people ahead so they can prepare quietly, rather than being forced to scrape money together in front of everyone when he arrives.

> The point: Paul wants giving to be willing, not forced; the gift is literally called a blessing, an overflow of grace, not money squeezed out. So he reminds the Corinthians to keep their promise, names shame only lightly, and deliberately avoids public pressure.

## Going Deeper 9:6–11 Sowing and Reaping

**1. Sow sparingly, reap sparingly.** "The point is this: whoever sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and whoever sows bountifully will also reap bountifully" (9:6). Every farmer knows this (cf. Prov 11:24–25). But Paul is not saying, "Giving is an investment: the more you give, the more you'll earn." He goes on to explain what the harvest is, and what it is for.

**2. As he has made up his mind.** "Each one must give as he has made up his mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver" (9:7).
- **"As he has made up his mind":** thought through before God and decided for oneself, not stirred up by emotion or by watching what others give, nor, as with a wedding red envelope, setting a "respectable" amount by social obligation.
- **"Not reluctantly":** not giving with a sorrowful, reluctant heart.
- **"Not under compulsion":** not from pressure, face or guilt.
- **"A cheerful giver":** from a line the Greek version adds after Proverbs 22:8 (absent from the Hebrew and the ESV): "God blesses a cheerful and giving man." Paul changes "blesses" to "loves." God loves not the amount but a heart made glad by grace. This does not mean God loves only cheerful givers; he loved us while we were still grudging and still sinners. "We love because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19); cheerfulness is the fruit of his love in us, not the price of it.

**3. God is able.** "And God is able to make all grace abound to you, so that having all sufficiency in all things at all times, you may abound in every good work" (9:8). This verse piles up "all," "all things," "at all times." God is not stingy. "Sufficiency" was a word philosophers used for self-sufficiency, not needing anyone else. Paul says true sufficiency comes from God, and it is so that we "may abound in every good work," not so that we can hoard.

**4. His righteousness endures forever.** "As it is written, 'He has distributed freely, he has given to the poor; his righteousness endures forever'" (9:9). This is Psalm 112:9, a psalm describing a man who fears God: he gives generously, and his righteous deeds endure. Most take "he" to be the generous believer; some think Paul applies it to God, the subject of 9:8 and 9:10 (cf. Ps 111:3). Either way, "righteousness" here is righteous conduct, not the righteousness by which we are justified; we are justified freely through faith in Christ, and generosity is the fruit of that grace.

**5. He who supplies seed to the sower.** "He who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed for sowing and increase the harvest of your righteousness" (9:10). This echoes Isaiah 55:10 ("giving seed to the sower and bread to the eater"), and "the harvest of your righteousness" recalls Hosea 10:12 (in the Greek version). God gives the seed and God makes it grow. What we give away came from God in the first place.

**6. Enriched in order to give.** "You will be enriched in every way for all your generosity, which through us will produce thanksgiving to God" (9:11). God enriches people "for all your generosity," and in the end "thanksgiving to God." Some teach today that if you give to the church (or to a certain preacher), God will make you rich. This is the so-called prosperity gospel: it treats God as an investment vehicle, no different from offering incense at a temple for wealth and repaying the vow, and it flatly contradicts Paul. The harvest God promises is first "the harvest of your righteousness," the means to keep doing good, not the satisfaction of greed.

> The point: God loves a cheerful giver. He loved us first; what we give came from God; he makes us sufficient so that we can abound in good works and bring thanks to God, not so that we can hoard or get rich.

## Going Deeper 9:12–15 Thanksgiving to God

**1. More than meeting needs.** "For the ministry of this service is not only supplying the needs of the saints, but is also overflowing in many thanksgivings to God" (9:12). The gift has two effects: practical help, and spiritual fruit, making many give thanks to God.

**2. Proof of submission to the gospel.** "By their approval of this service, they will glorify God because of your submission flowing from your confession of the gospel of Christ, and the generosity of your contribution for them and for all others" (9:13). Some Jewish believers in Jerusalem may have doubted whether Gentile believers had truly come to faith; Paul asked the Romans to pray that the gift "may be acceptable to the saints" (Rom 15:31). This gift would be evidence that these Gentiles really confessed the gospel of Christ. Generosity is a witness to the reality of the gospel.

**3. Longing for one another, praying for one another.** "...while they long for you and pray for you, because of the surpassing grace of God upon you" (9:14). Jewish and Gentile believers, once divided, Paul expects that because of this gift they will long for each other and pray for each other. Money here becomes a bridge of unity. Christ "has broken down... the dividing wall" (Eph 2:14), and this gift is one concrete expression of that in daily life.

**4. The inexpressible gift.** "Thanks be to God for his inexpressible gift!" (9:15). After two chapters on giving, Paul ends not with human generosity but with God's gift. The most natural reading of this "inexpressible gift" is God's giving of his Son (cf. 8:9; John 3:16; Rom 8:32). All our giving is only a small response to this gift.

> The point: Giving not only meets needs but leads people to thank God and unites believers of different backgrounds in Christ. The beginning and end of it all is God's inexpressible gift: his Son.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Each one must give as he has made up his mind, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver" (9:7). When I give, what moves me? Fear of losing face if others see I gave little? Fear that God won't bless me, as if I were bargaining with him? A sense of guilt, that "I owe"? Or because I have remembered once again that what God has done for me is beyond words? Perhaps I have felt pressure to give at church, or once gave more than I could afford for the sake of face, and had no peace afterwards. Perhaps I have always given very little, because I could not bear to let go. Behind either one, there is often a heart that values face or money above God, and that needs to be confessed. God's desire is not that I be forced but that I be moved by grace. God gave me his only Son; "how will he not also with him graciously give us all things?" (Rom 8:32). Today, can I set aside the question of how much and, before God, as I have made up my mind, ask him how he wants me to respond: perhaps, through a trustworthy and accountable channel, with a gift for a distant, poor or persecuted church, and with prayer for them? If I once gave beyond my means for the sake of face, God is not holding it against me, and I no longer need money to prove anything to anyone. If I give much, his gift does not grow larger; if I give little, it does not grow smaller. Paul spent two chapters on money, but his last sentence is not about what we give. It is about what God has given: "Thanks be to God for his inexpressible gift!"
