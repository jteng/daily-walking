## 本章脉络

从第十章起，信的语气变得严厉，保罗开始直接回应那些在哥林多攻击他的人。这一章有三段。**属灵的争战**（1-6节）：保罗「藉着基督的温柔、和平」劝他们；「我们争战的兵器本不是属血气的」，乃是要「将人所有的心意夺回，使他都顺服基督」。**为造就人的权柄**（7-11节）：有人说保罗「信又沉重又厉害，及至见面，却是气貌不扬，言语粗俗」；保罗说，主赐的权柄「是要造就你们，并不是要败坏你们」。**神所量给的界限**（12-18节）：那些人「用自己度量自己，用自己比较自己」；保罗只在神所量给他的范围里夸口，因为「夸口的，当指着主夸口」，「蒙悦纳的，不是自己称许的，乃是主所称许的」。

这一章的问题是：用什么标准来衡量一个属灵领袖，也衡量我们自己？

## 深读 10:1-6 不凭着血气争战

**1. 基督的温柔、和平。** 「我－保罗，就是与你们见面的时候是谦卑的，不在你们那里的时候向你们是勇敢的，如今亲自藉着基督的温柔、和平劝你们。」（10:1）对手讥笑保罗：当面胆小，写信才勇敢。保罗却不先为自己辩护，而是先提到「基督的温柔、和平」。主耶稣说：「我心里柔和谦卑」（太11:29）。保罗要用基督的方式来处理冲突。

**2. 在血气中行事，却不凭着血气争战。** 「因为我们虽然在血气中行事，却不凭着血气争战。」（10:3）「血气」原文就是「肉体」。保罗承认他是一个有限的人，活在软弱的身体里；但他争战不用世人的方法：不用操纵、威吓、拉帮结派、抹黑对手。

**3. 攻破坚固的营垒。** 「我们争战的兵器本不是属血气的，乃是在神面前有能力，可以攻破坚固的营垒，将各样的计谋，各样拦阻人认识神的那些自高之事，一概攻破了。」（10:4-5）「坚固的营垒」是古代城堡的形象（参箴21:22）。保罗说，这些营垒是「计谋」和「自高之事」，就是人用来抵挡认识神的种种想法、论证和骄傲。有些人把这段经文理解为要捆绑某个城市或地区的属灵势力；但保罗在这里讲的营垒，主要是人心中的思想和骄傲，他的兵器是真理、福音和祷告（参6:7）。

**4. 夺回心意，顺服基督。** 「又将人所有的心意夺回，使他都顺服基督。」（10:5）「夺回」原文是「掳来」，像军队把俘虏带回来。保罗在这里首先讲的是对手的论调，但这原则也适用于我们自己的思想：我们的想法、价值观、对神的看法，都要被带到基督面前，降服在他之下。这不是叫一位领袖去控制别人的思想；是叫每一个人的心意都「顺服基督」，不是顺服某个人。

**5. 预备责罚。** 「并且我已经预备好了，等你们十分顺服的时候，要责罚那一切不顺服的人。」（10:6）保罗希望教会先回转，然后他才处理那些仍然顽梗的人。他不愿意一来就动用权柄。

> 要点：属灵的争战不能用世人的方法，而要用基督的温柔、真理和祷告。要攻破的营垒，是人心中抵挡认识神的骄傲；要被夺回的，是每一个心意，叫它顺服基督。

## 深读 10:7-11 为造就人的权柄

**1. 看眼前的。** 「你们是看眼前的吗？倘若有人自信是属基督的，他要再想想，他如何属基督，我们也是如何属基督的。」（10:7）哥林多人只看外表。那些人自称特别「属基督」，大概是因为他们有特别的经历或背景；保罗说：我也是属基督的。

**2. 造就，不是败坏。** 「主赐给我们权柄，是要造就你们，并不是要败坏你们。」（10:8）这句话在13:10再次出现，可见这是保罗对属灵权柄最基本的理解。属灵权柄是用来建造人的，不是用来压制人、利用人、满足自己的。凡是用属灵权柄来叫人害怕、叫人依附自己、掩盖自己过错的，都违背了这节经文。

**3. 信又沉重又厉害。** 「因为有人说：『他的信又沉重又厉害，及至见面，却是气貌不扬，言语粗俗的。』」（10:10）当时的希腊社会很看重演说家的外表、声音和技巧。对手批评保罗不够体面，口才不好。保罗没有为自己的外表辩护，只是说：我信上怎么说，见面就会怎么做（10:11）。言行一致比口才更重要。

> 要点：主所赐的属灵权柄，是为造就人，不是为败坏人。衡量一位属灵领袖，不在于外表和口才，而在于言行一致，用权柄来建造人。

## 深读 10:12-18 主所称许的

**1. 用自己比较自己。** 「因为我们不敢将自己和那自荐的人同列相比。他们用自己度量自己，用自己比较自己，乃是不通达的。」（10:12）这些人互相比较，你夸我、我夸你，用他们自己定的标准来衡量谁更属灵。保罗说这是「不通达的」，就是愚昧的。用人的标准互相比较，永远找不到真正的答案，只会叫人骄傲或者自卑。

**2. 神所量给的界限。** 「我们不愿意分外夸口，只要照神所量给我们的界限搆到你们那里。」（10:13）「界限」原文是一把量尺，也可以指一块被划定的范围。神给保罗的范围，是向外邦人传福音（加2:7-9），而他是第一个把福音传到哥林多的人（10:14）。那些人却是到别人已经建立的教会里，坐享其成，还要夸口。保罗的原则是：「不在基督的名被称过的地方传福音，免得建造在别人的根基上」（罗15:20）。

**3. 更远的地方。** 「但指望你们信心增长的时候，所量给我们的界限，就可以因着你们更加开展，得以将福音传到你们以外的地方。」（10:15-16）保罗的眼光不是停在哥林多。他盼望哥林多教会稳固了，成为他往更远地方宣教的基地（参罗15:24、28，他计划往西班牙去）。

**4. 指着主夸口。** 「但夸口的，当指着主夸口。因为蒙悦纳的，不是自己称许的，乃是主所称许的。」（10:17-18）这又是引用耶利米书9:24（保罗在林前1:31也引用过）。耶利米说，智慧人、勇士、财主都不要夸口，夸口的要因认识耶和华而夸口。最后的问题不是我怎样看自己，也不是别人怎样看我，而是主怎样看我。

> 要点：用人的标准互相比较，只会叫人骄傲或自卑。保罗只在神所量给他的范围里服事，并且只指着主夸口；因为蒙悦纳的，不是自己称许的，乃是主所称许的。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他们用自己度量自己，用自己比较自己，乃是不通达的……因为蒙悦纳的，不是自己称许的，乃是主所称许的。」（10:12、18）我是否常常在比较中活着：比较谁的教会更大、谁的讲道更受欢迎、谁的服事更有果效、谁的孩子更出色、谁在朋友圈里过得更好？比较赢了，我就暗暗骄傲；比较输了，我就灰心，甚至嫉妒。我是否也像哥林多人一样，用口才、外表和名气来衡量一个人？比较的根，常常是想找到一个地方，证明自己「够好」。但我不必再这样证明自己。父神从天上说：「这是我的爱子，我所喜悦的」（太3:17）；而我在基督里，也已经「在爱子里」蒙了恩典（弗1:6）。主的称许不是我赚来的，而是因为基督。今天，我能不能把一个我常常拿来跟自己比较的人，带到神面前，为他感谢神、为他祷告？然后，我能不能安静地问：主啊，你量给我的界限是什么？在那里，我只要忠心。

## The Shape of the Chapter

From chapter 10 on the tone of the letter turns severe, and Paul begins to answer directly those attacking him in Corinth. This chapter has three parts. **Spiritual warfare** (vv. 1–6): Paul appeals to them "by the meekness and gentleness of Christ"; "the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh," and they "take every thought captive to obey Christ." **Authority for building up** (vv. 7–11): some say, "His letters are weighty and strong, but his bodily presence is weak, and his speech of no account"; Paul says the Lord gave his authority "for building you up and not for destroying you." **The area God assigned** (vv. 12–18): those men "measure themselves by one another and compare themselves with one another"; Paul boasts only within the area God assigned him, because "Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord," and "it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends."

The question of this chapter is: by what standard do we measure a spiritual leader, and ourselves?

## Going Deeper 10:1–6 Not Waging War According to the Flesh

**1. The meekness and gentleness of Christ.** "I, Paul, myself entreat you, by the meekness and gentleness of Christ- I who am humble when face to face with you, but bold toward you when I am away!-" (10:1). His opponents mocked Paul: timid in person, brave only in letters. Paul does not defend himself first. He first names "the meekness and gentleness of Christ." Jesus said, "I am gentle and lowly in heart" (Matt 11:29). Paul means to handle conflict in Christ's way.

**2. Walking in the flesh, not warring according to the flesh.** "For though we walk in the flesh, we are not waging war according to the flesh" (10:3). Paul admits he is a limited man, living in a weak body; but he does not fight with the world's methods: no manipulation, intimidation, faction-building or smearing opponents.

**3. Destroying strongholds.** "For the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but have divine power to destroy strongholds. We destroy arguments and every lofty opinion raised against the knowledge of God" (10:4–5). "Strongholds" is the image of an ancient fortress (cf. Prov 21:22). Paul says these strongholds are "arguments" and "lofty opinions," the ideas, reasoning and pride people raise against knowing God. Some read this passage as a call to bind the "spiritual powers" over a city or region. But the strongholds Paul speaks of here are chiefly thoughts and pride in human hearts, and his weapons are truth, the gospel and prayer (cf. 6:7).

**4. Taking thoughts captive to Christ.** "...and take every thought captive to obey Christ" (10:5). "Take captive" is the picture of an army leading prisoners home. Paul first has in mind his opponents' arguments, but the principle applies to our own thinking too: our ideas, values and views of God are all to be brought before Christ and made to submit to him. This is not a leader controlling other people's thoughts. It is every person's mind brought to "obey Christ," not to obey a man.

**5. Ready to punish.** "...being ready to punish every disobedience, when your obedience is complete" (10:6). Paul hopes the church will turn back first, and only then will he deal with those who remain stubborn. He does not want to arrive wielding his authority.

> The point: Spiritual warfare cannot use the world's methods; it uses the gentleness of Christ, the truth and prayer. The strongholds to be destroyed are the pride in human hearts that resists knowing God; what is to be taken captive is every thought, made to obey Christ.

## Going Deeper 10:7–11 Authority for Building Up

**1. Look at what is before you.** "Look at what is before your eyes. If anyone is confident that he is Christ's, let him remind himself that just as he is Christ's, so also are we" (10:7). The Corinthians looked only at the surface. Those men claimed to belong to Christ in some special way, probably because of special experiences or background. Paul says: I belong to Christ too.

**2. Building up, not destroying.** "For even if I boast a little too much of our authority, which the Lord gave for building you up and not for destroying you, I will not be ashamed" (10:8). This phrase appears again in 13:10, showing it is Paul's most basic understanding of spiritual authority. Spiritual authority is for building people up, not for suppressing them, using them or serving oneself. Any use of spiritual authority to make people afraid, to make them dependent on the leader, or to cover the leader's own wrongs violates this verse.

**3. Weighty letters.** "For they say, 'His letters are weighty and strong, but his bodily presence is weak, and his speech of no account'" (10:10). Greek society prized an orator's appearance, voice and technique. Paul's opponents criticized him as unimpressive and a poor speaker. Paul does not defend his appearance; he only says that what he says by letter, he will do in person (10:11). Integrity between word and deed matters more than eloquence.

> The point: The spiritual authority the Lord gives is for building up, not tearing down. A spiritual leader is measured not by appearance or eloquence but by whether his deeds match his words and whether he uses authority to build people up.

## Going Deeper 10:12–18 The One the Lord Commends

**1. Comparing themselves with one another.** "Not that we dare to classify or compare ourselves with some of those who are commending themselves. But when they measure themselves by one another and compare themselves with one another, they are without understanding" (10:12). These men compared themselves with each other, praising one another and measuring who was more spiritual by standards they had set themselves. Paul calls this "without understanding," that is, foolish. Comparing ourselves by human standards never yields a true answer; it only produces pride or a sense of inferiority.

**2. The area God assigned.** "But we will not boast beyond limits, but will boast only with regard to the area of influence God assigned to us, to reach even to you" (10:13). The word translated "area" is literally a measuring rule, and can mean a marked-out territory. The area God gave Paul was preaching the gospel to the Gentiles (Gal 2:7–9), and he was the first to bring the gospel to Corinth (10:14). Those men came into a church others had founded, took the credit and boasted of it. Paul's principle was "to preach the gospel, not where Christ has already been named, lest I build on someone else's foundation" (Rom 15:20).

**3. Lands beyond.** "But our hope is that as your faith increases, our area of influence among you may be greatly enlarged, so that we may preach the gospel in lands beyond you" (10:15–16). Paul's vision did not stop at Corinth. He hoped that once the Corinthian church was steady, it would become a base for mission farther on (cf. Rom 15:24, his plan to go to Spain).

**4. Boasting in the Lord.** "'Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord.' For it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends" (10:17–18). This again draws on Jeremiah 9:24 (which Paul also quotes in 1 Cor 1:31). Jeremiah says the wise, the mighty and the rich must not boast; whoever boasts should boast in knowing the LORD. In the end the question is not how I see myself, nor how others see me, but how the Lord sees me.

> The point: Comparing ourselves by human standards only breeds pride or inferiority. Paul served within the area God assigned him and boasted only in the Lord, because it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "When they measure themselves by one another and compare themselves with one another, they are without understanding... For it is not the one who commends himself who is approved, but the one whom the Lord commends" (10:12, 18). Do I live by comparison: whose church is bigger, whose preaching is more popular, whose ministry is more fruitful, whose children are more successful, whose life looks better online? When I win, I am secretly proud; when I lose, I am discouraged, even envious. Do I, like the Corinthians, measure people by eloquence, appearance and fame? The root of comparison is often a search for somewhere to prove that I am "good enough." But I no longer have to prove myself. The Father said from heaven, "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased" (Matt 3:17), and in Christ I too have been blessed "in the Beloved" (Eph 1:6). The Lord's commendation is not something I earn; it is mine because of Christ. Today, can I bring before God one person I often compare myself with, and thank God for him and pray for him? Then can I quietly ask: Lord, what is the area you have assigned me? There, I need only be faithful.
