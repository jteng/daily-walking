## 本章脉络

第十一章是保罗愚妄人的自夸的开始，有四段。**神那样的愤恨**（1-6节）：保罗把哥林多人「许配一个丈夫」，要把他们「如同贞洁的童女，献给基督」；他怕他们「失去那向基督所存纯一清洁的心」，接受另一个耶稣。**白白传福音与假使徒**（7-15节）：保罗没有向他们收取供给；那些人却是「假使徒，行事诡诈，装作基督使徒的模样」，「连撒但也装作光明的天使」。**愚妄人的话**（16-21节）：既然那些人凭着血气自夸，保罗也「像愚妄人」自夸；哥林多人能忍耐那些「强你们作奴仆」「打你们的脸」的人。**软弱的履历**（22-33节）：保罗列出他所受的鞭打、监禁、船坏和危险，以及「为众教会挂心的事」；最后讲到他在大马士革「在筐子里，从城墙上被人缒下去」。

保罗不愿意自夸，可是为了把哥林多人从假教师手中挽回，他不得不说。他夸的却不是成功，而是软弱：「我若必须自夸，就夸那关乎我软弱的事便了。」（11:30）

## 深读 11:1-6 献给基督的童女

**1. 神那样的愤恨。** 「我为你们起的愤恨，原是神那样的愤恨。」（11:2）「愤恨」原文也可以译作「妒忌」或「热心」。旧约常说神是「忌邪的神」：不是小气的嫉妒，而是一位丈夫不能容忍妻子另有所爱的那种爱。保罗为哥林多人所起的，就是这样的热心。

**2. 许配给基督。** 「因为我曾把你们许配一个丈夫，要把你们如同贞洁的童女，献给基督。」（11:2）在当时的犹太婚俗中，订婚以后到正式成婚之间有一段时间，女方的父亲要负责保守她的贞洁，到婚礼那天把她交给新郎。保罗像这位父亲：他传福音，把哥林多教会许配给基督；他要在主再来、羔羊婚娶的那一天（启19:7），把一个忠贞的教会献给基督。旧约早就把神和他百姓的关系比作婚姻：「新郎怎样喜悦新妇，你的神也要照样喜悦你。」（赛62:5）

**3. 蛇的诡诈。** 「我只怕你们的心或偏于邪，失去那向基督所存纯一清洁的心，就像蛇用诡诈诱惑了夏娃一样。」（11:3）蛇引诱夏娃，不是叫她公开拒绝神，而是叫她怀疑神的话：「神岂是真说……」（创3:1）。今天的迷惑也常常是这样：不是直接叫人离开基督，而是在基督旁边加上别的，或者悄悄改变基督的样子。「纯一」就是专一、不分心；「清洁」就是不掺杂。

**4. 另一个耶稣，另一个灵，另一个福音。** 「假如有人来另传一个耶稣，不是我们所传过的；或者你们另受一个灵，不是你们所受过的；或者另得一个福音，不是你们所得过的；你们容让他也就罢了。」（11:4）名字可以一样，内容却不同。在哥林多，那些人传的「耶稣」大概是一位只带来能力、荣耀和成功的耶稣，而不是那位「因软弱被钉在十字架上」的耶稣（13:4）。今天也有许多另一个耶稣：
- 只要你信就会发财、健康、凡事顺利的耶稣；
- 只是一位道德老师、不需要为人赎罪的耶稣；
- 一些异端所说已经再来、重新成为某个人的「基督」，例如在华人教会中造成许多伤害的「东方闪电」（自称「全能神教会」），宣称基督已经化身为一位中国女子。

保罗在加拉太书说：「那并不是福音」（加1:7）。我们要「试验那些灵是出于神的不是」（约一4:1），用圣经所启示的基督来分辨一切。

**5. 言语粗俗，知识却不粗俗。** 「我想，我一点不在那些最大的使徒以下。我的言语虽然粗俗，我的知识却不粗俗。」（11:5-6）保罗承认自己不是受过专业训练的演说家，但他对福音的认识是真实的。

> 要点：保罗像一位父亲，要把教会如同贞洁的童女献给基督。最大的危险不是公开离开基督，而是接受另一个耶稣，失去那向基督所存纯一清洁的心。

## 深读 11:7-15 白白传福音，假使徒

**1. 不收你们的钱，是罪吗？** 「我因为白白传神的福音给你们，就自居卑微，叫你们高升，这算是我犯罪吗？」（11:7）在当时的社会，一位哲学家或演说家若不收钱，常被人看不起，好像他的教导不值钱；有钱的人也喜欢用供养来使教师依附自己。保罗却坚持在哥林多不收供给（林前9:12、15-18），宁可亲手做工（徒18:3）。

**2. 「亏负」别的教会。** 「我亏负了别的教会，向他们取了工价来给你们效力。我在你们那里缺乏的时候，并没有累着你们一个人；因我所缺乏的，那从马其顿来的弟兄们都补足了。」（11:8-9）「亏负」原文是「掳掠」，保罗用了一个夸张的词：他接受马其顿教会（特别是腓立比，腓4:15-16）的供给，好在哥林多白白传福音。

**3. 是因我不爱你们吗？** 「为什么呢？是因我不爱你们吗？这有神知道。」（11:11）有人说，保罗不收哥林多人的钱，是因为他不把他们当自己人。保罗说：不是不爱，神知道我爱你们。

**4. 假使徒。** 「那等人是假使徒，行事诡诈，装作基督使徒的模样。这也不足为怪，因为连撒但也装作光明的天使。所以他的差役，若装作仁义的差役，也不算希奇。他们的结局必然照着他们的行为。」（11:13-15）这是全书最严厉的话。假教师看起来不一定可怕；他们可能很有魅力、很属灵、很会讲道。撒但最有效的作为，不是装成黑暗，而是装成光明。主耶稣也警告过：「假基督、假先知将要起来，显大神迹、大奇事，倘若能行，连选民也就迷惑了。」（太24:24）

> 要点：保罗宁可不收钱，也要叫福音不被阻隔。假教师常常装作光明的天使，所以我们分辨一个人，不能只看他的魅力和恩赐，要看他所传的是不是圣经中的基督。

## 深读 11:16-21 你们能忍耐他

**1. 像愚妄人说话。** 「我说的话不是奉主命说的，乃是像愚妄人放胆自夸；既有好些人凭着血气自夸，我也要自夸了。」（11:17-18）保罗清楚地说，接下来的自夸不是他平常说话的方式。他是被逼的，是要用对手的方式，揭穿对手的空洞。

**2. 你们都能忍耐他。** 「假若有人强你们作奴仆，或侵吞你们，或掳掠你们，或侮慢你们，或打你们的脸，你们都能忍耐他。」（11:20）这是一句带着讽刺的话，也是一幅可怕的图画：哥林多人对温柔、不收钱的保罗挑剔，对那些控制他们、榨取他们、看不起他们，甚至动手打人的人，却百般忍耐。

**3. 今天的提醒。** 这种事今天也会发生：一位领袖越强势、越会控制人，有些信徒反而越觉得他「有能力」「有恩膏」。保罗说，这不是属灵，而是被奴役。如果一位教会领袖：
- 要求人绝对服从，不许质疑；
- 在钱财上不清不楚，或者榨取信徒的钱财；
- 羞辱人、恐吓人，甚至对人动手或有性方面的侵犯；

那么，这不是信徒应当「忍耐」的事。受害的人应当寻求帮助；涉及暴力、性侵或诈骗的，报警、交由司法处理是对的（罗13:4）；教会不可以「家丑不可外扬」为理由遮盖，也不可让这样的人继续担任职分或接触受害者。

**4. 我们太软弱了。** 「我说这话是羞辱自己，好像我们从前是软弱的。」（11:21）保罗讽刺地说：说来惭愧，我们太「软弱」了，做不出那种事。在他看来，不辖制人，正是基督的样式。

> 要点：教会很容易对温柔的仆人挑剔，对强势、控制人的领袖却百般忍耐。基督的仆人不辖制人、不榨取人；凡是奴役、羞辱、侵犯人的，都不是信徒应当忍受的。

## 深读 11:22-33 软弱的履历

**1. 他们是希伯来人吗？我也是。** 「他们是希伯来人吗？我也是。他们是以色列人吗？我也是。他们是亚伯拉罕的后裔吗？我也是。」（11:22）论出身，保罗一点也不输他们（参腓3:5）。

**2. 一份苦难的清单。** 「他们是基督的仆人吗？（我说句狂话，）我更是。我比他们多受劳苦，多下监牢，受鞭打是过重的，冒死是屡次有的。」（11:23）接下来是一份令人震惊的清单：
- **「被犹太人鞭打五次，每次四十减去一下」（11:24）：** 律法规定「只可打他四十下，不可过数」（申25:3），后来的犹太传统为了不超过，只打三十九下。保罗一直回到会堂传福音，所以一次又一次受这样的刑罚。
- **「被棍打了三次」（11:25）：** 这是罗马官员的刑罚，例如在腓立比（徒16:22-23）。
- **「被石头打了一次」：** 在路司得（徒14:19）。
- **「遇着船坏三次」：** 这些都发生在使徒行传27章那次船难以前，使徒行传没有记载。
- **种种危险（11:26）：** 江河、盗贼、同族、外邦人、城里、旷野、海中，还有「假弟兄的危险」。
- **身体的缺乏（11:27）：** 不得睡、饥渴、寒冷、赤身露体。

使徒行传只记载了其中一小部分。我们看见的保罗，只是冰山一角。

**3. 为众教会挂心。** 「除了这外面的事，还有为众教会挂心的事，天天压在我身上。有谁软弱，我不软弱呢？有谁跌倒，我不焦急呢？」（11:28-29）比起身体的苦，保罗心里最重的担子，是教会。他与软弱的人一同软弱，为跌倒的人焦急。这是牧者的心，也是基督的心。

**4. 在筐子里被缒下去。** 「我若必须自夸，就夸那关乎我软弱的事便了……在大马士革的亚哩达王手下的提督把守大马士革城，要捉拿我，我就从窗户中，在筐子里，从城墙上被人缒下去，脱离了他的手。」（11:30、32-33；参徒9:23-25）在罗马军中，第一个攻上敌城城墙的士兵会得到特别的荣誉。许多解经家认为，保罗在这里是有意讽刺：他的「战功」不是第一个爬上城墙，而是在筐子里从城墙上被人缒下来逃命。这就是他要夸的软弱。

> 要点：保罗的履历不是成功的记录，而是为基督受苦、为教会挂心的记录。他夸的是软弱，因为在软弱中显明的是神的能力，不是他自己。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因为我曾把你们许配一个丈夫，要把你们如同贞洁的童女，献给基督。我只怕你们的心或偏于邪，失去那向基督所存纯一清洁的心。」（11:2-3）我对主的爱，是否也悄悄地分了心？我是否不知不觉地信了一个另一个耶稣：一位主要用来让我生活顺利、孩子成功、生意兴旺的耶稣，好像他是一位很灵的「保佑者」，而不是我的新郎和主？当他没有照我所想的给我，我是否就冷淡了？主耶稣的爱不是这样的。他「爱教会，为教会舍己」，要「把教会洗净，成为圣洁」（弗5:25-26）；他为我这个常常变心的人流了血，而且一直没有放弃我。「新郎怎样喜悦新妇，你的神也要照样喜悦你。」（赛62:5）他对我的爱是忌邪的爱，因为他要我整个人。所以，我可以向他承认那些分走我心的东西，不是因为害怕被他丢弃，而是因为我被这样的爱吸引，愿意回到起初的爱。主啊，求你保守我的心，叫我单单爱你，直到你再来迎娶你的新妇的那一天。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 11 begins Paul's "fool's boasting," in four parts. **A divine jealousy** (vv. 1–6): Paul "betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ"; he fears they will be "led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to Christ" and accept "another Jesus." **The gospel free of charge, and false apostles** (vv. 7–15): Paul took no support from them, but those men are "false apostles, deceitful workmen, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ," for "even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light." **Speaking as a fool** (vv. 16–21): since they boast according to the flesh, Paul too boasts "as a fool"; the Corinthians bear with those who "make slaves of you" and "strike you in the face." **A résumé of weakness** (vv. 22–33): Paul lists his beatings, imprisonments, shipwrecks and dangers, and "the daily pressure on me of my anxiety for all the churches"; finally he tells how at Damascus he "was let down in a basket through a window in the wall."

Paul did not want to boast, but to win the Corinthians back from false teachers he had to speak. Yet what he boasts of is not success but weakness: "If I must boast, I will boast of the things that show my weakness" (11:30).

## Going Deeper 11:1–6 A Pure Virgin Presented to Christ

**1. A divine jealousy.** "I feel a divine jealousy for you" (11:2). The word can also mean "zeal." The Old Testament often calls God a "jealous God": not petty envy, but the love of a husband who will not tolerate his wife loving another. This is the zeal Paul feels for the Corinthians.

**2. Betrothed to Christ.** "...for I betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ" (11:2). In Jewish custom there was a period between betrothal and the wedding, during which the bride's father was responsible for guarding her purity until he gave her to the bridegroom on the wedding day. Paul is like that father: by preaching the gospel he betrothed the Corinthian church to Christ, and he wants to present a faithful church to Christ on the day the Lord returns, at "the marriage of the Lamb" (Rev 19:7). The Old Testament had long pictured God and his people as a marriage: "as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall your God rejoice over you" (Isa 62:5).

**3. The serpent's cunning.** "But I am afraid that as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, your thoughts will be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to Christ" (11:3). The serpent did not tempt Eve to reject God openly, but to doubt his word: "Did God actually say...?" (Gen 3:1). Deception today often works the same way: not by telling people to leave Christ outright, but by adding something beside him, or quietly changing his face. "Sincere" means single-minded, undivided; "pure" means unmixed.

**4. Another Jesus, a different spirit, a different gospel.** "For if someone comes and proclaims another Jesus than the one we proclaimed, or if you receive a different spirit from the one you received, or if you accept a different gospel from the one you accepted, you put up with it readily enough" (11:4). The name can be the same while the content differs. In Corinth, the "Jesus" those men preached was probably one who brought only power, glory and success, not the Jesus who "was crucified in weakness" (13:4). Today too there are many "other Jesuses":
- a Jesus who guarantees wealth, health and success if you just believe;
- a Jesus who is only a moral teacher and need not atone for anyone;
- the "Christ" of certain sects who claim he has already returned as a particular person, such as Eastern Lightning (calling itself the Church of Almighty God), which has done great harm among Chinese churches by claiming Christ has returned as a Chinese woman.

In Galatians Paul says such a message is "not that there is another one" (Gal 1:7). We must "test the spirits to see whether they are from God" (1 John 4:1), measuring everything by the Christ revealed in Scripture.

**5. Unskilled in speaking, not in knowledge.** "I consider that I am not in the least inferior to these super-apostles. Even if I am unskilled in speaking, I am not so in knowledge" (11:5–6). Paul admits he is not a professionally trained orator, but his knowledge of the gospel is real.

> The point: Like a father, Paul wants to present the church to Christ as a pure virgin. The greatest danger is not openly leaving Christ but accepting "another Jesus," and losing a sincere and pure devotion to Christ.

## Going Deeper 11:7–15 The Gospel Free of Charge, and False Apostles

**1. A sin not to charge you?** "Or did I commit a sin in humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I preached God's gospel to you free of charge?" (11:7). In that society a philosopher or orator who took no fee was often looked down on, as if his teaching were worthless, and wealthy people liked to use patronage to make teachers dependent on them. Paul insisted on taking no support in Corinth (1 Cor 9:12, 15–18) and worked with his own hands (Acts 18:3).

**2. "Robbing" other churches.** "I robbed other churches by accepting support from them in order to serve you. And when I was with you and was in need, I did not burden anyone, for the brothers who came from Macedonia supplied my need" (11:8–9). "Robbed" is deliberate exaggeration: Paul accepted support from the Macedonian churches (especially Philippi, Phil 4:15–16) so that he could preach freely in Corinth.

**3. Because I do not love you?** "And why? Because I do not love you? God knows I do!" (11:11). Some said Paul refused their money because he did not count them as his own. Paul says: it is not that I don't love you; God knows I do.

**4. False apostles.** "For such men are false apostles, deceitful workmen, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ. And no wonder, for even Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. So it is no surprise if his servants, also, disguise themselves as servants of righteousness. Their end will correspond to their deeds" (11:13–15). These are the severest words in the letter. False teachers need not look frightening; they may be attractive, spiritual and fine preachers. Satan's most effective work is not disguising himself as darkness but as light. Jesus warned, "false christs and false prophets will arise and perform great signs and wonders, so as to lead astray, if possible, even the elect" (Matt 24:24).

> The point: Paul would rather take no money than let anything hinder the gospel. False teachers often come disguised as angels of light, so we must judge a teacher not by charisma or gifts but by whether he preaches the Christ of Scripture.

## Going Deeper 11:16–21 You Bear It

**1. Speaking as a fool.** "What I am saying with this boastful confidence, I say not with the Lord's authority but as a fool. Since many boast according to the flesh, I too will boast" (11:17–18). Paul says plainly that the boasting that follows is not his normal way of speaking. He is forced into it, using his opponents' method to expose their emptiness.

**2. You bear with it.** "For you bear it if someone makes slaves of you, or devours you, or takes advantage of you, or puts on airs, or strikes you in the face" (11:20). This is irony, and also a terrible picture: the Corinthians found fault with gentle Paul, who took nothing from them, yet put up with men who controlled them, exploited them, looked down on them and even struck them.

**3. A word for today.** This still happens. The more forceful and controlling a leader is, the more some believers think he has "power" or "anointing." Paul says this is not spirituality but slavery. If a church leader:
- demands absolute obedience and forbids questions;
- is unclear in money matters, or squeezes money out of believers;
- humiliates or threatens people, or strikes them, or abuses them sexually;

then this is not something believers should "bear." Those harmed should seek help; where there is violence, sexual abuse or fraud, reporting it to the police and the courts is right (Rom 13:4); the church must not cover it up for the sake of face (家丑不可外扬), nor let such a man keep his office or have access to those he harmed.

**4. We were too weak for that.** "To my shame, I must say, we were too weak for that!" (11:21). Paul says with irony: I'm ashamed to admit we were too "weak" to do such things. In his eyes, not lording it over people is the very way of Christ.

> The point: Churches easily find fault with gentle servants and put up with forceful, controlling leaders. Christ's servants do not dominate or exploit; enslaving, humiliating or abusing people is never something believers should endure.

## Going Deeper 11:22–33 A Résumé of Weakness

**1. Are they Hebrews? So am I.** "Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they offspring of Abraham? So am I" (11:22). In background Paul is in no way behind them (cf. Phil 3:5).

**2. A list of sufferings.** "Are they servants of Christ? I am a better one- I am talking like a madman- with far greater labors, far more imprisonments, with countless beatings, and often near death" (11:23). Then comes an astonishing list:
- **"Five times I received at the hands of the Jews the forty lashes less one" (11:24):** the law said, "Forty stripes may be given him, but not more" (Deut 25:3), and later Jewish practice gave thirty-nine to avoid exceeding it. Paul kept going back to the synagogues with the gospel, and so suffered this punishment again and again.
- **"Three times I was beaten with rods" (11:25):** a Roman punishment, as at Philippi (Acts 16:22–23).
- **"Once I was stoned":** at Lystra (Acts 14:19).
- **"Three times I was shipwrecked":** all before the shipwreck of Acts 27, and not recorded in Acts.
- **Dangers of every kind (11:26):** rivers, robbers, his own people, Gentiles, the city, the wilderness, the sea, and "danger from false brothers."
- **Physical want (11:27):** sleepless nights, hunger and thirst, cold and exposure.

Acts records only a small part of this. The Paul we see there is the tip of the iceberg.

**3. Anxiety for all the churches.** "And, apart from other things, there is the daily pressure on me of my anxiety for all the churches. Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to fall, and I am not indignant?" (11:28–29). Heavier for Paul than any bodily suffering was the burden of the churches. He was weak with the weak and burned for those who stumbled. This is a pastor's heart, and the heart of Christ.

**4. Let down in a basket.** "If I must boast, I will boast of the things that show my weakness... At Damascus, the governor under King Aretas was guarding the city of Damascus in order to seize me, but I was let down in a basket through a window in the wall and escaped his hands" (11:30, 32–33; cf. Acts 9:23–25). In the Roman army, the first soldier over the wall of an enemy city received a special honor. Many interpreters think Paul is being deliberately ironic: his "feat of arms" was not being first up the wall, but being lowered down it in a basket to run for his life. This is the weakness he will boast of.

> The point: Paul's résumé is not a record of success but of suffering for Christ and carrying the churches on his heart. He boasts of weakness, because what is shown in weakness is God's power, not his own.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For I betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ. But I am afraid that... your thoughts will be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to Christ" (11:2–3). Has my love for the Lord quietly become divided? Have I, without noticing, come to believe in "another Jesus": a Jesus mainly for making my life go smoothly, my children succeed and my business prosper, as though he were a powerful protector-deity rather than my bridegroom and Lord? When he does not give me what I expect, do I grow cold? The Lord Jesus' love is not like that. He "loved the church and gave himself up for her," to "sanctify her" (Eph 5:25–26). He shed his blood for me, who so often turns away, and he has never given up on me. "As the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so shall your God rejoice over you" (Isa 62:5). His love for me is a jealous love, because he wants all of me. So I can confess to him the things that have drawn my heart away, not out of fear that he will cast me off, but because I am drawn by such love and want to return to my first love. Lord, keep my heart, that I may love you alone, until the day you come for your bride.
