## 本章脉络

第十二章继续保罗「愚妄人」的自夸，然后转向他第三次去哥林多以前的挂虑，有三段。**异象与那根刺**（1-10节）：保罗讲到一个「被提到第三层天上去」的人，却不愿意多讲；他只夸自己的软弱。神给了他「一根刺」，他三次求主挪去，主却说：「我的恩典够你用的，因为我的能力是在人的软弱上显得完全。」**我所求的是你们**（11-18节）：保罗显出了「使徒的凭据」，却从不「累着」他们；他说：「我所求的是你们，不是你们的财物」，「我也甘心乐意为你们的灵魂费财费力」。**我怕**（19-21节）：保罗怕他再来的时候，看见教会中仍有纷争和不肯悔改的罪。

12:9-10是全书的高峰。保罗一直在讲软弱，到这里终于说出了原因：神的能力正是在人的软弱上显得完全。

## 深读 12:1-6 被提到第三层天

**1. 不得已的自夸。** 「我自夸固然无益，但我是不得已的。如今我要说到主的显现和启示。」（12:1）那些假使徒大概常常夸耀自己的异象和启示。保罗说，好吧，我也来说说。

**2. 「一个在基督里的人」。** 「我认得一个在基督里的人，他前十四年被提到第三层天上去。」（12:2）从12:7来看，这人就是保罗自己。他用第三人称讲述，好像在和这经历保持距离，不愿意把它当作自己的功劳。「前十四年」大约是主后40年代初，那是保罗信主以后在大数、叙利亚和基利家传道的那些年（徒9:30；加1:21），新约记载得很少。

**3. 第三层天，乐园。** 「他被提到乐园里，听见隐秘的言语，是人不可说的。」（12:4）「第三层天」和「乐园」很可能指同一个地方，就是神同在的地方（参路23:43）。保罗听见了，却「不可说」。

**4. 我宁可不说。** 「为这人，我要夸口；但是为我自己，除了我的软弱以外，我并不夸口……只是我禁止不说，恐怕有人把我看高了，过于他在我身上所看见所听见的。」（12:5-6）保罗有一次极大的属灵经历，看来他连在哥林多住了一年半的时候，都没有向他们提过。他不要人因为他的异象而看重他，只要人从他的生活和教导来认识他。今天有些人喜欢用自己的异梦、异象和「神对我说」来建立权威，保罗正好相反。

> 要点：保罗有过极大的属灵经历，却很少提起，也不用它来建立自己的权威。他宁可让人从他的生活和软弱中，看见基督。

## 深读 12:7-10 一根刺与够用的恩典

**1. 一根刺。** 「又恐怕我因所得的启示甚大，就过于自高，所以有一根刺加在我肉体上，就是撒但的差役要攻击我，免得我过于自高。」（12:7）这根刺是什么，保罗没有说，历代有许多猜测：
- 一种身体的疾病，例如眼疾（参加4:13-15，加拉太人愿意把眼睛剜出来给他）、癫痫或反复发作的热病；
- 不断攻击他的对手和迫害者（屈梭多模等古代教父的看法；旧约曾把敌人比作「肋下的荆棘」，民33:55，七十士译本用的正是同一个词）；
- 中世纪许多人认为是情欲的试探，加尔文则认为是各样属灵的试探；
- 也有人认为是撒但差役直接的搅扰。

我们不知道答案。也许保罗不说，正好让每一个带着「刺」的人，都能在这里找到自己。

**2. 谁给的？** 「有一根刺加在我肉体上，就是撒但的差役要攻击我。」这句话同时提到两方面：撒但的差役攻击他；但「加在」是被动的说法，目的又是「免得我过于自高」，这显然是神的心意。就像约伯记一样，撒但想要伤害，神却在掌管，并且使用这件事来成就他的美意（伯1:12；2:6）。神掌管，却不是恶的源头（参创50:20）。

**3. 三次求主。** 「为这事，我三次求过主，叫这刺离开我。」（12:8）保罗求的「主」，从下一节「基督的能力」来看，就是基督；保罗向基督祷告，这本身就默默见证了基督的神性。保罗求主挪去这根刺，这不是缺乏信心，而是正当的祷告。圣经鼓励病人请教会的长老为他祷告（雅5:14-15），主耶稣也医治过许多人（太8:16-17）。神有时医治；有时，他给的回答是另一种恩典。

**4. 我的恩典够你用的。** 「他对我说：『我的恩典够你用的，因为我的能力是在人的软弱上显得完全。』」（12:9）主没有挪去那根刺，却给了保罗自己的话。「他对我说」原文是完成时，意思是这句话说了，就一直有效；「够你用的」是现在时。许多解经家由此指出：这恩典不是将来才够用，而是现在就够用。主没有说软弱本身是好的，他是说，他的能力在软弱中「显得完全」，就是得以完全地彰显出来。

**5. 基督的能力覆庇我。** 「所以，我更喜欢夸自己的软弱，好叫基督的能力覆庇我。」（12:9）「覆庇」原文的意思是像帐幕一样住在上面。许多解经家认为，这里可能回响旧约的会幕：会幕完成时，「耶和华的荣光就充满了帐幕」（出40:34）；约翰说道成了肉身，「住在我们中间」（约1:14），原文也是「支搭帐幕」。基督的能力不是在保罗变强以后才来，而是像帐幕一样，搭在他软弱的身上。

**6. 什么时候软弱，什么时候就刚强。** 「我为基督的缘故，就以软弱、凌辱、急难、逼迫、困苦为可喜乐的；因我什么时候软弱，什么时候就刚强了。」（12:10）这不是要人喜欢受苦，也不是叫人不必求医治、不必改善处境；而是说，保罗不再把软弱看作神不在的记号。这句话的根，就是基督的十字架：「他因软弱被钉在十字架上，却因神的大能仍然活着」（13:4）。

**7. 两个误解。**
- 有人说：「如果你有足够的信心，神一定会医治你。」保罗的经历说明，一个满有信心的使徒，也可以带着一根神不挪去的刺。病没有得医治，不一定是因为信心不够，也不一定是因为犯了罪（参约9:2-3）。
- 也有人以为，软弱就要忍着、藏起来，不要麻烦人。保罗却公开说出自己的软弱，并且「夸」它，因为在那里，人看见的是基督。

> 要点：神不一定挪去我们的刺，却应许他的恩典每一天都够我们用。基督的能力像帐幕一样，搭在我们软弱的身上；所以我们不必隐藏软弱，反而可以夸它，因为在那里显明的是基督。

## 深读 12:11-18 我所求的是你们

**1. 使徒的凭据。** 「我在你们中间，用百般的忍耐，藉着神迹、奇事、异能显出使徒的凭据来。」（12:12）保罗并不否认神迹，他在哥林多也行过神迹。但这些凭据是在百般忍耐中、由神行出来的：真使徒的记号，不只是能力，也是在苦难中的忍耐。

**2. 这不公之处，求你们饶恕。** 「除了我不累着你们这一件事，你们还有什么事不及别的教会呢？这不公之处，求你们饶恕我吧。」（12:13）这是带着讽刺的话：我唯一「亏待」你们的，就是没有拿你们的钱！

**3. 父母为儿女积财。** 「如今，我打算第三次到你们那里去，也必不累着你们；因我所求的是你们，不是你们的财物。儿女不该为父母积财，父母该为儿女积财。」（12:14）保罗用的是当时人人都懂的家庭观念。他把自己看作哥林多教会的父亲，所以他不向他们拿，只为他们付出。这不是说儿女不需要孝敬父母（圣经也吩咐人孝敬父母，提前5:4），而是用父母为儿女付出的心，说明牧者对教会的爱。

**4. 费财费力。** 「我也甘心乐意为你们的灵魂费财费力。难道我越发爱你们，就越发少得你们的爱吗？」（12:15）「费财费力」原文是花费，并被花费：不只付出金钱，也把自己整个人耗尽。保罗爱得越多，得到的回应却越少。这是一位牧者心里很深的伤痛，也是基督的样式：他爱我们到底，我们却常常冷淡。

**5. 提多占过你们的便宜吗？** 「我所差到你们那里去的人，我藉着他们一个人占过你们的便宜吗？……提多占过你们的便宜吗？我们行事，不同是一个心灵吗？不同是一个脚踪吗？」（12:17-18）有人说保罗自己不收钱，却叫手下的人去收，再从中得利。保罗请他们回想：提多和那位弟兄，有没有占过他们一点便宜？

> 要点：保罗像父母一样爱教会：他所求的是人，不是人的财物，并且甘心为他们费财费力，即使爱得越多、得到的越少。这是基督爱我们的样式。

## 深读 12:19-21 我怕

**1. 不是为自己分诉。** 「你们到如今，还想我们是向你们分诉；我们本是在基督里当神面前说话。亲爱的弟兄啊，一切的事都是为造就你们。」（12:19）保罗讲了这么多，不是为了替自己辩护、挽回面子，而是为了造就他们。

**2. 纷争的罪。** 「我怕我再来的时候，见你们不合我所想望的，你们见我也不合你们所想望的；又怕有纷争、嫉妒、恼怒、结党、毁谤、谗言、狂傲、混乱的事。」（12:20）这八样罪都是破坏关系的罪。它们在教会中常常比明显的道德丑闻更普遍，也更容易被人忽略。

**3. 不肯悔改的罪。** 「且怕我来的时候，我的神叫我在你们面前惭愧，又因许多人从前犯罪，行污秽、奸淫、邪荡的事不肯悔改，我就忧愁。」（12:21）保罗怕的不是被人拒绝，而是看见人不肯悔改。他说「我的神叫我……惭愧」：他把教会的光景看作自己在神面前的事。他也说「我就忧愁」，原文是「哀恸」，这个词常用来指为死人哀哭，保罗在林前5:2也用它指为罪哀痛。一位牧者面对不肯悔改的罪，不是幸灾乐祸，也不是冷冷地定罪，而是哀恸。

> 要点：保罗所做的一切都是为了造就教会。他为教会中破坏关系的罪和不肯悔改的罪忧愁，为罪哀恸；真正的牧者面对罪，心里是痛，不是冷。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他对我说：『我的恩典够你用的，因为我的能力是在人的软弱上显得完全。』所以，我更喜欢夸自己的软弱，好叫基督的能力覆庇我。」（12:9）我身上是否也有一根「刺」：一种长期的病痛，一种身体或学习上的障碍，一种反复发作的忧郁或焦虑，一个改变不了的处境，一个一直没有得着答案的祷告？我是否曾经恳切地求主挪去它，后来渐渐不再提，心里却暗暗觉得，神不在乎，或者是我的信心不够？我是否因为这根刺，觉得自己是教会里的「问题」，不敢让人知道？保罗带着他的刺，主没有挪去，却亲自对他说话。主耶稣自己也曾「因软弱被钉在十字架上」（13:4），他的身体带着钉痕复活；他知道带着伤痕活着是什么滋味，也为我的罪受了伤。他的恩典不是等我的刺被拔掉以后才够用，而是此刻就够用。他的能力不是绕过我的软弱，而是像帐幕一样，搭在我的软弱上面。我仍然可以求他医治，也可以接受医生的帮助；但无论他今天怎样回答，我都可以把这根刺带到他面前，对他说：「主，你的恩典够我用。」然后，也许有一天，我能把这根刺告诉一位弟兄或姊妹，不是为了诉苦，而是为了让他们也看见，撑着我的是基督。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 12 continues Paul's "foolish" boasting and then turns to his concerns before his third visit to Corinth, in three parts. **Visions and the thorn** (vv. 1–10): Paul tells of a man "caught up to the third heaven" but will not say much; he boasts only of his weaknesses. God gave him "a thorn," and three times he asked the Lord to remove it, but the Lord said, "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness." **I seek not what is yours but you** (vv. 11–18): Paul showed "the signs of a true apostle" yet never "burdened" them; he says, "I seek not what is yours but you," and "I will most gladly spend and be spent for your souls." **I fear** (vv. 19–21): Paul fears that when he comes he will still find quarreling and unrepented sin in the church.

12:9–10 is the high point of the letter. Paul has been speaking of weakness all along; here at last he gives the reason: God's power is made perfect in human weakness.

## Going Deeper 12:1–6 Caught Up to the Third Heaven

**1. Boasting under compulsion.** "I must go on boasting. Though there is nothing to be gained by it, I will go on to visions and revelations of the Lord" (12:1). The false apostles probably boasted often of their visions and revelations. Paul says: very well, I will speak of them too.

**2. "A man in Christ."** "I know a man in Christ who fourteen years ago was caught up to the third heaven" (12:2). From 12:7 it is clear this man is Paul himself. He tells it in the third person, as if keeping his distance from the experience and refusing to take credit for it. "Fourteen years ago" would be in the early AD 40s, during the years after his conversion when Paul preached in Tarsus, Syria and Cilicia (Acts 9:30; Gal 1:21), years about which the New Testament says little.

**3. The third heaven, paradise.** "...and he heard things that cannot be told, which man may not utter" (12:4). "The third heaven" and "paradise" (12:3–4) probably refer to the same place, the place of God's presence (cf. Luke 23:43). Paul heard, but "may not utter" it.

**4. I would rather not say.** "On behalf of this man I will boast, but on my own behalf I will not boast, except of my weaknesses... But I refrain from it, so that no one may think more of me than he sees in me or hears from me" (12:5–6). Paul had an extraordinary spiritual experience and apparently had not spoken of it even to the Corinthians, among whom he had lived for eighteen months. He did not want people to think more of him because of his visions, only to know him from his life and teaching. Some today like to build their authority on their dreams, visions and "God told me." Paul does the opposite.

> The point: Paul had an extraordinary spiritual experience, yet seldom spoke of it and never used it to build his own authority. He would rather people saw Christ in his life and his weakness.

## Going Deeper 12:7–10 A Thorn and Sufficient Grace

**1. A thorn.** "So to keep me from being too elated by the surpassing greatness of the revelations, a thorn was given me in the flesh, a messenger of Satan to harass me, to keep me from being too elated" (12:7). Paul does not say what the thorn was, and there have been many guesses through the centuries:
- a physical ailment, such as an eye disease (cf. Gal 4:13–15, where the Galatians would have given him their eyes), epilepsy or recurring fever;
- the opponents and persecutors who constantly attacked him (the view of Chrysostom and other early fathers; the Old Testament calls enemies "thorns in your sides," Num 33:55, using the same word in the Greek version);
- many in the Middle Ages took it as sexual temptation, while Calvin took it as spiritual temptations of every kind;
- some take it as direct harassment by a messenger of Satan.

We do not know. Perhaps Paul's silence lets everyone who carries a "thorn" find himself here.

**2. Who gave it?** "A thorn was given me in the flesh, a messenger of Satan to harass me." The sentence holds two things together. A messenger of Satan harassed him; but "was given" is passive, and its purpose, "to keep me from being too elated," is plainly God's purpose. As in Job, Satan meant to harm, but God was in control and used it for his good purpose (Job 1:12; 2:6). God rules over evil without being its author (cf. Gen 50:20).

**3. Three times he pleaded.** "Three times I pleaded with the Lord about this, that it should leave me" (12:8). The "Lord" Paul prays to is Christ, as "the power of Christ" in the next verse shows; that Paul prays to Christ quietly witnesses to his deity. Asking the Lord to remove the thorn was not a lack of faith but right prayer. Scripture encourages the sick to call the elders to pray for them (Jas 5:14–15), and Jesus healed many (Matt 8:16–17). God sometimes heals; sometimes his answer is another kind of grace.

**4. My grace is sufficient.** "But he said to me, 'My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness'" (12:9). The Lord did not remove the thorn, but he gave Paul his own word. "He said" is a Greek perfect: the word, once spoken, stands. "Is sufficient" is present. Many draw from this that grace is not only sufficient later but sufficient now. The Lord does not say weakness itself is good. He says his power is "made perfect," brought to full display, in weakness.

**5. The power of Christ resting on me.** "Therefore I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may rest upon me" (12:9). "Rest upon" is literally "pitch a tent over." Many interpreters hear an echo of the tabernacle here: when the tabernacle was finished, "the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle" (Exod 40:34); John says the Word became flesh and "dwelt among us" (John 1:14), literally "tabernacled." Christ's power does not come only once Paul has become strong. It pitches its tent over his weak body.

**6. When I am weak, then I am strong.** "For the sake of Christ, then, I am content with weaknesses, insults, hardships, persecutions, and calamities. For when I am weak, then I am strong" (12:10). This does not mean we should enjoy suffering, or that we need not seek healing or better circumstances. It means Paul no longer sees weakness as a sign that God is absent. The root of this sentence is the cross: "he was crucified in weakness, but lives by the power of God" (13:4).

**7. Two misunderstandings.**
- Some say, "If you had enough faith, God would surely heal you." Paul's experience shows that an apostle full of faith could carry a thorn God would not remove. Illness that is not healed is not necessarily from lack of faith, nor necessarily because of sin (cf. John 9:2–3).
- Others think weakness must be endured in silence and hidden, so as not to trouble anyone. Paul spoke openly of his weakness, and even "boasted" of it, because there people saw Christ.

> The point: God does not always remove our thorns, but he promises that his grace is sufficient for us every day. Christ's power pitches its tent over our weakness, so we need not hide our weaknesses; we can even boast of them, because there Christ is shown.

## Going Deeper 12:11–18 I Seek Not What Is Yours but You

**1. The signs of an apostle.** "The signs of a true apostle were performed among you with utmost patience, with signs and wonders and mighty works" (12:12). Paul does not deny miracles; he did them in Corinth. These signs were worked, by God, amid utmost patience: the mark of a true apostle is not only power but endurance in suffering.

**2. Forgive me this wrong.** "For in what were you less favored than the rest of the churches, except that I myself did not burden you? Forgive me this wrong!" (12:13). This is irony: the only way I "wronged" you was by not taking your money!

**3. Parents save for their children.** "Here for the third time I am ready to come to you. And I will not be a burden, for I seek not what is yours but you. For children are not obligated to save up for their parents, but parents for their children" (12:14). Paul uses a family idea everyone understood. He sees himself as the Corinthian church's father, so he does not take from them; he only gives. This does not mean children need not honor and support their parents (Scripture commands that too, 1 Tim 5:4). It uses a parent's giving heart to show a pastor's love for the church.

**4. Spend and be spent.** "I will most gladly spend and be spent for your souls. If I love you more, am I to be loved less?" (12:15). "Spend and be spent" means not only giving money but being used up entirely. The more Paul loved, the less love came back. This is a deep wound in a pastor's heart, and it is the pattern of Christ: he loves us to the end, and we are so often cold.

**5. Did Titus take advantage of you?** "Did I take advantage of you through any of those whom I sent to you?... Did Titus take advantage of you? Did we not act in the same spirit? Did we not take the same steps?" (12:17–18). Some said Paul refused money himself but had his men collect it and profited through them. Paul asks them to remember: did Titus or that brother ever take the slightest advantage of them?

> The point: Paul loved the church as a parent: he sought the people, not their possessions, and gladly spent himself for them, even when the more he loved the less he was loved. This is how Christ loves us.

## Going Deeper 12:19–21 I Fear

**1. Not defending ourselves.** "Have you been thinking all along that we have been defending ourselves to you? It is in the sight of God that we have been speaking in Christ, and all for your upbuilding, beloved" (12:19). Paul has said so much not to defend himself or save face but to build them up.

**2. Sins of division.** "For I fear that perhaps when I come I may find you not as I wish, and that you may find me not as you wish- that perhaps there may be quarreling, jealousy, anger, hostility, slander, gossip, conceit, and disorder" (12:20). These eight are all sins that destroy relationships. In churches they are often more common than obvious moral scandals, and more easily overlooked.

**3. Sin not repented of.** "I fear that when I come again my God may humble me before you, and I may have to mourn over many of those who sinned earlier and have not repented of the impurity, sexual immorality, and sensuality that they have practiced" (12:21). What Paul fears is not rejection but seeing people refuse to repent. He says "my God may humble me": he treats the church's condition as his own before God. And he says he would "mourn," a word often used of grieving the dead, and used by Paul of grief over sin in 1 Cor 5:2. A pastor facing unrepented sin does not gloat or coldly condemn; he grieves.

> The point: Everything Paul did was to build up the church. He grieved over sins that destroy relationships and sins not repented of, grieving over sin; a true pastor faces sin with pain, not coldness.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But he said to me, 'My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.' Therefore I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may rest upon me" (12:9). Do I carry a "thorn": a long-term illness, a physical or learning disability, recurring depression or anxiety, a situation that will not change, a prayer that has never been answered? Did I once plead with the Lord to remove it, and then gradually stop mentioning it, while quietly feeling that God does not care, or that my faith is not enough? Because of this thorn, do I feel I am a "problem" in the church, and dare not let anyone know? Paul carried his thorn; the Lord did not remove it, but spoke to him himself. The Lord Jesus too "was crucified in weakness" (13:4), and rose with the nail marks in his body. He knows what it is to live with wounds, and he was wounded for my sins. His grace is not sufficient only after my thorn is pulled out; it is sufficient now. His power does not go around my weakness; it pitches its tent over it. I can still ask him for healing, and I can accept a doctor's help; but however he answers today, I can bring this thorn before him and say, "Lord, your grace is sufficient for me." And perhaps one day I can tell a brother or sister about it, not to complain, but so that they too may see that what holds me up is Christ.
