## 本章脉络

第十三章是全书的结尾，有三段。**第三次来的警告**（1-4节）：「这是我第三次要到你们那里去」；保罗说，若再来，「必不宽容」。他们要寻求「基督在我里面说话的凭据」，保罗就指向十字架：「他因软弱被钉在十字架上，却因神的大能仍然活着。」**自己省察**（5-10节）：哥林多人一直在试验保罗，保罗却请他们反过来试验自己：「岂不知……有耶稣基督在你们心里吗？」他所求的，是他们「作完全人」。**末了的话**（11-14节）：「要作完全人；要受安慰；要同心合意；要彼此和睦」；最后是三一神的祝福：「愿主耶稣基督的恩惠、神的慈爱、圣灵的感动常与你们众人同在！」

全书从「赐各样安慰的神」（1:3）开始，以「仁爱和平的神」（13:11）和三一神的祝福结束。这封充满眼泪、冲突和自我辩护的信，最后落在神的恩惠、慈爱和相交上。

## 深读 13:1-4 因软弱被钉，因神的大能活着

**1. 两三个人的口。** 「这是我第三次要到你们那里去。『凭两三个人的口作见证，句句都要定准。』」（13:1）这句话引自申命记19:15，主耶稣也用它来讲教会的劝诫（太18:16）。解经家对这里有两种理解：有人认为保罗是说他来时会按正当程序审断，凭证人，不凭传言；也有人认为保罗把自己的多次到访和警告（13:2）看作两三个见证。两种理解都说明，他不会草率定人的罪。

这原则的核心是「印证」，为了保护人不被冤枉，但今天有时被误用：有人说，性侵或虐待的事没有两三个证人，教会就不能处理，或者引用「控告长老的呈子，非有两三个见证就不要收」（提前5:19）来拒绝受理。这是错误的。
- 这段经文讲的是教会内部的审断，不是叫受害人不要报警；涉及罪行的，报警、交由司法处理是对的（罗13:4）。
- 虐待常常发生在没有旁人的地方，「两三个见证」也可以包括其他受害者、文字记录、医疗证据和专业调查。
- 教会不可以用这节经文叫受害人闭口，也不可以「家丑不可外扬」为理由遮盖。

**2. 必不宽容。** 「我若再来，必不宽容。」（13:2）保罗前面一直很温柔，但温柔不等于纵容。对那些持续犯罪、不肯悔改的人（12:21），他必须处理。

**3. 基督在我里面说话的凭据。** 「你们既然寻求基督在我里面说话的凭据，我必不宽容。因为，基督在你们身上不是软弱的，在你们里面是有大能的。」（13:3）哥林多人想要看到「凭据」，就是保罗有能力、有权柄的证明。保罗说，你们会看到的，但那能力的样式可能和你们想的不一样。

**4. 十字架的样式。** 「他因软弱被钉在十字架上，却因神的大能仍然活着。我们也是这样同他软弱，但因神向你们所显的大能，也必与他同活。」（13:4）这节经文是整卷书的总结。基督看起来最软弱的时候，就是在十字架上；神的大能却叫他从死里复活。保罗的服事也是这样：先是软弱，然后显出神的大能。这不是说保罗靠自己的软弱得着什么，而是说，他与那位钉十字架又复活的基督联合，他的生命就带着同样的样式。

> 要点：基督因软弱被钉在十字架上，却因神的大能仍然活着。真正属基督的能力，带着十字架的样式；它不绕过软弱，而是在软弱中显明出来。

## 深读 13:5-10 岂不知有耶稣基督在你们心里吗？

**1. 反过来试验自己。** 「你们总要自己省察有信心没有，也要自己试验。」（13:5）哥林多人一直在审查保罗：他是不是真使徒？他有没有凭据？保罗说：你们先省察自己吧。「试验」这个词（也包括「可弃绝的」，原文是试验不合格的）常用于验证金属或钱币的真假；「省察」是一般的察验、考验。

**2. 省察的目的是确信。** 「岂不知你们若不是可弃绝的，就有耶稣基督在你们心里吗？」（13:5）保罗省察的问题，不是「你们够不够好」，而是「耶稣基督是不是在你们里面」。他问「岂不知」，其实是盼望他们回答：是的，我们知道。他们信主、蒙圣灵、被福音改变，本身就是基督在他们里面的凭据，也是保罗作使徒的凭据（3:2-3）。

这样的省察不是叫人无止境地挖掘自己，越看越不确定。改革宗传统（参《威斯敏斯德信条》第18章）分辨两件事：
- 我们得救的根据，在基督为我们所成就的工作，不在我们自己里面；
- 省察是要看我们是否真的投靠了这位基督，并且看见圣灵在我们里面所结的果子（参罗8:16；约一3:19-20「神比我们的心大」）。

对那些良心软弱、常常怀疑自己是否得救的人，省察的方向是：把眼睛从自己身上移开，再一次投靠基督。对那些安然活在罪中、却自以为没事的人，这节经文是一个严肃的警告：「可弃绝的」原文就是试验不合格的意思。

**3. 只要你们行事端正。** 「我们求神，叫你们一件恶事都不做；这不是要显明我们是蒙悦纳的，是要你们行事端正，任凭人看我们是被弃绝的吧！」（13:7）保罗不在乎自己的名声，只在乎他们的好。哪怕他看起来「不合格」，只要他们行事端正，他就满足了。

**4. 你们作完全人。** 「即便我们软弱，你们刚强，我们也欢喜；并且我们所求的，就是你们作完全人。」（13:9）「完全人」原文是修补、复原的意思。这个词和林前1:10「彼此相合」、加6:1「挽回」属于同一组词；福音书用它来指「补网」（太4:21）。13:11「要作完全人」也是同一个字根。保罗求的，是一个破裂的教会被修复。

**5. 为造就人的权柄。** 「这权柄原是为造就人，并不是为败坏人。」（13:10）这是10:8的回响。保罗写这封信，正是为了到时候不必严厉。

> 要点：省察的目的不是叫人在自己身上找确据，而是问：耶稣基督是不是在我里面？软弱的人要再次投靠基督；安于罪中的人要听见警告。保罗所求的，是教会被修复，不是自己的名声。

## 深读 13:11-14 仁爱和平的神与三一神的祝福

**1. 末了的话。** 「还有末了的话：愿弟兄们都喜乐。要作完全人；要受安慰；要同心合意；要彼此和睦。如此，仁爱和平的神必常与你们同在。」（13:11）五个简短的吩咐，几乎把全书的主题都收在一起：喜乐（2:3；7:4）、修复（13:9）、安慰（1:3-7）、同心（不再分党）、和睦（与神和好，也彼此和好，5:18-20）。「要受安慰」这个词有几种理解：要受安慰（和合本），要彼此安慰，或者要听从我的劝勉。

**2. 应许在吩咐之后，却不是靠吩咐赚来的。** 「如此，仁爱和平的神必常与你们同在。」神的同在不是对他们表现良好的奖赏；神本来就是「仁爱和平的神」，他的同在是他们能够彼此相爱、彼此和睦的力量。

**3. 圣洁的亲嘴。** 「你们亲嘴问安，彼此务要圣洁。」（13:12）这是当时家人之间问安的方式。在教会里，奴隶和主人、犹太人和外邦人，都用家人的方式彼此问安。今天在不同的文化中，表达的方式可以不同，例如握手或拥抱，但其中的意思不变：我们是一家人。

**4. 三一神的祝福。** 「愿主耶稣基督的恩惠、神的慈爱、圣灵的感动常与你们众人同在！」（13:14，有些圣经版本编为13:13）这是新约中对三一神最清楚的表述之一（参太28:19），也是许多教会在聚会结束时的祝福。
- **主耶稣基督的恩惠：** 放在第一位，因为我们是藉着基督的恩惠，才认识神的慈爱（8:9）。
- **神的慈爱：** 父神的爱是一切救恩的源头（5:18-19）；这里的「神」是指父神，并不是说子和圣灵不是神。
- **圣灵的感动：** 原文是「相交」，可以指与圣灵的相交，也可以指圣灵所带来的、信徒彼此之间的相交；两种意思很可能都包括在内。哥林多教会最缺乏的正是这相交。

保罗没有说圣父、圣子、圣灵是三位神，也没有说他们只是一位神的三种表现。他把三位并列，同为一切祝福的源头。对照全本圣经（申6:4；太28:19），这正是教会所承认的：三个位格各自分明，每一位都完全是神，又同是一位神。这祝福「与你们众人同在」：包括那些曾经反对他、伤害他的人。

> 要点：全书以三一神的祝福结束：主耶稣基督的恩惠、父神的慈爱、圣灵的相交。这位仁爱和平的神，不是在我们和好以后才来，而是他的同在，叫一个破裂的教会能够被修复。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们总要自己省察有信心没有，也要自己试验。岂不知你们若不是可弃绝的，就有耶稣基督在你们心里吗？」（13:5）当我省察自己的时候，我通常在看什么？是一遍又一遍地清点自己的失败，越看越怀疑自己是否真的得救？还是根本不愿意省察，以为自己一直参加聚会、也受过洗，就一定没有问题？保罗的问题很简单：耶稣基督是不是在你里面？如果我曾经投靠他，现在仍然投靠他，那么我得救的根据就不在我的表现，而在那位为我的罪「因软弱被钉在十字架上，却因神的大能仍然活着」的主（13:4）。圣灵会在我心里作见证（罗8:16），而且「神比我们的心大」（约一3:20）。如果我发现自己一直活在某个罪中，却毫不在意，这正是神在呼唤我回转的时候；他的恩惠仍然向我敞开。读完哥林多后书，我不必再带着一张责任清单离开，而是可以安静地领受保罗给哥林多人的最后一句话，因为它也是给我的：「愿主耶稣基督的恩惠、神的慈爱、圣灵的感动常与你们众人同在！」（13:14）这祝福是给「众人」的，包括那个软弱、跌倒、常常怀疑自己的我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 13 closes the letter, in three parts. **Warning before the third visit** (vv. 1–4): "This is the third time I am coming to you"; if he comes again, "I will not spare them." They want "proof that Christ is speaking in me," and Paul points them to the cross: "he was crucified in weakness, but lives by the power of God." **Examine yourselves** (vv. 5–10): the Corinthians have been testing Paul; Paul asks them to turn the test on themselves: "do you not realize this about yourselves, that Jesus Christ is in you?" What he prays for is their "restoration." **Final words** (vv. 11–14): "Aim for restoration, comfort one another, agree with one another, live in peace"; and finally the blessing of the triune God: "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all."

The letter began with "the God of all comfort" (1:3) and ends with "the God of love and peace" (13:11) and the blessing of the triune God. A letter full of tears, conflict and self-defense comes to rest on God's grace, love and fellowship.

## Going Deeper 13:1–4 Crucified in Weakness, Living by God's Power

**1. Two or three witnesses.** "This is the third time I am coming to you. Every charge must be established by the evidence of two or three witnesses" (13:1). This comes from Deuteronomy 19:15, and Jesus used it of church discipline (Matt 18:16). Interpreters read this two ways: some think Paul means he will judge by proper process when he comes, with witnesses and not on rumor; others think he counts his repeated visits and warnings (13:2) as the two or three witnesses. Either way, he will not condemn anyone hastily.

The principle is corroboration, and it protects people from false accusation, but it is sometimes misused today: some say that if there are not two or three witnesses to sexual abuse or other abuse, the church cannot act, or they cite "Do not admit a charge against an elder except on the evidence of two or three witnesses" (1 Tim 5:19) to refuse a complaint. That is wrong.
- This passage concerns judgment within the church; it does not tell victims not to go to the police. Where a crime is involved, reporting it to the police and the courts is right (Rom 13:4).
- Abuse often happens where no one else is present. "Two or three witnesses" can include other victims, written records, medical evidence and professional investigation.
- The church must not use this verse to silence victims, nor cover things up for the sake of face (家丑不可外扬).

**2. I will not spare them.** "...if I come again I will not spare them" (13:2). Paul has been very gentle, but gentleness is not indulgence. Those who keep sinning and refuse to repent (12:21) must be dealt with.

**3. Proof that Christ speaks in me.** "...since you seek proof that Christ is speaking in me. He is not weak in dealing with you, but is powerful among you" (13:3). The Corinthians wanted "proof," evidence that Paul had power and authority. Paul says: you will see it, but the shape of that power may not be what you expect.

**4. The pattern of the cross.** "For he was crucified in weakness, but lives by the power of God. For we also are weak in him, but in dealing with you we will live with him by the power of God" (13:4). This verse sums up the whole letter. Christ looked weakest on the cross, yet God's power raised him from the dead. Paul's ministry has the same shape: weakness first, then God's power shown. This does not mean Paul gains anything by his own weakness; it means that, united with the crucified and risen Christ, his life bears the same pattern.

> The point: Christ was crucified in weakness but lives by the power of God. True power that belongs to Christ bears the shape of the cross; it does not bypass weakness but is shown in it.

## Going Deeper 13:5–10 Jesus Christ Is in You

**1. Turning the test around.** "Examine yourselves, to see whether you are in the faith. Test yourselves" (13:5). The Corinthians had been examining Paul: was he a true apostle? Where was his proof? Paul says: examine yourselves first. "Test" (dokimazō), like "fail to meet the test" (adokimos), is the word used for assaying metal or coins; "examine" (peirazō) is the more general word for putting something to the test.

**2. Examination for the sake of assurance.** "Or do you not realize this about yourselves, that Jesus Christ is in you?- unless indeed you fail to meet the test!" (13:5). Paul's question is not "are you good enough?" but "is Jesus Christ in you?" He asks "do you not realize," hoping they will answer: yes, we do. That they had believed, received the Spirit and been changed by the gospel was itself evidence that Christ was in them, and evidence of Paul's apostleship (3:2–3).

Such self-examination is not endless digging into oneself that only breeds uncertainty. The Reformed tradition (e.g. Westminster Confession 18) distinguishes two things:
- the ground of our salvation is Christ's work for us, not anything in ourselves;
- self-examination asks whether we have truly put our trust in this Christ, and looks for the fruit of the Spirit in us (cf. Rom 8:16; 1 John 3:19–20, "God is greater than our heart").

For those with tender consciences who often doubt their salvation, examination means taking their eyes off themselves and trusting Christ once again. For those living comfortably in sin and thinking all is well, this verse is a solemn warning: to "fail to meet the test" is to be found counterfeit.

**3. That you may do what is right.** "But we pray to God that you may not do wrong- not that we may appear to have met the test, but that you may do what is right, though we may seem to have failed" (13:7). Paul does not care about his own reputation, only about their good. Even if he seems to have "failed," he is content as long as they do right.

**4. Your restoration.** "For we are glad when we are weak and you are strong. Your restoration is what we pray for" (13:9). "Restoration" means mending, setting right, from the same word family as "united" in 1 Corinthians 1:10 and "restore" in Galatians 6:1; the Gospels use the verb for "mending" nets (Matt 4:21). The command "aim for restoration" in 13:11 shares the root. Paul prays for a broken church to be mended.

**5. Authority for building up.** "...the authority that the Lord has given me for building up and not for tearing down" (13:10). This echoes 10:8. Paul writes this letter precisely so that he will not have to be severe when he comes.

> The point: Self-examination is not about finding assurance in ourselves but asking: is Jesus Christ in me? The weak should trust Christ again; the complacent should hear the warning. Paul prays for the church's restoration, not for his own reputation.

## Going Deeper 13:11–14 The God of Love and Peace, and the Blessing of the Triune God

**1. Final words.** "Finally, brothers, rejoice. Aim for restoration, comfort one another, agree with one another, live in peace; and the God of love and peace will be with you" (13:11). Five short commands gather almost every theme of the letter: joy (2:3; 7:4), restoration (13:9), comfort (1:3–7), agreement (no more factions), peace (reconciled to God and to one another, 5:18–20). The verb can mean "be comforted" (CUV), "comfort one another" (ESV), or "listen to my appeal" (NIV, NRSV).

**2. The promise follows the commands, but is not earned by them.** "...and the God of love and peace will be with you." God's presence is not a reward for good behavior. God is "the God of love and peace," and his presence is the power by which they can love one another and live in peace.

**3. A holy kiss.** "Greet one another with a holy kiss" (13:12). This was how family members greeted each other. In the church, slaves and masters, Jews and Gentiles greeted one another as family. In different cultures today the form may differ, a handshake or an embrace, but the meaning is the same: we are one family.

**4. The blessing of the triune God.** "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all" (13:14; 13:13 in some editions). This is one of the clearest statements of the Trinity in the New Testament (cf. Matt 28:19), and the blessing with which many churches close their worship.
- **The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ:** placed first, because it is through Christ's grace that we come to know God's love (8:9).
- **The love of God:** the Father's love is the source of all salvation (5:18–19); here "God" designates the Father, without implying that the Son and Spirit are less than God.
- **The fellowship of the Holy Spirit:** this can mean fellowship with the Spirit, or the fellowship among believers that the Spirit creates; probably both are meant. It was exactly what the Corinthian church lacked.

Paul does not speak of three gods, nor of one God appearing in three modes. He names the Son, the Father and the Spirit side by side as the one source of blessing. Read with the rest of Scripture (Deut 6:4; Matt 28:19), this is the faith the church confesses: three distinct persons, each fully God, who are one God. And the blessing is "with you all," including those who had opposed and wounded him.

> The point: The letter ends with the blessing of the triune God: the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, the love of the Father and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit. The God of love and peace does not come only after we are reconciled; his presence is what lets a broken church be mended.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Examine yourselves, to see whether you are in the faith. Test yourselves. Or do you not realize this about yourselves, that Jesus Christ is in you?" (13:5). When I examine myself, what do I usually look at? Do I go over my failures again and again until I doubt whether I am really saved? Or do I refuse to examine myself at all, assuming that because I attend church and have been baptized, all must be well? Paul's question is simple: is Jesus Christ in you? If I have trusted him and trust him still, then the ground of my salvation is not my performance but the Lord who died for my sins, who "was crucified in weakness, but lives by the power of God" (13:4). The Spirit bears witness in my heart (Rom 8:16), and "God is greater than our heart" (1 John 3:20). If I find I have been living in some sin without caring, this is the very moment God is calling me back, and his grace is still open to me. Finishing 2 Corinthians, I need not walk away with a checklist of duties. I can quietly receive Paul's last words to the Corinthians, because they are for me too: "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all" (13:14). The blessing is for "all," including the weak, stumbling, often doubting me.
