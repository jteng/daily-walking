## 这卷书在讲什么

约翰二书只有十三节，是新约里最短的书卷之一，用一张蒲草纸就写得下。写信的人自称「作长老的」，收信的是「蒙拣选的太太和她的儿女」（1:1）。信很短，却有两个词一再出现：一个是「真理」，单在头四节里就出现了五次；另一个是「爱」。长老爱他们，是「诚心所爱的」，原文直译是在真理中所爱的；他爱他们，「是为真理的缘故」（1:2）；他祝福他们，是「在真理和爱心上」（1:3）。

这封信做了三件事：
- **为他们欢喜：** 长老见到这位「太太」的儿女中，有人「照我们从父所受之命令遵行真理」，就「甚欢喜」（1:4）。
- **劝他们彼此相爱：** 这不是一条新命令，「乃是我们从起初所受的命令」（1:5）。爱就是照神的命令行（1:6）。
- **警告他们防备迷惑人的：** 「世上有许多迷惑人的出来，他们不认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的。」（1:7）这样的人若来，「不要接他到家里，也不要问他的安」（1:10）。

在这封信里，真理和爱不是两样互相拉扯的东西。没有真理的爱，会变成一种只求和气的感情；没有爱的真理，会变成冷冰冰的争辩。约翰把两样放在一起，因为两样都是从同一位来的：「恩典和真理都是由耶稣基督来的。」（约1:17）他要信徒在爱里守住真理，也在真理里彼此相爱。有时候，爱也需要说「不」：爱羊群的人，不会把狼请进羊圈里（徒20:29）。

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 「作长老的」。** 约翰二书和三书的作者都没有写自己的名字，只自称「作长老的」（1:1；约三1:1）。这个词可以指年纪老的人，也可以指教会中的领袖；彼得也称自己是「作长老的」（彼前5:1）。这位长老不必说出自己的名字，读者也知道他是谁，可见他在这一带的教会中很有名望。这封信的用词和思路，和约翰一书非常接近：「从起初所受的命令」（1:5；约一2:7），「不认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的」（1:7；约一4:2-3），「敌基督的」（1:7；约一2:18、22），「认子的，连父也有了」（1:9；约一2:23）。多数学者认为，三封信出于同一位作者。

**2. 是哪一位约翰？** 约翰一书导读已经介绍过帕皮亚的那段话，以及长老约翰的问题。早期教会接受这封信的时间比约翰一书慢一些。优西比乌把约翰二书和三书列在有人质疑的书卷中，说有人不确定这两封信是福音书作者约翰写的，还是另一位同名的人写的（《教会史》第三卷第二十五章）。不过二世纪末的爱任纽已经引用这封信，说是「主的门徒约翰」写的（《驳异端》第一卷第十六章第三节）。本研读和约翰一书导读一样，采用传统的看法：作者是年老的使徒约翰。他称自己为「长老」，或许正是因为他是当时还活着的、最年长的一位见证人。

**3. 「蒙拣选的太太」是谁？** 这个问题有两种主要的看法：
- **一间教会：** 多数现代学者（如斯托得、克鲁斯、亚伯勒，以及天主教学者雷蒙德·布朗）认为，「太太」是用拟人的说法指一间地方教会，「她的儿女」是教会的会众。理由是：信里有好几处用的是复数的「你们」（1:6、8、10、12）；信的内容是对一群人说的；「一切知道真理之人」都爱她（1:1），不太像是指一个人；结尾「你那蒙拣选之姊妹的儿女都问你安」（1:13），也很像是一间教会向另一间教会问安。圣经也常把神的百姓比作母亲、妻子或新妇（赛54:1、13；何2:19-20；加4:26-27；弗5:25；启19:7），彼得也用「在巴比伦与你们同蒙拣选的」来指一间教会（彼前5:13，原文是阴性的「她」）。
- **一位姊妹：** 也有人认为收信的是一位真实的女信徒，她的儿女跟她住在一起，教会也可能在她家里聚会。原文的「太太」（*kyria*）也可以是一个人名，「蒙拣选的」（*eklektē*）也可能是人名。不过第13节也称她的姊妹为「蒙拣选的」，两姊妹都名叫依勒克他，不太可能，所以持这看法的人多半把 *kyria*（贵妇）当作人名。一段以拉丁文流传、归在亚历山大的革利免名下的残篇说，这封信是写给一位名叫依勒克他的妇人，又说这名字表明圣教会是蒙拣选的。

本研读采用第一种看法，但两种看法都不影响我们读懂这封信。无论「太太」是一间教会，还是一位在家中接待教会的姊妹，第10节说的「接他到家里」，都很可能和教会在家中聚会有关。

**4. 写作的时间和地点。** 这封信大概和约翰一书写在差不多的时候（约主后85-95年），从以弗所写给附近的一间教会。约翰一书所说「从我们中间出去」的人（约一2:19），这时可能正往各处走，到别的教会去传他们的教导。所以长老写这封短信，提醒这间教会要警醒。

**5. 巡回的传道人和家中的教会。** 一世纪的教会没有自己的礼拜堂，信徒在家中聚会（罗16:5；门1:2）。传道的人从一个城走到另一个城，路上的客店名声不好，所以他们常住在信徒家里，由信徒供给他们，再送他们往前走（约三1:5-8）。这本是福音传开的一条大路，却也给了假教师可乘之机。一份大约写于一世纪末或二世纪初的教会手册《十二使徒遗训》，就教导信徒怎样试验来访的传道人：他若教导的和所传的道不同，就不要听他；他若住上第三天，或开口要钱，就是假先知（第十一章）。约翰二书的警告，正是在这样的背景下写的。把一个传道人接到家里，不只是请他吃一顿饭，而是供给他，帮他到下一个地方去，很可能也包括让他在聚会中讲道。

## 钥匙经文：1:3；1:9

**1. 「恩惠、怜悯、平安……必常与我们同在」。** 「恩惠、怜悯、平安从父神和他儿子耶稣基督在真理和爱心上必常与我们同在！」（1:3）保罗书信开头的问安，原文常常没有动词；这里约翰却用了将来式的动词：恩惠、怜悯、平安「必」与我们同在。这是一个应许，不只是一个愿望。在约翰福音和约翰书信里，「怜悯」这个词只在这里出现。约翰称耶稣为「父的儿子」（和合本译作「他儿子」），这说法在新约中只出现在这里。这一切祝福，都「在真理和爱心上」与我们同在：神的恩典有真理的根基，不是一句空话；神的真理带着爱，不是冷冰冰的。

**2. 「就有父又有子」。** 「凡越过基督的教训不常守着的，就没有神；常守这教训的，就有父又有子。」（1:9）那些迷惑人的，自以为走在前面，比使徒的教导更进一步；约翰却说，离开了基督的教训，并没有走得更远，反倒失去了神。真正的长进，不是越过基督，而是更深地「常守」在他里面。常守这教训的人，得着的不只是一套正确的教义，而是父与子自己（参约一2:23；5:12）。

## 全书结构

约翰二书可以这样分：
- **问安**（1-3节）：长老写信给蒙拣选的太太和她的儿女；他们因真理彼此相爱；恩惠、怜悯、平安必与我们同在。
- **欢喜和劝勉**（4-6节）：有些儿女遵行真理，长老甚欢喜；要彼此相爱，这是从起初所受的命令；照他的命令行，就是爱。
- **警告**（7-11节）：许多迷惑人的出来了，他们不认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的；要小心，不要失去所做的工；凡越过基督的教训的，就没有神；不要接他到家里。
- **结语**（12-13节）：盼望当面谈论，使喜乐满足；蒙拣选之姊妹的儿女问安。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 和约翰一书对照着读。** 约翰一书像是一篇写给许多教会的长信，约翰二书像是一张写给一间教会的便条，两封信说的是同一件事。读约翰二书的每一节，都可以翻到约翰一书去找相关的经文：「从起初所受的命令」（1:5；约一2:7-8）；「成了肉身来的」（1:7；约一4:2）；「就有父又有子」（1:9；约一2:23）。约翰一书讲的道理，在约翰二书里成了一间教会具体要做的决定。

**2. 留意「真理」是什么意思。** 约翰说的「真理」，不只是一些正确的说法，而是神在耶稣基督里所启示的实在。耶稣说：「我就是道路、真理、生命。」（约14:6）所以真理能「存在我们里面，也必永远与我们同在」（1:2），这和耶稣所应许那「真理的圣灵」很相似：他要「永远与你们同在」（约14:16-17）。人可以「遵行真理」（1:4），因为真理不只要人相信，也要人活出来。

**3. 读第10-11节要小心，不要读过了头，也不要读得不够。** 约翰不是叫信徒不跟不信的人来往，也不是叫我们对别的宗教的邻舍冷淡无礼。耶稣吩咐我们要爱仇敌（太5:44），保罗也说「有了机会就当向众人行善」（加6:10）。约翰说的，是不可接待那些四处传讲否认基督的教导的人，不可让他们进到家中的聚会讲道，也不可供给他们、为他们送行。他所说的错误也不是枝节的问题，而是福音的根基：耶稣基督是不是成了肉身来的。把这两节用在次要的教义分歧上，会造成不必要的分裂；完全不理会这两节，又会把讲台交给否认基督的人。还有一点：信里警告的是四处迷惑人的人，不是叫信徒怀疑每一位忠心传道的仆人。圣经也吩咐我们，要敬重那些在主里劳苦、劝戒我们的人（帖前5:12-13）。

**4. 一封代替见面的信。** 「我还有许多事要写给你们，却不愿意用纸墨写出来，但盼望到你们那里，与你们当面谈论。」（1:12）这里的「纸」，是用蒲草做的书写材料。长老知道，信不能代替见面；最深的相交，是面对面的。这封信本身也提醒我们，信仰不只是一套写在纸上的教导，也是一个彼此相爱、彼此探望的家。

## 默想

**默想：** 「恩惠、怜悯、平安从父神和他儿子耶稣基督在真理和爱心上必常与我们同在！」（1:3）过年的时候，我们彼此说了许多吉祥话：「万事如意」「身体健康」「心想事成」。说的人是真心的，可是谁也不能保证。一年下来，有人失了业，有人进了医院，有的家里吵得更厉害了。这样的话说多了，我们心里也明白，这些不过是愿望。我听神的祝福，是不是也这样听？聚会结束时，牧师举手祝福，我低着头，心里却已经在想午饭吃什么、停车场会不会堵。那几句话我听了很多年，熟得像一句客套话，从耳边滑过去了。

约翰的问安不一样。他没有说「愿」，而是说「必常与我们同在」。这不是一句客气话，因为这祝福的源头不是长老的好意，而是「父神和他儿子耶稣基督」。恩惠是不配的人蒙了爱，怜悯是可怜的人蒙了顾惜，平安是与神和好。三样都不是我争取来的，而是父在他儿子里已经赐下的：子成了肉身来了（1:7；约一4:2），并且为我们的罪作了挽回祭（约一2:2）。「怜悯」放在这里，特别叫人得安慰。也许约翰想到，收信的人中间，不一定每一个都「遵行真理」（1:4）；何况在任何一间教会里，总有软弱的人、走偏的人。这祝福不是只给走得好的人，也是给需要怜悯的人。

「在真理和爱心上」：这祝福有真理的根基，也有爱的温度。我不必猜测神今天还肯不肯恩待我，我可以回到十字架去看。在那里有真理：神没有把罪说成不是罪。在那里也有爱：神使那无罪的神儿子替我成为罪（林后5:21）。也许这一年我在神面前失败了许多次，现在还不敢抬头；可是约翰说的是「与**我们**同在」，他把自己也放在里面。这位年老的使徒，也是靠着同样的恩惠、怜悯、平安活着。

今天，我可以把这一节慢慢读三遍，每读一遍，就把一个词放在心上：恩惠、怜悯、平安；尤其是那个我觉得自己最不配得的词。然后对主说：「父啊，这不是我的愿望，而是你在你儿子里对我说的话。」这祝福靠得住，不在于我今年过得顺不顺，而在于那位「父的儿子」。他已经成了肉身来了，他所带来的恩惠、怜悯、平安，也必常与我们同在。

## What This Book Is About

Second John has only thirteen verses. It is one of the shortest books in the New Testament and would fit on a single sheet of papyrus. The writer calls himself "the elder," and he writes "to the elect lady and her children" (1:1). The letter is short, but two words keep coming back. One is "truth," which appears five times in the first four verses alone. The other is "love." The elder loves them "in truth"; he loves them "because of the truth" (1:2); and he blesses them "in truth and love" (1:3).

The letter does three things:
- **It rejoices over them:** the elder has found some of the lady's children "walking in the truth, just as we were commanded by the Father," and he "rejoiced greatly" (1:4).
- **It urges them to love one another:** this is not a new commandment, "but the one we have had from the beginning" (1:5). Love is walking according to God's commandments (1:6).
- **It warns them about deceivers:** "many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who do not confess the coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh" (1:7). If such a person comes, "do not receive him into your house or give him any greeting" (1:10).

In this letter truth and love do not pull against each other. Love without truth turns into a feeling that only wants to keep the peace. Truth without love turns into cold argument. John holds them together because both come from the same person: "grace and truth came through Jesus Christ" (John 1:17). He wants believers to hold the truth in love and to love one another in the truth. Sometimes love also has to say no: someone who loves the flock does not invite the wolf into the fold (Acts 20:29).

## Author and Setting

**1. "The elder."** The writer of 2 and 3 John does not give his name. He calls himself only "the elder" (1:1; 3 John 1:1). The word can mean an older man or a leader in the church; Peter also calls himself "a fellow elder" (1 Pet 5:1). This elder does not need to give his name for his readers to know who he is, so he must have been well known among the churches of the region. The letter's words and thought are very close to 1 John: "the commandment we have had from the beginning" (1:5; 1 John 2:7), "those who do not confess the coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh" (1:7; 1 John 4:2–3), "the antichrist" (1:7; 1 John 2:18, 22), and having "both the Father and the Son" (1:9; 1 John 2:23). Most scholars think the three letters come from the same author.

**2. Which John?** The introduction to 1 John has already covered Papias's words and the question of "John the elder." The early church accepted this letter more slowly than 1 John. Eusebius lists 2 and 3 John among the books that some disputed, saying some were unsure whether they were written by John the evangelist or by another man of the same name (*Ecclesiastical History* 3.25). But Irenaeus, at the end of the second century, already quotes this letter as the work of "John, the disciple of the Lord" (*Against Heresies* 1.16.3). As in the introduction to 1 John, this study takes the traditional view that the author is the aged apostle John. He may have called himself "the elder" precisely because he was the oldest eyewitness still living.

**3. Who is "the elect lady"?** There are two main views:
- **A church:** Most modern scholars (for example Stott, Kruse, Yarbrough, and the Catholic scholar Raymond Brown) think "the lady" is a way of personifying a local church, and "her children" are its members. Their reasons: several times the letter uses the plural "you" (1:6, 8, 10, 12); what it says is addressed to a group; "all who know the truth" love her (1:1), which fits a church better than one person; and the closing words, "The children of your elect sister greet you" (1:13), sound like one church greeting another. Scripture often pictures God's people as a mother, a wife or a bride (Isa 54:1, 13; Hos 2:19–20; Gal 4:26–27; Eph 5:25; Rev 19:7), and Peter speaks of a church as "she who is at Babylon, who is likewise chosen" (1 Pet 5:13).
- **A woman:** Others think the letter was sent to a real Christian woman whose children lived with her and in whose home a church may have met. The Greek word for "lady" (*kyria*) could be a personal name, and so could "elect" (*eklektē*). But v. 13 calls her sister *eklektē* too, and two sisters both named Electa seems unlikely, so those who hold this view usually take *Kyria* as the name. A fragment that survives in Latin and is attributed to Clement of Alexandria says the letter was written to a woman named Electa, and adds that the name points to the election of the holy church.

This study takes the first view, but neither view changes how we understand the letter. Whether "the lady" is a church or a sister who hosted a church in her home, "receive him into your house" (1:10) very likely has to do with the church meeting in homes.

**4. Date and place.** The letter was probably written at about the same time as 1 John (around AD 85–95), from Ephesus to a church nearby. Those who "went out from us" (1 John 2:19) may by then have been traveling about, bringing their teaching to other churches. So the elder writes this short letter to put one church on its guard.

**5. Traveling preachers and house churches.** First-century churches had no buildings of their own; believers met in homes (Rom 16:5; Phlm 1:2). Preachers traveled from city to city. Inns had a poor reputation, so these preachers usually stayed in believers' homes, were provided for, and were sent on their way (3 John 1:5–8). This was one of the main roads by which the gospel spread, but it also gave false teachers their opening. A church manual probably written around the end of the first century or the beginning of the second, the *Didache*, tells believers how to test visiting preachers: if what he teaches differs from the faith they received, do not listen to him; if he stays a third day, or asks for money, he is a false prophet (chapter 11). The warning of 2 John was written against this background. Receiving a preacher into the house meant more than giving him a meal. It meant providing for him and helping him on to the next place, and very likely also letting him preach in the gathering.

## Key Verses: 1:3; 1:9

**1. "Grace, mercy, and peace will be with us."** "Grace, mercy, and peace will be with us, from God the Father and from Jesus Christ the Father's Son, in truth and love." (1:3) The greetings at the start of Paul's letters usually have no verb in the Greek. Here John uses a future verb: grace, mercy, and peace *will be* with us. It is a promise, not only a wish. In John's Gospel and letters the word "mercy" appears only here. And the title "the Father's Son" appears nowhere else in the New Testament. All these blessings come "in truth and love": God's grace stands on truth and is not empty talk, and God's truth comes with love and is not cold.

**2. "Has both the Father and the Son."** "Everyone who goes on ahead and does not abide in the teaching of Christ, does not have God. Whoever abides in the teaching has both the Father and the Son." (1:9) The deceivers thought they were out in front, a step beyond the apostles' teaching. John says that leaving the teaching of Christ does not take you further; it costs you God. Real progress is not going beyond Christ but abiding in him more deeply. Those who abide in this teaching gain more than a set of correct doctrines. They have the Father and the Son themselves (see 1 John 2:23; 5:12).

## Structure of the Book

Second John can be divided this way:
- **Greeting** (vv. 1–3): the elder writes to the elect lady and her children; they love one another because of the truth; grace, mercy, and peace will be with us.
- **Joy and appeal** (vv. 4–6): some of the children are walking in the truth, and the elder rejoices greatly; love one another, the commandment we have had from the beginning; this is love, that we walk according to his commandments.
- **Warning** (vv. 7–11): many deceivers have gone out, who do not confess the coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh; watch yourselves, so that you do not lose what was worked for; whoever goes on ahead of the teaching of Christ does not have God; do not receive him into your house.
- **Closing** (vv. 12–13): the hope of talking face to face, so that joy may be complete; greetings from the children of the elect sister.

## How to Read This Book

**1. Read it alongside 1 John.** First John reads like a long letter for many churches; 2 John reads like a note to one church. They say the same things. For each verse of 2 John you can turn to related verses in 1 John: "the one we have had from the beginning" (1:5; 1 John 2:7–8); "in the flesh" (1:7; 1 John 4:2); "has both the Father and the Son" (1:9; 1 John 2:23). What 1 John teaches becomes, in 2 John, a concrete decision one church has to make.

**2. Notice what "truth" means.** When John speaks of "the truth," he does not mean only a set of correct statements. He means the reality God has revealed in Jesus Christ. Jesus said, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" (John 14:6). That is why the truth can "abide in us and will be with us forever" (1:2), much as Jesus promised "the Spirit of truth" would be "with you forever" (John 14:16–17). People can "walk in the truth" (1:4), because the truth is not only to be believed but also to be lived.

**3. Read verses 10–11 carefully, neither stretching them nor shrinking them.** John is not telling believers to avoid unbelievers or to be cold and rude to neighbors of other religions. Jesus commands us to love our enemies (Matt 5:44), and Paul says, "as we have opportunity, let us do good to everyone" (Gal 6:10). John is speaking of traveling teachers who spread teaching that denies Christ: do not let them preach in the house church, do not provide for them, do not send them on their way. And the error he names is not a side issue. It is the foundation of the gospel: whether Jesus Christ came in the flesh. Using these verses against secondary differences in doctrine causes needless division; ignoring them hands the pulpit to people who deny Christ. One more thing: the letter warns against roaming deceivers, not against every faithful servant who preaches. Scripture also tells us to respect those who labor among us in the Lord and admonish us (1 Thess 5:12–13).

**4. A letter in place of a visit.** "Though I have much to write to you, I would rather not use paper and ink. Instead I hope to come to you and talk face to face." (1:12) The "paper" here is papyrus. The elder knows a letter cannot replace a visit; the deepest fellowship is face to face. The letter itself reminds us that the faith is not only teaching written on paper, but a family that loves and visits one another.

## Meditation

**Meditation:** "Grace, mercy, and peace will be with us, from God the Father and from Jesus Christ the Father's Son, in truth and love." (1:3) At Chinese New Year we trade a great many good-luck greetings: "May all go as you wish," "Good health," "May your heart's desires come true." People mean them, but no one can guarantee them. By the end of the year some have lost jobs, some have been in the hospital, and some homes have more fighting than before. After enough of these greetings we know they are only wishes. Do I hear God's blessing the same way? When the pastor raises his hands to bless us at the end of the service, I bow my head, but I am already thinking about lunch and whether the car park will be jammed. I have heard those words for years. They are as familiar as a polite formula, and they slide right past me.

John's greeting is different. He does not say "may." He says grace, mercy, and peace "will be with us." This is not mere politeness, because the blessing does not come from the elder's goodwill. It comes "from God the Father and from Jesus Christ the Father's Son." Grace is love shown to the undeserving. Mercy is kindness shown to the wretched. Peace is being reconciled to God. None of the three is something I win. The Father has already given them in his Son: the Son has come in the flesh (1:7; 1 John 4:2), and he is the propitiation for our sins (1 John 2:2). "Mercy" is a great comfort here. Perhaps John had in mind that not every reader was "walking in the truth" (1:4); and in any church there are some who are weak and some who have wandered. This blessing is not only for those who are doing well. It is also for those who need mercy.

"In truth and love": this blessing stands on truth, and it carries the warmth of love. I do not have to guess whether God will still be kind to me today. I can go back and look at the cross. There is truth there: God did not call sin something other than sin. There is love there too: God made his sinless Son to be sin for me (2 Cor 5:21). Perhaps this year I have failed before God many times, and even now I hardly dare look up. But John says grace "will be with *us*," and he includes himself. The aged apostle also lived on this same grace, mercy, and peace.

Today I can read this verse slowly three times, and each time hold one word in my heart: grace, mercy, peace, and above all the word I feel least worthy of. Then I can say to the Lord, "Father, this is not my wish. It is what you have said to me in your Son." This blessing holds, not because my year has gone well, but because of "the Father's Son." He has already come in the flesh, and the grace, mercy, and peace he brought will be with us.
