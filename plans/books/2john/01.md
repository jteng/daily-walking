## 本章脉络

约翰二书只有一章，十三节，可以分成四段。第1-3节是问安：长老写信给「蒙拣选的太太和她的儿女」，他们因真理彼此相爱，恩惠、怜悯、平安必与我们同在。第4-6节是欢喜和劝勉：长老见到有些儿女遵行真理，就甚欢喜，又劝他们彼此相爱，因为照神的命令行就是爱。第7-11节是警告：许多迷惑人的出来了，他们不认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的；信徒要小心，要常守基督的教训，也不可接待传别样教训的人。第12-13节是结语：长老盼望当面谈论，使喜乐满足。

全信的思路很清楚：先是真理和爱把信徒连在一起（1-3节）；然后是活出这真理和爱（4-6节）；最后是保护这真理和爱，不让迷惑人的破坏（7-11节）。

## 深读 1:1-3 因真理的缘故

**1. 「我诚心所爱的」。** 「作长老的写信给蒙拣选的太太和她的儿女，就是我诚心所爱的；不但我爱，也是一切知道真理之人所爱的。」（1:1）和合本译作「诚心」的地方，原文是在真理中。这可以只是说「真心地」爱，也可能还有更深的意思：他们的爱，是在真理里面、靠着真理而有的。下一节说得更清楚：「爱你们是为真理的缘故。」（1:2）把这些信徒连在一起的，不是同乡、同学或同一个社会阶层，而是同一个真理。所以「一切知道真理之人」，虽然多半没有见过这间教会，也都爱她。

**2. 「这真理存在我们里面，也必永远与我们同在」。** 「爱你们是为真理的缘故，这真理存在我们里面，也必永远与我们同在。」（1:2）这里说的真理，不只是我们脑子里的一些知识，而是「存在」我们里面的，原文就是约翰常用的「住在」（*menō*）。耶稣也这样应许那「真理的圣灵」：「叫他永远与你们同在……因他常与你们同在，也要在你们里面。」（约14:16-17）真理不是我们紧紧抓住、一松手就会失去的东西；是神的真理住在我们里面，并且「必永远」与我们同在。

**3. 「恩惠、怜悯、平安」。** 「恩惠、怜悯、平安从父神和他儿子耶稣基督在真理和爱心上必常与我们同在！」（1:3）导读已经讲过，这里的动词是将来式，是应许，不只是愿望。这里还有三个细节：
- **「怜悯」：** 提摩太前书和后书的问安也有「恩惠、怜悯、平安」（提前1:2；提后1:2）。约翰福音和约翰书信却只在这里用「怜悯」这个词。也许约翰想到这间教会的处境：有些儿女遵行真理（1:4），可能也有些没有。神的怜悯，是给软弱和失败的人的。
- **「父神和他儿子耶稣基督」：** 恩惠同样从父和子而来，子和父同列，可见子与父同等。「从父神」「从……耶稣基督」原文两次用「从」字：父与子是有分别的两个位格，却同是这祝福的源头。约翰特别称他为「父的儿子」（和合本译作「他儿子」），很可能是要对付那些否认子的人：「凡不认子的，就没有父。」（约一2:23）
- **「必常与我们同在」：** 约翰说的是「我们」，不是「你们」。长老自己也需要这恩惠、怜悯、平安。

> 要点：把信徒连在一起的是真理，而这真理不是我们抓住的，是神的真理住在我们里面，并且必永远与我们同在。

## 深读 1:4-6 照他的命令行，这就是爱

**1. 「有照……遵行真理的，就甚欢喜」。** 「我见你的儿女，有照我们从父所受之命令遵行真理的，就甚欢喜。」（1:4）这句话有两种读法：
- **有些人遵行，有些人没有：** 有释经者认为，「有」暗示不是全部，或许有些儿女已经被迷惑人的吸引去了。若是这样，长老先为遵行真理的人欢喜，再提出警告。
- **只是长老见到的那些人：** 也有人认为，长老只是说他所遇见的那些人（也许是在别处遇见的会众）都遵行真理，并没有说其余的人怎样。

无论哪一种读法，长老都先说他的欢喜，然后才劝勉和警告。这是牧者的心：他留意神在人身上已经做成的工作。

**2. 「遵行真理」。** 原文的意思是在真理中行。真理不只是要人知道，也是要人在其中行走，就像在一条路上走。耶稣说：「我就是道路、真理、生命。」（约14:6）在真理中行，就是跟随那位是真理的主。这也是「从父所受之命令」：真理不是信徒自己发明的，是父吩咐的。

**3. 「不是……新命令」。** 「太太啊，我现在劝你，我们大家要彼此相爱。这并不是我写一条新命令给你，乃是我们从起初所受的命令。」（1:5）约翰一书也这样说过（约一2:7-8）。这命令说新也新，说旧也旧：
- **旧：** 信徒「从起初」，也就是从他们刚听见福音的时候，就领受了这命令。耶稣在最后的晚餐上说：「我赐给你们一条新命令，乃是叫你们彼此相爱；我怎样爱你们，你们也要怎样相爱。」（约13:34）
- **新：** 它在耶稣身上有了新的标准：「我怎样爱你们」。而且「黑暗渐渐过去，真光已经照耀」（约一2:8），在新的时代，这命令在信徒身上真实地成就了。
那些迷惑人的人，大概喜欢讲新的东西。长老却不给读者新的东西，只领他们回到起初。

**4. 爱和命令，彼此说明。** 「我们若照他的命令行，这就是爱。你们从起初所听见当行的，就是这命令。」（1:6）这一节像是一个圆圈：爱就是照他的命令行，而他的命令就是要我们彼此相爱。耶稣也把这两样连在一起：「你们若爱我，就必遵守我的命令。」（约14:15）这里有两面：
- **爱不只是感觉：** 爱有具体的样子，就是顺服神的命令。说爱却不顺服，那爱是空的。
- **顺服不只是规条：** 神的命令最终是爱，爱神，也爱弟兄（约一3:23）。守了许多规矩却不爱人，那顺服也是空的。

> 要点：神的命令不是新的，也不复杂，就是照基督怎样爱我们，我们也怎样彼此相爱；我们能这样行，是因为他先这样爱了我们。

## 深读 1:7-9 不认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的

**1. 「许多迷惑人的出来」。** 「因为世上有许多迷惑人的出来，他们不认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的；这就是那迷惑人、敌基督的。」（1:7）「出来」和约翰一书说的「他们从我们中间出去」（约一2:19）、「世上有许多假先知已经出来了」（约一4:1）用的是同一个词。这些人很可能曾经在教会中间，后来离开，现在到各处传讲他们的教导。约翰称他们为「迷惑人的」，因为他们不只是自己错了，也把别人带离真理。

**2. 「成了肉身来的」。** 约翰一书4:2说「耶稣基督是成了肉身来的」，用的是完成式（已经来了）；这里用的却是现在式的分词（原文直译是在肉身中来）。这有几种读法：
- **指道成肉身：** 多数释经者认为，意思和约翰一书4:2一样，是指耶稣在历史上成了肉身。现在式只是把这件事当作耶稣身份的一部分来说：他是那在肉身中来的一位。
- **指基督再来：** 少数人认为，这是指基督将来要在肉身中再来。
- **两者都包括：** 也有人认为，约翰强调的是耶稣基督的身份：他是那在肉身中来的一位，过去如此，将来也如此。
无论怎样读，问题的核心都是：那位真实的、有血有肉的人耶稣，就是基督，就是神的儿子。否认这一点的人，不管说得多么属灵，都是「敌基督的」。救恩就靠这一点：神的儿子若没有真的成了肉身，就没有真的为我们流血（约一1:7；5:6）。

**3. 「不要失去你们所做的工」。** 「你们要小心，不要失去你们所做的工，乃要得着满足的赏赐。」（1:8）这里有一处抄本的差异：和合本照一些抄本译作「你们所做的工」；英文标准版照另一些抄本译作我们（为你们）所做的工，指使徒和传道人在他们身上的劳苦。还有一些抄本三个动词都作「我们」（英王钦定本）。几种读法都通。「满足的赏赐」指什么，也有不同的看法：
- **指永生的完满：** 有人认为，这里的「赏赐」就是永生本身。约翰的意思是：不要因跟从迷惑人的，到头来一无所得。这样读，就牵涉到信徒能不能失去救恩的问题：改革宗传统认为，这样的警告正是神保守真信徒坚忍到底所用的方法，那些离开的人显明他们本不属我们（约一2:19）；阿民念派和卫斯理宗则认为，真信徒确有离弃信仰的可能。
- **指赏赐的多少：** 也有人认为，这里说的是信徒在基督台前所得的赏赐（参林前3:14-15）。信徒的工作若被假教导破坏，就会受亏损。
无论哪一种，这赏赐都不是信徒自己赚来的工价。赏赐出于神的恩典；我们得救，是因为基督为我们作了挽回祭（约一2:2）。约翰说「要小心」，是叫我们不要离开那恩典。

**4. 「越过」和「常守」。** 「凡越过基督的教训不常守着的，就没有神；常守这教训的，就有父又有子。」（1:9）和合本的「越过」，跟随的是后期抄本的读法；较早的抄本作走在前面（*proagōn*）。迷惑人的人可能自称是「进步的」，说他们的教导比使徒的更高、更深。约翰的回答很尖锐：越过了基督，不是更进一步，而是离开了神。
- **「基督的教训」：** 可以指关于基督的教训（就是基督成了肉身来的真理），也可以指基督自己所传的教训。不少释经者认为从上下文看前一种比较合适，但两者其实分不开：基督所传的，就是他自己。
- **「就有父又有子」：** 信徒常守在这教训里，所得的不只是一套正确的教义，而是父和子自己。这和约翰一书的话一致：「认子的，连父也有了。」（约一2:23）

> 要点：真正的长进不是越过基督，而是更深地常守在他里面；那位成了肉身来的子，把父也带给了我们。

## 深读 1:10-11 不要接他到家里

**1. 「不要接他到家里」。** 「若有人到你们那里，不是传这教训，不要接他到家里，也不要问他的安。」（1:10）这里的「你们」是复数，「家」很可能是指教会聚会的家。导读讲过，当时的传道人从一处走到另一处，靠信徒接待和供给。接一个传道人到家里，就是供给他食宿，再帮他到下一个地方去，很可能也包括让他在聚会中讲道。约翰说的，是这些迷惑人的人「到你们那里」来，是带着他们的教导来的。

**2. 「也不要问他的安」。** 这里的「问安」，原文是当时见面或写信时常用的问候语（*chairein*，意思是愿你喜乐）。这里说的，不是在街上碰见一个人连招呼都不打，而是不要公开地欢迎他、认可他。约翰的理由是：「因为问他安的，就在他的恶行上有分。」（1:11）「有分」原文（*koinōneō*）和「相交」是同一个字根。约翰一书一开始就讲信徒与父和子相交（约一1:3）；这里却警告说，接待传假教导的人，就和他的恶行相交了。

**3. 早期教会怎样看这件事。** 爱任纽记载，坡旅甲有一次（据说是在罗马）碰见异端首领马吉安。马吉安问他：「你认识我吗？」坡旅甲回答说：「我认识，你是撒但的长子。」爱任纽接着说，使徒和他们的门徒连一句话也不肯和败坏真理的人交谈，并引用保罗的话（多3:10-11；《驳异端》第三卷第三章第四节）。在另一处，爱任纽直接引用了约翰二书第11节（《驳异端》第一卷第十六章第三节）。这个故事让我们看见，早期教会把否认基督的教导看得非常严重。不过，坡旅甲这样回答马吉安，是因为马吉安是一个教派的首领，正在把人带离福音。

**4. 这两节不是什么意思。** 读这两节，要留意它的范围：
- **不是叫我们不和不信的人来往：** 耶稣和税吏、罪人一同吃饭，也吩咐我们爱仇敌（太5:44）。我们可以邀请不信主的邻舍、亲戚到家里，向他们传福音。
- **不是叫我们在次要的问题上分门别类：** 约翰说的，是否认耶稣基督成了肉身来的人，是福音根基的问题，不是洗礼的方式、末世的次序或敬拜的风格。
- **不是叫我们怀疑每一位传道人：** 约翰在另一封信里，正称赞那些接待忠心传道人的信徒（约三1:5-8）。忠心的牧者和传道人，是我们应当敬重和供给的（帖前5:12-13；彼前5:2-3）。
- **也不是叫我们用恶言对待人：** 保罗说，主的仆人要「用温柔劝戒那抵挡的人；或者神给他们悔改的心，可以明白真道」（提后2:25）。拒绝给假教导讲台，和为传假教导的人祷告，可以同时进行。

> 要点：爱羊群，就不能把讲台交给否认基督的人；但守住门的人，自己也要记得，我们能站在真理里，全是因为那位成了肉身来的主怜悯了我们。

## 深读 1:12-13 当面谈论

**1. 「不愿意用纸墨写出来」。** 「我还有许多事要写给你们，却不愿意用纸墨写出来，但盼望到你们那里，与你们当面谈论，使你们的喜乐满足。」（1:12）「纸」是用蒲草做的书写材料，「墨」是用炭灰和胶调成的。长老有许多话要说，但他宁愿当面说。「当面」原文直译是「口对口」。神也曾这样说到摩西：「我要与他面对面说话。」（民12:8）最深的相交，是面对面的。

**2. 「使你们的喜乐满足」。** 这里和约翰一书1:4一样，有抄本的差异：有的抄本作「你们的喜乐」（和合本），有的作「我们的喜乐」。无论哪一种，喜乐都是在彼此相交中满足的。长老的喜乐，和这间教会的喜乐，是连在一起的。

**3. 「你那蒙拣选之姊妹的儿女都问你安」。** 「你那蒙拣选之姊妹的儿女都问你安。」（1:13）若「太太」指一间教会，这「姊妹」就是另一间教会，也许是长老所在的以弗所教会。一间教会向另一间教会问安，提醒我们，地方教会不是孤立的，而是同一个家中的姊妹。她们都是「蒙拣选的」，都是因神的恩典成为他的子民。

> 要点：真理和爱最终要带我们到面对面的相交里；有一天，我们也要面对面地见那位成了肉身来的主。

## 默想

**默想：** 「凡越过基督的教训不常守着的，就没有神；常守这教训的，就有父又有子。」（1:9）也许我有一个很亲近的人，一个表姐、一位老同学，或是从前一起聚会的朋友，后来加入了一个团体。他们说，耶稣只是一位开悟的老师，和孔子、佛陀差不多；或者说，他们的领袖才是基督再来。他很热心，常常发资料给我，也想在我家里办一个聚会，请他们的老师来讲。我心里很为难。他是我很爱的人，拒绝他，好像就是拒绝了这段感情；何况他们说的那一套，听起来更开阔、更新鲜，好像比我所信的更进一步。

约翰二书第10节说：「不要接他到家里。」这一节的根据在第7节：那些人「不认耶稣基督是成了肉身来的」。约翰在意的，不是谁的说法更开阔，而是那一位真实的人耶稣。我所信的，不是一套可以跟别的学说比高低的道理；我所信的，是那位道成了肉身、为我流血的主（约1:14；约一1:7）。离开他去追求更进一步，并没有走得更远，而是离开了神。

耶稣在世的时候，也有许多人这样离开他。「从此，他门徒中多有退去的，不再和他同行。」耶稣问那十二个门徒：「你们也要去吗？」彼得回答说：「主啊，你有永生之道，我们还归从谁呢？我们已经信了，又知道你是神的圣者。」（约6:66-69）彼得这样说，不是因为他比别人聪明或坚强；过不多久，他就三次不认主。他留下来，是因为他知道，除了这一位，再没有别处可以得着永生之道。我也是这样。我能常守在基督的教训里，不是因为我比那位亲人更有见识；我能站在这里，全是蒙了怜悯（1:3）。

所以，我可以温柔而清楚地对他说：我爱你，所以我不能在我家里为一个否认耶稣是神儿子的教导开聚会。我也可以继续请他吃饭，在他生病时去看他，为他祷告。如果我已经在这件事上说了伤人的话，或者为了怕伤感情，做了不该做的决定，我可以回到主面前认罪，也去向那个人道歉，为我说话的方式，不是为我所信的真理。然后我可以带着盼望读第9节：「常守这教训的，就有父又有子。」那位成了肉身来的主，至今还在呼召人；那位亲人若有一天也说「我们还归从谁呢」，他就会和我一样，有父又有子。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Second John has one chapter of thirteen verses, and it falls into four parts. Verses 1–3 are the greeting: the elder writes "to the elect lady and her children," who love one another because of the truth; grace, mercy, and peace will be with us. Verses 4–6 bring joy and an appeal: the elder rejoices to find some of the children walking in the truth, and he urges them to love one another, because love is walking according to God's commandments. Verses 7–11 give a warning: many deceivers have gone out who do not confess the coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh; believers must watch themselves, abide in the teaching of Christ, and not receive those who bring another teaching. Verses 12–13 close the letter: the elder hopes to talk face to face, so that joy may be complete.

The line of thought is clear. First, truth and love bind believers together (vv. 1–3). Then they live out this truth and love (vv. 4–6). Finally, they guard this truth and love so that deceivers cannot destroy it (vv. 7–11).

## Going Deeper: 1:1–3 Because of the Truth

**1. "Whom I love in truth."** "The elder to the elect lady and her children, whom I love in truth, and not only I, but also all who know the truth." (1:1) "In truth" can simply mean "sincerely," as the Chinese Union Version takes it. It may also mean more: their love exists in the truth and because of it. The next verse says so plainly: "because of the truth" (1:2). What binds these believers together is not a shared hometown, school or social class, but one truth. That is why "all who know the truth," though most of them have probably never seen this church, love her.

**2. "The truth that abides in us and will be with us forever."** "Because of the truth that abides in us and will be with us forever." (1:2) The truth here is not only some knowledge in our heads. It "abides" in us, using John's favorite word, *menō*. Jesus made the same promise about "the Spirit of truth": he will be "with you forever... he dwells with you and will be in you" (John 14:16–17). The truth is not something we must clutch, which we lose the moment we let go. God's truth dwells in us, and it "will be with us forever."

**3. "Grace, mercy, and peace."** "Grace, mercy, and peace will be with us, from God the Father and from Jesus Christ the Father's Son, in truth and love." (1:3) As the introduction noted, the verb is future: it is a promise, not only a wish. Three more details:
- **"Mercy":** The greetings of 1 and 2 Timothy also have "grace, mercy, and peace" (1 Tim 1:2; 2 Tim 1:2). But John's Gospel and letters use the word "mercy" only here. Perhaps John has this church's situation in mind: some of the children are walking in the truth (1:4), and perhaps some are not. God's mercy is for the weak and for those who have failed.
- **"From God the Father and from Jesus Christ the Father's Son":** Grace comes equally from the Father and the Son. The Son stands beside the Father, which shows that the Son is equal with the Father. The Greek repeats "from": Father and Son are distinct Persons, yet together the one source of this blessing. John calls him "the Father's Son" in particular, probably with an eye on those who deny the Son: "No one who denies the Son has the Father" (1 John 2:23).
- **"Will be with us":** John says "us," not "you." The elder himself needs this grace, mercy, and peace.

> The point: What binds believers together is the truth, and this truth is not something we hold on to; it is God's truth that dwells in us and will be with us forever.

## Going Deeper: 1:4–6 This Is Love, That We Walk According to His Commandments

**1. "I rejoiced greatly to find some of your children walking in the truth."** (1:4) This can be read two ways:
- **Some walk in the truth, and some do not:** Some interpreters think "some" implies not all; perhaps some of the children had already been drawn away by the deceivers. If so, the elder rejoices first over those walking in the truth, and then gives his warning.
- **Only those the elder met:** Others think the elder is simply saying that the members he happened to meet (perhaps elsewhere) were walking in the truth, without saying anything about the rest.

Either way, the elder speaks of his joy before he gives his appeal and warning. That is a shepherd's heart: he notices the work God has already done in people.

**2. "Walking in the truth."** The truth is not only to be known; it is a path to walk on. Jesus said, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" (John 14:6). Walking in the truth means following the Lord who is the truth. And this is "just as we were commanded by the Father": believers did not invent the truth; the Father commanded it.

**3. "Not as though I were writing you a new commandment."** "And now I ask you, dear lady—not as though I were writing you a new commandment, but the one we have had from the beginning—that we love one another." (1:5) First John says the same (1 John 2:7–8). This commandment is both old and new:
- **Old:** Believers received it "from the beginning," from the time they first heard the gospel. At the Last Supper Jesus said, "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another" (John 13:34).
- **New:** In Jesus it has a new standard: "just as I have loved you." And because "the darkness is passing away and the true light is already shining" (1 John 2:8), in the new age this commandment is truly fulfilled in believers.
The deceivers probably liked to talk about new things. The elder gives his readers nothing new. He takes them back to the beginning.

**4. Love and commandment explain each other.** "And this is love, that we walk according to his commandments; this is the commandment, just as you have heard from the beginning, so that you should walk in it." (1:6) The verse runs in a circle: love is walking according to his commandments, and his commandment is that we love one another. Jesus also tied the two together: "If you love me, you will keep my commandments" (John 14:15). There are two sides here:
- **Love is not only a feeling:** Love has a concrete shape, which is obeying God's commandments. Love that does not obey is empty.
- **Obedience is not only rules:** In the end God's commandment is love, love for God and love for the brothers (1 John 3:23). Keeping many rules without loving people is empty obedience.

> The point: God's commandment is neither new nor complicated: just as Christ has loved us, we are to love one another; and we can do so because he loved us first.

## Going Deeper: 1:7–9 Not Confessing the Coming of Jesus Christ in the Flesh

**1. "Many deceivers have gone out into the world."** "For many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who do not confess the coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh. Such a one is the deceiver and the antichrist." (1:7) "Gone out" is the same word as in "they went out from us" (1 John 2:19) and "many false prophets have gone out into the world" (1 John 4:1). These people had probably once been inside the church; then they left, and now they travel about spreading their teaching. John calls them "deceivers" because they are not only wrong themselves; they lead others away from the truth.

**2. "The coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh."** First John 4:2 says "Jesus Christ has come in the flesh," using a perfect tense (he has come). Here John uses a present participle (literally "coming in the flesh"). There are several readings:
- **The incarnation:** Most interpreters think the meaning is the same as 1 John 4:2, the historical incarnation of Jesus. The present tense simply speaks of it as part of who Jesus is: he is the one "coming in the flesh."
- **The second coming:** A few think it refers to Christ's future coming again in the flesh.
- **Both:** Others think John stresses Jesus Christ's identity: he is the one who comes in the flesh, in the past and in the future.
However it is read, the heart of the matter is this: the real Jesus, a man of flesh and blood, is the Christ, the Son of God. Anyone who denies this, however spiritual he sounds, is "the antichrist." Salvation depends on it: if the Son of God did not truly become flesh, he did not truly shed his blood for us (1 John 1:7; 5:6).

**3. "So that you may not lose what we have worked for."** "Watch yourselves, so that you may not lose what we have worked for, but may win a full reward." (1:8) The manuscripts differ here. The Chinese Union Version follows those that read "what *you* have worked for." The ESV follows others that read "what *we* have worked for," meaning the labor of the apostles and preachers among them. And some read "we" throughout (KJV). Each reading makes sense. What "a full reward" means is also debated:
- **The fullness of eternal life:** Some think the "reward" here is eternal life itself. John means: do not follow the deceivers and end up with nothing. Read this way, the verse raises whether a believer can lose salvation. The Reformed tradition reads such warnings as the means by which God keeps true believers to the end; those who leave show they were never "of us" (1 John 2:19). Arminian and Wesleyan traditions hold that a true believer can fall away.
- **Degrees of reward:** Others think John is speaking of the reward believers receive before the judgment seat of Christ (see 1 Cor 3:14–15). If false teaching wrecks believers' work, they suffer loss.
Either way, this reward is not wages believers earn. Reward comes from God's grace. We are saved because Christ is the propitiation for our sins (1 John 2:2). John says "watch yourselves" so that we do not leave that grace.

**4. "Goes on ahead" and "abides."** "Everyone who goes on ahead and does not abide in the teaching of Christ, does not have God. Whoever abides in the teaching has both the Father and the Son." (1:9) The earlier manuscripts read "goes on ahead" (*proagōn*); the Chinese Union Version's "transgresses" follows later manuscripts, as does the KJV. The deceivers may have called themselves "progressive," claiming their teaching was higher and deeper than the apostles'. John's answer is sharp: going beyond Christ is not a step forward; it is leaving God.
- **"The teaching of Christ":** This can mean the teaching about Christ (the truth that he came in the flesh) or the teaching Christ himself gave. Many think the context favors the first, but the two cannot really be separated: what Christ taught was himself.
- **"Has both the Father and the Son":** Those who abide in this teaching gain more than a set of correct doctrines. They have the Father and the Son themselves. This fits 1 John: "Whoever confesses the Son has the Father also" (1 John 2:23).

> The point: Real progress is not going beyond Christ but abiding in him more deeply; the Son who came in the flesh brings us the Father as well.

## Going Deeper: 1:10–11 Do Not Receive Him into Your House

**1. "Do not receive him into your house."** "If anyone comes to you and does not bring this teaching, do not receive him into your house or give him any greeting." (1:10) The "you" is plural, and "house" very likely means the home where the church met. As the introduction explained, preachers traveled from place to place and relied on believers to receive and provide for them. Receiving a preacher into the house meant giving him food and lodging and helping him on to the next place, and very likely also letting him preach in the gathering. John is speaking of deceivers who "come to you" bringing their teaching.

**2. "Or give him any greeting."** The "greeting" here is the word commonly used when people met or wrote letters (*chairein*, "rejoice," "greetings"). John does not mean refusing even to say hello to someone on the street. He means not publicly welcoming and endorsing such a person. His reason: "for whoever greets him takes part in his wicked works" (1:11). "Takes part" (*koinōneō*) is the verb related to "fellowship." First John opens with believers' fellowship with the Father and the Son (1 John 1:3); here John warns that receiving a false teacher means sharing in his wicked works.

**3. How the early church saw it.** Irenaeus records that Polycarp once met the heretic Marcion, apparently in Rome. Marcion asked, "Do you recognize me?" Polycarp answered, "I recognize you, the firstborn of Satan." Irenaeus goes on to say that the apostles and their disciples would not even exchange a word with those who corrupted the truth, and quotes Paul (Titus 3:10–11; *Against Heresies* 3.3.4). Elsewhere he quotes 2 John 11 directly (*Against Heresies* 1.16.3). The story shows how seriously the early church took teaching that denied Christ. But Polycarp spoke this way to Marcion because Marcion was the leader of a sect who was drawing people away from the gospel.

**4. What these verses do not mean.** Notice how far these verses reach:
- **They do not forbid contact with unbelievers:** Jesus ate with tax collectors and sinners, and he commands us to love our enemies (Matt 5:44). We can invite unbelieving neighbors and relatives into our homes and share the gospel with them.
- **They do not license division over secondary matters:** John is speaking about those who deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh. That is the foundation of the gospel, not the mode of baptism, the order of the last things, or styles of worship.
- **They do not make us suspicious of every preacher:** In another letter John praises believers who receive faithful preachers (3 John 1:5–8). Faithful pastors and preachers are to be respected and supported (1 Thess 5:12–13; 1 Pet 5:2–3).
- **They do not permit harsh words:** Paul says the Lord's servant should be "correcting his opponents with gentleness. God may perhaps grant them repentance leading to a knowledge of the truth" (2 Tim 2:25). Refusing false teaching a pulpit and praying for the one who teaches it can go together.

> The point: Loving the flock means not handing the pulpit to those who deny Christ; but those who guard the door must remember that we stand in the truth only because the Lord who came in the flesh has had mercy on us.

## Going Deeper: 1:12–13 Face to Face

**1. "I would rather not use paper and ink."** "Though I have much to write to you, I would rather not use paper and ink. Instead I hope to come to you and talk face to face, so that our joy may be complete." (1:12) The "paper" was papyrus, and the "ink" was made of soot and gum. The elder has much to say, but he would rather say it in person. "Face to face" is literally "mouth to mouth." God spoke this way of Moses: "With him I speak mouth to mouth" (Num 12:8). The deepest fellowship is face to face.

**2. "So that our joy may be complete."** As in 1 John 1:4, the manuscripts differ here. Some read "your joy" (as in the Chinese Union Version), and some read "our joy" (as in the ESV). Either way, joy is made complete in fellowship together. The elder's joy and this church's joy are bound up with each other.

**3. "The children of your elect sister greet you."** (1:13) If "the lady" is a church, this "sister" is another church, perhaps the church at Ephesus where the elder was. One church greeting another reminds us that a local church is not on its own; churches are sisters in one family. Both are "elect": both became God's people by his grace.

> The point: Truth and love lead us at last into face-to-face fellowship, and one day we will see face to face the Lord who came in the flesh.

## Meditation

**Meditation:** "Everyone who goes on ahead and does not abide in the teaching of Christ, does not have God. Whoever abides in the teaching has both the Father and the Son." (1:9) Perhaps someone close to me, a cousin, an old classmate, or a friend I used to worship with, has joined a group. They say Jesus was only an enlightened teacher, much like Confucius or the Buddha; or they say their own leader is Christ come again. He is very zealous. He keeps sending me material, and he wants to hold a meeting in my home and invite their teacher to speak. I am torn. I love him, and refusing him feels like refusing our friendship. Besides, what they teach sounds broader and fresher, as if it were a step beyond what I believe.

Verse 10 says, "Do not receive him into your house." The ground for this is verse 7: these people "do not confess the coming of Jesus Christ in the flesh." John's concern is not whose ideas are broader. His concern is the real man Jesus. What I believe is not a set of ideas to be ranked against other teachings. It is the Lord who became flesh and shed his blood for me (John 1:14; 1 John 1:7). Leaving him to go "a step beyond" does not take me further. It takes me away from God.

While Jesus was on earth, many left him in just this way. "After this many of his disciples turned back and no longer walked with him." Jesus asked the Twelve, "Do you want to go away as well?" Simon Peter answered, "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life, and we have believed, and have come to know, that you are the Holy One of God." (John 6:66–69) Peter did not say this because he was wiser or stronger than the others; before long he would deny the Lord three times. He stayed because he knew there was nowhere else to find the words of eternal life. So it is with me. I abide in the teaching of Christ, not because I see further than the person I love. I stand here only because I have received mercy (1:3).

So I can say to him, gently and clearly: I love you, and that is why I cannot host a meeting in my home for a teaching that denies Jesus is the Son of God. I can still invite him for a meal, visit him when he is ill, and pray for him. If I have already said hurtful things, or, afraid of hurting the friendship, made a decision I should not have made, I can go back to the Lord and confess it, and apologize to that person, for the way I spoke, not for the truth I believe. Then I can read verse 9 with hope: "Whoever abides in the teaching has both the Father and the Son." The Lord who came in the flesh is still calling people to himself. If one day the person I love also says, "Lord, to whom shall we go?", he too will have the Father and the Son.
