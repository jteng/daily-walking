## 这卷书在讲什么

彼得后书是一封告别的信。彼得知道自己快要离世了：「因为知道我脱离这帐棚的时候快到了，正如我们主耶稣基督所指示我的。」（1:14）多年前，复活的主在提比哩亚海边对他说过：「年老的时候，你要伸出手来，别人要把你束上，带你到不愿意去的地方。」约翰接着解释：「耶稣说这话是指着彼得要怎样死，荣耀神。」（约21:18-19）现在那一天近了。一个人在离世以前最后要说的话，往往是他心里最看重的。彼得最后要说的，不是新的道理，而是提醒：「你们虽然晓得这些事，并且在你们已有的真道上坚固，我却要将这些事常常提醒你们。」（1:12）他还说：「我要尽心竭力，使你们在我去世以后时常记念这些事。」（1:15）

彼得前书面对的是从外面来的苦难：邻舍的毁谤、社会的排斥。彼得后书面对的是从里面来的危险。全书可以看作是对三种危险的回应：
- **忘记：** 信徒若不在恩典上长进，就会「眼瞎，只看见近处的，忘了他旧日的罪已经得了洁净」（1:9）。所以彼得一再说「提醒」（1:12、13、15；3:1-2）。
- **假师傅：** 「从前在百姓中有假先知起来，将来在你们中间也必有假师傅，私自引进陷害人的异端，连买他们的主他们也不承认。」（2:1）他们放纵情欲，贪财，「应许人得以自由，自己却作败坏的奴仆」（2:19）。
- **讥诮的人：** 「主要降临的应许在哪里呢？因为从列祖睡了以来，万物与起初创造的时候仍是一样。」（3:4）他们否认主的再来，也就否认将来的审判，于是活着只「随从自己的私欲」（3:3）。

彼得的回答是：回到神已经赐下的。神的神能「已将一切关乎生命和虔敬的事赐给我们」（1:3）；使徒是「亲眼见过他的威荣」的见证人（1:16）；先知的话是「人被圣灵感动，说出神的话来」（1:21）；主的应许必定成就，他现在的迟延乃是「宽容」（3:9）。全书以一句命令和一句颂赞结束：「你们却要在我们主－救主耶稣基督的恩典和知识上有长进。愿荣耀归给他，从今直到永远。阿们！」（3:18）

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 信上怎样说。** 信一开头说是「作耶稣基督仆人和使徒的西门‧彼得」写的（1:1）。作者说自己在圣山上亲耳听见天上的声音：「这是我的爱子，我所喜悦的。」（1:17-18；参太17:5）他说主已经向他指示了他的死（1:14），又说这是他写给读者的「第二封信」（3:1），并称保罗为「我们所亲爱的兄弟」（3:15）。

**2. 新约中争议最多的一卷。** 在新约各卷中，彼得后书的作者问题争议最多，这一点要诚实地说出来。
- **早期教会的迟疑：** 三世纪的俄利根说，彼得留下一封公认的书信，或许还有第二封，不过第二封有争议（见优西比乌《教会史》第六卷第二十五章）；不过俄利根自己在别处也多次把彼得后书当作彼得的书信引用；二世纪初的《彼得启示录》很可能已经用过彼得后书。四世纪初，优西比乌把彼得后书列在有争议、但为许多人所知的书卷中（《教会史》第三卷第三章、第二十五章）。耶柔米说，许多人不接受这封信，因为它的文笔和彼得前书不同（《名人传》第一章）；他自己的解释是，彼得在两封信中用了不同的帮手执笔（《书信》第120封）。
- **最后被公认：** 现存最早的彼得后书抄本是埃及的博德默蒲草纸 P72（约主后300年），其中彼得前书和彼得后书前后相连，同一册里也有犹大书和几篇正典以外的作品；可见当时彼得后书已经被当作彼得的书信抄写、诵读。主后367年，亚他那修在他的第39封节期书信中，把彼得后书列在新约正典之内；397年迦太基会议也是这样。

**3. 认为是别人借名写的看法。** 许多学者认为，这封信是彼得死后，有人用彼得的名义写的，常定在主后80-130年之间。主要的理由是：
- **文笔：** 这封信的希腊文用词华丽、句子繁复，和彼得前书很不一样。
- **犹大书：** 第2章和第3章前段有许多地方和犹大书相近（例如犹4、6-7、12、17-18），多数学者认为是彼得后书借用了犹大书。他们问：使徒为什么要借用一封较短的书信？
- **「列祖睡了」：** 讥诮的人说「从列祖睡了以来」（3:4）。有人认为「列祖」指的是第一代的基督徒，若是这样，写信的时候第一代信徒已经去世了。
- **保罗的书信：** 作者说保罗「一切的信」，又把它们和「别的经书」并列（3:16）。有人认为这表示保罗的书信已经被收集成册，那是较晚的事。
- **遗训的体裁：** 理查德·包衡认为，这封信属于犹太人的「遗训」体裁，就是借一位古人临终前的口吻，劝勉后人。他认为当时的读者都明白这是一种文学手法，不算欺骗。

**4. 认为是彼得本人写的看法。** 也有许多学者（如迈克尔·格林、施莱纳、慕道格、吉恩·格林）认为，这封信出于彼得本人：
- **早期教会的标准：** 早期教会不接受借使徒名字写的作品。约主后200年，安提阿主教撒拉皮翁说：「我们接受彼得和其他使徒，如同接受基督；但那些冒用他们名字的作品，我们弃绝。」（优西比乌《教会史》第六卷第十二章）特土良也记载，亚细亚有一位长老编写了讲述保罗事迹的《保罗行传》，承认是自己编造的以后，就被革除了职分（《论洗礼》第十七章）。早期教会虽然迟疑过，最后仍然认定这封信是使徒的作品。
- **文笔：** 耶柔米已经提出：彼得可能请了不同的人帮忙执笔（也许像彼前5:12的西拉；不过西拉也可能只是送信的人）。两封信也有相近的地方，例如都用「美德」这个词称呼神或基督的作为（1:3；彼前2:9）。彼得后书面对的是假师傅，用的语言也可能针对他们的说法。
- **犹大书：** 两封信相近的地方，也可能是彼得后书借用了犹大书，或犹大书借用了彼得后书（迈克尔·格林倾向这个看法），或两封信都用了共同的资料。使徒采用别人的话，并不奇怪：保罗也把自己「所领受的」传给别人（林前15:3），犹大书本身也用了更早的资料（犹14）。
- **「列祖」：** 新约说「列祖」，通常是指旧约的先祖（罗9:5；来1:1）。讥诮的人的意思可能是：从创世以来，万物一直是这样。
- **保罗的书信：** 保罗在世的时候，他的书信已经在各教会之间传阅（西4:16）。「一切的信」不一定指全部的保罗书信，可能只是作者所知道的那几封。
- **遗训：** 若说当时的读者都把这封信看为文学手法，很难解释早期教会为什么那样认真地问这封信是不是彼得写的。

本研读采用彼得本人所写的看法。

**5. 什么时候写的？** 教会传统说，彼得在尼禄作皇帝的时候，在罗马殉道（约主后64-68年）。他说自己「脱离这帐棚的时候快到了」（1:14），所以这封信大概写在他殉道以前不久，约主后65-68年。信上没有说是在哪里写的，许多人认为也是在罗马。

**6. 收信人是谁？** 彼得说：「我现在写给你们的是第二封信。」（3:1）许多人认为第一封就是彼得前书，若是这样，收信人就是彼前1:1所列小亚细亚各地的教会，多半是外邦信徒。也有人认为「第一封」是一封已经失传的信。彼得也提到保罗「写了信给你们」（3:15），可见这些读者也知道保罗的书信。

## 钥匙经文：1:3-4；1:19-21；3:9；3:18

**1. 「一切关乎生命和虔敬的事」。** 「神的神能已将一切关乎生命和虔敬的事赐给我们，皆因我们认识那用自己荣耀和美德召我们的主。因此，他已将又宝贵又极大的应许赐给我们，叫我们既脱离世上从情欲来的败坏，就得与神的性情有分。」（1:3-4）全书的命令，都建立在这「已将」上。信徒不是靠自己去挣得敬虔，而是神已经把敬虔所需要的一切赐下了。「与神的性情有分」不是说人变成神，乃是说人藉着神的应许，在基督里得着新的生命，越来越像神。东正教以这节经文作为「成神」教义的根据，但也说人有分于神的「能」，不是神的本质；改革宗和多数福音派则认为，这是指人与基督联合，被圣灵更新，在圣洁上像神，人仍然是受造的人。第一章会再讲。

**2. 「人被圣灵感动」。** 「我们并有先知更确的预言，如同灯照在暗处……因为预言从来没有出于人意的，乃是人被圣灵感动，说出神的话来。」（1:19、21）圣经不是人的意见，而是神藉着人说话。在黑暗的世代中，神的话是灯，「直等到天发亮，晨星在你们心里出现的时候」（1:19）。

**3. 「乃是宽容你们」。** 「主所应许的尚未成就，有人以为他是耽延，其实不是耽延，乃是宽容你们，不愿有一人沉沦，乃愿人人都悔改。」（3:9）主还没有再来，不是因为他忘记了，也不是因为他软弱，而是因为他的怜悯。他等候的，是人的悔改。

**4. 「恩典和知识」。** 「你们却要在我们主－救主耶稣基督的恩典和知识上有长进。」（3:18）这是彼得留下的最后一句命令。抵挡假教训，最好的办法不只是辨认错误，更是在认识基督上不断长进。

## 全书结构

彼得后书很短，只有三章，思路却很清楚：
- **问安**（1:1-2）：写给那「与我们同得一样宝贵信心的人」；恩惠平安「因你们认识神和我们主耶稣」多多地加给他们。
- **神的恩赐和信徒的长进**（1:3-21）：神的神能已赐下一切；所以要在信心上加上德行、知识、节制、忍耐、虔敬、爱弟兄的心、爱众人的心；使你们所蒙的恩召和拣选坚定不移；彼得临终前的提醒；使徒亲眼见过主的威荣；先知的话是被圣灵感动说出来的。
- **假师傅和他们的结局**（2:1-22）：假师傅必要起来；神没有宽容犯罪的天使、上古的世代和所多玛、蛾摩拉，却保护了挪亚，搭救了罗得；假师傅的放纵、贪婪和他们的灭亡；他们应许人自由，自己却作败坏的奴仆。
- **主的日子和最后的劝勉**（3:1-18）：讥诮的人必要来；神曾用水审判世界，也必用火审判；主看一日如千年；主的迟延是宽容；主的日子要像贼来到；盼望新天新地；保罗书信中有难明白的；要防备，要在恩典和知识上有长进。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 留意「我们的神和救主耶稣基督」。** 彼得在第一节就说，信心是「因我们的神和救主耶稣基督之义」得着的（1:1）。多数释经者认为，按希腊文的文法，「神」和「救主」是同一位，就是耶稣基督。本书另外三处「我们主－救主耶稣基督」（1:11；2:20；3:18）的希腊文结构和1:1完全一样，3:2也说「主救主」；这几处指的显然都是同一位。也有人认为这里是说父神和耶稣基督两位，因为下一节分开说「神和我们主耶稣」（1:2）。按前一种读法，这节经文和约翰福音20:28、罗马书9:5、提多书2:13一样，直接称耶稣为神。无论怎样读，全书都把基督放在神的位置上：最后一节「愿荣耀归给他」（3:18），颂赞的就是耶稣基督。

**2. 留意「认识」这个词。** 彼得后书反复说「认识」：恩惠平安「因你们认识神和我们主耶稣」加给我们（1:2）；一切关乎生命和虔敬的事，「皆因我们认识」那召我们的主（1:3）；信徒要在「认识我们的主耶稣基督上」结果子（1:8）；全书以「在……恩典和知识上有长进」结束（3:18）。假师傅也许自称有更高的知识，彼得却说，真正的知识是认识一位活的主，而这认识必定改变人的生活。

**3. 对照犹大书来读。** 彼得后书第2章和第3章前段和犹大书有许多相近的地方：假师傅偷偷进来，不认买他们的主（2:1；犹4）；犯罪的天使被拘留在黑暗里（2:4；犹6）；所多玛、蛾摩拉作为鉴戒（2:6；犹7）；亵渎在尊位的、毁谤自己所不知道的（2:10-12；犹8-10）；巴兰（2:15；犹11）；「无水的井」和「没有雨的云彩」（2:17；犹12）；末世必有好讥诮的人（3:3；犹18）。对照来读，可以看出两位作者各自强调什么。例如彼得没有引用以诺的预言，也没有提天使长米迦勒为摩西的尸首争辩的事（犹9、14-15）；他特别加上了挪亚和罗得：神不但审判恶人，也「知道搭救敬虔的人」（2:9）。

**4. 读到3:10的时候，留意抄本的不同。** 和合本说「地和其上的物都要烧尽了」。英文标准版（ESV）则译为地和其上的物都要被显露出来，因为几份最早的希腊文抄本（西乃抄本、梵蒂冈抄本和1739号抄本）这样写；「烧尽」是亚历山大抄本和多数拜占庭抄本的读法，和合本跟随的就是这个读法；另外还有几种不同的读法。这不影响这段经文的要点：主的日子必定来到，人一切所行的都要在神面前显明，所以「你们为人该当怎样圣洁，怎样敬虔」（3:11）。第三章会再讲。

**5. 不要只读出警告。** 彼得后书第2章的言语很严厉，读的时候容易只看见审判。可是全书是从神的恩赐开始的（1:3-4），也以基督的恩典结束（3:18）。彼得严厉地揭露假师傅，是因为他爱羊群，不愿他们「从自己坚固的地步上坠落」（3:17）。

## 默想

**默想：** 「作耶稣基督仆人和使徒的西门‧彼得写信给那因我们的神和救主耶稣基督之义、与我们同得一样宝贵信心的人。」（1:1）我心里常常把信徒分成等级。最上面是使徒，然后是殉道的圣徒、有名的传道人，再下来是教会里那些祷告很有力、圣经很熟的人。我自己大概排在很后面：信主多年，还是常常软弱，从来没有经历过什么特别的事。彼得是亲眼见过主「威荣」的人（1:16），在圣山上亲耳听见父神的声音（1:17-18）。他写信给一群从来没有见过耶稣的人，却说他们的信心和「我们」的信心「一样宝贵」。原文这个词的意思是价值同等，当时也用来说公民享有同样的权利。他们这信心是「得」来的，这个词常用来说按分配领受，不是自己挣来的。信心的价值不在于信的人有多少经历，而在于所信的那一位，以及这信心是「因……耶稣基督之义」赐下的。这句话还有一个细节：多数希腊文抄本在这里写的不是希腊人常用的「西门」（Simōn），而是按希伯来名字音译的「西缅」（Symeōn）；和合本两处都译作「西门」，但在希腊文里，使徒行传15:14雅各称呼彼得时也用这个写法。临终之前，彼得用的是他作加利利渔夫时的名字。那个渔夫曾经对耶稣说：「主啊，离开我，我是个罪人！」（路5:8）也曾经在院子里三次不认主（路22:54-62）。耶稣在那以前就对他说过：「西门！西门！……我已经为你祈求，叫你不至于失了信心。」（路22:31-32）彼得的信心从来不是靠他自己站立得住的。若连使徒的信心也是这样蒙恩保守的，我的信心就没有排在后面的道理。我和彼得站在同一个根基上：是基督按他的公义，毫无偏待地，把同样宝贵的信心赐给使徒，也赐给我。这背后是同一个福音：我们蒙悦纳，不是靠自己的义，乃是靠他的义（参腓3:9）。将来进入那「永远的国」的时候，使徒和我走的是同一道门，那国是「我们主－救主耶稣基督」的（1:11）。

## What This Book Is About

2 Peter is a farewell letter. Peter knows he will soon die: "I know that the putting off of my body will be soon, as our Lord Jesus Christ made clear to me" (1:14). Years before, by the Sea of Tiberias, the risen Lord had told him, "when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you and carry you where you do not want to go." John explains: "This he said to show by what kind of death he was to glorify God" (John 21:18–19). Now that day is near. What someone says last, before dying, is often what matters most to them. Peter's last words are not new teaching but a reminder: "Therefore I intend always to remind you of these qualities, though you know them and are established in the truth that you have" (1:12). And: "I will make every effort so that after my departure you may be able at any time to recall these things" (1:15).

1 Peter faces suffering from outside: neighbors' slander, social rejection. 2 Peter faces danger from inside. The whole letter can be read as an answer to three dangers:
- **Forgetting:** a believer who does not grow in grace becomes "so nearsighted that he is blind, having forgotten that he was cleansed from his former sins" (1:9). So Peter keeps "reminding" (1:12, 13, 15; 3:1–2).
- **False teachers:** "But false prophets also arose among the people, just as there will be false teachers among you, who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, even denying the Master who bought them" (2:1). They indulge their desires and love money. "They promise them freedom, but they themselves are slaves of corruption" (2:19).
- **Scoffers:** "Where is the promise of his coming? For ever since the fathers fell asleep, all things are continuing as they were from the beginning of creation" (3:4). They deny that the Lord will return, and so deny the coming judgment, and live "following their own sinful desires" (3:3).

Peter's answer is to go back to what God has already given. God's "divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness" (1:3); the apostles "were eyewitnesses of his majesty" (1:16); the prophets "spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit" (1:21); the Lord's promise will surely be kept, and his delay is patience (3:9). The letter closes with one command and one doxology: "But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. To him be the glory both now and to the day of eternity. Amen" (3:18).

## Author and Setting

**1. What the letter says.** The letter opens, "Simeon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ" (1:1). The writer says he heard the voice from heaven on the holy mountain: "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased" (1:17–18; see Matt 17:5). He says the Lord has shown him his death (1:14), calls this "the second letter that I am writing to you" (3:1), and calls Paul "our beloved brother" (3:15).

**2. The most disputed book of the New Testament.** Of all the New Testament books, 2 Peter's authorship is the most disputed, and this should be said honestly.
- **Early hesitation:** In the third century, Origen said Peter left one acknowledged letter, "perhaps also a second, for it is disputed" (reported by Eusebius, *Church History* 6.25). Yet Origen himself quotes 2 Peter as Peter's in several places, and the *Apocalypse of Peter* (early second century) probably already draws on it. Early in the fourth century, Eusebius listed 2 Peter among the books "disputed, yet known to many" (*Church History* 3.3; 3.25). Jerome said many rejected it because its style differs from 1 Peter (*Lives of Illustrious Men* 1); his own explanation was that Peter used different helpers to write his two letters (*Letter* 120).
- **Final acceptance:** The earliest surviving manuscript of 2 Peter, the Bodmer papyrus P72 (Egypt, about AD 300), copies 1 and 2 Peter one after the other, and the same codex also contains Jude alongside several non-canonical writings; by then 2 Peter was being copied and read as Peter's. In AD 367 Athanasius listed 2 Peter in the New Testament canon in his thirty-ninth Festal Letter, and so did the Council of Carthage in 397.

**3. The view that it was written in Peter's name.** Many scholars think the letter was written after Peter's death by someone using his name, often dated between about AD 80 and 130. The main reasons:
- **Style:** the Greek is ornate and the sentences elaborate, quite unlike 1 Peter.
- **Jude:** much of chapter 2 and the start of chapter 3 resembles Jude (for example, Jude 4, 6–7, 12, 17–18), and most scholars think 2 Peter borrowed from Jude. Why, they ask, would an apostle borrow from a shorter letter?
- **"The fathers fell asleep":** the scoffers say "ever since the fathers fell asleep" (3:4). Some take "the fathers" as the first generation of Christians; if so, that generation had already died.
- **Paul's letters:** the writer speaks of "all his letters" and sets them beside "the other Scriptures" (3:16). Some think this means Paul's letters had already been collected, which happened later.
- **Testament genre:** Richard Bauckham argues that the letter belongs to the Jewish "testament" genre, which puts words of exhortation in the mouth of a figure from the past just before his death. He holds that readers would have understood this as a literary convention, not a deception.

**4. The view that Peter wrote it.** Many other scholars (for example, Michael Green, Thomas Schreiner, Douglas Moo and Gene Green) hold that the letter comes from Peter himself:
- **The early church's standard:** the early church did not accept writings composed in an apostle's name. Around AD 200, Bishop Serapion of Antioch wrote, "We receive both Peter and the other apostles as Christ, but the writings that falsely bear their names we reject" (Eusebius, *Church History* 6.12). Tertullian reports that an Asian presbyter who composed the *Acts of Paul*, a story about Paul, was removed from office once he confessed he had made it up (*On Baptism* 17). The early church hesitated over 2 Peter, but in the end judged it apostolic.
- **Style:** Jerome already suggested that Peter may have used different helpers (perhaps Silvanus, 1 Pet 5:12, though Silvanus may only have carried that letter). The two letters also share features; for example, both use the word "excellence" for the acts of God or Christ (1:3; 1 Pet 2:9). And 2 Peter, written against false teachers, may echo their own language.
- **Jude:** the likeness may mean 2 Peter used Jude, or that Jude used 2 Peter (Michael Green leans this way), or that both drew on common material. An apostle using another's words is not strange: Paul passed on what he had "received" (1 Cor 15:3), and Jude itself draws on earlier sources (Jude 14).
- **"The fathers":** in the New Testament "the fathers" usually means the Old Testament patriarchs (Rom 9:5; Heb 1:1). The scoffers may mean that since creation everything has stayed the same.
- **Paul's letters:** in Paul's lifetime his letters were already passed between churches (Col 4:16). "All his letters" need not mean the whole Pauline collection, only the ones the writer knew.
- **Testament:** if readers all took the letter as a literary convention, it is hard to explain why the early church asked so seriously whether Peter wrote it.

This study follows the view that Peter himself wrote it.

**5. When?** Church tradition says Peter was martyred in Rome under Nero (around AD 64–68). He says "the putting off of my body will be soon" (1:14), so the letter was probably written shortly before his death, around AD 65–68. The letter does not say where it was written; many think Rome.

**6. To whom?** Peter says, "This is now the second letter that I am writing to you" (3:1). Many take the first to be 1 Peter; if so, the readers are the churches of Asia Minor named in 1 Pet 1:1, mostly Gentile believers. Others think the first letter is one now lost. Peter also says Paul "wrote to you" (3:15), so these readers knew Paul's letters too.

## Key Verses: 1:3–4; 1:19–21; 3:9; 3:18

**1. "All things that pertain to life and godliness."** "His divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness, through the knowledge of him who called us to his own glory and excellence, by which he has granted to us his precious and very great promises, so that through them you may become partakers of the divine nature, having escaped from the corruption that is in the world because of sinful desire" (1:3–4). Every command in the letter rests on this "has granted." Believers do not earn godliness by themselves; God has already given all that godliness needs. "Partakers of the divine nature" does not mean that people become God. It means that through God's promises people receive new life in Christ and grow more like God. Eastern Orthodoxy grounds its teaching of *theosis* (deification) here, while insisting that believers share God's "energies," not his essence; Reformed and most evangelical readers take it as union with Christ and renewal by the Spirit, becoming like God in holiness while remaining creatures. Chapter 1 returns to this.

**2. "Carried along by the Holy Spirit."** "And we have something more sure, the prophetic word, to which you will do well to pay attention as to a lamp shining in a dark place... For no prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit" (1:19, 21). Scripture is not human opinion; God speaks through human writers. In a dark age God's word is a lamp, "until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts" (1:19).

**3. "But is patient toward you."** "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance" (3:9). The Lord has not yet returned, not because he has forgotten or is weak, but because of his mercy. What he waits for is repentance.

**4. "Grow in the grace and knowledge."** "But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ" (3:18). This is Peter's last command. The best defense against false teaching is not only to spot errors but to keep growing in the knowledge of Christ.

## Structure of the Book

2 Peter is short, only three chapters, but its line of thought is clear:
- **Greeting** (1:1–2): to "those who have obtained a faith of equal standing with ours"; grace and peace multiplied "in the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord."
- **God's gifts and the believer's growth** (1:3–21): God's divine power has granted everything; so supplement faith with virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection and love; make your calling and election sure; Peter's reminder before his death; the apostles were eyewitnesses of Christ's majesty; the prophets spoke as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.
- **False teachers and their end** (2:1–22): false teachers will arise; God did not spare the angels who sinned, the ancient world, or Sodom and Gomorrah, but preserved Noah and rescued Lot; the teachers' sensuality, greed and destruction; they promise freedom but are slaves of corruption.
- **The day of the Lord and final appeals** (3:1–18): scoffers will come; God once judged the world by water and will judge it by fire; with the Lord one day is as a thousand years; his delay is patience; the day of the Lord will come like a thief; waiting for new heavens and a new earth; things in Paul's letters hard to understand; take care, and grow in grace and knowledge.

## How to Read This Book

**1. Notice "our God and Savior Jesus Christ."** In the first verse Peter says faith is obtained "by the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ" (1:1). Most commentators take the Greek grammar to mean that "God" and "Savior" are one person, Jesus Christ. Three other times the letter uses the identical construction, "our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ" (1:11; 2:20; 3:18), and 3:2 has "the Lord and Savior"; in every one of these a single person is clearly meant. Others read 1:1 as naming God the Father and Jesus Christ, since the next verse speaks of "God and of Jesus our Lord" separately (1:2). On the first reading, this verse, like John 20:28, Romans 9:5 and Titus 2:13, calls Jesus God directly. Either way, the letter puts Christ in the place of God: the closing doxology, "To him be the glory" (3:18), is addressed to Jesus Christ.

**2. Notice the word "knowledge."** 2 Peter keeps returning to knowing: grace and peace are multiplied "in the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord" (1:2); all things for life and godliness come "through the knowledge of him who called us" (1:3); believers are not to be "unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ" (1:8); the letter ends, "grow in the grace and knowledge" (3:18). The false teachers may have claimed a higher knowledge. Peter says true knowledge is knowing a living Lord, and that this knowledge always changes how a person lives.

**3. Read it alongside Jude.** Chapter 2 and the start of chapter 3 share much with Jude: false teachers who slip in and deny the Master (2:1; Jude 4); angels who sinned, kept in darkness (2:4; Jude 6); Sodom and Gomorrah as an example (2:6; Jude 7); blaspheming the glorious ones and what they do not understand (2:10–12; Jude 8–10); Balaam (2:15; Jude 11); "waterless" clouds or springs (2:17; Jude 12); scoffers in the last days (3:3; Jude 18). Reading them together shows what each writer stresses. Peter, for example, leaves out the quotation from Enoch and the story of Michael disputing over the body of Moses (Jude 9, 14–15), and he adds Noah and Lot: God not only judges the wicked but "knows how to rescue the godly" (2:9).

**4. At 3:10, notice the manuscripts differ.** The Chinese Union Version says the earth and its works "will be burned up." The ESV has "will be exposed," because several of the earliest Greek manuscripts (Sinaiticus, Vaticanus and 1739) read "will be found"; "burned up" is the reading of Alexandrinus and the Byzantine majority, which the Chinese Union Version follows; there are other readings as well. The point of the passage is not affected: the day of the Lord will surely come, everything people have done will be laid bare before God, and so "what sort of people ought you to be in lives of holiness and godliness" (3:11). Chapter 3 returns to this.

**5. Do not read only the warnings.** The language of chapter 2 is severe, and it is easy to see only judgment. But the letter begins with God's gifts (1:3–4) and ends with the grace of Christ (3:18). Peter exposes the false teachers so sharply because he loves the flock and does not want them to "lose your own stability" (3:17).

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Simeon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ, To those who have obtained a faith of equal standing with ours by the righteousness of our God and Savior Jesus Christ" (1:1). In my heart I often rank believers. At the top are the apostles, then the martyrs and well-known preachers, then the people at church who pray with power and know the Bible well. I probably come near the bottom: a believer for years, still often weak, never having experienced anything special. Peter had been an eyewitness of Christ's "majesty" (1:16); on the holy mountain he heard the Father's voice (1:17–18). Yet he writes to people who had never seen Jesus and says their faith is "of equal standing with ours." The Greek word means "of equal value," and was used in his day for citizens holding the same rights. And they "obtained" this faith; the word was often used for receiving an allotted share, not for earning something. The worth of faith does not lie in how much the believer has experienced, but in the One believed, and in the fact that this faith is given "by the righteousness of... Jesus Christ." There is one more detail in this verse. Most manuscripts give Peter's name here not in the usual Greek form, "Simon," but in the Jewish form, "Simeon" (the same name in its Hebrew form, which James also uses of Peter in Acts 15:14). At the end of his life, Peter uses the name he had as a fisherman in Galilee. That fisherman once said to Jesus, "Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, O Lord" (Luke 5:8), and in a courtyard denied him three times (Luke 22:54–62). Before that night Jesus had told him, "Simon, Simon... I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail" (Luke 22:31–32). Peter's faith never stood by his own strength. If even an apostle's faith was kept like that, by grace, then my faith has no reason to stand at the back of the line. Peter and I stand on the same ground. Christ in his righteousness, his just and impartial giving, has given the apostle and me a faith of the same worth. And the gospel behind it is the same for us both: we are accepted not by a righteousness of our own, but by his (see Phil 3:9). When we enter the "eternal kingdom," the apostle and I will go in through the same door, for the kingdom belongs to "our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ" (1:11).
