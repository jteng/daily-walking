## 本章脉络

第一章末了，彼得说「经上所有的预言没有可随私意解说的」，乃是「人被圣灵感动，说出神的话来」（1:20-21）。第二章紧接着说：从前有真先知，也有「假先知」；将来在教会中间也必有「假师傅」（2:1）。全章可以分成四段：
- **假师傅要来**（2:1-3）：他们私自引进陷害人的异端，连买他们的主也不承认；他们放纵情欲，因贪心用捏造的言语取利。但他们的刑罚「并不迟延」。
- **三个旧约的例子**（2:4-10上）：犯罪的天使、挪亚时代的世界、所多玛和蛾摩拉。神审判不敬虔的人，却保护了挪亚，搭救了罗得。结论是：「主知道搭救敬虔的人脱离试探，把不义的人留在刑罚之下」（2:9）。
- **假师傅的画像**（2:10下-16）：他们胆大任性，像没有灵性的畜类；满眼淫色，引诱心不坚固的人；走了巴兰的路，贪爱不义的工价。
- **空洞的应许，更坏的结局**（2:17-22）：他们是「无水的井」；他们应许人得以自由，自己却作败坏的奴仆；认识了义路又背弃的，末后的景况比先前更不好。

这一章和犹大书4-16节很相似：同样讲偷偷进来的人、犯罪的天使、所多玛、巴兰，同样用「无水」「幽暗」这些形象。多数学者（如包衡、施莱纳）认为彼得后书采用了犹大书的材料；也有人（如迈克尔·格林）倾向认为是犹大书采用了彼得后书，或者两封信取材于同一个来源。值得留意的是彼得加上了什么：犹大书所举的几个例子都是审判的例子，彼得却加上挪亚和罗得这两个蒙搭救的例子。这一章虽然是全书最严厉的一章，中心却是一句安慰的话：主知道搭救敬虔的人。

## 深读 2:1-3 假师傅与买他们的主

**1. 「从前……将来」。** 「从前在百姓中有假先知起来，将来在你们中间也必有假师傅，私自引进陷害人的异端，连买他们的主他们也不承认，自取速速地灭亡。」（2:1）
- **一样的历史：** 以色列从来不缺假先知。主耶稣也警告：「你们要防备假先知。他们到你们这里来，外面披着羊皮，里面却是残暴的狼。」（太7:15）保罗对以弗所的长老说：「就是你们中间，也必有人起来说悖谬的话，要引诱门徒跟从他们。」（徒20:30）假师傅出现，不表示神失败了；这正是圣经早已预言的。
- **「私自引进」：** 他们在教会里面，用基督徒的话，悄悄把别的东西带进来（参犹4「偷着进来」）。今天也有异端团体专门派人混进教会，先建立关系，再把人拉走，例如东方闪电（自称全能神教会）。
- **「陷害人的异端」：** 「异端」这个词原来指派别、主张，这里指叫人灭亡的教导。从第三章看，他们很可能否认主的再来和审判：「主要降临的应许在哪里呢？」（3:4）所以彼得在这里特别说，他们的刑罚「并不迟延」，他们的灭亡「也必速速来到」（2:3）。他们以为审判在睡觉，彼得说审判没有睡觉。

**2. 「连买他们的主他们也不承认」。** 这里的「主」不是常用的那个词，而是「主宰」（*despotēs*），指奴仆的主人，新约也用来称呼神（路2:29；徒4:24）和基督（犹4）。「买」这个词，新约多用来讲基督用血把人赎回来：「你们是重价买来的」（林前6:20）；「用自己的血从各族、各方、各民、各国中买了人来，叫他们归于神」（启5:9）。一个奴仆不承认用重价买他的主人，这是何等的忘恩负义。

这节经文也牵涉到一个历代争论的问题：基督是为谁死的？如果这些人最后灭亡，基督的死是不是也为了他们？几种读法：
- **改革宗的特定救赎论：** 认为基督的死是为选民，确实而有效地救赎他们。持这种看法的人对这节经文有几种解释。约翰·欧文等人认为，这里的「买」是旧约的说法，就像摩西说神是「将你买来的」（申32:6；希伯来文这个词也可以译作「得着」），指神拯救以色列、使他们归于自己，指的是神和立约群体的关系，不一定是指十字架的救赎。施莱纳等人则认为，这是从人的角度来说的：这些人在教会中，自称蒙了基督的救赎，人人也都这样看他们，彼得就照他们所承认的来说话。
- **阿民念派和卫斯理宗（信义宗也认为基督为所有的人死，但根据不同）：** 认为基督的死是为所有的人，人能否得着，在于是否信而持守；这节正好说明，基督也买了那些后来背弃他的人。他们常把这节和「他舍自己作万人的赎价」（提前2:6）、「也是为普天下人的罪」（约一2:2）一起读。
- **亚目拉督派和假设性普世救赎论（如法国的亚目拉督、英国的达文南）：** 认为基督的死对所有人都足够，在某种意义上也是为所有人；但神无条件地拣选了一些人，只在他们身上有效地施行。照这看法，基督在某种意义上确实买了这些人，但他们没有得着。

无论采取哪一种读法，彼得要说的都一样：这些人不是不知道主，而是不承认他。他们口里称他为主，生活却否认他的主权（参多1:16）。

**3. 邪淫与贪心。** 「将有许多人随从他们邪淫的行为，便叫真道因他们的缘故被毁谤。他们因有贪心，要用捏造的言语在你们身上取利。」（2:2-3）
- **他们的生活：** 假师傅的问题不只是教义错了，也是生活败坏。「邪淫」指不知羞耻地放纵情欲。错误的教导和放纵的生活常常连在一起。他们的逻辑是：既然没有审判，怎样活都无所谓。
- **「捏造的言语」：** 「捏造」这个词原文是 *plastos*，有塑造出来的意思。第一章彼得说，使徒传讲的「并不是随从乖巧捏造的虚言，乃是亲眼见过他的威荣」（1:16；原文用的是另一个词，意思相近）。真使徒传的是亲眼见过的事实，假师傅说的是编造出来的话。
- **「在你们身上取利」：** 「取利」有作买卖的意思。假师傅拿信徒做买卖，把信仰当作赚钱的门路。今天有人宣扬「多奉献就必蒙福」的成功神学，有人借讲道、辅导、特殊恩赐敛财，正是这样。

> 要点：假师傅常常不是从外面攻击教会，而是在里面，用基督徒的话，悄悄引进叫人灭亡的东西，并且在生活上放纵、在钱财上贪心。他们不承认那用重价买了他们的主。信徒得保护，不在于自己够聪明，而在于常常回到那位真用自己的血买了我们的主那里。

## 深读 2:4-10上 主知道搭救敬虔的人

**1. 一句很长的话。** 从2:4到2:10上，原文是一句很长的条件句，和合本把它分成几句：三个例子，一个结论。每个例子都有审判；后两个例子还有搭救。

**2. 犯罪的天使。** 「就是天使犯了罪，神也没有宽容，曾把他们丢在地狱，交在黑暗坑中，等候审判。」（2:4）
- **「地狱」：** 原文是一个动词，只在这里出现，意思是丢进塔尔塔罗斯。在希腊神话中，塔尔塔罗斯是阴间最深的地方，关着反叛的神祇。七十士译本（约伯记、箴言）和《以诺一书》的希腊文本也用过这个词。彼得借用当时人熟悉的这个词，说明那是最深的拘禁之处，并不是接受了希腊神话。
- **「黑暗坑中」：** 有些古抄本作黑暗的锁链，英文标准版就是这样译的；犹大书也说「主用锁链把他们永远拘留在黑暗里」（犹6）。
- **天使犯了什么罪？** 彼得没有说。许多学者（如包衡、施莱纳）认为，彼得和犹大是照当时犹太人读创世记6:1-4的方式来说的：所谓「神的儿子们」就是天使，他们离开本位，娶了人的女子；《以诺一书》第六至十章就这样讲，并说这些天使被捆绑在黑暗里，等候审判。犹大书后面还引用了《以诺一书》（犹14-15），不过引用不等于把它当作圣经。另一些人（如奥古斯丁、加尔文）认为，创世记6章「神的儿子们」是指塞特的后裔；彼得这里讲的是天使最初跟随撒但的堕落，并不是指创世记6章。他们也指出主耶稣说天上的使者「不娶也不嫁」（太22:30）。不论哪一种，彼得的重点是：连天使犯罪，神也没有宽容。

**3. 「传义道的挪亚」。** 「神也没有宽容上古的世代，曾叫洪水临到那不敬虔的世代，却保护了传义道的挪亚一家八口。」（2:5）
- **「传义道的」：** 创世记没有记载挪亚讲道，但说他是「义人」，「与神同行」（创6:9）。犹太人的传统（如约瑟夫《犹太古史》第一卷）和早期教会的《革利免一书》第七章，都说挪亚曾劝当时的人悔改。他一锤一锤地造方舟，这件事本身就是对那世代的见证：「因此就定了那世代的罪，自己也承受了那从信而来的义」（来11:7）。
- **「一家八口」：** 原文直译作第八个人挪亚，意思是连他在内共八个人。彼得前书也说：「藉着水得救的不多，只有八个人。」（彼前3:20）全世界只有八个人得救，神却一个也没有忘记。

**4. 所多玛与「义人罗得」。** 「又判定所多玛、蛾摩拉，将二城倾覆，焚烧成灰，作为后世不敬虔人的鉴戒；只搭救了那常为恶人淫行忧伤的义人罗得。（因为那义人住在他们中间，看见听见他们不法的事，他的义心就天天伤痛。）」（2:6-8）
- **彼得三次称罗得为义人：** 「义人罗得」「那义人」「他的义心」。读过创世记的人可能会觉得奇怪。罗得一步步搬进了所多玛（创13:10-12；19:1）。暴徒围住他家的时候，他竟然要把两个女儿交给他们（创19:8）。这是极大的罪恶，不是什么款待客人的美德，圣经记下来，也不是叫人效法。他的女婿们以为他说的是「戏言」（创19:14）；最后在山洞里，他两次被女儿灌醉，与她们乱伦（创19:30-36），这是他家悲惨的结局。
- **那么彼得为什么称他为义人？** 第一，创世记本身已经把罗得和所多玛人分开：亚伯拉罕为所多玛代求，说「将义人与恶人同杀……这断不是你所行的」（创18:25），神也答应了。第二，罗得确实为城中的罪忧伤，他对暴徒说：「众弟兄，请你们不要做这恶事」（创19:7）。犹太人的传统（如《所罗门智训》第十章）也称他为义人。第三，也是最根本的：罗得的义不是无罪的完全。他属于那蒙神接纳的一边，心里真恨恶所多玛的恶（2:8）；而他蒙搭救，是出于神的怜恤（创19:16）和神对亚伯拉罕的记念（创19:29）。

**5. 结论。** 「主知道搭救敬虔的人脱离试探，把不义的人留在刑罚之下，等候审判的日子。」（2:9）
- **「主知道」：** 不是说主也许能做到，而是说他确实知道怎样做。挪亚只有八口人，罗得只有一家四口，后来又少了一个（创19:26），神都知道他们在哪里，也知道怎样把他们领出来。保罗也说：「在受试探的时候，总要给你们开一条出路。」（林前10:13）
- **两样确定的事：** 有些信徒看见假师傅得势，就以为神不管事。这两句话正好安慰他们：神不会把义人与恶人同杀，也不会让恶人永远逍遥。

> 要点：神审判不敬虔的人，却从不忘记属他的人，哪怕全世界得救的只有八个，哪怕那个人像罗得一样软弱迟延。我们得蒙搭救，不是靠自己够坚定，而是靠那位「知道搭救敬虔的人」的主，靠他的怜悯。

## 深读 2:10下-16 巴兰的路

**1. 胆大任性。** 「他们胆大任性，毁谤在尊位的，也不知惧怕。就是天使，虽然力量权能更大，还不用毁谤的话在主面前告他们。」（2:10下-11）
- **「在尊位的」：** 原文直译是众荣耀者，多数学者认为是指灵界的受造者。是指好天使还是邪恶的天使，解经家看法不一。许多人认为是指邪恶的灵界权势；假师傅可能自以为属灵，随意嘲弄魔鬼和邪灵，好像不必把罪和撒但当一回事。犹大书也说：「天使长米迦勒为摩西的尸首与魔鬼争辩的时候，尚且不敢用毁谤的话罪责他，只说：『主责备你吧！』」（犹9）连天使都把审判交给主，这些人却随口论断自己不明白的事。

**2. 与你们一同坐席。** 「这些人喜爱白昼宴乐，他们已被玷污，又有瑕疵，正与你们一同坐席，就以自己的诡诈为快乐。」（2:13）
- **在教会的饭桌上：** 早期教会常在一起吃饭，并在饭中守主的晚餐（林前11:20-22）。「诡诈」这个词，有些古抄本作爱席，两个希腊词只差几个字母。犹大书说，这样的人「在你们的爱席上与你们同吃的时候，正是礁石」（犹12）。不论哪一种读法，彼得都是说：他们把教会最亲密的团契变成了自己享乐和欺骗的场合。

**3. 引诱心不坚固的人。** 「他们满眼是淫色，止不住犯罪，引诱那心不坚固的人，心中习惯了贪婪，正是被咒诅的种类。」（2:14）
- **「满眼是淫色」：** 直译作眼中充满淫妇，就是眼目总在寻找犯奸淫的对象。
- **「引诱」：** 这个词原是钓鱼、设饵的意思。他们专找「心不坚固的人」下手：刚信主的、受过伤的、孤单的、对属灵领袖特别信任的人。保罗也讲到那种「偷进人家、牢笼无知妇女」的人（提后3:6）。
- **今天的教会要警醒：** 这一节描写的，正是今天所说的属灵虐待和性侵。凡是利用牧者、传道、导师、辅导的身份，操控、剥削、侵犯别人的，都是这里所说「引诱那心不坚固的人」。受害的人并不因为「心不坚固」就有罪；罪全在那设饵的人身上。

就是属灵上成熟的人也可能受害；容易受骗、受害，不等于道德上软弱。受害的人不必因为对方属灵、有恩赐就默默忍受，也不必留在危险中，不必勉强与不安全的人和好；涉及犯罪的，应当向警察举报（罗13:4）。教会绝不可为了面子、为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩；保罗说，犯罪的长老「当在众人面前责备他」（提前5:20）。即使施害的人后来认罪悔改，饶恕也不等于恢复他的职分，或让他再接近受害者和孩子。
- **也不必怀疑每一位牧者：** 彼得写这一章，不是要信徒对所有传道人都起疑心。他自己也是牧者，劝长老们「务要牧养在你们中间神的群羊」（彼前5:2）。分辨的标准不是传闻，也不是个人的好恶，而是使徒所传的福音（1:16；3:2）和生命结出的果子（太7:16）。真牧人的样子正好和这里相反：「也不是因为贪财，乃是出于乐意；也不是辖制所托付你们的，乃是作群羊的榜样」（彼前5:2-3）。对这样忠心劳苦的人，圣经叫我们「因他们所做的工，用爱心格外尊重他们」（帖前5:13）。

**4. 巴兰的路。** 「他们离弃正路，就走差了，随从比珥之子巴兰的路。巴兰就是那贪爱不义之工价的先知，他却为自己的过犯受了责备；那不能说话的驴以人言拦阻先知的狂妄。」（2:15-16）
- **巴兰的故事：** 摩押王巴勒用重金请巴兰来咒诅以色列（民22-24章）。神不许他咒诅，他口里只能祝福，心里却舍不得那份工价。后来以色列人在毗珥犯奸淫、拜偶像，正是「因巴兰的计谋」（民31:16；参启2:14）。所以巴兰成了一个典型：为钱出卖信仰，并引诱神的百姓犯罪。
- **驴开口说话：** 耶和华的使者拿着刀站在路上，驴看见了，巴兰却看不见，还三次打驴，直到「耶和华叫驴开口」（民22:28）。这是彼得的讽刺：自以为看见属灵奥秘的人，被贪心弄瞎了眼，看得还不如一头驴清楚。

> 要点：假师傅的标记常常不在他们的口才和恩赐上，而在他们的眼睛和心：眼里是淫色，心里是贪婪，手里拿着诱饵。教会必须保护那些容易受害的人，不可遮掩。可是我们自己也要问：巴兰的贪心，是否也在我心里？若是，就当认罪悔改。我们需要的，正是那位不为利益、却为我们舍命的好牧人（约10:11）。

## 深读 2:17-22 应许人自由，自己却作奴仆

**1. 无水的井。** 「这些人是无水的井，是狂风催逼的雾气，有墨黑的幽暗为他们存留。」（2:17）在干旱的地方，赶路的人远远看见一口井，满心盼望，走近一看，却是干的。假师傅给人的就是这样的盼望：说得很好听，却没有一滴活水。他们「说虚妄矜夸的大话」（2:18），听起来很有力量，其实是空的。

**2. 他们找谁？** 「他们……用肉身的情欲和邪淫的事引诱那些刚才脱离妄行的人。」（2:18）他们专找刚刚从旧生活中挣脱出来的初信者，所以教会特别要照顾和保护初信的人。

**3. 「应许人得以自由」。** 「他们应许人得以自由，自己却作败坏的奴仆，因为人被谁制伏就是谁的奴仆。」（2:19）
- **什么样的自由？** 假师傅大概说：你们既然在基督里得了自由，就不必再受道德的约束；既然没有审判，怎样活都可以。他们可能还扭曲了保罗论恩典的话（3:16；参罗6:1）。保罗自己说：「你们蒙召是要得自由，只是不可将你们的自由当作放纵情欲的机会。」（加5:13）
- **「被谁制伏就是谁的奴仆」：** 这是一句古代的格言，也是主耶稣的教导：「所有犯罪的就是罪的奴仆。」（约8:34）那些说「我要自由」而放纵私欲的人，最后发现自己被私欲捆绑，停不下来。真正的自由不是想做什么就做什么，而是从罪的辖制中被释放：「天父的儿子若叫你们自由，你们就真自由了。」（约8:36）

**4. 末后的景况。** 「倘若他们因认识主－救主耶稣基督，得以脱离世上的污秽，后来又在其中被缠住、制伏，他们末后的景况就比先前更不好了。他们晓得义路，竟背弃了传给他们的圣命，倒不如不晓得为妙。」（2:20-21）
- **「末后的景况比先前更不好」：** 这几乎就是主耶稣论污鬼回来时的话（太12:45）。
- **两句俗语：** 「狗所吐的，它转过来又吃；猪洗净了又回到泥里去滚。」（2:22）前一句来自箴言：「愚昧人行愚妄事，行了又行，就如狗转过来吃它所吐的。」（箴26:11）后一句不在旧约中。在犹太人眼中，狗和猪都是不洁净的动物。

**5. 这些人真的得救过吗？** 这是历代信徒读到这里都会问的问题。彼得说他们「认识主－救主耶稣基督」（这里的「认识」和1:2-3、8是同一个词），「脱离世上的污秽」，「晓得义路」。几种看法：
- **改革宗传统（如《威斯敏斯德信条》第十七章论圣徒的蒙保守）：** 认为真正重生的人必蒙神保守到底（约10:28；6:37）；这些人只是在表面上认识了福音，生活上也一度改变，但心从来没有被更新。他们认为俗语正好说明这一点：狗还是狗，猪洗净了还是猪，本性从来没有变。约翰也说：「他们从我们中间出去，却不是属我们的……他们出去，显明都不是属我们的。」（约一2:19）一些改革宗学者（如施莱纳）还补充说：这些人本身没有真正重生，但这样的警告对收信的真信徒是真实的，神正是用这些警告保守他们到底。
- **阿民念派和卫斯理宗（信义宗也认为真信徒可能失落救恩，但根据不同）：** 认为这些人确实认识了主，真的得过救，后来却背道，失去了救恩。这种看法认为，「认识」在本书中是得救的认识（1:3），警告是真实的，否则就失去意义。他们也常引用希伯来书6:4-6。

各方都同意几件事：认识真道而背弃，是极其严重的事；这一章是对假师傅和跟随他们的人说的，不是叫软弱跌倒、正在挣扎的信徒绝望。彼得在全书的结尾，称读者为「亲爱的弟兄」，劝他们防备，又叫他们在恩典和知识上有长进（3:17-18）。一个为自己的罪忧伤、怕自己离开主的人，和这些「说虚妄矜夸的大话」、引诱别人的人很不一样。

> 要点：假师傅应许的自由，最后是捆绑；只有神的儿子使人得的自由，才是真自由。读到这些严厉的话，我们不必去猜别人是不是真得救，而要回到那位使人真自由的主那里。他说他的羊「永不灭亡，谁也不能从我手里把他们夺去」（约10:28）。

## 默想

**默想：** 「只搭救了那常为恶人淫行忧伤的义人罗得。」（2:7）罗得不是一下子住进所多玛的。他先是看中了那片「滋润」的平原，然后「渐渐挪移帐棚，直到所多玛」（创13:10-12），后来就坐在城门口（创19:1），城里的人却仍把他看作「来寄居」的外人（创19:9）。他天天为城中的罪伤痛（2:8），却也天天住在那里。我也有这样的地方：一个靠回扣和假账维持的生意，一段我知道不对、却舍不得断的关系，一个我早就想离开、却一次次回去的习惯。我心里是伤痛的，可是伤痛并没有让我离开。（如果我不是舍不得罪，而是被困在别人的伤害里，那就不是罗得的迟延；离开危险、向人求助、必要时报警，都是对的。）

最叫我惊奇的，是创世记19:16。天使已经说了这城要毁灭，罗得还是「迟延不走」。经上没有说他终于下定决心，而是说：「二人因为耶和华怜恤罗得，就拉着他的手和他妻子的手，并他两个女儿的手，把他们领出来，安置在城外。」罗得被领出所多玛，不是靠他的决心，而是靠一双拉住他的手，和耶和华的怜恤。罗得的故事并没有在城外圆满结束，他后来在山洞里又跌倒了（创19:30-36），圣经没有替他遮掩。可是将近两千年后，彼得在圣灵感动下回头看他，三次称他为「义人」。这不是因为罗得配得；他能算为神的人，全是出于神的恩典。

我得以被称为义，也不是因为我比罗得坚定。那天，火从天上降在所多玛，罗得在城外；后来，主耶稣「背着自己的十字架出来」（约19:17），「要用自己的血叫百姓成圣，也就在城门外受苦」（来13:12）。罗得被领到城外，审判的火就落不到他身上；耶稣到了城外，却是要让神的审判落在他自己身上。「神使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义。」（林后5:21）如果我也在迟延，如果我已经因为迟延失去了很多，像罗得最后失去了家，女婿没有跟出来，妻子也没有走到城外，那位主仍然「知道搭救敬虔的人」（2:9）。这不是叫我放心继续迟延。离开有时也意味着偿还。撒该是在主耶稣先说「今天我必住在你家里」（路19:5）以后，才说「我若讹诈了谁，就还他四倍」（路19:8）。今天我在哪里迟延不走？那地方有什么，是我心里还舍不得的？我能离开那里，靠的不是我终于够坚定。主知道我在哪里，也知道怎样把我领出来；那位为我在城门外受了审判的主，不会把我留在所多玛。

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 1, Peter said that "no prophecy of Scripture comes from someone's own interpretation," but "men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit" (1:20–21). Chapter 2 follows at once: there were true prophets, and there were "false prophets"; so there will also be "false teachers" in the church (2:1). The chapter falls into four parts:
- **False teachers will come** (2:1–3): they secretly bring in destructive heresies, even denying the Master who bought them; they live in sensuality and in greed exploit people with false words. But their condemnation "is not idle."
- **Three Old Testament examples** (2:4–10a): the angels who sinned, the world of Noah's day, Sodom and Gomorrah. God judged the ungodly, but preserved Noah and rescued Lot. The conclusion: "the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trials, and to keep the unrighteous under punishment" (2:9).
- **A portrait of the false teachers** (2:10b–16): bold and willful, like irrational animals; eyes full of adultery, enticing unsteady souls; following the way of Balaam, who loved gain from wrongdoing.
- **Empty promises, a worse end** (2:17–22): they are "waterless springs"; they promise freedom but are themselves slaves of corruption; those who knew the way of righteousness and turned back are worse off at the last than at the first.

This chapter closely resembles Jude 4–16: both speak of people who slip in, the angels who sinned, Sodom and Balaam, and both use images of waterlessness and darkness. Most scholars (for example, Richard Bauckham and Thomas Schreiner) think 2 Peter drew on Jude; others think Jude drew on 2 Peter (Michael Green leans this way), or that both drew on a common source. It is worth noticing what Peter adds: Jude's examples are all examples of judgment; Peter adds two examples of rescue, Noah and Lot. Though this is the sternest chapter in the letter, at its center is a word of comfort: the Lord knows how to rescue the godly.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–3 The False Teachers and the Master Who Bought Them

**1. "Arose... will be."** "But false prophets also arose among the people, just as there will be false teachers among you, who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, even denying the Master who bought them, bringing upon themselves swift destruction" (2:1).
- **The same history:** Israel never lacked false prophets. The Lord Jesus warned, "Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves" (Matt 7:15). Paul told the Ephesian elders, "from among your own selves will arise men speaking twisted things, to draw away the disciples after them" (Acts 20:30). The appearance of false teachers does not mean God has failed; Scripture foretold it long ago.
- **"Secretly bring in":** they work inside the church, using Christian words, quietly bringing in something else (see Jude 4, "crept in unnoticed"). Some sects today still send people into churches to build relationships first and then draw members away, for example Eastern Lightning (which calls itself the Church of Almighty God).
- **"Destructive heresies":** the word "heresy" first meant a party or opinion; here it means teaching that leads to destruction. Judging from chapter 3, they very likely denied the Lord's return and the judgment: "Where is the promise of his coming?" (3:4). So Peter insists here that their condemnation "is not idle, and their destruction is not asleep" (2:3). They thought judgment was asleep; Peter says it is not.

**2. "Even denying the Master who bought them."** The word for "Master" here is not the usual word for "Lord" but *despotēs*, the owner of a slave; the New Testament uses it of God (Luke 2:29; Acts 4:24) and of Christ (Jude 4). The verb "bought" is used in the New Testament mostly for Christ's ransoming people with his blood: "you were bought with a price" (1 Cor 6:20); "by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation" (Rev 5:9). A slave who disowns the master who paid a great price for him: what ingratitude.

The verse also touches a question long debated: for whom did Christ die? If these people finally perish, did Christ's death include them? Several readings:
- **Reformed particular redemption:** Christ died for the elect, truly and effectively redeeming them. Those who hold this read the verse in several ways. John Owen and others take "bought" in its Old Testament sense, as when Moses speaks of God as the one who acquired his people (Deut 32:6; the Hebrew *qanah* can mean "acquire," and the KJV and CUV render it "bought thee"): God delivering Israel and making them his own, a covenant-community sense, not necessarily the redemption of the cross. Schreiner and others take it as spoken from the human point of view: these people were in the church and professed to be redeemed by Christ, and everyone regarded them so; Peter speaks of them according to their profession.
- **Arminian and Wesleyan (Lutherans also hold that Christ died for all, on different grounds):** Christ died for all people, and whether one receives the benefit depends on believing and continuing; this verse shows Christ bought even those who later reject him. They often read it with "who gave himself as a ransom for all" (1 Tim 2:6) and "also for the sins of the whole world" (1 John 2:2).
- **Amyraldian and hypothetical universalist (e.g., Moïse Amyraut in France, John Davenant in England):** Christ's death is sufficient for all and in some sense for all, but God unconditionally elected some, and the death is effectively applied only to them. On this view Christ did in some sense buy these false teachers, though they never received it.

Whichever reading one takes, Peter's point is the same: these people do not fail to know the Lord; they disown him. They call him Lord with their lips, but their lives deny his rule (see Titus 1:16).

**3. Sensuality and greed.** "And many will follow their sensuality, and because of them the way of truth will be blasphemed. And in their greed they will exploit you with false words" (2:2–3).
- **Their lives:** the false teachers' problem is not only wrong doctrine but corrupt living. "Sensuality" means shameless indulgence of desire. False teaching and loose living often go together. Their logic is: since there is no judgment, it does not matter how one lives.
- **"False words":** the Greek word is *plastos*, "molded" or "fabricated." In chapter 1 Peter said the apostles "did not follow cleverly devised myths... but we were eyewitnesses of his majesty" (1:16; a different but related expression in Greek). True apostles preached facts they had seen; false teachers speak words they have made up.
- **"Exploit you":** the word carries the sense of trading. The false teachers treat believers as a business and faith as a way to make money. Some today who preach a prosperity gospel ("give more and you will surely be blessed"), or who make money from preaching, counseling or special gifts, do exactly this.

> The point: False teachers usually do not attack the church from outside. They work within, using Christian words, quietly bringing in what destroys, while living in indulgence and greed. They disown the Master who paid a great price for them. Believers are not protected by being clever enough, but by coming back again and again to the Master who truly bought us with his own blood.

## Going Deeper: 2:4–10a The Lord Knows How to Rescue the Godly

**1. One long sentence.** From 2:4 to 2:10a the Greek is a single long conditional sentence ("if... if... if... then"): three examples, one conclusion. Every example has judgment; the last two also have rescue.

**2. The angels who sinned.** "For if God did not spare angels when they sinned, but cast them into hell and committed them to chains of gloomy darkness to be kept until the judgment" (2:4).
- **"Hell":** the Greek is a verb found only here, meaning "to cast into Tartarus." In Greek myth Tartarus was the lowest part of the underworld, where rebel gods were held. The Greek Old Testament (Job, Proverbs) and the Greek text of *1 Enoch* (20:2) already use the term. Peter borrows a word his readers knew to describe the deepest place of confinement; he is not adopting the myth.
- **"Chains of gloomy darkness":** some ancient manuscripts read "pits of darkness," as the CUV does. Jude says, "he has kept [them] in eternal chains under gloomy darkness" (Jude 6).
- **What was the angels' sin?** Peter does not say. Many scholars (for example, Bauckham and Schreiner) think Peter and Jude speak in line with the way Jews of the time read Genesis 6:1–4: "the sons of God" were angels who left their place and took human wives. *1 Enoch* 6–10 tells it this way, and says these angels were bound in darkness until the judgment; Jude later quotes *1 Enoch* (Jude 14–15), though quoting it does not make it Scripture. Others (for example, Augustine and Calvin) take the "sons of God" in Genesis 6 as the line of Seth, and think Peter refers here to the angels' original fall with Satan, not to Genesis 6. They also point out that Jesus says the angels "neither marry nor are given in marriage" (Matt 22:30). Either way, Peter's point is that God did not spare even angels when they sinned.

**3. "Noah, a herald of righteousness."** "If he did not spare the ancient world, but preserved Noah, a herald of righteousness, with seven others, when he brought a flood upon the world of the ungodly" (2:5).
- **"A herald of righteousness":** Genesis does not record Noah preaching, but calls him "a righteous man" who "walked with God" (Gen 6:9). Jewish tradition (for example, Josephus, *Antiquities*, book 1) and the early Christian letter *1 Clement* (chapter 7) say Noah urged his generation to repent. His building of the ark, blow by blow, was itself a witness: "By this he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith" (Heb 11:7).
- **"With seven others":** the Greek says literally "Noah, the eighth," that is, eight people including him. 1 Peter says the same: "a few, that is, eight persons, were brought safely through water" (1 Pet 3:20). Out of the whole world only eight were saved, and God forgot not one of them.

**4. Sodom and "righteous Lot."** "If by turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah to ashes he condemned them to extinction, making them an example of what is going to happen to the ungodly; and if he rescued righteous Lot, greatly distressed by the sensual conduct of the wicked (for as that righteous man lived among them day after day, he was tormenting his righteous soul over their lawless deeds that he saw and heard)" (2:6–8).
- **Peter calls Lot righteous three times:** "righteous Lot," "that righteous man," "his righteous soul." Readers of Genesis may be surprised. Lot moved step by step into Sodom (Gen 13:10–12; 19:1). When the mob surrounded his house, he offered them his two daughters (Gen 19:8). That was a great evil, not a virtue of hospitality, and Scripture does not record it for us to copy. His sons-in-law thought he was "jesting" (Gen 19:14); and at the end, in a cave, he let his daughters make him drunk two nights running, and incest followed (Gen 19:30–36), a wretched end for his household.
- **Why then does Peter call him righteous?** First, Genesis itself sets Lot apart from the people of Sodom: Abraham, pleading for Sodom, said, "Far be it from you... to put the righteous to death with the wicked" (Gen 18:25), and God agreed. Second, Lot really did grieve over the city's sin; he told the mob, "I beg you, my brothers, do not act so wickedly" (Gen 19:7). Jewish tradition (for example, the *Wisdom of Solomon*, chapter 10) also calls him righteous. Third, and most basic: Lot's righteousness was not sinless perfection. He belonged to the side God had accepted, and his heart truly hated Sodom's evil (2:8); and his rescue came from the LORD's mercy (Gen 19:16) and God's remembering Abraham (Gen 19:29).

**5. The conclusion.** "Then the Lord knows how to rescue the godly from trials, and to keep the unrighteous under punishment until the day of judgment" (2:9).
- **"The Lord knows how":** not that the Lord "might be able," but that he knows how. Noah's household was only eight; Lot's family only four, and then one fewer (Gen 19:26). God knew where they were and how to bring them out. Paul says the same: "with the temptation he will also provide the way of escape" (1 Cor 10:13).
- **Two certainties:** some believers watch false teachers prosper and think God does not care. These two clauses comfort them: God will not sweep away the righteous with the wicked, and the wicked will not go unpunished forever.

> The point: God judges the ungodly, but he never forgets his own, even if out of the whole world only eight are saved, even if one of them is as weak and slow as Lot. We are rescued not because we are steady enough, but because of the Lord who "knows how to rescue the godly," and by his mercy.

## Going Deeper: 2:10b–16 The Way of Balaam

**1. Bold and willful.** "Bold and willful, they do not tremble as they blaspheme the glorious ones, whereas angels, though greater in might and power, do not pronounce a blasphemous judgment against them before the Lord" (2:10b–11).
- **"The glorious ones":** literally "glories"; most scholars take these as created spiritual beings. Whether good angels or evil ones is debated. Many think evil spiritual powers are meant; the false teachers may have mocked the devil and evil spirits, thinking themselves spiritual, as if sin and Satan need not be taken seriously. Jude says, "when the archangel Michael, contending with the devil, was disputing about the body of Moses, he did not presume to pronounce a blasphemous judgment, but said, 'The Lord rebuke you'" (Jude 9). Even angels leave judgment to the Lord, but these people pass judgment on things they do not understand.

**2. Feasting with you.** "They count it pleasure to revel in the daytime. They are blots and blemishes, reveling in their deceptions, while they feast with you" (2:13).
- **At the church's table:** the early church often ate together and kept the Lord's Supper within the meal (1 Cor 11:20–22). For "deceptions" some ancient manuscripts read "love feasts"; the two Greek words differ by only a few letters. Jude calls such people "blemishes on your love feasts" (Jude 12; the Greek word can also mean hidden reefs, as the CUV takes it). Either reading, Peter means that they turned the church's closest fellowship into a setting for their own pleasure and deceit.

**3. Enticing unsteady souls.** "They have eyes full of adultery, insatiable for sin. They entice unsteady souls. They have hearts trained in greed. Accursed children!" (2:14).
- **"Eyes full of adultery":** literally "full of an adulteress": their eyes are always looking for someone to commit adultery with.
- **"Entice":** the word comes from fishing and setting bait. They aim at "unsteady souls": new believers, the wounded, the lonely, those who trust spiritual leaders most. Paul also speaks of those "who creep into households and capture weak women" (2 Tim 3:6).
- **The church today must be alert:** this verse describes what we now call spiritual abuse and sexual abuse. Anyone who uses the position of pastor, preacher, mentor or counselor to manipulate, exploit or violate others is doing what Peter calls "enticing unsteady souls." The victim is not guilty for being "unsteady"; the guilt lies wholly with the one who sets the bait.

Mature believers can be targeted too; being deceived or vulnerable is not a moral failing. Victims need not bear it in silence because the other person seems spiritual or gifted, need not stay in danger, nor need they reconcile with someone unsafe; where a crime is involved, it should be reported to the police (Rom 13:4). The church must never cover it up to save face or because "family shame must not go outside" (家丑不可外扬). Paul says an elder who persists in sin should be rebuked "in the presence of all" (1 Tim 5:20). Even if the abuser later confesses and repents, forgiveness does not restore his office or give him access again to victims or children.
- **Nor need we suspect every pastor:** Peter does not write this chapter to make believers wary of all their leaders. He was a shepherd himself and urged the elders to "shepherd the flock of God that is among you" (1 Pet 5:2). The test is not rumor or personal preference but the apostolic gospel (1:16; 3:2) and the fruit of a life (Matt 7:16). A true shepherd looks like the opposite of what Peter describes here: "not for shameful gain, but eagerly; not domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock" (1 Pet 5:2–3). Such faithful workers we are told "to esteem... very highly in love because of their work" (1 Thess 5:13).

**4. The way of Balaam.** "Forsaking the right way, they have gone astray. They have followed the way of Balaam, the son of Beor, who loved gain from wrongdoing, but was rebuked for his own transgression; a speechless donkey spoke with human voice and restrained the prophet's madness" (2:15–16).
- **Balaam's story:** Balak king of Moab offered Balaam a great fee to curse Israel (Num 22–24). God would not let him curse, and his mouth could only bless, but his heart could not let go of the fee. Later Israel fell into immorality and idolatry at Peor "on Balaam's advice" (Num 31:16; see Rev 2:14). So Balaam became a type: one who sells his faith for money and leads God's people into sin.
- **The donkey speaks:** the angel of the LORD stood in the road with a sword; the donkey saw him, but Balaam did not and struck her three times, until "the LORD opened the mouth of the donkey" (Num 22:28). This is Peter's irony: men who think they see spiritual mysteries are so blinded by greed that they see less clearly than a donkey.

> The point: The mark of false teachers is often not their eloquence or gifts, but their eyes and hearts: eyes full of lust, hearts full of greed, bait in their hands. The church must protect those who are easily harmed and must not cover things up. Yet we must also ask whether Balaam's greed is in our own hearts. If it is, we confess it and repent. What we need is the good shepherd who, not for gain but for us, laid down his life (John 10:11).

## Going Deeper: 2:17–22 They Promise Freedom but Are Slaves

**1. Waterless springs.** "These are waterless springs and mists driven by a storm. For them the gloom of utter darkness has been reserved" (2:17). In dry country a traveler sees a spring from far off and hurries toward it full of hope, only to find it dry. That is the hope false teachers give: fine words without one drop of living water. They speak "loud boasts of folly" (2:18) that sound powerful but are empty.

**2. Whom do they target?** "They entice by sensual passions of the flesh those who are barely escaping from those who live in error" (2:18). They look for new believers just breaking free from their old life, so the church must take special care of new believers and protect them.

**3. "They promise them freedom."** "They promise them freedom, but they themselves are slaves of corruption. For whatever overcomes a person, to that he is enslaved" (2:19).
- **What kind of freedom?** The false teachers probably said: since you are free in Christ, you are no longer bound by moral rules; since there is no judgment, you may live as you please. They may also have twisted Paul's teaching on grace (3:16; see Rom 6:1). Paul himself said, "you were called to freedom, brothers. Only do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh" (Gal 5:13).
- **"Whatever overcomes a person, to that he is enslaved":** this was an ancient proverb, and also the Lord Jesus' teaching: "everyone who commits sin is a slave to sin" (John 8:34). Those who say "I want to be free" and indulge their desires end up bound by those desires, unable to stop. True freedom is not doing whatever one likes, but being released from sin's rule: "if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed" (John 8:36).

**4. The last state.** "For if, after they have escaped the defilements of the world through the knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they are again entangled in them and overcome, the last state has become worse for them than the first. For it would have been better for them never to have known the way of righteousness than after knowing it to turn back from the holy commandment delivered to them" (2:20–21).
- **"The last state has become worse":** these are almost the Lord Jesus' own words about the unclean spirit that returns (Matt 12:45).
- **Two proverbs:** "The dog returns to its own vomit, and the sow, after washing herself, returns to wallow in the mire" (2:22). The first comes from Proverbs: "Like a dog that returns to his vomit is a fool who repeats his folly" (Prov 26:11). The second is not in the Old Testament. To Jews, dogs and pigs were both unclean animals.

**5. Were these people ever truly saved?** Believers in every age ask this when they reach these verses. Peter says they had "the knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ" (the same word for "knowledge" as in 1:2–3, 8), had "escaped the defilements of the world," and had "known the way of righteousness." Several views:
- **The Reformed tradition (for example, the *Westminster Confession*, chapter 17, on the perseverance of the saints):** those truly born again will be kept by God to the end (John 10:28; 6:37). These people knew the gospel outwardly, and their lives changed for a time, but their hearts were never renewed. They argue that the proverbs illustrate the point: the dog is still a dog, the washed sow still a sow; their nature never changed. John says, "They went out from us, but they were not of us... they went out, that it might become plain that they all are not of us" (1 John 2:19). Some Reformed scholars (for example, Schreiner) add that, while these false teachers were not truly regenerate, such warnings are real for genuine believers who read them, and God uses these very warnings to keep them to the end.
- **Arminian and Wesleyan (Lutherans also hold that true believers can lose salvation, on different grounds):** these people truly knew the Lord and were truly saved, but later fell away and lost their salvation. On this view "knowledge" in this letter is saving knowledge (1:3), and the warning is real, or it would lose its force. They also often cite Hebrews 6:4–6.

All sides agree on several things: to know the truth and turn from it is extremely serious; and this chapter is spoken about false teachers and those who follow them, not to drive weak, struggling believers who have stumbled into despair. At the end of the letter Peter calls his readers "beloved," warns them to take care, and urges them to grow in grace and knowledge (3:17–18). A person who grieves over his sin and fears drifting from the Lord is very different from those who speak "loud boasts of folly" and entice others.

> The point: The freedom false teachers promise ends in bondage; only the freedom the Son of God gives is true freedom. Reading these stern words, we need not guess whether others were truly saved; we come back ourselves to the Lord who truly sets people free. Of his sheep he says, "they will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of my hand" (John 10:28).

## Meditation

**Meditation:** "And if he rescued righteous Lot, greatly distressed by the sensual conduct of the wicked" (2:7). Lot did not move into Sodom all at once. First he set his eyes on the "well watered" valley, then he "moved his tent as far as Sodom" (Gen 13:10–12), and later he sat in the city gate (Gen 19:1), though the townsmen still called him a man who "came to sojourn" (Gen 19:9). He grieved over the city's sin day after day (2:8), and he stayed there day after day. I have places like that too: a business kept going by kickbacks and false accounts, a relationship I know is wrong but cannot bring myself to end, a habit I have long wanted to leave and keep going back to. My heart grieves, but the grief has not made me leave. (If I am not clinging to sin but trapped in someone else's harm, that is not Lot's lingering; leaving danger, asking for help, and where needed calling the police are right.)

What astonishes me most is Genesis 19:16. The angels had already said the city would be destroyed, and still "he lingered." Scripture does not say he finally made up his mind. It says: "So the men seized him and his wife and his two daughters by the hand, the LORD being merciful to him, and they brought him out and set him outside the city." Lot was brought out of Sodom not by his resolve, but by a hand that took hold of his, and by the LORD's mercy. Lot's story does not end happily outside the city; he fell again in the cave (Gen 19:30–36), and Scripture does not hide it. Yet nearly two thousand years later Peter, moved by the Spirit, looked back on him and called him "righteous" three times. This is not because Lot deserved it; that he belonged to God at all was grace.

I am counted righteous not because I am steadier than Lot. That day fire fell from heaven on Sodom while Lot stood outside the city. Later the Lord Jesus "went out, bearing his own cross" (John 19:17), and "suffered outside the gate in order to sanctify the people through his own blood" (Heb 13:12). Lot was brought outside the city so the fire of judgment would not fall on him; Jesus went outside the city so that God's judgment would fall on himself. "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (2 Cor 5:21). If I too am lingering, if lingering has already cost me much, as Lot in the end lost his home, his sons-in-law would not come, and his wife never reached safety, the Lord still "knows how to rescue the godly" (2:9). That is no permission to keep lingering. Leaving may also mean making restitution. Zacchaeus said "if I have defrauded anyone of anything, I restore it fourfold" (Luke 19:8) only after Jesus had said "I must stay at your house today" (19:5). Where am I lingering today, and what is it there that my heart still cannot let go of? If I leave, it will not be because I am finally steady enough. The Lord knows where I am and knows how to bring me out; the One who bore judgment for me outside the gate will not leave me in Sodom.
