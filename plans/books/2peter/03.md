## 本章脉络

第三章是彼得后书的最后一章，也是彼得留给教会最后的话，可以分成四段。**提醒与讥诮**（1-7节）：彼得说这是他写的第二封信，要提醒读者记念先知的话和主借着使徒所传的命令；末世必有好讥诮的人，问「主要降临的应许在哪里呢」，可是他们故意忘记：天地是凭神的命造成的，当时的世界也曾被洪水淹没，现在的天地也是凭着同一个命存留，等候火的审判。**主的宽容与主的日子**（8-10节）：主看一日如千年，千年如一日；他不是耽延，乃是宽容，不愿有一人沉沦，乃愿人人都悔改；但主的日子必要像贼来到一样。**新天新地**（11-16节）：既然这一切都要销化，我们为人该当怎样圣洁敬虔，仰望神的日子来到；我们照他的应许，盼望有义居在其中的新天新地；要以主长久的忍耐为得救的因由，正如亲爱的兄弟保罗信上所写的。**最后的劝勉**（17-18节）：要防备，免得被恶人的错谬诱惑；要在主耶稣基督的恩典和知识上有长进；愿荣耀归给他。

假师傅（第二章）和讥诮的人（第三章）说的是同一件事：主不会再来，审判不会临到，人可以随心所欲地活。彼得的回答很简单：神说过的话从来没有落空过。那看起来像「耽延」的，其实是怜悯。

## 深读 3:1-7 讥诮的人与神的话

**1. 「第二封信」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，我现在写给你们的是第二封信。这两封都是提醒你们，激发你们诚实的心，叫你们记念圣先知预先所说的话和主救主的命令，就是使徒所传给你们的。」（3:1-2）
- **哪一封是第一封？** 多数学者认为第一封就是彼得前书。也有人认为是一封已经失传的信，因为彼得前书讲苦难多，讲主再来比较少；不过彼得前书也多次讲到基督的「显现」（彼前1:7、13；4:13），也讲到「众先知」预先考察基督受苦和得荣耀的事（彼前1:10-11）。参看本书导读。
- **「提醒」：** 彼得在第一章已经说过，他知道自己快要离世，所以要「常常提醒」读者（1:12-13）。他要说的不是新的道理，而是读者早已领受、却容易忘记的真理。
- **先知和使徒并列：** 彼得把旧约「圣先知」的话和主借着「使徒」所传的命令放在一起，两者都有神的权柄。教会的根基，就是这一个整全的神的话。

**2. 「好讥诮的人」。** 「第一要紧的，该知道在末世必有好讥诮的人随从自己的私欲出来讥诮说：『主要降临的应许在哪里呢？因为从列祖睡了以来，万物与起初创造的时候仍是一样。』」（3:3-4）
- **「末世」：** 新约所说的「末世」，是从基督第一次来就开始了（来1:2）。这些讥诮的人不是遥远将来的人，彼得的读者已经遇见他们。犹大书也引用使徒的话说：「末世必有好讥诮的人随从自己不敬虔的私欲而行。」（犹18）
- **「随从自己的私欲」：** 讥诮的根源往往不在头脑，而在心里。如果主不再来，就没有审判；没有审判，人就可以照自己的意思活。人不愿意有审判，就容易说服自己审判不会来。
- **「主要降临的应许」：** 「降临」和1:16「他降临的事」原文是同一个词。假师傅说彼得所传的是「乖巧捏造的虚言」，讥诮的人则说这应许已经过期了。

**3. 「从列祖睡了以来」。** 「列祖」是指谁？
- **第一代信徒：** 有学者（如理查德·包衡）认为是指已经去世的第一代基督徒。他们原以为主会在自己有生之年再来，可是他们一个一个都「睡了」，主却还没有来。即使按这种看法，到六十年代中期，司提反、雅各等第一代信徒也已经去世了。
- **旧约的列祖：** 也有学者（如施莱纳）认为是指旧约的列祖，因为新约说「列祖」通常是指以色列的先祖（来1:1；罗9:5）。照这个看法，讥诮的人是说：从亚伯拉罕的时代到如今，世界一直都是这样，神从来没有介入过。
- **无论是哪一种：** 讥诮的人的论点是一样的：世界照着自己的规律运转，过去怎样，将来也怎样，神不会插手。

**4. 「他们故意忘记」。** 「他们故意忘记，从太古，凭神的命有了天，并从水而出、藉水而成的地。故此，当时的世界被水淹没就消灭了。但现在的天地还是凭着那命存留，直留到不敬虔之人受审判遭沉沦的日子，用火焚烧。」（3:5-7）
- **「故意忘记」：** 他们不是不知道，而是不愿意记得。这正与彼得所说的「提醒」（3:1）相对。
- **神的命：** 天地是凭着神的命造成的，地是「从水而出」的（创1:2、9）。万物并不是自己存在、自己运转的；从一开始，万物就倚靠神的话。
- **洪水：** 讥诮的人说「万物……仍是一样」，可是神已经介入过一次了。挪亚的时候，「当时的世界被水淹没就消灭了」。彼得在2:5已经讲过洪水，主耶稣也说：「挪亚的日子怎样，人子降临也要怎样。」（太24:37）那时的人也是照常吃喝嫁娶，「不知不觉洪水来了」（太24:39）。
- **「用火焚烧」：** 神曾应许不再用洪水毁灭地（创9:11），彼得说下一次的审判是用火。造天地的是神的话，存留天地的也是同一个话；审判什么时候来，也由这话决定。彼得在这里不是要讲一套宇宙论，而是说：历史不是一个封闭的机器，而是在神的话的掌管之下。

> 要点：讥诮的人说「万物与起初创造的时候仍是一样」，其实是不愿意面对审判。可是天地是凭神的话造成的，也曾被神的话审判过；同一个话也在保存现在的天地，等候主的日子。神说过的话从来没有落空过。

## 深读 3:8-10 主的宽容与主的日子

**1. 「一日如千年」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，有一件事你们不可忘记，就是主看一日如千年，千年如一日。」（3:8）
- **引诗篇90篇：** 「在你看来，千年如已过的昨日，又如夜间的一更。」（诗90:4）这是摩西的祷告。在永活的神看来，人觉得很长的时间并不长。
- **讥诮的人「故意忘记」，信徒却「不可忘记」：** 原文第5节和第8节用的是同一个词。
- **不是一个换算公式：** 有人用这节经文推算创造的六日各是一千年，或者推算世界历史只有六千年，或者推算千禧年什么时候开始。这都不是彼得的意思。他的意思是：神的时间和我们的不一样。在我们看来是长久的耽延，在神看来并不长；反过来说，神在一日之间，也能成就千年的事。

**2. 「其实不是耽延，乃是宽容你们」。** 「主所应许的尚未成就，有人以为他是耽延，其实不是耽延，乃是宽容你们，不愿有一人沉沦，乃愿人人都悔改。」（3:9）
- **「宽容」：** 这个词也可以译作「忍耐」「恒久忍耐」。神迟迟不施行审判，不是因为他软弱，也不是因为他忘记了，而是因为他有怜悯。
- **挪亚的日子：** 彼得前书也说，挪亚预备方舟的时候，是「神容忍等待的时候」（彼前3:20）。洪水来以前，神等了很久；最后审判来以前，神也在等。
- **「宽容你们」：** 神的忍耐首先是向着读者的。保罗也说：「还是你藐视他丰富的恩慈、宽容、忍耐，不晓得他的恩慈是领你悔改呢？」（罗2:4）

**3. 「不愿有一人沉沦」。** 这句话怎样理解，历代信徒有不同的看法：
- **理解之一，指所有人，人可以抗拒（阿民念／卫斯理传统）：** 有学者（如约翰·卫斯理、霍华德·马歇尔）认为，「一人」和「人人」是指世上所有的人。神真心愿意每一个人都得救，可是人可以抗拒他的恩典。他们也引用「他愿意万人得救，明白真道」（提前2:4）。
- **理解之二，指选民（改革宗）：** 有学者（如约翰·欧文）认为，这里的「人人」是指上文的「你们」，就是神的选民，或者所有将要信主的人。神等候，是要等到他所有的子民都悔改归向他。
- **理解之三，指所有人，分辨两种旨意（改革宗）：** 有学者（如加尔文、约翰·派博、施莱纳）认为，「人人」确实是指所有的人，但要分辨神所喜悦的旨意和他所预定的旨意。神确实不喜悦恶人死亡（结33:11），他向所有人发出的悔改呼召是真诚的；但神没有选择要拯救每一个人。派博称这为神里面的两种旨意。
- **共同的地方：** 无论哪一种看法，都同意：神「断不喜悦恶人死亡」（结33:11）；没有一个人沉沦是因为神乐意看见他沉沦；神的耽延是怜悯，他的悔改呼召是真诚的。

**4. 「主的日子要像贼来到一样」。** 「但主的日子要像贼来到一样。那日，天必大有响声废去，有形质的都要被烈火销化，地和其上的物都要烧尽了。」（3:10）
- **「像贼来到一样」：** 主耶稣也用过这个比喻（太24:43），保罗也说「主的日子来到，好像夜间的贼一样」（帖前5:2）。重点不是秘密地来，而是出人意料地来。神的宽容不是没有期限的。
- **「有形质的」：** 原文这个词可以指构成万物的元素，也可以指日月星辰等天体，英文标准版取后一个意思。
- **「都要烧尽了」：** 这里有一个经文异文。和合本所根据的古卷作「都要烧尽了」；可是许多最早的古卷（如西乃抄本、梵蒂冈抄本）作「都要被发现」，意思是都要显露出来，在神面前无可遮掩，英文标准版就是这样译的；最早的蒲草纸抄本P72也有类似的读法，亚历山大抄本和大多数较晚的抄本则作「烧尽了」。保罗也说，那日子要用火把各人的工程「表明出来」（林前3:13）。无论是哪一种读法，那日都是神审判的日子，没有什么能逃过他的鉴察。

> 要点：神的时间和我们的不一样。他看起来迟延，其实是在忍耐，因为他不愿意有人沉沦。可是这忍耐有一天会结束；主的日子必要像贼一样，出人意料地来到。

## 深读 3:11-16 新天新地

**1. 「你们为人该当怎样圣洁，怎样敬虔」。** 「这一切既然都要如此销化，你们为人该当怎样圣洁，怎样敬虔，切切仰望神的日子来到。」（3:11-12）
- **末世论带出生活：** 彼得讲主的日子，不是要满足人的好奇心，而是要改变人现在的生活。眼前的一切都要过去，那么什么是值得为它而活的？
- **不是出于惧怕：** 彼得不是用末日的恐怖来吓唬人，因为下一节马上说，我们是照着神的应许「盼望」那日子。信徒圣洁的生活，是从盼望里长出来的。

**2. 「切切仰望神的日子来到」。** 英文标准版把这句译作等候并催促神的日子来到。
- **「催促」：** 原文这个词可以指加速，也可以指热切地渴望。和合本把「仰望」和「催促」合译成「切切仰望」，取的是热切渴望的意思；英文标准版取的是加速的意思。
- **怎样「加速」？** 许多人认为，信徒的悔改、祷告和传福音，是神用来成就他计划的方法。彼得在五旬节以后也说：「所以，你们当悔改归正，使你们的罪得以涂抹，这样，那安舒的日子就必从主面前来到。」（徒3:19-20）主耶稣也说：「这天国的福音要传遍天下，对万民作见证，然后末期才来到。」（太24:14）我们每次祷告「愿你的国降临」，也是在仰望那一天。
- **但不是我们决定日期：** 「那日子，那时辰，没有人知道……惟独父知道。」（太24:36）神使用我们的祷告和见证，日期却在他手中。

**3. 「新天新地，有义居在其中」。** 「但我们照他的应许，盼望新天新地，有义居在其中。」（3:13）
- **「照他的应许」：** 神藉着以赛亚说：「看哪！我造新天新地；从前的事不再被记念，也不再追想。」（赛65:17；参66:22）约翰在异象中也看见「一个新天新地」（启21:1）。
- **「有义居在其中」：** 讥诮的人所留恋的世界，是一个不义可以长存的世界；信徒所盼望的世界，是义住在其中的世界。今天的不义、欺压、谎言，在那里都不再有。

**4. 是毁灭后重造，还是更新？** 对3:10-13，历代信徒有两种看法：
- **毁灭后重造：** 有些人（如十七世纪的路德宗神学家约翰·格哈德）认为，现在的天地要完全毁灭，神要另造一个全新的天地。
- **更新：** 许多人（如加尔文、巴文克、胡克玛）认为，火是炼净，不是消灭；神要更新这个受造的世界。他们指出，挪亚的时候「当时的世界……就消灭了」（3:6），地却没有消失；保罗也说「受造之物仍然指望脱离败坏的辖制，得享神儿女自由的荣耀」（罗8:21）。3:10「都要被发现」的读法，也常被用来支持这种看法。
- **无论是哪一种：** 信徒的盼望不是灵魂飘在云端，而是一个真实的新世界，神和他的子民住在一起，义住在其中。

**5. 「安然见主」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，你们既盼望这些事，就当殷勤，使自己没有玷污，无可指摘，安然见主。」（3:14）
- **「没有玷污，无可指摘」：** 这叫人想起彼得前书说的基督：「如同无瑕疵、无玷污的羔羊」（彼前1:19）。假师傅却「已被玷污，又有瑕疵」（2:13），信徒却要像他们的主。
- **先是恩典：** 能使我们「无瑕无疵」站在神面前的，是神自己（犹24），是基督的宝血。正因为这已经是神在基督里赐给我们的，我们才「殷勤」地追求，好叫我们的生活与这身份相称。
- **「见主」：** 原文直译是被他发现，和3:10「都要被发现」的读法用的是同一个动词。
- **「安然」：** 原文是在平安中。那日子来的时候，信徒不是惊慌失措地见主，而是平平安安地见他，因为他们已经与神和好了。

**6. 「要以我主长久忍耐为得救的因由」。** 「并且要以我主长久忍耐为得救的因由，就如我们所亲爱的兄弟保罗，照着所赐给他的智慧写了信给你们。」（3:15）
- **两种「看法」：** 3:9说「有人以为他是耽延」，这里的「以……为」原文和那里的「以为」是同一个词。讥诮的人把主的忍耐看作耽延，甚至看作主不会再来的证据；信徒却把同样的忍耐看作救恩。
- **「我们所亲爱的兄弟保罗」：** 保罗曾经在安提阿当面抵挡彼得（加2:11）。多年以后，彼得称他为「我们所亲爱的兄弟」。可见那次责备没有破坏他们在福音里的相交；福音的真理使他们和好，也使他们同工。

**7. 「如强解别的经书一样」。** 「他一切的信上也都是讲论这事。信中有些难明白的，那无学问、不坚固的人强解，如强解别的经书一样，就自取沉沦。」（3:16）
- **「有些难明白的」：** 连使徒彼得也承认，保罗的信有些地方不容易明白。这对今天读圣经觉得吃力的人是一种安慰：难懂不表示读错了，而是要谦卑、耐心地读。
- **强解保罗：** 彼得没有说是哪些教训被强解。许多人认为，假师傅可能把保罗所讲的恩典和自由，曲解成放纵的理由（参2:19「他们应许人得以自由」；罗6:1「我们可以仍在罪中、叫恩典显多吗？」）。
- **「别的经书」：** 彼得把保罗的信和「别的经书」放在一起，就是把保罗的信看作圣经。这是新约里最早承认使徒书信有圣经权威的话之一。（有学者以此认为彼得后书写成得较晚，参看本书导读。）
- **「自取沉沦」：** 强解圣经不是小事。读圣经的目的不是叫圣经说我们想听的话，而是让神藉着圣经对我们说话。

> 要点：信徒所盼望的，是神所应许、义住在其中的新天新地；这盼望使我们现在就追求圣洁。那位能使我们无瑕无疵站在他面前的，是基督自己。讥诮的人把主的忍耐当作耽延，信徒却把它当作救恩。

## 深读 3:17-18 在恩典和知识上长进

**1. 「就当防备」。** 「亲爱的弟兄啊，你们既然预先知道这事，就当防备，恐怕被恶人的错谬诱惑，就从自己坚固的地步上坠落。」（3:17）
- **「预先知道」：** 彼得在这封信里所写的，就是要叫读者事先有准备，不至于被假师傅和讥诮的人吓倒，也不至于被他们带走。
- **「坚固的地步」：** 「坚固」和1:12「在你们已有的真道上坚固」原文是同一个字根；3:16「不坚固的人」也是同一个字根：强解圣经的是不坚固的人，信徒却要守住坚固的地步。主耶稣曾经对彼得说：「但我已经为你祈求，叫你不至于失了信心。你回头以后，要坚固你的弟兄。」（路22:32）这封信，就是那个曾经跌倒、又被主扶起来的彼得，在坚固他的弟兄。

**2. 「要在……恩典和知识上有长进」。** 「你们却要在我们主－救主耶稣基督的恩典和知识上有长进。」（3:18）
- **对付错谬最好的方法是长进：** 彼得没有只说「要小心」，而是说「要长进」。只会防守的信心是脆弱的；在基督里扎根长大的信心，才站得稳。
- **先是恩典：** 在恩典上长进，不是越来越不需要恩典，而是越来越倚靠恩典，越来越享受恩典。
- **认识一个人：** 这里的「知识」不只是知道一些道理，而是认识「我们主－救主耶稣基督」这一位。全书开头说「愿恩惠、平安，因你们认识神和我们主耶稣，多多地加给你们」（1:2），结尾又回到恩典和认识主。

**3. 「愿荣耀归给他」。** 「愿荣耀归给他，从今直到永远。阿们！」（3:18）
- **荣耀归给基督：** 神说：「我必不将我的荣耀归给假神。」（赛42:8；「假神」原文是「别的」，英文标准版也译作别的）彼得却把荣耀归给耶稣基督。全书开头称他为「我们的神和救主耶稣基督」（1:1），结尾向他献上只有神才配得的颂赞。耶稣基督是真神。
- **「从今直到永远」：** 原文直译是直到永远的日子。本章讲了许多「主的日子」；有学者认为，彼得最后是说，那日子来到以后，就是一个永不结束的日子。

> 要点：防备错谬最好的方法，是在基督的恩典和对他的认识上不断长进。彼得最后的话，是把荣耀归给耶稣基督：他就是我们的神和救主。

## 默想

**默想：** 「主所应许的尚未成就，有人以为他是耽延，其实不是耽延，乃是宽容你们……并且要以我主长久忍耐为得救的因由。」（3:9、15）我也常常觉得主来得太慢。看到新闻里欺压弱者的人一直没有受到惩罚，看到自己身边的不公平，看到这个世界的病痛、战争和眼泪，我心里也会像启示录里的殉道者那样问：「圣洁真实的主啊……要等到几时呢？」（启6:10）有时候我说的更具体：那个伤害我的人，为什么还好好的？这样问并没有错，殉道者也这样问，神没有责备他们。可是彼得叫我换一个角度看这件事：我所埋怨的这份「迟延」，正是曾经等过我的那份忍耐。如果主在我信他以前的那一年就来了，我会站在哪一边？主耶稣讲过一个比喻：一棵无花果树三年不结果子，园主说「把它砍了吧」，管园的却说：「主啊，今年且留着。」（路13:7-8）这忍耐是有期限的（路13:9）。我就是那棵树。在我还不认识他、甚至讥笑他的日子，他没有砍；那时候我还以为一切「仍是一样」，以为什么都不会改变。原来那是他在等我。然而神的忍耐不是不在乎不义。保罗说，神「用忍耐的心宽容人先时所犯的罪」，是「好在今时显明他的义」（罗3:25-26）：神可以等候罪人悔改，又不失他的公义，因为他已经在十字架上把罪的刑罚放在他儿子身上。「他为我们的过犯受害，为我们的罪孽压伤。」（赛53:5）所以，主的忍耐既不是软弱，也不是不公平。每一件不义的事，最后不是在主的日子受审判（3:7），就是已经在十字架上被基督担当了——为着一切悔改、信靠他的人；神「称信耶稣的人为义」（罗3:26）。如果我是受了伤害的人，神的等候不表示他不在乎我所受的；他记得。圣经说「不要自己伸冤，宁可让步，听凭主怒」（罗12:19），可是今天向警察或法院举报犯罪，也仍然是对的（罗13:4）；等候主的日子，不表示我要沉默。可是当我心里发急的时候，我可以想起：那位迟迟没有来审判世界的主，当年却没有迟迟不去十字架；他为我受审判，没有一天耽延。我盼望他来；等我见到他的那一天，我会知道，他从来没有来迟。他宽容的那些年日里，也包括了我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 is the last chapter of 2 Peter, and Peter's last words to the church. It falls into four parts. **Reminder and scoffers** (vv. 1–7): Peter says this is the second letter he has written, to remind his readers of the predictions of the prophets and the commandment of the Lord through the apostles; in the last days scoffers will come asking, "Where is the promise of his coming?", but they deliberately overlook that the heavens and earth were made by God's word, that the world then existing was deluged with water, and that by the same word the present heavens and earth are stored up for fire. **The Lord's patience and the day of the Lord** (vv. 8–10): with the Lord one day is as a thousand years and a thousand years as one day; he is not slow but patient, not wishing that any should perish but that all should reach repentance; but the day of the Lord will come like a thief. **New heavens and a new earth** (vv. 11–16): since all these things are to be dissolved, what sort of people ought we to be in holiness and godliness, waiting for the day of God; according to his promise we wait for new heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells; count the patience of our Lord as salvation, as our beloved brother Paul also wrote. **Final appeal** (vv. 17–18): take care not to be carried away by the error of lawless people; grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ; to him be the glory.

The false teachers (chapter 2) and the scoffers (chapter 3) say the same thing: the Lord will not come back, judgment will not come, and people may live as they please. Peter's answer is simple: what God has said has never failed. What looks like "slowness" is in fact mercy.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–7 The Scoffers and the Word of God

**1. "The second letter."** "This is now the second letter that I am writing to you, beloved. In both of them I am stirring up your sincere mind by way of reminder, that you should remember the predictions of the holy prophets and the commandment of the Lord and Savior through your apostles" (3:1–2).
- **Which is the first letter?** Most scholars think the first letter is 1 Peter. Some think it is a letter now lost, because 1 Peter says much about suffering and less about the Lord's return; yet 1 Peter does speak several times of Christ's "revelation" (1 Pet 1:7, 13; 4:13), and of the prophets who searched out Christ's sufferings and glories (1 Pet 1:10–11). See the introduction to this book.
- **"Reminder":** Peter has already said in chapter 1 that he knows he will soon die, and so he intends "always to remind" his readers (1:12–13). He is not bringing new teaching, but truth they have already received and easily forget.
- **Prophets and apostles side by side:** Peter sets the words of the Old Testament's "holy prophets" beside the commandment of the Lord given "through your apostles"; both carry God's authority. The church stands on this one, whole word of God.

**2. "Scoffers."** "Knowing this first of all, that scoffers will come in the last days with scoffing, following their own sinful desires. They will say, 'Where is the promise of his coming? For ever since the fathers fell asleep, all things are continuing as they were from the beginning of creation'" (3:3–4).
- **"The last days":** in the New Testament, the last days began with Christ's first coming (Heb 1:2). These scoffers are not people of a distant future; Peter's readers have already met them. Jude also quotes the apostles: "In the last time there will be scoffers, following their own ungodly passions" (Jude 18).
- **"Following their own sinful desires":** the root of scoffing usually lies not in the head but in the heart. If the Lord does not come again, there is no judgment; if there is no judgment, people may live as they like. Those who do not want a judgment find it easy to persuade themselves it will not come.
- **"The promise of his coming":** "coming" is the same Greek word as "the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ" in 1:16. The false teachers called Peter's message "cleverly devised myths"; the scoffers say the promise has expired.

**3. "Ever since the fathers fell asleep."** Who are "the fathers"?
- **The first Christian generation:** some scholars (for example, Richard Bauckham) take them to be the first generation of Christians, who had died. They expected the Lord to return in their lifetime, but one by one they "fell asleep," and the Lord had not come. Even on this view, by the mid-60s Stephen, James and others of the first generation had already died.
- **The Old Testament fathers:** others (for example, Thomas Schreiner) take them to be the Old Testament patriarchs, since in the New Testament "the fathers" usually means Israel's ancestors (Heb 1:1; Rom 9:5). On this view the scoffers are saying: from Abraham's day until now the world has always been like this, and God has never stepped in.
- **Either way,** the scoffers' argument is the same: the world runs by its own laws; as it was, so it will be; God does not intervene.

**4. "They deliberately overlook."** "For they deliberately overlook this fact, that the heavens existed long ago, and the earth was formed out of water and through water by the word of God, and that by means of these the world that then existed was deluged with water and perished. But by the same word the heavens and earth that now exist are stored up for fire, being kept until the day of judgment and destruction of the ungodly" (3:5–7).
- **"Deliberately overlook":** it is not that they do not know, but that they do not want to remember. This stands against Peter's "reminder" (3:1).
- **The word of God:** the heavens and earth were made by God's word, and the earth was formed "out of water" (Gen 1:2, 9). The world does not exist or run on its own; from the start, all things have depended on God's word.
- **The flood:** the scoffers say "all things are continuing as they were," yet God has stepped in once already. In Noah's day "the world that then existed was deluged with water and perished." Peter has already spoken of the flood in 2:5, and Jesus said, "As were the days of Noah, so will be the coming of the Son of Man" (Matt 24:37). People then were eating and drinking and marrying, "and they were unaware until the flood came" (Matt 24:39).
- **"Stored up for fire":** God promised never again to destroy the earth by flood (Gen 9:11); Peter says the next judgment will be by fire. The word that made the heavens and earth is the same word that keeps them, and that same word decides when judgment will come. Peter is not offering a cosmology here; he is saying that history is not a closed machine but stands under the rule of God's word.

> The point: when the scoffers say "all things are continuing as they were from the beginning of creation," they are refusing to face judgment. But the heavens and earth were made by God's word and have already been judged by it once; the same word now keeps the present heavens and earth until the day of the Lord. What God has said has never failed.

## Going Deeper: 3:8–10 The Lord's Patience and the Day of the Lord

**1. "One day is as a thousand years."** "But do not overlook this one fact, beloved, that with the Lord one day is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day" (3:8).
- **Echoing Psalm 90:** "For a thousand years in your sight are but as yesterday when it is past, or as a watch in the night" (Ps 90:4). This is a prayer of Moses. To the everlasting God, what seems long to us is not long.
- **The scoffers "deliberately overlook"; believers must "not overlook":** verses 5 and 8 use the same Greek word.
- **Not a conversion formula:** some have used this verse to make each day of creation a thousand years, or to calculate that world history lasts six thousand years, or to date the start of the millennium. None of this is Peter's point. He means that God's time is not our time. What seems to us a long delay is not long to God; and the other way round, God can do in one day what would take a thousand years.

**2. "Not slow... but patient toward you."** "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance" (3:9).
- **"Patient":** the word can also be rendered "forbearing" or "long-suffering." God's delay in judging is not weakness or forgetfulness, but mercy.
- **The days of Noah:** 1 Peter also says that before the flood "God's patience waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared" (1 Pet 3:20). God waited long before the flood; he is waiting before the final judgment too.
- **"Toward you":** God's patience is first of all directed at the readers. Paul also writes: "Or do you presume on the riches of his kindness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that God's kindness is meant to lead you to repentance?" (Rom 2:4).

**3. "Not wishing that any should perish."** Believers have read this in different ways:
- **First reading: all people, who can resist (Arminian/Wesleyan):** some (for example, John Wesley and I. Howard Marshall) take "any" and "all" to mean every person in the world. God sincerely wants every person to be saved, but people can resist his grace. They also cite "who desires all people to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth" (1 Tim 2:4).
- **Second reading: the elect (Reformed):** some (for example, John Owen) take "all" to refer back to "you," that is, God's elect, all who will come to faith. God waits until all his people have turned to him.
- **Third reading: all people, two wills distinguished (Reformed):** some (for example, Calvin, John Piper and Thomas Schreiner) take "all" to mean all people, but distinguish what God delights in from what he has decreed. God truly has "no pleasure in the death of the wicked" (Ezek 33:11), and his call to all to repent is sincere; yet God has not chosen to save every person. Piper calls this "two wills in God."
- **Common ground:** all these readings agree that God has "no pleasure in the death of the wicked" (Ezek 33:11); that no one perishes because God was glad to see it; that his delay is mercy and his call to repent is sincere.

**4. "The day of the Lord will come like a thief."** "But the day of the Lord will come like a thief, and then the heavens will pass away with a roar, and the heavenly bodies will be burned up and dissolved, and the earth and the works that are done on it will be exposed" (3:10).
- **"Like a thief":** Jesus used this picture (Matt 24:43), and Paul wrote, "the day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night" (1 Thess 5:2). The point is not that it comes secretly but that it comes unexpectedly. God's patience is not without end.
- **"Heavenly bodies":** the Greek word can mean the "elements" that make up the world, or the sun, moon and stars (so the ESV, "heavenly bodies").
- **"Exposed":** there is a textual variant here. The manuscripts behind the Chinese Union Version read "will be burned up"; many of the earliest manuscripts (such as Sinaiticus and Vaticanus) read "will be found," that is, laid bare before God with nothing hidden (so the ESV, "exposed"); the early papyrus P72 has a form of "found," while Alexandrinus and most later manuscripts read "burned up." Paul also says the Day will "disclose" each one's work by fire (1 Cor 3:13). Either way, that day is the day of God's judgment, and nothing will escape it.

> The point: God's time is not ours. He seems slow, but he is being patient, because he does not wish anyone to perish. Yet this patience will one day end; the day of the Lord will come, unexpectedly, like a thief.

## Going Deeper: 3:11–16 New Heavens and a New Earth

**1. "What sort of people ought you to be."** "Since all these things are thus to be dissolved, what sort of people ought you to be in lives of holiness and godliness, waiting for and hastening the coming of the day of God" (3:11–12).
- **Hope shapes life:** Peter speaks of the day of the Lord not to satisfy curiosity but to change how we live now. If everything before our eyes will pass away, what is worth living for?
- **Not from fear:** Peter is not frightening us with the terrors of the end, for the next verse says we are "waiting for" that day according to God's promise. A believer's holy life grows out of hope.

**2. "Waiting for and hastening the coming of the day of God."**
- **"Hastening":** the Greek word can mean "speeding up" or "earnestly desiring." The Chinese Union Version merges the two verbs into 「切切仰望」 ("earnestly looking for"), taking the "eager desire" sense; the ESV takes the "speeding up" sense.
- **How could we "hasten" it?** Many think that believers' repentance, prayer and preaching of the gospel are means God uses to accomplish his plan. After Pentecost Peter said: "Repent therefore, and turn again, that your sins may be blotted out, that times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord" (Acts 3:19–20). Jesus also said: "This gospel of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the whole world as a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come" (Matt 24:14). Each time we pray "your kingdom come," we are looking for that day.
- **But we do not set the date:** "concerning that day and hour no one knows... but the Father only" (Matt 24:36). God uses our prayer and witness; the date is in his hands.

**3. "New heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells."** "But according to his promise we are waiting for new heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells" (3:13).
- **"According to his promise":** God said through Isaiah, "For behold, I create new heavens and a new earth, and the former things shall not be remembered or come into mind" (Isa 65:17; see 66:22). John in his vision also saw "a new heaven and a new earth" (Rev 21:1).
- **"In which righteousness dwells":** the world the scoffers cling to is one where injustice can last; the world believers hope for is one where righteousness is at home. Today's injustice, oppression and lies will have no place there.

**4. Destroyed and remade, or renewed?** Believers have read 3:10–13 in two ways:
- **Destroyed and remade:** some (for example, the seventeenth-century Lutheran theologian Johann Gerhard) hold that the present heavens and earth will be wholly destroyed and God will create an entirely new one.
- **Renewed:** many (for example, Calvin, Herman Bavinck and Anthony Hoekema) hold that the fire purifies rather than annihilates, and God will renew this created world. They point out that in Noah's day "the world that then existed... perished" (3:6), yet the earth did not vanish; and Paul says "the creation itself will be set free from its bondage to decay and obtain the freedom of the glory of the children of God" (Rom 8:21). The "found" reading of 3:10 is often cited in support of this view.
- **Either way,** the believer's hope is not souls floating in the clouds but a real new world, where God lives with his people and righteousness dwells.

**5. "At peace."** "Therefore, beloved, since you are waiting for these, be diligent to be found by him without spot or blemish, and at peace" (3:14).
- **"Without spot or blemish":** this recalls 1 Peter's description of Christ, "a lamb without blemish or spot" (1 Pet 1:19). The false teachers are "blots and blemishes" (2:13); believers are to be like their Lord.
- **Grace first:** it is God himself who presents us "blameless before the presence of his glory" (Jude 24), through Christ's precious blood. Because this is already God's gift to us in Christ, we are "diligent" to live in a way that fits it.
- **"Found by him":** "be found" is the same verb as the "found" reading of 3:10.
- **"At peace":** literally "in peace." When that day comes, believers will not meet the Lord in panic but in peace, because they have been reconciled to God.

**6. "Count the patience of our Lord as salvation."** "And count the patience of our Lord as salvation, just as our beloved brother Paul also wrote to you according to the wisdom given him" (3:15).
- **Two ways of "counting":** 3:9 says "as some count slowness"; "count" here is the same Greek word. The scoffers count the Lord's patience as slowness, even as proof he will not come; believers count the same patience as salvation.
- **"Our beloved brother Paul":** Paul once opposed Peter to his face at Antioch (Gal 2:11). Years later Peter calls him "our beloved brother." The rebuke had not broken their fellowship in the gospel; the truth of the gospel reconciled them and made them co-workers.

**7. "As they do the other Scriptures."** "As he does in all his letters when he speaks in them of these matters. There are some things in them that are hard to understand, which the ignorant and unstable twist to their own destruction, as they do the other Scriptures" (3:16).
- **"Hard to understand":** even the apostle Peter admits that some things in Paul's letters are not easy. That is a comfort to anyone today who finds the Bible hard going: difficulty does not mean we are reading it wrongly, but that we must read humbly and patiently.
- **Twisting Paul:** Peter does not say which teachings were twisted. Many think the false teachers may have turned Paul's teaching on grace and freedom into an excuse for license (see 2:19, "They promise them freedom"; Rom 6:1, "Are we to continue in sin that grace may abound?").
- **"The other Scriptures":** Peter places Paul's letters alongside "the other Scriptures," that is, he regards Paul's letters as Scripture. This is one of the earliest statements in the New Testament that the apostles' letters carry the authority of Scripture. (Some scholars take this as a sign that 2 Peter was written later; see the introduction.)
- **"To their own destruction":** twisting Scripture is no small matter. We read the Bible not to make it say what we want to hear but to let God speak to us through it.

> The point: believers wait for the new heavens and new earth God has promised, where righteousness dwells, and this hope moves us to pursue holiness now. The one who presents us without blemish before him is Christ himself. The scoffers count the Lord's patience as slowness; believers count it as salvation.

## Going Deeper: 3:17–18 Grow in Grace and Knowledge

**1. "Take care."** "You therefore, beloved, knowing this beforehand, take care that you are not carried away with the error of lawless people and lose your own stability" (3:17).
- **"Knowing this beforehand":** Peter has written this letter so that his readers are prepared in advance, neither frightened nor carried off by the false teachers and the scoffers.
- **"Stability":** the Greek word shares its root with "established in the truth that you have" in 1:12, and with "unstable" in 3:16: those who twist Scripture are unstable, while believers are to keep their stability. Jesus once said to Peter, "But I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail. And when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers" (Luke 22:32). In this letter, the Peter who once fell and was raised up by the Lord is strengthening his brothers.

**2. "Grow in the grace and knowledge."** "But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ" (3:18).
- **The best defense against error is growth:** Peter does not only say "be careful"; he says "grow." A faith that only defends itself is fragile; a faith rooted and growing in Christ stands firm.
- **Grace first:** to grow in grace is not to need grace less and less, but to rely on it and delight in it more and more.
- **Knowing a person:** "knowledge" here is not only knowing some doctrines, but knowing the person "our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ." The letter opened, "May grace and peace be multiplied to you in the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord" (1:2), and it ends by coming back to grace and the knowledge of the Lord.

**3. "To him be the glory."** "To him be the glory both now and to the day of eternity. Amen" (3:18).
- **Glory given to Christ:** God says, "my glory I give to no other" (Isa 42:8; the Chinese Union Version renders "other" as "false gods"). Yet Peter gives glory to Jesus Christ. The letter opened by calling him "our God and Savior Jesus Christ" (1:1); it ends with praise that God alone deserves, offered to him. Jesus Christ is truly God.
- **"To the day of eternity":** this chapter has spoken much of "the day of the Lord"; some commentators take Peter's last words to mean that when that day comes, it will be a day that never ends.

> The point: the best defense against error is to keep growing in the grace and knowledge of Christ. Peter's last words give glory to Jesus Christ, who is our God and Savior.

## Meditation

**Meditation:** "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you... And count the patience of our Lord as salvation" (3:9, 15). I often feel the Lord is coming too slowly. When I see in the news people who oppress the weak and are never punished, when I see injustice close to me, when I look at the sickness, war and tears of this world, I too ask, like the martyrs in Revelation, "O Sovereign Lord, holy and true, how long...?" (Rev 6:10). Sometimes my question is more personal: why is the one who hurt me still doing fine? There is nothing wrong with asking; the martyrs asked, and God did not rebuke them. But Peter asks me to look at it from another side: the "slowness" I complain about is the same patience that once waited for me. If the Lord had come the year before I believed in him, which side would I have been on? Jesus told a parable: a fig tree bore no fruit for three years, and the owner said, "Cut it down," but the vinedresser said, "Sir, let it alone this year also" (Luke 13:7–8). That patience has a limit (Luke 13:9). I was that tree. In the years when I did not know him, or even laughed at him, he did not cut me down; back then I thought "all things are continuing as they were," and that nothing would ever change. In fact he was waiting for me. Yet God's patience does not mean he shrugs at injustice. Paul says that "in his divine forbearance he had passed over former sins," in order "to show his righteousness at the present time" (Rom 3:25–26): God can wait for sinners to repent without ceasing to be just, because at the cross he laid the penalty of sin on his Son. "He was wounded for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities" (Isa 53:5). So the Lord's patience is neither weakness nor unfairness. Every injustice will in the end either be judged on the day of the Lord (3:7) or has already been borne by Christ on the cross, for all who repent and trust in him; God is "the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (Rom 3:26). If I am the one who was harmed, God's waiting does not mean he does not care what was done to me; he remembers. Scripture says, "never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God" (Rom 12:19), yet reporting a crime to the police or the courts today is still right (Rom 13:4); waiting for the day of the Lord does not mean I must stay silent. But when my heart grows impatient, I can remember this: the Lord who has not hurried to judge the world did not hold back from going to the cross; he was judged for me, and did not delay a single day. I long for him to come; and on the day I see him, I will know that he never came late. The years of his patience included me.
