## 这卷书在讲什么

帖撒罗尼迦后书是保罗写给同一个年轻教会的第二封信，大概在第一封信之后不久写成。教会的处境并没有好转：他们仍在「所受的一切逼迫患难中」（1:4）。不但如此，又有新的问题出现了。有人说「主的日子现在到了」（2:2），叫信徒心里动摇惊慌；教会中也有人「什么工都不做，反倒专管闲事」（3:11）。

这封信很短，只有三章，却回答了三个很实际的问题：
- **受苦的时候，神公义吗？** 保罗说，神必在主耶稣显现的时候施行公义的审判：加患难给信徒的人要受报应，受患难的人要得平安（1:5-10）。苦难不是神忘记了他们，他们的忍耐反倒是神公义判断的「明证」（1:5）。
- **主的日子已经到了吗？** 没有。在那日子以前，「必有离道反教的事，并有那大罪人，就是沉沦之子，显露出来」（2:3）。主耶稣必胜过他，「用口中的气灭绝他」（2:8）。所以信徒「不要轻易动心，也不要惊慌」（2:2）。
- **等候主来的时候，该怎样生活？** 不是放下工作、等着别人养活，而是「安静做工，吃自己的饭」（3:12）；「行善不可丧志」（3:13）。

整封信的根基不是信徒的刚强，而是神的信实：「但主是信实的，要坚固你们，保护你们脱离那恶者。」（3:3）

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 写信的人和时间。** 信的开头和第一封信几乎一样：「保罗、西拉、提摩太写信给帖撒罗尼迦……的教会」（1:1）。三位同工仍在一起，这与使徒行传18:5记载他们同在哥林多相合。所以多数学者认为，这封信也是在哥林多写的，大约在主后50-51年，离第一封信只有几个月。
- **先后次序：** 少数学者（如查尔斯·瓦纳梅克）认为这封信其实比「前书」更早。但多数人认为，本书2:15提到「信上写的」，很可能就是指前书；而且本书讲主的日子，像是在澄清前书4-5章引起的误会。本研读采用通常的次序。

**2. 作者问题。** 初期教会一致接受这封信是保罗写的。信中也两次提到保罗的书信：一次警告有人「冒我名」写信（2:2），一次说保罗亲笔问安，「凡我的信都以此为记」（3:17）。
- **怀疑的看法：** 从十九世纪起，有些学者（如威廉·弗雷德、沃尔夫冈·特里林）认为这是保罗死后有人托名写的。他们的理由主要有三：这封信和前书的用词、结构太相像，像是照着前书模仿的；语气比前书冷淡、正式；讲末世的方式似乎和前书不同，前书说主来得像贼一样突然（帖前5:2），这封信却列出主的日子以前必有的事。
- **支持保罗为作者的看法：** 多数福音派学者和不少其他学者（如亚伯拉罕·马勒布、戈登·费依、杰弗里·韦玛）认为是保罗写的。他们回答说：同一个人在几个月之内写信给同一个教会，用词相像是很自然的；语气比较严肃，是因为有人在传错误的教训，又有人不肯做工；至于末世的说法，耶稣自己也同时讲过「那日子，那时辰，没有人知道」（太24:36），又讲过在那以前必有的事（太24:4-28）。两种说法本来就并存。
- **本研读的立场：** 照信本身所说的（1:1；3:17），把它当作保罗的书信来读。

**3. 教会遇到的问题。**
- **逼迫：** 和前书一样，信徒因为信主受苦（1:4-7）。
- **错误的末世教训：** 有人宣称「主的日子现在到了」。这话可能来自自称从灵来的启示，可能来自某人的讲论，也可能来自一封「冒我名的书信」（2:2）。有人认为，这些人把主的日子理解为属灵上已经来到，或者把眼前的逼迫当作末日大灾难已经开始；保罗没有细说，只是说：那日子还没有到。
- **不肯做工的人：** 有人「不按规矩而行，什么工都不做」（3:11）。许多解经家认为，这和错误的末世热潮有关：既然主的日子到了，何必还要工作？也有学者（如布鲁斯·温特）认为，背后是当时恩主和依附者的社会习惯：一些人宁可依附有钱人生活，也不愿亲手做工。两种原因也可能同时存在。

## 钥匙经文：2:1-2；3:3

**1. 「论到我们主耶稣基督降临和我们到他那里聚集」。** 保罗没有否认主要再来，也没有否认信徒要「到他那里聚集」（参帖前4:17）。他所纠正的，不是盼望本身，而是对时间的错误说法。盼望是真的；只是那日子还没有到。

**2. 「不要轻易动心，也不要惊慌」。** 「动心」原文有摇动、被浪冲击的意思；「惊慌」是心里起了骚乱。错误的末世教训常常不是叫人更爱主，而是叫人惧怕、焦虑，甚至放下日常的责任。保罗说：不要被这样的话摇动。

**3. 「但主是信实的」。** 在3:2保罗刚说「人不都是有信心」，接着就说「但主是信实的」。人的信心会有不足，人的话也会骗人；但主是信实的。他「要坚固你们，保护你们脱离那恶者」。信徒能站稳，最终是因为主的信实，不是因为自己算准了日子，或分辨得比别人更精明。

## 全书结构

帖撒罗尼迦后书是一封书信，大致可以这样分：
- **问安**（1:1-2）。
- **在患难中感谢，神公义的审判**（1:3-12）：他们的信心增长，爱心充足；神必报应加患难给他们的人，使受患难的人得平安；主耶稣显现的日子，要在圣徒身上得荣耀。保罗为他们祷告，愿神看他们配得过所蒙的召。
- **主的日子还没有到**（2:1-12）：先有离道反教的事和那大罪人显露；现在有一个拦阻的；主耶稣必用口中的气灭绝那不法的人。
- **感谢和坚固**（2:13-17）：神从起初拣选了他们，召他们得着主耶稣基督的荣光；要站立得稳，坚守所领受的教训。
- **请他们代祷，主是信实的**（3:1-5）。
- **不按规矩而行的人**（3:6-15）：效法保罗亲手做工的榜样；不肯做工的就不可吃饭；不要以他为仇人，要劝他如弟兄。
- **祝福和亲笔问安**（3:16-18）。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 和前书一起读。** 前书安慰丧亲的人，说主必再来；后书提醒那些以为主已经来了的人，说那日子还没有到。两封信合起来，帮助我们既热切地盼望，又安静地生活。

**2. 读2:1-12时要谦卑。** 这段是新约中最难解的经文之一。「那大罪人」是谁？「那拦阻的」是什么？保罗说他在帖撒罗尼迦的时候已经讲过（2:5），帖撒罗尼迦人「知道」（2:6），我们却没有听到那些口头的教导。奥古斯丁在《上帝之城》里坦白说，他不知道保罗所说的「拦阻的」是指什么（第二十卷第十九章）。历世历代，忠心的信徒对这些问题有不同的看法；我们可以讨论，却不必把自己的看法当作唯一的答案，更不要用它来推算日期，或把它套在某个政治人物身上。

**3. 留意经文的目的。** 保罗讲末世，不是为了让人画出时间表，而是为了叫人「不要轻易动心」（2:2），叫人「站立得稳」（2:15），叫人在患难中仍有「永远的安慰并美好的盼望」（2:16）。读的时候，可以问：这段经文要我怎样信靠主、怎样生活？

**4. 留意神的公义和神的恩典。** 第一章讲神公义的审判，说得很严肃；但同一章也说，一切都是「照着我们的神并主耶稣基督的恩」（1:12）。第二章讲那些「不领受爱真理的心」的人被定罪，紧接着就说「主所爱的弟兄们哪……他从起初拣选了你们」（2:13）。信徒得救，不是因为比别人好，而是因为神的爱和拣选。

**5. 留意工作和教会生活。** 第三章看起来很平常：做工、吃饭、不要管闲事。但这正是保罗对末世热潮的回答。盼望主来的人，不是不食人间烟火，而是在平凡的工作中忠心，在教会里彼此劝勉。

## 默想

**默想：** 「无论有灵、有言语、有冒我名的书信，说主的日子现在到了，不要轻易动心，也不要惊慌。」（2:2）帖撒罗尼迦的信徒听到的话，似乎有属灵的来源，甚至好像出自使徒本人。今天我们也常听到各种说法：某位传道人说末日的记号已经应验，某个预言指出了年份，某段时事被说成就是启示录里的哪一节。这些话传来传去，有时叫我兴奋，有时叫我害怕，甚至叫我对眼前的工作、学业和家人提不起心来，好像这些都不重要了。我也许从来没有去查考这些说法从哪里来，只是被它们推着走。保罗没有叫帖撒罗尼迦人去找一个更准确的时间表。他提醒他们记得自己当初领受的教训（2:5、15），然后把他们的眼光转向主自己：「但主是信实的，要坚固你们，保护你们脱离那恶者。」（3:3）我不必靠自己算准日子才能平安，也不必因为没有看懂每一个记号而惊慌。耶稣说「那日子，那时辰，没有人知道」（太24:36），那位要来的，就是为我死而复活、「爱我们、开恩将永远的安慰并美好的盼望赐给我们」的主（2:16）。他来的时候，不是要吓我，而是要接我到他那里聚集（2:1）。

## What This Book Is About

2 Thessalonians is Paul's second letter to the same young church, probably written not long after the first. Their situation had not improved: they were still enduring "all your persecutions and... the afflictions" (1:4). And new problems had appeared. Some were saying that "the day of the Lord has come" (2:2), leaving believers shaken and alarmed; and some in the church were "not busy at work, but busybodies" (3:11).

The letter is short, only three chapters, but it answers three very practical questions:
- **When we suffer, is God just?** Paul says God will bring righteous judgment when the Lord Jesus is revealed: those who afflict believers will be repaid, and the afflicted will receive relief (1:5–10). Suffering does not mean God has forgotten them; their endurance is in fact "evidence of the righteous judgment of God" (1:5).
- **Has the day of the Lord already come?** No. Before that day, "the rebellion comes first, and the man of lawlessness is revealed, the son of destruction" (2:3). The Lord Jesus will overcome him and "kill with the breath of his mouth" (2:8). So believers are "not to be quickly shaken in mind or alarmed" (2:2).
- **How should we live while we wait for the Lord?** Not by laying down our work and waiting for others to support us, but by doing our "work quietly" and earning our "own living" (3:12), and by not growing "weary in doing good" (3:13).

The foundation of the whole letter is not the believers' strength but God's faithfulness: "But the Lord is faithful. He will establish you and guard you against the evil one" (3:3).

## Author and Setting

**1. Writers and date.** The opening is almost identical to the first letter: "Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy, To the church of the Thessalonians" (1:1). The three co-workers are still together, which fits Acts 18:5, where they are together in Corinth. So most scholars think this letter too was written from Corinth, around AD 50–51, only a few months after the first.
- **Which came first?** A few scholars (such as Charles Wanamaker) think this letter was actually written before "1 Thessalonians." But most hold that when 2:15 mentions "our letter" it very likely means 1 Thessalonians, and that this letter's teaching on the day of the Lord reads like a correction of misunderstandings that grew out of 1 Thessalonians 4–5. This study follows the usual order.

**2. The question of authorship.** The early church received this letter unanimously as Paul's. The letter itself twice refers to Paul's letters: once warning about "a letter seeming to be from us" (2:2), and once saying Paul writes the greeting with his own hand, "the sign of genuineness in every letter of mine" (3:17).
- **The doubting view:** since the nineteenth century some scholars (such as William Wrede and Wolfgang Trilling) have argued that someone wrote it in Paul's name after his death. Their main reasons are three: its wording and structure are so close to 1 Thessalonians that it looks like an imitation; its tone is cooler and more formal; and its way of speaking about the end seems different, since 1 Thessalonians says the Lord comes suddenly, like a thief (1 Thess 5:2), while this letter lists things that must happen before the day of the Lord.
- **The view that Paul wrote it:** most evangelical scholars and many others (such as Abraham Malherbe, Gordon Fee and Jeffrey Weima) hold that Paul wrote it. They reply that one person writing to the same church within a few months would naturally use similar wording; that the more serious tone fits a church where false teaching was spreading and some refused to work; and that on the end, Jesus himself said both that "concerning that day and hour no one knows" (Matt 24:36) and that certain things must come first (Matt 24:4–28). The two kinds of statement stand side by side.
- **This study's position:** we read it as Paul's letter, as it presents itself (1:1; 3:17).

**3. The church's problems.**
- **Persecution:** as in the first letter, believers were suffering for their faith (1:4–7).
- **False teaching about the end:** some were claiming that "the day of the Lord has come." This may have come from a claimed revelation "by a spirit," from someone's "spoken word," or from "a letter seeming to be from us" (2:2). Some think these people understood the day of the Lord as having already come in a spiritual sense, or took the persecution they faced as the start of the final tribulation. Paul does not explain in detail; he simply says the day has not yet come.
- **Those who would not work:** some were "walking in idleness, not busy at work" (3:11). Many commentators link this with the mistaken end-time excitement: if the day of the Lord has come, why keep working? Some scholars (such as Bruce Winter) point instead to the patron–client customs of the time, in which some preferred to attach themselves to a wealthy patron rather than work with their own hands. Both causes may have been at work together.

## Key Verses: 2:1–2; 3:3

**1. "Concerning the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and our being gathered together to him."** Paul does not deny that the Lord will come, or that believers will be "gathered together to him" (see 1 Thess 4:17). What he corrects is not the hope itself but a false claim about its timing. The hope is true; that day simply has not come yet.

**2. "Not to be quickly shaken in mind or alarmed."** The word for "shaken" suggests being tossed, as by waves; "alarmed" means inner turmoil. False teaching about the end often does not make people love the Lord more; it makes them afraid and anxious, and can even lead them to drop their daily responsibilities. Paul says: do not be shaken by such words.

**3. "But the Lord is faithful."** In 3:2 Paul has just said, "not all have faith," and at once he adds, "But the Lord is faithful." Human faith can fall short, and human words can deceive, but the Lord is faithful. He "will establish you and guard you against the evil one." In the end, believers stand firm because of the Lord's faithfulness, not because they have calculated the date correctly or are shrewder at discernment than others.

## The Shape of the Book

2 Thessalonians is a letter, and it can be roughly divided like this:
- **Greeting** (1:1–2).
- **Thanksgiving in affliction; God's righteous judgment** (1:3–12): their faith is growing and their love increasing; God will repay those who afflict them and grant relief to the afflicted; on the day the Lord Jesus is revealed he will be glorified in his saints. Paul prays that God will make them worthy of his calling.
- **The day of the Lord has not come** (2:1–12): first the rebellion and the revealing of the man of lawlessness; something now restrains; the Lord Jesus will kill the lawless one with the breath of his mouth.
- **Thanksgiving and strengthening** (2:13–17): God chose them to be saved and called them to obtain the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ; they are to stand firm and hold to the traditions they were taught.
- **A request for prayer; the Lord is faithful** (3:1–5).
- **Those walking in idleness** (3:6–15): imitate Paul's example of working with his own hands; anyone not willing to work should not eat; do not regard such a person as an enemy, but warn him as a brother.
- **Blessing and greeting in Paul's own hand** (3:16–18).

## How to Read This Book

**1. Read it with 1 Thessalonians.** The first letter comforts the grieving with the promise that the Lord will come; the second warns those who think he has already come that the day has not yet arrived. Together the two letters help us hope eagerly and live quietly.

**2. Read 2:1–12 humbly.** This is one of the hardest passages in the New Testament. Who is "the man of lawlessness"? What is "restraining"? Paul says he told them these things while he was with them (2:5), and the Thessalonians "know" (2:6), but we did not hear that spoken teaching. Augustine admitted frankly in *The City of God* that he did not know what Paul meant by what restrains (book 20, chapter 19). Faithful believers through the centuries have held different views on these questions. We can discuss them, but we need not treat our own view as the only answer, and we must not use it to calculate dates or fit it onto some political figure.

**3. Notice the purpose of the passage.** Paul speaks of the end not so that people can draw up a timetable, but so that they will "not be quickly shaken" (2:2), will "stand firm" (2:15), and will have "eternal comfort and good hope" even in affliction (2:16). As you read, ask: how does this passage call me to trust the Lord and to live?

**4. Notice both God's justice and God's grace.** Chapter 1 speaks very seriously of God's righteous judgment, yet the same chapter says all is "according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ" (1:12). Chapter 2 speaks of the condemnation of those who "refused to love the truth," and at once says, "brothers beloved by the Lord... God chose you" (2:13). Believers are saved not because they are better than others, but because of God's love and choosing.

**5. Notice work and church life.** Chapter 3 seems very ordinary: work, eat, do not meddle. But this is exactly Paul's answer to end-time excitement. People who hope for the Lord's coming do not float above ordinary life; they are faithful in ordinary work and warn and encourage one another in the church.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Not to be quickly shaken in mind or alarmed, either by a spirit or a spoken word, or a letter seeming to be from us, to the effect that the day of the Lord has come" (2:2). What the Thessalonians heard seemed to have a spiritual source, and even seemed to come from the apostle himself. We too hear all kinds of claims: a preacher says the signs of the end have now been fulfilled, a prophecy names a year, some news event is said to be exactly what a verse in Revelation describes. These claims get passed around. Sometimes they excite me, sometimes they frighten me, and sometimes they leave me unable to care about my work, my studies or my family, as if none of it mattered anymore. Perhaps I have never checked where these claims come from, and have simply let them push me along. Paul did not tell the Thessalonians to find a more accurate timetable. He reminded them of the teaching they had first received (2:5, 15), and then turned their eyes to the Lord himself: "But the Lord is faithful. He will establish you and guard you against the evil one" (3:3). I do not need to calculate the date correctly in order to have peace, nor be alarmed because I cannot make sense of every sign. Jesus said, "concerning that day and hour no one knows" (Matt 24:36); and the one who is coming is the Lord who died and rose for me, "who loved us and gave us eternal comfort and good hope through grace" (2:16). When he comes, it will not be to frighten me, but to gather me to himself (2:1).
