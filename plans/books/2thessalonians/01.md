## 本章脉络

第一章分成三段。**问安**（1-2节）：和前书几乎一样，只是加上恩惠、平安「从父神和主耶稣基督」而来。**感谢和神公义的审判**（3-10节）：保罗为他们增长的信心和充足的爱心感谢神；他们在逼迫中的忍耐，正是神公义判断的明证；主耶稣从天上显现的时候，神要报应加患难给他们的人，叫受患难的人得平安。**祷告**（11-12节）：愿神看他们配得过所蒙的召，用大能成就他们一切的善工，叫主耶稣的名在他们身上得荣耀。

这一章说到神的审判，说得很重；但它是写给受苦的信徒的。保罗不是要他们幸灾乐祸，而是要他们知道：神看见了，神是公义的，历史的最后一句话属于主耶稣。

## 深读 1:3-5 在逼迫中增长的信心

**1. 「我们该为你们常常感谢神，这本是合宜的」。** 「该」字带着欠债、有义务的意思。保罗不是说感谢是勉强的，而是说，看见神在他们身上的工作，不感谢就不对了。
- **有人认为这是回应：** 有些解经家（如韦玛）认为，帖撒罗尼迦人也许谦虚地说过，自己不配得保罗在前书中那样的称赞。保罗回答说：为你们感谢神，是「合宜的」。不过这只是推测。

**2. 信心增长，爱心充足。** 「因你们的信心格外增长，并且你们众人彼此相爱的心也都充足」（1:3）。保罗在前书曾求主叫他们的爱心「增长，充足」（帖前3:12），又想要「补满你们信心的不足」（帖前3:10）。现在他看见，神应允了那些祷告。
- **「众人」：** 不只是几个热心的人，而是「你们众人」。爱心在整个教会里充足起来。
- **没有提盼望：** 前书的开头提到信心、爱心和盼望（帖前1:3），这里只提信心和爱心。有人认为，这正是因为他们的盼望被错误的末世教训搅乱了，所以保罗在第二章要处理这个问题。

**3. 「仍旧存忍耐和信心」。** 「甚至我们在神的各教会里为你们夸口，都因你们在所受的一切逼迫患难中，仍旧存忍耐和信心」（1:4）。保罗不夸自己的事工，却在别的教会里夸他们。
- **忍耐：** 不是消极地咬牙忍受，而是在压力下站住、不放弃。
- **「仍旧」：** 逼迫没有停，他们的信心也没有停。

**4. 「这正是神公义判断的明证」。** 「这正是神公义判断的明证，叫你们可算配得神的国；你们就是为这国受苦」（1:5）。「这」指什么？多数解经家认为，指他们在逼迫中的忍耐和信心。
- **怎样是「明证」？** 有两种主要理解。一种认为，神现在藉着苦难炼净他们，这就显出神的判断是公义的：他正在预备他们进入他的国（参彼前4:17；腓1:28）。另一种认为，他们在苦难中仍有信心，这本身就是神工作的记号，证明将来神在审判的日子会判他们为义、接纳他们。两种理解都把重点放在神身上，不是放在他们的功劳上。
- **「可算配得」：** 不是说他们靠受苦赚到了神的国，而是说神「算」他们为配得。神的国是白白赐的恩典，受苦是走在这国路上的人必有的经历（参徒14:22）。

> 要点：信徒在逼迫中仍旧存着信心，不是因为自己特别刚强，而是神在他们里面做工。这样的忍耐本身就在宣告：神是公义的，神没有忘记他们。

## 深读 1:6-10 神公义的审判

**1. 「神既是公义的」。** 「神既是公义的，就必将患难报应那加患难给你们的人，也必使你们这受患难的人与我们同得平安」（1:6-7）。这里讲的是公义的报应：叫加患难的人受患难，叫受患难的人得安息。
- **「平安」：** 原文是放松、解脱的意思，好像弓弦松开，或者重担卸下。
- **「与我们同得」：** 保罗自己也在患难中。他不是站在远处安慰人，而是和他们一同等候。

**2. 伸冤在神。** 这段经文不是叫信徒自己报复，而是叫他们把伸冤的事交给神。保罗在罗马书说：「亲爱的弟兄，不要自己伸冤，宁可让步，听凭主怒」，因为主说：「伸冤在我，我必报应。」（罗12:19；参申32:35）正因为神必定公义地审判，信徒才可以不自己报仇，还可以为逼迫他们的人祷告（参太5:44）。
- **这不是叫人沉默：** 把最终的伸冤交给神，不是说受了欺压、虐待或犯罪伤害的人不可以报警，或不可以寻求法律的保护。保罗在罗马书13:4说，在上掌权的是「神的用人，是伸冤的，刑罚那作恶的」。向警察和法庭举报罪行是对的；受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必勉强和仍然不安全的人和好；教会更不可以为了面子，把这样的事压下来（「家丑不可外扬」）。

**3. 「主耶稣……从天上在火焰中显现」。** 「那时，主耶稣同他有能力的天使从天上在火焰中显现，要报应那不认识神和那不听从我主耶稣福音的人」（1:7-8）。这里的描写，用的是旧约描写耶和华降临的语言：「看哪，耶和华必在火中降临……以火焰施行责罚」（赛66:15）。保罗把旧约里耶和华的作为，直接归给主耶稣。
- **耶稣是审判的主：** 那位曾经被人审判、被钉十字架的耶稣，将来要作审判全地的主（参徒17:31；约5:22）。
- **两种人还是一种人？** 「不认识神的」和「不听从……福音的」，有人认为是两种人：前者指外邦人，后者指犹太人。但多数解经家认为，这是希伯来诗歌常用的平行句，说的是同一群人：拒绝神、不肯顺服福音的人。
- **「不认识神」：** 不是指从没有机会听见的人，而是指不肯认识神、不肯归荣耀给他的人（参罗1:21、28）。保罗在这里讲的，是那些「加患难给你们的人」，他们听过福音，却逼迫传福音的人。

**4. 「永远沉沦，离开主的面」。** 「他们要受刑罚，就是永远沉沦，离开主的面和他权能的荣光」（1:9）。这是新约中论到最后审判最严肃的经文之一。
- **「永远沉沦」：** 「沉沦」不是指不再存在，而是指毁坏、失丧；「永远」说明这刑罚没有尽头（参太25:46「这些人要往永刑里去」）。教会历来的主流教导（如奥古斯丁、加尔文、乔纳森·爱德华兹，今天的巴刻、卡森），都把这理解为人永远有知觉地在神的审判之下。也有少数福音派学者（如约翰·斯托得曾试探性地提出，爱德华·富奇）主张条件性永生，认为不信的人最后会被灭绝，「永远」是指结果永远不能挽回。多数福音派神学家认为，这种看法很难与太25:46等经文相合。
- **「离开主的面」：** 原文的介词可以理解为「离开」，也可以理解为「从……而来」。若是前者，地狱的可怕在于与主的同在和荣光永远隔绝（参太7:23「你们这些作恶的人，离开我去吧」）；若是后者，刑罚是从主的面前、从他权能的荣光发出来的（参赛2:10）。和合本和多数英文译本采取前一种理解。
- **为什么要这样说？** 不是为了吓唬人，而是因为这是真的。保罗说这话，是要信徒知道罪恶不会得胜，也要我们看见拒绝福音的人面对的是什么。今天还在「拖延」的人，这段话是一个慈爱的警告：主耶稣现在仍在呼召人悔改；「看哪，现在正是悦纳的时候！现在正是拯救的日子」（林后6:2）。

**5. 「在他圣徒的身上得荣耀」。** 「这正是主降临、要在他圣徒的身上得荣耀、又在一切信的人身上显为希奇的那日子」（1:10）。同一日，对不信的人是审判，对信徒却是荣耀的日子。
- **耶稣在圣徒身上得荣耀：** 主来的那一天，最奇妙的不是信徒自己有多好，而是看着这群曾经软弱、受苦、跌倒的人，所有人都要惊叹：主耶稣做了何等的事！
- **「我们对你们作的见证，你们也信了」：** 帖撒罗尼迦人能在那日站在主的一边，原因很简单：他们信了福音。不是因为他们的功劳，而是因为他们领受了关于耶稣的见证。

> 要点：神必公义地审判，罪恶和逼迫不会说最后的话。所以信徒可以把伸冤交给神，也可以寻求正当的保护。对不信的人，这是严肃的警告；对信的人，那日子是主耶稣在他们身上得荣耀的日子。

## 深读 1:11-12 配得过所蒙的召

**1. 「因此，我们常为你们祷告」。** 保罗讲完将来的日子，立刻回到现在：为他们祷告。他不是只叫他们等候，而是求神在等候的日子里在他们身上做工。

**2. 「愿我们的神看你们配得过所蒙的召」。** 「配得过」的意思，和第5节一样，不是说他们要靠自己的表现赚得神的召，而是求神「看」他们配得、「使」他们配得（英文译本作使你们配得过）。神召了他们，神也要成全他们（参腓1:6「那在你们心里动了善工的，必成全这工」）。

**3. 「用大能成就你们一切所羡慕的良善」。** 「又用大能成就你们一切所羡慕的良善和一切因信心所做的工夫」（1:11）。
- **「所羡慕的良善」：** 原文指心里的立志、渴望。信徒心里想要行善，这本身就是神的工作；但光有心愿还不够，保罗求神「用大能成就」。
- **「因信心所做的工夫」：** 和帖前1:3是同一个说法。信心不是空的，会做出工夫来；但这工夫也要靠神的大能。
- **神的大能：** 我们想做的善事常常做不成，或者半途而废。保罗不是叫他们更加努力，而是为他们祷告：愿神用他的大能完成他们所不能完成的。

**4. 「都照着我们的神并主耶稣基督的恩」。** 「叫我们主耶稣的名在你们身上得荣耀，你们也在他身上得荣耀，都照着我们的神并主耶稣基督的恩」（1:12）。
- **彼此得荣耀：** 主耶稣在信徒身上得荣耀，信徒也在他身上得荣耀。这荣耀的交流，开始于今天，完成于主来的那日（1:10）。
- **「照着……恩」：** 整个祷告的根基是恩典。被召、配得、行善、得荣耀，没有一样是靠人自己的。
- **一个原文的问题：** 「我们的神并主耶稣基督」在原文中，有人认为可以读成我们的神和主耶稣基督，指同一位（参多2:13；彼后1:1）。但多数解经家认为，这里「主耶稣基督」常常作专有名词，所以仍是指父和子两位。无论哪一种，保罗都把父和子并列为恩典的源头。

> 要点：神召我们，也要成全我们。我们心里向善的渴望，和因信心所做的工夫，都要靠神的大能才能完成；这一切都是照着恩典，为要叫主耶稣的名得荣耀。

## 默想

**默想：** 「愿我们的神看你们配得过所蒙的召，又用大能成就你们一切所羡慕的良善和一切因信心所做的工夫。」（1:11）我心里曾经有过一些向善的心愿：想和一位疏远的家人重新说话，想每个星期陪孩子读一段圣经，想去探望一位长期卧病的弟兄，想把一个藏了很久的罪向神和一位可靠的人承认。有些我开始过，后来停了；有些我连开始都没有。时间久了，这些心愿就变成了心里的一份清单，每次想起来都觉得惭愧，最后干脆不去想了。我以为，问题只在于我不够努力。可是保罗没有叫帖撒罗尼迦人更努力，而是为他们祷告：愿神「用大能成就」他们所羡慕的良善。那些心愿本来就是神放在我心里的；那位放下心愿的，也能用他的大能把它成全。今天，我可以把那份清单拿出来，不是为了再一次责备自己，而是把它带到神面前，求他照他的恩典做成。我也不必等到这些都做好了，才相信自己「配得过」神的召；配得过的原因不在我，而在那位为我舍命、如今在父面前为我代求的主耶稣。保罗说，这一切「都照着我们的神并主耶稣基督的恩」（1:12）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 falls into three parts. **Greeting** (vv. 1–2): almost the same as in the first letter, except that grace and peace now come "from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ." **Thanksgiving and God's righteous judgment** (vv. 3–10): Paul thanks God for their growing faith and increasing love; their endurance under persecution is evidence of God's righteous judgment; when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven, God will repay those who afflict them and grant relief to the afflicted. **Prayer** (vv. 11–12): may God make them worthy of his calling, fulfill every work of faith by his power, and glorify the name of the Lord Jesus in them.

This chapter speaks heavily of God's judgment, but it is written to suffering believers. Paul does not want them to gloat; he wants them to know that God has seen, that God is just, and that the last word in history belongs to the Lord Jesus.

## Going Deeper: 1:3–5 Faith That Grows Under Persecution

**1. "We ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers, as is right."** "Ought" carries the sense of a debt or obligation. Paul does not mean that thanksgiving is grudging; he means that, having seen God's work in them, it would be wrong not to give thanks.
- **Perhaps a reply:** some commentators (such as Weima) suggest the Thessalonians may have humbly said they did not deserve the praise Paul gave them in the first letter, and Paul answers that giving thanks for them is "right." But this is only a guess.

**2. Faith growing, love increasing.** "Because your faith is growing abundantly, and the love of every one of you for one another is increasing" (1:3). In the first letter Paul prayed that the Lord would make their love "increase and abound" (1 Thess 3:12), and longed to "supply what is lacking in your faith" (1 Thess 3:10). Now he sees that God has answered those prayers.
- **"Every one of you":** not just a few zealous members, but "every one of you." Love is increasing throughout the church.
- **Hope not mentioned:** the first letter opens with faith, love and hope (1 Thess 1:3); here only faith and love are named. Some think this is because their hope had been unsettled by false teaching about the end, which Paul takes up in chapter 2.

**3. "Your steadfastness and faith."** "Therefore we ourselves boast about you in the churches of God for your steadfastness and faith in all your persecutions and in the afflictions that you are enduring" (1:4). Paul does not boast about his own ministry; he boasts about them to other churches.
- **Steadfastness:** not passive teeth-gritting, but standing firm under pressure and not giving up.
- **"That you are enduring":** the persecution had not stopped, and neither had their faith.

**4. "This is evidence of the righteous judgment of God."** "This is evidence of the righteous judgment of God, that you may be considered worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you are also suffering" (1:5). What does "this" refer to? Most commentators take it as their steadfastness and faith under persecution.
- **Evidence in what sense?** There are two main readings. One holds that God is now refining them through suffering, which shows that his judgment is right: he is preparing them for his kingdom (see 1 Pet 4:17; Phil 1:28). The other holds that their faith in suffering is itself a sign of God's work, proving that on the day of judgment God will vindicate and welcome them. Both readings put the weight on God, not on their merit.
- **"Considered worthy":** this does not mean they earned the kingdom by suffering, but that God "considers" them worthy. The kingdom of God is a free gift of grace; suffering is what those on the road to that kingdom will meet (see Acts 14:22).

> The point: Believers keep their faith under persecution not because they are especially strong, but because God is at work in them. Such endurance itself declares that God is just and has not forgotten them.

## Going Deeper: 1:6–10 God's Righteous Judgment

**1. "God considers it just."** "Since indeed God considers it just to repay with affliction those who afflict you, and to grant relief to you who are afflicted as well as to us" (1:6–7). This is just recompense: affliction for those who afflict, rest for the afflicted.
- **"Relief":** the word means loosening, release, like a bowstring slackened or a heavy load set down.
- **"As well as to us":** Paul too is suffering. He does not comfort them from a distance; he waits with them.

**2. Vengeance belongs to God.** This passage does not tell believers to take revenge themselves; it tells them to leave vengeance to God. As Paul writes in Romans: "Beloved, never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God, for it is written, 'Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord'" (Rom 12:19; see Deut 32:35). Precisely because God will surely judge justly, believers can refrain from revenge, and can even pray for those who persecute them (see Matt 5:44).
- **This is not a call to silence:** leaving final vengeance to God does not mean that people who suffer oppression, abuse or criminal harm may not go to the police or seek the protection of the law. In Romans 13:4 Paul says the governing authority is "the servant of God, an avenger who carries out God's wrath on the wrongdoer." Reporting crime to the police and the courts is right; victims need not stay in danger, nor force themselves to reconcile with someone who is still unsafe; and the church must never hush such things up to save face (「家丑不可外扬」, "do not air the family's dirty laundry").

**3. "The Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven... in flaming fire."** "When the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven with his mighty angels in flaming fire, inflicting vengeance on those who do not know God and on those who do not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus" (1:7–8). The description uses the Old Testament language for the LORD's coming: "For behold, the LORD will come in fire... and his rebuke with flames of fire" (Isa 66:15). Paul assigns directly to the Lord Jesus what the Old Testament says of the LORD.
- **Jesus is the Lord who judges:** the Jesus who was once judged by men and crucified will judge the whole earth (see Acts 17:31; John 5:22).
- **Two groups or one?** Some take "those who do not know God" and "those who do not obey the gospel" as two groups, Gentiles and Jews. But most commentators see a Hebrew-style parallelism describing one group: those who reject God and refuse to obey the gospel.
- **"Do not know God":** this does not mean people who never had the chance to hear, but those who refuse to acknowledge God or give him glory (see Rom 1:21, 28). Paul is speaking here of "those who afflict you," people who had heard the gospel and persecuted those who preached it.

**4. "Eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord."** "They will suffer the punishment of eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of his might" (1:9). This is one of the most solemn statements about final judgment in the New Testament.
- **"Eternal destruction":** "destruction" here does not mean ceasing to exist but ruin and loss; "eternal" means the punishment has no end (see Matt 25:46, "these will go away into eternal punishment"). The church's mainstream teaching through history (Augustine, Calvin, Jonathan Edwards, and today J. I. Packer and D. A. Carson) has understood this as people remaining consciously under God's judgment forever. A minority of evangelical scholars (John Stott, tentatively, and Edward Fudge) hold to "conditional immortality," the view that unbelievers will in the end be destroyed, with "eternal" meaning the result can never be reversed. Most evangelical theologians judge that this view is hard to square with texts such as Matthew 25:46.
- **"Away from the presence":** the Greek preposition can mean "away from" or "from" in the sense of "coming from." On the first reading, the horror of hell is being shut out forever from the Lord's presence and glory (see Matt 7:23, "depart from me, you workers of lawlessness"); on the second, the punishment goes out from the Lord's presence, from the glory of his might (see Isa 2:10). The CUV and most English versions take the first reading.
- **Why say this?** Not to frighten people, but because it is true. Paul says it so that believers know evil will not win, and so that we see what those who reject the gospel face. For anyone still "putting it off," this is a loving warning: the Lord Jesus is still calling people to repent; "Behold, now is the favorable time; behold, now is the day of salvation" (2 Cor 6:2).

**5. "To be glorified in his saints."** "When he comes on that day to be glorified in his saints, and to be marveled at among all who have believed, because our testimony to you was believed" (1:10). The same day is judgment for unbelievers and glory for believers.
- **Jesus glorified in his saints:** the wonder of that day will not be how good believers are, but that, looking at these once-weak, suffering, stumbling people, everyone will marvel at what the Lord Jesus has done.
- **"Because our testimony to you was believed":** the Thessalonians will stand on the Lord's side on that day for a simple reason: they believed the gospel. Not because of their merit, but because they received the testimony about Jesus.

> The point: God will judge justly; evil and persecution will not have the last word. So believers can leave vengeance to God, and can also seek rightful protection. For unbelievers this is a solemn warning; for believers that day is the day the Lord Jesus is glorified in them.

## Going Deeper: 1:11–12 Worthy of His Calling

**1. "To this end we always pray for you."** Having spoken of the coming day, Paul turns at once to the present: he prays for them. He does not simply tell them to wait; he asks God to work in them while they wait.

**2. "That our God may make you worthy of his calling."** "Worthy," as in verse 5, does not mean they must earn God's call by their performance; Paul asks God to "make" them worthy. God called them, and God will complete them (see Phil 1:6, "he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion").

**3. "May fulfill every resolve for good."** "And may fulfill every resolve for good and every work of faith by his power" (1:11).
- **"Resolve for good":** the word means a desire or purpose of the heart. That believers want to do good is itself God's work; but desire alone is not enough, so Paul asks God to "fulfill" it "by his power."
- **"Work of faith":** the same phrase as 1 Thessalonians 1:3. Faith is not empty; it produces work. But this work too depends on God's power.
- **God's power:** the good we want to do often goes undone, or is abandoned halfway. Paul does not tell them to try harder; he prays that God by his power will complete what they cannot.

**4. "According to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ."** "So that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you, and you in him, according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ" (1:12).
- **Glory both ways:** the Lord Jesus is glorified in believers, and believers in him. This exchange of glory begins today and is completed on the day the Lord comes (1:10).
- **"According to... grace":** the whole prayer rests on grace. Being called, being made worthy, doing good, being glorified: none of it rests on us.
- **A question in the Greek:** some think "our God and the Lord Jesus Christ" could be read as "our God and Lord, Jesus Christ," referring to one person (compare Titus 2:13; 2 Pet 1:1). But most commentators think that "Lord Jesus Christ" works here as a proper name, so that the phrase still refers to two persons, Father and Son. Either way, Paul names Father and Son together as the source of grace.

> The point: God calls us, and God will complete us. Our heart's desire for good, and the work that faith produces, can be completed only by God's power; all of it is according to grace, so that the name of the Lord Jesus may be glorified.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "That our God may make you worthy of his calling and may fulfill every resolve for good and every work of faith by his power" (1:11). There have been good desires in my heart: to start speaking again with a family member I have drifted from, to read a passage of Scripture with my children every week, to visit a brother who has been ill for a long time, to confess to God and to a trustworthy person a sin I have hidden for years. Some of these I started and then stopped; some I never started at all. Over time they have become a list in my heart, and every time I think of it I feel ashamed, until in the end I stop thinking about it. I assumed the only problem was that I had not tried hard enough. But Paul did not tell the Thessalonians, "Try harder." He prayed for them, that God would "fulfill" their resolves for good "by his power." Those desires were put in my heart by God in the first place, and the one who put them there can complete them by his power. Today I can take out that list, not to accuse myself once more, but to bring it before God and ask him to accomplish it according to his grace. Nor do I have to wait until all of it is done before I believe I am "worthy" of God's calling. The reason I am made worthy is not in me, but in the Lord Jesus, who gave his life for me and now intercedes for me before the Father. All of this, Paul says, is "according to the grace of our God and the Lord Jesus Christ" (1:12).
