## 本章脉络

第二章是全信的中心，分成两大段。**主的日子还没有到**（1-12节）：有人说主的日子现在到了，保罗劝他们不要动心惊慌；那日子以前，必有离道反教的事，并有那大罪人显露；现在有一个拦阻的，等到拦阻的被除去，那不法的人才显露；主耶稣必用口中的气灭绝他；那些不领受爱真理之心的人，要被虚谎迷惑。**感谢、劝勉和祷告**（13-17节）：神从起初拣选了他们，藉福音召他们得着基督的荣光；所以要站立得稳，坚守所领受的教训；愿主耶稣基督和父神安慰他们的心，在一切善行善言上坚固他们。

这一章的前半段很难，历代解经家有许多不同的看法。但它的后半段很清楚：无论末世的细节怎样，信徒的安稳在于神的拣选、神的呼召和神的安慰。读这一章，要像保罗一样，从「不法的隐意」转到「主所爱的弟兄们」。

## 深读 2:1-3上 不要被诱惑

**1. 保罗纠正的是什么？** 「弟兄们，论到我们主耶稣基督降临和我们到他那里聚集，我劝你们：无论有灵、有言语、有冒我名的书信，说主的日子现在到了，不要轻易动心，也不要惊慌」（2:1-2）。
- **「降临」：** 原文就是前书多次用的那个词（帖前2:19；3:13；4:15；5:23），当时用来指君王驾临一座城。
- **「聚集」：** 指信徒被聚集到主那里，和帖前4:17「被提到云里，在空中与主相遇」说的是同一件事。
- **「现在到了」：** 原文的意思不是快要到了，而是已经来到、已经在这里了。

**2. 错误的教训从哪里来？** 保罗列了三个可能的来源：「灵」，就是自称从圣灵来的预言；「言语」，就是某人的讲论或教导；「冒我名的书信」，就是假托保罗写的信，或者有人歪曲了保罗写过的信（参彼后3:16）。保罗不确定是哪一种，但他说，不管从哪里来，都不要相信。
- **试验预言：** 保罗在前书说：「不要藐视先知的讲论」，「但要凡事察验」（帖前5:20-21）。这里正是一个例子：一个自称是从灵来的信息，若与使徒的教训不合，就要拒绝。

**3. 「人不拘用什么法子，你们总不要被他诱惑」。** 「诱惑」是欺骗、引人走错路。耶稣自己也这样警告过：「你们要谨慎，免得有人迷惑你们。因为将来有好些人冒我的名来……并且要迷惑许多人」（太24:4-5）。
- **为什么说主的日子已经到了是错的？** 这话会叫人以为，眼前的苦难就是最后的审判，主已经来了，自己却没有被聚集；也可能叫人以为，从此不必再工作，不必再过平常的生活（参3:6-12）。无论哪一种，都会叫信徒失去平安，离开正路。

> 要点：属灵的话也要试验。凡是叫人惊慌、离开使徒教训的末世信息，不管说得多么属灵，都不要轻易相信。

## 深读 2:3下-8 那大罪人和那拦阻的

**1. 「离道反教的事」。** 「因为那日子以前，必有离道反教的事，并有那大罪人，就是沉沦之子，显露出来」（2:3）。「离道反教」原文是一个词，常指背叛、叛乱。
- **背叛谁？** 有人认为指教会中大规模的背道，许多自称信主的人离弃真道（参太24:10-12）；也有人认为指整个世界公开起来反叛神。也可能两种意思都有。
- **和被提的次序：** 这段经文也是关于被提时间争论的重要经文（参帖前4章的讨论）。持灾后被提看法的人（如乔治·赖德、道格拉斯·穆尔）和主张只有一次再来的人（如胡克玛）指出，第1节的「聚集」和第2节的「主的日子」说的是同一件事，所以信徒被聚集到主那里，是在离道反教和那大罪人显露之后。持灾前被提看法的人（如约翰·沃尔沃德）则把第1节的「聚集」和第2-3节的「主的日子」分开，认为信徒在那日子以前已经被提。有些时代论作者（如肯尼斯·伍斯特）甚至把「离道反教」解作离开，指教会被提；但多数解经家，包括许多时代论者，都不接受这种解释，因为这个词在旧约希腊文译本和新约里都是指宗教上的背叛（参书22:22；徒21:21）。这段经文的重点不是排出时间表，而是说那日子还没有到。
- **「那日子以前」：** 原文在这里没有说完，英文译本补上那日子不会来到，除非……这样的话。保罗的意思很清楚：这些事还没有发生，所以主的日子还没有到。

**2. 「那大罪人，就是沉沦之子」。** 和合本作「大罪人」，是根据一些较晚的抄本，这些抄本作罪的人（如亚历山大抄本和多数拜占庭抄本）；较早的抄本（如西乃抄本、梵蒂冈抄本）作不法的人，现代多数译本采用这个读法，和第7-8节的「不法的隐意」「不法的人」一致。「沉沦之子」是说他注定灭亡（参约17:12）。
- **他的样子：** 「他是抵挡主，高抬自己，超过一切称为神的和一切受人敬拜的，甚至坐在神的殿里，自称是神」（2:4）。这话很像但以理书描写那位王：「王必任意而行，自高自大，超过所有的神」（但11:36）；也像以西结书对泰尔王说的：「因你心里高傲，说：我是神」（结28:2）。
- **历史的影子：** 主前167年，安提阿古四世在耶路撒冷的圣殿里设立偶像的坛；主后40年，罗马皇帝卡利古拉曾下令把自己的像立在圣殿里，后来没有成功。帖撒罗尼迦人也许听说过这类事（参但9:27；11:31；可13:14「那行毁坏可憎的」）。保罗所说的，是这种狂傲最后、最彻底的表现。

**3. 「那大罪人」是谁？** 历代信徒有几种主要看法。
- **将来的一个人：** 初期教会许多教父（如爱任纽）和今天许多福音派学者认为，这是在主再来之前要出现的一个具体的人，就是约翰书信所说「那敌基督的」（约一2:18）。持这种看法的人当中，又有人认为他会在一座重建的耶路撒冷圣殿里坐下（如时代论者），有人认为「坐在神的殿里」是借用旧约的说法，表示他狂妄地自居神的地位。
- **教会历史中的一种制度：** 宗教改革时期，马丁·路德、加尔文和许多改革宗信条（如1646年的威斯敏斯德信条第二十五章第6节）认为，这是指罗马教皇制度，因为它坐在「神的殿」也就是教会中，自称拥有属于神的权柄。今天大多数改革宗学者已不这样确指。后来美国的长老会修订信条时删去了这句话：美国北长老会在1903年删去了第二十五章第6节的这句话，今天美国的正统长老会和美洲长老会所用的版本也没有这句话。
- **已经过去的人物：** 过去论者认为，这是指主后70年以前的人物或势力，比如攻陷耶路撒冷、毁灭圣殿的罗马人。但保罗说主耶稣要「用降临的荣光废掉他」（2:8），这很难与主后70年的事件相合，所以多数学者不接受这种看法。
- **共同之处：** 无论哪一种看法，都同意几件事：邪恶会在历史的最后集中地显露；它的本质是人想坐在神的位置上；它最后必被主耶稣毁灭。约翰也说「现在已经有好些敌基督的出来了」（约一2:18），所以这种狂傲的灵在今天已经在运行。

**4. 「那拦阻的」。** 「现在你们也知道，那拦阻他的是什么，是叫他到了的时候才可以显露。因为那不法的隐意已经发动，只是现在有一个拦阻的，等到那拦阻的被除去，那时这不法的人必显露出来」（2:6-8）。保罗在第6节用中性（「那拦阻……的是什么」），在第7节用阳性（「有一个拦阻的」），所以拦阻的可能既是一种力量，又与一个位格有关。
- **罗马帝国和法律秩序：** 初期教会许多人（如特土良、屈梭多模）认为，这是指罗马帝国，或者更广地说，指政府和法律维持的秩序（参罗13:1-4）。只要还有秩序，不法就不能完全显露。
- **圣灵或教会：** 许多时代论者（如约翰·沃尔沃德）认为，这是指圣灵藉着教会在世上的工作；「被除去」就是教会被提，然后不法的人才显露。这种看法常常与灾前被提的看法连在一起。批评的人指出，很难说圣灵会「被除去」；持这看法的人回答说，被除去的不是无所不在的圣灵，而是他藉着内住教会所做的拦阻工作。
- **福音的传扬：** 有些学者（如奥斯卡·库尔曼）认为，这是指福音的传扬，甚至指保罗本人的宣教，因为耶稣说「这天国的福音要传遍天下……然后末期才来到」（太24:14）。
- **天使长米迦勒：** 也有学者（如科林·尼科尔）根据但以理书10章和12章，认为这是指保护神子民的天使长米迦勒。
- **神自己：** 也有人认为，拦阻的就是神，或者神的旨意；无论用什么方法，都是神在掌管时间。
- **奥古斯丁的谦卑：** 奥古斯丁坦白承认他不知道保罗指的是什么。帖撒罗尼迦人「知道」，因为保罗口头讲过（2:5）；我们没有听到，所以要谦卑。最重要的一点却很清楚：那不法的人要等到「到了的时候」才可以显露，而这时候是神所定的。

**5. 「主耶稣要用口中的气灭绝他」。** 「那时这不法的人必显露出来。主耶稣要用口中的气灭绝他，用降临的荣光废掉他」（2:8）。保罗用很长的篇幅描写那不法的人，却只用一句话写他的结局：主耶稣只要一口气，就把他灭绝了。
- **旧约的背景：** 以赛亚论到大卫的后裔说：「以口中的杖击打世界，以嘴里的气杀戮恶人。」（赛11:4）主耶稣就是那位弥赛亚王（参启19:15）。
- **不是两个势均力敌的对手：** 那不法的人虽然狂傲，却不是神的对手。主耶稣「降临的荣光」一显现，他就被废掉了。

> 要点：保罗讲那大罪人，不是为了叫我们惧怕，也不是为了叫我们去猜他是谁，而是为了告诉我们：主的日子还没有到；邪恶的时候在神手中；主耶稣一口气就能灭绝那不法的人。

## 深读 2:9-12 不领受爱真理的心

**1. 撒但的运动。** 「这不法的人来，是照撒但的运动，行各样的异能、神迹，和一切虚假的奇事，并且在那沉沦的人身上行各样出于不义的诡诈」（2:9-10）。
- **「来」：** 原文和第8节主耶稣的「降临」是同一个词。那不法的人有他自己的「降临」，是假冒的、模仿的。
- **虚假的神迹：** 神迹本身不能证明信息是真的。耶稣也说：「假基督、假先知将要起来，显大神迹、大奇事，倘若能行，连选民也就迷惑了」（太24:24）。撒但也会「装作光明的天使」（林后11:14）。

**2. 「因他们不领受爱真理的心」。** 「使他们得救」（2:10）。他们沉沦的原因，不是没有机会认识真理，而是不肯爱真理。
- **爱真理：** 保罗说的不只是知道真理，而是爱真理。人可以知道很多关于神的事，心里却不爱他，宁可相信自己想听的话（参提后4:3-4）。
- **真理是一个位格：** 耶稣说：「我就是道路、真理、生命。」（约14:6）爱真理，归根到底就是爱耶稣。

**3. 「神就给他们一个生发错误的心」。** 「故此，神就给他们一个生发错误的心，叫他们信从虚谎，使一切不信真理、倒喜爱不义的人都被定罪」（2:11-12）。这是很严肃的话。
- **先后次序：** 「故此」很重要。不是神先叫人信谎言，叫无辜的人沉沦；而是他们先「不领受爱真理的心」，「喜爱不义」，然后神把他们交给他们自己所选择的。这和罗马书第一章所说的「神任凭他们」（罗1:24、26、28）是一样的道理：神的审判，有时就是让人得到他们坚持要的东西。
- **神仍然掌管：** 「神就给他们」是神主动的审判，不只是任凭；但神不是罪的源头（参威斯敏斯德信条5.4）。连欺骗的工作也在神的主权之下，撒但不能越过神所定的界限。
- **一个安慰：** 若有一位读者心里害怕，担心自己是不是已经被神交给了谎言，这样的害怕本身就说明，你心里还在乎真理。保罗在这里描写的人，不是那些为自己的软弱忧伤的人，而是「喜爱不义」、不愿意得救的人。今天，你仍可以来到那位「就是真理」的耶稣面前（约6:37「到我这里来的，我总不丢弃他」）。

> 要点：人沉沦，是因为不肯爱真理。神没有欺骗任何寻求他的人；他把那些坚持拒绝真理的人交给他们自己所选的谎言。保护我们的不是聪明，而是爱那位就是真理的主。

## 深读 2:13-17 主所爱的弟兄们

**1. 「但」。** 「主所爱的弟兄们哪，我们本该常为你们感谢神」（2:13）。在讲完那些沉沦的人之后，保罗立刻转向帖撒罗尼迦人，语气完全不同。「主所爱的」：在一个可怕的世界里，他们是被主爱着的人。

**2. 「他从起初拣选了你们」。** 这里原文有两种抄本：一种读作「从起初」，和合本照这个读法翻译；一种读作初熟的果子，英文译本照这个读法翻译。两种读法的抄本证据都很强，学者意见不一（如莱昂·莫里斯取前者；杰弗里·韦玛、查尔斯·瓦纳梅克取后者，联合圣经公会希腊文新约的编委会也采用后者）。若是「从起初」，就和以弗所书1:4「从创立世界以前……拣选了我们」相近；若是初熟的果子，就是说他们好像庄稼中最先献给神的那一份，后面还有更多的人要被收进来（参雅1:18；启14:4）。无论哪一种，重点都是：救恩出于神的拣选。
- **拣选的理解：** 改革宗传统（如加尔文）认为，神的拣选是无条件的，不是因为预见人会信，而是出于神的怜悯（参罗9:16）；阿民念传统和卫斯理宗传统认为，神是照着他预知谁会信而拣选（参罗8:29）。两方面都同意：得救的人要因信而得救，也都同意得救的荣耀归于神。

**3. 拣选的方法和目标。** 「叫你们因信真道，又被圣灵感动，成为圣洁，能以得救。神藉我们所传的福音召你们到这地步，好得着我们主耶稣基督的荣光」（2:13-14）。
- **方法：** 被圣灵感动、成为圣洁，和信真道。神的拣选不是叫人坐着不动，而是藉着圣灵的工作和人的信心实现。
- **对比：** 第10-12节的人「不信真理」，这里的人「信真道」；那些人被「虚谎」迷惑，这些人被「福音」呼召。
- **目标：** 「得着我们主耶稣基督的荣光」。那不法的人想要自己的荣耀（2:4），信徒却要分享基督的荣耀。

**4. 「站立得稳」，「都要坚守」。** 「所以，弟兄们，你们要站立得稳，凡所领受的教训，不拘是我们口传的，是信上写的，都要坚守」（2:15）。「教训」原文指传下来的东西，就是使徒所传、他们所领受的福音和教导（参林前11:2；15:1-3）。
- **不同的理解：** 天主教认为这节经文支持圣经和圣传并重（参梵蒂冈第二次大公会议《启示宪章》8；《天主教教理》78、80-82条）：使徒的教导有一部分是口传的，由教会的传统保存下来。新教的唯独圣经原则认为，使徒口传的教导就是他们所传的福音，而这福音已经被圣灵引导写在新约圣经中（参威斯敏斯德信条1.1、1.6；1.6也说不可加上所谓圣灵的新启示，正与2:2「有灵」相关）；所以今天我们是藉着圣经来坚守使徒的教训。
- **对抗错误的方法：** 保罗没有给他们一个新的时间表，而是叫他们回到当初领受的教训。面对新奇的说法，最好的保护是牢牢守住已经领受的福音。

**5. 保罗的祷告。** 「但愿我们主耶稣基督和那爱我们、开恩将永远的安慰并美好的盼望赐给我们的父神，安慰你们的心，并且在一切善行善言上坚固你们」（2:16-17）。
- **先提主耶稣基督：** 保罗这里把主耶稣放在父神前面；后面的动词（第17节）和分词（第16节）都用单数（参帖前3:11）。许多解经家认为，这显出父与子在作为上的合一；至少，保罗把子放在父前面，一同作祷告的对象，这只有在子与父同等的时候才合理。
- **「永远的安慰并美好的盼望」：** 这是「开恩」赐的，是已经给了的。人的安慰会过去，神的安慰是「永远的」。错误的教训叫人惊慌，福音却给人盼望。
- **「善行善言」：** 安慰不是叫人安静地坐着，而是叫人在言语和行为上被坚固，继续行善（参3:13）。

> 要点：在一个充满欺骗的世界里，信徒的安稳不在于自己看透一切，而在于神的爱、神的拣选和神的呼召。所以我们可以站立得稳，守住福音，安心地行善。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因他们不领受爱真理的心，使他们得救。」（2:10）保罗说那些人沉沦，不是因为他们不够聪明，而是因为他们不爱真理。读到这里，我要老实问自己：我爱真理，还是爱我想听的话？我会转发那些证明我对、别人错的文章，却很少去查证；我喜欢听叫我舒服的讲道，听到说中我的罪的地方，就想换一个话题；在家人和弟兄姊妹面前，我常常只说对自己有利的那一半事实。也许我心里有一个地方，是我明知道不对，却不愿意让神的光照进去的。保罗所说的人「喜爱不义」，我不敢说那个人和我毫无关系。可是保罗接着说：「主所爱的弟兄们哪……他从起初拣选了你们。」（2:13）我能爱真理，不是因为我比那些人更诚实，而是因为那位就是真理的主先爱了我（约14:6）。他知道我所有不愿面对的事实，却没有离开我；他看见我最不想被看见的地方，仍然为我舍命。今天，我可以把那个躲着光的地方告诉他，不必害怕。耶稣说「真理必叫你们得以自由」（约8:32），而这真理就是他自己。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 is the center of the letter, in two main sections. **The day of the Lord has not come** (vv. 1–12): some are saying the day of the Lord has come, and Paul urges them not to be shaken or alarmed; before that day the rebellion must come and the man of lawlessness be revealed; something now restrains him, and when it is removed the lawless one will be revealed; the Lord Jesus will kill him with the breath of his mouth; those who refused to love the truth will be deceived by what is false. **Thanksgiving, exhortation and prayer** (vv. 13–17): God chose them to be saved and called them through the gospel to obtain Christ's glory; so they are to stand firm and hold to the traditions they were taught; may our Lord Jesus Christ and God our Father comfort their hearts and establish them in every good work and word.

The first half of this chapter is hard, and interpreters through the centuries have held many different views. But the second half is clear: whatever the details of the end, believers' security lies in God's choosing, God's calling and God's comfort. To read this chapter well we must move, as Paul does, from "the mystery of lawlessness" to "brothers beloved by the Lord."

## Going Deeper: 2:1–3a Let No One Deceive You

**1. What is Paul correcting?** "Now concerning the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and our being gathered together to him, we ask you, brothers, not to be quickly shaken in mind or alarmed, either by a spirit or a spoken word, or a letter seeming to be from us, to the effect that the day of the Lord has come" (2:1–2).
- **"Coming":** the same word the first letter uses many times (1 Thess 2:19; 3:13; 4:15; 5:23), used at the time for a ruler's arrival at a city.
- **"Being gathered":** believers being gathered to the Lord, the same event as 1 Thessalonians 4:17, "caught up together... in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air."
- **"Has come":** the Greek means not "is about to come" but "has arrived," "is here."

**2. Where did the false teaching come from?** Paul lists three possible sources: "a spirit," that is, a prophecy claimed to come from the Holy Spirit; "a spoken word," someone's message or teaching; or "a letter seeming to be from us," a forged letter in Paul's name, or a distortion of a letter Paul had written (see 2 Pet 3:16). Paul is not sure which, but whatever the source, they are not to believe it.
- **Testing prophecy:** in the first letter Paul said, "Do not despise prophecies, but test everything" (1 Thess 5:20–21). Here is a case in point: a message claimed to come from the Spirit must be rejected if it contradicts the apostolic teaching.

**3. "Let no one deceive you in any way."** To deceive is to mislead, to lead someone down the wrong path. Jesus himself warned, "See that no one leads you astray. For many will come in my name... and they will lead many astray" (Matt 24:4–5).
- **Why is "the day has come" wrong?** It could make people think their present suffering was the final judgment, that the Lord had come and they had not been gathered; or it could make them think they no longer needed to work or live ordinary lives (see 3:6–12). Either way, believers would lose their peace and leave the right path.

> The point: Even spiritual-sounding words must be tested. Any message about the end that spreads alarm and leads away from the apostles' teaching, however spiritual it sounds, should not be believed lightly.

## Going Deeper: 2:3b–8 The Man of Lawlessness and the Restrainer

**1. "The rebellion."** "For that day will not come, unless the rebellion comes first, and the man of lawlessness is revealed, the son of destruction" (2:3). "Rebellion" (*apostasia*) usually means revolt or defection.
- **Rebellion against whom?** Some take it as a large-scale apostasy within the church, many professing believers abandoning the faith (see Matt 24:10–12); others as an open revolt of the whole world against God. Both may be in view.
- **And the timing of the rapture:** this passage is also central in the debate over the timing of the rapture (see the discussion at 1 Thessalonians 4). Posttribulationists (such as George Ladd and Douglas Moo) and those who hold one undivided coming (such as Anthony Hoekema) argue that "our being gathered" in verse 1 and "the day of the Lord" in verse 2 are one event, so that believers are gathered to the Lord after the rebellion and the revealing of the man of lawlessness. Pretribulationists (such as John Walvoord) distinguish the gathering in verse 1 from the day of the Lord in verses 2–3, holding that believers are caught up before that day. Some dispensational writers (such as Kenneth Wuest) even read *apostasia* as "departure," meaning the rapture; but most interpreters, including many dispensationalists, reject this, because the word means religious rebellion in the Greek Old Testament and the New Testament (see Josh 22:22; Acts 21:21). The point of the passage is not to draw up a timetable but to say that the day has not yet come.
- **"That day will not come":** the Greek sentence breaks off, and English versions supply "that day will not come, unless..." Paul's meaning is clear: these things have not yet happened, so the day of the Lord has not yet come.

**2. "The man of lawlessness... the son of destruction."** The CUV's "great sinner" follows later manuscripts that read "man of sin" (such as Alexandrinus and the Byzantine majority); earlier manuscripts (such as Sinaiticus and Vaticanus) read "man of lawlessness," which most modern versions follow, matching "the mystery of lawlessness" and "the lawless one" in verses 7–8. "Son of destruction" means he is destined for ruin (see John 17:12).
- **What he is like:** he "opposes and exalts himself against every so-called god or object of worship, so that he takes his seat in the temple of God, proclaiming himself to be God" (2:4). This echoes Daniel's description of the king who "shall exalt himself and magnify himself above every god" (Dan 11:36), and Ezekiel's word to the prince of Tyre: "Because your heart is proud, and you have said, 'I am a god'" (Ezek 28:2).
- **Shadows in history:** in 167 BC Antiochus IV set up an idolatrous altar in the Jerusalem temple; in AD 40 the emperor Caligula ordered his own statue placed in the temple, though the plan failed. The Thessalonians may well have heard of such things (see Dan 9:27; 11:31; Mark 13:14, "the abomination of desolation"). Paul is describing the final and fullest form of this arrogance.

**3. Who is the man of lawlessness?** Believers through the ages have held several main views.
- **A future individual:** many early church fathers (such as Irenaeus) and many evangelical scholars today hold that this is a specific person who will appear before the Lord's return, the one John calls "antichrist" (1 John 2:18). Among those who hold this view, some think he will sit in a rebuilt temple in Jerusalem (such as dispensationalists), while others take "takes his seat in the temple of God" as Old Testament language for arrogantly claiming God's place.
- **An institution in church history:** at the Reformation, Martin Luther, Calvin and many Reformed confessions (such as the Westminster Confession of 1646, chapter 25, section 6) identified this with the papacy, which sits in "the temple of God," the church, claiming authority that belongs to God. Most Reformed scholars today no longer make this identification. Later American Presbyterian revisions removed the clause: the Presbyterian Church in the U.S.A. removed it from 25.6 in 1903, and it is also absent from the texts used today by the Orthodox Presbyterian Church and the Presbyterian Church in America.
- **A past figure:** preterists hold that this refers to a person or power before AD 70, such as the Romans who captured Jerusalem and destroyed the temple. But Paul says the Lord Jesus will "bring to nothing" this figure "by the appearance of his coming" (2:8), which is hard to fit with the events of AD 70, so most scholars do not accept this view.
- **Common ground:** all these views agree on several things: evil will show itself in concentrated form at the end of history; its essence is a human being seeking to sit in God's place; and it will finally be destroyed by the Lord Jesus. John also says that "now many antichrists have come" (1 John 2:18), so this spirit of arrogance is already at work today.

**4. "What is restraining."** "And you know what is restraining him now so that he may be revealed in his time. For the mystery of lawlessness is already at work. Only he who now restrains it will do so until he is out of the way" (2:6–7). In verse 6 Paul uses the neuter ("what is restraining"); in verse 7 the masculine ("he who now restrains"). So the restrainer may be both a force and connected with a person.
- **The Roman Empire and legal order:** many in the early church (such as Tertullian and Chrysostom) took this as the Roman Empire, or more broadly the order upheld by government and law (see Rom 13:1–4). As long as order remains, lawlessness cannot fully show itself.
- **The Holy Spirit or the church:** many dispensationalists (such as John Walvoord) take this as the Holy Spirit working through the church in the world; "out of the way" is the rapture of the church, after which the lawless one is revealed. This view is often tied to a pretribulational rapture. Critics point out that it is hard to speak of the Holy Spirit being "out of the way"; proponents reply that what is removed is not the omnipresent Spirit but his restraining ministry through the indwelt church.
- **The preaching of the gospel:** some scholars (such as Oscar Cullmann) take it as the preaching of the gospel, even Paul's own mission, since Jesus said, "this gospel of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the whole world... and then the end will come" (Matt 24:14).
- **The archangel Michael:** some scholars (such as Colin Nicholl), drawing on Daniel 10 and 12, take it as Michael, the archangel who protects God's people.
- **God himself:** others think the restrainer is God, or God's will; by whatever means, God governs the timing.
- **Augustine's humility:** Augustine admitted frankly that he did not know what Paul meant. The Thessalonians "know," because Paul had told them in person (2:5); we did not hear it, so we must be humble. Yet the most important point is clear: the lawless one can be revealed only "in his time," and that time is set by God.

**5. "The Lord Jesus will kill with the breath of his mouth."** "And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will kill with the breath of his mouth and bring to nothing by the appearance of his coming" (2:8). Paul spends many words describing the lawless one, but only one sentence on his end: with a single breath the Lord Jesus destroys him.
- **Old Testament background:** Isaiah says of David's descendant, "he shall strike the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips he shall kill the wicked" (Isa 11:4). The Lord Jesus is that messianic king (see Rev 19:15).
- **Not two equal opponents:** for all his arrogance, the lawless one is no match for God. When "the appearance of his coming" shines out, he is brought to nothing.

> The point: Paul speaks of the man of lawlessness not to make us afraid or to set us guessing who he is, but to tell us that the day of the Lord has not yet come, that the time of evil is in God's hands, and that the Lord Jesus can destroy the lawless one with a breath.

## Going Deeper: 2:9–12 Refusing to Love the Truth

**1. The activity of Satan.** "The coming of the lawless one is by the activity of Satan with all power and false signs and wonders, and with all wicked deception for those who are perishing" (2:9–10).
- **"Coming":** the same word as the Lord Jesus' "coming" in verse 8. The lawless one has his own "coming," a counterfeit and an imitation.
- **False signs:** miracles by themselves do not prove a message true. Jesus said, "false christs and false prophets will arise and perform great signs and wonders, so as to lead astray, if possible, even the elect" (Matt 24:24). Satan too "disguises himself as an angel of light" (2 Cor 11:14).

**2. "Because they refused to love the truth."** "And so be saved" (2:10). They perish not because they had no chance to know the truth, but because they would not love it.
- **Loving the truth:** Paul speaks not only of knowing the truth, but of loving it. A person can know much about God and yet not love him, preferring to believe what he wants to hear (see 2 Tim 4:3–4).
- **The truth is a person:** Jesus said, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" (John 14:6). To love the truth is, in the end, to love Jesus.

**3. "God sends them a strong delusion."** "Therefore God sends them a strong delusion, so that they may believe what is false, in order that all may be condemned who did not believe the truth but had pleasure in unrighteousness" (2:11–12). These are solemn words.
- **The order matters:** "Therefore" is important. It is not that God first makes people believe a lie and sends innocent people to ruin; rather, they first "refused to love the truth" and "had pleasure in unrighteousness," and then God gave them over to what they had chosen. This is the same principle as "God gave them up" in Romans 1 (Rom 1:24, 26, 28): God's judgment sometimes means letting people have what they insist on.
- **God is still in control:** "God sends" is an active act of judgment, not mere permission; yet God is not the author of sin (see Westminster Confession 5.4). Even the work of deception is under God's sovereignty; Satan cannot go beyond the limits God has set.
- **A comfort:** if a reader is afraid that God has already handed them over to a lie, that very fear shows that they still care about the truth. The people Paul describes here are not those grieving over their weakness, but those who "had pleasure in unrighteousness" and did not want to be saved. Today you may still come to Jesus, who is the truth (John 6:37, "whoever comes to me I will never cast out").

> The point: People perish because they will not love the truth. God deceives no one who seeks him; he gives those who persist in rejecting the truth over to the lie they have chosen. What protects us is not cleverness but love for the Lord who is the truth.

## Going Deeper: 2:13–17 Brothers Beloved by the Lord

**1. "But."** "But we ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers beloved by the Lord" (2:13). Having spoken of those who are perishing, Paul turns at once to the Thessalonians, and his tone changes completely. "Beloved by the Lord": in a frightening world, they are people the Lord loves.

**2. "God chose you as the first fruits."** The manuscripts differ here: some read "from the beginning," which the CUV follows; others read "as first fruits," which the ESV follows. Both readings have strong manuscript support, and scholars are divided (Leon Morris prefers the first; Jeffrey Weima and Charles Wanamaker the second, which the editorial committee of the UBS Greek New Testament also printed). If "from the beginning," it is close to Ephesians 1:4, "he chose us in him before the foundation of the world"; if "first fruits," they are like the first portion of the harvest offered to God, a pledge of more to come (see Jas 1:18; Rev 14:4). Either way, the point is that salvation comes from God's choosing.
- **How election is understood:** the Reformed tradition (such as Calvin) holds that God's election is unconditional, not based on foreseen faith but flowing from God's mercy (see Rom 9:16); the Arminian and Wesleyan traditions hold that God chose according to his foreknowledge of who would believe (see Rom 8:29). Both agree that those who are saved are saved through faith, and both give the glory of salvation to God.

**3. The means and the goal of election.** "Through sanctification by the Spirit and belief in the truth. To this he called you through our gospel, so that you may obtain the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ" (2:13–14).
- **The means:** sanctification by the Spirit and belief in the truth. God's choosing does not leave people passive; it is carried out through the Spirit's work and human faith.
- **The contrast:** the people in verses 10–12 "did not believe the truth"; these "believe the truth." Those were deceived by "what is false"; these are called by "the gospel."
- **The goal:** "that you may obtain the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ." The lawless one seeks his own glory (2:4); believers will share in Christ's glory.

**4. "Stand firm and hold to the traditions."** "So then, brothers, stand firm and hold to the traditions that you were taught by us, either by our spoken word or by our letter" (2:15). "Traditions" means what has been handed on, the gospel and teaching the apostles passed on and the Thessalonians received (see 1 Cor 11:2; 15:1–3).
- **Different understandings:** Catholic teaching sees this verse as supporting Scripture and Sacred Tradition together (see Vatican II, *Dei Verbum* 8; *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 78, 80–82): part of the apostles' teaching was oral, and is preserved in the church's tradition. The Protestant principle of *sola Scriptura* holds that the apostles' oral teaching was the gospel they preached, and that this gospel has been written down in the New Testament under the Spirit's guidance (see Westminster Confession 1.1, 1.6; 1.6 also rules out adding "new revelations of the Spirit," which bears directly on "by a spirit" in 2:2); so today we hold to the apostles' teaching through Scripture.
- **How to resist error:** Paul does not give them a new timetable; he calls them back to the teaching they first received. Faced with novel claims, the best protection is to hold fast to the gospel already received.

**5. Paul's prayer.** "Now may our Lord Jesus Christ himself, and God our Father, who loved us and gave us eternal comfort and good hope through grace, comfort your hearts and establish them in every good work and word" (2:16–17).
- **The Lord Jesus Christ named first:** here Paul puts the Lord Jesus before God the Father, and the verbs (v. 17) and participles (v. 16) that follow are singular (see 1 Thess 3:11). Many commentators see in this the unity of Father and Son in their action; at the least, Paul names the Son before the Father as one addressed together with him in prayer, which makes sense only if the Son is equal with the Father.
- **"Eternal comfort and good hope":** this is given "through grace," and has already been given. Human comfort passes; God's comfort is "eternal." False teaching brings alarm; the gospel brings hope.
- **"Every good work and word":** comfort does not leave people sitting still; it strengthens them in word and deed to keep doing good (see 3:13).

> The point: In a world full of deception, believers' security does not lie in seeing through everything themselves, but in God's love, God's choosing and God's calling. So we can stand firm, hold to the gospel, and do good with quiet hearts.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Because they refused to love the truth and so be saved" (2:10). Paul says these people perish not because they are not clever enough, but because they do not love the truth. Reading this, I have to ask myself honestly: do I love the truth, or what I want to hear? I pass along articles that prove I am right and others wrong, and rarely check them; I like sermons that make me comfortable, and when one names my sin I want to change the subject; before my family and fellow believers I often tell only the half of the facts that favors me. Perhaps there is a place in my heart that I know is wrong, where I do not want God's light to shine. Paul says these people "had pleasure in unrighteousness," and I dare not say that person has nothing to do with me. But Paul goes on: "brothers beloved by the Lord... God chose you" (2:13). I can love the truth not because I am more honest than they are, but because the Lord who is the truth loved me first (John 14:6). He knows every fact I do not want to face, and he has not left me; he sees the place I least want seen, and still gave his life for me. Today I can tell him about that place that hides from the light, without fear. Jesus said, "the truth will set you free" (John 8:32), and that truth is he himself.
