## 本章脉络

第三章分成三段。**请为我们祷告，主是信实的**（1-5节）：保罗请他们为福音的传扬和自己的安全祷告；人不都有信心，但主是信实的，要坚固他们，保护他们脱离那恶者。**不按规矩而行的人**（6-15节）：有人不肯做工，反倒专管闲事；保罗以自己亲手做工为榜样，吩咐他们安静做工、吃自己的饭；对不听从的人，教会要远离他，叫他自觉羞愧，但不要以他为仇人，要劝他如弟兄。**祝福和亲笔问安**（16-18节）：愿赐平安的主随时随事亲自给他们平安；保罗亲笔写下问安，作为这封信的记号。

这一章看起来很平常，讲的是工作、吃饭和教会纪律。但保罗在讲完主的再来之后才讲这些，正是要告诉我们：盼望主来的人，要在平常的日子里忠心。

## 深读 3:1-5 主是信实的

**1. 「请你们为我们祷告」。** 「弟兄们，我还有话说：请你们为我们祷告，好叫主的道理快快行开，得着荣耀，正如在你们中间一样，也叫我们脱离无理之恶人的手」（3:1-2）。保罗是使徒，却请这个年轻的教会为他祷告（参罗15:30；西4:3）。
- **「快快行开」：** 原文是奔跑的意思，好像赛跑的人跑向终点（参诗147:15）。保罗先求的不是自己的安全，而是神的道能畅通无阻，并且「得着荣耀」，就是被人尊重、接受。
- **「正如在你们中间一样」：** 帖撒罗尼迦人自己就是神的道奔跑的见证（参帖前1:8）。
- **「无理之恶人」：** 保罗写这封信的时候在哥林多，那里也有犹太人敌挡他（徒18:6）。

**2. 「人不都是有信心」。** 原文直译是信心不是众人都有的，也可以理解为那（福音的）信仰不是众人都有的。这话说得很平实：福音不会被所有人接受，有些人还会敌挡。保罗不惊讶，也不灰心。

**3. 「但主是信实的」。** 「但主是信实的，要坚固你们，保护你们脱离那恶者」（3:3）。原文里「信心」和「信实」是同一个词根，保罗在这里用了一个文字上的对比：人不都有信心，但主是信实的。
- **「那恶者」：** 可以指恶事，也可以指撒但。多数解经家认为指撒但（参2:9；帖前3:5），和主祷文「救我们脱离凶恶」相近（参太6:13）。
- **保罗请他们为自己代祷，转过来却安慰他们：** 他说完自己的危险，立刻转过来安慰他们。他的眼光不是停在自己身上。

**4. 「我们靠主深信」。** 「我们靠主深信，你们现在是遵行我们所吩咐的，后来也必要遵行」（3:4）。保罗对他们的信心，不是建立在他们自己的能力上，而是「靠主」。他相信主会继续在他们身上做工。

**5. 「叫你们爱神，并学基督的忍耐」。** 「愿主引导你们的心，叫你们爱神，并学基督的忍耐！」（3:5）
- **「引导」：** 原文是修直道路、除去障碍的意思（帖前3:11「引领」是同一个词）。
- **「爱神」：** 原文可以指我们对神的爱，也可以指神对我们的爱。许多解经家认为，这里可能两层意思都有：主引导我们的心进入神的爱里，叫我们因被爱而爱他。
- **「基督的忍耐」：** 可以指基督自己忍受苦难的那种忍耐（参来12:2-3），也可以指等候基督再来的忍耐（英文钦定本这样译）。和合本作「学基督的忍耐」，采取前一种理解。

> 要点：神的道要奔跑，靠的不是我们的力量，而是信实的主。人会不信，也会敌挡，但主要坚固我们，保护我们，引导我们的心进入神的爱里。

## 深读 3:6-12 安静做工，吃自己的饭

**1. 「不按规矩而行」。** 「弟兄们，我们奉主耶稣基督的名吩咐你们，凡有弟兄不按规矩而行，不遵守从我们所受的教训，就当远离他」（3:6）。「不按规矩」原文指士兵离开队伍、不守秩序；有些蒲草纸文献用它指旷工、不尽本分。在这一章，这种不守秩序具体表现为不肯做工（3:10-11）。前书已经劝过他们（帖前4:11-12；5:14），现在保罗的语气更重了：「奉主耶稣基督的名吩咐」。
- **为什么不做工？** 第11节说他们「什么工都不做，反倒专管闲事」。原文是一个双关语：不做工，却在别人的工作上忙。许多解经家认为，这与第二章错误的末世教训有关：既然主的日子到了，何必还要工作？也有学者（如布鲁斯·温特）认为，背后是当时恩主与门客的社会制度。也可能有人觉得，教会的弟兄姊妹既然彼此相爱，就可以一直靠别人的爱心生活。

**2. 保罗的榜样。** 「你们自己原知道应当怎样效法我们。因为我们在你们中间，未尝不按规矩而行，也未尝白吃人的饭，倒是辛苦劳碌，昼夜做工，免得叫你们一人受累」（3:7-8）。保罗在帖撒罗尼迦的时候，一边传福音，一边亲手做工（参帖前2:9；徒18:3「他们本是制造帐棚为业」）。
- **「这并不是因我们没有权柄」：** 保罗在别处说，传福音的人有权柄「靠着福音养生」（林前9:14）。他不用这权柄，「乃是要给你们作榜样，叫你们效法我们」（3:9）。
- **劳动的尊严：** 在当时的希腊罗马社会，有地位的人常常看不起手工劳动。保罗却甘心做一个手艺人。他让信徒看见，平凡的工作是可以荣耀神的（参西3:23；弗4:28）。

**3. 「若有人不肯做工，就不可吃饭」。** 「我们在你们那里的时候，曾吩咐你们说，若有人不肯做工，就不可吃饭」（3:10）。
- **关键在「不肯」：** 保罗说的是不愿意工作的人，不是因为疾病、残障、年老、照顾家人或失业而不能工作的人。对那些人，教会要「扶助软弱的人」（帖前5:14），要像早期教会照顾寡妇一样照顾他们（参提前5:16；徒6:1）。
- **是对教会说的，不是政治口号：** 这句话后来被人拿去作社会口号，例如列宁和1936年的苏联宪法都引用过类似的话，用来要求人民为国家劳动，却去掉了保罗所说的「不肯」。但保罗是在对教会说话：教会的爱心和饭桌，不应该被那些能工作却不肯工作的人利用。这不是叫我们对穷人冷漠，而是叫我们的爱心有智慧。
- **今天的应用：** 这也提醒那些可以工作、却长期依赖家人或教会的人，也提醒那些总是替别人收拾残局、使对方永远学不会负责的人。爱有时需要说「不」。

**4. 「要安静做工，吃自己的饭」。** 「我们靠主耶稣基督吩咐、劝戒这样的人，要安静做工，吃自己的饭」（3:12）。
- **「靠主耶稣基督」：** 保罗不是用自己的权威压人，而是在主里劝勉他们。这是吩咐，也是劝戒。
- **「安静」：** 和「专管闲事」相对。不是到处奔走、插手别人的事，而是专心做自己该做的事（参帖前4:11「立志作安静人」）。
- **工作是爱邻舍的一种方式：** 自己做工，就不叫人受累（3:8）；还能有余分给缺乏的人（弗4:28；徒20:35「施比受更为有福」）。

> 要点：等候主来，不是放下工作，而是在平凡的工作中忠心。保罗不是看不起不能工作的人，而是劝那些能工作却不肯工作的人：安静做工，吃自己的饭，这也是爱人的方式。我们不是靠工作在神面前站立；正因为已经蒙恩，才能把平凡的工作当作献给主的事奉。

## 深读 3:13-15 不要以他为仇人

**1. 「行善不可丧志」。** 「弟兄们，你们行善不可丧志」（3:13）。这话是对那些忠心做工、又一直帮助人的信徒说的。有人利用你的爱心，你很容易灰心，想从此不再帮助任何人。保罗说：不要因此停止行善（参加6:9）。
- **疲倦是真实的：** 长期照顾人、帮助人，会叫人疲倦。保罗没有责备疲倦的人，而是劝他们继续，并且要有智慧地分辨，谁真需要帮助，谁是不肯做工。保罗刚刚为他们祷告，愿主「引导你们的心，叫你们爱神，并学基督的忍耐」（3:5）；能继续行善的力量，不是来自对方的回报，而是来自那位先向我们忍耐的基督。

**2. 「要记下他，不和他交往」。** 「若有人不听从我们这信上的话，要记下他，不和他交往，叫他自觉羞愧」（3:14）。
- **「记下」：** 意思是留意、认出这个人，不是公开羞辱他。
- **「不和他交往」：** 原文是不要和他混在一起。许多解经家认为，这可能包括不让他在教会的饭桌上白吃（参3:10），或者不让他像平常一样参与教会的团契。保罗在这里用的「交往」，和林前5:9、11是同一个词；不同之处在于罪的性质，以及第15节「不要以他为仇人，要劝他如弟兄」。哥林多前书5章是严重的罪，要把人赶出去（林前5:11-13）；这里的语气比较轻，目的是叫他回转。这里是正式的教会处分，还是较轻的疏远，解经家看法不一。
- **「叫他自觉羞愧」：** 羞愧在这里不是为了惩罚，而是为了叫他醒悟。「自觉羞愧」是他自己醒悟、知耻回转，不是教会叫他当众丢脸。华人文化看重面子，教会更要小心，不可把纪律变成羞辱。这和耶稣在马太福音18:15-17所说的步骤相近：先私下劝，再带人同去，最后告诉教会，目的都是「得了你的弟兄」。

**3. 「但不要以他为仇人，要劝他如弟兄」。** 「但不要以他为仇人，要劝他如弟兄」（3:15）。这是全段的关键。教会的纪律不是为了赶走敌人，而是为了挽回弟兄。
- **他仍是弟兄：** 即使他犯了错，不肯听从，他仍是「弟兄」。教会对他的态度，不是厌恶，而是忧伤和盼望（参加6:1「用温柔的心把他挽回过来」）。
- **华人教会的处境：** 有些教会对不守规矩的人睁一只眼闭一只眼，怕伤了和气，怕得罪人；也有些教会一旦有人犯错，就在背后议论，叫他再也抬不起头来，甚至从此离开。保罗两样都不赞成：要劝戒，不要放任；要挽回，不要排斥。
- **纪律的界限：** 这段经文讲的是不肯做工、不听劝的弟兄。若是牵涉到虐待、性侵犯、诈骗等罪行，就不只是教会内部劝戒的事，应当向警察或法庭举报（参罗13:4）。受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必勉强和仍然不安全的人和好。挽回一个犯罪的人，不等于让他恢复原有的职分，或让他再接近受害人和孩子。教会也绝不可以为了面子，把这样的事压下来（「家丑不可外扬」）。

> 要点：教会的纪律要有爱，也要有真理。对不肯悔改的人要认真处理，目的却是挽回，不是排斥；他仍是弟兄，我们要像对待弟兄一样劝他。

## 深读 3:16-18 赐平安的主

**1. 「愿赐平安的主随时随事亲自给你们平安」。** 「愿赐平安的主随时随事亲自给你们平安！愿主常与你们众人同在！」（3:16）这封信处理了人心的惊慌（2:2），最后以平安作结。
- **「亲自」：** 平安不是靠我们把所有问题都处理好才得到的，而是主「亲自」赐的。
- **「随时随事」：** 不是只在顺境中，而是在逼迫中、在错误教训的混乱中、在教会处理纪律的难处中。耶稣说：「在世上，你们有苦难；但你们可以放心，我已经胜了世界。」（约16:33）
- **「众人」：** 包括那些忠心做工的人，也包括那些需要被劝戒的人。保罗的祝福没有把任何人排除在外。

**2. 「我－保罗亲笔问你们安」。** 「我－保罗亲笔问你们安。凡我的信都以此为记，我的笔迹就是这样」（3:17）。保罗常请人代笔（参罗16:22），到最后亲笔写下问安（参林前16:21；加6:11；西4:18；门19）。
- **为什么特别提这一点？** 因为2:2提到有「冒我名的书信」。保罗的亲笔，是这封信真实的记号。
- **怀疑作者的人怎样看？** 主张托名说的学者认为，这句话正是托名的作者故意加上去的，好叫人相信这是保罗写的信。支持保罗为作者的学者回答说，这句话最自然的理解，就是保罗本人对假信的回应（参导读）。

**3. 「愿我们主耶稣基督的恩常与你们众人同在」。** 「愿我们主耶稣基督的恩常与你们众人同在！」（3:18）这和前书的最后一句几乎一样（帖前5:28），只是加上了「众人」。整封信讲了审判、讲了那不法的人、讲了教会纪律，最后一句话却是恩典。

> 要点：信徒的平安不是从环境来的，而是赐平安的主亲自赐的。这封信处理了惊慌，最后以恩典作结；凡属主的人，一生的故事也必这样结束。

## 默想

**默想：** 「但不要以他为仇人，要劝他如弟兄。」（3:15）也许在我的教会里，或者在信主的亲戚中间，有一个人让我失望透了：他借了钱不还，承诺的事从来不做，或者总是让别人替他收拾残局。我帮过他好几次，后来就放弃了。现在我不再和他说话，提起他的时候，口气里只有冷淡和讽刺。我心里已经把他当作一个仇人，或者至少当作一个不值得再费心的人。保罗没有叫帖撒罗尼迦人假装没事，他叫他们认真处理；但他也说：不要以他为仇人。我想起耶稣复活的那个早晨。在他最需要的时候，「门徒都离开他，逃走了」（可14:50）；可是复活的主托人带给他们的话是：「你们去告诉我的弟兄」（太28:10）。他为他们死，也为他们复活，没有把他们记成仇人。他「称他们为弟兄也不以为耻」（来2:11），而这些弟兄里面也有我，一个常常辜负主的人。今天，我不必勉强自己去信任一个仍然不可靠的人，也不必回到一个不安全的关系里；但我可以求主除去我心里的那份敌意，在适当的时候、用适当的方式，再一次像对弟兄一样对他说话；在那以前，至少先在心里、在祷告中，重新称他为弟兄。保罗这封信的最后一句是：「愿我们主耶稣基督的恩常与你们众人同在！」（3:18）这「众人」里面有他，也有我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 falls into three parts. **Pray for us; the Lord is faithful** (vv. 1–5): Paul asks them to pray for the spread of the gospel and for his safety; not all have faith, but the Lord is faithful, and he will establish them and guard them against the evil one. **Those walking in idleness** (vv. 6–15): some will not work and are busybodies instead; Paul points to his own example of working with his hands and commands them to work quietly and earn their own living; the church is to keep away from anyone who will not obey, so that he may be ashamed, yet not regard him as an enemy but warn him as a brother. **Blessing and greeting in Paul's own hand** (vv. 16–18): may the Lord of peace himself give them peace at all times in every way; Paul writes the greeting himself as the sign of the letter's genuineness.

This chapter looks ordinary: work, food, church discipline. But Paul places these things right after the Lord's coming to show us that people who hope for the Lord's coming are faithful in ordinary days.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–5 The Lord Is Faithful

**1. "Pray for us."** "Finally, brothers, pray for us, that the word of the Lord may speed ahead and be honored, as happened among you, and that we may be delivered from wicked and evil men" (3:1–2). Paul is an apostle, yet he asks this young church to pray for him (see Rom 15:30; Col 4:3).
- **"Speed ahead":** the Greek means "run," like a runner racing toward the finish (see Ps 147:15). What Paul asks first is not his own safety but that God's word may run unhindered and "be honored," that is, be respected and received.
- **"As happened among you":** the Thessalonians themselves are evidence of God's word running (see 1 Thess 1:8).
- **"Wicked and evil men":** Paul was in Corinth when he wrote, and Jews there opposed him too (Acts 18:6).

**2. "Not all have faith."** The Greek could also be read "not all have the faith." It is a plain statement: the gospel will not be received by everyone, and some will oppose it. Paul is neither surprised nor discouraged.

**3. "But the Lord is faithful."** "But the Lord is faithful. He will establish you and guard you against the evil one" (3:3). In Greek "faith" and "faithful" share a root, and Paul plays on the words: not all have faith, but the Lord is faithful.
- **"The evil one":** this could mean evil in general or Satan. Most commentators take it as Satan (see 2:9; 1 Thess 3:5), close to the Lord's Prayer, "deliver us from evil" (see Matt 6:13).
- **Paul asks prayer for his own needs, yet turns to comfort them:** having spoken of his own danger, he turns at once to comfort them. His eyes do not stay on himself.

**4. "We have confidence in the Lord about you."** "And we have confidence in the Lord about you, that you are doing and will do the things that we command" (3:4). Paul's confidence in them does not rest on their own ability but is "in the Lord." He trusts that the Lord will go on working in them.

**5. "To the love of God and to the steadfastness of Christ."** "May the Lord direct your hearts to the love of God and to the steadfastness of Christ" (3:5).
- **"Direct":** the word means to make a road straight, to clear obstacles (the same word as "direct" in 1 Thess 3:11).
- **"The love of God":** this could mean our love for God or God's love for us. Many commentators think both may be in view: the Lord directs our hearts into God's love, so that, being loved, we love him.
- **"The steadfastness of Christ":** this could mean the steadfastness Christ himself showed in suffering (see Heb 12:2–3), or patient waiting for Christ's return (as the King James Version translates it). The CUV, "learn the steadfastness of Christ," takes the first view.

> The point: God's word runs not by our strength but by the faithful Lord. People will disbelieve and oppose, but the Lord will establish us, guard us, and direct our hearts into God's love.

## Going Deeper: 3:6–12 Work Quietly and Earn Your Own Living

**1. "Walking in idleness."** "Now we command you, brothers, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you keep away from any brother who is walking in idleness and not in accord with the tradition that you received from us" (3:6). The word for "idleness" describes a soldier out of rank, disorderly; some papyri use it of skipping work or neglecting one's duty. In this chapter the disorder shows itself concretely as refusing to work (3:10–11). The first letter had already urged them (1 Thess 4:11–12; 5:14); now Paul's tone is stronger: "we command you... in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ."
- **Why were they not working?** Verse 11 says they were "not busy at work, but busybodies," a wordplay in Greek: "not working, but working around" in others' business. Many commentators link this with the false teaching about the end in chapter 2: if the day of the Lord has come, why work? Some scholars (such as Bruce Winter) point to the patron–client customs of the time. Others may have felt that, since church members loved one another, they could simply live on that love indefinitely.

**2. Paul's example.** "For you yourselves know how you ought to imitate us, because we were not idle when we were with you, nor did we eat anyone's bread without paying for it, but with toil and labor we worked night and day, that we might not be a burden to any of you" (3:7–8). In Thessalonica Paul preached the gospel while working with his own hands (see 1 Thess 2:9; Acts 18:3, "they were tentmakers by trade").
- **"Not because we do not have that right":** elsewhere Paul says those who preach the gospel have the right to "get their living by the gospel" (1 Cor 9:14). He did not use this right, "but to give you in ourselves an example to imitate" (3:9).
- **The dignity of labor:** in Greco-Roman society people of standing often looked down on manual work. Paul willingly worked as a craftsman. He showed believers that ordinary work can glorify God (see Col 3:23; Eph 4:28).

**3. "If anyone is not willing to work, let him not eat."** "For even when we were with you, we would give you this command: If anyone is not willing to work, let him not eat" (3:10).
- **The key is "not willing":** Paul is speaking of those who will not work, not those who cannot work because of illness, disability, age, caring for family, or unemployment. For them the church is to "help the weak" (1 Thess 5:14), and to care for them as the early church cared for widows (see 1 Tim 5:16; Acts 6:1).
- **For the church, not a political slogan:** this saying was later taken up as a social slogan; Lenin and the 1936 Soviet constitution, for example, used similar words to demand that people labor for the state, dropping Paul's "not willing." But Paul is speaking to the church: the church's love and its table should not be exploited by those who can work and will not. This does not make us cold toward the poor; it makes our love wise.
- **Today:** this also speaks to those who could work but rely long-term on family or church, and to those who always clean up after others so that the other person never learns responsibility. Love sometimes has to say "no."

**4. "Do their work quietly and earn their own living."** "Now such persons we command and encourage in the Lord Jesus Christ to do their work quietly and to earn their own living" (3:12).
- **"In the Lord Jesus Christ":** Paul does not press them with his own authority, but exhorts them in the Lord. It is a command, and also an appeal.
- **"Quietly":** the opposite of being a busybody. Not running around meddling in others' affairs, but attending to one's own work (see 1 Thess 4:11, "aspire to live quietly").
- **Work as a way of loving one's neighbor:** working for one's own living means not being a burden (3:8), and even having something to share with those in need (Eph 4:28; Acts 20:35, "It is more blessed to give than to receive").

> The point: Waiting for the Lord does not mean laying down our work but being faithful in ordinary work. Paul does not look down on those who cannot work; he urges those who can work but will not: work quietly and earn your own living. This too is a way of loving others. We do not stand before God by our work; because we have already received grace, we can offer our ordinary work to the Lord.

## Going Deeper: 3:13–15 Do Not Regard Him as an Enemy

**1. "Do not grow weary in doing good."** "As for you, brothers, do not grow weary in doing good" (3:13). This is said to believers who work faithfully and keep helping others. When someone exploits your kindness, it is easy to become discouraged and decide never to help anyone again. Paul says: do not stop doing good because of it (see Gal 6:9).
- **Weariness is real:** long years of caring for and helping others make people tired. Paul does not rebuke the weary; he urges them to keep going, and to discern wisely who truly needs help and who will not work. Paul has just prayed that the Lord would "direct your hearts to the love of God and to the steadfastness of Christ" (3:5); the strength to keep doing good comes not from being repaid, but from Christ, who has been steadfast toward us first.

**2. "Take note of that person, and have nothing to do with him."** "If anyone does not obey what we say in this letter, take note of that person, and have nothing to do with him, that he may be ashamed" (3:14).
- **"Take note":** to mark or recognize the person, not to humiliate him publicly.
- **"Have nothing to do with him":** the Greek means "do not mix with him." Many commentators think this may include not letting him eat freely at the church's table (see 3:10), or not letting him take part in fellowship as usual. Paul uses the same verb in 1 Corinthians 5:9 and 11; the difference lies in the nature of the offense and in verse 15, "Do not regard him as an enemy, but warn him as a brother." In 1 Corinthians 5 the sin is serious and the person is to be put out (1 Cor 5:11–13); here the tone is milder, and the aim is his return. Whether this means formal church discipline or a lighter social distance, commentators differ.
- **"That he may be ashamed":** shame here is not punishment for its own sake but a means of waking him up; it is inner conviction, not public loss of face, and in a culture that prizes face the church must take special care that discipline never becomes humiliation. This is close to the steps Jesus gives in Matthew 18:15–17: speak privately first, then take others along, and finally tell the church; the aim throughout is that "you have gained your brother" (Matt 18:15).

**3. "Do not regard him as an enemy, but warn him as a brother."** "Do not regard him as an enemy, but warn him as a brother" (3:15). This is the key to the passage. Church discipline is not for driving out enemies but for winning back brothers.
- **He is still a brother:** even when he is in the wrong and will not listen, he is still a "brother." The church's attitude toward him is not disgust but grief and hope (see Gal 6:1, "restore him in a spirit of gentleness").
- **In Chinese churches:** some churches turn a blind eye to those who will not be corrected, for fear of spoiling the peace or offending people; others, once someone has done wrong, talk behind his back until he can never hold his head up again, and he leaves for good. Paul approves of neither: admonish rather than tolerate; restore rather than shut out.
- **The limits of this discipline:** this passage concerns a brother who will not work and will not be corrected. Where abuse, sexual assault, fraud or other crimes are involved, it is no longer only a matter for church admonition; it should be reported to the police or the courts (see Rom 13:4). Victims need not stay in danger, nor force themselves to reconcile with someone who is still unsafe. Restoring someone who has sinned does not mean returning him to his former office or giving him access again to victims or children. And the church must never hush such things up to save face (「家丑不可外扬」).

> The point: Church discipline must have both love and truth. It deals seriously with those who will not repent, but its aim is restoration, not exclusion; such a person is still a brother, and we warn him as a brother.

## Going Deeper: 3:16–18 The Lord of Peace

**1. "May the Lord of peace himself give you peace at all times in every way."** "Now may the Lord of peace himself give you peace at all times in every way. The Lord be with you all" (3:16). The letter deals with alarm (2:2) and ends with peace.
- **"Himself":** peace does not come because we have sorted out every problem; the Lord gives it "himself."
- **"At all times in every way":** not only in good times, but in persecution, in the confusion of false teaching, in the difficulty of church discipline. Jesus said, "In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world" (John 16:33).
- **"All":** including those who work faithfully and those who need to be warned. Paul's blessing leaves no one out.

**2. "I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand."** "I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand. This is the sign of genuineness in every letter of mine; it is the way I write" (3:17). Paul often had someone else write for him (see Rom 16:22), and then wrote the closing greeting himself (see 1 Cor 16:21; Gal 6:11; Col 4:18; Phlm 19).
- **Why point this out?** Because 2:2 mentioned "a letter seeming to be from us." Paul's own hand is the sign that this letter is genuine.
- **How do those who doubt the authorship see it?** Scholars who think the letter was written in Paul's name see this sentence as exactly what such a writer would add to make it believable. Scholars who hold that Paul wrote it reply that the most natural reading is Paul's own response to forged letters (see the introduction).

**3. "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all."** "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all" (3:18). This is almost the same as the last line of the first letter (1 Thess 5:28), with "all" added. The letter has spoken of judgment, of the lawless one and of church discipline, but its last word is grace.

> The point: Believers' peace does not come from their circumstances; the Lord of peace gives it himself. This letter deals with alarm and ends with grace; so will the story of everyone who belongs to him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Do not regard him as an enemy, but warn him as a brother" (3:15). Perhaps in my church, or among my believing relatives, there is someone who has thoroughly disappointed me: he borrowed money and never repaid it, never did what he promised, or always left others to clean up after him. I helped him several times, and then I gave up. Now I no longer speak to him, and when his name comes up my voice holds only coldness and sarcasm. In my heart I have already made him an enemy, or at least someone not worth any more effort. Paul did not tell the Thessalonians to pretend nothing was wrong; he told them to deal with it seriously. But he also said: do not regard him as an enemy. I think of the morning Jesus rose. When he needed them most, his disciples "all left him and fled" (Mark 14:50); yet the message the risen Lord sent them was, "go and tell my brothers" (Matt 28:10). He had died for them and risen for them, and he did not mark them down as enemies. He "is not ashamed to call them brothers" (Heb 2:11), and among those brothers is me, someone who has often failed the Lord. Today I do not have to force myself to trust someone who is still unreliable, nor go back into a relationship that is unsafe. But I can ask the Lord to take away the hostility in my heart, and at a fitting time and in a fitting way to speak to him once more as a brother; and until then, at least begin calling him brother again, in my heart and in my prayers. The last line of Paul's letter is, "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all" (3:18). That "all" includes him, and it includes me.
