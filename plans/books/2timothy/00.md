## 这卷书在讲什么

提摩太后书很可能是保罗写的最后一封信。写信的时候，他又在罗马坐监，这一次不是住在「自己所租的房子里」（徒28:30），而是戴着锁链（1:16），「甚至被捆绑，像犯人一样」（2:9）。他知道自己的日子不多了：「我现在被浇奠，我离世的时候到了。」（4:6）许多同工离开了他，「凡在亚细亚的人都离弃我」（1:15），「底马贪爱现今的世界，就离弃我」（4:10），初次申诉的时候「没有人前来帮助」（4:16）。他请提摩太赶紧来，在冬天以前来，把留在特罗亚的外衣和书卷带来（4:9、13、21）。

在这样的处境中，保罗写信给他「亲爱的儿子」（1:2），信里却很少为自己担忧，满纸都是对提摩太和福音的托付：
- **不要以福音为耻：** 「你不要以给我们的主作见证为耻，也不要以我这为主被囚的为耻；总要按神的能力，与我为福音同受苦难。」（1:8）
- **守住，并传下去：** 要牢牢守着所交托的善道（1:14），又要把所听见的「交托那忠心能教导别人的人」（2:2）。
- **在恩典上刚强：** 「我儿啊，你要在基督耶稣的恩典上刚强起来。」（2:1）
- **住在圣经里：** 末世有危险的日子，人有敬虔的外貌，却背了敬虔的实意（3:1-5）；提摩太却要住在从小明白的圣经里，因为「圣经都是神所默示的」（3:16）。
- **务要传道：** 「无论得时不得时总要专心」（4:2），因为人必「耳朵发痒」，掩耳不听真道（4:3-4）。

这是一封交接的信。老兵要离开战场了，他把旗帜交在年轻人手里。可是保罗最后的盼望不在提摩太身上，而在主身上：「惟有主站在我旁边，加给我力量……主必救我脱离诸般的凶恶，也必救我进他的天国。」（4:17-18）

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 作者问题。** 提摩太后书和提摩太前书、提多书合称教牧书信；关于作者的讨论，可参看提摩太前书的导读。这里只补充两点：
- **这封信的个人色彩最浓：** 信里有许多人名和琐碎的嘱咐（外衣、书卷、皮卷，谁去了哪里，谁病了）。主张托名的学者也承认这一点；有人（如哈里森）认为第四章里有保罗真实书信的片段，后人把它们编进了信里。
- **有些学者把它和另外两封分开：** 例如墨菲-奥康纳认为，提摩太后书是保罗写的，提摩太前书和提多书则不是。
- **支持保罗为作者的学者**（如乔治·奈特、威廉·孟恩斯、菲利普·汤纳）则认为，三封信都出于保罗；提摩太后书的语气不同，是因为处境不同：这是一位将死的使徒写的告别信。
- **本研读的立场：** 照信本身所说的（1:1），把它当作保罗的书信来读。

**2. 第二次坐监。** 使徒行传结束的时候，保罗在罗马被软禁两年，「放胆传讲神国的道……并没有人禁止」（徒28:31）。许多学者认为他后来获释，再出外旅行，写了提摩太前书和提多书；后来又被捕，押回罗马。这一次的处境严酷得多：
- **不容易找到他：** 阿尼色弗在罗马要「殷勤地找我，并且找着了」（1:17）。
- **被当作犯人：** 「像犯人一样」（2:9）的「犯人」，原文和路加福音说与耶稣同钉的「两个犯人」（路23:32）是同一个词。
- **他预料自己会死：** 他不再像腓立比书那样盼望获释（腓1:25；2:24），而是说「我离世的时候到了」（4:6）。

**3. 写作时间。** 主后64年罗马大火以后，尼禄归罪于基督徒，教会受到逼迫。教会的传统说，保罗在尼禄手下殉道，被斩首（优西比乌《教会史》2.22、2.25）。所以许多人把提摩太后书放在主后64-67年之间。

**4. 提摩太在哪里？** 信中提到许米乃（2:17，参提前1:20）、阿尼色弗一家（1:16；4:19）、百基拉和亚居拉（4:19），又说「我已经打发推基古往以弗所去」（4:12），这些线索都叫人想到以弗所。所以许多人认为提摩太当时仍在以弗所，或在附近的亚细亚一带。

**5. 提摩太的处境。** 提摩太前书里的问题还没有解决，甚至更严重了：有人说「复活的事已过」（2:18），他们的话「如同毒疮，越烂越大」（2:17）；有人「偷进人家」，牢笼无知的妇女（3:6）。提摩太自己可能有些胆怯（1:7），也可能因保罗被囚而感到羞耻（1:8）。保罗知道，他走了以后，提摩太要独自面对这一切。

## 钥匙经文：1:12；2:2；4:7-8

**1. 「知道我所信的是谁」。** 「为这缘故，我也受这些苦难。然而我不以为耻；因为知道我所信的是谁，也深信他能保全我所交付他的，直到那日。」（1:12）保罗在锁链中不以为耻，不是因为他对自己有把握，而是因为他认识那一位。他不是说知道我所信的是什么，而是说「知道我所信的是谁」。

**2. 「也要交托那忠心能教导别人的人」。** 「你在许多见证人面前听见我所教训的，也要交托那忠心能教导别人的人。」（2:2）这一节经文里有四代人：保罗、提摩太、忠心的人、别人。福音就是这样一代一代传下来，直传到我们。

**3. 「那美好的仗我已经打过了」。** 「那美好的仗我已经打过了，当跑的路我已经跑尽了，所信的道我已经守住了。从此以后，有公义的冠冕为我存留……不但赐给我，也赐给凡爱慕他显现的人。」（4:7-8）这是一位老兵回头看自己的一生。他不是在夸自己，而是在为提摩太作榜样：你也可以这样跑到终点。那冠冕不只为保罗存留，也为一切爱慕主显现的人存留。

## 全书结构

提摩太后书是一封书信，大致可以这样分：
- **问安和感恩**（1:1-5）：保罗昼夜想念提摩太，记念他的眼泪，想到他心里无伪之信。
- **不要以福音为耻**（1:6-18）：把恩赐再如火挑旺起来；神赐的不是胆怯的心；福音的内容（9-10节）；我知道我所信的是谁；亚细亚的人离弃了保罗，阿尼色弗却不以他的锁链为耻。
- **在恩典上刚强，为福音受苦**（2:1-13）：交托忠心的人；精兵、运动员、农夫；记念从死里复活的耶稣基督；神的道不被捆绑；「可信的话」。
- **作无愧的工人**（2:14-26）：按着正意分解真理的道；许米乃和腓理徒；神坚固的根基立住了；贵重的器皿；主的仆人不可争竞。
- **末世的危险，圣经的能力**（3:1-17）：有敬虔的外貌，却背了敬虔的实意；保罗的榜样和所受的逼迫；圣经都是神所默示的。
- **最后的嘱咐**（4:1-8）：务要传道；那美好的仗我已经打过了。
- **私人的消息和问安**（4:9-22）：底马离弃了保罗，路加仍在；把马可带来；初次申诉时无人帮助，惟有主站在保罗旁边。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 把它当作遗言来读。** 人在临终时说的话，往往是他心里最看重的事。保罗最后要说的，不是自己的成就，也不是对别人的埋怨，而是福音、圣经、忠心传道，和那位站在他旁边的主。读的时候，可以问：一位快要见主的人，为什么最挂念这些事？

**2. 留意「不以为耻」。** 这个主题在第一章出现了三次：「你不要……为耻」（1:8），「我不以为耻」（1:12），阿尼色弗「不以我的锁链为耻」（1:16）。在那个时代，跟随一个被钉十字架的主，与一个被锁链捆着的使徒站在一起，是很丢脸的事。在面子很重要的华人文化中，这个主题很贴近我们。

**3. 留意苦难与恩典的关系。** 保罗没有说跟随基督会一帆风顺，反而说：「凡立志在基督耶稣里敬虔度日的也都要受逼迫。」（3:12）但他一再把提摩太带回恩典：救我们是「按他的旨意和恩典」（1:9），刚强是「在基督耶稣的恩典上」（2:1），最后一句话也是「愿恩惠常与你们同在」（4:22）。

**4. 留意那些人名。** 第一章和第四章有许多人名：有离弃保罗的，有服事保罗的，有曾经离开、后来又成为有益之人的（马可）。神的工作不是一个人的工作。保罗在最孤单的时候，仍然记得每一个人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你在许多见证人面前听见我所教训的，也要交托那忠心能教导别人的人。」（2:2）福音传到我这里，中间经过了多少人？有一个人第一次把耶稣的名字告诉我；也许是一位老师、一个同学、一位街头派单张的阿姨，也许是我的外婆或母亲，像罗以和友妮基那样（1:5）。他们未必知道自己做的事有多大；有的人已经不在了，我从来没有好好谢过他们。也许我是家里第一个信主的，背后没有一位信主的外祖母，我觉得自己的信仰好像没有根。可是罗以的信也是「先」在她心里的，在她以前也有一个人把福音告诉了她。再往前追，这一条线一直追到一位从死里复活的主；他「已经把死废去，藉着福音，将不能坏的生命彰显出来」（1:10）。福音能传到我这里，不是因为每一个人都很忠心，因为中间也有退缩的人、离开的人。保罗在锁链里说「神的道却不被捆绑」（2:9）。真正一代一代保守这福音的，是那位主自己。今天，我可以在主面前说出一个名字：那个把福音带给我的人。我可以为他感谢神；如果他还在，也可以对他说一声谢谢。然后我可以安静地说：主啊，谢谢你没有让这条线在我以前断掉；你也必保守它，在我以后不断掉。

## What This Book Is About

Second Timothy is very likely the last letter Paul wrote. He is again a prisoner in Rome, but this time not in a rented house (Acts 28:30). He is in chains (1:16), "bound with chains as a criminal" (2:9). He knows his time is short: "For I am already being poured out as a drink offering, and the time of my departure has come" (4:6). Many coworkers have left him: "all who are in Asia turned away from me" (1:15); "Demas, in love with this present world, has deserted me" (4:10); at his first defense "no one came to stand by me" (4:16). He asks Timothy to come quickly, to come before winter, and to bring the cloak and the books he left at Troas (4:9, 13, 21).

In this situation Paul writes to his "beloved child" (1:2). Yet the letter says little about his own worries. It is full of a charge to Timothy and a concern for the gospel:
- **Do not be ashamed of the gospel:** "Therefore do not be ashamed of the testimony about our Lord, nor of me his prisoner, but share in suffering for the gospel by the power of God" (1:8).
- **Guard it, and pass it on:** guard the good deposit (1:14), and entrust what you have heard "to faithful men who will be able to teach others also" (2:2).
- **Be strong in grace:** "You then, my child, be strengthened by the grace that is in Christ Jesus" (2:1).
- **Live in Scripture:** in the last days there will be times of difficulty, when people have "the appearance of godliness, but [deny] its power" (3:1–5); but Timothy is to continue in the Scriptures he has known from childhood, because "all Scripture is breathed out by God" (3:16).
- **Preach the word:** "be ready in season and out of season" (4:2), because people will have "itching ears" and turn away from the truth (4:3–4).

This is a letter of handing over. The old soldier is leaving the field and puts the standard into a younger man's hands. Yet Paul's final hope is not in Timothy but in the Lord: "But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me... The Lord will rescue me from every evil deed and bring me safely into his heavenly kingdom" (4:17–18).

## Author and Setting

**1. Authorship.** Second Timothy, First Timothy and Titus are together called the Pastoral Letters; for the authorship debate, see the introduction to 1 Timothy. Two further points here:
- **This is the most personal of the three:** it is full of names and small instructions (a cloak, books, parchments; who has gone where; who is ill). Scholars who hold that the letters are pseudonymous recognize this too; some (such as P.N. Harrison) thought chapter 4 contains genuine fragments of Paul's letters, later worked into the letter.
- **Some scholars separate it from the other two:** Jerome Murphy-O'Connor, for example, argued that 2 Timothy is by Paul, while 1 Timothy and Titus are not.
- **Scholars who hold to Pauline authorship** (such as George Knight, William Mounce and Philip Towner) take all three letters as Paul's. They explain the different tone of 2 Timothy by its different setting: it is the farewell letter of an apostle facing death.
- **The position of this study:** we read it as the letter itself presents itself (1:1), as a letter of Paul.

**2. A second imprisonment.** When Acts ends, Paul has spent two years under house arrest in Rome, "proclaiming the kingdom of God... with all boldness and without hindrance" (Acts 28:31). Many scholars think he was later released and traveled again, writing 1 Timothy and Titus, and was then arrested again and brought back to Rome. This time conditions were much harsher:
- **He was hard to find:** Onesiphorus, in Rome, "searched for me earnestly and found me" (1:17).
- **He was treated as a criminal:** the word "criminal" in 2:9 is the same Greek word Luke uses for the two "criminals" crucified with Jesus (Luke 23:32).
- **He expected to die:** he no longer hopes for release as he did in Philippians (Phil 1:25; 2:24), but says, "the time of my departure has come" (4:6).

**3. Date.** After the great fire of Rome in AD 64, Nero blamed the Christians, and the church was persecuted. Church tradition says Paul was martyred under Nero, by beheading (Eusebius, *Church History* 2.22; 2.25). So many date 2 Timothy between AD 64 and 67.

**4. Where was Timothy?** The letter mentions Hymenaeus (2:17; see 1 Tim 1:20), the household of Onesiphorus (1:16; 4:19), and Prisca and Aquila (4:19), and says, "Tychicus I have sent to Ephesus" (4:12). These hints all point to Ephesus. So many think Timothy was still in Ephesus, or nearby in the province of Asia.

**5. Timothy's situation.** The problems of 1 Timothy had not been solved; if anything they were worse. Some were saying "that the resurrection has already happened" (2:18), and their talk was spreading "like gangrene" (2:17). Some were creeping into households and capturing weak women (3:6). Timothy himself may have been somewhat timid (1:7), and may have felt ashamed that Paul was in prison (1:8). Paul knew that after he was gone, Timothy would face all this alone.

## Key Verses: 1:12; 2:2; 4:7–8

**1. "I know whom I have believed."** "Which is why I suffer as I do. But I am not ashamed, for I know whom I have believed, and I am convinced that he is able to guard until that Day what has been entrusted to me" (1:12). Paul is not ashamed in his chains, not because he is sure of himself, but because he knows the One he trusts. He does not say "I know *what* I have believed," but "*whom*."

**2. "Entrust to faithful men who will be able to teach others also."** "And what you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses entrust to faithful men who will be able to teach others also" (2:2). This one verse contains four generations: Paul, Timothy, faithful men, and others. This is how the gospel has been handed down, generation after generation, until it reached us.

**3. "I have fought the good fight."** "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith. Henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness... and not only to me but also to all who have loved his appearing" (4:7–8). Here an old soldier looks back on his life. He is not boasting; he is setting an example for Timothy: you too can run to the finish. The crown is laid up not only for Paul but for all who love the Lord's appearing.

## Structure of the Book

Second Timothy is a letter, and it can be outlined like this:
- **Greeting and thanksgiving** (1:1–5): Paul remembers Timothy night and day, remembers his tears, and is reminded of his sincere faith.
- **Do not be ashamed of the gospel** (1:6–18): fan into flame the gift; God did not give a spirit of fear; the content of the gospel (vv. 9–10); I know whom I have believed; those in Asia turned away from Paul, but Onesiphorus was not ashamed of his chains.
- **Be strong in grace; suffer for the gospel** (2:1–13): entrust to faithful men; soldier, athlete, farmer; remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead; the word of God is not bound; a trustworthy saying.
- **A worker with no need to be ashamed** (2:14–26): rightly handling the word of truth; Hymenaeus and Philetus; God's firm foundation stands; a vessel for honorable use; the Lord's servant must not be quarrelsome.
- **Dangers of the last days, the power of Scripture** (3:1–17): the appearance of godliness without its power; Paul's example and the persecutions he endured; all Scripture is breathed out by God.
- **The final charge** (4:1–8): preach the word; I have fought the good fight.
- **Personal news and greetings** (4:9–22): Demas has deserted Paul, Luke alone is with him; bring Mark; at his first defense no one stood by him, but the Lord stood by him.

## How to Read This Book

**1. Read it as last words.** What people say near death is often what matters most to them. Paul's last words are not about his achievements, nor complaints about others. They are about the gospel, Scripture, faithful preaching, and the Lord who stood by him. As you read, ask: why does a man about to meet the Lord care most about these things?

**2. Watch for "not ashamed."** This theme appears three times in chapter 1: "do not be ashamed" (1:8), "I am not ashamed" (1:12), and Onesiphorus "was not ashamed of my chains" (1:16). In that world it was a disgrace to follow a crucified Lord and to stand with an apostle in chains. In a Chinese culture where face matters so much, this theme comes very close to home.

**3. Watch how suffering and grace go together.** Paul does not say that following Christ will be smooth. He says, "all who desire to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted" (3:12). But again and again he brings Timothy back to grace: God saved us "because of his own purpose and grace" (1:9); Timothy is to be strengthened "by the grace that is in Christ Jesus" (2:1); and the last words of the letter are "Grace be with you" (4:22).

**4. Watch the names.** Chapters 1 and 4 are full of names: some who deserted Paul, some who served him, and one who had once left and later became useful again (Mark). God's work is never one person's work. Even at his loneliest, Paul remembers each of them.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And what you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses entrust to faithful men who will be able to teach others also" (2:2). How many people did the gospel pass through before it reached me? Someone first told me the name of Jesus: perhaps a teacher, a classmate, an older woman handing out tracts on the street; perhaps my grandmother or my mother, like Lois and Eunice (1:5). They may never have known how much it mattered. Some of them are gone now, and I never really thanked them. Or perhaps I am the first in my family to believe. There was no believing grandmother behind me, and my faith feels as if it has no roots. But Lois's faith "dwelt first" in her, too. Before her, someone had told her the gospel. Trace the line back far enough and it reaches a Lord risen from the dead, "who abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel" (1:10). The gospel did not reach me because everyone along the way was faithful; some along the line drew back, and some left. In his chains Paul wrote, "But the word of God is not bound!" (2:9). The One who has truly kept this gospel from generation to generation is the Lord himself. Today I can say a name before the Lord: the name of the person who brought me the gospel. I can thank God for them, and if they are still living, I can thank them too. Then I can quietly say: Lord, thank you for not letting the line break before it reached me; and you will keep it from breaking after me.
