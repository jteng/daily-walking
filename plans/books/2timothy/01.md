## 本章脉络

第一章分成五段。**问安和感恩**（1-5节）：保罗称提摩太为「我亲爱的儿子」；他昼夜在祈祷中想念提摩太，记念他的眼泪，想到他心里无伪之信，这信先在他外祖母罗以和母亲友妮基心里。**把恩赐再如火挑旺起来**（6-7节）：因为神赐给我们的，不是胆怯的心，乃是刚强、仁爱、谨守的心。**不要以福音为耻**（8-12节）：要按神的能力为福音同受苦难；神救了我们，不是按我们的行为，乃是按他的旨意和恩典；基督已经把死废去；保罗为这福音受苦，却不以为耻，因为知道他所信的是谁。**牢牢守着善道**（13-14节）：要常常守着纯正话语的规模，靠着住在我们里面的圣灵，守着所交托的善道。**离弃的人和不以为耻的人**（15-18节）：亚细亚的人都离弃了保罗；阿尼色弗却屡次使他畅快，在罗马殷勤地找他，并且找着了。

这一章有一个反复出现的词：「耻」。保罗劝提摩太不要以福音为耻（8节），说自己不以为耻（12节），又称赞阿尼色弗不以他的锁链为耻（16节）。在这三处中间，保罗说明了不以为耻的理由：福音是神「按他的旨意和恩典」救人的好消息（9节），而保罗所信的那一位，能保全他所交付的，直到那日（12节）。

## 深读 1:1-5 想到你心里无伪之信

**1. 「照着在基督耶稣里生命的应许」。** 「奉神旨意，照着在基督耶稣里生命的应许，作基督耶稣使徒的保罗」（1:1），「写信给我亲爱的儿子提摩太」（1:2）。
- **生命的应许：** 保罗是一个预料自己将被处死的囚犯，信的第一句话却讲到「生命」。他作使徒，是为了传扬在基督里的生命；这生命连死也夺不去（10节）。
- **「我亲爱的儿子」：** 提摩太前书称他为「因信主作我真儿子」（提前1:2）。保罗不是提摩太的生父（提摩太的父亲是希腊人，徒16:1），却是带他信主、培养他的属灵父亲（参林前4:17；腓2:22）。

**2. 「我接续祖先用清洁的良心所事奉的神」。** 「我感谢神，就是我接续祖先用清洁的良心所事奉的神。」（1:3）
- **与以色列的信仰相连：** 保罗没有觉得自己背弃了祖宗的信仰。他所事奉的，就是亚伯拉罕、以撒、雅各的神；在他看来，信耶稣正是这信仰的成全（参徒24:14）。
- **「祖先」不是敬拜的对象：** 在华人的处境中，「祖先」很容易叫人想到祭祖。保罗所说的，是接续祖先所事奉的神，而不是事奉祖先。我们可以记念、感谢先人，却只敬拜神；向祖先上香跪拜、求祖先保佑，就是把只属于神的敬拜给了受造之物。
- **「清洁的良心」：** 保罗从前逼迫教会，他也承认那是罪（提前1:13、15）。他这里说的，是他一向真诚地按良心事奉神（参徒23:1）；如今良心被基督的血洁净，他在神面前坦然无惧。

**3. 「记念你的眼泪」。** 「祈祷的时候，不住的想念你，记念你的眼泪，昼夜切切地想要见你，好叫我满心快乐。」（1:3-4）
- **什么眼泪？** 保罗没有说明。许多人认为是他们上一次分别时提摩太流的眼泪（参徒20:37，以弗所长老与保罗分别时也痛哭）。
- **属灵的领袖也会流泪：** 保罗没有因为提摩太流泪就看轻他，反而把这眼泪记在心里。教会中服事的人也是有血有肉的人，会想念，会难过，会害怕；这不是不属灵。

**4. 「这信是先在你外祖母罗以和你母亲友妮基心里的」。** 「想到你心里无伪之信，这信是先在你外祖母罗以和你母亲友妮基心里的，我深信也在你的心里。」（1:5）
- **信仰的传承：** 提摩太的父亲是希腊人，看来并不信主（徒16:1）。提摩太的信仰是从外祖母和母亲那里来的。看来她们从他小时候就教他圣经（3:14-15）。
- **「无伪之信」：** 原文是没有假装的信心。这信不是表演给人看的，也不是从家里继承来的外表。这信「先在」罗以心里，「也在」提摩太心里。每一代人都要亲自信。
- **给作父母和祖父母的：** 许多华人家庭里，常常是母亲或外婆、奶奶先信主，在家里默默地祷告，带孩子去主日学。罗以和友妮基的名字能写进圣经，是因为神看重这样的忠心。可是父母不能替孩子信主；神却常常使用家里一个人长年的祷告和榜样。

> 要点：信仰常常在家里一代一代传下去，但每一代都要有自己的「无伪之信」。罗以和友妮基能做的，是把圣经教给孩子；使这信「也在」提摩太心里的，是神。

## 深读 1:6-7 把恩赐再如火挑旺起来

**1. 「再如火挑旺起来」。** 「为此我提醒你，使你将神藉我按手所给你的恩赐再如火挑旺起来。」（1:6）
- **「挑旺」：** 这个词原指把快熄的火重新拨旺。这不一定表示提摩太的火已经熄了；保罗也可能只是要他继续保持火旺。但在保罗被囚、同工离散、假教师兴起的时候，提摩太很可能感到灰心。
- **「藉我按手」：** 提摩太前书说是「在众长老按手的时候」（提前4:14）。很可能是同一件事，保罗和众长老一同按手；也有人认为是两个不同的场合。
- **恩赐是神给的：** 恩赐不是提摩太的才干，是神藉着按手赐给他的。他不需要另外去找一样新的东西，只要把神已经给他的再挑旺起来。
- **主不吹灭将残的灯火：** 以赛亚论到神的仆人说：「压伤的芦苇，他不折断；将残的灯火，他不吹灭。」（赛42:3；参太12:20）一个灰心的服事者，在主那里不会被丢弃。

**2. 「不是胆怯的心，乃是刚强、仁爱、谨守的心」。** 「因为神赐给我们，不是胆怯的心，乃是刚强、仁爱、谨守的心。」（1:7）
- **「心」还是「灵」？** 和合本译作「心」，原文这个词也可以译作「灵」。许多解经家认为保罗指的是圣灵：神赐给我们的圣灵，不叫我们胆怯，而是带来能力、爱和自制（参14节「那住在我们里面的圣灵」；罗8:15）。也有人认为是指圣灵在人心里所造成的态度。两种理解都说明：这样的心是神赐的，不是靠我们自己鼓起来的勇气。
- **「刚强」：** 原文是能力。提摩太的力量不在于他的性格，而在于神的能力（8节「按神的能力」）。
- **「仁爱」：** 能力若没有爱，就会变成压人的权势。神所赐的能力，是为了爱人、服事人。
- **「谨守」：** 原文的意思是头脑清醒、有节制，也有人译作自律。在恐惧中，人容易慌乱、冲动；圣灵使人清醒、稳重。
- **「我们」：** 保罗说「神赐给我们」，不是只赐给提摩太。性格内向、容易害怕的人，也可以从这节经文得着安慰：圣灵不会把我变成另一个人，却会在我软弱的地方给我力量。

> 要点：神赐给我们的不是胆怯的心。我们仍会害怕，但神没有要我们靠自己鼓起勇气；他把自己的灵赐给我们，在我们软弱的地方给我们能力、爱心和清醒的头脑。

## 深读 1:8-12 不要以福音为耻

**1. 「你不要以给我们的主作见证为耻」。** 「你不要以给我们的主作见证为耻，也不要以我这为主被囚的为耻；总要按神的能力，与我为福音同受苦难。」（1:8）
- **两种羞耻：** 一是以主为耻。在那个时代，传讲一位被钉十字架的主，许多人看为愚拙（林前1:23）。二是以保罗为耻。和一个被锁链捆着、像犯人一样的人来往，会连累自己的名声，甚至招来危险。
- **耶稣的话：** 「凡在这淫乱罪恶的世代，把我和我的道当作可耻的，人子在他父的荣耀里，同圣天使降临的时候，也要把那人当作可耻的。」（可8:38）保罗也说：「我不以福音为耻；这福音本是神的大能。」（罗1:16）
- **「按神的能力」：** 保罗没有说：你要咬紧牙关，鼓起勇气。他说：要按神的能力。这能力就是第7节所说，神所赐的那刚强的心。

**2. 「不是按我们的行为，乃是按他的旨意和恩典」。** 「神救了我们，以圣召召我们，不是按我们的行为，乃是按他的旨意和恩典；这恩典是万古之先，在基督耶稣里赐给我们的。」（1:9）
- **福音的内容：** 保罗劝提摩太不要以福音为耻，就随即说出福音是什么。福音不是一套要人去做的规矩，而是神已经做成的事：神「救了我们」，「召我们」。
- **「不是按我们的行为」：** 我们得救，不是因为我们比别人好，也不是因为我们将来会做得好。我们得救的根据，完全在于神的旨意和恩典。
- **「万古之先」：** 这恩典是在创世以前、在基督里赐给我们的（参弗1:4）。在我还没有出生，还没有做过任何好事或坏事以前，神已经在基督里定意施恩。神拣选的根据和方式，历代信徒有不同的理解：改革宗传统（如加尔文、《威斯敏斯德信条》第三章）认为，神按他自己的旨意拣选人，不是因为预先看见人会信；阿米念传统（如卫斯理）认为，神预先知道谁会信，就在基督里拣选他们。两种看法都承认：救恩完全出于恩典，不是按人的行为。

**3. 「他已经把死废去」。** 「但如今藉着我们救主基督耶稣的显现才表明出来了。他已经把死废去，藉着福音，将不能坏的生命彰显出来。」（1:10）
- **「显现」：** 这里指基督第一次的来临，就是他的降生、受死和复活（参约1:14）。在这封信别处，同一个词也指他将来的显现（4:1、8）。
- **「把死废去」：** 原文的意思是使它失去效力。死还没有完全消失，信徒仍然会死；但基督藉着死，「败坏那掌死权的，就是魔鬼，并要释放那些一生因怕死而为奴仆的人」（来2:14-15）。死仍是仇敌，最后要被毁灭（林前15:26），但已经不能辖制属基督的人。
- **在锁链中说这话：** 保罗写这句话的时候，正戴着锁链，预料自己将被处死。他不是在安稳的书房里谈论死亡，而是面对死亡说：死已经被废去了。

**4. 「知道我所信的是谁」。** 「我为这福音奉派作传道的，作使徒，作师傅。为这缘故，我也受这些苦难。然而我不以为耻；因为知道我所信的是谁，也深信他能保全我所交付他的，直到那日。」（1:11-12）
- **不以为耻的根据：** 保罗不以为耻，不是因为他不觉得痛苦，而是因为他认识那一位。他所信的不只是一套道理，而是一位又真又活的神。
- **「我所交付他的」：** 这半句有两种理解。和合本照传统的理解（如加尔文和英文钦定本），译作「我所交付他的」，就是保罗把自己的生命、灵魂、一生的工作交托给神（参彼前4:19「将自己灵魂交与那信实的造化之主」）。另一种理解是许多现代译本和解经家采用的：译作神交付给我的，就是神托付给保罗的福音；因为14节和提前6:20用同一个词说「所交托你的善道」。两种理解都可以从原文读出来；无论哪一种，保罗的信心都在神身上：是神「能保全」，直到那日。
- **「那日」：** 指主再来、审判的日子（参18节；4:8）。保罗的盼望不在今天的判决，而在那日。

> 要点：我们不以福音为耻，不是因为我们比别人勇敢，而是因为福音是神按他的恩典救我们的好消息，而我们所信的那一位，能保全我们直到那日。

## 深读 1:13-14 牢牢守着善道

**1. 「纯正话语的规模」。** 「你从我听的那纯正话语的规模，要用在基督耶稣里的信心和爱心，常常守着。」（1:13）
- **「规模」：** 原文指一个样式、一个轮廓，像画家先画的草图，或建筑的蓝图。保罗把福音的要点教给了提摩太，提摩太要照着这个样式去教导人。
- **「纯正」：** 原文是健康的意思（参提前1:10；6:3）。纯正的道理使人属灵的生命健康。
- **「用……信心和爱心」：** 守着纯正的道理，不是冷冰冰地守着条文，也不是拿真理去打人。保罗说，要在基督耶稣里的信心和爱心中守着。

**2. 「靠着那住在我们里面的圣灵」。** 「从前所交托你的善道，你要靠着那住在我们里面的圣灵牢牢地守着。」（1:14）
- **「交托你的善道」：** 原文和提摩太前书6:20「所托付你的」是同一个词，指托人保管的贵重财物。福音不是提摩太的私产，是神托付给他的。
- **靠着圣灵：** 提摩太要守住这善道，但他不是靠自己的聪明或意志，而是靠着住在信徒里面的圣灵（参7节）。

> 要点：福音是神托付给我们的宝贵财物。我们靠着住在我们里面的圣灵，在信心和爱心中守住它。

## 深读 1:15-18 离弃的人和不以为耻的人

**1. 「凡在亚细亚的人都离弃我」。** 「凡在亚细亚的人都离弃我，这是你知道的，其中有腓吉路和黑摩其尼。」（1:15）
- **「凡在亚细亚的人」：** 这不太可能是指亚细亚所有的信徒，因为提摩太和阿尼色弗一家很可能都在那里。许多人认为，这是指保罗原本可以倚靠的亚细亚同工或朋友，在他被捕、受审时没有站在他一边（参4:16）；也有人认为是指亚细亚的一些教会离开了保罗的教导。
- **腓吉路和黑摩其尼：** 除了这里，圣经没有再提到这两个人。他们的名字留下来，只因为他们离开了。
- **保罗的痛苦：** 保罗在亚细亚住了将近三年（徒19:10；20:31），「一切住在亚细亚的……都听见主的道」（徒19:10）。如今他在最需要的时候，却被那里的人离弃。被自己服事过的人离弃，是很深的痛。

**2. 「他屡次使我畅快，不以我的锁链为耻」。** 「愿主怜悯阿尼色弗一家的人；因他屡次使我畅快，不以我的锁链为耻，反倒在罗马的时候，殷勤地找我，并且找着了。」（1:16-17）
- **「使我畅快」：** 原文有使人重新得着气力、吸一口清新空气的意思。我们不知道阿尼色弗具体做了什么，也许是带来食物、衣服，也许只是陪伴和安慰。
- **「殷勤地找我，并且找着了」：** 罗马是一座大城，要找到一个被关押的犯人并不容易，而且和这样的犯人来往是危险的。阿尼色弗却不怕麻烦、不怕连累，一直找，直到找着。
- **耶稣的话：** 「我在监里，你们来看我……这些事你们既做在我这弟兄中一个最小的身上，就是做在我身上了。」（太25:36、40）

**3. 「愿主使他在那日得主的怜悯」。** 「愿主使他在那日得主的怜悯。他在以弗所怎样多多地服事我，是你明明知道的。」（1:18）
- **阿尼色弗还活着吗？** 保罗为他「一家的人」求怜悯（16节），在信末也只问候他「一家的人」（4:19），所以许多人推想阿尼色弗当时已经去世了。但经文没有明说；他也可能只是还没有回到以弗所。
- **两种理解：** 天主教教导可以为亡者祷告（《天主教教理》958、1032，引马加比二书12章46节），也有天主教解经家把1:18看作保罗为已故的阿尼色弗祷告的例子。新教传统，如《威斯敏斯德信条》21.4，认为不应当为亡者祷告；新教的解经家回应说，经文并没有说阿尼色弗已死；即使他已经去世，这句话也只是保罗把他交托给主的怜悯，类似一句祝福，并不能据此建立炼狱或为亡者代求的教义。
- **「那日」：** 保罗在12节说主能保全他所交付的「直到那日」，这里又求主使阿尼色弗「在那日」得着怜悯。无论是保罗还是阿尼色弗，最后所需要的，都是主的怜悯。

> 要点：在许多人离开的时候，阿尼色弗殷勤地寻找被锁链捆着的保罗。这样的爱是从主那里学来的：主自己先来寻找我们，而我们在那日所需要的，也是他的怜悯。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因他屡次使我畅快，不以我的锁链为耻，反倒在罗马的时候，殷勤地找我，并且找着了。」（1:16-17）我身边有没有一个被「锁链」捆住的人：长期住院的，坐过牢的，得了抑郁症不再来聚会的，生意失败、欠了一身债、大家都不好意思提起的？起初还有人去探望，后来去的人越来越少。我也想过去看看他，可是怕场面尴尬，不知道该说什么；有时也怕别人知道我和他来往，会怎样看我。就这样，一个月又一个月过去了。我心里有点愧疚，却又找到许多理由。阿尼色弗在罗马那座大城里，一条街一条街地找，「殷勤地找我，并且找着了」。我读到这里，先想到的不是我应当怎样，而是我自己也曾是那个被找着的人。我曾被罪捆住，自己也羞于见人；主耶稣却没有以我为耻。他说：「人子来，为要寻找、拯救失丧的人。」（路19:10）他像那个牧人，找那只失去的羊，「直到找着」，找着了，「就欢欢喜喜地扛在肩上」（路15:4-5）。为了找着我，他自己被捆绑，被当作犯人，与犯人同钉；他「轻看羞辱，忍受了十字架的苦难」（来12:2）。我的罪债，他在十字架上替我担当了。如果我想起一个名字，我可以先在主面前承认：我躲开了，因为我怕丢脸。主不是只赦免我，他也正在寻找那个人，比我更早，也更殷勤。我去不去，不能决定那个人在主面前的价值；但我可以求主让我跟着他去，哪怕只是发一条信息。如果那个人曾经伤害过我或我的家人，这段经文不是要我回到他身边，主也会差别人去找他；探望一个曾因伤害孩子坐牢的人，也不等于让他再接近孩子。即使我又一次退缩了，那位找着我的主，也不会以我为耻。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 falls into five parts. **Greeting and thanksgiving** (vv. 1–5): Paul calls Timothy "my beloved child"; he remembers him constantly in prayer night and day, remembers his tears, and is reminded of his sincere faith, which dwelt first in his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice. **Fan into flame the gift** (vv. 6–7): for God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control. **Do not be ashamed of the gospel** (vv. 8–12): share in suffering for the gospel by the power of God; God saved us not because of our works but because of his own purpose and grace; Christ has abolished death; Paul suffers for this gospel but is not ashamed, for he knows whom he has believed. **Guard the good deposit** (vv. 13–14): follow the pattern of sound words, and by the Holy Spirit who dwells within us, guard the good deposit. **Those who turned away and one who was not ashamed** (vv. 15–18): all in Asia turned away from Paul; but Onesiphorus often refreshed him, and in Rome searched for him earnestly and found him.

One word keeps returning in this chapter: "ashamed." Paul urges Timothy not to be ashamed of the gospel (v. 8), says that he himself is not ashamed (v. 12), and praises Onesiphorus, who was not ashamed of his chains (v. 16). Between these three, Paul gives the reason not to be ashamed: the gospel is the good news that God saves "because of his own purpose and grace" (v. 9), and the One Paul has believed is able to guard what has been entrusted until that Day (v. 12).

## Going Deeper: 1:1–5 Your Sincere Faith

**1. "According to the promise of the life that is in Christ Jesus."** "Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by the will of God according to the promise of the life that is in Christ Jesus, to Timothy, my beloved child" (1:1–2).
- **The promise of life:** Paul is a prisoner expecting execution, yet the first sentence of his letter speaks of "life." He is an apostle in order to proclaim the life that is in Christ, a life that even death cannot take away (v. 10).
- **"My beloved child":** First Timothy called him "my true child in the faith" (1 Tim 1:2). Paul was not Timothy's natural father (Timothy's father was a Greek, Acts 16:1), but his spiritual father, who led him to faith and trained him (see 1 Cor 4:17; Phil 2:22).

**2. "God whom I serve, as did my ancestors, with a clear conscience."** "I thank God whom I serve, as did my ancestors, with a clear conscience" (1:3).
- **Continuity with Israel's faith:** Paul did not think he had abandoned the faith of his fathers. The God he served was the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob; in his eyes, faith in Jesus was the fulfillment of that faith (see Acts 24:14).
- **Ancestors are not objects of worship:** in a Chinese setting, "ancestors" easily brings to mind ancestor veneration. Paul speaks of serving the God his ancestors served, not of serving the ancestors. We may remember our forebears and give thanks for them, but we worship God alone; burning incense and bowing to ancestors, or asking them for protection, gives to creatures the worship that belongs to God alone.
- **"A clear conscience":** Paul had once persecuted the church, and he confesses that it was sin (1 Tim 1:13, 15). Here he means that he had always sincerely sought to serve God according to conscience (see Acts 23:1), and that now, with his conscience cleansed by Christ's blood, he stands before God without fear.

**3. "As I remember your tears."** "As I remember you constantly in my prayers night and day. As I remember your tears, I long to see you, that I may be filled with joy" (1:3–4).
- **What tears?** Paul does not say. Many think they were the tears Timothy shed when they last parted (compare Acts 20:37, where the Ephesian elders wept as they parted from Paul).
- **Spiritual leaders weep too:** Paul does not think less of Timothy for his tears; he keeps them in his heart. Those who serve in the church are flesh and blood. They miss people, they grieve, they are afraid, and none of this is unspiritual.

**4. "A faith that dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice."** "I am reminded of your sincere faith, a faith that dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice and now, I am sure, dwells in you as well" (1:5).
- **Faith handed down:** Timothy's father was a Greek and apparently not a believer (Acts 16:1). Timothy's faith came through his grandmother and his mother, who evidently taught him the Scriptures from childhood (3:14–15).
- **"Sincere faith":** literally, faith without pretense. It is not a performance for others, nor an outward thing inherited from the family. It "dwelt first" in Lois, and "dwells" in Timothy "as well." Each generation must believe for itself.
- **For parents and grandparents:** in many Chinese families, it is often the mother or grandmother who comes to faith first, praying quietly at home and taking the children to Sunday school. Lois and Eunice are named in Scripture because God values such faithfulness. Parents cannot believe on their children's behalf; but God often uses one person's long years of prayer and example in a home.

> The point: Faith is often handed down in families from one generation to the next, but each generation needs its own "sincere faith." What Lois and Eunice could do was teach the child the Scriptures; it was God who made that faith dwell in Timothy "as well."

## Going Deeper: 1:6–7 Fan into Flame the Gift of God

**1. "Fan into flame."** "For this reason I remind you to fan into flame the gift of God, which is in you through the laying on of my hands" (1:6).
- **"Fan into flame":** the word was used for stirring up a fire that is dying down. It does not necessarily mean Timothy's fire had gone out; Paul may simply want him to keep it burning. But with Paul in prison, coworkers scattered and false teachers rising, Timothy may well have been discouraged.
- **"Through the laying on of my hands":** First Timothy says it was "when the council of elders laid their hands on you" (1 Tim 4:14). Most likely this was one occasion, with Paul and the elders laying on hands together; some think there were two occasions.
- **The gift is God's:** the gift is not Timothy's talent but what God gave him through the laying on of hands. He does not need to find something new; he needs to stir up what God has already given.
- **The Lord does not quench a faint flame:** Isaiah says of God's Servant, "a bruised reed he will not break, and a faintly burning wick he will not quench" (Isa 42:3; see Matt 12:20). A discouraged servant is not thrown away by the Lord.

**2. "Not of fear but of power and love and self-control."** "For God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control" (1:7).
- **"Spirit" or "heart"?** The Chinese Union Version renders it "heart" (心); the Greek word can also mean "spirit." Many commentators think Paul means the Holy Spirit: the Spirit God has given us does not make us timid but brings power, love and self-control (see v. 14, "the Holy Spirit who dwells within us"; Rom 8:15). Others take it as the disposition the Spirit produces in us. Either way, this spirit is God's gift, not courage we work up ourselves.
- **"Power":** Timothy's strength does not lie in his personality but in God's power (v. 8, "by the power of God").
- **"Love":** power without love becomes domineering. The power God gives is for loving and serving people.
- **"Self-control":** the word means a clear, sober, disciplined mind. Fear makes people panic and act rashly; the Spirit makes them clear-headed and steady.
- **"Us":** Paul says "God gave *us*," not only Timothy. The introverted and the easily frightened can take comfort here: the Spirit does not turn me into someone else, but gives me strength where I am weak.

> The point: The spirit God gave us is not one of fear. We will still be afraid at times, but God does not ask us to summon courage by ourselves; he has given us his Spirit, who gives power, love and a clear mind where we are weak.

## Going Deeper: 1:8–12 Do Not Be Ashamed of the Gospel

**1. "Do not be ashamed of the testimony about our Lord."** "Therefore do not be ashamed of the testimony about our Lord, nor of me his prisoner, but share in suffering for the gospel by the power of God" (1:8).
- **Two kinds of shame:** one is being ashamed of the Lord: in that world, preaching a crucified Lord seemed foolish to many (1 Cor 1:23). The other is being ashamed of Paul: associating with a man in chains, treated as a criminal, could ruin one's reputation and even bring danger.
- **Jesus' words:** "For whoever is ashamed of me and of my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, of him will the Son of Man also be ashamed when he comes in the glory of his Father with the holy angels" (Mark 8:38). Paul also says, "For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation" (Rom 1:16).
- **"By the power of God":** Paul does not say, "Grit your teeth and be brave." He says, "by the power of God." This is the power of verse 7, the spirit of power God has given.

**2. "Not because of our works but because of his own purpose and grace."** "Who saved us and called us to a holy calling, not because of our works but because of his own purpose and grace, which he gave us in Christ Jesus before the ages began" (1:9).
- **The content of the gospel:** as soon as Paul urges Timothy not to be ashamed of the gospel, he says what the gospel is. It is not a set of rules for us to keep, but what God has done: God "saved us" and "called us."
- **"Not because of our works":** we are not saved because we are better than others, nor because we will do well in the future. The ground lies wholly in God's purpose and grace.
- **"Before the ages began":** this grace was given us in Christ before creation (see Eph 1:4). Before I was born, before I had done anything good or bad, God had already purposed to be gracious in Christ. Believers have understood the basis and manner of God's choosing differently: the Reformed tradition (for example, Calvin and the Westminster Confession, chapter 3) holds that God chose according to his own purpose, not because he foresaw that people would believe; the Arminian tradition (for example, Wesley) holds that God foreknew who would believe and chose them in Christ. Both agree that salvation is wholly of grace, not according to our works.

**3. "Who abolished death."** "And which now has been manifested through the appearing of our Savior Christ Jesus, who abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel" (1:10).
- **"Appearing":** here it means Christ's first coming: his birth, death and resurrection (see John 1:14). Elsewhere in this letter the same word refers to his future appearing (4:1, 8).
- **"Abolished death":** the word means to render powerless. Death has not yet disappeared; believers still die. But through death Christ destroyed "the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil," and delivered "all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery" (Heb 2:14–15). Death is still an enemy, "the last enemy to be destroyed" (1 Cor 15:26), but it can no longer rule those who belong to Christ.
- **Said in chains, facing death:** Paul wrote this in chains, expecting to be executed. He was not discussing death in a comfortable study; he faced death and said that death has been abolished.

**4. "I know whom I have believed."** "For which I was appointed a preacher and apostle and teacher, which is why I suffer as I do. But I am not ashamed, for I know whom I have believed, and I am convinced that he is able to guard until that Day what has been entrusted to me" (1:11–12).
- **The ground of not being ashamed:** Paul is not ashamed, not because he feels no pain, but because he knows the One he trusts. What he believes is not just a set of teachings but the true and living God.
- **"What has been entrusted to me":** this phrase can be read two ways. The Chinese Union Version, following the traditional reading (as in Calvin and the KJV), renders it "what I have entrusted to him": Paul has committed his life, his soul and his life's work to God (see 1 Pet 4:19, "entrust their souls to a faithful Creator"). The other reading, followed by many modern translations (such as the ESV) and commentators, takes it as what God has entrusted to Paul, the gospel, since verse 14 and 1 Timothy 6:20 use the same word for "the good deposit entrusted to you." Both readings are possible from the Greek. Either way, Paul's confidence rests on God: it is God who "is able to guard" it until that Day.
- **"That Day":** the day of the Lord's return and judgment (see v. 18; 4:8). Paul's hope is not in today's verdict but in that Day.

> The point: We are not ashamed of the gospel, not because we are braver than others, but because the gospel is the good news that God saves us by his grace, and the One we have believed is able to keep us until that Day.

## Going Deeper: 1:13–14 Guard the Good Deposit

**1. "The pattern of the sound words."** "Follow the pattern of the sound words that you have heard from me, in the faith and love that are in Christ Jesus" (1:13).
- **"Pattern":** the word means a model or outline, like an artist's first sketch or a builder's plan. Paul taught Timothy the outline of the gospel, and Timothy is to teach others according to it.
- **"Sound":** literally "healthy" (see 1 Tim 1:10; 6:3). Sound teaching makes spiritual life healthy.
- **"In the faith and love":** holding to sound teaching does not mean coldly keeping a set of rules, or using the truth as a club. Paul says to hold it in the faith and love that are in Christ Jesus.

**2. "By the Holy Spirit who dwells within us."** "By the Holy Spirit who dwells within us, guard the good deposit entrusted to you" (1:14).
- **"The good deposit":** the same word as in 1 Timothy 6:20, "guard the deposit entrusted to you," used for valuables left in someone's safekeeping. The gospel is not Timothy's private property; God entrusted it to him.
- **By the Spirit:** Timothy must guard this deposit, but not by his own cleverness or willpower. He guards it by the Holy Spirit who dwells in believers (see v. 7).

> The point: The gospel is a treasure God has entrusted to us. We guard it by the Holy Spirit who dwells within us, in faith and love.

## Going Deeper: 1:15–18 Those Who Turned Away, and One Who Was Not Ashamed

**1. "All who are in Asia turned away from me."** "You are aware that all who are in Asia turned away from me, among whom are Phygelus and Hermogenes" (1:15).
- **"All who are in Asia":** this is unlikely to mean every believer in Asia, since Timothy and the household of Onesiphorus were probably there. Many think it refers to coworkers or friends from Asia on whom Paul could have relied, who did not stand with him when he was arrested and tried (see 4:16). Others think some churches in Asia had turned away from Paul's teaching.
- **Phygelus and Hermogenes:** Scripture mentions them nowhere else. Their names are remembered only because they left.
- **Paul's pain:** Paul had spent nearly three years in Asia (Acts 19:10; 20:31), "so that all the residents of Asia heard the word of the Lord" (Acts 19:10). Now, when he most needed help, people from there turned away from him. To be abandoned by those you have served is a deep wound.

**2. "He often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chains."** "May the Lord grant mercy to the household of Onesiphorus, for he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chains, but when he arrived in Rome he searched for me earnestly and found me" (1:16–17).
- **"Refreshed me":** the word suggests giving fresh strength, letting someone breathe again. We do not know exactly what Onesiphorus did. Perhaps he brought food or clothing; perhaps it was simply his company and comfort.
- **"Searched for me earnestly and found me":** Rome was a great city. Finding one prisoner in custody was not easy, and associating with such a prisoner was dangerous. Onesiphorus did not mind the trouble or the risk. He kept searching until he found him.
- **Jesus' words:** "I was in prison and you came to me... as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me" (Matt 25:36, 40).

**3. "May the Lord grant him to find mercy from the Lord on that Day."** "May the Lord grant him to find mercy from the Lord on that Day!—and you well know all the service he rendered at Ephesus" (1:18).
- **Was Onesiphorus still alive?** Paul asks mercy for his "household" (v. 16) and at the end greets only "the household of Onesiphorus" (4:19), so many infer that Onesiphorus had died. But the text does not say so; he may simply not have returned to Ephesus yet.
- **Two readings:** Catholic teaching affirms prayer for the dead (*Catechism of the Catholic Church* 958, 1032, citing 2 Maccabees 12:46), and some Catholic interpreters read 1:18 as Paul praying for the departed Onesiphorus. Protestant tradition, as in the Westminster Confession 21.4, holds that prayer is not to be made for the dead; Protestant commentators reply that the text does not say Onesiphorus had died, and that even if he had, Paul's words simply commit him to the Lord's mercy, something like a blessing; no doctrine of purgatory or intercession for the dead can be built on them.
- **"That Day":** in verse 12 Paul said the Lord is able to guard what is entrusted "until that Day"; here he prays that Onesiphorus will find mercy "on that Day." For Paul and for Onesiphorus alike, what they finally need is the Lord's mercy.

> The point: When many had left, Onesiphorus searched earnestly for Paul in his chains. He learned that kind of love from the Lord, who first came searching for us; and what we need on that Day is his mercy.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chains, but when he arrived in Rome he searched for me earnestly and found me" (1:16–17). Is there someone near me bound by "chains": someone in hospital for months, someone who has been in prison, someone with depression who no longer comes to church, someone whose business failed and who is buried in debt, whom no one quite likes to mention? At first people visited. Then fewer and fewer went. I have thought of going too, but I am afraid it will be awkward and I won't know what to say; sometimes I am also afraid of what others will think if they know I keep company with him. And so the months go by. I feel a little guilty, and I find plenty of reasons. In that great city of Rome, Onesiphorus searched street by street; "he searched for me earnestly and found me." When I read this, the first thing I think of is not what I ought to do, but that I too was once the one who was found. I was bound by sin, and ashamed to be seen; but the Lord Jesus was not ashamed of me. He said, "For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost" (Luke 19:10). He is like the shepherd who goes after the lost sheep "until he finds it," and "when he has found it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing" (Luke 15:4–5). To find me, he himself was bound, treated as a criminal and crucified among criminals; he "endured the cross, despising the shame" (Heb 12:2). The debt of my sin is what he bore there in my place. If a name comes to mind, I can first confess before the Lord: I stayed away because I was afraid of losing face. The Lord does not only forgive me; he is already seeking that person, earlier and more earnestly than I. Whether I go or not does not decide that person's worth before the Lord; but I can ask the Lord to let me follow him there, even if it is only a message. If that person once harmed me or my family, this passage is not calling me back to him; the Lord will send others to seek him. And visiting someone jailed for harming children never means giving him access to children again. And if I draw back again, the Lord who found me will not be ashamed of me.
