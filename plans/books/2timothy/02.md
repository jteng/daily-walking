## 本章脉络

第二章分成四段。**在恩典上刚强**（1-7节）：提摩太要在基督耶稣的恩典上刚强起来，把所听见的交托那忠心能教导别人的人；要和保罗同受苦难，好像精兵、比武的人和劳力的农夫。**记念从死里复活的耶稣基督**（8-13节）：保罗为福音被捆绑，像犯人一样，神的道却不被捆绑；他为选民凡事忍耐；「可信的话」说：我们若与基督同死，也必与他同活。**作无愧的工人**（14-19节）：不可为言语争辩，要按着正意分解真理的道，远避世俗的虚谈；许米乃和腓理徒说复活的事已过；然而神坚固的根基立住了。**贵重的器皿，温和的仆人**（20-26节）：人若自洁，就必作贵重的器皿；要逃避少年的私欲，追求公义、信德、仁爱、和平；主的仆人不可争竞，要用温柔劝戒那抵挡的人，或者神给他们悔改的心。

这一章的中心是第8节：「你要记念耶稣基督乃是大卫的后裔，他从死里复活。」保罗要提摩太受苦、忍耐、忠心、温柔，但在这一切嘱咐中间，他先叫提摩太记念一个人。忍耐的力量不是从提摩太自己来的，而是从那位已经复活的主来的。

## 深读 2:1-7 在恩典上刚强

**1. 「你要在基督耶稣的恩典上刚强起来」。** 「我儿啊，你要在基督耶稣的恩典上刚强起来。」（2:1）
- **被动的刚强：** 原文是被动语态，许多人理解为要被加给力量。提摩太刚强起来，不是靠自己鼓劲，而是被基督耶稣的恩典加给力量。这正接着第一章说的：神赐给我们的，不是胆怯的心（1:7）。
- **旧约的回声：** 摩西临终前把百姓交给约书亚，对他说：「你当刚强壮胆！」（申31:7；参31:23）摩西死后，神又对约书亚说：「你当刚强壮胆！不要惧怕，也不要惊惶；因为你无论往哪里去，耶和华－你的神必与你同在。」（书1:9）大卫临终时也嘱咐所罗门：「你当刚强，作大丈夫」（王上2:2）。保罗临终时对提摩太说的话，也站在这交接的传统里；不同的是，提摩太的力量是在「基督耶稣的恩典」里。

**2. 「也要交托那忠心能教导别人的人」。** 「你在许多见证人面前听见我所教训的，也要交托那忠心能教导别人的人。」（2:2）
- **四代人：** 保罗、提摩太、「忠心的人」、「别人」。保罗知道自己快要离世，就关心福音怎样传到下一代、再下一代。
- **「交托」：** 这和1:12、14所说的「交托」是同一个词根。神把福音交托给保罗，保罗交托给提摩太，提摩太要再交托给别人。
- **两个条件：** 他们要「忠心」，也要「能教导别人」。忠心是品格，能教导是恩赐。教会选立带领和教导的人，两样都要看。
- **今天的应用：** 一个教会如果只有一位能干的牧者，却没有培养下一代，牧者一离开，教会就容易散。主日学老师、团契小组长、带一位初信者读经的弟兄姊妹，都在做2:2所说的事。

**3. 精兵、比武的人、农夫。** 「你要和我同受苦难，好像基督耶稣的精兵。凡在军中当兵的，不将世务缠身，好叫那招他当兵的人喜悦。人若在场上比武，非按规矩，就不能得冠冕。劳力的农夫理当先得粮食。」（2:3-6）
- **精兵：** 当兵的人不被日常的事务缠住，因为他要讨招他当兵的人喜悦。这不是说信徒不可以工作、成家；保罗自己也织帐棚。意思是：不要让别的事缠住我们，使我们忘了是谁召了我们。
- **比武的人：** 运动员要「按规矩」比赛才能得冠冕。据古代的记载（保撒尼亚斯《希腊志》5.24.9），参加奥林匹克运动会的人还要起誓，说自己已经按规定训练了十个月。服事主也有规矩：要忠于主的道，也要用主的方法，不可为了成果走捷径。
- **农夫：** 劳力的农夫理当「先」得粮食。农夫要辛苦耕种，常常要等很久才看见收成；但他必会得着。保罗在别处也用这个比喻（林前9:7）。
- **三个比喻的共同点：** 都要付出辛苦，都要专心，都有将来的奖赏：讨主喜悦，得冠冕，得粮食。

**4. 「凡事主必给你聪明」。** 「我所说的话，你要思想，因为凡事主必给你聪明。」（2:7）
- **思想和光照：** 提摩太要自己思想，用心去想这些比喻；但真正的明白是主赐的。读经也是这样：我们要用心，却也要求主光照。

> 要点：提摩太要像精兵、运动员、农夫一样辛苦专心，但他的力量来自基督耶稣的恩典。在恩典上刚强的人，才能把福音忠心地交托给下一代。

## 深读 2:8-13 记念从死里复活的耶稣基督

**1. 「你要记念耶稣基督」。** 「你要记念耶稣基督乃是大卫的后裔，他从死里复活，正合乎我所传的福音。」（2:8）
- **记念一个人：** 保罗在受苦的嘱咐中间，叫提摩太记念的不是一条原则，而是一位主。
- **「从死里复活」：** 原文的语气是一个持续的状态：他是那位已经从死里复活、现在仍然活着的主。提摩太所跟随的，不是一位死了的老师。
- **「大卫的后裔」：** 这说明耶稣是真正的人，也是神应许给大卫的那位王（撒下7:12-13）。保罗在罗马书开头也把这两件事连在一起：耶稣「按肉体说，是从大卫后裔生的；按圣善的灵说，因从死里复活，以大能显明是神的儿子」（罗1:3-4）。
- **为什么在这里提？** 受苦的提摩太要记得：耶稣也走过受苦的路，然后从死里复活。受苦不是故事的结局。

**2. 「然而神的道却不被捆绑」。** 「我为这福音受苦难，甚至被捆绑，像犯人一样。然而神的道却不被捆绑。」（2:9）
- **「像犯人一样」：** 「犯人」这个词，路加福音用来指与耶稣同钉的「两个犯人」（路23:32）。保罗被当作重犯看待，正像他的主一样。
- **「神的道却不被捆绑」：** 保罗被锁链捆着，福音却没有。他从前在罗马被软禁时就说过：「我所遭遇的事更是叫福音兴旺」（腓1:12），连御营全军都知道他是为基督受捆锁的（腓1:13）。使徒行传原文的最后一个词，就是和合本译作「并没有人禁止」的那个词（徒28:31）。
- **神的话必然成就：** 「我口所出的话也必如此，决不徒然返回，却要成就我所喜悦的。」（赛55:11）传道的人会被关、会死，神的道却继续前行。

**3. 「我为选民凡事忍耐」。** 「所以，我为选民凡事忍耐，叫他们也可以得着那在基督耶稣里的救恩和永远的荣耀。」（2:10）
- **为了别人：** 保罗忍受锁链，不是为了自己的名声，而是为了「选民」能得着救恩。
- **拣选和传福音：** 「选民」指神所拣选的人。保罗相信神有他所拣选的人，这不叫他懒惰，反而叫他更甘心忍耐，因为神要藉着福音的传扬把他们召来（参徒18:9-10「在这城里我有许多的百姓」）。改革宗传统认为，选民是神照他的旨意所拣选、必然会信的人，传福音正是神叫他们得救的方法；阿米念传统则多认为，选民是在基督里、凭信心归于他的人，人可以接受或拒绝福音的呼召。两种看法都同意：神要用传福音的人和他们的忍耐来成就救恩。
- **「选民」在这里指谁？** 有人认为是还没有信主、将要藉福音被召的人（参徒18:10）；也有人认为也包括已经信主的人，保罗忍耐，是为了他们能坚持到底，得着「永远的荣耀」。
- **保罗的忍耐不是赎价：** 保罗的受苦不能为人赎罪，只有基督的死才能（提前2:6）。保罗忍耐，是为了把这福音传给人，好叫人得着「那在基督耶稣里的救恩」。

**4. 「我们纵然失信，他仍是可信的」。** 「有可信的话说：我们若与基督同死，也必与他同活；我们若能忍耐，也必和他一同作王。我们若不认他，他也必不认我们；我们纵然失信，他仍是可信的，因为他不能背乎自己。」（2:11-13）
- **一首诗：** 这是教牧书信中的第四句「可信的话」。许多学者认为这是早期教会的一首诗或信条，有四行，每行都以「我们若……」开始。
- **「与基督同死」：** 很可能指信徒在受洗时与基督联合，归入他的死（罗6:4、8）；在这里的上下文中，也包括为基督受苦，甚至殉道。
- **「一同作王」：** 忍耐到底的人，将来要与基督一同掌权（参启3:21）。
- **「我们若不认他，他也必不认我们」：** 这是耶稣自己的话：「凡在人面前不认我的，我在我天上的父面前也必不认他。」（太10:33）这是很严肃的警告。
- **「我们纵然失信，他仍是可信的」：** 这一行打破了前面的对称。按前三行的样式，我们以为会读到他也必失信，却读到「他仍是可信的」。解经家对这一行有两种理解。一种认为这是安慰：软弱、跌倒的信徒，主仍然信实地保守他们，因为主不会背弃他自己的应许（参约10:28；罗3:3-4）。另一种认为这也包含警告：主忠于他自己，所以他对不认他的人所说的话，也必定成就。许多解经家认为，这一行至少包括安慰的意思。
- **「不认」和「失信」不同：** 许多人认为，第12节的「不认」指的是最终、彻底地离弃基督；「失信」指的是信心的软弱和失败。彼得也「不认」主，原文用的是同一个动词，但他痛哭悔改，主恢复了他（路22:61-62）；可见第12节所警告的，是持续、不悔改的不认主。这个警告和第13节的安慰怎样相合，历代有不同的理解：改革宗传统（如《威斯敏斯德信条》第十七章）认为，真正属基督的人必蒙保守到底，不会最终离弃他（圣徒恒忍）；阿米念传统（如卫斯理）认为，真信徒有可能最终离弃信仰。两种看法都认真对待第12节的警告，也都在第13节看见神的信实给软弱信徒的安慰。

> 要点：受苦的时候，我们要记念从死里复活的耶稣基督。传道的人会被捆绑，神的道却不被捆绑；我们会失信，他却仍是可信的，因为他不能背乎自己。

## 深读 2:14-19 作无愧的工人

**1. 「不可为言语争辩」。** 「你要使众人回想这些事，在主面前嘱咐他们：不可为言语争辩；这是没有益处的，只能败坏听见的人。」（2:14）
- **「败坏」：** 原文的意思是推翻、毁坏。无谓的争论不能建立人，只会拆毁人的信心。
- **今天的「言语争辩」：** 网上为了一个次要的教义问题彼此攻击，在小组里为了一个字争得面红耳赤，常常没有人因此更爱主。这不是说真理不重要（下一节就讲要按正意分解真理），而是要分辨什么是值得争的。

**2. 「按着正意分解真理的道」。** 「你当竭力在神面前得蒙喜悦，作无愧的工人，按着正意分解真理的道。」（2:15）
- **「在神面前」：** 传道和教导的人最终是向神交账，不是向听众交账。
- **「无愧的工人」：** 工人的手艺要经得起检验。和合本译作「无愧」，原文是不必羞愧。这又回到第一章的主题：不以为耻。
- **「按着正意分解」：** 这个词字面的意思是笔直地切；许多人理解为像农夫犁出直的沟，或工人铺一条直路。解释圣经要照着它本来的意思，不可歪曲，也不可为了迎合人而绕弯。

**3. 「说复活的事已过」。** 「但要远避世俗的虚谈，因为这等人必进到更不敬虔的地步。他们的话如同毒疮，越烂越大；其中有许米乃和腓理徒，他们偏离了真道，说复活的事已过，就败坏好些人的信心。」（2:16-18）
- **「毒疮」：** 原文是坏疽，就是肉体腐烂、不断扩散的病。错误的教训也会这样在教会里蔓延。
- **许米乃：** 他在提摩太前书已经出现过：「其中有许米乃和亚历山大；我已经把他们交给撒但」（提前1:20）。看来他没有悔改，而且继续影响人。
- **「复活的事已过」：** 他们可能把复活完全属灵化了，说信徒在受洗或得知识时已经「复活」了，将来不会有身体的复活。保罗确实说信徒已经与基督「一同复活」（弗2:6），但他同时坚持将来身体的复活（林前15:20-23、42-44）。哥林多教会也有人说没有死人复活的事（林前15:12）。二世纪的一些诺斯底文献（如《论复活》）也有类似的说法。否认将来身体的复活，就是否认神对受造世界和人身体的救赎，也会叫受苦的信徒失去盼望。

**4. 「神坚固的根基立住了」。** 「然而，神坚固的根基立住了；上面有这印记说：『主认识谁是他的人』；又说：『凡称呼主名的人总要离开不义。』」（2:19）
- **「然而」：** 有人偏离了，有人的信心被败坏了，「然而」神的根基没有动摇。教会的根基不是哪一位领袖，而是神自己的工作。
- **两个印记：** 古时的建筑物，有时在奠基石上刻字，表明属谁、为何而建。保罗说，神的根基上刻着两句话。
- **「主认识谁是他的人」：** 这句话出自民数记可拉党叛乱的故事。摩西说：「到了早晨，耶和华必指示谁是属他的」（民16:5）；保罗的话和这节的希腊文旧约译本几乎一字不差。在混乱中，人分辨不清谁是真信徒，主却认识。这是安慰。
- **「凡称呼主名的人总要离开不义」：** 这句话可能综合了几处旧约经文（参民16:26「你们离开这恶人的帐棚吧」；赛26:13；52:11）。属主的人，生活要有分别。这是呼召。
- **两句话要一起读：** 第一句讲神的拣选和保守，第二句讲人的责任和圣洁。只讲第一句，人会放纵；只讲第二句，人会焦虑。保罗把两句话刻在同一个根基上。

> 要点：在错误教训蔓延的时候，神坚固的根基仍然立住。主认识谁是他的人，这叫我们安心；称呼主名的人要离开不义，这叫我们警醒。

## 深读 2:20-26 贵重的器皿，温和的仆人

**1. 「就必作贵重的器皿」。** 「在大户人家，不但有金器银器，也有木器瓦器；有作为贵重的，有作为卑贱的。人若自洁，脱离卑贱的事，就必作贵重的器皿，成为圣洁，合乎主用，预备行各样的善事。」（2:20-21）
- **大户人家：** 教会像一个大户人家，里面有各样的器皿。这个比喻和罗马书9:21不同，那里讲的是神的主权；这里讲的是人的回应：人若自洁，就必作贵重的器皿。
- **「脱离卑贱的事」：** 原文直译是脱离这些。有人认为「这些」指卑贱的器皿，就是许米乃那样的假教师，意思是不要与他们同流；也有人认为指卑贱的事，就是错误的教训和不敬虔的行为。两种理解都要求信徒有分别。
- **「合乎主用」：** 这里的关键不在材料，而在洁净：人若自洁，脱离卑贱的事，就必作贵重的器皿，合乎主用。

**2. 「你要逃避少年的私欲」。** 「你要逃避少年的私欲，同那清心祷告主的人追求公义、信德、仁爱、和平。」（2:22）
- **「少年的私欲」：** 这可能包括性方面的试探。但在这段讲争论的上下文中，许多解经家认为主要包括年轻人容易有的其他私欲：急躁、好胜、爱争论、喜欢新奇的东西。
- **「同那清心祷告主的人」：** 追求圣洁不是一个人的事。提摩太要找那些清心祷告主的人一同追求。

**3. 「主的仆人不可争竞」。** 「惟有那愚拙无学问的辩论，总要弃绝，因为知道这等事是起争竞的。然而主的仆人不可争竞，只要温温和和地待众人，善于教导，存心忍耐，用温柔劝戒那抵挡的人。」（2:23-25上）
- **「主的仆人」：** 旧约称摩西、约书亚为「耶和华的仆人」（申34:5；书24:29）；也有人在这里听见以赛亚书那位仆人的回声：「压伤的芦苇，他不折断」（赛42:3）。主的仆人在争论中的样子，也要像主自己。
- **温和不是软弱：** 提摩太仍要「劝戒那抵挡的人」，他不是不讲是非。但他的态度要温和、忍耐，因为他的目的不是赢一场辩论，而是挽回一个人。

**4. 「或者神给他们悔改的心」。** 「或者神给他们悔改的心，可以明白真道，叫他们这已经被魔鬼任意掳去的，可以醒悟，脱离他的网罗。」（2:25下-26）
- **悔改是神赐的：** 提摩太不能使人悔改，他只能温柔地教导；给人悔改的心的，是神（参徒11:18「神也赐恩给外邦人，叫他们悔改得生命了」）。这叫传道的人谦卑，也叫他有盼望：再顽固的人，神也能改变。
- **魔鬼的网罗：** 抵挡真道的人不只是有错误的观点，他们是被魔鬼掳去的人。所以我们要恨恶错误，却要怜悯被掳的人。
- **一个难解的地方：** 第26节最后一句，原文的代名词可以有不同的理解。和合本和许多现代译本都理解为：他们被魔鬼掳去，随从魔鬼的意思。也有人理解为：他们脱离魔鬼的网罗以后，被神（或神的仆人）得着，去遵行神的旨意。无论哪一种，神都是能救人脱离网罗的那一位。

> 要点：主的仆人在真理上不可妥协，在态度上却要温和。我们劝戒抵挡的人，不是为了赢，而是盼望神给他们悔改的心，使他们脱离魔鬼的网罗。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我为这福音受苦难，甚至被捆绑，像犯人一样。然而神的道却不被捆绑。」（2:9）有时候我为福音的前途担心。我看到教会的人越来越少，主日学空了一半；看到年轻人一个个离开，我的孩子长大了也不再去聚会；看到有的地方教会被关闭，传道人被抓。我心里慢慢生出一种绝望：福音是不是在退后？教会是不是快要撑不住了？有时我把这份担心变成焦虑，好像一切都要靠我们这些人拼命撑住，撑不住就完了。保罗写这句话的时候，处境比我坏得多：他被锁在牢里，同工离散，亚细亚的人都离弃了他，他自己也快要被处死。从人看，这正是福音最失败的时候。可是他说：「然而神的道却不被捆绑。」他怎么能这样说？前一节告诉我：「你要记念耶稣基督……他从死里复活。」坟墓都没有捆住他，锁链也捆不住他的道。那位主为我的罪死了，也为我复活了；他今天仍然活着，仍然在召他的百姓。我可以为教会的衰微难过，也可以为离开的孩子流泪，这不是不信。但我可以把绝望交给他，不再背着一切都靠我的重担。今天，我可以把那个让我最担心的人或教会说出来，求主照他的方法和时间，让他的道在那里运行。然后我可以记念他：耶稣基督，从死里复活的主。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 falls into four parts. **Be strong in grace** (vv. 1–7): Timothy is to be strengthened by the grace that is in Christ Jesus and to entrust what he has heard to faithful men who will be able to teach others also; he is to share in suffering like a soldier, an athlete and a hard-working farmer. **Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead** (vv. 8–13): Paul is bound with chains as a criminal for the gospel, but the word of God is not bound; he endures everything for the sake of the elect; a trustworthy saying: if we have died with him, we will also live with him. **A worker with no need to be ashamed** (vv. 14–19): do not quarrel about words; rightly handle the word of truth; avoid irreverent babble; Hymenaeus and Philetus say the resurrection has already happened; but God's firm foundation stands. **Honorable vessels and a gentle servant** (vv. 20–26): whoever cleanses himself will be a vessel for honorable use; flee youthful passions and pursue righteousness, faith, love and peace; the Lord's servant must not be quarrelsome but must correct his opponents with gentleness, in the hope that God may grant them repentance.

The center of this chapter is verse 8: "Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, the offspring of David." Paul asks Timothy to suffer, to endure, to be faithful and gentle. But in the middle of all these charges, he first tells Timothy to remember a person. The strength to endure does not come from Timothy himself; it comes from the Lord who has risen.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–7 Strengthened by Grace

**1. "Be strengthened by the grace that is in Christ Jesus."** "You then, my child, be strengthened by the grace that is in Christ Jesus" (2:1).
- **A passive strength:** the verb is passive (many take it as "be strengthened"). Timothy's strength does not come from pumping himself up; he is to be strengthened by the grace of Christ Jesus. This follows from chapter 1: God gave us a spirit not of fear (1:7).
- **An Old Testament echo:** before his death Moses handed the people over to Joshua, saying, "Be strong and courageous" (Deut 31:7; see 31:23); after Moses died, God said it again: "Be strong and courageous. Do not be frightened, and do not be dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go" (Josh 1:9). David on his deathbed charged Solomon, "Be strong, and show yourself a man" (1 Kgs 2:2). Paul's last words to Timothy stand in this tradition of handing over; the difference is that Timothy's strength is in "the grace that is in Christ Jesus."

**2. "Entrust to faithful men who will be able to teach others also."** "And what you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses entrust to faithful men who will be able to teach others also" (2:2).
- **Four generations:** Paul, Timothy, "faithful men," and "others." Knowing that he will soon die, Paul is concerned with how the gospel will pass to the next generation and the one after.
- **"Entrust":** the same root as "entrusted" in 1:12, 14. God entrusted the gospel to Paul; Paul entrusted it to Timothy; Timothy must entrust it to others.
- **Two qualifications:** they must be "faithful" and "able to teach others." Faithfulness is character; the ability to teach is a gift. When a church chooses leaders and teachers, it must look for both.
- **Today:** if a church has one capable pastor but trains no one after him, it easily scatters when he leaves. Sunday school teachers, small group leaders, a brother or sister reading the Bible with a new believer, are all doing what 2:2 describes.

**3. Soldier, athlete, farmer.** "Share in suffering as a good soldier of Christ Jesus. No soldier gets entangled in civilian pursuits, since his aim is to please the one who enlisted him. An athlete is not crowned unless he competes according to the rules. It is the hard-working farmer who ought to have the first share of the crops" (2:3–6).
- **The soldier:** a soldier does not get entangled in civilian affairs, because he wants to please the one who enlisted him. This does not mean believers may not work or marry; Paul himself made tents. It means: do not let other things so entangle us that we forget who called us.
- **The athlete:** an athlete is crowned only if he competes "according to the rules." Ancient sources (Pausanias, *Description of Greece* 5.24.9) record that competitors at the Olympic Games even swore an oath that they had trained as required for ten months. Serving the Lord has its rules too: faithfulness to his word, and his ways of working, with no shortcuts for the sake of results.
- **The farmer:** the hard-working farmer ought to have the "first" share of the crops. A farmer works hard and often waits a long time for the harvest, but he will receive it. Paul uses the same picture elsewhere (1 Cor 9:7).
- **What the three share:** each takes hard work, each takes single-mindedness, and each has a future reward: pleasing the Lord, a crown, a harvest.

**4. "The Lord will give you understanding in everything."** "Think over what I say, for the Lord will give you understanding in everything" (2:7).
- **Thinking and illumination:** Timothy must think these pictures through for himself; but true understanding is the Lord's gift. Bible reading is the same: we must give it our minds, and we must also ask the Lord to give light.

> The point: Timothy must be as hard-working and single-minded as a soldier, an athlete and a farmer, but his strength comes from the grace that is in Christ Jesus. Only those strengthened by grace can faithfully entrust the gospel to the next generation.

## Going Deeper: 2:8–13 Remember Jesus Christ, Risen from the Dead

**1. "Remember Jesus Christ."** "Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead, the offspring of David, as preached in my gospel" (2:8).
- **Remember a person:** in the middle of his charge to suffer, Paul tells Timothy to remember not a principle but a Lord.
- **"Risen from the dead":** the Greek form describes a lasting state: he is the one who has been raised and is alive now. Timothy is not following a dead teacher.
- **"The offspring of David":** this shows that Jesus is truly human, and that he is the king God promised to David (2 Sam 7:12–13). Paul joins the same two things at the start of Romans: Jesus "was descended from David according to the flesh and was declared to be the Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by his resurrection from the dead" (Rom 1:3–4).
- **Why mention it here?** Suffering Timothy needs to remember that Jesus also walked the road of suffering, and then rose from the dead. Suffering is not the end of the story.

**2. "But the word of God is not bound!"** "For which I am suffering, bound with chains as a criminal. But the word of God is not bound!" (2:9).
- **"As a criminal":** Luke uses this word for the two "criminals" crucified with Jesus (Luke 23:32). Paul is treated as a serious offender, just like his Lord.
- **"The word of God is not bound":** Paul is in chains, but the gospel is not. During his earlier house arrest in Rome he had written, "what has happened to me has really served to advance the gospel" (Phil 1:12), so that the whole imperial guard knew his imprisonment was for Christ (Phil 1:13). The last word of Acts in Greek is "without hindrance" (Acts 28:31).
- **God's word will accomplish its purpose:** "so shall my word be that goes out from my mouth; it shall not return to me empty, but it shall accomplish that which I purpose" (Isa 55:11). Preachers can be imprisoned and can die, but the word of God keeps going.

**3. "I endure everything for the sake of the elect."** "Therefore I endure everything for the sake of the elect, that they also may obtain the salvation that is in Christ Jesus with eternal glory" (2:10).
- **For others:** Paul endures the chains not for his own reputation but so that "the elect" may obtain salvation.
- **Election and evangelism:** "the elect" are those God has chosen. Paul's belief that God has a chosen people does not make him lazy; it makes him more willing to endure, because God will call them through the preaching of the gospel (see Acts 18:9–10, "I have many in this city who are my people"). The Reformed tradition holds that the elect are those God has chosen according to his purpose, who will surely believe, and that preaching the gospel is the means by which God saves them. The Arminian tradition generally holds that the elect are those who are in Christ and belong to him by faith, and that people may accept or reject the gospel call. Both agree that God accomplishes salvation through those who preach the gospel and through their endurance.
- **Who are "the elect" here?** Some take them as people not yet converted, who will be called through the gospel (see Acts 18:10); others include believers as well, for whom Paul endures so that they persevere to final salvation "with eternal glory."
- **Paul's endurance is not a ransom:** Paul's suffering cannot atone for anyone's sin; only Christ's death can (1 Tim 2:6). Paul endures in order to bring this gospel to people, so that they may obtain "the salvation that is in Christ Jesus."

**4. "If we are faithless, he remains faithful."** "The saying is trustworthy, for: If we have died with him, we will also live with him; if we endure, we will also reign with him; if we deny him, he also will deny us; if we are faithless, he remains faithful—for he cannot deny himself" (2:11–13).
- **A hymn:** this is the fourth "trustworthy saying" in the Pastoral Letters. Many scholars think it was an early Christian hymn or confession, with four lines, each beginning "if we..."
- **"Died with him":** most likely the believer's union with Christ in baptism, being baptized into his death (Rom 6:4, 8). In this context it also includes suffering for Christ, even martyrdom.
- **"Reign with him":** those who endure will share in Christ's rule (see Rev 3:21).
- **"If we deny him, he also will deny us":** this is Jesus' own warning: "but whoever denies me before men, I also will deny before my Father who is in heaven" (Matt 10:33). It is a solemn warning.
- **"If we are faithless, he remains faithful":** this line breaks the pattern. Following the first three lines, we expect "he also will be faithless," but we read "he remains faithful." Interpreters read this line in two ways. Some take it as comfort: even when believers are weak and fall, the Lord faithfully keeps them, because he will not go back on his own promise (see John 10:28; Rom 3:3–4). Others think it also includes a warning: because the Lord is faithful to himself, what he has said about those who deny him will also come true. Many interpreters hold that the line includes at least the note of comfort.
- **"Deny" and "faithless" are different:** many take "deny" in verse 12 to mean finally and completely renouncing Christ, and "faithless" to mean the weakness and failure of faith. Peter too "denied" the Lord, with the same Greek verb, but he wept and repented, and the Lord restored him (Luke 22:61–62); so verse 12 warns of persistent, unrepentant denial. Christians have understood how this warning fits with the comfort of verse 13 in different ways. The Reformed tradition (for example, the Westminster Confession, chapter 17) holds that those who truly belong to Christ will be kept to the end and will not finally renounce him (the perseverance of the saints). The Arminian tradition (for example, Wesley) holds that true believers can finally fall away from faith. Both take the warning of verse 12 seriously, and both find in verse 13 God's faithfulness as comfort for weak believers.

> The point: In suffering, we are to remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead. Preachers can be bound, but the word of God is not bound; we may be faithless, but he remains faithful, for he cannot deny himself.

## Going Deeper: 2:14–19 A Worker with No Need to Be Ashamed

**1. "Not to quarrel about words."** "Remind them of these things, and charge them before God not to quarrel about words, which does no good, but only ruins the hearers" (2:14).
- **"Ruins":** the Greek word is the source of the English "catastrophe," meaning to overturn or destroy. Pointless quarrels build no one up; they only tear down people's faith.
- **Today's "quarrels about words":** believers attack one another online over a secondary point of doctrine, or a small group argues heatedly over a single word, and usually no one comes away loving the Lord more. This does not mean truth is unimportant (the next verse says to handle the word of truth rightly); it means we must discern what is worth fighting over.

**2. "Rightly handling the word of truth."** "Do your best to present yourself to God as one approved, a worker who has no need to be ashamed, rightly handling the word of truth" (2:15).
- **"To God":** those who preach and teach finally give account to God, not to their hearers.
- **"A worker who has no need to be ashamed":** a workman's craft must stand inspection. This returns to the theme of chapter 1: not being ashamed.
- **"Rightly handling":** the word literally means "cut straight"; many take it as a farmer plowing a straight furrow, or a worker laying a straight road. Scripture is to be explained according to its own meaning, not twisted, and not bent to please people.

**3. "Saying that the resurrection has already happened."** "But avoid irreverent babble, for it will lead people into more and more ungodliness, and their talk will spread like gangrene. Among them are Hymenaeus and Philetus, who have swerved from the truth, saying that the resurrection has already happened. They are upsetting the faith of some" (2:16–18).
- **"Gangrene":** the decay of flesh that keeps spreading. False teaching spreads through a church the same way.
- **Hymenaeus:** he already appears in 1 Timothy: "among whom are Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom I have handed over to Satan" (1 Tim 1:20). He seems not to have repented and was still influencing people.
- **"The resurrection has already happened":** they may have spiritualized the resurrection entirely, saying believers had already been "raised" at baptism or through knowledge, and that there would be no future bodily resurrection. Paul does say that believers have already been "raised up with him" (Eph 2:6), but he also insists on the future resurrection of the body (1 Cor 15:20–23, 42–44); some in Corinth had also said there is no resurrection of the dead (1 Cor 15:12). Some second-century Gnostic writings (such as the *Treatise on the Resurrection*) say something similar. To deny the future bodily resurrection is to deny God's redemption of the created world and of our bodies, and it takes hope away from suffering believers.

**4. "God's firm foundation stands."** "But God's firm foundation stands, bearing this seal: 'The Lord knows those who are his,' and, 'Let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart from iniquity'" (2:19).
- **"But":** some have swerved, and the faith of some has been upset, "but" God's foundation has not been shaken. The church's foundation is not any leader but God's own work.
- **Two inscriptions:** ancient buildings sometimes had words carved on the foundation stone, showing whose the building was and what it was for. Paul says God's foundation bears two sayings.
- **"The Lord knows those who are his":** this comes from the story of Korah's rebellion in Numbers. Moses said, "In the morning the LORD will show who is his" (Num 16:5); Paul's words follow the Greek Old Testament of this verse almost word for word. In the confusion, people could not tell who truly belonged to God, but the Lord knew. This is comfort.
- **"Let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart from iniquity":** this saying may draw together several Old Testament texts (see Num 16:26, "Depart, please, from the tents of these wicked men"; Isa 26:13; 52:11). Those who belong to the Lord must live differently. This is a call.
- **Read the two together:** the first speaks of God's choosing and keeping; the second of our responsibility and holiness. Hear only the first, and people grow careless; hear only the second, and they grow anxious. Paul carves both on the same foundation.

> The point: When false teaching spreads, God's firm foundation still stands. The Lord knows those who are his, and that gives us rest; everyone who names his name must depart from iniquity, and that keeps us watchful.

## Going Deeper: 2:20–26 Honorable Vessels and a Gentle Servant

**1. "A vessel for honorable use."** "Now in a great house there are not only vessels of gold and silver but also of wood and clay, some for honorable use, some for dishonorable. Therefore, if anyone cleanses himself from what is dishonorable, he will be a vessel for honorable use, set apart as holy, useful to the master of the house, ready for every good work" (2:20–21).
- **A great house:** the church is like a great household with many kinds of vessels. This picture differs from Romans 9:21, which speaks of God's sovereignty; here the emphasis is on our response: if anyone cleanses himself, he will be a vessel for honorable use.
- **"From what is dishonorable":** the Greek reads literally "from these." Some think "these" are the dishonorable vessels, false teachers like Hymenaeus, so that the meaning is: do not go along with them. Others think it means dishonorable things, that is, false teaching and ungodly living. Either way, believers are called to be distinct.
- **"Useful to the master":** the point here is not the material but cleansing: whoever cleanses himself from what is dishonorable will be a vessel for honorable use, useful to the master.

**2. "Flee youthful passions."** "So flee youthful passions and pursue righteousness, faith, love, and peace, along with those who call on the Lord from a pure heart" (2:22).
- **"Youthful passions":** this may include sexual temptation. But in this context of quarrels, many interpreters think it mainly refers to other desires the young are prone to: impatience, the urge to win, love of argument, love of novelty.
- **"Along with those who call on the Lord from a pure heart":** pursuing holiness is not a solitary project. Timothy is to pursue it with others who call on the Lord from a pure heart.

**3. "The Lord's servant must not be quarrelsome."** "Have nothing to do with foolish, ignorant controversies; you know that they breed quarrels. And the Lord's servant must not be quarrelsome but kind to everyone, able to teach, patiently enduring evil, correcting his opponents with gentleness" (2:23–25a).
- **"The Lord's servant":** the Old Testament calls Moses and Joshua "the servant of the LORD" (Deut 34:5; Josh 24:29); some also hear an echo of Isaiah's Servant: "a bruised reed he will not break" (Isa 42:3). The Lord's servant in a dispute should look like the Lord himself.
- **Gentleness is not weakness:** Timothy must still correct his opponents; he does not stop caring about right and wrong. But his manner must be gentle and patient, because his aim is not to win an argument but to win back a person.

**4. "God may perhaps grant them repentance."** "God may perhaps grant them repentance leading to a knowledge of the truth, and they may escape from the snare of the devil, after being captured by him to do his will" (2:25b–26).
- **Repentance is God's gift:** Timothy cannot make anyone repent; he can only teach gently. It is God who grants repentance (see Acts 11:18, "Then to the Gentiles also God has granted repentance that leads to life"). This humbles the teacher and also gives him hope: God can change even the most stubborn.
- **The devil's snare:** those who oppose the truth do not merely hold wrong views; they have been taken captive by the devil. So we hate the error, but pity the captive.
- **A difficult phrase:** the pronouns in the last clause of verse 26 can be read in different ways. The Chinese Union Version and many modern translations, including the ESV, take it as: they were captured by the devil to do the devil's will. Others read it as: having escaped the devil's snare, they are taken alive by God (or by God's servant) to do God's will. Either way, God is the one who can rescue people from the snare.

> The point: The Lord's servant does not compromise on the truth, but he is gentle in manner. We correct our opponents not to win, but in the hope that God will grant them repentance and free them from the devil's snare.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For which I am suffering, bound with chains as a criminal. But the word of God is not bound!" (2:9). Sometimes I worry about the future of the gospel. I see fewer people in church, and the Sunday school half empty; I see young people leaving one by one, and my own children, grown up, no longer going to church; I see churches closed in some places and preachers arrested. Slowly a kind of despair grows in me: is the gospel in retreat? Is the church about to give way? Sometimes the worry turns into anxiety, as if everything depended on those of us who are left holding on with all our might, and if we cannot hold on, it is over. When Paul wrote this verse, his situation was far worse than mine. He was chained in prison, his coworkers scattered, all in Asia had turned away from him, and he himself would soon be executed. From a human point of view, this was the gospel's darkest hour. Yet he says, "But the word of God is not bound!" How can he say it? The verse before tells me: "Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead." The tomb could not hold him, and chains cannot hold his word. That Lord died for my sins and was raised for me; he is alive today, and he is still calling his people. I can grieve over a church in decline, and weep over a child who has left; that is not unbelief. But I can hand my despair to him and stop carrying the weight of "everything depends on me." Today I can name the person or the church I worry about most, and ask the Lord to let his word run there, in his way and his time. Then I can remember him: Jesus Christ, the Lord risen from the dead.
