## 本章脉络

第三章分成四段。**末世危险的日子**（1-5节）：那时人要专顾自己，贪爱钱财，爱宴乐，不爱神，有敬虔的外貌，却背了敬虔的实意；这等人要躲开。**偷进人家的人**（6-9节）：他们牢笼无知的妇女，叫她们常常学习，终久不能明白真道；他们像雅尼和佯庇敌挡摩西一样敌挡真道，他们的愚昧必显露出来。**保罗的榜样**（10-13节）：提摩太已经服从了保罗的教训、品行和忍耐，也知道他在安提阿、以哥念、路司得所受的逼迫，主都把他救出来了；凡立志在基督耶稣里敬虔度日的，也都要受逼迫。**住在圣经里**（14-17节）：提摩太要持守所学习、所确信的，因为他知道是跟谁学的，也从小明白圣经；圣经都是神所默示的，叫属神的人得以完全，预备行各样的善事。

这一章前后两半形成对比。前一半讲那些「常常学习，终久不能明白真道」的人（7节）；后一半讲提摩太「从小明白圣经」，这圣经「能使你因信基督耶稣，有得救的智慧」（15节）。分别不在于学多学少，而在于人是不是在圣经里遇见基督。

## 深读 3:1-5 有敬虔的外貌，却背了敬虔的实意

**1. 「末世必有危险的日子来到」。** 「你该知道，末世必有危险的日子来到。」（3:1）
- **「末世」是什么时候？** 新约常用「末世」指基督第一次来临到他再来之间的整个时期。彼得在五旬节说，约珥所说「在末后的日子」的应许已经应验了（徒2:17）；希伯来书也说神「就在这末世藉着他儿子晓谕我们」（来1:2）。所以保罗不是只讲遥远的将来，他在第5节就叫提摩太「这等人你要躲开」，可见这些人当时已经在以弗所了。
- **「危险」：** 原文的意思是艰难、凶险，甚至凶猛。马太福音用同一个词形容两个被鬼附、「极其凶猛」的人（太8:28）。

**2. 一张长长的清单。** 「因为那时，人要专顾自己，贪爱钱财，自夸，狂傲」（3:2），「卖主卖友，任意妄为，自高自大，爱宴乐，不爱神」（3:4）。
- **头和尾：** 这张清单大约有十八项。开头是「专顾自己，贪爱钱财」，结尾是「爱宴乐，不爱神」。原文这几个词都和「爱」有关：爱自己、爱钱、爱宴乐，却不爱神。人的问题，归根到底是爱错了对象。奥古斯丁说，美德就是有次序的爱（《上帝之城》15.22）；罪就是爱的次序乱了。
- **家庭的破裂：** 清单里有「违背父母」、「无亲情」（3:2-3）。人不爱神的时候，最亲近的关系也会坏掉。
- **不是只指外面的人：** 第5节说这些人「有敬虔的外貌」，可见他们在教会里面，至少自称是信徒。这张清单不是叫我们去指着外面的世界，而是先照一照自己。

**3. 「有敬虔的外貌，却背了敬虔的实意」。** 「有敬虔的外貌，却背了敬虔的实意；这等人你要躲开。」（3:5）
- **「实意」：** 原文是能力。他们有宗教的外表：参加聚会，会说属灵的话，甚至带领别人；却否认敬虔的能力，就是神改变人心、使人爱神爱人的能力。
- **「这等人你要躲开」：** 保罗在第二章叫提摩太「用温柔劝戒那抵挡的人」（2:25），这里又叫他「躲开」。这两样并不矛盾：要温柔地劝戒，盼望他们悔改；但不要让他们成为自己的同伴和榜样，也不要让他们继续影响教会。

> 要点：人的问题是爱错了对象，爱自己、爱钱、爱宴乐胜过爱神。真敬虔不在外貌，而在神改变人心的能力：神在基督里先爱了我们，叫我们的爱重新有了次序（约一4:19）。

## 深读 3:6-9 常常学习，终久不能明白真道

**1. 「那偷进人家、牢笼无知妇女的」。** 「那偷进人家、牢笼无知妇女的，正是这等人。这些妇女担负罪恶，被各样的私欲引诱，常常学习，终久不能明白真道。」（3:6-7）
- **「偷进人家」：** 早期教会多在家中聚会。假教师不是公开地站出来，而是悄悄地进到信徒家里，一家一家地影响人。
- **「无知妇女」：** 原文是一个带轻视意味的指小词，有点像说那些小女人，可能指以弗所某一群容易被骗的妇女，而不是说女人比男人更容易受骗。提摩太前书也提到一些年轻寡妇「挨家闲游」，「说些不当说的话」（提前5:13）。保罗在这里责备的主要是那些利用她们的假教师。
- **为什么她们容易被牢笼？** 保罗说她们「担负罪恶，被各样的私欲引诱」。一个被罪疚压着、却没有在基督里得着赦免和平安的人，很容易被一个许诺新知识、新经历的人吸引。被人利用、被人欺骗，绝不是受害者的错；罪责在利用人的人身上。
- **「常常学习，终久不能明白真道」：** 她们不是不学习，而是一直在学。可是她们学的东西没有把她们带到真理，就是基督那里。今天也有人上了很多课程、听了很多讲座，却从来没有在基督面前悔改、相信。

**2. 今天的「偷进人家」。** 这段经文对今天的教会仍然很贴切。
- **异端的方法：** 有些异端，例如在华人教会中造成很大伤害的东方闪电（自称全能神教会），专门找教会里的信徒下手：先以查经、交朋友的名义接近人，进到家里，慢慢让人离开原来的教会。我们要提醒弟兄姊妹，特别是初信的和孤单的人，学会分辨。
- **属灵的操控：** 这段经文也提醒我们，有人会利用属灵的权威，进到别人的家庭和生活里，控制人、剥削人，甚至在性方面侵犯人。这样的事若发生在教会里，教会不可为了面子、「家丑不可外扬」而掩盖；受害的人应当得到保护；向警方举报是正当的，也是应当的（罗13:4）。施害的人即使认罪悔改，也不能因此恢复原来的职分，或再接触受害的人和孩子。

**3. 「雅尼和佯庇怎样敌挡摩西」。** 「从前雅尼和佯庇怎样敌挡摩西，这等人也怎样敌挡真道。他们的心地坏了，在真道上是可废弃的。然而他们不能再这样敌挡；因为他们的愚昧必在众人面前显露出来，像那二人一样。」（3:8-9）
- **雅尼和佯庇是谁？** 旧约没有提到这两个名字。按照犹太人的传统，他们就是在法老面前用邪术与摩西对抗的埃及术士（出7:11、22）。死海古卷中的《大马士革文献》（5.18-19）提到雅尼和他的兄弟；两个名字一起出现在亚兰文的旧约意译本《伪约拿单他尔根》（如解释出埃及记7:11的地方）；罗马作家老普林尼（《自然史》30.11）也提到雅尼是一个术士。保罗引用当时大家熟悉的传统来举例，并不表示他认可那些传说中的每一个细节。
- **假的能模仿，却不能胜过：** 埃及的术士起初也能把杖变成蛇，「但亚伦的杖吞了他们的杖」（出7:12）。到了后来，他们自己也承认：「这是神的手段」（出8:19）。假教师能模仿敬虔的外貌，却不能长久；他们的愚昧终必显露出来。

> 要点：假教师会悄悄进到人的家里，利用人的罪疚和渴望。一直学习却不能明白真道的人，所缺的不是更多的知识，而是在基督里得赦免、得自由。

## 深读 3:10-13 凡立志敬虔度日的也都要受逼迫

**1. 「但你已经服从了我的教训」。** 「但你已经服从了我的教训、品行、志向、信心、宽容、爱心、忍耐，以及我在安提阿、以哥念、路司得所遭遇的逼迫、苦难。」（3:10-11上）
- **「但你」：** 和前面那些假教师相对。提摩太不是跟随一套抽象的理论，而是跟随一个人的生命：保罗的教训、品行、志向、忍耐，以及他所受的逼迫。
- **看得见的榜样：** 保罗把「教训」和「品行」放在一起。真正的教师，不只是讲得好，也活得出来。这也是今天培养下一代的方法（参2:2）。
- **为什么提这三座城？** 安提阿、以哥念、路司得是保罗第一次旅行布道时到过的地方（徒13-14章）。路司得就是提摩太的家乡（徒16:1）。保罗在那里被人用石头打，「以为他是死了，便拖到城外」（徒14:19）。提摩太很可能亲眼看见，或者亲耳听说过。不久以后，保罗回到这些城里，「坚固门徒的心……又说：『我们进入神的国，必须经历许多艰难。』」（徒14:22）

**2. 「从这一切苦难中，主都把我救出来了」。** 「我所忍受是何等的逼迫；但从这一切苦难中，主都把我救出来了。」（3:11下）
- **怎样的救法？** 主没有让保罗免去逼迫，他被打了、被赶出城了；但主在每一次的逼迫中保守他，领他走过来。如今保罗面对的可能是死亡，但他在4:18仍然说：「主必救我脱离诸般的凶恶，也必救我进他的天国。」最终的拯救，是进入神的国。

**3. 「凡立志在基督耶稣里敬虔度日的也都要受逼迫」。** 「不但如此，凡立志在基督耶稣里敬虔度日的也都要受逼迫。只是作恶的和迷惑人的，必越久越恶，他欺哄人，也被人欺哄。」（3:12-13）
- **「凡……都要」：** 这不是只给使徒的，是给每一个立志敬虔度日的信徒的。耶稣也说过：「他们若逼迫了我，也要逼迫你们。」（约15:20）
- **逼迫的样子不同：** 有的地方，信徒被关、被打、被杀；有的地方，是被家人反对、被同事讥笑、在升职时被排挤，或者因为不肯在账目上作假而丢了工作。在华人家庭中，有人因为不肯在丧礼上拜祭，被说成不孝。其实信徒不拜，是因为敬拜只归给神，并不是不尊敬父母；我们仍要用追思、照顾家人来尽孝。大小不同，都是为主的缘故受苦。
- **不是每一种苦都是逼迫：** 彼得提醒我们，不要因为自己的过错受苦（彼前4:15）。如果别人疏远我，是因为我态度高傲、说话刻薄，那就不是为义受逼迫，而是要悔改。
- **「欺哄人，也被人欺哄」：** 骗人的人最后也被骗了。他们以为自己掌控一切，其实自己也被罪和魔鬼欺骗了（参2:26）。

> 要点：跟随基督不保证一帆风顺；凡立志敬虔度日的都要受逼迫。但主在每一次逼迫中与我们同在，最后必救我们进他的天国。

## 深读 3:14-17 圣经都是神所默示的

**1. 「因为你知道是跟谁学的」。** 「但你所学习的，所确信的，要存在心里；因为你知道是跟谁学的，并且知道你是从小明白圣经，这圣经能使你因信基督耶稣，有得救的智慧。」（3:14-15）
- **「跟谁学的」：** 按较好的抄本，原文这里的「谁」是复数，很可能包括保罗，也包括罗以和友妮基（1:5）。提摩太所信的道，是他从信得过的人那里学来的，他看见过他们的生命。
- **「从小」：** 原文指婴孩。按照犹太人的习惯，孩子很小就开始学习圣经；后来的犹太文献《密西拿·先贤篇》5.21说，五岁就可以开始读经。提摩太的母亲是犹太人，她从小就教他。
- **「圣经」：** 在这里指的是旧约。提摩太小时候还没有新约。
- **「因信基督耶稣，有得救的智慧」：** 旧约本身就指向基督。耶稣说：「给我作见证的就是这经。」（约5:39）他复活以后，也「从摩西和众先知起，凡经上所指着自己的话都给他们讲解明白了」（路24:27）。读圣经的目的，不只是增加知识，而是认识基督，因信他得救。这正和第7节「常常学习，终久不能明白真道」的人相反。

**2. 「圣经都是神所默示的」。** 「圣经都是神所默示的，于教训、督责、使人归正、教导人学义都是有益的。」（3:16）
- **「神所默示的」：** 原文是一个很少见的词，意思是神呼出来的。圣经当然能启发人，但保罗在这里说的是：圣经本身是从神口中出来的。诗篇说：「诸天藉耶和华的命而造；万象藉他口中的气而成。」（诗33:6）神用他口中的气造了天地，也用他口中的气说出了圣经。普林斯顿神学家华腓德（十九、二十世纪之交）仔细研究过这个词，指出它的意思是由神呼出，不是能启发人。
- **神和人：** 这不是说神口授、人笔录。彼得说：「人被圣灵感动，说出神的话来。」（彼后1:21）圣经的作者有各自的文风和背景，路加也说自己「从起头都详细考察了」（路1:3）。神藉着这些人的思想和文字，说出了他自己的话。
- **「都」：** 原文可以理解为圣经的全部，也可以理解为圣经的每一部分。另外，也有人把这句读成凡神所默示的圣经都是有益的；但大多数译本和解经家认为，保罗说的是两件事：圣经都是神所默示的，也都是有益的。无论哪一种译法，圣经都不是有一部分是神的话，另一部分只是人的话。
- **也包括新约吗？** 保罗这里首先指旧约。但新约的作者已经把使徒的著作和旧约放在一起：彼得把保罗的书信和「别的经书」相提并论（彼后3:16）。许多人认为，保罗自己在提前5:18也把路加福音10:7的话和旧约一起称为「经」。所以教会从早期就认为，这节经文所说的，也适用于整本新约。

**3. 四样益处。** 「于教训、督责、使人归正、教导人学义都是有益的。」（3:16）
- **教训：** 告诉我们什么是真的：神是谁，人是谁，基督做了什么。
- **督责：** 指出我们错在哪里，在思想上，也在行为上。
- **使人归正：** 不只指出错误，还把人带回正路。原文有把倒下的东西重新扶正的意思。
- **教导人学义：** 像父母教养孩子一样，一步一步地训练我们过公义的生活。
- **顺序的意思：** 有人留意到，这四样有一个顺序：先教导真理，再指出错误，然后扶正，最后训练。圣经不只告诉我们错了，也领我们回到正路，在正路上继续走。

**4. 「叫属神的人得以完全」。** 「叫属神的人得以完全，预备行各样的善事。」（3:17）
- **「属神的人」：** 这是旧约对摩西、以利亚等神仆的称呼，提摩太前书6:11也用它称呼提摩太。这里可能首先指传道的人，但也适用于每一个属神的信徒。
- **「得以完全」：** 原文的意思是装备齐全，能胜任。圣经足以装备我们去做神要我们做的一切善事。我们不需要另找新启示、秘传的道理，也不需要拿儒家经典或佛经来补足圣经。

> 要点：圣经是神呼出来的话，能使我们因信基督得救，也足以装备我们行各样的善事。读圣经的目的不是只求知识，而是在圣经里认识基督。

## 默想

**默想：** 「不但如此，凡立志在基督耶稣里敬虔度日的也都要受逼迫。」（3:12）我信主以后，曾经以为日子会顺一点：家里会更和睦，工作会更平顺。可是事情不是这样。父亲的丧礼上，我没有跟着拜，亲戚当着大家的面说我「不孝」，到现在还有人不跟我说话；在公司，我不肯在报销单上多写一笔，同事觉得我假清高，有些机会也不再找我。我心里有一个问题，不太敢说出口：主啊，我跟随你，为什么反而更难了？保罗没有骗提摩太。他早就说了：「凡……都要受逼迫。」提摩太也亲眼看过，保罗在路司得被人用石头打，被拖到城外。可是保罗回头看的时候说：「从这一切苦难中，主都把我救出来了。」我也要先在主面前诚实：父亲在世的时候，我有没有好好孝敬他？在丧礼上和以后，我有没有用别的方式尊敬他、照顾家人？有时候别人疏远我，不是因为我敬虔，而是因为我说话刻薄、态度高傲。这些都是我要悔改的地方，也都是主愿意赦免的地方。如果有人用暴力对待我，我也不必默默忍受，可以寻求帮助和保护。但如果我真是为主的缘故被人误会、被人排挤，我可以想起那位先走在前面的主。他说：「世人若恨你们，你们知道，恨你们以先已经恨我了。」（约15:18）他被自己的家乡人厌弃，被门徒离弃，最后被钉在十字架上，在那里替我受了我该受的刑罚，使我这个罪人蒙神接纳。所以那些不再跟我说话的亲戚，不能决定我在神面前的地位。今天我可以把那件最让我难受的事告诉主，也可以为那些反对我的人祷告。然后我可以记得彼得的话：「你们若为基督的名受辱骂，便是有福的；因为神荣耀的灵常住在你们身上。」（彼前4:14）若我被人看为不孝、假清高，真是为了基督的名，神就称我为有福的；他荣耀的灵就在这羞辱里与我同在。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 falls into four parts. **Times of difficulty in the last days** (vv. 1–5): people will be lovers of self, lovers of money, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, having the appearance of godliness but denying its power; avoid such people. **Those who creep into households** (vv. 6–9): they capture weak women who are always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth; as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, so these oppose the truth, and their folly will be plain to all. **Paul's example** (vv. 10–13): Timothy has followed Paul's teaching, conduct and steadfastness, and knows the persecutions he endured at Antioch, Iconium and Lystra, from which the Lord rescued him; all who desire to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted. **Continue in the Scriptures** (vv. 14–17): Timothy is to continue in what he has learned and firmly believed, knowing from whom he learned it, and how from childhood he has known the sacred writings; all Scripture is breathed out by God, that the man of God may be competent, equipped for every good work.

The two halves of this chapter stand in contrast. The first half speaks of those "always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth" (v. 7); the second half speaks of Timothy, who "from childhood" has known the sacred writings, "which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus" (v. 15). The difference is not how much one learns, but whether one meets Christ in Scripture.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–5 The Appearance of Godliness, Denying Its Power

**1. "In the last days there will come times of difficulty."** "But understand this, that in the last days there will come times of difficulty" (3:1).
- **When are "the last days"?** The New Testament often uses "the last days" for the whole period between Christ's first coming and his return. At Pentecost Peter said that Joel's promise "in the last days" had been fulfilled (Acts 2:17); Hebrews says that God "in these last days... has spoken to us by his Son" (Heb 1:2). So Paul is not speaking only of the distant future. In verse 5 he tells Timothy, "Avoid such people," which shows that they were already in Ephesus.
- **"Difficulty":** the word means hard, dangerous, even savage. Matthew uses it of the two demon-possessed men who were "so fierce" (Matt 8:28).

**2. A long list.** "For people will be lovers of self, lovers of money, proud, arrogant" (3:2), "treacherous, reckless, swollen with conceit, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God" (3:4).
- **First and last:** the list has about eighteen items. It begins with "lovers of self, lovers of money" and ends with "lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God." In Greek these words are all built on "love": love of self, love of money, love of pleasure, but not love of God. At root, humanity's problem is loving the wrong things. Augustine called virtue "rightly ordered love" (*City of God* 15.22); sin is love out of order.
- **Families breaking down:** the list includes "disobedient to their parents" and "heartless" (3:2–3). When people do not love God, even their closest relationships go bad.
- **Not only outsiders:** verse 5 says these people have "the appearance of godliness," so they are inside the church, or at least claim to be believers. This list is not for pointing at the world outside; it is first a mirror for ourselves.

**3. "Having the appearance of godliness, but denying its power."** "Having the appearance of godliness, but denying its power. Avoid such people" (3:5).
- **"Power":** they have religion's outward form: they attend meetings, speak spiritual language, even lead others. But they deny the power of godliness, the power of God to change the heart and make people love God and others.
- **"Avoid such people":** in chapter 2 Paul told Timothy to correct opponents "with gentleness" (2:25); here he tells him to avoid them. The two do not conflict: correct them gently, hoping they will repent, but do not make them companions and examples, and do not let them keep influencing the church.

> The point: Humanity's problem is loving the wrong things, self, money and pleasure more than God. True godliness is not an outward form but God's power to change the heart: God first loved us in Christ, and that puts our loves back in order (1 John 4:19).

## Going Deeper: 3:6–9 Always Learning, Never Arriving at the Truth

**1. "Those who creep into households and capture weak women."** "For among them are those who creep into households and capture weak women, burdened with sins and led astray by various passions, always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth" (3:6–7).
- **"Creep into households":** the early church met mostly in homes. The false teachers did not stand up openly; they slipped quietly into believers' homes and influenced people one household at a time.
- **"Weak women":** the Greek is a diminutive with a dismissive tone. It probably refers to a particular group of women in Ephesus who were easily deceived, not to women as more easily deceived than men. First Timothy also mentions some younger widows "going about from house to house" and "saying what they should not" (1 Tim 5:13). Paul's rebuke here falls mainly on the false teachers who exploited them.
- **Why were they easily captured?** Paul says they were "burdened with sins and led astray by various passions." Someone weighed down by guilt, who has not found forgiveness and peace in Christ, is easily drawn to anyone who promises new knowledge or new experiences. Being exploited or deceived is never the victim's fault; the guilt lies with the one who exploits.
- **"Always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth":** they were not failing to learn; they were always learning. But what they learned never brought them to the truth, to Christ himself. Today too some people take course after course and attend lecture after lecture, yet never repent and believe before Christ.

**2. "Creeping into households" today.** This passage still speaks very directly to the church today.
- **The methods of cults:** some cults, such as Eastern Lightning (the Church of Almighty God), which has done great harm in Chinese churches, deliberately target church members. They approach people in the name of Bible study or friendship, come into their homes, and gradually draw them away from their churches. We need to help brothers and sisters, especially new believers and lonely people, learn to discern.
- **Spiritual manipulation:** this passage also warns that some will use spiritual authority to enter other people's homes and lives, to control and exploit them, even to abuse them sexually. If this happens in a church, the church must not cover it up to save face ("don't air the family's dirty laundry"). Those who are harmed must be protected, and it is right to report it to the police (Rom 13:4). Even if the abuser confesses and repents, that does not restore his office or give him access to victims or children.

**3. "Just as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses."** "Just as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, so these men also oppose the truth, men corrupted in mind and disqualified regarding the faith. But they will not get very far, for their folly will be plain to all, as was that of those two men" (3:8–9).
- **Who were Jannes and Jambres?** The Old Testament does not give these names. In Jewish tradition they were the Egyptian magicians who opposed Moses with their secret arts before Pharaoh (Exod 7:11, 22). Jannes appears in the Damascus Document among the Dead Sea Scrolls (5.18–19, "Jannes and his brother"); both names appear together in the Aramaic paraphrase known as Targum Pseudo-Jonathan (for example, on Exodus 7:11); and the Roman writer Pliny the Elder (*Natural History* 30.11) mentions Jannes as a magician. Paul uses a tradition his readers knew as an illustration; this does not mean he endorses every detail of the legends.
- **The false can imitate but not prevail:** at first the Egyptian magicians could also turn staffs into serpents, "but Aaron's staff swallowed up their staffs" (Exod 7:12). Later they themselves admitted, "This is the finger of God" (Exod 8:19). False teachers can imitate the appearance of godliness, but not for long; their folly will be exposed.

> The point: False teachers slip into homes and exploit people's guilt and longings. What those who are always learning but never arrive at the truth lack is not more knowledge, but forgiveness and freedom in Christ.

## Going Deeper: 3:10–13 All Who Desire to Live a Godly Life Will Be Persecuted

**1. "You, however, have followed my teaching."** "You, however, have followed my teaching, my conduct, my aim in life, my faith, my patience, my love, my steadfastness, my persecutions and sufferings that happened to me at Antioch, at Iconium, and at Lystra" (3:10–11a).
- **"You, however":** in contrast to the false teachers. Timothy has not followed an abstract theory but a man's life: Paul's teaching, and also his conduct, aim, steadfastness and sufferings.
- **A visible example:** Paul puts "teaching" and "conduct" side by side. A true teacher does not only speak well; he lives it. This is also how the next generation is trained today (see 2:2).
- **Why these three cities?** Antioch, Iconium and Lystra were places Paul visited on his first missionary journey (Acts 13–14). Lystra was Timothy's hometown (Acts 16:1). There Paul was stoned and "dragged... out of the city, supposing that he was dead" (Acts 14:19). Timothy may well have seen it, or at least heard of it firsthand. Soon afterward Paul returned to these cities, "strengthening the souls of the disciples... saying that through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God" (Acts 14:22).

**2. "Yet from them all the Lord rescued me."** "Which persecutions I endured; yet from them all the Lord rescued me" (3:11b).
- **What kind of rescue?** The Lord did not spare Paul from persecution: he was beaten and driven out of cities. But in each persecution the Lord kept him and brought him through. Now Paul may be facing death, yet in 4:18 he still says, "The Lord will rescue me from every evil deed and bring me safely into his heavenly kingdom." The final rescue is entrance into God's kingdom.

**3. "All who desire to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted."** "Indeed, all who desire to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted, while evil people and impostors will go on from bad to worse, deceiving and being deceived" (3:12–13).
- **"All":** this is not only for apostles but for every believer who desires to live a godly life. Jesus said, "If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you" (John 15:20).
- **Persecution takes different forms:** in some places believers are imprisoned, beaten, killed. In others they are opposed by their families, mocked by colleagues, passed over for promotion, or lose their jobs for refusing to falsify accounts. In Chinese families, some are called "unfilial" for refusing to bow and offer sacrifices at a funeral. But believers do not bow because worship belongs to God alone, not because they do not honor their parents; we still honor them by remembering them and caring for the family. Great or small, these are sufferings for the Lord's sake.
- **Not every suffering is persecution:** Peter reminds us not to suffer for our own wrongdoing (1 Pet 4:15). If people keep their distance because I am proud or my words are cutting, that is not being persecuted for righteousness; it is a call to repent.
- **"Deceiving and being deceived":** those who deceive end up deceived. They think they are in control, but they themselves have been deceived by sin and the devil (see 2:26).

> The point: Following Christ does not guarantee a smooth life; all who desire to live a godly life will be persecuted. But the Lord is with us in every persecution, and in the end he will bring us safely into his heavenly kingdom.

## Going Deeper: 3:14–17 All Scripture Is Breathed Out by God

**1. "Knowing from whom you learned it."** "But as for you, continue in what you have learned and have firmly believed, knowing from whom you learned it and how from childhood you have been acquainted with the sacred writings, which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus" (3:14–15).
- **"From whom":** in the best manuscripts, "whom" here is plural. It probably includes Paul, and also Lois and Eunice (1:5). The faith Timothy holds was learned from people he could trust, whose lives he had seen.
- **"From childhood":** the Greek word means infancy. Jewish children began learning Scripture very young; a later Jewish text, the Mishnah (*Avot* 5.21), says a child is ready for Scripture at five. Timothy's mother was Jewish, and she taught him from his earliest years.
- **"The sacred writings":** here this means the Old Testament. When Timothy was a child, there was no New Testament yet.
- **"Wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus":** the Old Testament itself points to Christ. Jesus said, "it is they that bear witness about me" (John 5:39). After he rose, "beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27). The aim of reading Scripture is not just more knowledge, but knowing Christ and being saved through faith in him. This is the opposite of those in verse 7, "always learning and never able to arrive at a knowledge of the truth."

**2. "All Scripture is breathed out by God."** "All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness" (3:16).
- **"Breathed out by God":** the Greek is a rare word meaning "God-breathed." Paul does not say that Scripture is inspiring (though it is), but that Scripture itself has come from God's mouth. The psalm says, "By the word of the LORD the heavens were made, and by the breath of his mouth all their host" (Ps 33:6). By the breath of his mouth God made the heavens, and by the breath of his mouth he spoke the Scriptures. The Princeton theologian B.B. Warfield, at the turn of the twentieth century, studied this word with great care and argued that it means "breathed out by God," not "breathing out God" or simply "inspiring."
- **God and human authors:** this does not mean God dictated and people took notes. Peter says, "men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit" (2 Pet 1:21). The biblical authors had their own styles and backgrounds; Luke says he had "followed all things closely for some time past" (Luke 1:3). Through these people's thoughts and words, God spoke his own word.
- **"All":** the Greek can be translated "all Scripture" or "every Scripture." Some also translate it, "every God-breathed Scripture is also profitable." But most translations and interpreters understand Paul to say that all Scripture is God-breathed and all of it is profitable. On any of these readings, Scripture is not partly God's word and partly merely human words.
- **Does it include the New Testament?** Paul first means the Old Testament. But New Testament writers already set the apostles' writings alongside the Old Testament: Peter puts Paul's letters with "the other Scriptures" (2 Pet 3:16). Many think Paul himself, in 1 Timothy 5:18, quotes Luke 10:7 alongside the Old Testament as "Scripture." So from early on the church has held that what this verse says applies to the whole New Testament as well.

**3. Four uses.** "Profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness" (3:16).
- **Teaching:** it tells us what is true: who God is, who we are, what Christ has done.
- **Reproof:** it shows us where we are wrong, in thought and in action.
- **Correction:** it does not only point out error, but brings us back to the right way. The Greek has the sense of setting upright what has fallen.
- **Training in righteousness:** like parents bringing up a child, it trains us step by step to live righteously.
- **The order:** some notice that the four come in an order: first teaching the truth, then exposing error, then setting right, then training. Scripture does not only tell us we are wrong; it leads us back to the right path and helps us keep walking on it.

**4. "That the man of God may be competent."** "That the man of God may be competent, equipped for every good work" (3:17).
- **"The man of God":** an Old Testament title for servants of God like Moses and Elijah, which 1 Timothy 6:11 also uses for Timothy. Here it may refer first to ministers, but it applies to every believer who belongs to God.
- **"Competent, equipped":** the words mean fully fitted out, able for the task. Scripture is enough to equip us for every good work God asks of us. We do not need new revelations or secret teachings, nor Confucian classics or Buddhist scriptures, to complete it.

> The point: Scripture is the word God has breathed out. It is able to make us wise for salvation through faith in Christ, and it is enough to equip us for every good work. We read it not just for knowledge, but to know Christ in it.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Indeed, all who desire to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted" (3:12). When I first believed, I thought life would become a little smoother: more peace at home, things going better at work. It has not been like that. At my father's funeral I did not bow with the others, and a relative called me "unfilial" in front of everyone; some still do not speak to me. At the office I would not add an extra line to an expense claim, and my colleagues think I am putting on airs; some opportunities no longer come my way. There is a question in my heart I hardly dare say aloud: Lord, I am following you, so why has it become harder? Paul did not deceive Timothy. He said it plainly: "all... will be persecuted." Timothy had seen it himself: Paul stoned at Lystra and dragged out of the city. Yet looking back, Paul said, "from them all the Lord rescued me." I also need to be honest before the Lord: while my father was alive, did I honor him? At the funeral and since, have I shown respect and cared for the family in other ways? Sometimes people keep their distance not because I am godly, but because my words are cutting or my manner proud. These are things to repent of, and things the Lord is glad to forgive. If someone treats me with violence, I need not suffer it in silence; I can seek help and protection. But if I truly am misunderstood and pushed aside for the Lord's sake, I can remember the Lord who went ahead of me. He said, "If the world hates you, know that it has hated me before it hated you" (John 15:18). He was rejected by his own hometown, deserted by his disciples, and at last nailed to a cross, where he bore the punishment my sin deserved, so that I, a sinner, could be welcomed by God. So the relatives who no longer speak to me do not decide where I stand before God. Today I can tell the Lord about the thing that hurts most, and I can pray for those who oppose me. Then I can remember Peter's words: "If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the Spirit of glory and of God rests upon you" (1 Pet 4:14). If I am called unfilial and accused of putting on airs for the name of Christ, God calls me blessed; his Spirit of glory rests on me in that very shame.
