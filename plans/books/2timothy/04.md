## 本章脉络

第四章分成四段。**务要传道**（1-5节）：保罗在神面前，并在将来审判活人死人的基督耶稣面前嘱咐提摩太：无论得时不得时，总要传道；因为时候要到，人必厌烦纯正的道理，耳朵发痒，增添好些师傅；提摩太却要凡事谨慎，忍受苦难，做传道的工夫，尽他的职分。**那美好的仗我已经打过了**（6-8节）：保罗现在被浇奠，离世的时候到了；他已经打完那美好的仗，跑尽当跑的路，守住所信的道；有公义的冠冕为他存留，也为凡爱慕主显现的人存留。**来的时候要带的人和东西**（9-15节）：底马贪爱现今的世界离弃了保罗，独有路加在他那里；提摩太要把马可带来，也要带外衣、书和皮卷；要防备铜匠亚历山大。**惟有主站在我旁边**（16-22节）：保罗初次申诉的时候，没有人帮助他，惟有主站在他旁边，加给他力量；主必救他进他的天国；最后的问安和祝福。

这一章是保罗留下的最后的话。他先把传道的职分郑重地交给提摩太，然后回头看自己的一生，最后谈到身边的人和一些很平常的需要：一件外衣，几本书。在这一切中间，他说出了全章最深的一句话：「惟有主站在我旁边。」（17节）

## 深读 4:1-5 务要传道

**1. 「在将来审判活人死人的基督耶稣面前」。** 「我在神面前，并在将来审判活人死人的基督耶稣面前，凭着他的显现和他的国度嘱咐你。」（4:1）
- **郑重的嘱咐：** 这是一句像起誓一样的话。保罗请神和基督作见证，因为接下来的嘱咐太重要了。
- **「审判活人死人」：** 彼得在哥尼流家里也说，耶稣「是神所立定的，要作审判活人、死人的主」（徒10:42）。后来的《使徒信经》也这样认信。传道的人要记得：最终审判他的，是基督。
- **「他的显现和他的国度」：** 保罗把提摩太的眼光引向将来：基督要显现，他的国要完全来到。传道是在这个盼望中进行的。

**2. 「务要传道」。** 「务要传道，无论得时不得时总要专心，并用百般的忍耐、各样的教训责备人，警戒人，劝勉人。」（4:2）
- **「传道」：** 原文的意思是像传令官一样宣告。传道的人不是发表自己的意见，而是宣告王的话。
- **「得时不得时」：** 不管方便不方便，不管人爱听不爱听，也不管自己有没有感觉。有人认为这主要是指听的人：人欢迎的时候要传，人不欢迎的时候也要传；也有人认为同时指传道的人自己。
- **「责备人，警戒人，劝勉人」：** 传道包括指出罪，也包括安慰和鼓励。只有责备没有劝勉，会叫人灰心；只有劝勉没有责备，会叫人安于罪中。
- **「用百般的忍耐、各样的教训」：** 传道不是一次就能见效的，要有耐心，也要讲清楚。

**3. 「耳朵发痒」。** 「因为时候要到，人必厌烦纯正的道理，耳朵发痒，就随从自己的情欲，增添好些师傅，并且掩耳不听真道，偏向荒渺的言语。」（4:3-4）
- **「耳朵发痒」：** 就是想听新鲜、好听、合自己口味的话。人不是不想听道，而是只想听自己想听的道。
- **「增添好些师傅」：** 人会给自己找很多教师，越多越好，只要他们说的合自己的意思。今天，人可以在网上随意选择讲道，算法也会不断推送我们爱听的内容。我们很容易只听那些叫我们舒服的讲员，而避开那些叫我们不安的经文。
- **「荒渺的言语」：** 原文是神话、传说（参提前1:4；4:7）。人掩耳不听真道，并不是什么都不信了，而是转去相信更动听的故事。

**4. 「你却要凡事谨慎」。** 「你却要凡事谨慎，忍受苦难，做传道的工夫，尽你的职分。」（4:5）
- **「你却要」：** 别人怎样，提摩太不能控制；他能做的，是忠心做好自己的事。
- **「谨慎」：** 原文是清醒，像没有喝醉的人那样头脑清楚。
- **「做传道的工夫」：** 原文的「传道」是传福音的意思（参徒21:8，腓利被称为「传福音的」）。提摩太在教会里牧养、教导，但不可忘记向未信的人传福音。
- **「尽你的职分」：** 把神交给你的工作完全做完。保罗在下一节说，他自己已经跑尽了当跑的路。

> 要点：传道的人在审判活人死人的基督面前领受了托付。人会耳朵发痒、只想听好听的话，但传道的人要无论得时不得时，忠心传讲神的道，因为他最终是向基督交账。

## 深读 4:6-8 那美好的仗我已经打过了

**1. 「我现在被浇奠」。** 「我现在被浇奠，我离世的时候到了。」（4:6）
- **「浇奠」：** 旧约献祭时，有时会把酒浇在祭物上或祭坛旁，称为奠祭（民28:7）。保罗把自己即将到来的死，看作献给神的奠祭。他在腓立比书也用过这个比喻：「我若被浇奠在其上，也是喜乐」（腓2:17）。那时他说「若」，现在他说「现在」。
- **「离世」：** 原文这个词有解开的意思，可以用来指船解缆起航，也可以指拆下帐棚、拔营离开。保罗不把死看作终点，而看作启程。他在腓立比书说：「情愿离世与基督同在，因为这是好得无比的。」（腓1:23）

**2. 「那美好的仗我已经打过了」。** 「那美好的仗我已经打过了，当跑的路我已经跑尽了，所信的道我已经守住了。」（4:7）
- **三幅图画：** 打仗（或竞技场上的较力，参提前6:12）、赛跑、守住托付。原文三个动词都是完成时态，表示已经完成、结果仍在。
- **「当跑的路」：** 保罗多年前在米利都对以弗所长老说：「只要行完我的路程，成就我从主耶稣所领受的职事」（徒20:24）。现在他可以说，他已经跑完了。
- **「所信的道我已经守住了」：** 这呼应了第一章的「牢牢地守着」（1:14）。保罗把福音原原本本地守住了，现在把它交给提摩太。
- **这是夸口吗？** 保罗不是说自己完美无缺，他仍称自己是「罪魁」（提前1:15）。他是在说：靠着神的恩典，我没有半途而废。这句话是给提摩太的鼓励：你也可以跑到终点。

**3. 「有公义的冠冕为我存留」。** 「从此以后，有公义的冠冕为我存留，就是按着公义审判的主到了那日要赐给我的；不但赐给我，也赐给凡爱慕他显现的人。」（4:8）
- **「公义的冠冕」：** 有人认为是指由公义构成的冠冕，就是在那日被主宣告为义；也有人认为是指赐给过公义生活之人的奖赏。新约也讲到「生命的冠冕」（雅1:12；启2:10）、「荣耀冠冕」（彼前5:4）。
- **冠冕是赏赐，不是工价：** 保罗的义不是靠自己得的，而是「因信神而来的义」（腓3:9）。主赏赐冠冕，是他按着恩典，认可他自己在信徒身上所做成的工作。
- **「按着公义审判的主」：** 罗马的法官可能会不公正地判保罗死刑，但还有一位公义的审判者。保罗把自己的案子交给了他。
- **「凡爱慕他显现的人」：** 冠冕不只是给使徒的，也给每一个盼望、爱慕主再来的人。爱慕主显现，就是爱主自己，盼望见他的面。

> 要点：保罗面对死亡，不是恐惧，而是看作献给神的奠祭、一次启程。他靠着恩典跑完了当跑的路，那公义的冠冕也为一切爱慕主显现的人存留。

## 深读 4:9-15 来的时候要带的人和东西

**1. 「底马贪爱现今的世界」。** 「你要赶紧地到我这里来。因为底马贪爱现今的世界，就离弃我往帖撒罗尼迦去了；革勒士往加拉太去；提多往挞马太去；独有路加在我这里。」（4:9-11上）
- **底马：** 他从前是保罗的同工，保罗第一次在罗马坐监时，他也在保罗身边（西4:14；门24）。现在他却「贪爱现今的世界」，离开了。我们不知道他具体为了什么：也许是怕死，也许是放不下舒适的生活。这是一个警告：曾经和使徒同工的人，也可能离开。
- **对照：** 第8节说「凡爱慕他显现的人」，第10节说底马「贪爱现今的世界」。原文这两处用的是同一个动词。人心总有所爱，问题是爱什么。
- **革勒士和提多：** 他们不是离弃保罗，而是被派往别处事奉。不是每一个离开的人都是背弃。
- **「独有路加在我这里」：** 路加是「所亲爱的医生」（西4:14），一直陪着保罗。在最孤单的时候，至少还有一个人在身边。

**2. 「要把马可带来」。** 「你来的时候，要把马可带来，因为他在传道的事上于我有益处。我已经打发推基古往以弗所去。」（4:11下-12）
- **马可的故事：** 多年前，马可在第一次旅行布道途中离开了保罗和巴拿巴（徒13:13）。后来巴拿巴要带他同去，「但保罗因为马可从前在旁非利亚离开他们……就以为不可带他去。于是二人起了争论，甚至彼此分开」（徒15:38-39）。
- **后来的和好：** 后来马可在保罗第一次坐监时陪在他身边（西4:10；门24），彼得也称他为「我儿子马可」（彼前5:13）。如今保罗特别要他来，说他「于我有益处」。曾经被保罗认为不可带的人，成了保罗临终时想见的人。这叫我们看见，神能改变人，人与人之间也能和好。
- **推基古：** 他是常替保罗送信的人（弗6:21；西4:7）。有人认为，这封信可能就是由他带到以弗所的。

**3. 外衣、书和皮卷。** 「我在特罗亚留于加布的那件外衣，你来的时候可以带来，那些书也要带来，更要紧的是那些皮卷。」（4:13）
- **外衣：** 这是一件厚重的斗篷式外衣。冬天快到了（21节），罗马的牢房又冷又湿。使徒保罗需要一件外衣，这很真实。属灵的伟人也是血肉之躯，也会冷。
- **书和皮卷：** 「书」原文可能指莎草纸的书卷；「皮卷」是用兽皮制成、比较贵重的书写材料。我们不知道里面是什么。有人认为是旧约圣经的抄本，有人认为是保罗自己的笔记或文件。无论如何，保罗到了生命的最后，仍然想要读书。
- **平常的需要：** 这节经文提醒我们：圣经不只记载伟大的属灵真理，也记载一个人冬天需要一件外衣。神关心我们身体的需要，也不嫌弃我们的平常。

**4. 「铜匠亚历山大多多地害我」。** 「铜匠亚历山大多多地害我；主必照他所行的报应他。你也要防备他，因为他极力敌挡了我们的话。」（4:14-15）
- **他是谁？** 提摩太前书提到一个被保罗「交给撒但」的亚历山大（提前1:20），使徒行传也提到以弗所有一个亚历山大（徒19:33）。他们可能是同一个人，也可能不是；这个名字在当时很常见。
- **「主必照他所行的报应他」：** 这句话接近诗篇所说的「你照着各人所行的报应他」（诗62:12）。保罗没有自己报复，而是把审判交给主（参罗12:19）。
- **「你也要防备他」：** 把审判交给主，不等于对危险的人毫无防备。保罗清楚地提醒提摩太要小心这个人。今天也是这样：饶恕一个伤害过我们的人，并不等于要继续让他接近我们或别人；提醒别人防备一个危险的人，也不是不饶恕。

> 要点：在保罗身边，有离开的底马，有忠心的路加，也有曾经离开、后来又成为有益之人的马可。神能挽回人，也能使人和好；他也关心他仆人身上的冷，和手边的书。

## 深读 4:16-22 惟有主站在我旁边

**1. 「没有人前来帮助，竟都离弃我」。** 「我初次申诉，没有人前来帮助，竟都离弃我；但愿这罪不归与他们。」（4:16）
- **「初次申诉」：** 许多学者认为，这是指保罗这一次受审时的初步聆讯；也有人（如优西比乌）认为是指他第一次在罗马坐监时的受审。无论如何，在保罗最需要有人站在他身边作证、辩护的时候，没有一个人来。
- **「但愿这罪不归与他们」：** 这叫人想起司提反临死时的祷告：「主啊，不要将这罪归于他们！」（徒7:60）也叫人想起主耶稣。保罗没有怨恨那些因为害怕而没有来的人，他把他们交给主的怜悯。
- **这和饶恕伤害人的人不完全一样：** 这里说的是朋友在危险中退缩，不是有人加害。饶恕亏负我们的人，不等于要否认那是错，也不等于受伤害的人要回到危险里去。

**2. 「惟有主站在我旁边，加给我力量」。** 「惟有主站在我旁边，加给我力量，使福音被我尽都传明，叫外邦人都听见；我也从狮子口里被救出来。」（4:17）
- **「站在我旁边」：** 多年前，保罗在耶路撒冷被捕后，「当夜，主站在保罗旁边，说：『放心吧！……也必怎样在罗马为我作见证。』」（徒23:11）现在他真的在罗马为主作见证了，主又一次站在他旁边。
- **「使福音被我尽都传明」：** 保罗在罗马的法庭上为自己申诉，却把这当作传福音的机会。在帝国的中心，在官长面前，福音被传开了。这正应验了主当初对他说的话：要在外邦人和君王面前作见证（徒9:15）。
- **「从狮子口里被救出来」：** 这很可能是一个比喻，指死亡的危险，不一定是真的狮子。诗篇说：「救我脱离狮子的口」（诗22:21），但以理也从狮子坑中被救（但6:22）。保罗这一次没有被判死刑。

**3. 「主必救我脱离诸般的凶恶，也必救我进他的天国」。** 「主必救我脱离诸般的凶恶，也必救我进他的天国。愿荣耀归给他，直到永永远远。阿们。」（4:18）
- **怎样的「救」？** 保罗刚刚说自己离世的时候到了（6节），所以他不是说主会救他免于一死。他的意思是：主必保守他，不让任何凶恶使他离开主，最后把他平安带进天国。
- **「愿荣耀归给他」：** 这里的「主」很可能指主耶稣（参1节「他的国度」）。保罗把只归给神的荣耀归给基督，这表明他相信基督就是神。
- **在死亡面前的颂赞：** 保罗的最后一封信，在讲完自己被离弃以后，不是以怨叹结束，而是以颂赞结束。

**4. 最后的问安。** 「问百基拉、亚居拉，和阿尼色弗一家的人安。以拉都在哥林多住下了。特罗非摩病了，我就留他在米利都。你要赶紧在冬天以前到我这里来。有友布罗、布田、利奴、革老底亚，和众弟兄都问你安。愿主与你的灵同在！愿恩惠常与你们同在！」（4:19-22）
- **百基拉、亚居拉：** 这对夫妇是保罗多年的同工（徒18:2；罗16:3）。保罗先提妻子的名字，这在新约中好几次出现。
- **「特罗非摩病了」：** 保罗曾经行过许多神迹，却把生病的同工留在米利都。神迹医治不是使徒可以随意使用的能力，而是照着神的旨意。在今天是否仍有医治的恩赐，持终止论和延续论的信徒有不同的看法；但双方都同意，神不一定每一次都医治，而他的恩典够我们用（林后12:9）。
- **「在冬天以前」：** 冬天地中海停航，如果提摩太来晚了，就要等到春天。他赶到了没有？圣经没有告诉我们。
- **利奴：** 按照爱任纽的记载（《驳异端》3.3.3），使徒以后罗马教会的第一位监督名叫利奴，他认为就是这里提到的人。
- **「你的灵」和「你们」：** 「愿主与你的灵同在」是单数，是给提摩太的；「愿恩惠常与你们同在」是复数，是给整个教会的。保罗留下的最后一句话，是恩惠。

> 要点：在所有人都离开的时候，主仍然站在保罗旁边。他加给保罗力量，使福音传开，也必把他平安带进天国。保罗最后留下的话，不是怨言，而是颂赞和恩惠。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我初次申诉，没有人前来帮助，竟都离弃我；但愿这罪不归与他们。惟有主站在我旁边，加给我力量。」（4:16-17）我也有过这样的时候：在最需要人的那一刻，身边一个人也没有。也许是在医院等一个检查结果，也许是在法庭上，也许是在一场没有人来的丧礼上，也许是我被冤枉、被调查的时候，平时说会支持我的人一个也没有出现。事后，我心里有一个很深的伤口，也有一份说不出口的怨。保罗没有假装这件事不痛，他说「竟都离弃我」。可是他接着说：「但愿这罪不归与他们。」他能这样说，是因为他知道那天法庭里并不是只有他一个人。我想起主耶稣。在客西马尼园，「门徒都离开他，逃走了」（可14:50）。他早就知道：「你们要分散……留下我独自一人；其实我不是独自一人，因为有父与我同在。」（约16:32）可是到了十字架上，他喊着说：「我的神！我的神！为什么离弃我？」（太27:46）他在那里真正被离弃了，担当了我的罪，好叫我这个罪人永远不会被神离弃。所以那一天我以为自己独自站着的时候，其实也有一位站在我旁边。我也可以先承认：有时候我也是那个没有去的人。今天，我可以把那个伤口带到主面前，不必假装已经不痛；我也可以求他帮助我，慢慢地把那些没有来的人交给他。如果那件事里有人真的伤害了我，我不必回到危险里，也可以寻求帮助。然后我可以安静地对他说：主啊，那一天你站在我旁边；直到我离世的时候，你也必站在我旁边。愿主与我的灵同在。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 falls into four parts. **Preach the word** (vv. 1–5): Paul charges Timothy in the presence of God and of Christ Jesus, who is to judge the living and the dead: preach the word in season and out of season, for the time is coming when people will not endure sound teaching, but having itching ears will accumulate teachers for themselves; Timothy must be sober-minded, endure suffering, do the work of an evangelist and fulfill his ministry. **I have fought the good fight** (vv. 6–8): Paul is already being poured out as a drink offering, and the time of his departure has come; he has fought the good fight, finished the race and kept the faith; the crown of righteousness is laid up for him, and for all who have loved the Lord's appearing. **People and things to bring** (vv. 9–15): Demas, in love with this present world, has deserted Paul; Luke alone is with him; Timothy is to bring Mark, and also the cloak, the books and the parchments; beware of Alexander the coppersmith. **But the Lord stood by me** (vv. 16–22): at Paul's first defense no one stood by him, but the Lord stood by him and strengthened him; the Lord will bring him safely into his heavenly kingdom; final greetings and blessing.

This chapter contains Paul's last words. First he solemnly hands the work of preaching to Timothy; then he looks back over his life; finally he speaks of the people around him and of some very ordinary needs: a cloak, a few books. In the middle of it all comes the deepest sentence of the chapter: "But the Lord stood by me" (v. 17).

## Going Deeper: 4:1–5 Preach the Word

**1. "In the presence of... Christ Jesus, who is to judge the living and the dead."** "I charge you in the presence of God and of Christ Jesus, who is to judge the living and the dead, and by his appearing and his kingdom" (4:1).
- **A solemn charge:** this is almost an oath. Paul calls God and Christ as witnesses, because the charge that follows is so important.
- **"Judge the living and the dead":** in Cornelius's house Peter also said that Jesus "is the one appointed by God to be judge of the living and the dead" (Acts 10:42). The Apostles' Creed later confessed the same. Preachers must remember that the one who finally judges them is Christ.
- **"His appearing and his kingdom":** Paul turns Timothy's eyes to the future: Christ will appear, and his kingdom will fully come. Preaching goes on in this hope.

**2. "Preach the word."** "Preach the word; be ready in season and out of season; reprove, rebuke, and exhort, with complete patience and teaching" (4:2).
- **"Preach":** the word means to proclaim as a herald does. The preacher is not offering his own opinions but announcing the King's word.
- **"In season and out of season":** whether convenient or not, whether people want to hear or not, whether the preacher feels like it or not. Some think it refers mainly to the hearers: preach when people welcome it and when they do not. Others think it also refers to the preacher himself.
- **"Reprove, rebuke, and exhort":** preaching includes exposing sin, and also comforting and encouraging. Rebuke without exhortation discourages people; exhortation without rebuke leaves them comfortable in sin.
- **"With complete patience and teaching":** preaching does not bear fruit all at once; it takes patience, and it takes clear teaching.

**3. "Itching ears."** "For the time is coming when people will not endure sound teaching, but having itching ears they will accumulate for themselves teachers to suit their own passions, and will turn away from listening to the truth and wander off into myths" (4:3–4).
- **"Itching ears":** a desire to hear what is new, pleasant and to one's taste. People do not stop wanting to hear preaching; they want to hear only the preaching they like.
- **"Accumulate for themselves teachers":** people will collect many teachers, the more the better, so long as they say what suits them. Today we can choose any sermon online, and algorithms keep feeding us what we like to hear. It is easy to listen only to speakers who make us comfortable, and to avoid the passages that unsettle us.
- **"Myths":** the word means myths or legends (see 1 Tim 1:4; 4:7). Those who turn away from the truth do not stop believing anything; they turn to stories that are more pleasant to hear.

**4. "As for you, always be sober-minded."** "As for you, always be sober-minded, endure suffering, do the work of an evangelist, fulfill your ministry" (4:5).
- **"As for you":** Timothy cannot control what others do; what he can do is faithfully do his own work.
- **"Sober-minded":** the word means clear-headed, like someone who is not drunk.
- **"The work of an evangelist":** that is, preaching the gospel (see Acts 21:8, where Philip is called "the evangelist"). Timothy shepherds and teaches in the church, but must not forget to bring the gospel to those who do not yet believe.
- **"Fulfill your ministry":** carry out fully the work God has given you. In the next verse Paul says he himself has finished his race.

> The point: The preacher receives his charge in the presence of Christ, the judge of the living and the dead. People will have itching ears and want to hear only pleasant things, but the preacher must faithfully proclaim God's word in season and out of season, because in the end he gives account to Christ.

## Going Deeper: 4:6–8 I Have Fought the Good Fight

**1. "I am already being poured out as a drink offering."** "For I am already being poured out as a drink offering, and the time of my departure has come" (4:6).
- **"Drink offering":** in Old Testament worship, wine was sometimes poured out on or beside the sacrifice as a drink offering (Num 28:7). Paul sees his coming death as a drink offering poured out to God. He used the same picture in Philippians: "Even if I am to be poured out as a drink offering... I am glad" (Phil 2:17). There he said "if"; now he says "already."
- **"Departure":** the Greek word has the sense of loosing, and could be used for a ship casting off or for taking down a tent and breaking camp. Paul does not see death as the end, but as setting out. He wrote in Philippians, "My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better" (Phil 1:23).

**2. "I have fought the good fight."** "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith" (4:7).
- **Three pictures:** a fight (or a contest in the arena, see 1 Tim 6:12), a race, and keeping a trust. All three Greek verbs are in the perfect tense: completed, with lasting results.
- **"Finished the race":** years earlier, at Miletus, Paul told the Ephesian elders, "if only I may finish my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus" (Acts 20:24). Now he can say that he has finished it.
- **"I have kept the faith":** this echoes "guard the good deposit" in chapter 1 (1:14). Paul has guarded the gospel intact, and now hands it to Timothy.
- **Is this boasting?** Paul does not claim to be flawless; he still calls himself the "foremost" of sinners (1 Tim 1:15). He is saying that by God's grace he did not give up halfway. These words are an encouragement to Timothy: you too can run to the finish.

**3. "There is laid up for me the crown of righteousness."** "Henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will award to me on that Day, and not only to me but also to all who have loved his appearing" (4:8).
- **"The crown of righteousness":** some take it as the crown that consists of righteousness, being declared righteous by the Lord on that Day. Others take it as the reward given to those who have lived righteously. The New Testament also speaks of "the crown of life" (Jas 1:12; Rev 2:10) and "the unfading crown of glory" (1 Pet 5:4).
- **A crown is a gift, not wages:** Paul's righteousness was not his own achievement but "the righteousness from God that depends on faith" (Phil 3:9). When the Lord gives the crown, he is graciously honoring the work he himself has done in his people.
- **"The Lord, the righteous judge":** a Roman judge might unjustly sentence Paul to death, but there is a righteous Judge. Paul has committed his case to him.
- **"All who have loved his appearing":** the crown is not only for apostles but for every believer who longs for and loves the Lord's return. To love his appearing is to love the Lord himself and to long to see his face.

> The point: Facing death, Paul does not fear; he sees it as a drink offering poured out to God and as a setting out. By grace he has finished his race, and the crown of righteousness is laid up for all who love the Lord's appearing.

## Going Deeper: 4:9–15 People and Things to Bring

**1. "Demas, in love with this present world."** "Do your best to come to me soon. For Demas, in love with this present world, has deserted me and gone to Thessalonica. Crescens has gone to Galatia, Titus to Dalmatia. Luke alone is with me" (4:9–11a).
- **Demas:** he had been Paul's coworker and was with him during his first Roman imprisonment (Col 4:14; Phlm 24). Now, "in love with this present world," he has left. We do not know exactly why: perhaps fear of death, perhaps unwillingness to give up a comfortable life. It is a warning: even one who worked alongside an apostle can leave.
- **A contrast:** verse 8 speaks of "all who have loved his appearing"; verse 10 says Demas was "in love with this present world." In Greek it is the same verb. The heart always loves something; the question is what.
- **Crescens and Titus:** they had not deserted Paul but had been sent to serve elsewhere. Not everyone who leaves is a deserter.
- **"Luke alone is with me":** Luke, "the beloved physician" (Col 4:14), stayed with Paul. At his loneliest, there was still one person beside him.

**2. "Get Mark and bring him with you."** "Get Mark and bring him with you, for he is very useful to me for ministry. Tychicus I have sent to Ephesus" (4:11b–12).
- **Mark's story:** years before, Mark had left Paul and Barnabas partway through the first missionary journey (Acts 13:13). Later Barnabas wanted to take him again, "But Paul thought best not to take with them one who had withdrawn from them in Pamphylia... And there arose a sharp disagreement, so that they separated from each other" (Acts 15:38–39).
- **Reconciliation later:** later Mark was with Paul during his first imprisonment (Col 4:10; Phlm 24), and Peter calls him "Mark, my son" (1 Pet 5:13). Now Paul asks for him in particular, saying he is "very useful to me." The man Paul once thought should not come became the man Paul wanted near him at the end. Here we see that God can change people, and that people can be reconciled.
- **Tychicus:** he often carried Paul's letters (Eph 6:21; Col 4:7). Some think he may have carried this letter to Ephesus.

**3. The cloak, the books and the parchments.** "When you come, bring the cloak that I left with Carpus at Troas, also the books, and above all the parchments" (4:13).
- **The cloak:** a heavy, cape-like outer garment. Winter was coming (v. 21), and a Roman cell was cold and damp. The apostle Paul needed a coat. That is very real. Great saints are flesh and blood too; they also get cold.
- **The books and parchments:** "books" probably means papyrus scrolls; "parchments" were more costly writing materials made from animal skin. We do not know what was in them. Some think they were copies of the Old Testament; others think they were Paul's own notes or documents. Either way, at the end of his life Paul still wanted to read.
- **Ordinary needs:** this verse reminds us that Scripture records not only great spiritual truths but also a man's need for a coat in winter. God cares about our bodily needs, and he does not despise our ordinariness.

**4. "Alexander the coppersmith did me great harm."** "Alexander the coppersmith did me great harm; the Lord will repay him according to his deeds. Beware of him yourself, for he strongly opposed our message" (4:14–15).
- **Who was he?** First Timothy mentions an Alexander whom Paul "handed over to Satan" (1 Tim 1:20), and Acts mentions an Alexander in Ephesus (Acts 19:33). They may be the same person or not; the name was common.
- **"The Lord will repay him according to his deeds":** this is close to the psalm: "you will render to a man according to his work" (Ps 62:12). Paul does not take revenge himself but leaves judgment to the Lord (see Rom 12:19).
- **"Beware of him yourself":** leaving judgment to the Lord does not mean being unguarded against a dangerous person. Paul plainly warns Timothy to be careful of this man. The same is true today: forgiving someone who has harmed us does not mean continuing to let him near us or others, and warning others about a dangerous person is not unforgiveness.

> The point: Around Paul were Demas, who left; Luke, who stayed faithful; and Mark, who once left and later became useful again. God can win people back and reconcile them; and he cares about the cold his servant feels and the books he wants at hand.

## Going Deeper: 4:16–22 But the Lord Stood by Me

**1. "No one came to stand by me, but all deserted me."** "At my first defense no one came to stand by me, but all deserted me. May it not be charged against them!" (4:16).
- **"My first defense":** many scholars think this was the preliminary hearing in Paul's present trial; some (such as Eusebius) think it refers to his trial during his first Roman imprisonment. Either way, when Paul most needed someone to stand beside him as a witness or advocate, no one came.
- **"May it not be charged against them!":** this recalls Stephen's dying prayer, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them" (Acts 7:60), and it recalls the Lord Jesus. Paul does not hold a grudge against those who stayed away out of fear; he commits them to the Lord's mercy.
- **This is not quite the same as forgiving someone who did harm:** this is about friends who drew back in danger, not someone who attacked him. Forgiving those who have wronged us does not mean pretending it was not wrong, nor does it mean that someone who was harmed must return to danger.

**2. "But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me."** "But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me, so that through me the message might be fully proclaimed and all the Gentiles might hear it. So I was rescued from the lion's mouth" (4:17).
- **"Stood by me":** years earlier, after Paul's arrest in Jerusalem, "The following night the Lord stood by him and said, 'Take courage, for as you have testified to the facts about me in Jerusalem, so you must testify also in Rome'" (Acts 23:11). Now Paul is indeed testifying in Rome, and the Lord stands by him again.
- **"That through me the message might be fully proclaimed":** Paul made his defense before a Roman court and used it as an opportunity to proclaim the gospel. At the center of the empire, before its officials, the gospel was proclaimed. This fulfilled what the Lord had said at the beginning: Paul would carry his name before the Gentiles and kings (Acts 9:15).
- **"Rescued from the lion's mouth":** most likely a figure of speech for mortal danger, not a literal lion. The psalmist prays, "Save me from the mouth of the lion!" (Ps 22:21), and Daniel was rescued from the lions' den (Dan 6:22). This time Paul was not sentenced to death.

**3. "The Lord will rescue me from every evil deed and bring me safely into his heavenly kingdom."** "The Lord will rescue me from every evil deed and bring me safely into his heavenly kingdom. To him be the glory forever and ever. Amen" (4:18).
- **What kind of rescue?** Paul has just said that the time of his departure has come (v. 6), so he does not mean the Lord will spare him from death. He means that the Lord will keep him, will not let any evil separate him from the Lord, and will finally bring him safely into the kingdom.
- **"To him be the glory":** "the Lord" here most likely means the Lord Jesus (see v. 1, "his kingdom"). Paul gives to Christ the glory that belongs to God alone, which shows that he believed Christ is God.
- **Praise in the face of death:** Paul's last letter, after speaking of being deserted, does not end in complaint but in praise.

**4. Final greetings.** "Greet Prisca and Aquila, and the household of Onesiphorus. Erastus remained at Corinth, and I left Trophimus, who was ill, at Miletus. Do your best to come before winter. Eubulus sends greetings to you, as do Pudens and Linus and Claudia and all the brothers. The Lord be with your spirit. Grace be with you" (4:19–22).
- **Prisca and Aquila:** this couple had been Paul's coworkers for many years (Acts 18:2; Rom 16:3). Paul names the wife first, as the New Testament does several times.
- **"I left Trophimus, who was ill":** Paul had done many miracles, yet he left a sick coworker at Miletus. Miraculous healing was not a power the apostles could use at will; it depended on God's will. Cessationist and continuationist believers differ on whether gifts of healing continue today; but both agree that God does not always heal, and that his grace is sufficient (2 Cor 12:9).
- **"Before winter":** in winter, shipping on the Mediterranean stopped. If Timothy came late, he would have to wait until spring. Did he arrive in time? Scripture does not tell us.
- **Linus:** according to Irenaeus (*Against Heresies* 3.3.3), the first overseer of the church in Rome after the apostles was named Linus, and Irenaeus identifies him with the Linus mentioned here.
- **"Your spirit" and "you":** "The Lord be with your spirit" is singular, for Timothy; "Grace be with you" is plural, for the whole church. Paul's last word is grace.

> The point: When everyone else had left, the Lord still stood by Paul. He strengthened Paul so that the gospel was proclaimed, and he will bring him safely into his kingdom. Paul's last words are not complaint, but praise and grace.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "At my first defense no one came to stand by me, but all deserted me. May it not be charged against them! But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me" (4:16–17). I have known times like this too: at the moment I most needed someone, no one was there. Perhaps it was waiting in a hospital for a test result; perhaps it was in a courtroom; perhaps at a funeral where no one came; perhaps when I was falsely accused or under investigation, and not one of those who said they would support me showed up. Afterward I carried a deep wound, and a resentment I could not quite put into words. Paul does not pretend it did not hurt: "all deserted me." But then he says, "May it not be charged against them!" He can say this because he knows he was not alone in that courtroom. I think of the Lord Jesus. In Gethsemane, "they all left him and fled" (Mark 14:50). He had known it would happen: "you will be scattered... and will leave me alone. Yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me" (John 16:32). But on the cross he cried out, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Matt 27:46). There he was truly forsaken, bearing my sin, so that I, a sinner, would never be forsaken by God. So on the day I thought I stood alone, there was One standing beside me. I can also admit that sometimes I have been the one who did not come. Today I can bring that wound to the Lord without pretending it no longer hurts, and ask him to help me, slowly, to hand over to him those who did not come. If someone in that situation truly harmed me, I need not go back into danger, and I can seek help. Then I can quietly say to him: Lord, on that day you stood by me; and until the day of my departure, you will stand by me. The Lord be with my spirit.
