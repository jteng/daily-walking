## 本章脉络

约翰三书只有一章，十五节，可以分成五段。第1-4节是问安：长老为该犹祷告，愿他凡事兴盛，并且为他按真理而行而喜乐。第5-8节称赞该犹接待出外传道的弟兄，又劝他继续帮助他们往前行，因为这样就是与他们一同为真理做工。第9-10节责备丢特腓：他好为首，不接待长老所差的人，还把接待弟兄的人赶出教会。第11-12节劝该犹效法善，不要效法恶，并且推荐低米丢。第13-15节是结语：长老盼望快快见他，并且按着姓名问候众位朋友。

这封信的中心，是两种人在教会里的两种心：一种人打开门，帮助别人为主的名往前行；一种人关上门，要自己在教会中居首位。

## 深读 1:1-4 按真理而行

**1. 「亲爱的该犹」。** 「作长老的写信给亲爱的该犹，就是我诚心所爱的。」（1:1）短短十五节里，长老四次称该犹为「亲爱的」（1:1、2、5、11）。这不是客套话。这位年老的长老，心里实在爱这位弟兄。约翰二书已经讲过，「诚心所爱的」原文是在真理中所爱的，可以只是说真心地爱，也可能是说他们的爱是因同一个真理而有的。

**2. 「凡事兴盛，身体健壮，正如你的灵魂兴盛一样」。** 「亲爱的兄弟啊，我愿你凡事兴盛，身体健壮，正如你的灵魂兴盛一样。」（1:2）导读已经讲过，这是当时书信常用的问安格式，不是成功神学的应许。这里要留意的是长老祷告的次序：他看见该犹的灵魂兴盛，所以也为他的身体和一切事祷告。
- **神关心整个人：** 长老没有说身体不重要。神造了我们的身体，主耶稣也医治人的身体。我们可以为自己和别人的健康、工作向神祈求。
- **灵魂的兴盛是最要紧的：** 一个人可以身体健壮、凡事顺利，灵魂却很贫穷（参启3:17）；也可以身体软弱，灵魂却很兴盛（林后4:16）。

**3. 「心里存的真理」。** 「有弟兄来证明你心里存的真理，正如你按真理而行，我就甚喜乐。」（1:3）和合本的「你心里存的真理」，原文直译是「你的真理」，意思是该犹对真理的忠心。弟兄们回来，向长老见证该犹怎样「按真理而行」。真理在他身上，不只是他所信的，也是他所行的。别人看得见。

**4. 「我的喜乐就没有比这个大的」。** 「我听见我的儿女们按真理而行，我的喜乐就没有比这个大的。」（1:4）长老称他们为「我的儿女们」，这可能表示该犹是他带领信主的，也可能是一位年老的牧者对他所牧养之人的称呼。
- **牧者的喜乐：** 保罗也说过同样的话：「我们的盼望和喜乐，并所夸的冠冕是什么呢？岂不是我们主耶稣来的时候、你们在他面前站立得住吗？」（帖前2:19）
- **喜乐在别人的生命里：** 长老的喜乐，不在于他自己有多少成就，而在于别人「按真理而行」。这和丢特腓正好相反：丢特腓的「喜乐」，是自己居首位。

> 要点：长老最大的喜乐，是看见别人在真理中行；当我们的喜乐在于别人跟从基督，而不在于自己被人看重，我们的心就在主的心意里。

## 深读 1:5-8 配得过神，帮助他们往前行

**1. 「凡你向作客旅之弟兄所行的都是忠心的」。** 「亲爱的兄弟啊，凡你向作客旅之弟兄所行的都是忠心的。」（1:5）「作客旅」原文的意思是「陌生人」：这些弟兄，该犹原本不认识。接待自己的朋友不难，接待陌生人却需要信心和爱心。圣经多次吩咐信徒要接待客旅：「不可忘记用爱心接待客旅；因为曾有接待客旅的，不知不觉就接待了天使。」（来13:2）亚伯拉罕在幔利橡树下跑去迎接三位陌生人，正是这样（创18:1-3）。

**2. 「在教会面前证明了你的爱」。** 「他们在教会面前证明了你的爱。」（1:6上）这些弟兄回到长老所在的教会，在聚会中报告该犹怎样接待他们。该犹在自己家里做的事，在别的教会里被人感恩记念。该犹的爱是看得见的，也就是约翰一书所说的，「在行为和诚实上」相爱（约一3:18）。

**3. 「你若配得过神，帮助他们往前行」。** 「你若配得过神，帮助他们往前行，这就好了。」（1:6下）「帮助他们往前行」原文是一个词（*propempō*），新约中常用来指教会为传道人送行，并且供给他们下一段路程所需要的（罗15:24；林前16:11；多3:13）。保罗吩咐提多给西纳和亚波罗送行，「叫他们没有缺乏」（多3:13）。
- **「配得过神」：** 这是供给的标准。我们怎样送传道人上路，就显出我们怎样看那差他们的神。不是随便给一点剩下的，而是以配得过神的方式供给他们。
- **不只是一顿饭：** 送行包括路费、食物，也可能包括为他们写荐信、为他们祷告。

**4. 「为主的名出外」。** 「因他们是为主的名出外，对于外邦人一无所取。」（1:7）这些弟兄出外，不是为了自己的名，而是为了「那名」，就是主耶稣的名。他们「对于外邦人一无所取」，这里的「外邦人」指的是不信主的人。他们不向那些听他们传福音的外邦人收钱，免得福音被人误会成一门生意（参林前9:12、18）。这不是说传道人不该接受供给；耶稣说「工人得工价是应当的」（路10:7）。只是这供给应当来自信徒。

**5. 「与他们一同为真理做工」。** 「所以我们应该接待这样的人，叫我们与他们一同为真理做工。」（1:8）这里的「接待」，原文的意思是扶持、支持。长老说「我们应该」，他把自己也放在里面。这里有两个重点：
- **每个信徒都可以参与：** 不能出外传道的人，可以接待、供给那些出外的人。耶稣说：「人因为先知的名接待先知，必得先知所得的赏赐。」（太10:41）在家里预备一张床、一顿饭的信徒，和讲台上的传道人，都是「一同做工的」。
- **为真理，也与真理同工：** 「为真理做工」也可以译作与真理同工。真理自己在世上运行，我们是和真理一同做工的人；这真理就是关于基督的福音，基督自己就是真理（约14:6）。

> 要点：接待为主名出外的人，不是小事，而是与他们一同为真理做工；我们能这样「配得过神」地供给人，是因为神先在他儿子里慷慨地供给了我们。

## 深读 1:9-10 那好为首的丢特腓

**1. 「好为首的丢特腓」。** 「我曾略略地写信给教会，但那在教会中好为首的丢特腓不接待我们。」（1:9）「好为首」原文是一个罕见的词（*philoprōteuō*），意思是喜爱居首位，新约中只出现在这里。这个词是由「爱」和「居首位」（*prōteuō*）合成的；保罗在西1:18说到基督「使他可以在凡事上居首位」，用的正是 *prōteuō*。约翰不是在引用保罗，但把两处放在一起读，就看见：只有基督配得在教会中居首位；丢特腓却喜爱自己居首位。「不接待」这个词（*epidechomai*），第10节也用来说他「不接待弟兄」。用在长老身上，意思大概是他不接受长老的信和长老的权柄。

**2. 他所做的四件事。** 「所以我若去，必要提说他所行的事，就是他用恶言妄论我们。还不以此为足，他自己不接待弟兄，有人愿意接待，他也禁止，并且将接待弟兄的人赶出教会。」（1:10）丢特腓的「好为首」，表现在四件事上：
- **用恶言妄论：** 「妄论」原文的意思是说空洞、无聊的话。他在背后说长老的坏话，破坏长老的名声。
- **自己不接待弟兄：** 长老所差的传道人来了，他不接待。
- **禁止别人接待：** 他不但自己不做，也不让别人做。
- **把接待弟兄的人赶出教会：** 他用自己在教会中的权力，把不听他的人赶出去。
一个人想在教会中居首位，往往就是这样：先说别人的坏话，然后控制谁可以进来、谁要出去。

**3. 「我若去，必要提说」。** 长老没有在信里和丢特腓争吵，也没有用同样的恶言回敬他。他说，他若去，就要「提说他所行的事」，就是当着教会把这件事摆出来。这是一个牧者处理问题的方式：不是在背后议论，而是公开、按着真理来处理（参太18:15-17；提前5:19-20）。长老没有说丢特腓传了假道理。不过第11节「行恶的未曾见过神」紧接着丢特腓的事说出来，是一个很重的警告：一个人若一直这样行，就要问他是否真认识神。

**4. 今天的丢特腓。** 教会中的丢特腓，不一定是牧师，也可能是一位长执、一位小组长、一位资深的会友，或是一位主持家庭教会多年的弟兄姊妹。若有领袖像丢特腓这样，在背后中伤人，控制谁能进来，把不同意他的人赶出教会，这就是一种属灵上的欺压。
- **受害的人没有错：** 被这样赶出来的人，不是因为他们的信心有问题。该犹和那些接待弟兄的人，正是长老所称赞的。
- **教会不要为了面子遮掩：** 这样的事，不可用家丑不可外扬的想法来掩盖。教会应当像长老一样，把事情公开地摆出来，按着真理处理；有必要时，也要寻求其他教会或宗派的帮助。若牵涉到侵害人身或钱财的罪行（包括性侵犯和伤害儿童），就应当报警，交给有关部门处理（罗13:4）。
- **受伤的人可以先求安全：** 被欺压的人不必为了「顺服」而留在危险中，也不必和一个仍然不安全的人和好或单独见面。
- **饶恕不等于恢复职分：** 欺压过别人的领袖若真心悔改，教会可以饶恕他、接纳他作弟兄，但饶恕并不恢复他原来的职分，也不让他再接近受害的人或孩子。
- **不是每一位有权柄的领袖都是丢特腓：** 长老自己也用他的权柄（1:10）。按着圣经、公开而有爱心地执行的教会纪律（太18:15-17），和丢特腓的控制不一样。忠心的牧者是我们应当敬重的（帖前5:12-13；彼前5:2-3）。

> 要点：只有基督配得在凡事上居首位；当我们想自己居首位，就会开始中伤人、控制人、把人赶出去；但那位真正居首位的，却用他的首位为我们舍命。

## 深读 1:11-12 效法善

**1. 「不要效法恶，只要效法善」。** 「亲爱的兄弟啊，不要效法恶，只要效法善。行善的属乎神；行恶的未曾见过神。」（1:11）该犹面前有两个榜样：丢特腓和低米丢。长老叫他效法善的那一个。
- **「效法」：** 原文就是我们常说的「模仿」。人会不知不觉地学身边的人。该犹若常看丢特腓的样子，也可能慢慢学会用权力来保护自己。
- **「行善的属乎神」：** 这和约翰一书的话一致：「凡不行义的就不属神」（约一3:10）。约翰的意思不是说行善可以叫人得救，而是说，从神生的人，他的生命会显出神的样子。
- **「行恶的未曾见过神」：** 这和约翰一书3:6的话很像：「凡犯罪的，是未曾看见他，也未曾认识他。」约翰说的，是一个人持续的生命方向，不是一时的跌倒（约一1:8-2:1）。

**2. 「低米丢行善」。** 「低米丢行善，有众人给他作见证，又有真理给他作见证，就是我们也给他作见证。你也知道我们的见证是真的。」（1:12）和合本译作「低米丢行善」，原文直译是低米丢有众人的好见证。他有三重见证：
- **众人的见证：** 认识他的人都说他好。
- **真理的见证：** 他的生命和真理相符，好像真理自己在为他作见证。
- **长老的见证：** 「我们也给他作见证。」
许多人认为，低米丢就是带这封信去给该犹的人，也可能是长老所差的另一位传道人。在丢特腓关上门的时候，长老请该犹打开门接待这个人。
- **他和以弗所的银匠底米丢是同一个人吗？** 使徒行传19:24提到以弗所有一个银匠叫底米丢，曾鼓动人反对保罗。有人猜测，他后来信了主，就是这里的低米丢。不过这只是猜测，没有证据；这名字在当时也很常见。

**3. 「你也知道我们的见证是真的」。** 这句话很像约翰福音的结尾：「我们也知道他的见证是真的。」（约21:24）约翰一生都在作见证，见证他所听见、看见的主（约一1:1-3）。现在他也为一个弟兄作见证。

> 要点：人会模仿他所注目的人；与其注目丢特腓，不如注目像低米丢这样的人，最终是注目那位我们所要效法的基督。

## 深读 1:13-15 按着姓名问众位朋友安

**1. 「当面谈论」。** 「我原有许多事要写给你，却不愿意用笔墨写给你，但盼望快快地见你，我们就当面谈论。」（1:13-14）这和约翰二书的结尾很相似（约二1:12）。长老盼望「快快地」去，一方面是要见该犹，另一方面也是要处理丢特腓的事（1:10）。

**2. 「愿你平安」。** 「愿你平安。」（1:15）这是犹太人常用的问安，也是复活的主向聚集的门徒说的第一句话：「愿你们平安！」（约20:19）在教会有纷争、有人被赶出去的时候，长老把基督的平安留给该犹。

**3. 「众位朋友」。** 「众位朋友都问你安。请你替我按着姓名问众位朋友安。」（1:15）长老称信徒为「朋友」。这让我们想起耶稣的话：「以后我不再称你们为仆人……我乃称你们为朋友。」（约15:15）
- **「按着姓名」：** 长老不是笼统地问候大家，而是请该犹一个一个地按名字问候。原文「按着姓名」和约翰福音10:3说好牧人「按着名」叫自己的羊，是同一个说法：「他按着名叫自己的羊。」（约10:3）
- **在一个有纷争的教会里：** 丢特腓把一些人赶出了教会，长老却请该犹按着名字问候每一位朋友。没有一个人被忘记。

> 要点：主耶稣称我们为朋友，并且按着名字认识我们；在一个有人被排挤的地方，按着姓名问候每一个人，就是活出他的心。

## 默想

**默想：** 「但那在教会中好为首的丢特腓不接待我们。」（1:9）读到丢特腓，我很容易想到别人：那个总要别人听他的同工，那个把小组当作自己地盘的组长，那个在会议上一定要最后拍板的长执。可是我也要问自己：我心里有没有一个丢特腓？那个由我开始的事工，我是不是不知不觉把它当成了「我的」？开会的时候，谁可以发言、谁的意见算数，是不是常常由我来定？新来的人提出一个不同的想法，我是欢迎他，还是让他觉得这里没有他的位置？在家里，是不是也总要我说了算？

这封信里有一个词，可以照亮这一切。丢特腓「好为首」，喜爱居首位；保罗说到基督的时候，用的正是「居首位」这个词：「使他可以在凡事上居首位。」（西1:18）教会中那个首位，早就有人坐了。那一位真正有资格居首位的，却没有用他的首位来控制人、排挤人。他「从死里首先复生」，是因为他先为我们死了；他藉着「十字架上所流的血」，叫万有与神和好（西1:20）。他居首位，是为了把我们这些曾经与神为敌的人接进来。

施洗约翰明白这一点。他的门徒看见众人都往耶稣那里去，就来向他诉说。约翰却说：「娶新妇的就是新郎；新郎的朋友站着，听见新郎的声音就甚喜乐。故此，我这喜乐满足了。他必兴旺，我必衰微。」（约3:29-30）我不是新郎，教会也不是我的新妇；那事工、那小组、那个家，都不是我的。我最多只是新郎的朋友。这不是一个叫人丧气的位置，而是一个满了喜乐的位置：我不必守住首位，不必证明自己比别人重要，因为首位已经属于那位爱我、为我舍己的主。

如果我已经像丢特腓一样，在背后说过人，或者把人挤到边上，我可以把这罪带到主面前；基督的血也洗净这样的罪。新郎的朋友不必站在新郎前面。他只是站着，听新郎的声音，就甚喜乐。我的喜乐不在于我居首位，而在于那位居首位的，是我的主。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Third John has one chapter of fifteen verses, and it falls into five parts. Verses 1–4 are the greeting: the elder prays that all may go well with Gaius, and he rejoices that Gaius is walking in the truth. Verses 5–8 commend Gaius for receiving the brothers who travel out to preach, and urge him to keep sending them on their way, because in this way he becomes their fellow worker for the truth. Verses 9–10 rebuke Diotrephes: he likes to put himself first, refuses those the elder sends, and even puts those who welcome the brothers out of the church. Verses 11–12 urge Gaius to imitate good rather than evil, and commend Demetrius. Verses 13–15 close the letter: the elder hopes to see Gaius soon and greets the friends by name.

At the heart of the letter are two kinds of people in the church with two kinds of heart. One opens the door and helps others go on their way for the sake of the name. The other shuts the door because he wants to be first in the church himself.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–4 Walking in the Truth

**1. "The beloved Gaius."** "The elder to the beloved Gaius, whom I love in truth." (1:1) In fifteen short verses the elder calls Gaius "beloved" four times (1:1, 2, 5, 11). This is not mere politeness. The aged elder truly loves this brother. As in 2 John, "in truth" may mean simply "sincerely," or a love that exists because of a shared truth.

**2. "That all may go well with you and that you may be in good health, as it goes well with your soul."** (1:2) As the introduction explained, this is a common letter greeting of the time, not a promise of prosperity. What we should notice here is the order of the elder's prayer: he sees that Gaius's soul is doing well, and so he also prays for his body and all his affairs.
- **God cares for the whole person:** The elder does not say the body is unimportant. God made our bodies, and the Lord Jesus healed people's bodies. We can ask God for health and work for ourselves and others.
- **The health of the soul matters most:** A person can be healthy and prosperous and yet poor in soul (see Rev 3:17); a person can be weak in body and yet thriving in soul (2 Cor 4:16).

**3. "Testified to your truth."** "For I rejoiced greatly when the brothers came and testified to your truth, as indeed you are walking in the truth." (1:3) "Your truth" means Gaius's faithfulness to the truth. The brothers came back and testified to the elder how Gaius was "walking in the truth." In him the truth was not only something he believed but something he did, and others could see it.

**4. "I have no greater joy."** "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth." (1:4) "My children" may mean that the elder led Gaius to faith, or it may be how an aged pastor speaks of those in his care.
- **A pastor's joy:** Paul said the same kind of thing: "For what is our hope or joy or crown of boasting before our Lord Jesus at his coming? Is it not you?" (1 Thess 2:19)
- **Joy in other people's lives:** The elder's joy is not in his own achievements but in others "walking in the truth." Diotrephes is the opposite: his "joy" is in being first himself.

> The point: The elder's greatest joy is seeing others walk in the truth; when our joy is in others following Christ rather than in being thought important ourselves, our hearts share the Lord's purpose.

## Going Deeper: 1:5–8 In a Manner Worthy of God

**1. "Beloved, it is a faithful thing you do in all your efforts for these brothers, strangers as they are."** (1:5) These brothers were strangers whom Gaius had not known before. Welcoming one's own friends is easy; welcoming strangers takes faith and love. Scripture often tells believers to welcome strangers: "Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for thereby some have entertained angels unawares" (Heb 13:2). Abraham did just this when he ran from his tent by the oaks of Mamre to meet three strangers (Gen 18:1–3).

**2. "Who testified to your love before the church."** (1:6a) The brothers went back to the elder's church and told the gathering how Gaius had welcomed them. What Gaius did in his own home was remembered with thanks in another church. His love could be seen. It was love "in deed and in truth," as 1 John says (1 John 3:18).

**3. "You will do well to send them on their journey in a manner worthy of God."** (1:6b) "Send them on their journey" is one Greek word (*propempō*). The New Testament often uses it for a church seeing preachers off and providing what they need for the next stage of the journey (Rom 15:24; 1 Cor 16:11; Titus 3:13). Paul tells Titus to speed Zenas and Apollos on their way and "see that they lack nothing" (Titus 3:13).
- **"In a manner worthy of God":** This is the standard for support. How we send preachers on their way shows how we regard the God who sent them. We do not give them whatever is left over; we provide for them in a way worthy of God.
- **More than a meal:** Sending them on their way included travel money and food, and might include writing letters of commendation and praying for them.

**4. "For the sake of the name."** "For they have gone out for the sake of the name, accepting nothing from the Gentiles." (1:7) These brothers went out, not for their own name, but for "the name," the name of the Lord Jesus. They accepted "nothing from the Gentiles," meaning unbelievers. They took no money from the pagans who heard them preach, so that the gospel would not be mistaken for a business (see 1 Cor 9:12, 18). This does not mean preachers should not be supported; Jesus said, "the laborer deserves his wages" (Luke 10:7). But that support should come from believers.

**5. "That we may be fellow workers for the truth."** "Therefore we ought to support people like these, that we may be fellow workers for the truth." (1:8) "Support" means to hold up, to back. The elder says "we ought," and includes himself. Two points stand out:
- **Every believer can take part:** Those who cannot go out to preach can receive and support those who do. Jesus said, "The one who receives a prophet because he is a prophet will receive a prophet's reward" (Matt 10:41). The believer who makes up a bed and cooks a meal and the preacher in the pulpit are both "fellow workers."
- **For the truth, and with the truth:** "Fellow workers for the truth" can also be translated "fellow workers with the truth." The truth itself is at work in the world, and we work alongside it; that truth is the gospel of Christ, who is himself the truth (John 14:6).

> The point: Receiving those who go out for the sake of the name is no small thing; it makes us fellow workers for the truth. We can support others "in a manner worthy of God" because God first provided for us generously in his Son.

## Going Deeper: 1:9–10 Diotrephes, Who Likes to Put Himself First

**1. "Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first."** "I have written something to the church, but Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first, does not acknowledge our authority." (1:9) "Likes to put himself first" is a rare Greek word (*philoprōteuō*), "to love being first," found only here in the New Testament. The word joins "love" to *prōteuō*, "to be first," the very verb Paul uses of Christ in Col 1:18: "that in everything he might be preeminent." John is not quoting Paul, but read side by side, the two show that only Christ is worthy to be first in the church; Diotrephes loves to be first himself. The verb (*epidechomai*, "receive, welcome") is the one used in verse 10 for refusing to welcome the brothers. Applied to the elder, it most likely means Diotrephes rejected his letter and his authority, which is how the ESV renders it.

**2. Four things he does.** "So if I come, I will bring up what he is doing, talking wicked nonsense against us. And not content with that, he refuses to welcome the brothers, and also stops those who want to and puts them out of the church." (1:10) Diotrephes's love of being first shows in four things:
- **Talking wicked nonsense:** The word means empty, idle talk. He speaks against the elder behind his back and damages his name.
- **Refusing to welcome the brothers:** When preachers sent by the elder arrive, he will not receive them.
- **Stopping others who want to:** He not only refuses himself; he will not let others do it.
- **Putting them out of the church:** He uses his power in the church to drive out those who will not obey him.
This is often what it looks like when someone wants to be first in the church: first speak ill of others, then control who may come in and who must go.

**3. "If I come, I will bring up what he is doing."** The elder does not quarrel with Diotrephes in the letter, and he does not answer wicked talk with wicked talk. He says that if he comes, he will "bring up what he is doing," that is, set the matter before the church. This is how a shepherd deals with a problem: not by talking behind someone's back, but openly and according to the truth (see Matt 18:15–17; 1 Tim 5:19–20). The elder does not say Diotrephes taught false doctrine. Yet verse 11, "whoever does evil has not seen God," follows directly on his case. It is a grave warning: someone who keeps on acting this way must ask whether he has truly known God.

**4. Diotrephes today.** A Diotrephes in the church need not be a pastor. He may be an elder or deacon, a small-group leader, a long-standing member, or a brother or sister who has led a house church for years. When a leader acts as Diotrephes did, slandering people behind their backs, controlling who may come in, and driving out those who disagree, that is spiritual abuse.
- **Those who are harmed are not at fault:** People driven out this way are not driven out because something is wrong with their faith. Gaius and those who welcomed the brothers are the very people the elder commends.
- **The church must not cover it up to save face:** Such things must not be hidden under "don't air the family's dirty laundry" (家丑不可外扬). Like the elder, the church should bring the matter into the open and deal with it according to the truth, and where needed seek help from other churches or from its denomination. Where there are crimes against persons or property (including sexual abuse and harm to children), they should be reported to the police and the proper authorities (Rom 13:4).
- **Those who are hurt may seek safety first:** People who have been abused need not stay in danger in the name of "submission," nor reconcile with or meet alone with someone who is still unsafe.
- **Forgiveness does not restore office:** If a leader who has abused others truly repents, the church can forgive him and receive him as a brother. But forgiveness does not restore his office or give him access again to those he harmed or to children.
- **Not every leader with authority is a Diotrephes:** The elder himself uses his authority (1:10). Church discipline carried out openly, by Scripture and in love (Matt 18:15–17), is not Diotrephes's control. Faithful shepherds are to be respected (1 Thess 5:12–13; 1 Pet 5:2–3).

> The point: Only Christ is worthy to be first in everything. When we want to be first ourselves, we begin to slander, to control, and to drive people out; but the one who truly holds first place used it to lay down his life for us.

## Going Deeper: 1:11–12 Imitate Good

**1. "Do not imitate evil but imitate good."** "Beloved, do not imitate evil but imitate good. Whoever does good is from God; whoever does evil has not seen God." (1:11) Gaius has two examples before him: Diotrephes and Demetrius. The elder tells him to imitate the good one.
- **"Imitate":** The Greek word gives us "mimic." People learn from those around them without noticing. If Gaius keeps watching Diotrephes, he too may slowly learn to use power to protect himself.
- **"Whoever does good is from God":** This fits 1 John: "whoever does not practice righteousness is not of God" (1 John 3:10). John does not mean that doing good saves a person. He means that the life of someone born of God will show God's likeness.
- **"Whoever does evil has not seen God":** This is very close to 1 John 3:6: "no one who keeps on sinning has either seen him or known him." John is speaking of the settled direction of a person's life, not a single fall (1 John 1:8–2:1).

**2. "Demetrius has received a good testimony."** "Demetrius has received a good testimony from everyone, and from the truth itself. We also add our testimony, and you know that our testimony is true." (1:12) The Chinese Union Version says "Demetrius does good"; the Greek says he has a good testimony from everyone. He has a threefold witness:
- **The witness of everyone:** All who know him speak well of him.
- **The witness of the truth:** His life fits the truth, as though the truth itself were testifying for him.
- **The witness of the elder:** "We also add our testimony."
Many take Demetrius to be the one carrying this letter to Gaius, or another preacher the elder was sending. While Diotrephes shuts the door, the elder asks Gaius to open his door to this man.
- **Is he the silversmith Demetrius of Ephesus?** Acts 19:24 mentions a silversmith in Ephesus named Demetrius who stirred people up against Paul. Some have guessed that he later came to faith and is the Demetrius here. But this is only a guess without evidence, and the name was common.

**3. "You know that our testimony is true."** This is very like the end of John's Gospel: "we know that his testimony is true" (John 21:24). John spent his life bearing witness to the Lord he had heard and seen (1 John 1:1–3). Now he bears witness to a brother.

> The point: People imitate those they keep their eyes on; rather than watching Diotrephes, watch people like Demetrius, and in the end fix your eyes on Christ, the one we are to imitate.

## Going Deeper: 1:13–15 Greet the Friends by Name

**1. "Talk face to face."** "I had much to write to you, but I would rather not write with pen and ink. I hope to see you soon, and we will talk face to face." (1:13–14) This is very like the end of 2 John (2 John 1:12). The elder hopes to come "soon," both to see Gaius and to deal with Diotrephes (1:10).

**2. "Peace be to you."** (1:15) This was the common Jewish greeting, and it was the first thing the risen Lord said to his gathered disciples: "Peace be with you" (John 20:19). In a church torn by conflict, where people have been put out, the elder leaves Gaius with Christ's peace.

**3. "The friends."** "The friends greet you. Greet the friends, every one of them." (1:15) The elder calls believers "friends." This recalls Jesus' words: "No longer do I call you servants... but I have called you friends" (John 15:15).
- **"Every one of them":** The Greek says "by name." The elder does not send a general greeting; he asks Gaius to greet each one by name. It is the same phrase used of the good shepherd: "he calls his own sheep by name" (John 10:3).
- **In a divided church:** Diotrephes has put some people out of the church, but the elder asks Gaius to greet every friend by name. No one is forgotten.

> The point: The Lord Jesus calls us friends and knows us by name; in a place where some have been pushed out, greeting each person by name is living out his heart.

## Meditation

**Meditation:** "But Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first, does not acknowledge our authority." (1:9) When I read about Diotrephes, I easily think of others: the co-worker who always needs everyone to listen to him, the small-group leader who treats the group as his territory, the church officer who must always have the final say. But I have to ask myself too: Is there a Diotrephes in my own heart? The ministry that began with me: have I quietly come to think of it as "mine"? In meetings, am I usually the one who decides who may speak and whose opinion counts? When a newcomer offers a different idea, do I welcome him, or let him feel there is no place for him here? At home, must I always be the one whose word goes?

One word in this letter sheds light on all of this. Diotrephes "likes to put himself first," he loves to be first. When Paul speaks of Christ, he uses that very verb, "to be first": "that in everything he might be preeminent" (Col 1:18). The first place in the church was taken long ago. And the one who truly has the right to be first did not use his first place to control people or push them out. He is "the firstborn from the dead" because he first died for us; he reconciled all things to God "by the blood of his cross" (Col 1:20). He holds first place in order to bring in people like us, who were once God's enemies.

John the Baptist understood this. When his disciples saw the crowds going to Jesus, they came and told him. But John said, "The one who has the bride is the bridegroom. The friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly at the bridegroom's voice. Therefore this joy of mine is now complete. He must increase, but I must decrease." (John 3:29–30) I am not the bridegroom, and the church is not my bride; the ministry, the small group, the home are not mine. At most I am the bridegroom's friend. This is not a discouraging place to stand but a place full of joy. I do not have to guard first place or prove I am more important than others, because first place already belongs to the Lord who loved me and gave himself for me.

If I have already acted like Diotrephes, speaking against people behind their backs or pushing someone to the edge, I can bring that sin to the Lord; the blood of Christ cleanses this sin too. The bridegroom's friend need not stand in front of the bridegroom. He simply stands and hears his voice, and rejoices greatly. My joy is not in being first, but in this: the one who is first is my Lord.
