## 08.md

### 1. should-fix: doxology ending, and the slain-Lamb-on-the-throne close repeats 06 (and 05)

**Location:** 默想 / Meditation, final two sentences.

> 在宝座前的金坛上，众圣徒的祈祷正和香一同上升；使那香烟蒙悦纳的，是曾被杀、如今站在宝座中间的羔羊。愿颂赞归给他，他使我们这些软弱的人可以来到神面前，他也使我们最小的祷告在天上被纪念。

> On the golden altar before the throne, the prayers of all the saints are rising with the incense, and what makes that smoke acceptable is the Lamb who was slain and now stands in the midst of the throne. Praise be to him, who brings weak people like us before God, and who makes even our smallest prayers remembered in heaven.

**Problem:** WORKFLOW (after 1 Peter) says doxology endings stay retired, and 「愿颂赞归给他……」 is a formal doxology. The image just before it, the slain Lamb who now stands in the midst of the throne, is also how 06 closes ("The Lamb who was slain rose from the dead and now stands at the center of the throne (5:6)"), and 05 already ends on the Lamb. The chapter has its own distinctive image, Christ as the fragrance that makes prayer acceptable at the golden altar (Eph 5:2, already in paragraph 3). Ending there, and on Auntie Li's actual sentence, keeps Christ at the center, fits the brief, and leaves the reader seeing her small prayer inside his offering instead of reading a formula.

**Suggested rewrite (replace the two sentences):**

zh:
> 在宝座前的金坛上，众圣徒的祈祷正和香一同上升。那香不是我们带去的，是神赐下的；那馨香的祭，是为我们舍了自己的基督。李阿姨那句结结巴巴的「主啊，求你看顾我妈的腿」，也在这烟里，和众圣徒的祈祷一同升到神面前。

en:
> On the golden altar before the throne, the prayers of all the saints are rising with the incense. We did not bring that incense; God gave it. The fragrant offering is Christ, who gave himself up for us. Auntie Li's halting "Lord, please look after my mother's legs" is in that smoke too, rising before God with the prayers of all the saints.

(If the drafter wants a note of adoration, put it before this, not as the closing line, and not as 「愿颂赞归给他」.)

### 2. consider: the chapter's own link between prayer and God's work in history never reaches Auntie Li

**Location:** 默想 / Meditation, paragraph 2 (after 「……是在同一个时辰献上的。」 / "...at the same hour as the incense.").

**Problem:** The Shape of the Chapter and the 8:3-6 point both say God's work in history is bound up with the saints' prayers (8:5-6). The meditation shows only that her prayers are *accepted*, not that they *matter*. Her quiet belief is "these few words of mine don't count for much". One sentence would let her, and the reader, delight that God gives such prayers a real place in his purposes. It should stay a gift and not become a duty ("so pray more").

**Suggested addition (end of paragraph 2):**

zh:
> 接下来，同一个香炉盛满了坛上的火，倒在地上，号声就响起来了（8:5-6）。神在历史中的作为，和这些祷告连在一起。李阿姨为楼下那位邻居说的几句话，不是可有可无的。神愿意让「愿你的国降临」的祷告，也从她那个角落升上去。

en:
> Then the same censer is filled with fire from the altar and thrown on the earth, and the trumpets begin (8:5–6). God's work in history is bound up with these prayers. The few words Auntie Li says for the widow downstairs are not an extra. God is pleased to let the prayer "your kingdom come" rise from her corner too.

### 3. consider: the list of three readers in paragraph 4 ends on a vague "these things"

**Location:** 默想 / Meditation, paragraph 4.

> 我们都可以把这些带到他面前。 / All of us can bring these things to him.

**Problem:** This line usefully speaks to the reader who has failed: the one who stopped praying, the cynic, and the proud fluent pray-er. But "these things" is abstract, so the grace does not land on any of them in particular. Naming the sins (despair, contempt, pride) makes the cleansing in the next sentence specific and honest.

**Suggested rewrite:**

zh:
> 不祷告的灰心，看不起别人的冷眼，暗暗自以为会祷告的骄傲，都可以原样带到他面前。

en:
> The discouragement that stopped praying, the contempt that sneers at others' prayers, the quiet pride of the one who prays well: all of it can be brought to him just as it is.

(Variety note, no change asked of 08: 22's scene also has a group prayer where one person cannot say the words while an older believer prays at once. The reasons differ, inability in 08 and unwillingness in 22, so they do not clash. Keep it in mind when 22 is reviewed. James 05 used "my prayers do not count"; 08's pivot on the altar, the blood and the given incense is different enough.)

### What works well in 08 (keep)
- Auntie Li is concrete, dignified and recognizable: the Wednesday prayer meeting, a factory life, her heart pounding, and very specific small prayers. The scene carries no hint of guilt or pressure. "Next time Auntie Li may still stay silent, or she may say just one sentence" is pastorally exactly right.
- Grace comes before any step. The pivot (the incense is given from God's side; even the incense altar needed atoning blood, Exod 30:10; Christ the fragrant offering) grounds acceptance wholly in Christ rather than eloquence, and it humbles the fluent and the silent alike.
- The Luke 1:10 people praying outside, unnamed, is a lovely and fresh touch.
- The 要点 lines are God-centered. The 8:5 section's "not revenge but leaving vengeance to God (Rom 12:19)" and the 8:13 "woe is also a lament" (Matt 23:37) keep judgment and compassion together.

## 09.md

### 1. consider: after exposing repentance as "paying off a vow", the meditation never returns to the kickback or the brother

**Location:** 默想 / Meditation, paragraph 3, after 「这颗新心的代价，不是我们付的。」 / "The price of that new heart is not one we pay."

**Problem:** The 还愿 diagnosis is the best thing in the chapter: it names the motive (bargaining) and not only the behavior. But the concrete sins in paragraph 1 (an illicit kickback and a brother he has not spoken to in years) then drop out completely, so a reader could take repentance to be wholly inward. One clause can show that the new heart does turn back to them, as the walk home of a forgiven man and not as payment. Keep it brief: restitution as the freedom of the forgiven is already Hebrews 09's and Zacchaeus is 2 Peter 02's, so do not build on either.

**Suggested addition (after that sentence):**

zh:
> 一个被神接回来的人，迟早也会去面对那笔回扣，拨通弟弟的电话；不过那是回家的人走的路，不是向神交的押金。

en:
> Someone God has received back will sooner or later face that kickback and make that call to his brother; but that is the road of someone coming home, not a deposit paid to God.

(Variety note: kickbacks also appear in 2 Peter 02's Lot meditation. Here they are one item among several and the theme is different, so no change of scenario is needed.)

### 2. consider: double ending (closing prayer, then a verse), and closing prayers are now frequent in Revelation 06-13

**Location:** 默想 / Meditation, last two paragraphs (「主耶稣，我承认……」 and 「那位为不肯回转的城哀哭的主……」).

**Problem:** 06 (Lord, I come out...), 11 (Lord, my witness...), 13 (Lord Jesus... Amen) and 09 all close with a short prayer. 09 also adds a final verse after its prayer, so the meditation ends twice. The prayer itself is good: it confesses, gives thanks for patience, and refuses to trade. Two options:
(a) keep the prayer and cut the final Mark 1:15 line, ending on 「……只求你接纳我回到你面前。」; or
(b) preferably for variety, drop the prayer and merge its confession into the final line, so that the chapter ends on Christ still calling, as the brief assigns.

**Suggested rewrite for (b) (replace both paragraphs):**

zh:
> 我也一次又一次地说过「以后再说」。可是那位为不肯回转的城哀哭的主，没有照我的刚硬待我；他今天还在呼召：「日期满了，神的国近了。你们当悔改，信福音！」（可1:15）

en:
> I too have said "later" again and again. Yet the Lord who wept over the city that would not turn has not treated me as my hardness deserves; today he is still calling: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel" (Mark 1:15).

### 3. consider: the zh and en differ at the climax of 1 Pet 2:24

**Location:** 默想 / Meditation, paragraph 3, final sentence.

> zh: 「……亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24），连那些一再说「以后再说」的人的罪也在其中。
> en: "...the sins of people who kept saying 'later,' mine among them."

**Problem:** The English makes it personal ("mine among them"), which is the point of the whole meditation. The Chinese stays third-person ("those people"), so the Chinese reader is left looking at Lao Zheng rather than at Christ bearing *my* sin.

**Suggested rewrite (zh only):**
> ……「被挂在木头上，亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24），连我这样一再说「以后再说」的人的罪，也在其中。

### What works well in 09 (keep)
- The scenario is honest, current and very Chinese (a business dinner, an ER scare, the hospital-bed bargaining, a brother he no longer speaks to). The reader's "isn't that me?" comes from the text and not from accusation.
- "His illness was not necessarily God's punishment" (Luke 13:4-5) protects sufferers from reading illness as 报应 while keeping the call to repent.
- The 还愿 / temple-vow diagnosis is gospel application to culture of a high order: repentance is not trading good works for safety, but coming back for a new heart that we cannot pay for (Ezek 36:26). Christ weeping over Jerusalem and then bearing sin outside it holds judgment and tenderness together.
- It speaks to the one who has failed more than once ("perhaps this has happened more than once"; "that you remember them today is itself a sign that God is still calling you"), and it ends on God's patience as mercy (2:21; Rom 2:4), as the brief asks.
- The 9:6 crisis-help line, the 9:4 sealing comfort ("evil spirits cannot take away the fact that they belong to God") and the 9:20-21 section's "only God's grace" with the witnesses of chs 10-11 are pastorally strong. The 要点 lines are Christ-centered ("Only Christ gives life").
