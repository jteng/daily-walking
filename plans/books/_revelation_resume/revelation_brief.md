## Shared interpretive framework (all files must agree)
- Author: John, who names himself (1:1, 4, 9); this study takes the traditional view that he is the apostle John (Justin Martyr, *Dialogue* 81; Irenaeus). Note Dionysius of Alexandria's doubts on style (Eusebius, *Church History* 7.25) as the main alternative ("John the elder" or another John). Keep consistent with 1 John 00 / 2 John 00.
- Date: most scholars c. AD 95 under Domitian (Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.30.3); some argue c. AD 68 under Nero. Present both; this study follows the later date but nothing in chs. 1-5 depends on it. Don't overstate Domitian's persecution (evidence for an empire-wide persecution is debated; local pressure, the imperial cult in Asia, synagogue conflict).
- Genre: apocalypse, prophecy and letter at once (1:1-4). Symbols drawn mainly from the OT (Daniel, Ezekiel, Zechariah, Isaiah, Exodus). Numbers are symbolic in many readings.
- Four approaches, labelled symmetrically with representatives: preterist (mainly fulfilled in the first century), historicist (church history in sequence; many Reformers), futurist (mostly chs. 4-22 still future; dispensationalists and many historic premillennialists), idealist/spiritual (timeless conflict). This study reads eclectically, as many evangelical commentators do (e.g. G. K. 毕尔 Beale, 包衡 Bauckham, Grant Osborne): written to seven real churches, speaking to the church in every age, and looking to Christ's real, bodily return and the new creation.
- Millennium (ch. 20) is not settled here: name amillennial (e.g. Anthony Hoekema 安东尼·胡克玛), historic premillennial (George Eldon Ladd 乔治·赖德), dispensational premillennial, postmillennial — only in 00, briefly.
- Chinese church context: Revelation misused by date-setters and cults (东方闪电（自称全能神教会）claims about the sealed scroll), 666 speculation, fear-driven preaching; also churches under pressure (Smyrna, Philadelphia). Faithful-pastor balance wherever false teachers appear. Safeguarding wording (WORKFLOW) wherever abusive leaders or sexual exploitation come up (e.g. "Jezebel," 2:20).
- Debated points to present as options with names: 1:4 "seven spirits" (Holy Spirit via Isa 11:2/Zech 4, or seven angels); 1:10 "Lord's day" (Sunday; or day of the Lord); 1:20 "angels" of the churches (angels, messengers/leaders, or the church's personified spirit); the seven churches as successive church ages (Scofield-style dispensational reading) vs historical churches speaking to all churches; 4:1 "Come up here" (dispensational: picture of the rapture; others: John's visionary call); 24 elders (representing OT and NT people of God, or angelic beings); 4 living creatures (Ezek 1; Isa 6); 5:1 scroll (God's plan of judgment and redemption; covenant/title-deed reading; etc.).
- Names in ::zh: 包衡, 毕尔, 乔治·赖德, 安东尼·胡克玛, 优西比乌《教会史》, 爱任纽《驳异端》, 游斯丁; 英文钦定本, 英文标准版, 七十士译本, 不定过去时. Prose spells CUV names (拔摩, 以弗所, 士每拿, 别迦摩, 推雅推喇, 撒狄, 非拉铁非, 老底嘉, 尼哥拉一党, 安提帕, 耶洗别).

## Meditation assignments (fresh; not in WORKFLOW's used lists)
- 00: Revelation as something to "hear and keep" (1:3), not a code to crack or a book to avoid out of fear; it is "the revelation of Jesus Christ" (1:1). Speak to the reader frightened by end-times preaching and the one fascinated by timelines; end on Christ himself (not a doxology).
- 01: John falls at his feet as though dead (1:17); fear of the holy Christ whose eyes are like flame seeing my sin; "Fear not... I died, and behold I am alive forevermore, and I have the keys of Death and Hades" (1:17-18). Do NOT end on Christ's hand (hand endings are used): end on the keys.
- 02 (ch. 2: Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira): Ephesus, orthodox and hard-working but having "abandoned the love you had at first" (2:4); busy service with love gone cold; for the one who never "felt" first love; end on Christ who walks among the lampstands and the tree of life in the paradise of God (2:1, 7).
- 03 (ch. 3: Sardis, Philadelphia, Laodicea): Laodicea's "I am rich... I need nothing" (3:17) — comfortable self-sufficiency; Christ offers to sell what cannot be bought (cf. Isa 55:1); "Those whom I love, I reprove and discipline" (3:19). Do NOT end on the 3:20 door (door endings are used); end on the white garments / being loved.
- 04: anxiety about a world (news, economy, politics, illness) that seems out of control; John sees first "a throne stood in heaven" (4:2); end on the rainbow around the throne (4:3; Gen 9:13-16 covenant mercy), not on a doxology formula.
- 05: John weeps because no one is worthy to open the scroll (5:4); "Weep no more; behold, the Lion... has conquered" — and he sees "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain" (5:5-6). Wanting God to act with power in my crisis, and finding his power in the slain Lamb. Do not build the meditation on 5:9 "from every tribe" (used in Titus); end on the slain-yet-standing Lamb.

Each meditation: honest scenario from Chinese church/family life, gospel ground before any step, a word for the reader who has failed, ending on Christ and grace. Vary shape: avoid "the one who said it may have been me" openings, "came near" endings, and the "So I can... / If I have already..." two-paragraph close used in 2 John, 3 John and Jude.
