## 这卷书在讲什么

使徒行传的书名是后来加上的，书中其实不只记使徒的事，主角也不是使徒。作者一开头就说：「我已经作了前书，论到耶稣开头一切所行所教训的。」（1:1）「开头」这两个字是全书的钥匙：路加福音记的是耶稣「开头」所做的；使徒行传记的，是复活升天的耶稣借着圣灵、借着他的教会**继续**做的。所以有人说，这卷书更合适的名字是「圣灵行传」，或「复活的主行传」。

## 作者与写作背景

使徒行传和路加福音是同一位作者写给同一个人的上下两卷。两卷都是写给「提阿非罗」（路1:3；徒1:1），名字的意思是「爱神的人」；路加福音称他「大人」，他很可能是一位资助这部著作的贵人。早期教会一致认为作者是路加，就是保罗所说的「所亲爱的医生路加」（西4:14）和同工（门24；提后4:11）。书中有几段忽然从「他们」变成「我们」（16:10-17；20:5–21:18；27:1–28:16），最自然的解释是：作者在这些时候亲身同行。

路加在福音书开头说，他「从起头都详细考察了」，「要按着次序」写，「使你知道所学之道都是确实的」（路1:3-4）。他是一位细心的史家：书中提到的官职、地名、头衔，许多都已被考古和古代文献证实。

成书年代有两种主要看法。一种认为在主后62年左右：全书在保罗于罗马软禁两年时结束（28:30），没有提到保罗受审的结果、尼禄对基督徒的大逼迫（64年）或圣殿被毁（70年）。另一种认为在主后70至80年代。两种看法在福音派学者中都有人支持。

## 钥匙经文：1:8

**1. 一节经文就是全书的目录。** 「但圣灵降临在你们身上，你们就必得着能力，并要在耶路撒冷、犹太全地，和撒马利亚，直到地极，作我的见证。」福音的路线图就在这里：
- **耶路撒冷**（1–7章）：圣灵降临，教会诞生，司提反殉道。
- **犹太全地和撒玛利亚**（8–12章）：逼迫使信徒分散，腓利到撒玛利亚，扫罗归主，彼得到外邦人哥尼流家。
- **直到地极**（13–28章）：保罗三次宣教旅程，最后被押解到帝国的中心罗马。

**2. 能力从哪里来。** 门徒不是靠自己的勇气或口才出去的。「圣灵降临在你们身上，你们就必得着能力。」这卷书提到圣灵的次数，远多于新约其他书卷。每一个突破，福音传到撒玛利亚人、外邦人，都由圣灵带领。

**3. 「作我的见证」。** 门徒的任务不是推广一套理念，而是为一个人作见证：复活的耶稣。「见证」这个词贯穿全书（1:22；2:32；3:15；10:39-41；22:15；26:16）。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 留意「道兴旺」的进展报告。** 路加在书中几次停下来作总结，像路标一样标出福音的进展：「神的道兴旺起来」（6:7），「教会……都得平安，被建立」（9:31），「神的道日见兴旺，越发广传」（12:24），「众教会信心越发坚固，人数天天加增」（16:5），「主的道大大兴旺，而且得胜」（19:20）。最后一节以保罗在罗马「放胆传讲神国的道……并没有人禁止」作结（28:31）。「没有人禁止」在原文是全书的最后一个字。保罗被锁着，神的道却没有被捆绑。

**2. 叙事与教导的分别。** 使徒行传大部分是叙事：记载当时发生了什么。不是每一件被记下来的事都是要今天的教会照样做的。例如，门徒用摇签选出马提亚（1:26），初期教会凡物公用（2:44），圣灵降临的方式在不同场合各不相同（2章；8章；10章；19章）。读的时候要问：路加记这件事，是要我们效法，还是要我们看见神在救恩历史中独特的一步？最可靠的方法，是把叙事和新约书信中清楚的教导一起读。

**3. 留意讲道。** 书中的讲道（彼得、司提反、保罗）约占全书的三分之一。这些讲道一再回到同一个核心：耶稣被钉、神叫他复活、旧约在他身上应验、人要悔改信他。读这些讲道，就是在学习使徒怎样传福音。

**4. 一个未完的结尾。** 使徒行传没有交代保罗最后的结局。这也许是有意的：福音的故事还没有结束。每一个世代的教会，都在继续写第29章。

## What This Book Is About

The title "Acts of the Apostles" was added later. The book isn't only about the apostles, and they aren't its main character. The author opens, "In the first book, O Theophilus, I have dealt with all that Jesus began to do and teach" (1:1). "Began" is the key to the whole book. The Gospel of Luke recorded what Jesus *began* to do; Acts records what the risen, ascended Jesus *continues* to do through the Spirit and through his church. That's why some have said a better title would be "the Acts of the Holy Spirit," or "the Acts of the Risen Lord."

## Author and Setting

Acts and the Gospel of Luke are two volumes by the same author to the same person. Both are addressed to "Theophilus" (Luke 1:3; Acts 1:1), whose name means "lover of God." Luke calls him "most excellent," so he was likely a patron who sponsored the work. The early church agreed that the author was Luke, "the beloved physician" (Col 4:14) and Paul's fellow worker (Philem 24; 2 Tim 4:11). In several passages the narrative suddenly switches from "they" to "we" (16:10–17; 20:5–21:18; 27:1–28:16); the most natural explanation is that the author was traveling with Paul at those times.

At the start of his Gospel, Luke says he has "followed all things closely," to write "an orderly account... that you may have certainty concerning the things you have been taught" (Luke 1:3–4). He is a careful historian: many of the officials, places and titles he mentions have been confirmed by archaeology and ancient sources.

There are two main views on the date. One places it around AD 62: the book ends with Paul under house arrest in Rome for two years (28:30) and says nothing of the outcome of his trial, Nero's persecution of Christians (64) or the destruction of the temple (70). The other places it in the 70s or 80s. Both views have evangelical support.

## The Key Verse: 1:8

**1. One verse is the book's table of contents.** "But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth." The gospel's route map is here:
- **Jerusalem** (chs. 1–7): the Spirit comes, the church is born, Stephen is martyred.
- **All Judea and Samaria** (chs. 8–12): persecution scatters the believers, Philip goes to Samaria, Saul is converted, Peter goes to the Gentile Cornelius.
- **The end of the earth** (chs. 13–28): Paul's three missionary journeys, and finally his transfer under guard to Rome, the heart of the empire.

**2. Where the power comes from.** The disciples didn't go out on their own courage or eloquence. "You will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you." Acts mentions the Spirit far more often than any other New Testament book, and every breakthrough, as the gospel reaches Samaritans and then Gentiles, is led by the Spirit.

**3. "My witnesses."** The disciples' task isn't to promote an idea but to bear witness to a person: the risen Jesus. "Witness" runs through the whole book (1:22; 2:32; 3:15; 10:39–41; 22:15; 26:16).

## How to Read This Book

**1. Watch for the progress reports.** Several times Luke pauses to sum up, like signposts marking the gospel's advance: "the word of God continued to increase" (6:7); "the church... had peace and was being built up" (9:31); "the word of God increased and multiplied" (12:24); "the churches were strengthened in the faith, and they increased in numbers daily" (16:5); "the word of the Lord continued to increase and prevail mightily" (19:20). The book closes with Paul in Rome "proclaiming the kingdom of God... with all boldness and without hindrance" (28:31). "Without hindrance" is the very last word of the book in Greek. Paul is in chains, but the word of God is not bound.

**2. The difference between narrative and teaching.** Most of Acts is narrative: a record of what happened. Not everything it records is meant for the church to repeat today. For example, the disciples chose Matthias by casting lots (1:26), the early church held everything in common (2:44), and the way the Spirit came differed from one occasion to another (chs. 2, 8, 10, 19). As you read, ask: is Luke recording this for us to imitate, or to show a unique step in God's plan of salvation? The safest approach is to read the narrative alongside the clear teaching of the New Testament letters.

**3. Watch the sermons.** The speeches in Acts (Peter's, Stephen's, Paul's) make up about a third of the book. They return again and again to the same core: Jesus was crucified, God raised him, the Old Testament is fulfilled in him, and people must repent and believe in him. Reading these sermons is learning how the apostles preached the gospel.

**4. An unfinished ending.** Acts doesn't tell us how Paul's story finally ends. That may be deliberate: the gospel story isn't over. The church in every generation keeps writing chapter 29.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Acts says that what Jesus "began" to do, he continues to do after his resurrection through the Spirit. Do I see Jesus' work as past history, or do I believe he is still at work today through the Spirit and through his church? On the "to the end of the earth" map, where are my own "Jerusalem" and "Samaria"? Who are the people I think least likely ever to hear the gospel?
