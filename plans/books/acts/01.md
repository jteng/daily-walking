## 本章脉络

第一章是一座桥，连接路加福音的结尾和教会的开始。**序言**（1-5节）：复活的耶稣四十天向使徒显现，吩咐他们等候父的应许。**最后的问题与托付**（6-8节）：「主啊，你复兴以色列国就在这时候吗？」**升天**（9-11节）：一朵云彩把他接去，两个穿白衣的人说他「还要怎样来」。**楼房里的祷告**（12-14节）。**补选马提亚**（15-26节）：十二使徒的数目恢复。

这一章里，门徒什么大事都还没有做。他们在等候、在祷告。教会的历史，不是从行动开始，而是从等候神的应许开始。

## 深读 1:1-5「耶稣开头一切所行所教训的」

**1. 「开头」。** 「提阿非罗啊，我已经作了前书，论到耶稣开头一切所行所教训的。」（1:1）路加没有说「耶稣一切所行所教训的」，而是「**开头**」所行所教训的。言下之意：耶稣的工作没有在升天时结束，使徒行传要记的，就是他继续在做的事。

**2. 四十天。** 「他受害之后，用许多的凭据将自己活活地显给使徒看，四十天之久向他们显现，讲说神国的事。」（1:3）「许多的凭据」，路加是一位重视证据的作者（路1:1-4）。四十天也让人想起以色列在旷野四十年、耶稣在旷野受试探四十天：一段预备的时间。

**3. 「受圣灵的洗」。** 「约翰是用水施洗，但不多几日，你们要受圣灵的洗。」（1:5）施洗约翰在路加福音3:16早就预言：「他要用圣灵与火给你们施洗。」现在耶稣说，这应许「不多几日」就要成就。第二章的五旬节，就是这个应许的实现。

## 深读 1:6-8「你复兴以色列国就在这时候吗？」

**1. 门徒的问题。** 「主啊，你复兴以色列国就在这时候吗？」（1:6）听了四十天关于「神国」的教导，门徒想的仍是以色列国的复兴。这个问题不全是错的：旧约先知确实应许神要复兴以色列（如结37章；摩9:11-15）。问题在于他们想的是时间（「就在这时候吗？」），和范围（只是以色列）。

**2. 耶稣的回答。** 「父凭着自己的权柄所定的时候、日期，不是你们可以知道的。」（1:7）耶稣没有说以色列的盼望是错的，而是把门徒的目光从「什么时候」转向「现在要做什么」。

**3. 范围的扩大。** 「但圣灵降临在你们身上，你们就必得着能力，并要在耶路撒冷、犹太全地，和撒马利亚，直到地极，作我的见证。」（1:8）门徒问的是以色列，耶稣的回答是「直到地极」。以赛亚书49:6说，神的仆人不只要复兴雅各众支派，还要「作外邦人的光，叫你施行我的救恩，直到地极」。神复兴他子民的方式，是把福音带到万国。保罗后来在安提阿就引用了这节以赛亚书（徒13:47）。

> 要点：门徒想知道时间表，耶稣给他们一个使命和一份能力。基督徒对末世的盼望，不是让我们猜日期，而是推动我们去作见证。

## 深读 1:9-11「他还要怎样来」

**1. 云彩。** 「他就被取上升，有一朵云彩把他接去，便看不见他了。」（1:9）在圣经里，云彩常是神同在的记号：旷野的云柱、会幕上的云、登山变像时的云（路9:34-35）。但以理书7:13更说，有一位「像人子的，驾着天云而来，被领到亘古常在者面前」，得着权柄、荣耀和国度。耶稣升天，就是这位人子被领到父面前、坐上宝座。

**2. 升天的意义。** 升天不是耶稣离开了我们，而是他登基了。使徒行传接下来一再说，他被高举在神的右边（2:33-36；5:31；7:55-56）。正因为他坐在宝座上，他才能把圣灵浇灌下来（2:33）。

**3. 「为什么站着望天呢？」** 两个穿白衣的人说：「加利利人哪，你们为什么站着望天呢？这离开你们被接升天的耶稣，你们见他怎样往天上去，他还要怎样来。」（1:11）这句话有安慰，也有提醒：安慰是，同一位耶稣，会以同样可见的方式再来；提醒是，不要只站着望天，回到耶路撒冷去，等候圣灵，然后去作见证。

## 深读 1:12-14「同心合意地恒切祷告」

**1. 楼房里的人。** 十一位使徒，「同着几个妇人和耶稣的母亲马利亚，并耶稣的弟兄，都同心合意地恒切祷告」（1:14）。路加特别提到妇女，这与他福音书一贯的关注一致。耶稣的弟兄们也在这里，在约翰福音7:5他们还不信，现在他们在祷告的人当中。复活改变了他们。

**2. 「同心合意」。** 这个词（*homothymadon*）在使徒行传里一再出现（2:46；4:24；5:12），形容初期教会的合一。他们等候的方式，是一起祷告。

**3. 等候不是消极的。** 耶稣叫他们等候，他们就祷告。等候神的应许，不是什么都不做，而是把自己放在一个领受的姿态里。

## 深读 1:15-26 补选马提亚

**1. 为什么要补足十二个？** 耶稣拣选十二个使徒，对应以色列的十二支派（路22:30）。犹大的位置空了，彼得说必须补足（1:21-22）。在圣灵降临、福音传给以色列之前，这个代表新以色列的核心群体要先完整。

**2. 犹大的死。** 路加说犹大「身子仆倒，肚腹崩裂」（1:18），马太福音说他「出去吊死了」（太27:5）。马太说祭司长用那钱买了一块田（太27:7），路加说「这人用他作恶的工价买了一块田」。解经家常见的解释是：两处是同一件事的不同面向，祭司长用犹大的钱买了田，所以可以说是犹大买的；他吊死以后，身体坠落破裂。两卷书各自从不同角度记载。

**3. 使徒的资格。** 被选的人必须是「从约翰施洗起，直到主离开我们被接上升的日子为止」一直与使徒同在的人，并且要「与我们同作耶稣复活的见证」（1:21-22）。使徒的核心身份，是复活的亲眼见证人。这也说明为什么使徒的职分是独特、不能延续的：之后的世代，没有人能再满足这个条件。

**4. 摇签。** 「于是众人为他们摇签，摇出马提亚来。」（1:26）这是圣经里最后一次记载用摇签来寻求神的旨意，旧约的大祭司也用乌陵和土明来求问（出28:30）。五旬节以后，使徒行传里再没有出现摇签：圣灵降临以后，教会在祷告、圣经和众人的商议中寻求神的引导（如15:28「圣灵和我们定意」）。这是叙事记载，不是要今天的教会照着做的方法。

## 默想

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 is a bridge between the end of Luke's Gospel and the beginning of the church. **The prologue** (vv. 1–5): for forty days the risen Jesus appears to the apostles and tells them to wait for the Father's promise. **The last question and the charge** (vv. 6–8): "Lord, will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?" **The ascension** (vv. 9–11): a cloud takes him, and two men in white say he "will come in the same way." **Prayer in the upper room** (vv. 12–14). **Matthias chosen** (vv. 15–26): the number of the Twelve is restored.

In this chapter the disciples haven't yet done anything great. They are waiting and praying. The church's history begins not with action but with waiting for God's promise.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–5 "All that Jesus began to do and teach"

**1. "Began."** "In the first book, O Theophilus, I have dealt with all that Jesus began to do and teach" (1:1). Luke doesn't say "all that Jesus did and taught" but all he *began* to do and teach. The implication: Jesus' work didn't end at the ascension, and Acts records what he goes on doing.

**2. Forty days.** "He presented himself alive after his suffering by many proofs, appearing to them during forty days and speaking about the kingdom of God" (1:3). "Many proofs": Luke is an author who cares about evidence (Luke 1:1–4). Forty days also recalls Israel's forty years in the wilderness and Jesus' forty days of temptation, a time of preparation.

**3. "Baptized with the Holy Spirit."** "For John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now" (1:5). John the Baptist had foretold it in Luke 3:16: "He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire." Now Jesus says the promise will be fulfilled "not many days from now." Pentecost in chapter 2 is its fulfillment.

## Going Deeper: 1:6–8 "Will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?"

**1. The disciples' question.** "Lord, will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?" (1:6). After forty days of teaching about "the kingdom of God," the disciples are still thinking of Israel's restoration. The question isn't entirely wrong: the prophets did promise that God would restore Israel (e.g., Ezek 37; Amos 9:11–15). The problem lies in their focus on timing ("at this time?") and scope (Israel only).

**2. Jesus' answer.** "It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father has fixed by his own authority" (1:7). Jesus doesn't say Israel's hope is mistaken; he turns the disciples' eyes from "when?" to "what now?"

**3. A wider scope.** "But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth" (1:8). The disciples asked about Israel; Jesus answers, "to the end of the earth." Isaiah 49:6 says God's servant will not only raise up the tribes of Jacob but be "a light for the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth." God restores his people by carrying the gospel to the nations. Paul later quotes this very verse of Isaiah at Antioch (Acts 13:47).

> The point: The disciples wanted a timetable; Jesus gave them a mission and the power for it. Christian hope about the last things isn't meant to set us guessing dates but to send us out as witnesses.

## Going Deeper: 1:9–11 "He will come in the same way"

**1. The cloud.** "He was lifted up, and a cloud took him out of their sight" (1:9). In Scripture a cloud is often a sign of God's presence: the pillar of cloud in the wilderness, the cloud over the tabernacle, the cloud at the transfiguration (Luke 9:34–35). Daniel 7:13 describes "one like a son of man" coming "with the clouds of heaven" and presented before the Ancient of Days, to receive dominion, glory and a kingdom. Jesus' ascension is this Son of Man being brought before the Father and taking his throne.

**2. What the ascension means.** The ascension isn't Jesus leaving us; it's his enthronement. Acts repeatedly says he is exalted at the right hand of God (2:33–36; 5:31; 7:55–56). It is precisely because he sits on the throne that he can pour out the Spirit (2:33).

**3. "Why do you stand looking into heaven?"** The two men in white say, "Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw him go into heaven" (1:11). These words bring comfort and a nudge. The comfort: the same Jesus will return in the same visible way. The nudge: don't just stand gazing at the sky; go back to Jerusalem, wait for the Spirit, then bear witness.

## Going Deeper: 1:12–14 "With one accord... devoting themselves to prayer"

**1. Who was in the upper room.** The eleven apostles, "with one accord were devoting themselves to prayer, together with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brothers" (1:14). Luke mentions the women, consistent with his concern throughout his Gospel. Jesus' brothers are here too. In John 7:5 they didn't yet believe; now they are among those praying. The resurrection changed them.

**2. "With one accord."** This word (*homothymadon*) recurs in Acts (2:46; 4:24; 5:12), describing the unity of the early church. They waited by praying together.

**3. Waiting isn't passive.** Jesus told them to wait, so they prayed. Waiting for God's promise isn't doing nothing; it's putting yourself in a posture to receive.

## Going Deeper: 1:15–26 Matthias Chosen

**1. Why restore the Twelve?** Jesus chose twelve apostles to correspond to Israel's twelve tribes (Luke 22:30). Judas's place was empty, and Peter says it must be filled (1:21–22). Before the Spirit comes and the gospel goes to Israel, this core group representing the new Israel needs to be complete.

**2. Judas's death.** Luke says that "falling headlong he burst open in the middle" (1:18); Matthew says he "went and hanged himself" (Matt 27:5). Matthew says the chief priests bought a field with the money (Matt 27:7); Luke says Judas "bought a field with the reward of his wickedness." A common explanation is that these are two sides of the same event: the chief priests bought the field with Judas's money, so it could be called his purchase, and after he hanged himself his body fell and burst open. Each book records it from a different angle.

**3. The qualification for an apostle.** The one chosen had to be someone who had been with the apostles "beginning from the baptism of John until the day when he was taken up from us," and who would "become with us a witness to his resurrection" (1:21–22). The core of an apostle's identity was being an eyewitness of the resurrection. This also explains why the apostolic office was unique and couldn't continue: no later generation could meet that condition.

**4. Casting lots.** "And they cast lots for them, and the lot fell on Matthias" (1:26). This is the last time Scripture records casting lots to seek God's will; Israel's high priest likewise inquired by the Urim and Thummim (Ex 28:30). After Pentecost, lots never appear again in Acts. Once the Spirit has come, the church seeks God's guidance through prayer, Scripture and shared deliberation (e.g., 15:28, "it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us"). This is narrative, recording what happened, not a method for the church today.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The disciples asked "when?"; Jesus gave them the Spirit and a mission. Do I often ask "when?" about the future, while overlooking what he has given me to do today? The ascended Jesus sits on the throne. Do I believe he reigns today and is with me by his Spirit? Is there a season of "waiting" in my life? Am I waiting anxiously, or, like the disciples in the upper room, waiting in prayer for his promise?
