## 本章脉络

第二章是使徒行传的第一个高峰。**圣灵降临**（1-13节）：大风的响声、舌头如火焰，门徒说起别国的话来，各国来的犹太人都听见他们用自己的乡谈讲说神的大作为。**彼得的讲道**（14-36节）：引用约珥书、诗篇16篇和110篇，宣告「你们钉在十字架上的这位耶稣，神已经立他为主，为基督了」。**众人的回应**（37-41节）：「我们当怎样行？」三千人受洗。**初期教会的生活**（42-47节）。

第一章结束时，一百二十个门徒关在楼房里祷告；第二章结束时，有三千多人，「主将得救的人天天加给他们」。改变这一切的，不是门徒变得更聪明或更勇敢，而是圣灵降临了。

## 深读 2:1-13 风、火与乡谈

**1. 五旬节。** 五旬节是逾越节后第五十天的「七七节」，是收割初熟麦子的节期（利23:15-21）。耶稣在逾越节被钉，在五旬节浇灌圣灵：庄稼初熟的果子，就在这一天收进来了（2:41）。后来的犹太传统也把这个节期和西奈山颁布律法联系起来，但这个说法是否早到耶稣的时代，学者看法不一。

**2. 风与火。** 「从天上有响声下来，好像一阵大风吹过……又有舌头如火焰显现出来，分开落在他们各人头上。」（2:2-3）风让人想起以西结书37章的「气息」，也呼应耶稣对尼哥德慕说的「风随着意思吹」（约3:8）；火让人想起西奈山上神降临时的火（出19:18），也可能呼应施洗约翰所说「用圣灵与火……施洗」（路3:16）。在旧约里，神的同在曾以火柱、以荆棘中的火彰显；现在，这火分开落在每一个门徒头上。神的同在不再只住在一座殿里，而是住在他的百姓里面。

**3. 别国的话。** 「他们就都被圣灵充满，按着圣灵所赐的口才说起别国的话来。」（2:4）路加说得很清楚：这些是真实的语言，各国来的人「各人听见门徒用众人的乡谈说话」（2:6），而且他们说的是「神的大作为」（2:11）。

**4. 巴别塔的反转？** 许多解经家在这里看见创世记11章巴别塔的反转：在巴别，人想为自己传扬名声，神变乱了他们的口音，使他们分散；在五旬节，神的灵使分散各地的人听懂同一个信息，聚集在一起。路加没有明说，但这个对照很有启发：福音不要求万民先说同一种语言，而是用各民各族自己的语言，讲说神的作为。所以福音不是哪一个民族的「洋教」；神从一开始，就要用中国人自己的话，向中国人讲说他的大作为。

## 深读 2:14-21「在末后的日子」

**1. 约珥书的应验。** 彼得说：「这正是先知约珥所说的。」（2:16）约珥书2:28说：「以后，我要将我的灵浇灌凡有血气的。」彼得用先知常用的「末后的日子」（参赛2:2）来解释约珥书的「以后」（2:17），而约珥书的「以后」本来就指向「耶和华大而可畏的日子」（珥2:31）。他是在宣告：末后的日子已经开始了。

**2. 「凡有血气的」。** 旧约里，圣灵通常临到特定的人：先知、君王、士师。摩西曾盼望：「惟愿耶和华的百姓都受感说话。」（民11:29）约珥书应许，有一天圣灵要浇灌在「儿女」「少年人」「老年人」「仆人和使女」身上，不分性别、年龄、身份。五旬节，这个盼望成就了。

**3. 「凡求告主名的，就必得救」。** 彼得的引文以这句结束（2:21；珥2:32）。在约珥书里，「主」是耶和华；在彼得的讲道里，他接着就说明，这位「主」就是耶稣（2:36）。求告耶稣的名，就是求告耶和华的名。

## 深读 2:22-36「神已经立他为主，为基督了」

**1. 神的定旨与人的罪责。** 「他既按着神的定旨先见被交与人，你们就藉着无法之人的手，把他钉在十字架上，杀了。」（2:23）同一句话里，彼得说了两件事：十字架是神预先定下的计划；钉死耶稣的人仍要负完全的责任。圣经从不让神的主权取消人的罪责。

**2. 诗篇16篇：不见朽坏。** 彼得引用诗篇16篇：「因你必不将我的灵魂撇在阴间，也不叫你的圣者见朽坏。」（2:27）然后他指出，大卫「死了，也葬埋了，并且他的坟墓直到今日还在我们这里」（2:29）。所以这话不是指大卫自己，而是指他的后裔弥赛亚。大卫的坟墓还在，耶稣的坟墓却空了。

**3. 诗篇110篇：坐在右边。** 「主对我主说：你坐在我的右边。」（2:34）大卫并没有升到天上，所以这里的「我主」指的是比大卫更大的那一位。耶稣自己也曾用这篇诗篇问文士（路20:39-44；参太22:41-46）。复活升天的耶稣，就坐在神的右边，并且「从父受了所应许的圣灵，就把你们所看见所听见的，浇灌下来」（2:33）。五旬节是升天的结果：坐在宝座上的王，把圣灵赐下来。

**4. 讲道的高潮。** 「故此，以色列全家当确实地知道，你们钉在十字架上的这位耶稣，神已经立他为主，为基督了。」（2:36）「你们钉在十字架上的」与「神已经立他为主」并列：人弃绝的，神高举了。「立」不是说耶稣到复活时才成为神。这位圣子道成肉身、被钉十字架；如今神叫他复活，公开高举他登上弥赛亚的宝座，证明他是谁（参罗1:4；腓2:9-11）。何况彼得刚才已经把约珥书里的「主」（耶和华）用在耶稣身上。

> 要点：五旬节的中心不是方言，也不是神迹，而是耶稣：被钉、复活、升天、坐在宝座上，并且把圣灵浇灌下来。圣灵的工作，总是把人指向基督。

## 深读 2:37-41「我们当怎样行？」

**1. 扎心。** 「众人听见这话，觉得扎心。」（2:37）这正是约翰福音16:8所说的：圣灵使世界为罪自己责备自己。彼得没有操控人心，也没有煽动情绪，只是清楚地传讲被钉、复活的基督。

**2. 悔改与受洗。** 「你们各人要悔改，奉耶稣基督的名受洗，叫你们的罪得赦，就必领受所赐的圣灵。」（2:38）「悔改」是心意的转变：转离罪，转向神。「叫你们的罪得赦」与受洗的关系，在不同传统中有不同的理解。福音派一般认为，罪得赦免是借着悔改和信心领受的。因为在使徒行传其他地方，赦罪常常只和悔改、信心相连（3:19；10:43；13:38-39）；哥尼流一家甚至在受洗以前就领受了圣灵（10:44-48）。至于洗礼，有人认为它主要是信心的公开表明；改革宗也称它为赦罪的记号和印证（参罗4:11）。另有传统认为洗礼本身带来重生（他们也常引用22:16）。各方都同意：悔改和信靠基督是不可少的。

**3. 应许给你们的儿女和远方的人。** 「这应许是给你们和你们的儿女，并一切在远方的人，就是主我们神所召来的。」（2:39）「远方的人」暗示福音要传给外邦人（参弗2:13、17），这正是全书要展开的。

## 深读 2:42-47 初期教会的生活

**1. 四样恒心遵守的事。** 「都恒心遵守使徒的教训，彼此交接，擘饼，祈祷。」（2:42）教导、团契、擘饼（很可能包括主的晚餐）、祷告（原文有冠词，「那些祷告」，可能包括固定时间的祷告）。这四样至今仍是教会生活的骨架。

**2. 凡物公用。** 「信的人都在一处，凡物公用，并且卖了田产、家业，照各人所需用的分给各人。」（2:44-45）「卖了」在原文是未完成时，表示有需要时陆续变卖，不是一次全部变卖。这也不是强制取消私有财产：彼得后来对亚拿尼亚说，田地「还没有卖，不是你自己的吗？既卖了，价银不是你作主吗？」（5:4）这是被福音改变的人，自愿地把别人的需要看作自己的事。路加记下他们怎样做；我们要学的是其中的精神，就是慷慨、彼此照顾，具体做法则可以不同。

**3. 喜乐与见证。** 「存着欢喜、诚实的心用饭，赞美神，得众民的喜爱。主将得救的人天天加给他们。」（2:46-47）注意最后一句：加添人数的是「主」，不是使徒的策略。

## 默想

**默想：** 五旬节的中心是被高举的耶稣把圣灵浇灌下来。我是把圣灵只看成一种能力或经历，还是认识他是那位把我指向基督的神？众人扎心，是因为明白了：被他们钉死的，正是主。但彼得紧接着说：「这应许是给你们……的。」当神的话刺痛我的时候，我是躲开、自责，还是相信这应许也是给我的，转向他？初期教会恒心遵守那四样事，并且「存着欢喜」的心。我对教会生活的投入，是出于义务，还是出于这样的喜乐？如果冷淡了，我需要的也许首先不是更努力，而是重新看见那位被高举、为我浇灌圣灵的主。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 is the first high point of Acts. **The Spirit comes** (vv. 1–13): the sound of a mighty wind, tongues as of fire, and the disciples speaking in other languages, so that Jews from every nation hear them telling the mighty works of God in their own tongues. **Peter's sermon** (vv. 14–36): quoting Joel, Psalm 16 and Psalm 110, he proclaims that "God has made him both Lord and Christ, this Jesus whom you crucified." **The crowd responds** (vv. 37–41): "What shall we do?" and three thousand are baptized. **The life of the early church** (vv. 42–47).

At the end of chapter 1, a hundred and twenty disciples were praying in an upper room. At the end of chapter 2 there are over three thousand, and "the Lord added to their number day by day those who were being saved." What changed everything wasn't that the disciples became smarter or braver, but that the Spirit came.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–13 Wind, Fire and Languages

**1. Pentecost.** Pentecost was the Feast of Weeks, fifty days after Passover, celebrating the firstfruits of the wheat harvest (Lev 23:15–21). Jesus was crucified at Passover and poured out the Spirit at Pentecost, and on that day the firstfruits of the harvest were gathered in (2:41). Later Jewish tradition also linked this feast with the giving of the law at Sinai, though scholars differ on whether that link goes back to Jesus' time.

**2. Wind and fire.** "A sound like a mighty rushing wind... And divided tongues as of fire appeared to them and rested on each one of them" (2:2–3). The wind recalls the "breath" of Ezekiel 37 and Jesus' words to Nicodemus, "The wind blows where it wishes" (John 3:8). The fire recalls God descending on Sinai in fire (Ex 19:18) and may echo John the Baptist's words, "baptize you with the Holy Spirit and with fire" (Luke 3:16). In the Old Testament God's presence was shown in a pillar of fire and a burning bush; now that fire divides and rests on each disciple. God's presence no longer dwells only in a temple but in his people.

**3. Other languages.** "And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit gave them utterance" (2:4). Luke is clear that these were real languages: people from many nations "were hearing them speak in his own language" (2:6), and what they spoke of was "the mighty works of God" (2:11).

**4. Babel reversed?** Many interpreters see here a reversal of the tower of Babel in Genesis 11. At Babel people tried to make a name for themselves, and God confused their language and scattered them. At Pentecost God's Spirit enables scattered people to understand one message and gathers them together. Luke doesn't say so explicitly, but the contrast is illuminating: the gospel doesn't require all peoples first to speak one language; it speaks of God's works in the languages of every people. So the gospel is no one nation's "foreign religion"; from the start God meant to tell of his mighty works to every people in their own tongue.

## Going Deeper: 2:14–21 "In the last days"

**1. Joel fulfilled.** Peter says, "this is what was uttered through the prophet Joel" (2:16). Joel 2:28 says, "And it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh." Peter interprets Joel's "afterward," which already looks toward "the great and awesome day of the LORD" (Joel 2:31), with the prophetic phrase "in the last days" (2:17; cf. Isa 2:2). He is declaring that the last days have begun.

**2. "All flesh."** In the Old Testament the Spirit usually came on particular people: prophets, kings, judges. Moses once longed, "Would that all the LORD's people were prophets" (Num 11:29). Joel promised that one day the Spirit would be poured out on "sons and daughters," "young men," "old men," "male servants and female servants," regardless of sex, age or status. At Pentecost that longing was fulfilled.

**3. "Everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."** Peter's quotation ends with this line (2:21; Joel 2:32). In Joel, "the Lord" is the LORD, Yahweh; in Peter's sermon he goes on to show that this "Lord" is Jesus (2:36). To call on Jesus' name is to call on the name of the LORD.

## Going Deeper: 2:22–36 "God has made him both Lord and Christ"

**1. God's plan and human guilt.** "This Jesus, delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God, you crucified and killed by the hands of lawless men" (2:23). In one sentence Peter says two things: the cross was God's predetermined plan, and those who crucified Jesus remain fully responsible. Scripture never lets God's sovereignty cancel human guilt.

**2. Psalm 16: no corruption.** Peter quotes Psalm 16: "For you will not abandon my soul to Hades, or let your Holy One see corruption" (2:27). Then he points out that David "both died and was buried, and his tomb is with us to this day" (2:29). So these words don't refer to David himself but to his descendant, the Messiah. David's tomb is still occupied; Jesus' tomb is empty.

**3. Psalm 110: seated at the right hand.** "The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand" (2:34). David didn't ascend into heaven, so "my Lord" here is someone greater than David. Jesus himself used this psalm to question the scribes (Luke 20:39–44; cf. Matt 22:41–46). The risen and ascended Jesus sits at God's right hand, and "having received from the Father the promise of the Holy Spirit, he has poured out this that you yourselves are seeing and hearing" (2:33). Pentecost is the result of the ascension: the King on the throne gives the Spirit.

**4. The climax of the sermon.** "Let all the house of Israel therefore know for certain that God has made him both Lord and Christ, this Jesus whom you crucified" (2:36). "Whom you crucified" stands beside "God has made him Lord": the one people rejected, God has exalted. "Made" doesn't mean Jesus became divine at the resurrection; God publicly installed and vindicated the crucified, incarnate Son in his messianic reign (cf. Rom 1:4; Phil 2:9–11). Peter has just applied Joel's "Lord," the LORD himself, to him.

> The point: The center of Pentecost isn't tongues or miracles but Jesus: crucified, risen, ascended, enthroned, and pouring out the Spirit. The Spirit's work always points people to Christ.

## Going Deeper: 2:37–41 "What shall we do?"

**1. Cut to the heart.** "Now when they heard this they were cut to the heart" (2:37). This is exactly what John 16:8 described: the Spirit convicting the world concerning sin. There was no manipulation or emotional pressure, only the clear proclamation of Christ crucified and risen.

**2. Repentance and baptism.** "Repent and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit" (2:38). "Repent" means a change of mind and heart: turning from sin to God. How "for the forgiveness of your sins" relates to baptism is understood differently across traditions. Evangelicals hold that forgiveness is received through repentance and faith, because elsewhere in Acts forgiveness is often tied to repentance and faith alone (3:19; 10:43; 13:38–39), and Cornelius's household even received the Spirit before being baptized (10:44–48). As for baptism, some see it chiefly as faith's public confession; Reformed churches also call it a sign and seal of that forgiveness (cf. Rom 4:11). Other traditions see baptism itself as conveying regeneration (often citing 22:16). All agree that repentance and faith in Christ are essential.

**3. A promise for your children and for those far off.** "For the promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off, everyone whom the Lord our God calls to himself" (2:39). "All who are far off" hints at the gospel going to the Gentiles (cf. Eph 2:13, 17), which is exactly what the book goes on to unfold.

## Going Deeper: 2:42–47 The Life of the Early Church

**1. Four devotions.** "And they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers" (2:42). Teaching, fellowship, breaking bread (very likely including the Lord's Supper) and prayer (the Greek has the article, "the prayers," which may include set times of prayer). These four remain the backbone of church life today.

**2. All things in common.** "And all who believed were together and had all things in common. And they were selling their possessions and belongings and distributing the proceeds to all, as any had need" (2:44–45). "Were selling" is imperfect tense: sales happened as needs arose, not in one liquidation. Nor was this a compulsory abolition of private property: Peter later tells Ananias, "While it remained unsold, did it not remain your own? And after it was sold, was it not at your disposal?" (5:4). It was people changed by the gospel freely treating others' needs as their own. The narrative tells us what they did; its spirit (generosity and mutual care) is what today's church must learn, though the specific form may differ.

**3. Joy and witness.** "They received their food with glad and generous hearts, praising God and having favor with all the people. And the Lord added to their number day by day those who were being saved" (2:46–47). Notice the last sentence: it is "the Lord" who adds to their number, not the apostles' strategy.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** At the center of Pentecost is the exalted Jesus pouring out the Spirit. Do I think of the Spirit only as a power or an experience, or do I know him as the God who points me to Christ? The crowd was cut to the heart because they saw that the one they had crucified was the Lord. Yet Peter's next words were, "The promise is for you." When God's word wounds me, do I hide or punish myself, or do I believe the promise is for me too, and turn to him? The early church devoted itself to these four things "with glad and generous hearts." Is my part in church life driven by duty or by that kind of gladness? If it has gone cold, what I need first may not be more effort but a fresh sight of the exalted Lord who poured out his Spirit on me.
