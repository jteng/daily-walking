## 本章脉络

第三章是一个神迹带出一篇讲道。**美门口的医治**（1-10节）：生来瘸腿的人，被彼得拉起来，「走着，跳着，赞美神」进了圣殿。**所罗门廊下的讲道**（11-26节）：彼得说明医治这人的不是他们，而是耶稣的名；他宣告被他们弃绝的耶稣，神已经叫他复活，并呼召他们悔改归正。

2:43说「使徒又行了许多奇事神迹」，第三章挑出其中一件来细写。这个神迹不只是一个人的身体得了医治，也是一幅图画：耶稣的名，使被拦阻在门外的人可以进来敬拜神。下一章，这件事就引来了第一次的逼迫。

## 深读 3:1-10「金银我都没有」

**1. 天天被抬到门口的人。** 「有一个人，生来是瘸腿的，天天被人抬来，放在殿的一个门口（那门名叫美门），要求进殿的人周济。」（3:2）他四十多岁了（4:22），一辈子都在门外。他所能盼望的，只是几个钱。

**2. 「金银我都没有」。** 「彼得说：『金银我都没有，只把我所有的给你：我奉拿撒勒人耶稣基督的名，叫你起来行走！』」（3:6）彼得给的，比那人所求的大得多。教会也许没有金银，但它有一样世界给不了的东西：耶稣的名。

**3. 「奉……名」。** 在圣经的思想里，「名」代表一个人的身份、权柄和同在。彼得不是在念一个有魔力的咒语（19:13-16就记载有人想这样用耶稣的名，结果被恶鬼制伏），而是宣告：复活的耶稣亲自在这里行事。

**4. 跳跃像鹿。** 「就跳起来，站着，又行走，同他们进了殿，走着，跳着，赞美神。」（3:8）以赛亚书35:6描写神来拯救他百姓的日子：「那时，瘸子必跳跃像鹿。」路加让读者看见，先知所说的那日子来了。而且，这个人不只是能走路了，他「同他们进了殿」：一生在门外的人，也许是第一次走进了敬拜神的地方（参撒下5:8的俗语：「在那里有瞎子、瘸子，他不能进屋去」）。

## 深读 3:11-16「为什么定睛看我们？」

**1. 拒绝功劳。** 众人都跑来看，彼得说：「为什么定睛看我们，以为我们凭自己的能力和虔诚使这人行走呢？」（3:12）彼得立刻把注意力从自己身上移开。使徒行传里，每当有人想把荣耀归给使徒，他们都拒绝（10:25-26；14:14-15）。

**2. 「神的仆人」。** 「亚伯拉罕、以撒、雅各的神……已经荣耀了他的仆人耶稣。」（3:13）「荣耀了他的仆人」，让人想起以赛亚书52:13（七十士译本）：「我的仆人……必被高举，大得荣耀。」紧接着的以赛亚书53章，就是那位受苦的仆人。彼得在说：被你们弃绝的耶稣，就是以赛亚所说那位受苦而后得荣耀的仆人。

**3. 一连串的对比。** 「你们弃绝了那圣洁公义者，反求着释放一个凶手给你们。你们杀了那生命的主，神却叫他从死里复活了。」（3:14-15）他们要了一个夺去生命的人（巴拉巴），却杀了那赐生命的主。「生命的主」（*archēgos*）也可以译作「元首」「开创者」，希伯来书用同一个字称耶稣为「救他们的元帅」（来2:10）、「为我们信心创始成终的」（来12:2）。

**4. 是谁的信心？** 「我们因信他的名，他的名便叫……这人健壮了；正是他所赐的信心，叫这人在你们众人面前全然好了。」（3:16）彼得强调：这信心本身也是「他所赐的」。医治的能力在耶稣，连信心也是从他来的。

## 深读 3:17-21「当悔改归正」

**1. 「出于不知」。** 「弟兄们，我晓得你们做这事是出于不知，你们的官长也是如此。」（3:17）这呼应耶稣在十字架上的祷告：「父啊！赦免他们；因为他们所做的，他们不晓得。」（路23:34）彼得的语气很温柔，称他们为「弟兄们」。但「不知」不等于无罪，他们仍然需要悔改。

**2. 神的计划在人的罪中成就。** 「但神曾藉众先知的口，预言基督将要受害，就这样应验了。」（3:18）和2:23一样：人的罪恶没有打乱神的计划，反而成了神成就救恩的方式。

**3. 悔改归正，罪得涂抹。** 「所以，你们当悔改归正，使你们的罪得以涂抹。」（3:19）「涂抹」是擦去、抹掉的意思，正如神说：「惟有我为自己的缘故涂抹你的过犯。」（赛43:25）悔改（改变心意）和归正（转身回来）是同一个动作的两面。注意这里罪得赦免是与悔改归正相连，没有提到洗礼（参2:38的讨论）。

**4. 安舒的日子与万物复兴。** 悔改以后，「那安舒的日子就必从主面前来到；主也必差遣所预定给你们的基督降临。天必留他，等到万物复兴的时候。」（3:20-21）「安舒的日子」可能指现在因悔改而得的属灵复兴，也指将来的完全；「万物复兴」（与1:6的「复兴」同一个字根）指基督再来时神对整个创造的更新，范围是「神从创世以来、藉着圣先知的口所说的」，不是说人人最终都会得救（3:23正好排除这种理解）。这回答了1:6门徒的问题：复兴会来，但它要在基督再来时完全实现；在那之前，天「必留他」，他坐在神的右边。

> 要点：彼得没有因为众人钉死了耶稣就弃绝他们，而是称他们为「弟兄们」，邀请他们悔改。福音的邀请，正是给那些亲手弃绝了基督的人的。

## 深读 3:22-26 像摩西的先知与亚伯拉罕的应许

**1. 像摩西的先知。** 「摩西曾说：『主神要从你们弟兄中间给你们兴起一位先知像我，凡他向你们所说的，你们都要听从。』」（3:22；申18:15）约翰福音6:14众人说耶稣「这真是那要到世间来的先知」，彼得在这里明说：那位先知就是耶稣。他接着警告：「凡不听从那先知的，必要从民中全然灭绝。」（3:23）这邀请是迫切的。

**2. 亚伯拉罕的应许。** 「你们是先知的子孙，也承受神与你们祖宗所立的约，就是对亚伯拉罕说：『地上万族都要因你的后裔得福。』」（3:25；参创12:3；22:18）神对亚伯拉罕的应许，从一开始就包括万族。这为后来福音传给外邦人埋下伏笔。

**3. 「先差他到你们这里来」。** 「神既兴起他的仆人，就先差他到你们这里来，赐福给你们，叫你们各人回转，离开罪恶。」（3:26）福音「先」给犹太人（参罗1:16），然后临到万族。而神所赐的福，就是「叫你们各人回转，离开罪恶」。最大的福不是金银，不是健康，而是罪得赦免、与神和好。

## 默想

**默想：** 那瘸腿的人天天在门口，只求几个钱，彼得给他的却是耶稣的名。我向神所求的，是否常常只是「金银」，而错过了他要给我的更大的福？彼得对亲手钉死耶稣的人说「弟兄们」，邀请他们悔改。我是否相信，即使是我最深的罪，也能被「涂抹」？我身边有没有一个「在门外」的人，是我可以带他认识耶稣的名的？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 is a miracle that leads to a sermon. **The healing at the Beautiful Gate** (vv. 1–10): a man lame from birth is raised up by Peter and enters the temple "walking and leaping and praising God." **The sermon in Solomon's Portico** (vv. 11–26): Peter explains that it wasn't he and John who healed the man but the name of Jesus. He proclaims that God has raised the Jesus they rejected, and calls them to repent and turn back.

Acts 2:43 says "many wonders and signs were being done through the apostles"; chapter 3 picks one and tells it in detail. This miracle isn't only one man's body being healed; it's also a picture: Jesus' name lets those kept outside the gate come in to worship God. In the next chapter this event provokes the first persecution.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–10 "I have no silver and gold"

**1. Carried to the gate every day.** "A man lame from birth was being carried, whom they laid daily at the gate of the temple that is called the Beautiful Gate to ask alms of those entering the temple" (3:2). He was over forty (4:22) and had spent his whole life outside the gate. All he could hope for was a few coins.

**2. "I have no silver and gold."** "But Peter said, 'I have no silver and gold, but what I do have I give to you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk!'" (3:6). Peter gave far more than the man asked for. The church may have no silver or gold, but it has something the world can't give: the name of Jesus.

**3. "In the name of."** In biblical thought a "name" stands for a person's identity, authority and presence. Peter isn't reciting a magic formula (19:13–16 records men who tried to use Jesus' name that way and were overpowered by an evil spirit). He's declaring that the risen Jesus himself is at work here.

**4. Leaping like a deer.** "And leaping up he stood and began to walk, and entered the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God" (3:8). Isaiah 35:6 describes the day God comes to save his people: "then shall the lame man leap like a deer." Luke lets readers see that the day the prophet spoke of has come. And the man doesn't just walk; he "entered the temple with them." A man who had spent his life outside the gate walks into the place of worship, perhaps for the first time (cf. the old saying in 2 Sam 5:8, "The blind and the lame shall not come into the house").

## Going Deeper: 3:11–16 "Why do you stare at us?"

**1. Refusing the credit.** As the crowd runs to see, Peter says, "why do you stare at us, as though by our own power or piety we have made him walk?" (3:12). Peter immediately turns attention away from himself. In Acts, whenever people try to give the glory to the apostles, they refuse it (10:25–26; 14:14–15).

**2. "His servant."** "The God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob... glorified his servant Jesus" (3:13). "Glorified his servant" recalls the Septuagint of Isaiah 52:13: "my servant... shall be lifted up and glorified." Isaiah 53, which follows, is about the suffering servant. Peter is saying: the Jesus you rejected is the servant Isaiah described, who suffered and then was glorified.

**3. A string of contrasts.** "But you denied the Holy and Righteous One, and asked for a murderer to be granted to you, and you killed the Author of life, whom God raised from the dead" (3:14–15). They asked for a man who took life (Barabbas) and killed the one who gives life. "Author of life" (*archēgos*) can also be rendered "leader" or "founder"; Hebrews uses the same word for Jesus as "the founder of their salvation" (Heb 2:10) and "the founder and perfecter of our faith" (Heb 12:2).

**4. Whose faith?** "And his name, by faith in his name, has made this man strong whom you see and know, and the faith that is through Jesus has given the man this perfect health in the presence of you all" (3:16). Peter stresses that this faith itself comes "through Jesus." The power to heal is in Jesus, and even the faith comes from him.

## Going Deeper: 3:17–21 "Repent therefore, and turn again"

**1. "In ignorance."** "And now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did also your rulers" (3:17). This echoes Jesus' prayer on the cross: "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34). Peter speaks gently and calls them "brothers." But ignorance isn't innocence; they still need to repent.

**2. God's plan fulfilled through human sin.** "But what God foretold by the mouth of all the prophets, that his Christ would suffer, he thus fulfilled" (3:18). As in 2:23, human evil didn't derail God's plan; it became the very means by which God accomplished salvation.

**3. Repent and turn, and sins are blotted out.** "Repent therefore, and turn again, that your sins may be blotted out" (3:19). "Blotted out" means wiped away, as God says: "I, I am he who blots out your transgressions for my own sake" (Isa 43:25). Repenting (changing one's mind) and turning again (turning around) are two sides of one movement. Notice that here forgiveness is tied to repenting and turning, with no mention of baptism (see the discussion of 2:38).

**4. Times of refreshing and the restoration of all things.** After repentance, "times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord, and... he may send the Christ appointed for you, Jesus, whom heaven must receive until the time for restoring all the things" (3:20–21). "Times of refreshing" may mean the spiritual renewal that comes with repentance now as well as its completion later; "restoring all the things" (the same root as "restore" in 1:6) is God's renewal of the whole creation when Christ returns. Its scope is "the things about which God spoke by the mouth of his holy prophets," not that everyone will finally be saved; 3:23 rules that out. This answers the disciples' question in 1:6: restoration will come, but it will be fully realized when Christ returns. Until then, heaven "must receive" him, and he sits at God's right hand.

> The point: Peter doesn't write off the crowd for crucifying Jesus. He calls them "brothers" and invites them to repent. The gospel invitation is offered precisely to those who rejected Christ with their own hands.

## Going Deeper: 3:22–26 The Prophet Like Moses and the Promise to Abraham

**1. The prophet like Moses.** "Moses said, 'The Lord God will raise up for you a prophet like me from your brothers. You shall listen to him in whatever he tells you'" (3:22; Deut 18:15). In John 6:14 the crowd said Jesus was "indeed the Prophet who is to come into the world." Here Peter says it plainly: that prophet is Jesus. And he adds a warning: "every soul who does not listen to that prophet shall be destroyed from the people" (3:23). The invitation is urgent.

**2. The promise to Abraham.** "You are the sons of the prophets and of the covenant that God made with your fathers, saying to Abraham, 'And in your offspring shall all the families of the earth be blessed'" (3:25; cf. Gen 12:3; 22:18). God's promise to Abraham included all peoples from the start. This lays the groundwork for the gospel going to the Gentiles later in the book.

**3. "To you first."** "God, having raised up his servant, sent him to you first, to bless you by turning every one of you from your wickedness" (3:26). The gospel goes "first" to the Jews (cf. Rom 1:16), then to all peoples. And the blessing God gives is "turning every one of you from your wickedness." The greatest blessing isn't silver or gold, or even health, but forgiveness of sins and peace with God.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The lame man sat at the gate every day asking for a few coins, and Peter gave him the name of Jesus. Do I often ask God only for "silver and gold," and miss the greater blessing he means to give me? Peter called the very people who crucified Jesus "brothers" and invited them to repent. Do I believe that even my deepest sin can be "blotted out"? Is there someone near me who is "outside the gate," whom I could help come to know the name of Jesus?
