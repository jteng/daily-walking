## 本章脉络

第四章是教会第一次面对逼迫。**被捉拿受审**（1-12节）：彼得、约翰因医治瘸腿的人、传讲耶稣复活而被押，在公会面前，彼得宣告「除他以外，别无拯救」。**公会的恐吓**（13-22节）：「这在神面前合理不合理，你们自己酌量吧！」**教会的祷告**（23-31节）：他们不是求平安，而是求胆量；祷告完了，聚会的地方震动。**一心一意的群体**（32-37节）：巴拿巴卖了田地，把银钱放在使徒脚前。

这一章的问题，是第三章留下的：「你们用什么能力，奉谁的名做这事呢？」（4:7）全章的答案只有一个：耶稣的名。

## 深读 4:1-12「除他以外，别无拯救」

**1. 为什么烦恼？** 祭司、守殿官和撒都该人「因他们教训百姓，本着耶稣，传说死人复活，就很烦恼」（4:2）。撒都该人不信死人复活（23:8），而使徒说的不只是一个理论，而是一个已经复活的人。同时，「听道之人有许多信的，男丁数目约到五千」（4:4）：逼迫开始的同一天，教会仍在增长。

**2. 被圣灵充满的回答。** 「那时彼得被圣灵充满，对他们说……」（4:8）几十天前，这个人在大祭司的院子里，连一个使女的问话都不敢承认（路22:56-57）；现在，他站在同一批领袖面前，直接说：这人得医治，是「因你们所钉十字架、神叫他从死里复活的拿撒勒人耶稣基督的名」（4:10）。这正是耶稣应许过的：「人带你们到会堂……面前，不要思虑怎么分诉……因为正在那时候，圣灵要指教你们当说的话。」（路12:11-12）

**3. 被弃的石头。** 「他是你们匠人所弃的石头，已成了房角的头块石头。」（4:11；诗118:22）彼得把诗篇的话直接加上「你们」：盖房子的匠人，就是坐在他面前的宗教领袖。耶稣自己也曾用这节经文（路20:17）。

**4. 「别无拯救」。** 「除他以外，别无拯救；因为在天下人间，没有赐下别的名，我们可以靠着得救。」（4:12）这节经文在一个讲求宽容的时代很刺耳，在华人「条条大路通罗马」「各宗教都是劝人为善」的观念里也一样。但彼得说这话的场合，正是一个人得了医治（「得救」和「得痊愈」在原文是同一个字根，4:9）。他不是在骄傲地排斥别人，而是在宣告一个事实：唯一能真正使人得救的，是那位被钉、复活的耶稣。正因为这是真的，才值得冒着坐监的危险去传。

> 要点：「别无拯救」不是傲慢的宣告，而是一个被救的人所能给出最慷慨的消息：有一个名，能救所有求告他的人。

## 深读 4:13-22「认明他们是跟过耶稣的」

**1. 「没有学问的小民」。** 「他们见彼得、约翰的胆量，又看出他们原是没有学问的小民，就希奇。」（4:13）「没有学问」不是说他们不识字，而是说他们没有受过拉比学校的正式训练；「小民」指平信徒、外行人。公会的人都是受过训练的专家，却在两个渔夫面前说不出话。

**2. 胆量的来源。** 「认明他们是跟过耶稣的。」（4:13）这是这一章里最美的一句话。公会的人在他们身上，认出了耶稣的影子：同样的胆量，同样对圣经的掌握（参约7:15「这个人没有学过，怎么明白书呢？」）。

**3. 「不能不说」。** 公会吩咐他们「总不可奉耶稣的名讲论教训人」（4:18）。彼得、约翰说：「听从你们，不听从神，这在神面前合理不合理，你们自己酌量吧！我们所看见所听见的，不能不说。」（4:19-20）他们不是故意反抗权柄，而是当人的命令和神的命令冲突时，他们必须听从神（参5:29）。「不能不说」，因为他们是见证人：见证人不能假装没有看见。

## 深读 4:23-31 求胆量的祷告

**1. 回到自己的人那里。** 「二人既被释放，就到会友那里去。」（4:23）受了威胁以后，他们第一件事是回到教会，一起祷告。

**2. 从神是谁开始。** 「主啊！你是造天、地、海，和其中万物的。」（4:24）「主」在这里是 *Despota*，意思是「至高的主宰」。他们的祷告不是从问题开始，而是从神的主权开始：面对公会的威胁，他们先想起，造天地的那一位才是真正掌权的。

**3. 用诗篇2篇读当下的处境。** 他们引用诗篇2:1-2：「外邦为什么争闹？……世上的君王一齐起来……要敌挡主，并主的受膏者。」然后说：「希律和本丢彼拉多，外邦人和以色列民，果然在这城里聚集，要攻打你所膏的圣仆耶稣。」（4:25-27）他们用圣经来理解自己正在经历的事：这不是意外，诗篇早就说过。

**4. 神的主权包括十字架。** 「成就你手和你意旨所预定必有的事。」（4:28）这是新约里关于神主权最强烈的说法之一：连杀害耶稣的阴谋，也在神的手中，成就了他预定的救恩。如果连十字架都在神的掌管之下，那么今天公会的恐吓，当然也在他手中。

**5. 求的不是平安，而是胆量。** 「他们恐吓我们，现在求主鉴察，一面叫你仆人大放胆量讲你的道。」（4:29）他们没有求神除去逼迫，而是求神给他们胆量，在逼迫中继续传道。「祷告完了，聚会的地方震动，他们就都被圣灵充满，放胆讲论神的道。」（4:31）

## 深读 4:32-37 一心一意与劝慰子

**1. 一心一意。** 「那许多信的人都是一心一意的，没有一人说他的东西有一样是自己的。」（4:32）这里的「凡物公用」，和2:44一样，是出于爱的自愿分享，不是制度（参5:4）。

**2. 恩典是源头。** 「使徒大有能力，见证主耶稣复活；众人也都蒙大恩。」（4:33）这一节夹在描写分享的两段经文中间，显出慷慨的源头：他们分享，是因为他们先蒙了大恩。「内中也没有一个缺乏的。」（4:34）这呼应申命记15:4神的心意：「必在你们中间没有穷人了。」

**3. 巴拿巴。** 「有一个利未人……名叫约瑟，使徒称他为巴拿巴（巴拿巴翻出来就是劝慰子）。」（4:36）路加在这里介绍巴拿巴，因为他后来会成为关键人物：是他把刚归主、人人害怕的扫罗带到使徒面前（9:27），也是他去安提阿找扫罗来一同事奉（11:25-26）。他的好名声，正与下一章亚拿尼亚和撒非喇的故事形成对比。

## 默想

**默想：** 彼得几十天前还不敢承认主，现在却在公会面前放胆作见证。改变他的，是复活的主和圣灵，不是他自己的勇气。在我觉得最胆怯的地方，我是否也能求主赐我同样的胆量？受了威胁的教会，第一件事是一同祷告，从「造天地的主」开始。今天让我害怕的事，在那位连十字架都掌管的神面前，看起来有什么不同？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 is the church's first encounter with persecution. **Arrest and trial** (vv. 1–12): Peter and John are arrested for healing the lame man and proclaiming the resurrection, and before the council Peter declares, "there is salvation in no one else." **The council's threats** (vv. 13–22): "Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, you must judge." **The church prays** (vv. 23–31): they ask not for safety but for boldness, and when they have prayed, the place is shaken. **A community of one heart** (vv. 32–37): Barnabas sells a field and lays the money at the apostles' feet.

The question running through this chapter is the one chapter 3 left: "By what power or by what name did you do this?" (4:7). The chapter has only one answer: the name of Jesus.

## Going Deeper: 4:1–12 "There is salvation in no one else"

**1. Why were they annoyed?** The priests, the captain of the temple and the Sadducees were "greatly annoyed because they were teaching the people and proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection from the dead" (4:2). The Sadducees didn't believe in resurrection (23:8), and the apostles weren't presenting a theory but a man who had risen. Meanwhile, "many of those who had heard the word believed, and the number of the men came to about five thousand" (4:4): on the very day persecution began, the church kept growing.

**2. An answer filled with the Spirit.** "Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them..." (4:8). A few weeks earlier this man couldn't own Jesus before a servant girl in the high priest's courtyard (Luke 22:56–57). Now he stands before the same leaders and says plainly that the man was healed "by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead" (4:10). This is what Jesus had promised: "when they bring you before the synagogues... do not be anxious about how you should defend yourself... for the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say" (Luke 12:11–12).

**3. The rejected stone.** "This Jesus is the stone that was rejected by you, the builders, which has become the cornerstone" (4:11; Ps 118:22). Peter adds "by you" to the psalm: the builders are the religious leaders sitting in front of him. Jesus himself used this verse (Luke 20:17).

**4. "No other name."** "And there is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved" (4:12). This verse grates in an age that prizes tolerance, as it does in the common Chinese saying that "all roads lead to Rome" and the belief that "all religions just teach people to be good." But Peter says it in the very setting of a man made well ("saved" and "healed" share a root in Greek, 4:9). He isn't proudly excluding others; he's announcing a fact: the only one who can truly save is the crucified and risen Jesus. Precisely because it's true, it's worth risking prison to proclaim.

> The point: "No other name" isn't an arrogant claim. It's the most generous news a rescued person can give: there is a name that saves everyone who calls on it.

## Going Deeper: 4:13–22 "They recognized that they had been with Jesus"

**1. "Uneducated, common men."** "Now when they saw the boldness of Peter and John, and perceived that they were uneducated, common men, they were astonished" (4:13). "Uneducated" doesn't mean illiterate; it means they had no formal rabbinic training. "Common" means laymen, non-specialists. The council were trained experts, yet they were silenced by two fishermen.

**2. Where the boldness came from.** "And they recognized that they had been with Jesus" (4:13). It's the most beautiful sentence in the chapter. The council saw in them a reflection of Jesus: the same boldness, the same grasp of Scripture (cf. John 7:15, "How is it that this man has learning, when he has never studied?").

**3. "We cannot but speak."** The council charged them "not to speak or teach at all in the name of Jesus" (4:18). Peter and John answered, "Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, you must judge, for we cannot but speak of what we have seen and heard" (4:19–20). They weren't defying authority for its own sake; when human commands conflict with God's, they must obey God (cf. 5:29). They "cannot but speak" because they are witnesses, and witnesses can't pretend they didn't see.

## Going Deeper: 4:23–31 A Prayer for Boldness

**1. Back to their own people.** "When they were released, they went to their friends" (4:23). After being threatened, the first thing they did was go back to the church and pray together.

**2. Starting with who God is.** "Sovereign Lord, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and everything in them" (4:24). "Sovereign Lord" here is *Despota*, "absolute master." Their prayer doesn't begin with the problem but with God's sovereignty: facing the council's threats, they first remember that the one who made heaven and earth is the one who truly rules.

**3. Reading the moment through Psalm 2.** They quote Psalm 2:1–2: "Why did the Gentiles rage... The kings of the earth set themselves... against the Lord and against his Anointed." Then they say, "for truly in this city there were gathered together against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, along with the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel" (4:25–27). They understand what they're going through by Scripture: this isn't an accident; the psalm already said it.

**4. God's sovereignty includes the cross.** "To do whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place" (4:28). This is one of the New Testament's strongest statements of God's sovereignty: even the plot to kill Jesus was in God's hand, accomplishing the salvation he had planned. If even the cross was under God's rule, then of course today's threats from the council are in his hand too.

**5. Asking not for safety but for boldness.** "And now, Lord, look upon their threats and grant to your servants to continue to speak your word with all boldness" (4:29). They didn't ask God to remove the persecution but to give them boldness to keep preaching in it. "And when they had prayed, the place in which they were gathered together was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and continued to speak the word of God with boldness" (4:31).

## Going Deeper: 4:32–37 One Heart and the Son of Encouragement

**1. One heart and soul.** "Now the full number of those who believed were of one heart and soul, and no one said that any of the things that belonged to him was his own" (4:32). As in 2:44, this sharing was love's free choice, not a system (cf. 5:4).

**2. Grace is the source.** "And with great power the apostles were giving their testimony to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace was upon them all" (4:33). This verse sits between the two descriptions of sharing and shows where the generosity came from: they shared because they had first received great grace. "There was not a needy person among them" (4:34), echoing God's intention in Deuteronomy 15:4: "there will be no poor among you."

**3. Barnabas.** "Thus Joseph, who was also called by the apostles Barnabas (which means son of encouragement), a Levite..." (4:36). Luke introduces Barnabas here because he will become a key figure. He brings the newly converted Saul, whom everyone feared, to the apostles (9:27), and he goes to find Saul and bring him to Antioch to minister together (11:25–26). His good name also sets up the contrast with Ananias and Sapphira in the next chapter.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** A few weeks earlier Peter didn't dare own the Lord; now he bears bold witness before the council. What changed him was the risen Lord and the Spirit, not his own courage. In the place where I feel most timid, can I ask the Lord for the same boldness? The threatened church's first act was to pray together, beginning with "the Lord who made heaven and earth." Before the God who ruled even over the cross, how does what frightens me today look different?
