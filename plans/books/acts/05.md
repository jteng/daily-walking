## 本章脉络

第五章里，教会同时面对内部和外部的危机。**亚拿尼亚和撒非喇**（1-11节）：一对夫妇假装奉献全部田价，结果双双倒毙。**神迹与增长**（12-16节）：信主的人越发增添。**再次被捕**（17-26节）：主的使者夜里开了监门。**在公会面前**（27-32节）：「顺从神，不顺从人，是应当的。」**迦玛列的劝告**（33-39节）。**受辱而欢喜**（40-42节）：使徒被打，「心里欢喜，因被算是配为这名受辱」。

第四章结尾，巴拿巴真心地把田价放在使徒脚前；第五章开头，亚拿尼亚和撒非喇也把钱放在使徒脚前，却是假的。路加把两件事放在一起，让我们看见：教会最大的危险，不只来自外面的逼迫，也来自里面的虚假。

## 深读 5:1-11 亚拿尼亚和撒非喇

**1. 罪不在留下钱，而在假装。** 彼得说得很清楚：「田地还没有卖，不是你自己的吗？既卖了，价银不是你作主吗？」（5:4）他们完全可以留下一部分，没有人要求他们全部奉献。他们的罪，是假装全部奉献，看来是想得到像巴拿巴那样的名声。这是在神面前演戏。

**2. 亚干的回声。** 「把价银私自留下几分」（5:2）的「私自留下」，是一个少见的希腊动词（新约另外只在多2:10出现，译作「私拿」）。七十士译本在约书亚记7:1用同一个字描写亚干在耶利哥城「取了当灭的物」。以色列刚进迦南，就因亚干的罪受挫；教会刚开始，也面对同样的试探。神在一个新的开始，特别显明他的圣洁。

**3. 欺哄圣灵，就是欺哄神。** 「为什么撒但充满了你的心，叫你欺哄圣灵……你不是欺哄人，是欺哄神了。」（5:3-4）彼得在同一段话里，先说「欺哄圣灵」，再说「欺哄神」。这是新约中显明圣灵是神的重要经文之一：圣灵不是一种力量，而是有位格的神，可以被欺哄、被试探（5:9）；却又与父有别：他是神所「赐给」的（5:32）。

**4. 「都甚惧怕」。** 「全教会和听见这事的人都甚惧怕。」（5:11）这是使徒行传第一次清楚地用「教会」（*ekklēsia*）这个词。这件事很难读，因为审判来得这么快、这么重。但它提醒我们：恩典不是轻忽罪的理由。这审判显明一切的罪本当受的结局；教会能存活，只因为基督在木头上替我们承受了这审判（5:30）。路加没有说他们是否永远灭亡，我们也不必揣测。神的百姓是圣灵的殿，神是圣洁的。初期教会并没有因此变得冷漠，反而更敬畏神，而且「信而归主的人越发增添」（5:14）。

> 要点：教会的力量不在于看起来多么属灵，而在于真实。神宁愿我们诚实地给出一部分，也不要我们虚假地假装给了全部。

## 深读 5:12-16 彼得的影儿

「甚至有人将病人抬到街上……指望彼得过来的时候，或者得他的影儿照在什么人身上。」（5:15）这是叙事记载，描写当时众人的盼望和神迹的丰盛；路加接着说「全都得了医治」（5:16）。但他没有说影子本身有能力，而是说神迹是「藉使徒的手」行的（5:12；和合本加了「主」字，原文没有，却正合使徒自己的见证：医治是出于神，3:12、16；4:30）。读的时候要分辨：医治的是主，不是彼得的影子，也不是任何物件。

## 深读 5:17-32「顺从神，不顺从人，是应当的」

**1. 打开的监门。** 主的使者夜里开了监门，吩咐他们：「你们去站在殿里，把这生命的道都讲给百姓听。」（5:20）天使救他们出来，不是叫他们逃跑，而是叫他们回到最危险的地方继续传道。

**2. 公会的指控。** 「我们不是严严地禁止你们，不可奉这名教训人吗？你们倒……想要叫这人的血归到我们身上！」（5:28）讽刺的是，在耶稣受审时，众人曾喊：「他的血归到我们和我们的子孙身上。」（太27:25）

**3. 顺从神。** 「顺从神，不顺从人，是应当的。」（5:29）这不是鼓励基督徒随便违抗政府。新约也教导要顺服掌权的（罗13:1-7；彼前2:13-17）。但当人的命令直接要求我们违背神的命令时，基督徒的效忠是清楚的。对许多华人信徒来说，这也可能是在家族中、在工作中，当传统或上司的要求与神的话冲突时，要做的选择。

**4. 挂在木头上。** 「你们挂在木头上杀害的耶稣，我们祖宗的神已经叫他复活。」（5:30）「挂在木头上」回响申命记21:23：「被挂的人是在神面前受咒诅的。」保罗后来解释这个意义：「基督既为我们受了咒诅，就赎出我们脱离律法的咒诅。」（加3:13）耶稣在木头上承受了我们该受的咒诅。

**5. 悔改也是恩赐。** 「神且用右手将他高举，叫他作君王，作救主，将悔改的心和赦罪的恩赐给以色列人。」（5:31）注意：连悔改的心都是恩赐：神高举基督作君王、作救主，为要把悔改和赦罪赐下（参11:18）。

## 深读 5:33-39 迦玛列的劝告

**1. 一位受敬重的老师。** 迦玛列是当时最受敬重的法利赛人教师之一，也是保罗的老师（22:3）。他劝公会：「他们所谋的、所行的，若是出于人，必要败坏；若是出于神，你们就不能败坏他们，恐怕你们倒是攻击神了。」（5:38-39）

**2. 这个劝告的限度。** 迦玛列的话很有智慧，神也借着它保护了使徒。但这是一个观望的立场，不是信心：「看结果再说」不是判断真理的可靠标准，因为有些错误的运动也会兴旺一时。路加没有记载迦玛列因此信主。

**3. 一个年代问题。** 迦玛列提到「从前杜达起来」（5:36）。约瑟夫记载的杜达在巡抚法督任内（约主后44-46年），比迦玛列说这话还晚，也远在加利利的犹大（主后6年报名上册时）之后；迦玛列却说犹大在杜达「此后」（5:37）。有人因此认为路加弄错了。但约瑟夫也说，希律王死后（主前4年）犹太地叛乱首领四起，多数没有留下名字；迦玛列说的「约有四百」人，也和约瑟夫所记的大批群众不同；约瑟夫本人的年代也并非毫无差错。所以许多福音派解经家认为，迦玛列说的是那段时期另一个更早的杜达。这问题目前无法完全确定，但路加在其他细节上已被证实的准确，值得我们重视。

## 深读 5:40-42「配为这名受辱」

**1. 被打以后。** 公会「就叫使徒来，把他们打了，又吩咐他们不可奉耶稣的名讲道，就把他们释放了」（5:40）。「打」很可能是犹太人的鞭刑，四十减去一下（申25:3；林后11:24）。

**2. 心里欢喜。** 「他们离开公会，心里欢喜，因被算是配为这名受辱。」（5:41）这是全章最惊人的一句话。他们欢喜，不是因为喜欢受苦，而是因为被算为「配」与耶稣站在一起。耶稣早就说过：「人若因我辱骂你们……你们就有福了！应当欢喜快乐，因为你们在天上的赏赐是大的。」（太5:11-12）

**3. 不住地传。** 「他们就每日在殿里、在家里不住地教训人，传耶稣是基督。」（5:42）公会的禁令和鞭打，换来的是更多的传讲：在殿里公开传，在家里一家一家地传。

## 默想

**默想：** 亚拿尼亚和撒非喇想要的，是属灵的好名声。我在教会里，有没有哪一处在「演」给别人看？神宁愿我诚实地承认自己的有限。使徒受了鞭打，却因「配为这名受辱」而欢喜。耶稣在木头上为我受了咒诅，当我因信他而受到冷眼或损失时，我能否把它看作与他站在一起的荣幸？

## The Shape of the Chapter

In chapter 5 the church faces crises from inside and outside at once. **Ananias and Sapphira** (vv. 1–11): a couple pretend to give the whole price of a field, and both fall dead. **Signs and growth** (vv. 12–16): more and more believers are added. **Arrested again** (vv. 17–26): an angel of the Lord opens the prison doors at night. **Before the council** (vv. 27–32): "We must obey God rather than men." **Gamaliel's counsel** (vv. 33–39). **Rejoicing in dishonor** (vv. 40–42): the apostles are beaten and leave "rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the name."

At the end of chapter 4, Barnabas sincerely laid the price of his field at the apostles' feet. At the start of chapter 5, Ananias and Sapphira also lay money at the apostles' feet, but falsely. Luke sets the two side by side to show that the church's greatest danger comes not only from persecution outside but from pretense inside.

## Going Deeper: 5:1–11 Ananias and Sapphira

**1. The sin wasn't keeping money but pretending.** Peter is clear: "While it remained unsold, did it not remain your own? And after it was sold, was it not at your disposal?" (5:4). They were entirely free to keep part; no one required them to give it all. Their sin was pretending to give everything, apparently to win a reputation like Barnabas's. It was putting on a show before God.

**2. An echo of Achan.** "Kept back for himself some of the proceeds" (5:2): "kept back" is a rare Greek verb (its only other New Testament use is Titus 2:10, "pilfering"). The Septuagint uses the same word in Joshua 7:1 for Achan taking devoted things at Jericho. Israel stumbled through Achan's sin just after entering Canaan; the church faces the same temptation just as it begins. At a new beginning, God especially displays his holiness.

**3. Lying to the Spirit is lying to God.** "Why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit... You have not lied to men but to God" (5:3–4). In the same breath Peter says "lie to the Holy Spirit" and then "lied to God." This is one of the key New Testament texts showing that the Spirit is God: not a force but a personal God who can be lied to and tested (5:9), yet distinct from the Father, who "has given" him (5:32).

**4. "Great fear."** "And great fear came upon the whole church and upon all who heard of these things" (5:11). This is the first clear use in Acts of the word "church" (*ekklēsia*). The story is hard to read, because judgment came so quickly and so heavily. But it reminds us that grace is no excuse for taking sin lightly. This judgment shows what all sin deserves; the church lives only because Christ bore that judgment for us on the tree (5:30). Luke doesn't say whether they were eternally lost, and we needn't speculate. God's people are the temple of the Spirit, and God is holy. The early church didn't grow cold as a result; it grew in reverence, and "more than ever believers were added to the Lord" (5:14).

> The point: The church's strength lies not in how spiritual it looks but in how real it is. God would rather we honestly give a part than falsely pretend to give it all.

## Going Deeper: 5:12–16 Peter's Shadow

"So that they even carried out the sick into the streets... that as Peter came by at least his shadow might fall on some of them" (5:15). This is narrative, describing the people's hope and the abundance of signs at the time; Luke adds that "they were all healed" (5:16). But he doesn't say the shadow itself had power; he says the signs were done "by the hands of the apostles" (5:12), and the apostles themselves insisted that healing comes from God (3:12, 16; 4:30). As we read, we should discern that it is the Lord who heals, not Peter's shadow or any object.

## Going Deeper: 5:17–32 "We must obey God rather than men"

**1. The opened prison doors.** An angel of the Lord opens the prison doors at night and tells them, "Go and stand in the temple and speak to the people all the words of this Life" (5:20). The angel frees them not so they can escape but so they can go back to the most dangerous place and keep preaching.

**2. The council's charge.** "We strictly charged you not to teach in this name, yet here you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching, and you intend to bring this man's blood upon us" (5:28). The irony is that at Jesus' trial the crowd had cried, "His blood be on us and on our children!" (Matt 27:25).

**3. Obeying God.** "We must obey God rather than men" (5:29). This doesn't encourage Christians to defy government casually; the New Testament also teaches submission to governing authorities (Rom 13:1–7; 1 Pet 2:13–17). But when a human command directly requires us to disobey God's command, a Christian's allegiance is clear. For many Chinese believers, this may also be the choice they face within the family or at work, when tradition or a superior's demand conflicts with God's word.

**4. Hanging on a tree.** "The God of our fathers raised Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree" (5:30). "Hanging on a tree" echoes Deuteronomy 21:23: "a hanged man is cursed by God." Paul later explains its meaning: "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us" (Gal 3:13). On the tree Jesus bore the curse we deserved.

**5. Repentance is a gift too.** "God exalted him at his right hand as Leader and Savior, to give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins" (5:31). Notice that even repentance is a gift: God exalted Christ as Leader and Savior in order to give repentance and forgiveness (cf. 11:18).

## Going Deeper: 5:33–39 Gamaliel's Counsel

**1. A respected teacher.** Gamaliel was one of the most respected Pharisee teachers of the day, and Paul's teacher (22:3). He counsels the council: "if this plan or this undertaking is of man, it will fail; but if it is of God, you will not be able to overthrow them. You might even be found opposing God!" (5:38–39).

**2. The limits of this advice.** Gamaliel's words were wise, and God used them to protect the apostles. But his stance is wait-and-see, not faith. "Let's see how it turns out" isn't a reliable test of truth, since some false movements also flourish for a time. Luke gives no sign that Gamaliel came to faith.

**3. A question of dates.** Gamaliel mentions that "before these days Theudas rose up" (5:36). Josephus records a Theudas under the governor Fadus (c. AD 44–46), later than Gamaliel's speech and long after Judas the Galilean (AD 6), whom Gamaliel places *after* Theudas (5:37). Some conclude Luke erred. But Josephus says that after Herod's death (4 BC) Judea was full of rebel leaders he does not name, Gamaliel's "about four hundred" doesn't match Josephus's large following, and Josephus is himself not free of chronological slips. So many evangelical interpreters think Gamaliel refers to an earlier Theudas from those years. The question can't be settled with certainty, but Luke's proven accuracy elsewhere counts in his favor.

## Going Deeper: 5:40–42 "Counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the name"

**1. After the beating.** The council "called in the apostles, they beat them and charged them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and let them go" (5:40). The beating was likely the Jewish flogging of forty lashes less one (Deut 25:3; 2 Cor 11:24).

**2. Rejoicing.** "Then they left the presence of the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the name" (5:41). This is the most astonishing sentence in the chapter. They rejoiced not because they liked suffering but because they were counted "worthy" to stand with Jesus. Jesus had said, "Blessed are you when others revile you... on my account. Rejoice and be glad, for your reward is great in heaven" (Matt 5:11–12).

**3. They did not stop.** "And every day, in the temple and from house to house, they did not cease teaching and preaching Jesus as the Christ" (5:42). The council's ban and beating produced more preaching: openly in the temple, and from house to house.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** What Ananias and Sapphira wanted was a spiritual reputation. Is there somewhere in church where I'm "performing" for others? God would rather I honestly admit my limits. The apostles were flogged yet rejoiced to be "counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the name." Jesus bore the curse on the tree for me. When believing in him costs me a cold shoulder or a loss, can I see it as the honor of standing with him?
