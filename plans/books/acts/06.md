## 本章脉络

第六章篇幅不长，却是一个转折点。**饭食的纷争**（1-7节）：说希腊话的犹太信徒抱怨，他们的寡妇在每天的供给上被忽略了；使徒请众人选出七个人来管理这事，「神的道兴旺起来」。**司提反被诬告**（8-15节）：七人之一的司提反大有能力，对手辩不过他，就设下假见证，把他带到公会；众人看他的面貌，「好像天使的面貌」。

前两章的危机，一个来自外面的逼迫（第4章），一个来自里面的虚假（第5章）；第六章的危机，是群体内部的分裂。而这一章选出的人当中，司提反和腓利，将要把福音带出耶路撒冷（第7、8章）。

## 深读 6:1-4 管理饭食与祈祷传道

**1. 两种犹太信徒。** 「有说希腊话的犹太人向希伯来人发怨言，因为在天天的供给上忽略了他们的寡妇。」（6:1）「说希腊话的犹太人」多半是在海外长大、后来回到耶路撒冷的犹太人；「希伯来人」是本地说亚兰话的犹太人。语言、文化和背景的不同，使一部分人被忽略了。这在今天的华人教会里也很常见：说国语的和说粤语的、本地的和新移民、第一代和在海外长大的第二代。

**2. 寡妇是谁的责任？** 旧约一再吩咐神的百姓要照顾孤儿寡妇（申14:29；24:19-21）。初期教会延续了这个传统，「天天」供给有需要的人。被忽略的寡妇，不是小事。

**3. 同一个字，两种服事。** 「天天的供给」的「供给」，原文是 *diakonia*（服事）；使徒说「我们要专心以祈祷、传道为事」，「传道」的原文是「道的服事」（*diakonia tou logou*，6:4）。管理饭食和传讲神的道，用的是同一个字：都是服事。使徒不是说管饭食比较低等，而是说他们被呼召的服事，是专心祈祷和传道；照顾寡妇同样重要，需要另外有人专门负责。

**4. 不合宜的不是管饭食，而是撇下神的道。** 「我们撇下神的道去管理饭食，原是不合宜的。」（6:2）使徒看见，如果他们把所有时间都放在行政上，教会就会失去祈祷和神的话这两样根基。

> 要点：使徒没有说照顾寡妇不重要，也没有说传道比较高贵。他们看见，教会需要不同的人，用不同的恩赐，去完成同一位主所交托的服事。

## 深读 6:3-7 七个人

**1. 资格。** 「当从你们中间选出七个有好名声、被圣灵充满、智慧充足的人。」（6:3）这是管理饭食的工作，资格却是属灵的：好名声、被圣灵充满、有智慧。在神的家里，没有「只是行政」的工作。

**2. 七个希腊名字。** 被选出的七个人，名字都是希腊名字（6:5），最后一位尼哥拉还是「进教的安提阿人」，也就是归信犹太教的外邦人。这很可能表示，他们大多来自说希腊话的群体，正是被忽略的那一方。多数人把照顾的责任交给了少数群体自己的领袖：这是在合一中显出的谦卑和信任。

**3. 他们是执事吗？** 这里没有出现「执事」（*diakonos*）这个名词，但许多人认为这七个人是后来教会执事职分的雏形（参提前3:8-13）。不论如何，他们的服事很快就超出了管理饭食：司提反大有能力地传道，腓利成了传福音的（21:8）。

**4. 按手。** 「使徒祷告了，就按手在他们头上。」（6:6）按手在旧约里表示认同、托付，例如摩西按手在约书亚头上，把职分交给他（民27:18-23）。这是公开的委任。

**5. 道兴旺。** 「神的道兴旺起来；在耶路撒冷门徒数目加增的甚多，也有许多祭司信从了这道。」（6:7）这是使徒行传的第一个「进展报告」（参导读）。内部的纷争以谦卑和智慧处理之后，福音更兴旺了，连祭司也信了。

## 深读 6:8-15 司提反被诬告

**1. 满有恩惠和能力。** 「司提反满得恩惠、能力，在民间行了大奇事和神迹。」（6:8）这个被选来管理饭食的人，也是一位大有能力的传道人。

**2. 辩论的对手。** 和司提反辩论的，是「利百地拿会堂」（意思是「自由人的会堂」，可能是从前作奴隶、后来获释的犹太人的后代）和来自古利奈、亚历山大、基利家、亚细亚的人（6:9）。基利家的首府是大数，就是扫罗的家乡；有学者推测，年轻的扫罗可能就在这些辩论者当中（参7:58）。

**3. 和耶稣一样的指控。** 他们辩不过他，「就买出人来」，「设下假见证，说：『这个人说话，不住地糟践圣所和律法。我们曾听见他说，这拿撒勒人耶稣要毁坏此地。』」（6:11-14）这和耶稣受审时的假见证几乎一样（可14:57-58）。门徒正走在他们的主走过的路上。

**4. 好像天使的面貌。** 「在公会里坐着的人都定睛看他，见他的面貌，好像天使的面貌。」（6:15）这里有一个深刻的反讽：他被控告「说谤摩西」，他的脸却发光，好像摩西从西奈山下来、与神说话以后面皮发光一样（出34:29-35）。被控告亵渎律法的人，脸上带着与神同在的光。

## 默想

**默想：** 初期教会的纷争，起因是有人被「忽略」了。在我的教会或团契里，有没有因为语言、背景或年龄不同而被忽略的人？使徒没有因为传道「比较重要」而轻看饭食的服事，也没有因为饭食的需要而撇下神的道。在我的服事中，哪一样是神要我专心的？我能不能欢喜地让别人在别的服事上发光？司提反被诬告时，脸上反而有神同在的光。我在被误解的时候，是否也能把眼目放在主身上？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 is short but marks a turning point. **A dispute over food** (vv. 1–7): Greek-speaking Jewish believers complain that their widows are being overlooked in the daily distribution; the apostles ask the church to choose seven men to oversee it, and "the word of God continued to increase." **Stephen falsely accused** (vv. 8–15): Stephen, one of the seven, is full of power; his opponents can't out-argue him, so they set up false witnesses and bring him before the council, where they see that "his face was like the face of an angel."

The crises of the previous chapters came from persecution outside (ch. 4) and pretense inside (ch. 5); this chapter's crisis is division within the community. And among the men chosen here, Stephen and Philip will carry the gospel out of Jerusalem (chs. 7–8).

## Going Deeper: 6:1–4 Serving Tables and the Ministry of the Word

**1. Two kinds of Jewish believers.** "A complaint by the Hellenists arose against the Hebrews because their widows were being neglected in the daily distribution" (6:1). The "Hellenists" were mostly Jews raised abroad who had moved to Jerusalem; the "Hebrews" were local, Aramaic-speaking Jews. Differences of language, culture and background meant some were overlooked. This is common in Chinese churches today too: Mandarin and Cantonese speakers, locals and new immigrants, the first generation and a second generation raised overseas.

**2. Whose responsibility are widows?** The Old Testament repeatedly told God's people to care for orphans and widows (Deut 14:29; 24:19–21). The early church continued this, providing for those in need "daily." Overlooked widows were no small matter.

**3. One word, two kinds of service.** "Distribution" in "daily distribution" is *diakonia* ("service") in Greek, and when the apostles say "we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word," "ministry" is the same word: "service of the word" (*diakonia tou logou*, 6:4). Serving tables and preaching the word share one word: both are service. The apostles aren't saying that serving food is lower; they're saying their calling is to devote themselves to prayer and the word, while caring for widows is just as important and needs others dedicated to it.

**4. What wasn't right was neglecting the word, not serving food.** "It is not right that we should give up preaching the word of God to serve tables" (6:2). The apostles saw that if they spent all their time on administration, the church would lose its two foundations: prayer and the word of God.

> The point: The apostles didn't say caring for widows was unimportant, or that preaching was nobler. They saw that the church needs different people with different gifts to carry out the one service the one Lord has entrusted to it.

## Going Deeper: 6:3–7 The Seven

**1. The qualifications.** "Pick out from among you seven men of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom" (6:3). The job was overseeing food, yet the qualifications were spiritual: good repute, full of the Spirit, wise. In God's house there is no "merely administrative" work.

**2. Seven Greek names.** All seven chosen have Greek names (6:5), and the last, Nicolaus, was "a proselyte of Antioch," a Gentile convert to Judaism. This very likely means most came from the Greek-speaking group, the side that had been overlooked. The majority entrusted the care to leaders from the minority group itself: humility and trust shown in unity.

**3. Were they deacons?** The noun "deacon" (*diakonos*) doesn't appear here, but many see these seven as the forerunners of the later office of deacon (cf. 1 Tim 3:8–13). Either way, their service soon went beyond serving food: Stephen preached with power, and Philip became "the evangelist" (21:8).

**4. Laying on of hands.** "They prayed and laid their hands on them" (6:6). In the Old Testament, laying on hands signified identification and commissioning, as when Moses laid his hands on Joshua to hand over his charge (Num 27:18–23). It was a public appointment.

**5. The word increased.** "And the word of God continued to increase, and the number of the disciples multiplied greatly in Jerusalem, and a great many of the priests became obedient to the faith" (6:7). This is the first of Acts' "progress reports" (see the introduction). Once the internal dispute was handled with humility and wisdom, the gospel spread all the more; even priests believed.

## Going Deeper: 6:8–15 Stephen Falsely Accused

**1. Full of grace and power.** "And Stephen, full of grace and power, was doing great wonders and signs among the people" (6:8). The man chosen to oversee food was also a powerful preacher.

**2. His opponents.** Stephen's opponents were from "the synagogue of the Freedmen" (likely descendants of Jews once enslaved and later freed) and from Cyrene, Alexandria, Cilicia and Asia (6:9). The capital of Cilicia was Tarsus, Saul's hometown; some scholars suggest the young Saul may have been among these debaters (cf. 7:58).

**3. The same charges as against Jesus.** Unable to out-argue him, "they secretly instigated men" and "set up false witnesses who said, 'This man never ceases to speak words against this holy place and the law, for we have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place'" (6:11–14). These are almost the same false charges brought at Jesus' trial (Mark 14:57–58). The disciples are walking the road their Lord walked.

**4. The face of an angel.** "And gazing at him, all who sat in the council saw that his face was like the face of an angel" (6:15). There's a deep irony here: he's accused of speaking "against Moses," yet his face shines like Moses' face when he came down from Sinai after speaking with God (Ex 34:29–35). The man accused of blaspheming the law bears on his face the light of God's presence.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The early church's dispute began because some people were "overlooked." In my church or fellowship, is anyone overlooked because of a different language, background or age? The apostles didn't despise serving tables because preaching was "more important," nor neglect the word because of the need for food. In my service, what is God calling me to give myself to? Can I gladly let others shine in other kinds of service? Falsely accused, Stephen's face shone with God's presence. When I'm misunderstood, can I keep my eyes on the Lord?
