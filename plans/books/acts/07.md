## 本章脉络

第七章是使徒行传里最长的一篇讲论。司提反被控告「糟践圣所和律法」（6:13），大祭司问他：「这些事果然有吗？」（7:1）他的回答不是直接为自己辩护，而是把以色列的历史从头讲一遍：**亚伯拉罕**（2-8节）、**约瑟**（9-16节）、**摩西**（17-43节）、**帐幕与圣殿**（44-50节），最后是一段严厉的**控诉**（51-53节）。听的人恼怒，把他推到城外用石头打死；他临死时看见「人子站在神的右边」，并为杀他的人祷告（54-60节）。

这篇讲论贯穿着两条线。第一条：**神从不被关在一个地方。** 神在美索不达米亚向亚伯拉罕显现，在埃及与约瑟同在，在西奈旷野的荆棘中向摩西说话：这些地方都不是「圣地」耶路撒冷。第二条：**以色列一再弃绝神所差来的拯救者。** 约瑟被弟兄卖了，摩西被百姓推开，众先知被逼迫，如今「那义者」也被他们杀了。

## 深读 7:2-16 亚伯拉罕与约瑟：神在应许之地以外

**1. 荣耀的神在外邦显现。** 「当日我们的祖宗亚伯拉罕在美索不达米亚还未住哈兰的时候，荣耀的神向他显现。」（7:2）司提反的第一句话就点出主题：神的荣耀不是从耶路撒冷开始的。亚伯拉罕蒙召的时候，还在拜偶像的迦勒底人之地（参书24:2）。

**2. 连立足之地也没有。** 「在这地方，神并没有给他产业，连立足之地也没有给他；但应许要将这地赐给他和他的后裔为业；那时他还没有儿子。」（7:5）亚伯拉罕一生活在应许里，不是活在拥有里。他没有地，也还没有儿子，只有神的话。这正是信心的样式（来11:8-10）。

**3. 弟兄的嫉妒，神的同在。** 「先祖嫉妒约瑟，把他卖到埃及去；神却与他同在。」（7:9）这里第一次出现整篇讲论的模式：神所拣选的拯救者，先被自己的弟兄弃绝；神却与他同在，「救他脱离一切苦难」（7:10），后来他成了救全家的人。

**4. 一个难解的细节。** 司提反说雅各的众祖宗葬在「亚伯拉罕在示剑用银子从哈抹子孙买来的坟墓里」（7:16）。按创世记，亚伯拉罕买的是希伯仑的麦比拉洞（创23章），在示剑向哈抹子孙买地的是雅各（创33:19；书24:32）。有人认为司提反把两件事浓缩在一起讲，有人认为这是当时流传的说法，也有人认为「亚伯拉罕」在这里代表整个先祖家族。无论如何，路加忠实记录了司提反的话；这些细节不影响讲论的要旨。

> 要点：神的同在从来不受地界限制。亚伯拉罕没有立足之地，约瑟被卖到外邦，神却在那里与他们同在。被控告「糟践圣所」的司提反，是在提醒听众：神从来不只住在一个地方。

## 深读 7:17-43 摩西：被弃绝的拯救者

**1. 以为弟兄必明白。** 「他以为弟兄必明白神是藉他的手搭救他们；他们却不明白。」（7:25）摩西四十岁时出来看望弟兄，却被推开：「谁立你作我们的首领和审判官呢？」（7:27）这就是讲论的第二个例子：神所差来的拯救者，先被自己的百姓弃绝。

**2. 圣地在旷野里。** 「过了四十年，在西奈山的旷野，有一位天使从荆棘火焰中向摩西显现。」（7:30）主对他说：「把你脚上的鞋脱下来，因为你所站之地是圣地。」（7:33）请注意这个对比：控告司提反的人在维护「这圣所」（6:13），司提反却提醒他们，神第一次称一块地为「圣地」，是在离耶路撒冷很远的旷野里。使地成圣的，是神的同在，不是地点本身。

**3. 被弃绝的，正是神所立的。** 「这摩西就是百姓弃绝说『谁立你作我们的首领和审判官』的；神却藉那在荆棘中显现之使者的手差派他作首领、作救赎的。」（7:35）百姓问「谁立你」，神的回答是：「我立的。」这句话的分量在于，听众要自己联想到耶稣：他们也弃绝了神所立的那一位（参2:36；4:11）。

**4. 「一位先知像我。」** 司提反特别提到，摩西曾说：「神要从你们弟兄中间给你们兴起一位先知像我。」（7:37；申18:15）彼得已经把这句话应用在耶稣身上（3:22）。被控告「说谤摩西」（6:11）的司提反，正是在用摩西的话指向基督。真正尊重摩西的，是听从摩西所指向的那一位。

**5. 心里归向埃及。** 「我们的祖宗不肯听从，反弃绝他，心里归向埃及。」（7:39）他们造了金牛犊，「欢喜自己手中的工作」（7:41）。「手中的工作」这个说法很重要，几节之后，司提反会说至高者「并不住人手所造的」（7:48）。偶像是人手所造的；人若把圣殿当成可以掌控神的地方，圣殿也会变成「手中的工作」。

> 要点：以色列的历史有一个反复出现的模式：神差来拯救者，百姓先弃绝他。约瑟如此，摩西如此，众先知如此，耶稣也是如此。司提反讲的是历史，指的是眼前的听众。

## 深读 7:44-50 帐幕与圣殿

**1. 照所看见的样式。** 「我们的祖宗在旷野，有法柜的帐幕，是神吩咐摩西叫他照所看见的样式做的。」（7:44）帐幕是神亲自吩咐的，而且可以移动，跟着百姓走。

**2. 大卫祈求，所罗门建造。** 大卫「祈求为雅各的神预备居所；却是所罗门为神造成殿宇」（7:46-47）。司提反没有说建殿是错的；圣殿本是神所允许、所悦纳的（王上8章）。他要指出的是下一句。

**3. 至高者并不住人手所造的。** 「其实，至高者并不住人手所造的，就如先知所言：主说：天是我的座位，地是我的脚凳；你们要为我造何等的殿宇？哪里是我安息的地方呢？这一切不都是我手所造的吗？」（7:48-50；赛66:1-2）所罗门献殿时自己也说过：「天和天上的天尚且不足你居住的，何况我所建的这殿呢？」（王上8:27）问题不在圣殿，而在把圣殿当作把神关在里面、由人掌管的地方。

**4. 真正的殿。** 耶稣曾说：「你们拆毁这殿，我三日内要再建立起来。」约翰告诉我们，「耶稣这话是以他的身体为殿」（约2:19-21）。司提反被控告说耶稣「要毁坏此地」（6:14）；其实耶稣自己就是神与人相会的真殿。从此，神的同在要藉着圣灵住在信他的人中间（林前3:16），一直到地极。

> 要点：司提反没有反对圣殿，他反对的是把神限制在圣殿里的心态。神比人所建的一切都大，他亲自在基督里来到人中间。

## 深读 7:51-60 控诉、异象与饶恕

**1. 「你们的祖宗怎样，你们也怎样。」** 「你们这硬着颈项、心与耳未受割礼的人，常时抗拒圣灵！你们的祖宗怎样，你们也怎样。」（7:51）这段话很严厉，却和旧约先知的语言一致（出33:5；耶9:26）。「心与耳未受割礼」是说：他们身上有立约的记号，心却没有归向神。

**2. 那义者。** 「哪一个先知不是你们祖宗逼迫呢？他们也把预先传说那义者要来的人杀了；如今你们又把那义者卖了，杀了。」（7:52）整篇讲论到这里达到高潮：约瑟、摩西、众先知的被弃绝，都指向「那义者」耶稣的被弃绝。

**3. 人子站着。** 「但司提反被圣灵充满，定睛望天，看见神的荣耀，又看见耶稣站在神的右边。」（7:55）在四福音以外，这是新约里唯一一次有人直接称耶稣为「人子」（7:56；启示录1:13只说「好像人子」）。耶稣受审时曾说：「从今以后，人子要坐在神权能的右边。」（路22:69）司提反却看见他「站着」。解经家有几种理解：站起来迎接他的见证人；作为辩护者站起来为他作证（参路12:8）；或作为审判者站起来。不论哪一种，地上的公会定了司提反的罪，天上的人子却为他站起来。

**4. 像他的主一样死去。** 「求主耶稣接收我的灵魂！」「主啊，不要将这罪归于他们！」（7:59-60）这两句话几乎就是耶稣在十字架上的话：「父啊！我将我的灵魂交在你手里。」（路23:46）「父啊！赦免他们；因为他们所做的，他们不晓得。」（路23:34）只是司提反向「主耶稣」祷告，把耶稣放在父的位置上。第一位殉道者的祷告，就清楚承认耶稣是神。

**5. 就睡了。** 「说了这话，就睡了。」（7:60）被石头打死，路加却说「睡了」。对信主的人，死亡已经失去了它最后的权势（帖前4:13-14）。

**6. 一个名叫扫罗的少年人。** 「作见证的人把衣裳放在一个少年人名叫扫罗的脚前。」（7:58）「扫罗也喜悦他被害。」（7:60，和合本；英文译本列在8:1）路加在最黑暗的时刻，悄悄把下一个主角带进来。很多年后，保罗自己回想：「你的见证人司提反被害流血的时候，我也站在旁边欢喜。」（22:20）司提反为杀他的人祷告，这祷告的第一个答案，或许就是那个站在旁边看守衣裳的少年人。

## 默想

**默想：** 司提反临死时，天上的人子为他站起来。这不是他赢来的赏赐，而是那位先为他死、又复活的主，在他最孤单的时候亲自迎接他。今天我所面对的，也许不是石头，而是误解、冷落或不公；我是否知道，在一切人的评断之上，还有一位为我站起来的主？司提反说，神不住在人手所造的殿里。我的信仰有没有被关在某个地方、某段时间、某种聚会里，离开了这些就好像离开了神？神在美索不达米亚、在埃及的监牢、在旷野的荆棘中与他的人同在；他也在我的工作、家庭和软弱中与我同在。最后，司提反为打他的人祷告。我心里有没有一个人，是我还不愿意为他祷告的？我若想起，主先在十字架上为我这个仇敌祷告，就可以把这个人的名字带到他面前。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 7 is the longest speech in Acts. Charged with speaking against "this holy place and the law" (6:13), Stephen is asked by the high priest, "Are these things so?" (7:1). He doesn't answer with a direct defense. Instead he retells Israel's history from the beginning: **Abraham** (vv. 2–8), **Joseph** (vv. 9–16), **Moses** (vv. 17–43), **the tabernacle and the temple** (vv. 44–50), and finally a sharp **indictment** (vv. 51–53). Enraged, his hearers drive him out of the city and stone him; as he dies he sees "the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God," and prays for those killing him (vv. 54–60).

Two threads run through the speech. First, **God is never confined to one place.** He appeared to Abraham in Mesopotamia, was with Joseph in Egypt, and spoke to Moses from a bush in the wilderness of Sinai: none of these was the "holy place" of Jerusalem. Second, **Israel has repeatedly rejected the deliverers God sent.** Joseph was sold by his brothers, Moses was pushed aside by his people, the prophets were persecuted, and now "the Righteous One" has been murdered.

## Going Deeper: 7:2–16 Abraham and Joseph: God Outside the Land

**1. The God of glory appears abroad.** "The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia, before he lived in Haran" (7:2). Stephen's first sentence states his theme: God's glory didn't begin in Jerusalem. When Abraham was called, he was still in the land of the idol-worshiping Chaldeans (cf. Josh 24:2).

**2. Not even a foot's length.** "Yet he gave him no inheritance in it, not even a foot's length, but promised to give it to him as a possession and to his offspring after him, though he had no child" (7:5). Abraham lived his whole life on a promise, not a possession. He had no land and as yet no son, only God's word. That is the shape of faith (Heb 11:8–10).

**3. Brothers' jealousy, God's presence.** "And the patriarchs, jealous of Joseph, sold him into Egypt; but God was with him" (7:9). Here the pattern of the whole speech first appears: the deliverer God chooses is first rejected by his own brothers; but God is with him, "and rescued him out of all his afflictions" (7:10), and he becomes the one who saves the family.

**4. A difficult detail.** Stephen says the patriarchs were laid "in the tomb that Abraham had bought for a sum of silver from the sons of Hamor in Shechem" (7:16). In Genesis, Abraham bought the cave of Machpelah at Hebron (Gen 23), and it was Jacob who bought land at Shechem from the sons of Hamor (Gen 33:19; Josh 24:32). Some think Stephen telescopes the two events; some think he follows a tradition current in his day; others think "Abraham" here stands for the patriarchal family as a whole. Either way, Luke faithfully records what Stephen said, and the detail doesn't affect the speech's point.

> The point: God's presence has never been bound by borders. Abraham had no foothold; Joseph was sold to a foreign land; yet God was with them there. Stephen, accused of speaking against "the holy place," is reminding his hearers that God never lived in only one place.

## Going Deeper: 7:17–43 Moses: The Rejected Deliverer

**1. He supposed his brothers would understand.** "He supposed that his brothers would understand that God was giving them salvation by his hand, but they did not understand" (7:25). At forty, Moses went out to visit his brothers and was pushed aside: "Who made you a ruler and a judge over us?" (7:27). This is the speech's second example: the deliverer God sends is first rejected by his own people.

**2. Holy ground in the wilderness.** "Now when forty years had passed, an angel appeared to him in the wilderness of Mount Sinai, in a flame of fire in a bush" (7:30). The Lord said to him, "Take off the sandals from your feet, for the place where you are standing is holy ground" (7:33). Notice the contrast: Stephen's accusers are defending "this holy place" (6:13), but Stephen reminds them that the first ground God called "holy" was in a wilderness far from Jerusalem. What makes a place holy is God's presence, not the place itself.

**3. The rejected one is the one God appointed.** "This Moses, whom they rejected, saying, 'Who made you a ruler and a judge?'—this man God sent as both ruler and redeemer by the hand of the angel who appeared to him in the bush" (7:35). The people asked, "Who made you?" God's answer: "I did." The force of this is that the hearers are meant to think of Jesus: they too have rejected the one God appointed (cf. 2:36; 4:11).

**4. "A prophet like me."** Stephen makes a point of recalling that Moses said, "God will raise up for you a prophet like me from your brothers" (7:37; Deut 18:15). Peter has already applied this to Jesus (3:22). Stephen, charged with speaking "against Moses" (6:11), is using Moses' own words to point to Christ. Those who truly honor Moses listen to the one Moses pointed to.

**5. In their hearts they turned to Egypt.** "Our fathers refused to obey him, but thrust him aside, and in their hearts they turned to Egypt" (7:39). They made a calf and "were rejoicing in the works of their hands" (7:41). That phrase, "works of their hands," matters: a few verses later Stephen will say that the Most High "does not dwell in houses made by hands" (7:48). Idols are made by hands; and if people treat the temple as a place to control God, the temple too becomes a "work of their hands."

> The point: Israel's history has a recurring pattern: God sends a deliverer, and the people first reject him. So it was with Joseph, with Moses, with the prophets, and with Jesus. Stephen is telling history, but he is pointing at the people in front of him.

## Going Deeper: 7:44–50 Tabernacle and Temple

**1. According to the pattern.** "Our fathers had the tent of witness in the wilderness, just as he who spoke to Moses directed him to make it, according to the pattern that he had seen" (7:44). The tabernacle was commanded by God himself, and it could move: it went where the people went.

**2. David asked, Solomon built.** David "asked to find a dwelling place for the God of Jacob. But it was Solomon who built a house for him" (7:46–47). Stephen doesn't say building the temple was wrong; God permitted and accepted it (1 Kgs 8). His point comes in the next sentence.

**3. The Most High does not dwell in houses made by hands.** "Yet the Most High does not dwell in houses made by hands, as the prophet says, 'Heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool. What kind of house will you build for me, says the Lord, or what is the place of my rest? Did not my hand make all these things?'" (7:48–50; Isa 66:1–2). Solomon said as much when he dedicated it: "Behold, heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain you; how much less this house that I have built!" (1 Kgs 8:27). The problem isn't the temple; it's treating the temple as a place to shut God in and keep him under human management.

**4. The true temple.** Jesus said, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up," and John tells us "he was speaking about the temple of his body" (John 2:19–21). Stephen was accused of saying Jesus would "destroy this place" (6:14); in fact Jesus himself is the true temple where God and humanity meet. From now on God's presence will dwell by the Spirit among those who believe in him (1 Cor 3:16), all the way to the ends of the earth.

> The point: Stephen isn't against the temple; he's against the mindset that confines God to it. God is greater than anything humans build, and he has come among us in person, in Christ.

## Going Deeper: 7:51–60 Indictment, Vision and Forgiveness

**1. "As your fathers did, so do you."** "You stiff-necked people, uncircumcised in heart and ears, you always resist the Holy Spirit. As your fathers did, so do you" (7:51). These are hard words, but they are the language of the Old Testament prophets (Ex 33:5; Jer 9:26). "Uncircumcised in heart and ears" means they bear the covenant sign in their bodies, but their hearts have not turned to God.

**2. The Righteous One.** "Which of the prophets did not your fathers persecute? And they killed those who announced beforehand the coming of the Righteous One, whom you have now betrayed and murdered" (7:52). Here the whole speech reaches its climax: the rejection of Joseph, Moses and the prophets all points to the rejection of Jesus, "the Righteous One."

**3. The Son of Man standing.** "But he, full of the Holy Spirit, gazed into heaven and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at the right hand of God" (7:55). Outside the Gospels, this is the only place in the New Testament where someone calls Jesus "the Son of Man" outright (7:56; Rev 1:13 says only "one like a son of man"). At his trial Jesus said, "From now on the Son of Man shall be seated at the right hand of the power of God" (Luke 22:69). Stephen sees him *standing*. Interpreters offer several readings: he rises to welcome his witness; he stands as advocate to testify for him (cf. Luke 12:8); or he stands as judge. On any reading, the council on earth has condemned Stephen, but the Son of Man in heaven has risen for him.

**4. Dying like his Lord.** "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit." "Lord, do not hold this sin against them" (7:59–60). These two prayers almost echo Jesus' words on the cross: "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit!" (Luke 23:46) and "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34). But Stephen prays to "the Lord Jesus," putting Jesus where Jesus put the Father. The first martyr's prayer plainly confesses that Jesus is God.

**5. He fell asleep.** "And when he had said this, he fell asleep" (7:60). He was stoned to death, yet Luke says he "fell asleep." For those who belong to Christ, death has lost its final power (1 Thess 4:13–14).

**6. A young man named Saul.** "And the witnesses laid down their garments at the feet of a young man named Saul" (7:58). "And Saul approved of his execution" (8:1; the Chinese Union Version places this at the end of 7:60). In the darkest moment, Luke quietly brings in the next main character. Years later Paul himself recalled, "When the blood of Stephen your witness was being shed, I myself was standing by and approving" (22:20). Stephen prayed for those who killed him; perhaps the first answer to that prayer was the young man standing by, guarding the coats.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** As Stephen died, the Son of Man in heaven rose for him. That wasn't a reward Stephen earned; it was the Lord who had died and risen for him, meeting him in person at his loneliest hour. What I face today may not be stones but misunderstanding, neglect or injustice. Do I know that above every human verdict there is a Lord who stands up for me? Stephen said God doesn't live in houses made by hands. Has my faith been shut up in a certain place, a certain time, a certain kind of meeting, so that leaving them feels like leaving God? God was with his people in Mesopotamia, in an Egyptian prison and in a burning bush in the desert; he is with me in my work, my family and my weakness. Finally, Stephen prayed for the men stoning him. Is there someone I'm still unwilling to pray for? If I remember that the Lord first prayed on the cross for me when I was his enemy, I can bring that person's name to him.
