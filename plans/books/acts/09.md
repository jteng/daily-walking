## 本章脉络

第九章记载了教会历史上最重要的一次归主。逼迫教会最凶的扫罗，在往大马士革的路上被复活的耶稣拦住。这件事在使徒行传里讲了三次（9章；22章；26章），可见路加多么看重它。

本章有四段：**大马士革路上**（1-9节），扫罗遇见主，眼睛瞎了；**亚拿尼亚**（10-19节上），一个普通门徒奉差去为仇敌按手；**扫罗传道与逃离**（19节下-31节），从大马士革到耶路撒冷，巴拿巴接纳了他；**彼得在吕大和约帕**（32-43节），以尼雅得医治，多加复活。第31节是另一个「进展报告」：各处的教会「都得平安，被建立」。

## 深读 9:1-9 大马士革路上

**1. 口吐威吓凶杀的话。** 「扫罗仍然向主的门徒口吐威吓凶杀的话。」（9:1）「口吐」原文是「呼吸」：他呼吸的，就是威吓和凶杀。他向大祭司要文书，要把大马士革「信奉这道的人，无论男女，都准他捆绑带到耶路撒冷」（9:2）。这是使徒行传第一次称基督徒为「这道」的人（参19:9，23；24:14）：信耶稣是一条路，一种生活方式。

**2. 「你为什么逼迫我？」** 「他就仆倒在地，听见有声音对他说：『扫罗！扫罗！你为什么逼迫我？』」（9:4）扫罗逼迫的是门徒，耶稣却说「逼迫我」。复活升天的主，和他地上的百姓是连在一起的。触摸他的教会，就是触摸他自己。保罗后来教导，教会是基督的身体（林前12:27；弗1:22-23），这个真理，他第一次是在大马士革路上，从主自己的口里听到的。

**3. 「主啊！你是谁？」** 「主说：『我就是你所逼迫的耶稣。』」（9:5）对扫罗来说，这是最可怕的一刻：他以为自己在为神发热心，却发现自己在攻击神所立的弥赛亚。他所认定被神咒诅、挂在木头上的耶稣（参申21:23；加3:13），竟然活着，而且在荣耀里。他整个神学在这一刻被翻转了。

**4. 三日不能看见。** 「扫罗从地上起来，睁开眼睛，竟不能看见什么。」「三日不能看见，也不吃也不喝。」（9:8-9）这个原本要「拉着男女」捆绑别人的人，现在要被别人「拉他的手」领进城。这三天的黑暗，是他从旧生命走向新生命的过渡。

> 要点：扫罗不是被一个道理说服的，而是被一位活着的主拦住的。主说「你为什么逼迫我」，表明他与他的百姓同受苦难，没有一个受苦的门徒是孤单的。

## 深读 9:10-19 亚拿尼亚：奉差去见仇敌

**1. 「主，我在这里。」** 「当下，在大马士革有一个门徒，名叫亚拿尼亚。主在异象中对他说：『亚拿尼亚。』他说：『主，我在这里。』」（9:10）亚拿尼亚不是使徒，也不是长老，只是「一个门徒」。神改变历史的工作，常常是交给一个普通人去完成的。

**2. 合理的害怕。** 主叫他去找扫罗，亚拿尼亚回答：「主啊，我听见许多人说，这人怎样在耶路撒冷多多苦害你的圣徒，并且他在这里有从祭司长得来的权柄捆绑一切求告你名的人。」（9:13-14）他的害怕完全合理。主没有责备他，只是告诉他一件他不知道的事。

**3. 「他是我所拣选的器皿。」** 「你只管去！他是我所拣选的器皿，要在外邦人和君王，并以色列人面前宣扬我的名。我也要指示他，为我的名必须受许多的苦难。」（9:15-16）扫罗被拣选，不是因为他有什么可取之处；他后来说：「我从前是亵渎神的，逼迫人的，侮慢人的；然而我还蒙了怜悯。」（提前1:13）这完全是恩典。而这恩典也包括受苦：从前使人受苦的人，将要为主的名受苦。

**4. 「兄弟扫罗」。** 「亚拿尼亚就去了，进入那家，把手按在扫罗身上，说：『兄弟扫罗，在你来的路上向你显现的主，就是耶稣，打发我来，叫你能看见，又被圣灵充满。』」（9:17）亚拿尼亚的第一句话，就称这个仇敌为「兄弟」。这是福音最具体的样子：基督把仇敌变成了家人。

**5. 鳞片掉下来。** 「扫罗的眼睛上，好像有鳞立刻掉下来，他就能看见。于是起来受了洗。」（9:18）肉身的眼睛开了，属灵的眼睛也开了。他接受了自己曾经要消灭的洗礼，加入了自己曾经要捆绑的群体。

> 要点：神叫一个普通的门徒去见最可怕的仇敌，并叫他称仇敌为「兄弟」。扫罗的归主，是一个人的恩典；亚拿尼亚的顺服，是另一个人的恩典。

## 深读 9:19-31 从逼迫者到传道者

**1. 就在各会堂里。** 「就在各会堂里宣传耶稣，说他是神的儿子。」（9:20）他本来要带着文书到会堂去捆绑人，现在却在同样的会堂里宣讲耶稣。听见的人都惊奇：「在耶路撒冷残害求告这名的，不是这人吗？」（9:21）

**2. 越发有能力。** 「但扫罗越发有能力，驳倒住大马士革的犹太人，证明耶稣是基督。」（9:22）他熟悉旧约，现在用同样的经文证明耶稣就是弥赛亚。从加拉太书我们知道，这期间他也曾往阿拉伯去，「过了三年，才上耶路撒冷」（加1:17-18）。路加在这里把这几年浓缩在一起讲。

**3. 筐子里的使徒。** 「他的门徒就在夜间用筐子把他从城墙上缒下去。」（9:25）不久前，他带着大祭司的权柄要进城；现在，他坐在筐子里在夜里逃出城。保罗后来在夸自己的「软弱」时，特别提到这件事（林后11:30-33）。主曾说「我也要指示他，为我的名必须受许多的苦难」（9:16），这是第一次。

**4. 惟有巴拿巴。** 「扫罗到了耶路撒冷，想与门徒结交，他们却都怕他，不信他是门徒。惟有巴拿巴接待他，领去见使徒，把他在路上怎么看见主……都述说出来。」（9:26-27）巴拿巴的名字意思是「劝慰子」（4:36）。在所有人都怕扫罗的时候，他愿意冒险为他担保。没有巴拿巴，耶路撒冷的教会可能不会接纳这位后来的外邦人使徒。

**5. 教会得平安。** 「那时，犹太、加利利、撒马利亚各处的教会都得平安，被建立；凡事敬畏主，蒙圣灵的安慰，人数就增多了。」（9:31）请注意，路加用的是单数的「教会」：遍布三个地区的信徒，是一个教会。最凶的逼迫者归主之后，教会进入了一段平安和成长的时期。

> 要点：恩典不只是赦免扫罗的过去，也把他带进一个新的群体。亚拿尼亚称他为兄弟，巴拿巴为他担保；一个被主接纳的人，也需要被主的百姓接纳。

## 深读 9:32-43 彼得在吕大和约帕

**1. 「耶稣基督医好你了。」** 彼得在吕大遇见瘫痪八年的以尼雅，对他说：「以尼雅，耶稣基督医好你了，起来！收拾你的褥子。」（9:34）彼得说得很清楚，医治的不是他，而是耶稣基督。结果，「凡住吕大和沙仑的人都看见了他，就归服主」（9:35）。

**2. 广行善事的多加。** 「在约帕有一个女徒，名叫大比大，翻希腊话就是多加；她广行善事，多施周济。」（9:36）这是新约里唯一一次用阴性的「女门徒」（*mathētria*）这个字。她死后，「众寡妇都站在彼得旁边哭，拿多加与她们同在时所做的里衣外衣给他看」（9:39）。她留下的不是名声，而是一件件为穷寡妇缝的衣服。

**3. 「大比大，起来！」** 「彼得叫她们都出去，就跪下祷告，转身对着死人说：『大比大，起来！』」（9:40）这几乎就是耶稣使睚鲁女儿复活时的情景：叫众人出去，然后说「大利大，古米！」，意思是「闺女，我吩咐你起来！」（可5:40-41）。彼得跪下祷告，因为能叫死人复活的，不是他，而是他的主。

**4. 住在硝皮匠家里。** 「此后，彼得在约帕一个硝皮匠西门的家里住了多日。」（9:43）硝皮匠常常接触死动物的尸体，按犹太人的洁净条例，这是一个不洁的行业。彼得已经开始走出他熟悉的边界；下一章，神要带他走得更远，进入外邦人哥尼流的家。

> 要点：福音的进展有两种样子：扫罗那样戏剧性的转变，和多加那样默默的服事。两者都是主的工作，也都在主的眼中被记念。

## 默想

**默想：** 主没有对扫罗说「你为什么逼迫他们」，而是说「你为什么逼迫我」。当我为主受委屈的时候，他不是站在远处看，而是与我一同受苦。我是否把自己的痛苦带到这位感同身受的主面前？扫罗被拣选，在他自己身上找不到任何理由；我被拣选，也是一样。我是否还在心里暗暗以为，自己总有一点值得神选的地方？亚拿尼亚走进那个家，第一句话就是「兄弟扫罗」。在我的生命中，有没有一个我很难称为「弟兄」或「姊妹」的人，也许他曾经伤害我，也许他和我很不一样？我不需要假装不害怕；我可以像亚拿尼亚一样，把害怕告诉主，然后照主的话去。因为基督先把我这个仇敌称为家人，我也可以试着向那个人伸出手。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 9 records the most important conversion in church history. Saul, the church's fiercest persecutor, is stopped on the road to Damascus by the risen Jesus. Acts tells this story three times (chs. 9, 22 and 26), which shows how much weight Luke gives it.

The chapter has four parts: **the Damascus road** (vv. 1–9), where Saul meets the Lord and is blinded; **Ananias** (vv. 10–19a), an ordinary disciple sent to lay hands on an enemy; **Saul preaching and escaping** (vv. 19b–31), from Damascus to Jerusalem, where Barnabas takes him in; and **Peter at Lydda and Joppa** (vv. 32–43), where Aeneas is healed and Dorcas is raised. Verse 31 is another "progress report": the church everywhere "had peace and was being built up."

## Going Deeper: 9:1–9 The Damascus Road

**1. Breathing threats and murder.** "But Saul, still breathing threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord" (9:1). The air he breathed was threat and murder. He asked the high priest for letters "so that if he found any belonging to the Way, men or women, he might bring them bound to Jerusalem" (9:2). This is the first time Acts calls Christians people of "the Way" (cf. 19:9, 23; 24:14): following Jesus is a road, a way of life.

**2. "Why are you persecuting me?"** "And falling to the ground he heard a voice saying to him, 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?'" (9:4). Saul was persecuting disciples, yet Jesus says "me." The risen, ascended Lord is bound up with his people on earth. To touch his church is to touch him. Paul would later teach that the church is the body of Christ (1 Cor 12:27; Eph 1:22–23); he first heard that truth on the Damascus road, from the Lord's own mouth.

**3. "Who are you, Lord?"** "And he said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting'" (9:5). For Saul this was the most terrifying moment: he thought he was zealous for God, and discovered he had been attacking God's Messiah. The Jesus he had taken to be cursed by God, hung on a tree (cf. Deut 21:23; Gal 3:13), was alive and in glory. His whole theology was overturned in an instant.

**4. Three days without sight.** "Saul rose from the ground, and although his eyes were opened, he saw nothing. So they led him by the hand." "And for three days he was without sight, and neither ate nor drank" (9:8–9). The man who came to drag others off in chains now had to be led by the hand into the city. Those three days of darkness were his passage from the old life to the new.

> The point: Saul wasn't persuaded by an argument; he was stopped by a living Lord. When the Lord says, "Why are you persecuting me?" he shows that he suffers with his people; no suffering disciple is alone.

## Going Deeper: 9:10–19 Ananias: Sent to Meet an Enemy

**1. "Here I am, Lord."** "Now there was a disciple at Damascus named Ananias. The Lord said to him in a vision, 'Ananias.' And he said, 'Here I am, Lord'" (9:10). Ananias wasn't an apostle or an elder, just "a disciple." God's history-changing work is often entrusted to ordinary people.

**2. Reasonable fear.** When the Lord told him to go to Saul, Ananias answered, "Lord, I have heard from many about this man, how much evil he has done to your saints at Jerusalem. And here he has authority from the chief priests to bind all who call on your name" (9:13–14). His fear was entirely reasonable. The Lord didn't rebuke him; he told him something he didn't know.

**3. "A chosen instrument of mine."** "Go, for he is a chosen instrument of mine to carry my name before the Gentiles and kings and the children of Israel. For I will show him how much he must suffer for the sake of my name" (9:15–16). Saul wasn't chosen for anything commendable in him. He later wrote, "Formerly I was a blasphemer, persecutor, and insolent opponent. But I received mercy" (1 Tim 1:13). It was pure grace. And that grace included suffering: the man who had made others suffer would now suffer for the Lord's name.

**4. "Brother Saul."** "So Ananias departed and entered the house. And laying his hands on him he said, 'Brother Saul, the Lord Jesus who appeared to you on the road by which you came has sent me so that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit'" (9:17). Ananias's first word to this enemy was "brother." This is the gospel at its most concrete: Christ turns enemies into family.

**5. Scales fell.** "And immediately something like scales fell from his eyes, and he regained his sight. Then he rose and was baptized" (9:18). His physical eyes were opened, and his spiritual eyes too. He received the baptism he had tried to stamp out, and joined the community he had come to bind.

> The point: God sent an ordinary disciple to meet the most feared enemy, and had him call the enemy "brother." Saul's conversion was grace to one man; Ananias's obedience was grace to another.

## Going Deeper: 9:19–31 From Persecutor to Preacher

**1. In the synagogues.** "And immediately he proclaimed Jesus in the synagogues, saying, 'He is the Son of God'" (9:20). He had come with letters to arrest people in the synagogues; now he preached Jesus in those very synagogues. Everyone who heard him was amazed: "Is not this the man who made havoc in Jerusalem of those who called upon this name?" (9:21).

**2. Increasing in strength.** "But Saul increased all the more in strength, and confounded the Jews who lived in Damascus by proving that Jesus was the Christ" (9:22). He knew the Old Testament well, and now used the same Scriptures to prove that Jesus was the Messiah. From Galatians we learn that during this period he also went into Arabia, and "then after three years I went up to Jerusalem" (Gal 1:17–18). Luke compresses those years here.

**3. An apostle in a basket.** "But his disciples took him by night and let him down through an opening in the wall, lowering him in a basket" (9:25). Not long before, he had been heading into the city with the high priest's authority; now he left it at night in a basket. When Paul later boasted of his "weakness," this is the episode he chose (2 Cor 11:30–33). The Lord had said, "I will show him how much he must suffer for the sake of my name" (9:16); this was the first lesson.

**4. But Barnabas.** "And when he had come to Jerusalem, he attempted to join the disciples. And they were all afraid of him, for they did not believe that he was a disciple. But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles and declared to them how on the road he had seen the Lord..." (9:26–27). Barnabas's name means "son of encouragement" (4:36). When everyone was afraid of Saul, he was willing to risk vouching for him. Without Barnabas, the Jerusalem church might never have received the man who became the apostle to the Gentiles.

**5. The church had peace.** "So the church throughout all Judea and Galilee and Samaria had peace and was being built up. And walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit, it multiplied" (9:31). Notice that Luke says "the church," singular: believers spread across three regions are one church. After its fiercest persecutor was converted, the church entered a season of peace and growth.

> The point: Grace didn't only forgive Saul's past; it brought him into a new community. Ananias called him brother and Barnabas vouched for him. One the Lord has received also needs to be received by the Lord's people.

## Going Deeper: 9:32–43 Peter at Lydda and Joppa

**1. "Jesus Christ heals you."** At Lydda Peter met Aeneas, paralyzed for eight years, and said, "Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you; rise and make your bed" (9:34). Peter made it plain that the healer was not he but Jesus Christ. As a result, "all the residents of Lydda and Sharon saw him, and they turned to the Lord" (9:35).

**2. Dorcas, full of good works.** "Now there was in Joppa a disciple named Tabitha, which, translated, means Dorcas. She was full of good works and acts of charity" (9:36). This is the only place in the New Testament where the feminine word for "disciple" (*mathētria*) is used. When she died, "all the widows stood beside him weeping and showing tunics and other garments that Dorcas made while she was with them" (9:39). What she left behind wasn't a reputation but garments, one after another, sewn for poor widows.

**3. "Tabitha, arise."** "But Peter put them all outside, and knelt down and prayed; and turning to the body he said, 'Tabitha, arise'" (9:40). This closely mirrors Jesus raising Jairus's daughter: he put everyone outside and said, "Talitha cumi," which means, "Little girl, I say to you, arise" (Mark 5:40–41). Peter knelt to pray, because the one who raises the dead wasn't he but his Lord.

**4. Staying with a tanner.** "And he stayed in Joppa for many days with one Simon, a tanner" (9:43). Tanners constantly handled dead animals, and by Jewish purity rules it was an unclean trade. Peter was already beginning to step outside familiar boundaries; in the next chapter God will take him much further, into the house of the Gentile Cornelius.

> The point: The gospel advances in two ways: dramatic turnarounds like Saul's, and quiet service like Dorcas's. Both are the Lord's work, and both are remembered in his sight.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The Lord didn't say to Saul, "Why are you persecuting them?" but "Why are you persecuting me?" When I'm wronged for the Lord's sake, he isn't watching from a distance; he suffers with me. Do I bring my pain to this Lord who feels it with me? Nothing in Saul himself explains why he was chosen, and the same is true of me. Do I still quietly assume there must be something in me that made me worth choosing? Ananias walked into that house, and his first words were "Brother Saul." Is there someone in my life I find it hard to call "brother" or "sister," perhaps someone who once hurt me, or someone very different from me? I don't need to pretend I'm not afraid; like Ananias, I can tell the Lord my fear and then go as he says. Because Christ first called me, his enemy, family, I can try to reach out my hand to that person.
