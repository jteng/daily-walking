## 本章脉络

第十章是使徒行传的分水岭。到目前为止，信主的都是犹太人、撒玛利亚人，或像衣索匹亚太监那样已经去耶路撒冷敬拜的人。现在，神要把福音带进一个外邦人的家。路加把这件事讲得很详细，第十一章又让彼得重述一遍，第十五章耶路撒冷会议上再提一次，可见它多么关键。

本章有五段：**哥尼流的异象**（1-8节）；**彼得的异象**（9-16节）；**彼得到凯撒利亚**（17-33节）；**彼得的讲道**（34-43节）；**圣灵降在外邦人身上**（44-48节）。这一章真正被改变的，不只是哥尼流，也是彼得。

## 深读 10:1-8 一个敬畏神的外邦人

**1. 罗马的百夫长。** 「在凯撒利亚有一个人，名叫哥尼流，是『意大利营』的百夫长。」（10:1）凯撒利亚是罗马在犹太地的行政中心；百夫长是罗马军队里的中级军官。对犹太人来说，他代表占领他们土地的外邦势力。

**2. 虔诚，却还没有得救。** 「他是个虔诚人，他和全家都敬畏神，多多周济百姓，常常祷告神。」（10:2）他属于当时所谓「敬畏神的人」：相信以色列的神，参加会堂，却没有受割礼成为犹太人。天使说他的祷告和周济「已蒙记念了」（10:4），却仍叫他去请彼得来，因为彼得「有话告诉你，可以叫你和你的全家得救」（11:14）。他的敬虔是真实的，神也看见了；但敬虔本身不能救他，他还需要听见耶稣的福音。

**3. 约帕。** 天使叫他差人往约帕去请彼得（10:5）。很多年前，先知约拿正是「下到约帕」，坐船逃走，不愿意把神的话带给外邦的尼尼微（拿1:3）。如今，在同一个城里，另一位犹太先知要被神差到外邦人那里去。这一次，他会去吗？

> 要点：哥尼流的敬虔是真实的，神也记念了；但神没有说「这样就够了」，而是差人把耶稣的名带给他。人在神面前最好的光景，仍然需要福音。

## 深读 10:9-16 彼得的异象：神所洁净的

**1. 大布与走兽。** 彼得在房顶上祷告，饿了，「魂游象外，看见天开了，有一物降下，好像一块大布，系着四角」，里面有各样走兽、昆虫和飞鸟（10:10-12）。声音说：「彼得，起来，宰了吃！」（10:13）

**2. 「主啊，这是不可的！」** 「彼得却说：『主啊，这是不可的！凡俗物和不洁净的物，我从来没有吃过。』」（10:14）这句话本身就是矛盾：既然称他为「主」，又怎能对他说「不可」？彼得一生遵守洁净条例（利11章），这不是小事，而是犹太人身份的核心记号。

**3. 「神所洁净的，你不可当作俗物。」** 「第二次有声音向他说：『神所洁净的，你不可当作俗物。』这样一连三次。」（10:15-16）耶稣在地上时已经说过，「各样的食物都是洁净的」（可7:19）。但彼得要到现在才明白，这句话的意义远远超过饮食。「一连三次」：主对这个固执的门徒，有耐心再说一次。

**4. 异象真正的意思。** 彼得很快就明白：「神已经指示我，无论什么人都不可看作俗而不洁净的。」（10:28）异象讲的是食物，指的是人。饮食条例本是把以色列和列国分开的界线；神现在要拆掉这道墙（参弗2:14）。

> 要点：神必须先改变他的传道人，才能把福音带给外邦人。彼得最难跨过的，不是从约帕到凯撒利亚的路，而是他心里「俗而不洁净」的那条界线。

## 深读 10:17-33 两个异象相遇

**1. 「不要疑惑。」** 彼得还在思想异象，圣灵对他说：「有三个人来找你。起来，下去，和他们同往，不要疑惑，因为是我差他们来的。」（10:19-20）「疑惑」的原文（*diakrinō*）也有「分别」的意思；彼得后来复述这事时，同一个字可以译作「不要分别」（11:12，英文ESV作 making no distinction）。而「分别」正是彼得一生被教导要做的事。神的时间安排得很精确：两个人分别在两个城里看见异象，彼得刚看完，哥尼流差来的人就到了门口。

**2. 留外邦人住宿。** 「彼得就请他们进去，住了一宿。」（10:23）对一个守洁净条例的犹太人来说，这已经是一大步了。

**3. 「我也是人。」** 「彼得一进去，哥尼流就迎接他，俯伏在他脚前拜他。彼得却拉他，说：『你起来，我也是人。』」（10:25-26）外邦人不必在犹太人面前低人一等，彼得也不肯被人敬拜。在福音面前，传道的和听道的都只是人，都同样需要神的怜悯。

**4. 「现今我们都在神面前。」** 哥尼流说：「现今我们都在神面前，要听主所吩咐你的一切话。」（10:33）这是每一个听道的人该有的心：不是来听一个人的意见，而是来到神面前，听他的话。

## 深读 10:34-43 彼得的讲道

**1. 「神是不偏待人。」** 「我真看出神是不偏待人。原来，各国中那敬畏主、行义的人都为主所悦纳。」（10:34-35）「不偏待人」原文是「不看人的脸面」，旧约早就这样形容神（申10:17）。彼得的意思不是说人只要敬虔就可以得救，不需要基督；否则他不必接着传讲耶稣。他是说：神接纳人，不看种族、国籍或身份；任何国家的人，都可以凭着信来到他面前。

**2. 万有的主。** 「神藉着耶稣基督（他是万有的主）传和平的福音。」（10:36）在罗马军官的家里，彼得宣告真正的「万有的主」不是凯撒，而是耶稣。

**3. 福音的内容。** 彼得接着讲耶稣的一生：受圣灵膏抹，「周流四方，行善事，医好凡被魔鬼压制的人」（10:38）；被挂在木头上杀了；「第三日，神叫他复活」（10:40）；他是「神所立定的，要作审判活人、死人的主」（10:42）。这几乎就是马可福音的大纲。古代教会相传，马可福音就是根据彼得的讲道写成的。

**4. 凡信他的人。** 「众先知也为他作见证说：『凡信他的人必因他的名得蒙赦罪。』」（10:43）这是整篇讲道的高峰。「凡」字打开了门：不论犹太人或外邦人，赦罪的条件都一样，就是信他。

> 要点：神不看人的脸面，却看他的儿子。人得蒙悦纳，不是因为种族或敬虔，而是因为信那位被挂在木头上、又复活的万有之主。

## 深读 10:44-48 外邦人的五旬节

**1. 讲道还没讲完。** 「彼得还说这话的时候，圣灵降在一切听道的人身上。」（10:44）彼得没有呼召，没有按手，圣灵就降下了。这一次，神没有等任何人批准。

**2. 奉割礼的信徒都希奇。** 「那些奉割礼、和彼得同来的信徒，见圣灵的恩赐也浇在外邦人身上，就都希奇。」（10:45）他们听见外邦人「说方言，称赞神为大」（10:46），和五旬节那天一模一样。彼得后来说：「圣灵便降在他们身上，正像当初降在我们身上一样。」（11:15）

**3. 「谁能禁止？」** 「这些人既受了圣灵，与我们一样，谁能禁止用水给他们施洗呢？」（10:47）这和衣索匹亚太监的问题相呼应：「我受洗有什么妨碍呢？」（8:36）神已经接纳了他们，教会就不能拒绝他们。

**4. 他们又请彼得住了几天。** 「他们又请彼得住了几天。」（10:48）这句话看似平常，却意味深长：一个犹太使徒住在外邦人家里，和他们同吃同住。这正是下一章耶路撒冷的信徒要质问他的事（11:3）。

## 默想

**默想：** 哥尼流敬虔、慷慨、常常祷告，神却仍差彼得去告诉他「可以叫你和你的全家得救」的话。我是否以为，自己的教会背景、多年的服事、良好的品格，就足以让我站在神面前？我最好的光景，也和哥尼流一样，需要那位被挂在木头上的主。彼得说「主啊，这是不可的」，因为他心里有一条界线，把某些人划在「俗而不洁净」的那一边。我心里的界线在哪里？也许是某个省份、某个种族、某种口音，或某个社会阶层的人；也许是我从小听长辈说「那种人」的那些人。如果神曾向我这个不洁净的人说「我已经洁净你了」，我还能把谁看作是俗的呢？最后，哥尼流说：「现今我们都在神面前，要听主所吩咐你的一切话。」下一次我坐在讲台下，或打开圣经的时候，我是带着这样的心来的吗？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 10 is the watershed of Acts. Until now those who believed were Jews, Samaritans, or someone like the Ethiopian eunuch who had already gone to Jerusalem to worship. Now God brings the gospel into a Gentile home. Luke tells the story in great detail, has Peter retell it in chapter 11, and has it raised again at the Jerusalem council in chapter 15, which shows how decisive it is.

The chapter has five parts: **Cornelius's vision** (vv. 1–8); **Peter's vision** (vv. 9–16); **Peter goes to Caesarea** (vv. 17–33); **Peter's sermon** (vv. 34–43); and **the Spirit falls on the Gentiles** (vv. 44–48). The one truly changed in this chapter isn't only Cornelius; it's also Peter.

## Going Deeper: 10:1–8 A God-Fearing Gentile

**1. A Roman centurion.** "At Caesarea there was a man named Cornelius, a centurion of what was known as the Italian Cohort" (10:1). Caesarea was Rome's administrative center in Judea; a centurion was a mid-ranking officer in the Roman army. To Jews, he represented the foreign power occupying their land.

**2. Devout, but not yet saved.** He was "a devout man who feared God with all his household, gave alms generously to the people, and prayed continually to God" (10:2). He was one of the so-called "God-fearers": Gentiles who believed in Israel's God and attended the synagogue but had not been circumcised as Jews. The angel says his prayers and alms "have ascended as a memorial before God" (10:4), yet still tells him to send for Peter, because Peter "will declare to you a message by which you will be saved, you and all your household" (11:14). His piety was real, and God saw it; but piety itself couldn't save him. He still needed to hear the gospel of Jesus.

**3. Joppa.** The angel tells him to send to Joppa for Peter (10:5). Long before, the prophet Jonah "went down to Joppa" and took a ship to run away, unwilling to bring God's word to Gentile Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). Now, in the same town, another Jewish prophet is about to be sent by God to Gentiles. Will he go this time?

> The point: Cornelius's piety was real, and God remembered it; but God didn't say, "That's enough." He sent someone to bring him the name of Jesus. Even a person at their best before God still needs the gospel.

## Going Deeper: 10:9–16 Peter's Vision: What God Has Made Clean

**1. The sheet and the animals.** Peter was praying on the housetop and became hungry; "he fell into a trance and saw the heavens opened and something like a great sheet descending, being let down by its four corners," full of all kinds of animals, reptiles and birds (10:10–12). A voice said, "Rise, Peter; kill and eat" (10:13).

**2. "By no means, Lord!"** "But Peter said, 'By no means, Lord; for I have never eaten anything that is common or unclean'" (10:14). The sentence contradicts itself: how can you call someone "Lord" and tell him "No"? Peter had kept the food laws all his life (Lev 11). This was no small thing; it was a core marker of Jewish identity.

**3. "What God has made clean, do not call common."** "And the voice came to him again a second time, 'What God has made clean, do not call common.' This happened three times" (10:15–16). On earth Jesus had already "declared all foods clean" (Mark 7:19). But only now does Peter begin to see that this reaches far beyond food. "Three times": with this stubborn disciple, the Lord has patience to say it again.

**4. What the vision really meant.** Peter soon understood: "God has shown me that I should not call any person common or unclean" (10:28). The vision was about food; its point was people. The food laws had been a boundary separating Israel from the nations; now God was tearing down that wall (cf. Eph 2:14).

> The point: God had to change his preacher before he could bring the gospel to the Gentiles. The hardest road for Peter wasn't the one from Joppa to Caesarea; it was the line in his own heart marked "common and unclean."

## Going Deeper: 10:17–33 Two Visions Meet

**1. "Without hesitation."** While Peter was still pondering the vision, the Spirit said, "Behold, three men are looking for you. Rise and go down and accompany them without hesitation, for I have sent them" (10:19–20). The verb (*diakrinō*) can also mean "to make a distinction"; when Peter retells the story, the ESV renders it "making no distinction" (11:12). Making distinctions was exactly what Peter had been trained all his life to do. God's timing is precise: two men in two cities see visions, and just as Peter's ends, the men from Cornelius arrive at the gate.

**2. Gentile guests.** "So he invited them in to be his guests" (10:23). For a Jew who kept the purity laws, that was already a big step.

**3. "I too am a man."** "When Peter entered, Cornelius met him and fell down at his feet and worshiped him. But Peter lifted him up, saying, 'Stand up; I too am a man'" (10:25–26). The Gentile needn't bow before the Jew, and Peter refused to be worshiped. Before the gospel, preacher and hearer are alike only human, alike in need of God's mercy.

**4. "We are all here in the presence of God."** Cornelius said, "Now therefore we are all here in the presence of God to hear all that you have been commanded by the Lord" (10:33). That is the heart every hearer should bring: not coming to hear one man's opinions, but coming before God to hear his word.

## Going Deeper: 10:34–43 Peter's Sermon

**1. "God shows no partiality."** "Truly I understand that God shows no partiality, but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him" (10:34–35). "Shows no partiality" literally means "doesn't look at the face," and the Old Testament had long described God this way (Deut 10:17). Peter doesn't mean that devout people can be saved without Christ; otherwise he wouldn't go on to preach Jesus. He means that God's acceptance doesn't depend on race, nationality or status; people from any nation can come to him by faith.

**2. Lord of all.** "Preaching good news of peace through Jesus Christ (he is Lord of all)" (10:36). In a Roman officer's house, Peter announces that the true "Lord of all" isn't Caesar but Jesus.

**3. The content of the gospel.** Peter goes on to tell Jesus' life: anointed with the Spirit, he "went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil" (10:38); he was put to death on a tree; "God raised him on the third day" (10:40); he is "the one appointed by God to be judge of the living and the dead" (10:42). This is almost the outline of Mark's Gospel. Early church tradition held that Mark's Gospel was based on Peter's preaching.

**4. Everyone who believes.** "To him all the prophets bear witness that everyone who believes in him receives forgiveness of sins through his name" (10:43). This is the sermon's peak. "Everyone" opens the door: for Jew and Gentile alike, the condition of forgiveness is the same, faith in him.

> The point: God doesn't look at our faces; he looks at his Son. People are accepted not because of race or piety but through faith in the Lord of all, who was hanged on a tree and raised.

## Going Deeper: 10:44–48 The Gentiles' Pentecost

**1. Before the sermon was over.** "While Peter was still saying these things, the Holy Spirit fell on all who heard the word" (10:44). Peter made no appeal and laid no hands on anyone; the Spirit simply came. This time God waited for no one's approval.

**2. The circumcised believers were amazed.** "And the believers from among the circumcised who had come with Peter were amazed, because the gift of the Holy Spirit was poured out even on the Gentiles" (10:45). They heard the Gentiles "speaking in tongues and extolling God" (10:46), just as at Pentecost. Peter later said, "The Holy Spirit fell on them just as on us at the beginning" (11:15).

**3. "Can anyone withhold?"** "Can anyone withhold water for baptizing these people, who have received the Holy Spirit just as we have?" (10:47). This echoes the eunuch's question: "What prevents me from being baptized?" (8:36). God had received them, so the church could not refuse them.

**4. They asked him to stay.** "Then they asked him to remain for some days" (10:48). It sounds ordinary, but it means a great deal: a Jewish apostle staying in a Gentile home, eating and living with them. It is exactly what the Jerusalem believers will challenge him about in the next chapter (11:3).

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Cornelius was devout, generous and prayerful, yet God still sent Peter to tell him a message "by which you will be saved." Do I assume that my church background, years of service or good character are enough to stand on before God? At my best, like Cornelius, I need the Lord who was hanged on a tree. Peter said, "By no means, Lord," because there was a line in his heart that put certain people on the "common and unclean" side. Where is the line in my heart? Perhaps people from a certain province, race, accent or social class; perhaps the people my elders always called "that kind." If God has said to me, unclean as I was, "I have made you clean," whom can I still call common? Finally, Cornelius said, "We are all here in the presence of God to hear all that you have been commanded by the Lord." The next time I sit under preaching or open my Bible, will I come with that heart?
