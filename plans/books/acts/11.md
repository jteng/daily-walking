## 本章脉络

第十一章有两段，分别发生在两座城。**在耶路撒冷**（1-18节），奉割礼的信徒质问彼得为什么进外邦人的家，彼得把哥尼流的事从头讲一遍，众人「就不言语了，只归荣耀与神」。**在安提阿**（19-30节），一些分散的门徒开始直接向外邦人传讲主耶稣，一个新的、跨越种族的教会诞生了；巴拿巴和扫罗在那里教导一年，门徒第一次被称为「基督徒」；这个年轻的教会又捐钱帮助犹太的弟兄。

第十章是神打开了门；第十一章是教会学习走进这扇门。从此，使徒行传的重心将渐渐从耶路撒冷转到安提阿，再从安提阿走向地极。

## 深读 11:1-18 彼得的辩护

**1. 他们质问的不是讲道，而是吃饭。** 「那些奉割礼的门徒和他争辩说：『你进入未受割礼之人的家和他们一同吃饭了。』」（11:2-3）他们没有问「外邦人真的信了吗」，而是先问「你和他们一同吃饭了吗」。在古代，一同吃饭表示接纳、认同和团契。对许多人来说，接受别人得救比较容易，和他们同桌吃饭却难得多。

**2. 「挨次」讲述。** 「彼得就开口把这事挨次给他们讲解。」（11:4）彼得没有用使徒的权柄压人，也没有发怒，而是把事情经过照实讲清楚：异象、三个人、圣灵的吩咐，还有同去的「六位弟兄」作见证（11:12）。在教会的争议中，耐心而诚实地讲明神的作为，比争辩更有力。

**3. 「正像当初降在我们身上一样。」** 「我一开讲，圣灵便降在他们身上，正像当初降在我们身上一样。」（11:15）彼得指出，外邦人领受的不是二等的恩赐，而是同一位圣灵。他又想起主的话：「约翰是用水施洗，但你们要受圣灵的洗。」（11:16；参1:5）

**4. 「我是谁，能拦阻神呢？」** 「神既然给他们恩赐，像在我们信主耶稣基督的时候给了我们一样；我是谁，能拦阻神呢？」（11:17）这句话是全段的关键。彼得承认，他原本的顾虑若坚持下去，就是在拦阻神。请注意，他把恩赐和「信主耶稣基督」连在一起：犹太人和外邦人领受圣灵，都是因着信。

**5. 悔改得生命。** 「众人听见这话，就不言语了，只归荣耀与神，说：『这样看来，神也赐恩给外邦人，叫他们悔改得生命了。』」（11:18）他们从争辩转为沉默，又从沉默转为赞美。请注意他们怎么说：悔改是神「赐」的。人能回转，本身就是恩典（参5:31；提后2:25）。

> 要点：教会最难接受的，往往不是别人得救，而是要和他们同桌吃饭。当神的作为摆在眼前，最好的回应不是继续辩护自己的界线，而是安静下来，归荣耀与神。

## 深读 11:19-26 安提阿：一个新的教会

**1. 无名的宣教士。** 「那些因司提反的事遭患难四散的门徒直走到腓尼基和塞浦路斯，并安提阿；他们不向别人讲道，只向犹太人讲。但内中有塞浦路斯和古利奈人，他们到了安提阿也向希腊人传讲主耶稣。」（11:19-20）路加没有记下他们的名字。第一批在一座外邦大城里主动向外邦人传福音、建立第一个由犹太人和外邦人一同组成的教会的，是一群无名的信徒。（「希腊人」，有些抄本作「说希腊话的人」，英文ESV译作 Hellenists；在6:1、9:29，「说希腊话的人」指说希腊话的犹太人；但这里既与前一节「只向犹太人讲」对照，所指的应当是讲希腊话的外邦人。）

**2. 安提阿。** 安提阿是罗马帝国第三大城，仅次于罗马和亚历山大，是叙利亚省的首府，商业发达，各族杂居，道德风气也以败坏出名。神选择在这样一座城里，建立第一个由犹太人和外邦人共同组成的教会。

**3. 「主与他们同在。」** 「主与他们同在，信而归主的人就很多了。」（11:21）这里「主与他们同在」原文是「主的手与他们同在」。他们没有使徒的头衔，却有主的手。

**4. 看见神所赐的恩就欢喜。** 耶路撒冷教会打发巴拿巴去看看。「他到了那里，看见神所赐的恩就欢喜，劝勉众人，立定心志，恒久靠主。」（11:23）巴拿巴没有先去查他们是否按照耶路撒冷的规矩，而是先看见神的恩典。他本人就生在塞浦路斯（4:36），正适合去看望这些从塞浦路斯来的人所建立的教会。路加说：「这巴拿巴原是个好人，被圣灵充满，大有信心。」（11:24）

**5. 去找扫罗。** 「他又往大数去找扫罗，找着了，就带他到安提阿去。」（11:25-26）巴拿巴知道这个工作太大，自己一个人做不来；他也记得那个几年前被他领到使徒面前的人。好的领袖不怕把比自己更有恩赐的人带进来。

**6. 「基督徒」。** 「门徒称为『基督徒』是从安提阿起首。」（11:26）这个名字很可能是外人起的，意思是「属基督的人」或「基督党的人」，可能原本还带有一点讥讽（参26:28；彼前4:16）。可是这个名字说中了要点：这群人既不是单纯的犹太人，也不是单纯的希腊人，他们唯一共同的身份，就是属于基督。

> 要点：安提阿的教会不是由使徒建立的，而是由一群无名的信徒；它最大的特点不是种族或文化，而是基督。巴拿巴看见神的恩典就欢喜，这是一切健康服事的起点。

## 深读 11:27-30 一个慷慨的年轻教会

**1. 亚迦布的预言。** 「内中有一位，名叫亚迦布，站起来，藉着圣灵指明天下将有大饥荒。（这事到克劳第年间果然有了。）」（11:28）罗马皇帝克劳第（主后41-54年）在位期间，各地确实发生过几次饥荒，犹太历史学家约瑟夫记载，约主后46-48年犹太地有大饥荒。

**2. 照各人的力量。** 「于是门徒定意照各人的力量捐钱，送去供给住在犹太的弟兄。」（11:29）这个由犹太人和外邦人组成的年轻教会，主动帮助母会；他们听见预言就定意行动。「照各人的力量」：不是强迫，也不是平均分摊，而是各人按自己所能的甘心乐意地给（参林后8:3，12）。

**3. 恩典的流动。** 福音从耶路撒冷流到安提阿；现在，爱心从安提阿流回耶路撒冷。保罗后来说，「外邦人既然在他们属灵的好处上有分，就当把养身之物供给他们」（罗15:27）。捐献把犹太人和外邦人连在一起，成为一个身体。

> 要点：一个真正被恩典改变的教会，不只是领受，也会给予。安提阿的信徒主动想到远方的弟兄。

## 默想

**默想：** 耶路撒冷的信徒问彼得的，不是「他们信了吗」，而是「你和他们一同吃饭了吗」。我会不会接受某些人「也得救了」，却不愿意和他们坐在同一张桌子旁、请他们来我家吃饭？彼得说：「我是谁，能拦阻神呢？」我是否在某件事上，用自己的习惯和偏好拦阻了神正在做的工作？也许是教会里一种新的聚会方式，也许是一群我不熟悉的新来的人。巴拿巴到了安提阿，第一件事是「看见神所赐的恩就欢喜」。当我看见别的教会、别的同工、甚至我不太欣赏的人蒙神使用时，我心里先有的是欢喜，还是挑剔？我能这样欢喜，是因为我自己也是白白蒙恩的；神赐给别人的恩典，不会减少他给我的。最后，「基督徒」这个名字的意思，就是「属基督的人」。今天在我的公司、学校和家里，别人若只能用一个词来形容我，他们会想到基督吗？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 11 has two parts in two cities. **In Jerusalem** (vv. 1–18), the circumcised believers challenge Peter for entering a Gentile home; Peter retells the Cornelius story from the beginning, and they "fell silent. And they glorified God." **In Antioch** (vv. 19–30), some of the scattered disciples begin to preach the Lord Jesus directly to Gentiles, and a new, multi-ethnic church is born; Barnabas and Saul teach there for a year, the disciples are first called "Christians," and this young church sends relief to the brothers in Judea.

In chapter 10, God opened the door; in chapter 11, the church learns to walk through it. From here on, the center of Acts gradually shifts from Jerusalem to Antioch, and from Antioch toward the ends of the earth.

## Going Deeper: 11:1–18 Peter's Defense

**1. Their question was about eating, not preaching.** "The circumcision party criticized him, saying, 'You went to uncircumcised men and ate with them'" (11:2–3). They didn't first ask, "Did the Gentiles really believe?" They asked, "Did you eat with them?" In the ancient world, eating together meant acceptance, identification and fellowship. For many people it is easier to accept that others are saved than to sit at the same table with them.

**2. "In order."** "But Peter began and explained it to them in order" (11:4). Peter didn't pull apostolic rank or lose his temper; he simply told what happened, step by step: the vision, the three men, the Spirit's instruction, and the "six brothers" who went with him as witnesses (11:12). In church controversy, patiently and honestly explaining what God has done is more powerful than arguing.

**3. "Just as on us at the beginning."** "As I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell on them just as on us at the beginning" (11:15). Peter points out that the Gentiles received no second-class gift but the same Spirit. And he remembered the Lord's word: "John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit" (11:16; cf. 1:5).

**4. "Who was I that I could stand in God's way?"** "If then God gave the same gift to them as he gave to us when we believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I that I could stand in God's way?" (11:17). This is the key sentence. Peter admits that if he had held on to his original scruples, he would have been obstructing God. Notice that he ties the gift to believing "in the Lord Jesus Christ": Jews and Gentiles alike receive the Spirit through faith.

**5. Repentance that leads to life.** "When they heard these things they fell silent. And they glorified God, saying, 'Then to the Gentiles also God has granted repentance that leads to life'" (11:18). They moved from argument to silence, and from silence to praise. Notice what they said: repentance is something God "granted." That a person can turn at all is itself grace (cf. 5:31; 2 Tim 2:25).

> The point: What the church finds hardest is often not that others are saved, but that it must eat with them. When God's work is plain before us, the best response isn't to keep defending our boundaries but to fall silent and give God the glory.

## Going Deeper: 11:19–26 Antioch: A New Church

**1. Nameless missionaries.** "Now those who were scattered because of the persecution that arose over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia and Cyprus and Antioch, speaking the word to no one except Jews. But there were some of them, men of Cyprus and Cyrene, who on coming to Antioch spoke to the Hellenists also, preaching the Lord Jesus" (11:19–20). Luke doesn't record their names. The first to take the initiative of preaching to Gentiles in a great Gentile city, and to found the first church of Jews and Gentiles together, were a group of unnamed believers. (Manuscripts differ between "Greeks" and "Hellenists"; the Chinese Union Version has "Greeks." In 6:1 and 9:29 "Hellenists" means Greek-speaking Jews; here, set against "no one except Jews," it must mean Greek-speaking Gentiles.)

**2. Antioch.** Antioch was the third-largest city of the Roman Empire after Rome and Alexandria, the capital of the province of Syria, a commercial hub where many peoples lived side by side, and notorious for its moral corruption. In such a city God chose to found the first church made up of both Jews and Gentiles.

**3. "The hand of the Lord was with them."** "And the hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number who believed turned to the Lord" (11:21). They had no apostolic title, but they had the Lord's hand.

**4. He saw the grace of God and was glad.** The Jerusalem church sent Barnabas to see. "When he came and saw the grace of God, he was glad, and he exhorted them all to remain faithful to the Lord with steadfast purpose" (11:23). Barnabas didn't first check whether they followed Jerusalem's ways; he first saw God's grace. He himself was from Cyprus (4:36), well suited to visit a church founded by men from Cyprus. Luke says, "He was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith" (11:24).

**5. Looking for Saul.** "So Barnabas went to Tarsus to look for Saul, and when he had found him, he brought him to Antioch" (11:25–26). Barnabas knew the work was too big for him alone, and he remembered the man he had brought to the apostles years before. Good leaders aren't afraid to bring in people more gifted than themselves.

**6. "Christians."** "And in Antioch the disciples were first called Christians" (11:26). The name was probably coined by outsiders, meaning "Christ's people" or "the Christ party," and may at first have carried a hint of mockery (cf. 26:28; 1 Pet 4:16). Yet it named the heart of the matter: these people were neither simply Jews nor simply Greeks; their one shared identity was that they belonged to Christ.

> The point: The church at Antioch wasn't founded by apostles but by unnamed believers, and its defining mark wasn't race or culture but Christ. Barnabas saw the grace of God and was glad; that is where all healthy ministry begins.

## Going Deeper: 11:27–30 A Generous Young Church

**1. Agabus's prophecy.** "And one of them named Agabus stood up and foretold by the Spirit that there would be a great famine over all the world (this took place in the days of Claudius)" (11:28). During the reign of the emperor Claudius (AD 41–54) there were indeed several famines, and the Jewish historian Josephus records a severe famine in Judea around AD 46–48.

**2. According to ability.** "So the disciples determined, everyone according to his ability, to send relief to the brothers living in Judea" (11:29). A young church of Jews and Gentiles took the initiative to help its mother church; they resolved to act on the prophecy. "According to his ability": not forced, not an equal levy, but each giving willingly as he was able (cf. 2 Cor 8:3, 12).

**3. The flow of grace.** The gospel flowed from Jerusalem to Antioch; now love flowed back from Antioch to Jerusalem. Paul later wrote that "if the Gentiles have come to share in their spiritual blessings, they ought also to be of service to them in material blessings" (Rom 15:27). Giving bound Jews and Gentiles together as one body.

> The point: A church truly changed by grace doesn't only receive; it gives. The believers at Antioch took the initiative to care for their distant brothers.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The Jerusalem believers didn't ask Peter, "Did they believe?" but "Did you eat with them?" Might I accept that certain people are "saved too," yet be unwilling to sit at a table with them or invite them home for a meal? Peter said, "Who was I that I could stand in God's way?" Am I standing in the way of something God is doing because of my own habits and preferences: a new kind of gathering in the church, perhaps, or a group of newcomers I'm not used to? When Barnabas reached Antioch, the first thing he did was see "the grace of God" and be glad. When I see another church, another coworker, even someone I don't much admire, being used by God, is my first response gladness or fault-finding? I can be glad because I too received grace freely; the grace God gives others doesn't take away from what he gives me. Finally, "Christian" means "one who belongs to Christ." Today at work, at school and at home, if people had only one word to describe me, would they think of Christ?
