## 本章脉络

第十二章把镜头从安提阿拉回耶路撒冷，讲的是两种力量的对照：一边是王的权势，一边是教会的祷告。**希律逼迫教会**（1-5节）：雅各被刀杀，彼得被囚，「教会却为他切切地祷告神」。**彼得出监**（6-19节）：天使在夜里领他出来，祷告的人却不敢相信。**希律之死**（20-23节）：他任由百姓称他为神，「不归荣耀给神」，被虫所咬而死。最后是一句总结：「神的道日见兴旺，越发广传。」（24节）

这一章开头，希律似乎掌握一切：他能杀人、能抓人、能把彼得锁在重重看守之下。结尾却是希律死了，神的道兴旺了。这也是使徒行传前半卷的结束：从第十三章起，故事的焦点将转到保罗和他的宣教旅程。

## 深读 12:1-5 一个死了，一个被囚

**1. 希律王。** 这里的希律是希律亚基帕一世（主后37年受封为王，41-44年统治整个犹太地），是耶稣降生时那位大希律的孙子。他很想讨犹太领袖的欢心，以巩固自己的王位。

**2. 雅各被杀。** 「用刀杀了约翰的哥哥雅各。」（12:2）路加只用了短短一句话。雅各是耶稣最亲近的三个门徒之一，曾和约翰一起求坐在耶稣的左右；耶稣对他们说：「我所喝的杯，你们也要喝。」（可10:39）他是十二使徒中第一个殉道的。

**3. 他见犹太人喜欢这事。** 「他见犹太人喜欢这事，又去捉拿彼得。那时正是除酵的日子。」（12:3）希律看见杀雅各讨了人的喜欢，就再抓彼得。那时又是逾越节，正是耶稣受难的那个节期。

**4. 教会却……** 「于是彼得被囚在监里；教会却为他切切地祷告神。」（12:5）这节经文的两半形成对比：一边是监牢、兵丁和王的权势；一边是祷告。「切切地」原文有「伸展、竭力」的意思。路22:44描写耶稣「祷告更加恳切」，用的是同一个字的比较级（有些古抄本没有路22:43-44这两节）；彼得自己也用这个字劝信徒「彼此切实相爱」（彼前1:22）。教会没有武器，没有政治影响力，只有祷告。

> 要点：在同一个王手下，雅各被杀，彼得却蒙了拯救。神没有向我们解释为什么；但雅各喝的，是主早已先喝过的杯。教会该做的，就是切切地祷告，把结果交在那位自己也走过死荫的主手里。

## 深读 12:6-19 天使、铁门与不敢相信的祷告者

**1. 睡在两个兵丁当中。** 「希律将要提他出来的前一夜，彼得被两条铁链锁着，睡在两个兵丁当中。」（12:6）明天就可能被处死，彼得却睡着了，而且睡得很沉，天使得「拍彼得的肋旁」，才「拍醒了他」（12:7）。这让人想起诗篇：「我必安然躺下睡觉，因为独有你——耶和华使我安然居住。」（诗4:8）

**2. 一步一步的拯救。** 天使说：「快快起来！」铁链就脱落了；又说：「束上带子，穿上鞋。」「披上外衣，跟着我来。」（12:7-8）他们经过两层看守，「来到临街的铁门，那门自己开了」（12:10）。神的拯救是大能的，也是细致的，连鞋子和外衣都吩咐了。

**3. 「我现在真知道。」** 彼得起先「只当见了异象」（12:9）；到了街上，他才醒悟过来：「我现在真知道主差遣他的使者，救我脱离希律的手。」（12:11）天使吩咐「束上带子，穿上鞋」，正像逾越节的吩咐「腰间束带，脚上穿鞋」（出12:11）；「脱离希律的手」也呼应「脱离埃及人和法老的手」（出18:10）。这和以色列出埃及很相似：也是逾越节，也是在夜里，也是神亲自从王的手中拯救他的百姓。

**4. 「你是疯了！」** 彼得来到马可的母亲马利亚家，那里「有好些人聚集祷告」（12:12）。使女罗大听出是彼得的声音，「就欢喜的顾不得开门」，跑进去告诉众人；他们却说：「你是疯了！」又说：「必是他的天使！」（12:14-15）他们正为彼得祷告，彼得站在门外，他们却不相信。路加记这件事，带着一点幽默，也带着很大的安慰：神垂听祷告，不是因为我们的信心有多完全，而是因为他信实。

**5. 「彼得不住地敲门。」** 「彼得不住地敲门。他们开了门，看见他，就甚惊奇。」（12:16）蒙拯救的人站在门外，祷告的人还在屋里争论。这个画面既好笑，又真实。

**6. 告诉雅各。** 彼得说：「你们把这事告诉雅各和众弟兄。」（12:17）这个雅各不是刚被杀的使徒雅各，而应当是主的兄弟雅各，他后来成了耶路撒冷教会的领袖（15:13；加1:19）。然后彼得「往别处去了」。从此，使徒行传很少再提到彼得，只在第十五章耶路撒冷会议时再出现一次。

> 要点：神的回答，常常比我们的祷告更大，也比我们的信心更大。教会切切地祷告，却不敢相信祷告蒙了应允；神还是应允了。

## 深读 12:20-24 不归荣耀给神的王

**1. 朝服与讲论。** 「希律在所定的日子，穿上朝服，坐在位上，对他们讲论一番。百姓喊着说：『这是神的声音，不是人的声音。』」（12:21-22）犹太历史学家约瑟夫也记载了这件事（《犹太古史》19.343-350）：希律穿着银色的袍子，在阳光下闪闪发亮，众人称他为神，他没有拒绝；不久就腹痛，五天后死去。

**2. 不归荣耀给神。** 「希律不归荣耀给神，所以主的使者立刻罚他，他被虫所咬，气就绝了。」（12:23）对比很清楚：哥尼流向彼得下拜，彼得说「你起来，我也是人」（10:26）；百姓称希律为神，希律却接受了。他的罪不是坐在王位上，而是把只属于神的荣耀据为己有。

**3. 同一位主，同一个动作。** 在12:7，「有主的一个使者」来拯救彼得；在12:23，「主的使者」来审判希律。「拍」（12:7）和「罚」（12:23）在原文是同一个动词「击打」：主的使者击打彼得，是叫他醒来得救；击打希律，是叫他倒下受审。同一位主，向谦卑祷告的教会施行拯救，向骄傲逼迫的王施行审判。

**4. 神的道日见兴旺。** 「神的道日见兴旺，越发广传。」（12:24）这一章以希律逼迫开始，以希律被虫所咬结束；而神的道却一直在增长。这是使徒行传的又一个「进展报告」。王会死，神的道却长存（赛40:8）。

> 要点：这一章的主角不是希律，也不是彼得，而是神。他拯救、他审判，他的道兴旺；一切荣耀都只归给他。

## 深读 12:25 回到安提阿

**带着马可。** 「巴拿巴和扫罗办完了他们供给的事，就从耶路撒冷回来，带着称呼马可的约翰同去。」（12:25）这节经文把故事接回第十一章的赈灾，也为第十三章的宣教旅程铺路。马可就是第12节那位马利亚的儿子，他的家正是教会聚集祷告的地方。他后来会在宣教途中离开（13:13），多年以后，保罗却说他「在传道的事上于我有益处」（提后4:11）。

## 默想

**默想：** 同一章里，雅各被杀，彼得被救。我是否有过这样的经历：为两个人同样迫切地祷告，一个得了医治，一个却离世了？神没有在这里向我们解释；但他让我们知道，雅各和彼得都在他的手中，雅各的死并没有逃出他的掌管，在神眼中也同样宝贵：「在耶和华眼中，看圣民之死极为宝贵。」（诗116:15）我可以在他面前带着眼泪问；在不明白的时候，我能否仍然信靠那位为我舍了自己儿子的父？那些祷告的人，听见彼得在敲门，却说「你是疯了」。我的祷告是否也常常比我的信心走得更远？我可以感恩，神应允祷告，不是按照我信心的大小，而是按照他的信实。最后，希律接受了只属于神的荣耀。当我的工作、事业或服事有成果的时候，我心里会悄悄地把功劳归给谁？把荣耀归给神，不是我的损失，而是我的释放：我不必作自己的神，也不必扛起那份只有神扛得起的荣耀。「耶和华啊，荣耀不要归与我们，不要归与我们；要因你的慈爱和诚实归在你的名下！」（诗115:1）

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 12 turns the camera from Antioch back to Jerusalem and sets a king against a praying church. **Herod persecutes the church** (vv. 1–5): James is killed with the sword and Peter is imprisoned, "but earnest prayer for him was made to God by the church." **Peter freed** (vv. 6–19): an angel leads him out at night, but those praying can't believe it. **Herod's death** (vv. 20–23): he accepts the people's acclaim as a god, "did not give God the glory," and is eaten by worms. Then a summary: "But the word of God increased and multiplied" (v. 24).

At the start of the chapter, Herod seems to control everything: he can kill, arrest, and lock Peter under heavy guard. By the end, Herod is dead and the word of God is growing. This also closes the first half of Acts: from chapter 13 the story's focus turns to Paul and his missionary journeys.

## Going Deeper: 12:1–5 One Killed, One Imprisoned

**1. King Herod.** This Herod is Herod Agrippa I (king from AD 37, ruling all Judea AD 41–44), grandson of the Herod the Great who ruled when Jesus was born. He was eager to please the Jewish leaders to secure his throne.

**2. James killed.** "He killed James the brother of John with the sword" (12:2). Luke gives it a single sentence. James was one of Jesus' three closest disciples; he and John once asked to sit at Jesus' right and left, and Jesus told them, "The cup that I drink you will drink" (Mark 10:39). He was the first of the Twelve to be martyred.

**3. He saw that it pleased the Jews.** "And when he saw that it pleased the Jews, he proceeded to arrest Peter also. This was during the days of Unleavened Bread" (12:3). Seeing that killing James won him favor, Herod went after Peter too. And it was Passover again, the same feast as Jesus' death.

**4. But the church...** "So Peter was kept in prison, but earnest prayer for him was made to God by the church" (12:5). The two halves of the verse stand in contrast: on one side, prison, soldiers and royal power; on the other, prayer. "Earnest" means "stretched out, intense." Luke 22:44 uses the comparative of the same adverb for Jesus, who "prayed more earnestly" (though some early manuscripts lack vv. 43–44); Peter himself uses it in "love one another earnestly" (1 Pet 1:22). The church had no weapons and no political influence; it had prayer.

> The point: Under the same king, James died and Peter was rescued. God doesn't explain why; but the cup James drank was one his Lord had drunk first. What the church can do is pray earnestly and leave the outcome in the hands of the Lord who has himself passed through death.

## Going Deeper: 12:6–19 An Angel, an Iron Gate and Unbelieving Intercessors

**1. Sleeping between two soldiers.** "Now when Herod was about to bring him out, on that very night, Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains" (12:6). He might be executed in the morning, yet Peter was asleep, and so soundly that the angel had to strike him on the side to wake him (12:7). It recalls the psalm: "In peace I will both lie down and sleep; for you alone, O LORD, make me dwell in safety" (Ps 4:8).

**2. A step-by-step rescue.** The angel said, "Get up quickly," and the chains fell off; then, "Dress yourself and put on your sandals," and "Wrap your cloak around you and follow me" (12:7–8). They passed two guards and "came to the iron gate leading into the city. It opened for them of its own accord" (12:10). God's rescue is mighty, and also attentive to detail, down to sandals and cloak.

**3. "Now I am sure."** At first Peter "thought he was seeing a vision" (12:9); out in the street he came to himself: "Now I am sure that the Lord has sent his angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod" (12:11). "Dress yourself and put on your sandals" echoes the Passover command "with your belt fastened, your sandals on your feet" (Exod 12:11), and "from the hand of Herod" echoes "out of the hand of Pharaoh" (Exod 18:10). It closely resembles the exodus: also at Passover, also at night, also God himself rescuing his people from a king's hand.

**4. "You are out of your mind."** Peter went to the house of Mary, Mark's mother, "where many were gathered together and were praying" (12:12). The servant girl Rhoda recognized his voice, and "in her joy she did not open the gate" but ran in to tell everyone; they said, "You are out of your mind," and "It is his angel!" (12:14–15). They were praying for Peter, Peter was at the gate, and they didn't believe it. Luke tells this with a touch of humor and a great deal of comfort: God answers prayer not because our faith is perfect but because he is faithful.

**5. "Peter continued knocking."** "But Peter continued knocking, and when they opened, they saw him and were amazed" (12:16). The rescued man stands outside while the intercessors argue inside. The scene is funny, and true to life.

**6. Tell James.** Peter said, "Tell these things to James and to the brothers" (12:17). This isn't the apostle James just killed but almost certainly James the Lord's brother, who would become the leader of the Jerusalem church (15:13; Gal 1:19). Then Peter "went to another place." From here on Acts rarely mentions Peter; he reappears only once, at the Jerusalem council in chapter 15.

> The point: God's answers are often bigger than our prayers, and bigger than our faith. The church prayed earnestly yet couldn't believe its prayer was answered; God answered anyway.

## Going Deeper: 12:20–24 A King Who Did Not Give God the Glory

**1. Royal robes and an oration.** "On an appointed day Herod put on his royal robes, took his seat upon the throne, and delivered an oration to them. And the people were shouting, 'The voice of a god, and not of a man!'" (12:21–22). The Jewish historian Josephus also records the event (*Antiquities* 19.343–350): Herod wore a robe of silver that shone in the sunlight; the crowd hailed him as a god and he didn't refuse; soon after he was seized with abdominal pain and died five days later.

**2. He did not give God the glory.** "Immediately an angel of the Lord struck him down, because he did not give God the glory, and he was eaten by worms and breathed his last" (12:23). The contrast is clear: when Cornelius bowed before Peter, Peter said, "Stand up; I too am a man" (10:26); when the people called Herod a god, he accepted it. His sin wasn't sitting on a throne but claiming for himself the glory that belongs to God alone.

**3. The same Lord, the same verb.** In 12:7 "an angel of the Lord" comes to rescue Peter; in 12:23 "an angel of the Lord" comes to judge Herod. In Greek, "struck" in 12:7 and "struck down" in 12:23 are the same verb: the angel of the Lord strikes Peter to wake him for rescue and strikes Herod for judgment. The same Lord rescues a humble, praying church and judges a proud, persecuting king.

**4. The word of God increased.** "But the word of God increased and multiplied" (12:24). The chapter opens with Herod persecuting and ends with Herod eaten by worms, while God's word keeps growing. This is another of Acts' "progress reports." Kings die; the word of God stands forever (Isa 40:8).

> The point: The main character of this chapter isn't Herod, or even Peter, but God. He rescues, he judges, his word grows; all glory belongs to him alone.

## Going Deeper: 12:25 Back to Antioch

**Bringing Mark.** "And Barnabas and Saul returned from Jerusalem when they had completed their service, bringing with them John, whose other name was Mark" (12:25). This verse ties the story back to the famine relief of chapter 11 and prepares for the missionary journey of chapter 13. Mark is the son of the Mary of verse 12, whose house was where the church gathered to pray. He would later leave the mission partway (13:13), yet years afterward Paul would call him "very useful to me for ministry" (2 Tim 4:11).

## Meditation

**Reflect:** In one chapter James is killed and Peter is rescued. Have I ever prayed just as earnestly for two people, and one was healed while the other died? God gives no explanation here; but he lets us know that James and Peter were both in his hands, and James's death did not slip out of his control; it was no less precious to God: "Precious in the sight of the LORD is the death of his saints" (Ps 116:15). I may come to him with tears and questions; when I don't understand, can I still trust the Father who gave up his own Son for me? Those praying heard Peter knocking and said, "You are out of your mind." Do my prayers often run ahead of my faith? I can be thankful that God answers prayer not according to the size of my faith but according to his faithfulness. Finally, Herod accepted glory that belongs to God alone. When my work, career or ministry bears fruit, to whom do I quietly give the credit in my heart? Giving God the glory isn't my loss but my release: I don't have to be my own god, or carry a glory only God can bear. "Not to us, O LORD, not to us, but to your name give glory, for the sake of your steadfast love and your faithfulness!" (Ps 115:1)
