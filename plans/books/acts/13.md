## 本章脉络

第十三章是使徒行传的第二个起点。从这里开始，故事的中心从耶路撒冷转到安提阿，焦点从彼得转到保罗；福音也开始有计划地向「地极」推进。

本章有四段：**安提阿的差遣**（1-3节），圣灵在敬拜中说话，教会差出巴拿巴和扫罗；**塞浦路斯**（4-12节），行法术的以吕马瞎了眼，罗马方伯信了主；**彼西底的安提阿**（13-41节），保罗在会堂里的第一篇长篇讲道，以「称义」作为高峰；**两种回应**（42-52节），外邦人欢喜，犹太人嫉妒，使徒被赶出境外，门徒却「满心喜乐」。

## 深读 13:1-3 在敬拜中被差遣

**1. 一个多元的领袖群体。** 「在安提阿的教会中，有几位先知和教师，就是巴拿巴和称呼尼结的西面、古利奈人路求，与分封之王希律同养的马念，并扫罗。」（13:1）巴拿巴来自塞浦路斯；「尼结」是拉丁文「黑」的意思，西面可能来自非洲；路求来自北非的古利奈；马念是和分封之王希律安提帕一同长大的人（原文这个词也可指宫廷近臣）；扫罗是大数的法利赛人。安提阿教会的领袖，本身就是福音拆毁隔断之墙的见证。

**2. 事奉主、禁食的时候。** 「他们事奉主、禁食的时候，圣灵说：『要为我分派巴拿巴和扫罗，去做我召他们所做的工。』」（13:2）教会历史上第一次有计划的宣教差遣，不是从策略会议开始的，而是从敬拜和禁食中开始的。「事奉主」原文是 *leitourgeō*，常用来指圣殿里的敬拜事奉。

**3. 「为我」分派。** 圣灵说「要为我分派」：宣教士首先是属于神的，然后才是属于教会的。「我召他们」：呼召来自圣灵。可是圣灵把这个呼召交给教会去确认、去差遣：「于是禁食祷告，按手在他们头上，就打发他们去了。」（13:3）第4节说：「他们既被圣灵差遣。」差遣的，是圣灵，也是教会；两者不冲突。

**4. 差出最好的。** 安提阿教会差出的，不是闲着没事的人，而是他们最有恩赐的两位教师。神先把他最好的，就是他的独生子，赐给了我们；真正明白这福音的教会，也会甘心把最好的献出去。

> 要点：宣教不是教会的一项活动，而是圣灵的工作；教会在敬拜中聆听，又甘心把最好的人交给神。

## 深读 13:4-12 塞浦路斯：方伯与行法术的人

**1. 先到会堂。** 他们到了撒拉米，「就在犹太人各会堂里传讲神的道」（13:5）。这是保罗一贯的做法：先向犹太人，再向外邦人（参罗1:16）。

**2. 方伯士求·保罗。** 在帕弗，他们遇见罗马方伯士求·保罗，「是个通达人」，「要听神的道」（13:7）。罗马帝国的高官，愿意听两位犹太巡回传道人讲道。

**3. 以吕马的敌挡。** 行法术的巴·耶稣（又名以吕马）「敌挡使徒，要叫方伯不信真道」（13:8）。保罗「被圣灵充满，定睛看他」，说：「你这充满各样诡诈奸恶，魔鬼的儿子……你混乱主的正道还不止住吗？」（13:9-10）「巴·耶稣」意思是「耶稣（约书亚）的儿子」，保罗却称他为「魔鬼的儿子」。「混乱主的正道」原文是「把主笔直的道路弄弯」，呼应以赛亚书「在沙漠地修平我们神的道」（赛40:3）。

**4. 暂且瞎眼。** 「现在主的手加在你身上，你要瞎眼，暂且不见日光。」（13:11）保罗自己也曾在大马士革路上瞎眼三天，后来眼睛得开、归向主。以吕马的瞎眼是审判；但「暂且」二字，或许也给他留下了悔改的余地。

**5. 「很希奇主的道」。** 「方伯看见所做的事，很希奇主的道，就信了。」（13:12）路加说得很细：方伯看见的是神迹，希奇的却是「主的道」。神迹的作用，是叫人注意神的话。

**6. 扫罗又名保罗。** 「扫罗又名保罗。」（13:9）从这里开始，除了保罗自己讲述归主经历的地方（22:7，13；26:14），路加都称他为「保罗」。扫罗是他的希伯来名字，保罗是他的罗马名字。保罗开始向外邦人传福音，路加也就改用外邦人熟悉的这个名字。「巴拿巴和扫罗」也渐渐变成了「保罗和他的同人」（13:13）。

## 深读 13:13-41 保罗在会堂里的讲道

**1. 约翰马可离开。** 「约翰就离开他们，回耶路撒冷去。」（13:13）路加没有说原因。这件事后来在保罗和巴拿巴之间引起了一场严重的争执（15:37-39）。

**2. 「若有什么劝勉众人的话」。** 在彼西底的安提阿，管会堂的读完律法和先知书以后，请他们说话（13:15）。保罗「站起来，举手」（13:16），这是路加记下的第一篇保罗讲道。他的听众有两种人：「以色列人和一切敬畏神的人」，也就是犹太人和像哥尼流那样敬畏神的外邦人。

**3. 从出埃及到大卫。** 保罗先回顾以色列的历史：神拣选列祖，领他们出埃及，在旷野容忍他们，赐他们迦南地，兴起士师和扫罗王，然后「选立大卫作他们的王」（13:17-22）。他引用神的话：「我寻得耶西的儿子大卫，他是合我心意的人。」（13:22；参诗89:20；撒上13:14）

**4. 从大卫到耶稣。** 「从这人的后裔中，神已经照着所应许的，为以色列人立了一位救主，就是耶稣。」（13:23）整段历史都指向这一位。和司提反一样，保罗讲历史，是要讲基督；不同的是，司提反的重点是「你们弃绝了他」，保罗的重点是「神应验了应许」。

**5. 不认识他，却应验了预言。** 「耶路撒冷居住的人和他们的官长，因为不认识基督，也不明白每安息日所读众先知的书，就把基督定了死罪，正应了先知的预言。」（13:27）他们每个安息日都读先知书，却没有认出先知书所讲的那一位；而他们定他的罪，正好应验了他们所读的经文。

**6. 三处经文证明复活。** 保罗用三处经文讲耶稣的复活：
- 「你是我的儿子，我今日生你」（13:33；诗2:7）。这里的「生」不是指耶稣的起源，他本是永远的神子；而是指神藉着复活，公开宣告并高举他为有能力的神子、受膏的王（参罗1:4「以大能显明是神的儿子」）。
- 「我必将所应许大卫那圣洁、可靠的恩典赐给你们」（13:34；赛55:3）。神给大卫的应许是永远的，只有一位永远活着的大卫子孙，才能使这应许「可靠」。
- 「你必不叫你的圣者见朽坏」（13:35；诗16:10）。在七十士译本里，「圣洁……的恩典」（*ta hosia*）和「你的圣者」（*ton hosion sou*）是同一个字，所以保罗把这两处经文放在一起读。保罗的论证和彼得在五旬节的论证一样（2:25-31）：大卫「已见朽坏」，「惟独神所复活的，他并未见朽坏」（13:36-37）。

**7. 讲道的高峰：称义。** 「所以，弟兄们，你们当晓得：赦罪的道是由这人传给你们的。你们靠摩西的律法，在一切不得称义的事上信靠这人，就都得称义了。」（13:38-39）整卷使徒行传，只有这里用到保罗书信的核心词「称义」（*dikaioō*）。和合本这一句不容易读，意思是：凡靠摩西的律法在一切事上都不能称义的，信靠这人就都得称义了。这不是说律法能使人在某些事上称义、耶稣补足其余的；而是说律法在任何事上都不能使人称义，耶稣却使信他的人在一切事上称义。这正是保罗在加拉太书和罗马书里要详细阐明的福音。

**8. 警告。** 保罗最后引用哈巴谷书：「你们这轻慢的人要观看，要惊奇，要灭亡；因为在你们的时候，我行一件事，虽有人告诉你们，你们总是不信。」（13:41；哈1:5）哈巴谷当年说的「一件事」，是神要兴起巴比伦来审判犹大；现在神行的「一件事」，是在耶稣里施行拯救。（保罗的引文跟随七十士译本「你们这轻慢的人」；希伯来文本作「你们要向列国中观看」。）轻慢这救恩，比当年轻慢审判的警告更危险。

> 要点：保罗讲以色列的整段历史，是为了讲一个人：大卫的子孙耶稣。他的死应验了经文，他的复活证明了神的应许；律法不能叫人称义，他却叫一切信靠他的人「都得称义了」。

## 深读 13:42-52 欢喜与嫉妒

**1. 合城的人几乎都来了。** 「到下安息日，合城的人几乎都来聚集，要听神的道。」（13:44）福音向所有人敞开，这消息一传开，几乎全城的人都来了。

**2. 满心嫉妒。** 「但犹太人看见人这样多，就满心嫉妒，硬驳保罗所说的话，并且毁谤。」（13:45）他们嫉妒的，或许不只是人数，更是这些外邦人不必先成为犹太人，就可以直接蒙神接纳。

**3. 「断定自己不配得永生」。** 「神的道先讲给你们原是应当的；只因你们弃绝这道，断定自己不配得永生，我们就转向外邦人去。」（13:46）路加强调的是他们自己的责任：他们弃绝了道，就是「断定自己不配得永生」。

**4. 外邦人的光。** 「因为主曾这样吩咐我们说：我已经立你作外邦人的光，叫你施行救恩，直到地极。」（13:47；赛49:6）以赛亚书这句话原是讲「耶和华的仆人」，耶稣就是那位仆人（参路2:32，圣殿里的老人西面称耶稣为「照亮外邦人的光」）。现在保罗和巴拿巴说，这是主「吩咐我们」的：他们跟随那位仆人，也一同承担仆人的使命。

**5. 「凡预定得永生的人都信了」。** 「外邦人听见这话，就欢喜了，赞美神的道；凡预定得永生的人都信了。」（13:48）把这节和第46节放在一起读，就看见圣经的两面：人弃绝福音，要为自己负责（46节）；人相信福音，是因为神的预定和恩典（48节）。神的拣选不叫人骄傲，因为信心本身就是恩典；也不叫人灰心，因为福音仍然向每一个人宣讲。

**6. 跺下脚上的尘土。** 他们被赶出境外，「二人对着众人跺下脚上的尘土，就往以哥念去了」（13:51）。这是照着耶稣的吩咐（路9:5），表示「我们已经尽了责任，后果由你们自己承担」。

**7. 满心喜乐。** 「门徒满心喜乐，又被圣灵充满。」（13:52）使徒被赶走了，留下来的初信者却满心喜乐。他们的喜乐不倚靠传道人在不在，而倚靠那位住在他们里面的圣灵。

> 要点：同一篇讲道，有人嫉妒，有人欢喜。弃绝福音的人，是自己断定自己不配；相信福音的人，却只能把功劳归给神的预定和恩典。

## 默想

**默想：** 圣灵在安提阿说话，不是在一场「求带领」的会议上，而是在教会「事奉主、禁食」的时候。他们不是把敬拜当作得到答案的手段，而是在寻求主自己；差遣正是在这样的敬拜中临到的。我来到神面前，是不是常常只为要一个答案：这份工作接不接，这段关系要不要继续？这一周，我能不能找一段时间，先不急着求什么，单单为他是谁而敬拜他，相信他会在他的时候说话？保罗说，律法在一切事上都不能叫人称义，凡信靠耶稣的，却「就都得称义了」。我心里有没有一件事，是我觉得这个「都」盖不过去的：一次堕胎、一段不能告人的关系、一句伤透家人的话？我也许已经认过很多次罪，心里却还觉得要再多做一点，才算真的被赦免。可是称义不是神慢慢消气，而是一次宣判：在基督里，「无罪」。今天我可以把那件事带到十字架前，不是为了说服他赦免我，而是相信他已经这样宣告了。最后，使徒被赶出城，初信的门徒却「满心喜乐，又被圣灵充满」。如果我所依靠的牧者、带我信主的弟兄姊妹离开了，我的喜乐还在吗？人会离开；那位住在我里面的圣灵却从来没有离开，他一直把我指向那位称我为义的主。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 13 is Acts' second starting point. From here the story's center moves from Jerusalem to Antioch, its focus from Peter to Paul, and the gospel begins a deliberate advance toward "the end of the earth."

The chapter has four parts: **sending from Antioch** (vv. 1–3), where the Spirit speaks in worship and the church sends out Barnabas and Saul; **Cyprus** (vv. 4–12), where the magician Elymas is blinded and a Roman proconsul believes; **Pisidian Antioch** (vv. 13–41), Paul's first long synagogue sermon, which peaks in "justification"; and **two responses** (vv. 42–52): Gentiles rejoice, Jews are jealous, the apostles are driven out, and the disciples are "filled with joy."

## Going Deeper: 13:1–3 Sent Out in Worship

**1. A diverse leadership.** "Now there were in the church at Antioch prophets and teachers, Barnabas, Simeon who was called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen a member of the court of Herod the tetrarch, and Saul" (13:1). Barnabas was from Cyprus; "Niger" is Latin for "black," and Simeon may have come from Africa; Lucius was from Cyrene in North Africa; Manaen had been brought up with Herod Antipas (the word can also mean a close member of his court); Saul was a Pharisee from Tarsus. The leadership of the Antioch church was itself a witness to the gospel breaking down dividing walls.

**2. While they were worshiping and fasting.** "While they were worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, 'Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them'" (13:2). The first planned missionary sending in church history didn't begin in a strategy meeting; it began in worship and fasting. "Worshiping" is *leitourgeō*, a word often used for the worship service of the temple.

**3. Set apart "for me."** The Spirit says "set apart for me": missionaries belong first to God, and only then to the church. "I have called them": the call comes from the Spirit. Yet the Spirit gives the church the task of confirming and sending: "Then after fasting and praying they laid their hands on them and sent them off" (13:3). Verse 4 says they were "sent out by the Holy Spirit." The sending is the Spirit's and the church's; the two don't conflict.

**4. Sending their best.** The Antioch church didn't send people who had nothing else to do; it sent its two most gifted teachers. God first gave us his best, his only Son; a church that has understood that gospel will gladly give away its best.

> The point: Mission isn't one of the church's programs; it is the Spirit's work. The church listens in worship and gladly hands its best people to God.

## Going Deeper: 13:4–12 Cyprus: The Proconsul and the Magician

**1. Synagogues first.** At Salamis "they proclaimed the word of God in the synagogues of the Jews" (13:5). This was Paul's consistent pattern: to the Jew first, and also to the Greek (cf. Rom 1:16).

**2. The proconsul Sergius Paulus.** At Paphos they met the Roman proconsul Sergius Paulus, "a man of intelligence," who "sought to hear the word of God" (13:7). A high Roman official was willing to listen to two traveling Jewish preachers.

**3. Elymas's opposition.** The magician Bar-Jesus, also called Elymas, "opposed them, seeking to turn the proconsul away from the faith" (13:8). Paul, "filled with the Holy Spirit, looked intently at him and said, 'You son of the devil, you enemy of all righteousness, full of all deceit and villainy, will you not stop making crooked the straight paths of the Lord?'" (13:9–10). "Bar-Jesus" means "son of Jesus (Joshua)," but Paul calls him "son of the devil." "Making crooked the straight paths" echoes Isaiah's "make straight in the desert a highway for our God" (Isa 40:3).

**4. Blind for a time.** "And now, behold, the hand of the Lord is upon you, and you will be blind and unable to see the sun for a time" (13:11). Paul himself had been blinded for three days on the Damascus road before his eyes were opened. Elymas's blindness was judgment, but "for a time" may also leave room for repentance.

**5. Astonished at the teaching.** "Then the proconsul believed, when he saw what had occurred, for he was astonished at the teaching of the Lord" (13:12). Luke is careful: the proconsul saw a miracle, but what astonished him was "the teaching of the Lord." Miracles serve to draw attention to God's word.

**6. Saul, also called Paul.** "But Saul, who was also called Paul" (13:9). From here on Luke calls him "Paul," except where Paul retells his conversion (22:7, 13; 26:14). Saul was his Hebrew name and Paul his Roman name; as he begins taking the gospel into the Gentile world, Luke uses the name that world knew. "Barnabas and Saul" also gradually becomes "Paul and his companions" (13:13).

## Going Deeper: 13:13–41 Paul's Synagogue Sermon

**1. John Mark leaves.** "And John left them and returned to Jerusalem" (13:13). Luke gives no reason. Later this would cause a sharp disagreement between Paul and Barnabas (15:37–39).

**2. "Any word of exhortation."** At Pisidian Antioch, after the reading of the Law and the Prophets, the synagogue rulers invited them to speak (13:15). Paul "stood up, and motioning with his hand" (13:16), began the first sermon of Paul's that Luke records for us. He addressed two groups: "Men of Israel and you who fear God," that is, Jews and God-fearing Gentiles like Cornelius.

**3. From the exodus to David.** Paul first reviews Israel's history: God chose the fathers, led them out of Egypt, put up with them in the wilderness, gave them Canaan, raised up judges and King Saul, and then "raised up David to be their king" (13:17–22). He quotes God's word: "I have found in David the son of Jesse a man after my heart" (13:22; cf. Ps 89:20; 1 Sam 13:14).

**4. From David to Jesus.** "Of this man's offspring God has brought to Israel a Savior, Jesus, as he promised" (13:23). The whole history points to this one person. Like Stephen, Paul tells history to tell of Christ; but where Stephen's emphasis was "you rejected him," Paul's is "God has kept his promise."

**5. Not recognizing him, yet fulfilling the prophets.** "For those who live in Jerusalem and their rulers, because they did not recognize him nor understand the utterances of the prophets, which are read every Sabbath, fulfilled them by condemning him" (13:27). They read the prophets every Sabbath but didn't recognize the one the prophets spoke of; and by condemning him they fulfilled the very Scriptures they read.

**6. Three Scriptures for the resurrection.** Paul uses three texts to speak of Jesus' resurrection:
- "You are my Son, today I have begotten you" (13:33; Ps 2:7). "Begotten" here doesn't refer to Jesus' origin, for he is the eternal Son. It refers to God publicly declaring and enthroning him, through the resurrection, as the messianic King, "the Son of God in power" (Rom 1:4).
- "I will give you the holy and sure blessings of David" (13:34; Isa 55:3). God's promise to David was everlasting; only a Son of David who lives forever can make those blessings "sure."
- "You will not let your Holy One see corruption" (13:35; Ps 16:10). In the Septuagint, "holy [blessings]" (*ta hosia*) and "your Holy One" (*ton hosion sou*) share one word, which is why Paul reads the two texts together. Paul's argument is the same as Peter's at Pentecost (2:25–31): David "saw corruption," "but he whom God raised up did not see corruption" (13:36–37).

**7. The sermon's peak: justification.** "Let it be known to you therefore, brothers, that through this man forgiveness of sins is proclaimed to you, and by him everyone who believes is freed from everything from which you could not be freed by the law of Moses" (13:38–39). "Freed" here translates *dikaioō*, "justify," the key word of Paul's letters, and the only place it appears in Acts. Luke is not saying the law justifies in some matters and Jesus completes the rest; the law could justify from nothing, and Jesus justifies the believer from everything. This is the gospel Paul will unfold in detail in Galatians and Romans.

**8. A warning.** Paul closes with Habakkuk: "Look, you scoffers, be astounded and perish; for I am doing a work in your days, a work that you will not believe, even if one tells it to you" (13:41; Hab 1:5). The "work" in Habakkuk's day was God raising up Babylon to judge Judah (Paul's wording, "you scoffers," follows the Septuagint; the Hebrew reads "Look among the nations"); the "work" God is doing now is salvation in Jesus. Scorning this salvation is more dangerous than scorning that warning of judgment.

> The point: Paul tells Israel's whole history to speak of one person: Jesus, the Son of David. His death fulfilled the Scriptures, his resurrection proved God's promises, and for everyone who believes in him he has done what the law could not: they are justified.

## Going Deeper: 13:42–52 Joy and Jealousy

**1. Almost the whole city came.** "The next Sabbath almost the whole city gathered to hear the word of the Lord" (13:44). The news that the gospel was open to all drew the entire city.

**2. Filled with jealousy.** "But when the Jews saw the crowds, they were filled with jealousy and began to contradict what was spoken by Paul, reviling him" (13:45). What provoked their jealousy may not have been only the crowd's size but the fact that these Gentiles could be accepted by God without first becoming Jews.

**3. "Judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life."** "It was necessary that the word of God be spoken first to you. Since you thrust it aside and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we are turning to the Gentiles" (13:46). Luke puts the weight on their own responsibility: by thrusting the word aside, they "judge themselves unworthy of eternal life."

**4. A light for the Gentiles.** "For so the Lord has commanded us, saying, 'I have made you a light for the Gentiles, that you may bring salvation to the ends of the earth'" (13:47; Isa 49:6). In Isaiah these words are spoken of "the Servant of the LORD," and Jesus is that Servant (cf. Luke 2:32, where Simeon calls Jesus "a light for revelation to the Gentiles"). Now Paul and Barnabas say the Lord has "commanded us": following the Servant, they share in the Servant's mission.

**5. "As many as were appointed to eternal life believed."** "And when the Gentiles heard this, they began rejoicing and glorifying the word of the Lord, and as many as were appointed to eternal life believed" (13:48). Read alongside verse 46, this shows both sides of Scripture: those who reject the gospel are responsible for doing so (v. 46); those who believe it do so because of God's appointment and grace (v. 48). God's choosing doesn't make anyone proud, since faith itself is grace; nor does it make anyone despair, since the gospel is still proclaimed to everyone.

**6. Shaking off the dust.** Driven out of the district, "they shook off the dust from their feet against them and went to Iconium" (13:51). This followed Jesus' instruction (Luke 9:5) and meant, "We have done our duty; you bear the consequences."

**7. Filled with joy.** "And the disciples were filled with joy and with the Holy Spirit" (13:52). The apostles were driven away, yet the new believers left behind were full of joy. Their joy didn't depend on whether the preachers stayed but on the Spirit who lived in them.

> The point: The same sermon drew jealousy from some and joy from others. Those who reject the gospel judge themselves unworthy; those who believe can credit only God's appointment and grace.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The Spirit spoke at Antioch not at a meeting called to seek guidance, but while the church was "worshiping the Lord and fasting." They weren't using worship to get an answer; they were seeking the Lord himself, and the sending came in the middle of that worship. When I come to God, is it often only to get an answer: should I take this job, should this relationship go on? This week, could I set aside some time to ask for nothing at first, and simply worship him for who he is, trusting him to speak in his time? Paul said that everyone who believes in Jesus is freed from everything the law could never free them from. Is there one thing in my heart that I feel this "everything" can't cover: an abortion, a relationship I've never told anyone about, words that wounded my family? I may have confessed it many times and still feel I must do a little more before I'm really forgiven. But justification isn't God slowly cooling down; it is a verdict, once for all: in Christ, "not guilty." Today I can bring that thing to the cross, not to persuade him to forgive me, but to believe that he has already said so. Finally, the apostles were driven out of town, yet the new disciples were "filled with joy and with the Holy Spirit." If the pastor I rely on, or the believers who led me to faith, were to leave, would my joy still be there? People leave; the Spirit who lives in me never has, and he keeps pointing me to the Lord who declared me righteous.
