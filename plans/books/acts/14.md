## 本章脉络

第十四章完成了第一次宣教旅程。**以哥念**（1-7节）：许多人信了，城里却分了党，使徒被迫逃走。**路司得**（8-20节）：一个生来瘸腿的人得医治，众人把巴拿巴和保罗当作希腊的神明，要向他们献祭；没过多久，同一群人却用石头打保罗，以为他死了。**回程**（21-28节）：他们回到刚刚逼迫过他们的城，坚固门徒，选立长老，然后回到差派他们的安提阿，述说「神怎样为外邦人开了信道的门」。

这一章有一个强烈的对比：人一会儿要拜他们，一会儿要杀他们。传道人若把信心建立在人的反应上，很快就会垮掉；保罗和巴拿巴所倚靠的，是那位「证明他的恩道」的主（14:3）。

## 深读 14:1-7 以哥念：恩道与分裂

**1. 犹太人和希腊人信的很多。** 「二人在以哥念同进犹太人的会堂，在那里讲的，叫犹太人和希腊人信的很多。」（14:1）福音一传开，就同时得着犹太人和外邦人。

**2. 他的恩道。** 「二人在那里住了多日，倚靠主放胆讲道，主藉他们的手施行神迹奇事，证明他的恩道。」（14:3）路加用一个美丽的名称称呼福音：「他的恩道」，就是关于神恩典的道。神迹在这里的作用，是「证明」这恩典的话是真的。

**3. 城里的人分了党。** 「城里的众人就分了党，有附从犹太人的，有附从使徒的。」（14:4）耶稣曾说，他来是要叫人「纷争」（路12:51）。福音带来平安，也带来分别。

**4. 逃走，然后继续传。** 有人要「用石头打他们」，「使徒知道了，就逃往吕高尼的路司得、特庇两个城和周围地方去，在那里传福音」（14:5-7）。逃避逼迫不是不忠心（参太10:23）；他们离开一个地方，只是为了在另一个地方继续传福音。

## 深读 14:8-18 路司得：「我们也是人」

**1. 有信心可得痊愈。** 路司得有一个人「生来是瘸腿的，从来没有走过」。「他听保罗讲道，保罗定睛看他，见他有信心，可得痊愈，就大声说：『你起来，两脚站直！』那人就跳起来，而且行走。」（14:8-10）这件事几乎和彼得在圣殿美门口医治瘸腿的人一模一样（3:1-10）。路加有意让我们看见：在外邦人中工作的保罗，和在犹太人中工作的彼得，是同一位主所用的。

**2. 神藉着人形降临。** 众人用本地的吕高尼话大喊：「有神藉着人形降临在我们中间了。」（14:11）他们称巴拿巴为宙斯，称保罗为希耳米，「因为他说话领首」（14:12）。这一带流传一个古老的传说：宙斯和希耳米曾化成人形到这里来，却没有人接待他们，只有一对贫穷的老夫妇款待了他们，后来全城遭了灾。也许路司得人不想再犯同样的错误。

**3. 撕开衣裳。** 宙斯庙的祭司牵着牛、拿着花圈要献祭，「巴拿巴、保罗二使徒听见，就撕开衣裳，跳进众人中间」（14:13-14）。撕开衣裳，是犹太人听见亵渎神的话时的反应。对他们来说，被人当作神来敬拜，不是荣耀，而是可怕的亵渎。

**4. 「我们也是人，性情和你们一样。」** 「诸君，为什么做这事呢？我们也是人，性情和你们一样。我们传福音给你们，是叫你们离弃这些虚妄，归向那创造天、地、海，和其中万物的永生神。」（14:15）对这些不认识旧约的外邦人，保罗不是从摩西和大卫讲起，而是从创造主讲起。这是向没有圣经背景的人传福音的第一个例子，到第十七章雅典的讲道会更完整。

**5. 神为自己留下的证据。** 「他在从前的世代，任凭万国各行其道；然而为自己未尝不显出证据来，就如常施恩惠，从天降雨，赏赐丰年，叫你们饮食饱足，满心喜乐。」（14:16-17）神学上称这为「普遍恩典」：神赐雨水和丰收给所有人，不论他们是否认识他。保罗在罗马书说，神的永能和神性「藉着所造之物就可以晓得，叫人无可推诿」（罗1:20）。每一碗饭、每一场雨，都在为神作见证。

**6. 仅仅拦住。** 「二人说了这些话，仅仅地拦住众人不献祭与他们。」（14:18）人心多么容易拜偶像：就在使徒面前，他们还几乎要把使徒当成偶像。

> 要点：路司得人不认识真神，就把行神迹的人当作神。保罗不肯接受这份荣耀，却把他们指向那位一直藉着雨水和丰收向他们施恩的创造主。

## 深读 14:19-20 被石头打，又起来

**1. 同一群人。** 「但有些犹太人从安提阿和以哥念来，挑唆众人，就用石头打保罗，以为他是死了，便拖到城外。」（14:19）前一天要向他献祭的人，现在要用石头打死他。人的赞美和人的愤怒，常常只隔着一点点距离。

**2. 保罗记得这件事。** 保罗后来说：「被石头打了一次。」（林后11:25）他也写信给提摩太，提到「我在安提阿、以哥念、路司得所遭遇的逼迫、苦难」，又说「从这一切苦难中，主都把我救出来了」（提后3:11）。提摩太就是路司得人（16:1）；他很可能亲眼看见了这件事。

**3. 他就起来，走进城去。** 「门徒正围着他，他就起来，走进城去。第二天，同巴拿巴往特庇去。」（14:20）路加没有说这是神迹，但这个画面很有力量：被石头打得像死了一样的人，站起来，走回打他的那座城。

## 深读 14:21-28 回程：坚固、劝勉、交托

**1. 原路回去。** 在特庇传福音以后，他们「就回路司得、以哥念、安提阿去」（14:21）。从特庇往东，就可以回到保罗的家乡大数，这是最近的路；他们却选择往回走，回到刚刚逼迫他们的城。为什么？为了那些初信的门徒。

**2. 「必须经历许多艰难」。** 他们「坚固门徒的心，劝他们恒守所信的道；又说：『我们进入神的国，必须经历许多艰难。』」（14:22）这句话很诚实。保罗没有对初信的人说「信了主，一切都会顺利」，而是说艰难是「必须」的。「必须」这个字（*dei*），路加常用来讲神的计划（参路24:26，和合本译作「应当」：「基督这样受害，又进入他的荣耀，岂不是应当的吗？」）。门徒所走的路，是他们的主先走过的路。

**3. 选立长老。** 「二人在各教会中选立了长老，又禁食祷告，就把他们交托所信的主。」（14:23）这些教会才成立几个月，保罗就为他们选立领袖。他相信，即使没有使徒在场，主也能保守他的教会。

**4. 交托所信的主。** 「就把他们交托所信的主」：保罗没有把这些教会看作是他自己的。他尽了责任，然后放手，把他们交在主的手里。

**5. 神为外邦人开了信道的门。** 回到叙利亚的安提阿，「就述说神藉他们所行的一切事，并神怎样为外邦人开了信道的门」（14:27）。请注意主语：不是「我们为外邦人开了门」，而是「神」。他们所报告的，是神的作为。

> 要点：保罗和巴拿巴没有隐瞒艰难，也没有把初信的人留给自己掌管；他们说出实话，选立长老，然后把一切交托给主。

## 默想

**默想：** 路司得人一天要向保罗献祭，第二天却用石头打他。我是否太在意别人对我的看法，以致被称赞时飘飘然，被批评时就一蹶不振？保罗的稳定，来自他知道自己「也是人」，也知道那位创造天地的永生神是谁。保罗对外邦人说，神「从天降雨，赏赐丰年，叫你们饮食饱足，满心喜乐」。我今天吃的每一顿饭，是否都让我想起那位赐食物的神？许多华人吃饭前会说「感谢老天爷」，却不认识那位真正赐雨水的主；我能不能在今天的饭桌上，向家人说出那位赐恩者的名字？最后，保罗对初信的人说：「我们进入神的国，必须经历许多艰难。」然后「把他们交托所信的主」。在我的生命中，有没有一个我很牵挂的人：一个将要离家的孩子，一位刚信主的朋友，一个我带过的小组？我已经尽了我能尽的；今天，我能不能把他交托在那位比我更爱他的主手里？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 14 completes the first missionary journey. **Iconium** (vv. 1–7): many believe, but the city is divided and the apostles have to flee. **Lystra** (vv. 8–20): a man crippled from birth is healed, and the crowds take Barnabas and Paul for Greek gods and try to offer sacrifices to them; soon after, the same crowds stone Paul and leave him for dead. **The journey home** (vv. 21–28): they go back to the very cities that had just persecuted them, strengthen the disciples, appoint elders, and return to Antioch, which had sent them, to report "how he had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles."

The chapter turns on a sharp contrast: one moment people want to worship them, the next they want to kill them. A preacher who builds his confidence on people's reactions will soon collapse; Paul and Barnabas relied on the Lord "who bore witness to the word of his grace" (14:3).

## Going Deeper: 14:1–7 Iconium: The Word of Grace and Division

**1. Many Jews and Greeks believed.** "Now at Iconium they entered together into the Jewish synagogue and spoke in such a way that a great number of both Jews and Greeks believed" (14:1). As soon as the gospel spread, it won both Jews and Gentiles.

**2. The word of his grace.** "So they remained for a long time, speaking boldly for the Lord, who bore witness to the word of his grace, granting signs and wonders to be done by their hands" (14:3). Luke gives the gospel a beautiful name: "the word of his grace," the message about God's grace. The signs here serve to "bear witness" that this word of grace is true.

**3. The city was divided.** "But the people of the city were divided; some sided with the Jews and some with the apostles" (14:4). Jesus had said he came to bring "division" (Luke 12:51). The gospel brings peace, and it also divides.

**4. Fleeing, and preaching on.** When there was an attempt "to mistreat them and to stone them, they learned of it and fled to Lystra and Derbe, cities of Lycaonia, and to the surrounding country, and there they continued to preach the gospel" (14:5–7). Escaping persecution isn't unfaithfulness (cf. Matt 10:23); they left one place only to keep preaching in another.

## Going Deeper: 14:8–18 Lystra: "We Also Are Men"

**1. Faith to be made well.** At Lystra there was a man "crippled from birth" who "had never walked." "He listened to Paul speaking. And Paul, looking intently at him and seeing that he had faith to be made well, said in a loud voice, 'Stand upright on your feet.' And he sprang up and began walking" (14:8–10). It closely mirrors Peter healing the lame man at the temple's Beautiful Gate (3:1–10). Luke wants us to see that Paul among the Gentiles and Peter among the Jews are used by the same Lord.

**2. Gods in the likeness of men.** The crowds shouted in their local Lycaonian language, "The gods have come down to us in the likeness of men!" (14:11). They called Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes, "because he was the chief speaker" (14:12). An old legend was told in this region: Zeus and Hermes once visited in human form and no one welcomed them except a poor elderly couple, and later the town was destroyed. Perhaps the people of Lystra didn't want to make the same mistake.

**3. Tearing their garments.** The priest of Zeus brought oxen and garlands to offer sacrifice, but "when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard of it, they tore their garments and rushed out into the crowd" (14:13–14). Tearing one's garments was the Jewish response to hearing blasphemy. To them, being worshiped as gods was no honor but a dreadful blasphemy.

**4. "We also are men, of like nature with you."** "Men, why are you doing these things? We also are men, of like nature with you, and we bring you good news, that you should turn from these vain things to a living God, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and all that is in them" (14:15). For these Gentiles who didn't know the Old Testament, Paul doesn't start with Moses and David but with the Creator. This is the first example of preaching to people with no biblical background; the Athens sermon in chapter 17 will develop it more fully.

**5. The witness God left himself.** "In past generations he allowed all the nations to walk in their own ways. Yet he did not leave himself without witness, for he did good by giving you rains from heaven and fruitful seasons, satisfying your hearts with food and gladness" (14:16–17). Theologians call this "common grace": God gives rain and harvest to everyone, whether or not they know him. In Romans Paul says God's eternal power and divine nature "have been clearly perceived... in the things that have been made. So they are without excuse" (Rom 1:20). Every bowl of rice and every rainfall bears witness to God.

**6. Scarcely restrained.** "Even with these words they scarcely restrained the people from offering sacrifice to them" (14:18). How easily the human heart turns to idols: right in front of the apostles, they nearly made the apostles themselves into idols.

> The point: Not knowing the true God, the people of Lystra took the miracle-workers for gods. Paul refused that glory and pointed them instead to the Creator who had been showing them kindness all along through rain and harvest.

## Going Deeper: 14:19–20 Stoned, and Rising Again

**1. The same crowd.** "But Jews came from Antioch and Iconium, and having persuaded the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing that he was dead" (14:19). The people who had wanted to sacrifice to him now wanted to stone him to death. Human praise and human rage are often only a short step apart.

**2. Paul remembered.** Paul later wrote, "Once I was stoned" (2 Cor 11:25). He also wrote to Timothy of "my persecutions and sufferings that happened to me at Antioch, at Iconium, and at Lystra," adding, "yet from them all the Lord rescued me" (2 Tim 3:11). Timothy was from Lystra (16:1); he may well have seen it happen.

**3. He rose up and entered the city.** "But when the disciples gathered about him, he rose up and entered the city, and on the next day he went on with Barnabas to Derbe" (14:20). Luke doesn't call it a miracle, but the picture is powerful: a man stoned until he looked dead stands up and walks back into the city that stoned him.

## Going Deeper: 14:21–28 The Return: Strengthening, Encouraging, Entrusting

**1. Back the way they came.** After preaching in Derbe, "they returned to Lystra and to Iconium and to Antioch" (14:21). From Derbe the shortest way home lay east, through Paul's hometown of Tarsus; instead they went back, to the very cities that had just persecuted them. Why? For the sake of the new disciples.

**2. "Through many tribulations."** They were "strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to continue in the faith, and saying that through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God" (14:22). This is honest. Paul didn't tell new believers, "Believe in the Lord and everything will go well"; he said hardship "must" come. Luke often uses this "must" for God's plan (*dei*; cf. Luke 24:26, "Was it not necessary that the Christ should suffer these things and enter into his glory?"). The disciples' road is the road their Lord walked first.

**3. Appointing elders.** "And when they had appointed elders for them in every church, with prayer and fasting they committed them to the Lord in whom they had believed" (14:23). These churches were only months old, yet Paul appointed leaders for them. He trusted that the Lord could keep his church even without an apostle present.

**4. Committed to the Lord.** "They committed them to the Lord in whom they had believed": Paul didn't treat these churches as his own. He did his part, then let go and placed them in the Lord's hands.

**5. God opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.** Back in Syrian Antioch, "they declared all that God had done with them, and how he had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles" (14:27). Notice the subject: not "we opened a door for the Gentiles" but "God." What they reported was God's work.

> The point: Paul and Barnabas didn't hide the hardships, nor keep the new believers under their own control. They told the truth, appointed elders, and entrusted everything to the Lord.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** One day the people of Lystra wanted to offer sacrifices to Paul; the next they stoned him. Do I care so much about what others think of me that praise makes me giddy and criticism flattens me? Paul's steadiness came from knowing he was "also a man," and from knowing who the living God, the Maker of heaven and earth, is. Paul told the Gentiles that God gives "rains from heaven and fruitful seasons, satisfying your hearts with food and gladness." Does each meal I eat today remind me of the God who provides it? Many people thank "Heaven" before a meal without knowing the Lord who actually sends the rain; could I name that Giver at my table today, with my family? Finally, Paul told the new believers, "Through many tribulations we must enter the kingdom of God," and then "committed them to the Lord." Is there someone I carry on my heart: a child about to leave home, a friend newly come to faith, a small group I've led? I have done what I can; today, can I entrust them into the hands of the Lord who loves them more than I do?
