## 本章脉络

第十五章接近使徒行传的中间，许多人认为这是全书神学上最关键的一章。外邦人大量归主以后，一个问题再也回避不了：外邦人要得救，是否必须先受割礼、遵守摩西的律法？换句话说：人得救，是靠「耶稣加上别的」，还是单单靠耶稣的恩典？

本章有四段：**争论的起因**（1-5节）；**耶路撒冷会议**（6-21节），彼得、巴拿巴和保罗、雅各先后发言；**会议的书信**（22-35节），外邦教会读了信「就欢喜了」；**保罗和巴拿巴分开**（36-41节），两位同工因为马可的事起了争论，各自出发。这一章既有教会最清楚的教义决定，也有同工之间最真实的冲突。

## 深读 15:1-5 「若不受割礼，不能得救」

**1. 从犹太下来的人。** 「有几个人从犹太下来，教训弟兄们说：『你们若不按摩西的规条受割礼，不能得救。』」（15:1）他们不是说「受割礼会更好」，而是说「不受割礼，不能得救」。这把割礼变成了得救的条件。

**2. 大大地纷争辩论。** 「保罗、巴拿巴与他们大大地纷争辩论。」（15:2）保罗没有为了表面的和睦而让步；若在这里让步，福音就不再是福音了。保罗在加拉太书中，也把类似的争论称为「福音的真理」的问题（加2:5）。加拉太书第2章所记的耶路撒冷之行，究竟是指这次会议，还是指11:30的赈灾之行，福音派学者看法不一；但两处的立场完全一致：外邦人不必受割礼，单凭恩典称义。

**3. 法利赛教门的信徒。** 在耶路撒冷，「有几个信徒是法利赛教门的人，起来说：『必须给外邦人行割礼，吩咐他们遵守摩西的律法。』」（15:5）请注意，他们是「信徒」。这场争论不是教会和外人的争论，而是教会内部的争论。

> 要点：问题不在于割礼本身，而在于把任何东西加在基督身上，作为得救的条件。「耶稣加上别的」，就不再是福音。

## 深读 15:6-12 彼得：「我们得救乃是因主耶稣的恩」

**1. 辩论已经多了。** 「使徒和长老聚会商议这事。辩论已经多了，彼得就起来。」（15:6-7）教会没有回避辩论；有分歧的时候，他们聚在一起，公开讨论。

**2. 神并不分他们我们。** 彼得提醒大家哥尼流家里发生的事：「知道人心的神也为他们作了见证，赐圣灵给他们，正如给我们一样；又藉着信洁净了他们的心，并不分他们我们。」（15:8-9）神已经用行动作了决定：外邦人的心是「藉着信」洁净的，不是藉着割礼。

**3. 不能负的轭。** 「现在为什么试探神，要把我们祖宗和我们所不能负的轭放在门徒的颈项上呢？」（15:10）这是一个犹太使徒很诚实的承认：律法本身是圣洁的（罗7:12），但作为得救之路，它是「我们祖宗和我们所不能负的轭」。没有人能靠遵守律法站在神面前。耶稣却说：「我的轭是容易的，我的担子是轻省的。」（太11:30）

**4. 「和他们一样」。** 「我们得救乃是因主耶稣的恩，和他们一样，这是我们所信的。」（15:11）请注意彼得的说法。他没有说「外邦人得救是因恩典，和我们一样」，而是倒过来说：「我们（犹太人）得救是因恩典，和他们（外邦人）一样。」犹太人并没有比外邦人多一条得救的路。在恩典面前，人人站在同一个地方。

**5. 众人都默默无声。** 「众人都默默无声，听巴拿巴和保罗述说神藉他们在外邦人中所行的神迹奇事。」（15:12）巴拿巴和保罗没有提出新的论证，只是述说神的作为。

> 要点：犹太人和外邦人得救，都是「因主耶稣的恩」，没有第二条路。律法是人所不能负的轭；恩典却是神白白的礼物。

## 深读 15:13-21 雅各：经文与智慧

**1. 主的兄弟雅各。** 「他们住了声，雅各就说：『诸位弟兄，请听我的话。』」（15:13）这位雅各是耶稣的兄弟，耶路撒冷教会的领袖，后来的教会传统记得他是一位严守律法的人。由他来作总结，分量特别重。

**2. 阿摩司书的预言。** 雅各引用阿摩司书：「此后，我要回来，重新修造大卫倒塌的帐幕……叫余剩的人，就是凡称为我名下的外邦人，都寻求主。」（15:16-17；摩9:11-12）神应许要恢复大卫的国度，而这个恢复，正包括外邦人归向神。雅各的引文跟随希腊文的七十士译本；希伯来文本作「使以色列人得以东所余剩的和所有称为我名下的国」。两种文本都指向同一件事：大卫的国度恢复的时候，列国要归入神的名下。

**3. 「不可难为」。** 「所以据我的意见，不可难为那归服神的外邦人。」（15:19）结论很清楚：外邦人不必受割礼，不必成为犹太人。

**4. 四件事。** 「只要写信，吩咐他们禁戒偶像的污秽和奸淫，并勒死的牲畜和血。」（15:20）这四件事不是得救的条件（第11节已经说清楚了）；其中关于饮食的几件，是为了让外邦信徒和犹太信徒能够在一起生活、一同吃饭。这几件事大多是犹太人最敏感的地方，有些也和利未记17-18章对「寄居的外人」的要求相似。雅各接着说：「因为从古以来，摩西的书在各城有人传讲。」（15:21）意思是：各城都有犹太人，外邦信徒要顾及他们的良心。

**5. 奸淫为什么也在其中？** 其他几件主要和饮食、祭祀有关，奸淫却直接是道德问题。这里的「奸淫」（*porneia*）很可能包括利未记18章所禁止的各样性关系；在外邦世界，这类事极为普遍，也常和拜偶像的宴席连在一起。前三件是为了体贴犹太弟兄的良心；禁戒奸淫却是神对一切信徒永不改变的命令（林前6:18；帖前4:3）。

> 要点：耶路撒冷会议守住了两件事：在得救的事上，单单靠恩典，绝不妥协；在可以自由的事上，为了爱弟兄，甘心放下自己的自由；在神的道德命令上，则一同顺服。

## 深读 15:22-35 「圣灵和我们」

**1. 一封信和两位使者。** 「使徒和长老并全教会定意」，差派犹大和西拉，带着书信同保罗、巴拿巴往安提阿去（15:22-23）。他们不只寄一封信，还差人亲自去解释。

**2. 「圣灵和我们定意」。** 「因为圣灵和我们定意不将别的重担放在你们身上，惟有几件事是不可少的。」（15:28）这句话很特别：教会在讨论、见证神的作为、查考经文以后作出的决定，他们相信也是圣灵的意思。这是使徒时代的决定，已经成为圣经的一部分；今天教会的决定却会出错，必须时时用圣经来检验。但当教会谦卑顺服神的话、彼此商议时，可以信靠圣灵的带领。

**3. 就欢喜了。** 「众人念了，因为信上安慰的话就欢喜了。」（15:31）外邦信徒最怕听到的，是「你还不够」；他们收到的信，却告诉他们：在基督里，你已经完全被接纳了。

## 深读 15:36-41 保罗和巴拿巴分开

**1. 为马可起争论。** 保罗提议回去看望各城的弟兄，「巴拿巴有意要带称呼马可的约翰同去；但保罗因为马可从前在旁非利亚离开他们，不和他们同去做工，就以为不可带他去」（15:37-38）。两人都有道理：保罗看重工作的需要，巴拿巴看重马可这个人。巴拿巴是马可的表兄（西4:10），自然更愿意再给他一次机会。

**2. 「甚至彼此分开」。** 「于是二人起了争论，甚至彼此分开。」（15:39）「争论」原文是很强烈的字，指激烈的冲突。圣经没有替任何一方掩饰：刚刚帮助教会解决了最大分歧的两个人，自己却因为一个人选问题分开了。

**3. 神仍然掌权。** 结果，一个宣教队变成了两个：「巴拿巴带着马可，坐船往塞浦路斯去；保罗拣选了西拉，也出去，蒙弟兄们把他交于主的恩中。」（15:39-40）神没有因为他们的冲突而停止工作。这不是说冲突是好事，而是说神的恩典比人的软弱更大。

**4. 后来的和好。** 多年以后，保罗在书信中提到马可，吩咐教会「接待他」（西4:10），又说「他在传道的事上于我有益处」（提后4:11）。曾经半途离开的马可，后来成了保罗的同工，早期教父帕皮亚说他记下了彼得的讲道，传统上也认为他就是马可福音的作者。巴拿巴给马可的第二次机会，并没有白费。

> 要点：同一章里，教会在教义上达成了合一，两位领袖却在人事上分开了。圣经不掩饰神仆人的软弱；但神的工作没有停，马可也没有被放弃。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们若不受割礼，不能得救。」今天，很少人会这样说；可是我心里会不会有一个类似的「若不」：若不每天灵修、若不参加某种聚会、若不守某些属灵规矩，就不算真正的基督徒？彼得说：「我们得救乃是因主耶稣的恩，和他们一样。」在恩典面前，我和我最看不上的那位信徒站在同一个地方。我能否放下那份「我比较好」的感觉，单单为同一份恩典感恩？耶路撒冷会议也提醒我，在得救的事上不能妥协，在生活的事上却可以为了爱而让步。有没有一件事，在神面前我原本有自由去做，可是为了不绊倒身边的弟兄姊妹，我愿意暂时放下？最后，马可曾经半途离开，后来却成了「在传道的事上有益处」的人。我是否因为过去的一次退缩或失败，就认定自己永远不能被神使用？那位没有放弃马可的主，今天也可以重新使用我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 15 stands near the center of Acts, and many regard it as the book's most important chapter theologically. Once Gentiles were coming to faith in large numbers, one question could no longer be avoided: must Gentiles first be circumcised and keep the law of Moses in order to be saved? In other words, are people saved by "Jesus plus something else," or by the grace of Jesus alone?

The chapter has four parts: **the dispute arises** (vv. 1–5); **the Jerusalem council** (vv. 6–21), where Peter, Barnabas and Paul, and James speak in turn; **the council's letter** (vv. 22–35), which the Gentile church reads and "rejoiced"; and **Paul and Barnabas part ways** (vv. 36–41), as the two co-workers clash over Mark and set out separately. The chapter holds both the church's clearest doctrinal decision and its most honest conflict between co-workers.

## Going Deeper: 15:1–5 "Unless You Are Circumcised, You Cannot Be Saved"

**1. Men from Judea.** "But some men came down from Judea and were teaching the brothers, 'Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved'" (15:1). They weren't saying circumcision would be better; they were saying that without it you cannot be saved. That turns circumcision into a condition of salvation.

**2. No small dissension.** "Paul and Barnabas had no small dissension and debate with them" (15:2). Paul didn't give way for the sake of surface harmony; give way here, and the gospel would no longer be the gospel. In Galatians Paul calls a similar dispute a matter of "the truth of the gospel" (Gal 2:5). Evangelical scholars differ on whether the Jerusalem visit of Galatians 2 is this council or the famine visit of 11:30, but the stance is the same in both: Gentiles need not be circumcised; justification is by grace alone.

**3. Believers from the Pharisees.** In Jerusalem, "some believers who belonged to the party of the Pharisees rose up and said, 'It is necessary to circumcise them and to order them to keep the law of Moses'" (15:5). Notice they were "believers." This wasn't a dispute between the church and outsiders but within the church.

> The point: The issue isn't circumcision itself; it is adding anything to Christ as a condition of salvation. "Jesus plus something" is no longer the gospel.

## Going Deeper: 15:6–12 Peter: "We Will Be Saved Through the Grace of the Lord Jesus"

**1. Much debate.** "The apostles and the elders were gathered together to consider this matter. And after there had been much debate, Peter stood up" (15:6–7). The church didn't avoid debate; when they disagreed, they met and discussed it openly.

**2. No distinction between us and them.** Peter reminded them of what happened in Cornelius's house: "And God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them, by giving them the Holy Spirit just as he did to us, and he made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith" (15:8–9). God had already decided by his actions: Gentile hearts were cleansed "by faith," not by circumcision.

**3. A yoke we could not bear.** "Now, therefore, why are you putting God to the test by placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear?" (15:10). This is a strikingly honest admission from a Jewish apostle: the law itself is holy (Rom 7:12), but as a way to stand before God it is a yoke "neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear." No one can stand before God by keeping the law. But Jesus said, "My yoke is easy, and my burden is light" (Matt 11:30).

**4. "Just as they will."** "But we believe that we will be saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, just as they will" (15:11). Notice how Peter puts it. He doesn't say, "Gentiles are saved by grace, just as we are," but the reverse: "We (Jews) are saved by grace, just as they (Gentiles) are." Jews have no second road to salvation. Before grace, everyone stands in the same place.

**5. All fell silent.** "And all the assembly fell silent, and they listened to Barnabas and Paul as they related what signs and wonders God had done through them among the Gentiles" (15:12). Barnabas and Paul offered no new argument; they simply told what God had done.

> The point: Jews and Gentiles alike are saved "through the grace of the Lord Jesus"; there is no other road. The law is a yoke no one can bear; grace is God's free gift.

## Going Deeper: 15:13–21 James: Scripture and Wisdom

**1. James the Lord's brother.** "After they finished speaking, James replied, 'Brothers, listen to me'" (15:13). This James was Jesus' brother, leader of the Jerusalem church, remembered in later tradition for his strict observance of the law. A summary from him carried special weight.

**2. Amos's prophecy.** James quotes Amos: "After this I will return, and I will rebuild the tent of David that has fallen... that the remnant of mankind may seek the Lord, and all the Gentiles who are called by my name" (15:16–17; Amos 9:11–12). God promised to restore David's kingdom, and that restoration includes the Gentiles turning to God. James's quotation follows the Greek Septuagint; the Hebrew text reads "that they may possess the remnant of Edom and all the nations who are called by my name." Both point to the same thing: when David's kingdom is restored, the nations will come under God's name.

**3. "We should not trouble them."** "Therefore my judgment is that we should not trouble those of the Gentiles who turn to God" (15:19). The conclusion is clear: Gentiles need not be circumcised or become Jews.

**4. Four things.** "But should write to them to abstain from the things polluted by idols, and from sexual immorality, and from what has been strangled, and from blood" (15:20). These four aren't conditions of salvation (verse 11 has settled that); the food rules among them are so that Gentile and Jewish believers can live together and share meals. Most of them touched Jewish sensitivities most deeply, and several resemble what Leviticus 17–18 required of "the stranger who sojourns" among Israel. James adds, "For from ancient generations Moses has had in every city those who proclaim him" (15:21): there are Jews in every city, and Gentile believers should be mindful of their consciences.

**5. Why sexual immorality?** The others mainly concern food and sacrifice, but sexual immorality is directly a moral matter. *Porneia* here probably covers the sexual relations forbidden in Leviticus 18, which were common in the Gentile world and often tied to idol feasts. The food rules served a brother's conscience; abstaining from sexual immorality is God's unchanging command for every believer (1 Cor 6:18; 1 Thess 4:3).

> The point: The Jerusalem council held two things together: on salvation, grace alone, with no compromise; in matters of freedom, a willingness to set it aside out of love; in God's moral commands, shared obedience.

## Going Deeper: 15:22–35 "The Holy Spirit and Us"

**1. A letter and two messengers.** "It seemed good to the apostles and the elders, with the whole church," to send Judas and Silas with a letter, along with Paul and Barnabas, to Antioch (15:22–23). They didn't only send a letter; they sent people to explain it in person.

**2. "It has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us."** "For it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to lay on you no greater burden than these requirements" (15:28). This is remarkable: a decision reached after debate, testimony to God's work and the Scriptures, they believed was also the Spirit's will. This apostolic decision is now part of Scripture. Church decisions today can err and must be tested by Scripture; yet when a church humbly submits to God's word and confers together, it may trust the Spirit's leading.

**3. They rejoiced.** "And when they had read it, they rejoiced because of its encouragement" (15:31). What Gentile believers most feared to hear was "You're not enough yet"; the letter they received told them that in Christ they were fully accepted.

## Going Deeper: 15:36–41 Paul and Barnabas Part Ways

**1. A dispute over Mark.** Paul proposed revisiting the brothers in each city. "Now Barnabas wanted to take with them John called Mark. But Paul thought best not to take with them one who had withdrawn from them in Pamphylia and had not gone with them to the work" (15:37–38). Both had a point: Paul weighed the needs of the work, Barnabas weighed Mark as a person. Barnabas was Mark's cousin (Col 4:10), and naturally more willing to give him another chance.

**2. "So that they separated."** "And there arose a sharp disagreement, so that they separated from each other" (15:39). "Sharp disagreement" is a strong word for a heated conflict. Scripture covers for neither side: the two men who had just helped the church settle its greatest dispute parted ways over a personnel question.

**3. God still reigns.** The result was that one mission team became two: "Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed away to Cyprus, but Paul chose Silas and departed, having been commended by the brothers to the grace of the Lord" (15:39–40). God didn't stop working because of their conflict. That doesn't make conflict good; it means God's grace is greater than human weakness.

**4. Reconciliation later.** Years later Paul mentions Mark in his letters, telling a church to "welcome him" (Col 4:10) and saying "he is very useful to me for ministry" (2 Tim 4:11). The Mark who once turned back became Paul's co-worker, and the early church father Papias says he recorded Peter's preaching; tradition holds he wrote the Gospel of Mark. The second chance Barnabas gave him was not wasted.

> The point: In the same chapter the church reached unity on doctrine, while two leaders parted over people. Scripture doesn't hide the weakness of God's servants; but God's work didn't stop, and Mark wasn't given up on.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Unless you are circumcised, you cannot be saved." Few people would say that today; but might there be a similar "unless" in my heart: unless you have devotions every day, unless you attend a certain kind of meeting, unless you keep certain spiritual rules, you're not really a Christian? Peter said, "We will be saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, just as they will." Before grace, I stand in the same place as the believer I think least of. Can I let go of the feeling that "I'm better" and simply give thanks for the same grace? The Jerusalem council also reminds me that I can't compromise on salvation, yet in daily life I can give way out of love. Is there something I'm free before God to do, which I'm willing to set aside for now so as not to trip up a brother or sister near me? Finally, Mark once turned back halfway, yet later became "very useful for ministry." Have I decided that because of one retreat or failure in my past, God can never use me again? The Lord who didn't give up on Mark can use me again today.
