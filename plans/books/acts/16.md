## 本章脉络

第十六章记载保罗第二次宣教旅程（始于15:36）的头一段，也是福音第一次进入欧洲。**提摩太加入**（1-5节）；**关闭的门与马其顿的异象**（6-10节），圣灵两次「禁止」「不许」，然后在夜间异象中呼召他们过到马其顿；**腓立比**（11-40节），福音临到三个截然不同的人：卖紫色布疋的富商吕底亚、被巫鬼所附的使女、看守监牢的禁卒。吕底亚和禁卒信了主；使女得了释放（路加没有记载她后来是否信主）。

从第10节开始，叙述忽然从「他们」变成「我们」。许多学者认为，这表示作者路加本人在特罗亚加入了保罗的同工团队。

## 深读 16:1-5 提摩太加入

**1. 一个混合家庭的青年。** 在路司得，有一个门徒名叫提摩太，「是信主之犹太妇人的儿子，他父亲却是希腊人」（16:1）。保罗后来写信给他，提到他的信心「是先在你外祖母罗以和你母亲友妮基心里的」（提后1:5）。一个家庭里母亲和外祖母的信心，栽培出了一位日后的教会领袖。

**2. 为什么给他行割礼？** 「保罗要带他同去，只因那些地方的犹太人都知道他父亲是希腊人，就给他行了割礼。」（16:3）这看起来好像和第十五章的决定矛盾。其实不然：耶路撒冷会议决定的是，割礼不是得救的条件；保罗也曾坚持不让希腊人提多受割礼（加2:3），因为那时有人要把割礼当作得救的条件。提摩太的情况不同：他母亲是犹太人，当地犹太人很可能视他为犹太人，却是一个未受割礼的犹太人（经文说「他们都知道他父亲是希腊人」）；保罗让他受割礼，是为了传福音时不在犹太人面前造成不必要的拦阻。保罗说：「向犹太人，我就作犹太人，为要得犹太人。」（林前9:20）

**3. 坚固与加增。** 他们把耶路撒冷会议的决定交给各城的门徒，「于是众教会信心越发坚固，人数天天加增」（16:5）。这是使徒行传的又一个「进展报告」。

> 要点：在得救的事上，保罗绝不让步；在传福音的方法上，他却甘心让步。同一个保罗，不让提多受割礼，却让提摩太受割礼，因为他看重的是福音的真理和福音的传开。

## 深读 16:6-10 关闭的门与马其顿的异象

**1. 两次被拦阻。** 「圣灵既然禁止他们在亚细亚讲道」，他们想往庇推尼去，「耶稣的灵却不许」（16:6-7）。亚细亚和庇推尼都是人口稠密、需要福音的地方，保罗的计划并没有错。可是圣灵关上了门。路加没有告诉我们圣灵是怎样禁止的，也许是借着环境、疾病，或先知的话。

**2. 「耶稣的灵」。** 新约只有这里用「耶稣的灵」这个说法（参腓1:19；罗8:9）。第6节的「圣灵」在第7节被称为「耶稣的灵」：圣灵是升天的主所差来的（2:33），带领宣教的，是父、子、圣灵同工的神。

**3. 马其顿人的呼声。** 他们来到特罗亚，「在夜间有异象现与保罗。有一个马其顿人站着求他说：『请你过到马其顿来帮助我们。』」（16:9）两扇门关了，一扇新的门开了。「保罗既看见这异象，我们随即想要往马其顿去，以为神召我们传福音给那里的人听。」（16:10）「以为」原文有「综合推断」的意思：他们把关闭的门、异象和彼此商议放在一起，得出了结论。

> 要点：神的带领不只是开门，也包括关门。圣灵两次说「不」，是为了引他们走向一个他们没有想到的「是」：福音要进入欧洲。

## 深读 16:11-15 吕底亚：主开导她的心

**1. 河边的祷告处。** 腓立比是罗马的殖民城，是马其顿那一带的重要城市。那里似乎连一个会堂也没有（按后来拉比文献的记载，某些会众礼仪需要十个男人；若当时已是如此，可见那里犹太男人很少）。「当安息日，我们出城门，到了河边，知道那里有一个祷告的地方，我们就坐下对那聚会的妇女讲道。」（16:13）欧洲第一次的福音聚会，是在河边对几个妇女讲道。

**2. 「主就开导她的心」。** 「有一个卖紫色布疋的妇人，名叫吕底亚，是推雅推喇城的人，素来敬拜神。她听见了，主就开导她的心，叫她留心听保罗所讲的话。」（16:14）紫色布料是贵重的货品，吕底亚很可能是一位富裕的商人。路加说得很清楚：保罗讲道，但开她心的是主。人能明白、相信福音，是神的工作。

**3. 打开的心，打开的家。** 「她和她一家既领了洗，便求我们说：『你们若以为我是真信主的，请到我家里来住』；于是强留我们。」（16:15）主打开了她的心，她就打开了她的家。后来弟兄们就聚在她家（16:40）。腓立比教会也一直慷慨地支持保罗的事工（腓4:15）。

## 深读 16:16-24 使女的释放与代价

**1. 被巫鬼所附的使女。** 有一个使女「被巫鬼所附，用法术，叫她主人们大得财利」（16:16）。「巫鬼」原文是「皮同的灵」，皮同是希腊神话中守护德尔斐神谕的巨蛇；人们相信被这灵附着的人能说预言。她是一个被双重捆绑的人：被鬼捆绑，也被主人当作赚钱的工具。

**2. 真话，却出自错的来源。** 她一连多日跟着喊：「这些人是至高神的仆人，对你们传说救人的道。」（16:17）她说的话是真的，可是福音不需要鬼来作见证。保罗「转身对那鬼说：『我奉耶稣基督的名，吩咐你从她身上出来！』那鬼当时就出来了」（16:18）。

**3. 失去财利的愤怒。** 「使女的主人们见得利的指望没有了」（16:19），就揪住保罗和西拉，诬告他们「骚扰我们的城」。他们真正的问题不是宗教，而是钱。一个女孩得了自由，她的主人只看见收入断了。官长吩咐把他们剥了衣裳，用棍打，下在内监，两脚上了木狗（16:22-24）。

> 要点：福音释放人，也会触动靠捆绑人得利的势力。使女的主人看重钱过于看重人；福音却把一个被当作工具的女孩，当作一个值得被释放的人。

## 深读 16:25-40 半夜的诗歌与禁卒一家

**1. 半夜唱诗。** 「约在半夜，保罗和西拉祷告，唱诗赞美神，众囚犯也侧耳而听。」（16:25）他们的背被打得皮开肉绽，脚被木狗锁着，却在黑暗中唱诗。多年以后，保罗从另一座监牢写信给腓立比教会：「你们要靠主常常喜乐。」（腓4:4）腓立比人知道，这不是一句空话。

**2. 地震与「我们都在这里」。** 忽然地大震动，监门全开，锁链都松开了。禁卒以为囚犯都逃走了，按罗马的规矩他要替逃犯受死（参12:19；27:42），就拔刀要自杀。「保罗大声呼叫说：『不要伤害自己！我们都在这里。』」（16:28）他们本可以逃走，却留了下来；他们关心的，不是自己的自由，而是这个刚刚把他们锁起来的人的性命。

**3. 「我当怎样行才可以得救？」** 禁卒战战兢兢地俯伏在保罗、西拉面前，问：「二位先生，我当怎样行才可以得救？」他们说：「当信主耶稣，你和你一家都必得救。」（16:30-31）这是新约中最简洁的福音宣告之一。不是「你要做什么」，而是「当信主耶稣」。「你和你一家」不是说一个人信了，全家就自动得救，而是说这应许也同样向他的全家敞开；接着他们「就把主的道讲给他和他全家的人听」（16:32）。全家都听了道，也一同因他信了神而喜乐（16:34）。

**4. 洗伤，受洗。** 「当夜，就在那时候，禁卒把他们带去，洗他们的伤；他和属乎他的人立时都受了洗。」（16:33）同一个夜晚，禁卒洗了使徒的伤，自己也受了洗礼，这洗礼是他因信罪得洗净的记号。一个刚刚用木狗锁住他们的人，现在为他们摆上饭，「他和全家，因为信了神，都很喜乐」（16:34）。

**5. 「我们是罗马人」。** 天亮以后，官长要私下释放他们。保罗却说：「我们是罗马人，并没有定罪，他们就在众人面前打了我们……叫他们自己来领我们出去吧！」（16:37）他不是为了报复，而很可能是为了保护腓立比初生的教会：让官长公开承认他们无罪，以后的信徒就不容易被随意欺压。

> 要点：福音在腓立比临到的三个人，一个是富有的商人，一个是被奴役的女孩，一个是罗马的狱卒。福音没有因为身份、地位或民族而改变它的内容，也没有一个人在它的大能以外。

## 默想

**默想：** 圣灵两次关上了保罗想走的门。我生命中有没有一扇我很想进去、却被关上的门：一份没有得到的工作，一个没有实现的计划，一段没有开始的关系？我能否相信，那位关门的主，也是那位要开另一扇门的主？吕底亚能相信，是因为「主就开导她的心」。我为一位亲人或朋友祷告了很久，他却还没有信；我是否以为，他得救要靠我说得够好、够有说服力？我可以继续忠心地讲，也可以安心地把开心的工作交给主。最后，保罗和西拉在半夜、在伤痛和锁链中唱诗。我的喜乐是否只在一切顺利的时候才有？他们能唱，不是因为不痛，而是因为他们知道，那位为他们受鞭打、被钉十字架的主，与他们一同在内监里。今天在我自己的「半夜」，我能不能也开口唱一首诗？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 16 follows the opening stage of Paul's second missionary journey (begun in 15:36) and brings the gospel into Europe for the first time. **Timothy joins** (vv. 1–5); **closed doors and the Macedonian vision** (vv. 6–10), where the Spirit twice "forbids" and "does not allow," then calls them in a night vision to cross into Macedonia; and **Philippi** (vv. 11–40), where the gospel reaches three very different people: Lydia, a wealthy seller of purple goods; a slave girl with a spirit of divination; and the jailer of the prison. Lydia and the jailer believe; the slave girl is set free (Luke does not tell us whether she later believed).

From verse 10 the narrative suddenly shifts from "they" to "we." Many scholars take this to mean that Luke, the author, joined Paul's team at Troas.

## Going Deeper: 16:1–5 Timothy Joins

**1. A young man from a mixed family.** At Lystra was a disciple named Timothy, "the son of a Jewish woman who was a believer, but his father was a Greek" (16:1). Paul later wrote to him of his faith, which "dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice" (2 Tim 1:5). The faith of a mother and grandmother in one household raised up a future church leader.

**2. Why circumcise him?** "Paul wanted Timothy to accompany him, and he took him and circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those places, for they all knew that his father was a Greek" (16:3). This might seem to contradict chapter 15. It doesn't: the Jerusalem council decided that circumcision is not a condition of salvation, and Paul had insisted that Titus, a Greek, not be circumcised (Gal 2:3), because some wanted to make it a condition of salvation. Timothy's case was different: his mother was Jewish, so local Jews would likely have regarded him as a Jew, but an uncircumcised one (the text says "they all knew that his father was a Greek"); Paul had him circumcised so as not to put an unnecessary obstacle before Jews when preaching the gospel. As Paul said, "To the Jews I became as a Jew, in order to win Jews" (1 Cor 9:20).

**3. Strengthened and growing.** They delivered the Jerusalem decisions to the disciples in each city, "so the churches were strengthened in the faith, and they increased in numbers daily" (16:5). This is another of Acts' "progress reports."

> The point: On salvation Paul would not give an inch; in how he preached the gospel, he gladly gave way. The same Paul refused to circumcise Titus but circumcised Timothy, because what he cared about was the truth of the gospel and its spread.

## Going Deeper: 16:6–10 Closed Doors and the Macedonian Vision

**1. Blocked twice.** They had been "forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia," and when they tried to go into Bithynia, "the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them" (16:6–7). Asia and Bithynia were populous regions that needed the gospel; Paul's plan wasn't wrong. But the Spirit shut the door. Luke doesn't say how the Spirit forbade them; perhaps through circumstances, illness or a prophetic word.

**2. "The Spirit of Jesus."** Only here does the New Testament use exactly the phrase "the Spirit of Jesus" (cf. Phil 1:19; Rom 8:9). The "Holy Spirit" of v. 6 is called "the Spirit of Jesus" in v. 7: the Spirit is sent by the ascended Lord (2:33), so the mission is directed by Father, Son and Spirit together.

**3. The Macedonian call.** They came to Troas, "and a vision appeared to Paul in the night: a man of Macedonia was standing there, urging him and saying, 'Come over to Macedonia and help us'" (16:9). Two doors closed; a new one opened. "And when Paul had seen the vision, immediately we sought to go on into Macedonia, concluding that God had called us to preach the gospel to them" (16:10). "Concluding" means putting things together: they weighed the closed doors, the vision and their discussion together and reached a conclusion.

> The point: God's guidance includes closing doors as well as opening them. The Spirit said "no" twice to lead them to a "yes" they hadn't imagined: the gospel was to enter Europe.

## Going Deeper: 16:11–15 Lydia: The Lord Opened Her Heart

**1. A place of prayer by the river.** Philippi was a Roman colony and a leading city of that part of Macedonia. It seems to have had no synagogue (later rabbinic sources require ten men for certain communal prayers; if that applied then, there were few Jewish men there). "On the Sabbath day we went outside the gate to the riverside, where we supposed there was a place of prayer, and we sat down and spoke to the women who had come together" (16:13). The first gospel meeting in Europe was a talk with a few women by a river.

**2. "The Lord opened her heart."** "One who heard us was a woman named Lydia, from the city of Thyatira, a seller of purple goods, who was a worshiper of God. The Lord opened her heart to pay attention to what was said by Paul" (16:14). Purple cloth was a luxury good; Lydia was probably a wealthy businesswoman. Luke is clear: Paul preached, but the Lord opened her heart. That anyone understands and believes the gospel is God's work.

**3. An open heart, an open home.** "And after she was baptized, and her household as well, she urged us, saying, 'If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come to my house and stay.' And she prevailed upon us" (16:15). The Lord opened her heart, and she opened her home. At 16:40 the brothers were gathered at her house, and the Philippians went on supporting Paul's ministry generously (Phil 4:15).

## Going Deeper: 16:16–24 The Slave Girl Set Free, and the Cost

**1. A girl with a spirit of divination.** A slave girl "had a spirit of divination and brought her owners much gain by fortune-telling" (16:16). The phrase is literally "a python spirit," after the serpent of Greek myth that guarded the oracle at Delphi; people believed those possessed by it could foretell the future. She was doubly enslaved: bound by a spirit and used by her owners to make money.

**2. True words from the wrong source.** For many days she followed them, crying, "These men are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to you the way of salvation" (16:17). What she said was true, but the gospel needs no testimony from demons. Paul "turned and said to the spirit, 'I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.' And it came out that very hour" (16:18).

**3. Anger at lost income.** "When her owners saw that their hope of gain was gone" (16:19), they seized Paul and Silas and accused them of "disturbing our city." Their real problem wasn't religion but money. A girl had been set free; her owners saw only that their income had stopped. The magistrates had them stripped and beaten with rods, thrown into the inner prison, and their feet fastened in the stocks (16:22–24).

> The point: The gospel sets people free, and it also disturbs those who profit from others' bondage. The girl's owners valued money above a person; the gospel treated a girl used as a tool as a person worth setting free.

## Going Deeper: 16:25–40 Midnight Hymns and the Jailer's Household

**1. Singing at midnight.** "About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them" (16:25). Their backs were torn open, their feet locked in stocks, yet they sang in the dark. Years later Paul wrote to the Philippian church from another prison: "Rejoice in the Lord always" (Phil 4:4). The Philippians knew those weren't empty words.

**2. An earthquake, and "we are all here."** Suddenly a great earthquake shook the prison, the doors opened and everyone's bonds came loose. The jailer, thinking the prisoners had escaped, and knowing that under Roman practice he would answer with his life (cf. 12:19; 27:42), drew his sword to kill himself. "But Paul cried with a loud voice, 'Do not harm yourself, for we are all here'" (16:28). They could have fled, but stayed; what they cared about was not their own freedom but the life of the man who had just locked them up.

**3. "What must I do to be saved?"** Trembling, the jailer fell down before Paul and Silas and asked, "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?" They said, "Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household" (16:30–31). This is one of the simplest gospel statements in the New Testament. Not "here is what you must do," but "believe in the Lord Jesus." "You and your household" doesn't mean that one person's faith automatically saves a family, but that the same promise is open to his whole household; and so "they spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all who were in his house" (16:32); the whole household heard the word, and they rejoiced with him that he had believed in God (16:34).

**4. Washing wounds, washed in baptism.** "And he took them the same hour of the night and washed their wounds; and he was baptized at once, he and all his family" (16:33). That same night the jailer washed the apostles' wounds and himself received baptism, the sign of the cleansing he had received through faith. The man who had just locked them in the stocks now set food before them, "and he rejoiced along with his entire household that he had believed in God" (16:34).

**5. "We are Roman citizens."** At daybreak the magistrates wanted to release them quietly. But Paul said, "They have beaten us publicly, uncondemned, men who are Roman citizens... Let them come themselves and take us out" (16:37). This wasn't revenge but, most likely, protection for the newborn Philippian church: with the magistrates publicly acknowledging their innocence, believers there would be harder to mistreat at will.

> The point: The three people the gospel reached at Philippi were a wealthy merchant, an enslaved girl and a Roman jailer. The gospel did not change its message for anyone's status, rank or nation, and no one stood outside its power.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Twice the Spirit closed a door Paul wanted to go through. Is there a door in my life I badly wanted to enter that has been shut: a job I didn't get, a plan that didn't happen, a relationship that never began? Can I trust that the Lord who closes doors is the Lord who will open another? Lydia believed because "the Lord opened her heart." I've prayed a long time for a relative or friend who still doesn't believe; do I think their salvation depends on my being persuasive enough? I can keep speaking faithfully, and leave the opening of hearts to the Lord in peace. Finally, Paul and Silas sang at midnight, in pain and in chains. Is my joy there only when everything goes well? They could sing not because they felt no pain, but because they knew the Lord who was flogged and crucified for them was with them in the inner prison. Today, in my own "midnight," could I also open my mouth and sing?
