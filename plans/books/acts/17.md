## 本章脉络

第十七章记载了三座城、三种回应。**帖撒罗尼迦**（1-9节）：保罗在会堂讲解基督必须受害、复活，有人信了，也有人嫉妒，掀起暴动，指控他们是「那搅乱天下的」。**庇哩亚**（10-15节）：这里的人「甘心领受这道，天天考查圣经」。**雅典**（16-34节）：在哲学与偶像之城，保罗站在亚略‧巴古当中，向从未读过旧约的听众讲论那位「未识之神」。

雅典的讲论和第十四章在路司得的简短讲话一样，是向没有圣经背景的人传福音；只是这一次，保罗面对的是世界上最有学问的听众。

## 深读 17:1-9 帖撒罗尼迦：另有一个王耶稣

**1. 本着圣经辩论。** 「保罗照他素常的规矩进去，一连三个安息日，本着圣经与他们辩论，讲解陈明基督必须受害，从死里复活；又说：『我所传与你们的这位耶稣就是基督。』」（17:2-3）在会堂里，保罗的方法是从犹太人自己的圣经出发。犹太人等候的是一位得胜的弥赛亚；保罗要证明，弥赛亚「必须」先受害，然后复活。

**2. 「那搅乱天下的」。** 不信的犹太人心里嫉妒，招聚市井匪类，闯进收留保罗的耶孙家，找不着保罗，就把耶孙拉到地方官那里，喊叫说：「那搅乱天下的也到这里来了。」（17:6）这是敌人的控诉，却无意中说出了真相：福音确实在翻转整个世界。

**3. 「另有一个王耶稣」。** 他们又说：「这些人都违背凯撒的命令，说另有一个王耶稣。」（17:7）这个指控在政治上是诬告，在属灵上却是真的：基督徒确实承认耶稣是万王之王。这不是说基督徒要推翻地上的政权（参罗13:1-7），而是说一切地上的权柄，都在那位更高的王以下。

> 要点：福音不只是给个人的安慰，也是一个宣告：耶稣是王。这宣告会搅乱一切以为自己是最高权柄的势力。

## 深读 17:10-15 庇哩亚：天天考查圣经

**1. 贤于帖撒罗尼迦的人。** 弟兄们连夜送保罗和西拉往庇哩亚去。「这地方的人贤于帖撒罗尼迦的人，甘心领受这道，天天考查圣经，要晓得这道是与不是。」（17:11）「贤」原文是「高贵」：不是出身高贵，而是心态高贵。

**2. 两样并存。** 庇哩亚人的高贵在于两件事同时存在：「甘心领受」，也「天天考查」。他们不是多疑、挑剔的人，也不是什么都照单全收的人。他们愿意听，又用圣经来检验所听的，连使徒保罗的话也不例外。

**3. 结果。** 「所以他们中间多有相信的。」（17:12）认真考查圣经，没有叫他们远离信心，反而带领他们相信。可是帖撒罗尼迦的犹太人又追到庇哩亚来挑唆，弟兄们只好把保罗送到雅典。

> 要点：庇哩亚人既敞开心领受，又认真用圣经检验。真正的信心不怕查考；神的话经得起最仔细的考查。

## 深读 17:16-21 满城都是偶像

**1. 心里着急。** 「保罗在雅典等候他们的时候，看见满城都是偶像，就心里着急。」（17:16）雅典是希腊文化的中心，有宏伟的神庙和艺术品；游客看见的是美，保罗看见的却是偶像。「着急」原文是很强烈的字，在七十士译本中用来形容神对偶像崇拜的「激怒」（参申9:18）。保罗的心与神的心一同被激动。

**2. 在会堂，也在市场。** 他「在会堂里与犹太人和虔敬的人，并每日在市上所遇见的人，辩论」（17:17）。保罗没有只待在宗教的场所，他也走进雅典人每天讨论哲学和新闻的市场。

**3. 「胡言乱语的」。** 伊壁鸠鲁和斯多亚两派的学士与他争论。有人说：「这胡言乱语的要说什么？」（17:18）「胡言乱语的」原文是「捡种子的」，原指在地上啄食零星种子的鸟，后来用来讥笑东拼西凑、学问肤浅的人。还有人以为他在传「外邦鬼神」，「这话是因保罗传讲耶稣与复活的道」：他们也许以为「耶稣」和「复活」是一男一女两位新的神明。

## 深读 17:22-34 亚略‧巴古的讲论

**1. 从「未识之神」讲起。** 「众位雅典人哪，我看你们凡事很敬畏鬼神。我游行的时候……遇见一座坛，上面写着『未识之神』。你们所不认识而敬拜的，我现在告诉你们。」（17:22-23）保罗从他们自己的处境开始，不是为了肯定他们的宗教，而是要指出：你们承认有一位你们不认识的神，我要告诉你们他是谁。

**2. 创造主不住人手所造的殿。** 「创造宇宙和其中万物的神，既是天地的主，就不住人手所造的殿，也不用人手服事，好像缺少什么；自己倒将生命、气息、万物，赐给万人。」（17:24-25）这几句话推翻了雅典人的整个宗教：神不需要我们的神庙、祭物和服事，是我们需要他。这也呼应司提反的话（7:48）。

**3. 「他离我们各人不远」。** 「他从一本造出万族的人」，定准他们的年限和疆界，「要叫他们寻求神，或者可以揣摩而得，其实他离我们各人不远」（17:26-27）。人类都出于一个祖先，没有哪个民族天生比别人高贵。神安排人的历史，是为了叫人寻求他。

**4. 引用他们的诗人。** 「我们生活、动作、存留，都在乎他。就如你们作诗的，有人说：『我们也是他所生的。』」（17:28）前一句一般认为出自克里特诗人伊庇米尼底，后一句出自基利家诗人亚拉图。保罗借用异教诗人的话，却赋予它们圣经的意义：我们是神所造的，所以不当以为神像人手所雕刻的金、银、石（17:29）。

**5. 悔改与审判。** 讲论的高峰是：「世人蒙昧无知的时候，神并不监察，如今却吩咐各处的人都要悔改。因为他已经定了日子，要藉着他所设立的人按公义审判天下，并且叫他从死里复活，给万人作可信的凭据。」（17:30-31）保罗没有停在哲学讨论上，而是讲到悔改、审判和复活。耶稣的复活，是神给全人类的「凭据」。

**6. 三种回应。** 「众人听见从死里复活的话，就有讥诮他的；又有人说：『我们再听你讲这个吧！』」（17:32）但也「有几个人贴近他，信了主」，其中有亚略‧巴古的官丢尼修和一个名叫大马哩的妇人（17:34）。讥笑、拖延、相信，这三种回应，至今仍然一样。

> 要点：保罗从雅典人熟悉的东西开始，却没有停在他们熟悉的地方。他讲创造、讲人人都出于一本，最后讲悔改、审判和那位复活的人。福音可以从任何地方开始，却总要走到基督的十字架和复活。

## 默想

**默想：** 庇哩亚人「甘心领受这道，天天考查圣经」。我听讲道、读属灵书籍、看网上的短视频时，是什么都照单全收，还是习惯性地挑剔？我能不能像他们一样，既敞开心，又打开圣经去查考？保罗看见雅典满城的偶像，就「心里着急」。我所生活的城市也有它的偶像：成功、名校、房子、健康、安全感。我是对这些习以为常，还是像保罗一样，心里为那些把一生献给它们的人着急？那位赐给我生命、气息、万物的神，并不缺少什么；我所拥有的一切都是他白白赐的。最后，有人听了复活的道，说：「我们再听你讲这个吧！」我是否也对神的某一个呼召说「以后再说」？神「如今却吩咐各处的人都要悔改」；他已经叫耶稣从死里复活，作为可信的凭据。今天就是回应的日子。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 17 records three cities and three responses. **Thessalonica** (vv. 1–9): Paul explains in the synagogue that the Christ had to suffer and rise; some believe, others become jealous and stir up a riot, accusing them of being men "who have turned the world upside down." **Berea** (vv. 10–15): here the people "received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily." **Athens** (vv. 16–34): in the city of philosophy and idols, Paul stands in the midst of the Areopagus and speaks to an audience that has never read the Old Testament about the "unknown god."

Like his brief words at Lystra in chapter 14, the Athens speech is preaching to people with no biblical background; but this time Paul faces the most learned audience in the world.

## Going Deeper: 17:1–9 Thessalonica: Another King, Jesus

**1. Reasoning from the Scriptures.** "And Paul went in, as was his custom, and on three Sabbath days he reasoned with them from the Scriptures, explaining and proving that it was necessary for the Christ to suffer and to rise from the dead, and saying, 'This Jesus, whom I proclaim to you, is the Christ'" (17:2–3). In the synagogue, Paul starts from the Jews' own Scriptures. They expected a conquering Messiah; Paul shows that the Messiah "had to" suffer first and then rise.

**2. "These men who have turned the world upside down."** The unbelieving Jews, jealous, gathered a mob and attacked the house of Jason, who had taken Paul in. Not finding Paul, they dragged Jason before the city authorities, shouting, "These men who have turned the world upside down have come here also" (17:6). It was an enemy's charge, yet it unwittingly told the truth: the gospel really was overturning the world.

**3. "Another king, Jesus."** They added, "They are all acting against the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, Jesus" (17:7). Politically the charge was false; spiritually it was true: Christians do confess Jesus as King of kings. This doesn't mean Christians aim to overthrow earthly governments (cf. Rom 13:1–7), but that every earthly authority stands under that higher King.

> The point: The gospel is not only comfort for individuals; it is an announcement that Jesus is King. That announcement unsettles every power that thinks itself the highest authority.

## Going Deeper: 17:10–15 Berea: Examining the Scriptures Daily

**1. More noble than those in Thessalonica.** The brothers sent Paul and Silas away to Berea by night. "Now these Jews were more noble than those in Thessalonica; they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so" (17:11). "Noble" here describes not their birth but their attitude.

**2. Two things together.** The Bereans' nobility lay in holding two things together: they "received with all eagerness" and they "examined daily." They were neither suspicious and fault-finding nor ready to swallow anything. They were willing to listen, and they tested what they heard by Scripture, even the words of the apostle Paul.

**3. The result.** "Many of them therefore believed" (17:12). Careful study of Scripture didn't lead them away from faith; it led them to it. But Jews from Thessalonica came to Berea to stir up the crowds, and the brothers sent Paul on to Athens.

> The point: The Bereans opened their hearts to receive and searched the Scriptures to test. Real faith is not afraid of investigation; God's word stands up to the most careful examination.

## Going Deeper: 17:16–21 A City Full of Idols

**1. Provoked within.** "Now while Paul was waiting for them at Athens, his spirit was provoked within him as he saw that the city was full of idols" (17:16). Athens was the center of Greek culture, full of magnificent temples and art; tourists saw beauty, Paul saw idols. "Provoked" is a strong word; in the Septuagint it describes God being provoked by idolatry (cf. Deut 9:18). Paul's heart was stirred along with God's.

**2. In the synagogue, and in the marketplace.** He "reasoned in the synagogue with the Jews and the devout persons, and in the marketplace every day with those who happened to be there" (17:17). Paul didn't stay in religious settings; he went into the marketplace where Athenians discussed philosophy and news every day.

**3. "This babbler."** Epicurean and Stoic philosophers debated with him. Some said, "What does this babbler wish to say?" (17:18). "Babbler" is literally "seed-picker," a bird pecking at scattered seeds, used to mock someone who picks up scraps of learning. Others thought he was proclaiming "foreign divinities," "because he was preaching Jesus and the resurrection": they may have thought "Jesus" and "Resurrection" (*Anastasis*) were a new pair of gods, male and female.

## Going Deeper: 17:22–34 The Areopagus Speech

**1. Beginning with the "unknown god."** "Men of Athens, I perceive that in every way you are very religious. For as I passed along and observed the objects of your worship, I found also an altar with this inscription, 'To the unknown god.' What therefore you worship as unknown, this I proclaim to you" (17:22–23). Paul begins where they are, not to affirm their religion, but to point out that they admit there is a God they don't know, and he will tell them who he is.

**2. The Creator does not live in temples made by hands.** "The God who made the world and everything in it, being Lord of heaven and earth, does not live in temples made by man, nor is he served by human hands, as though he needed anything, since he himself gives to all mankind life and breath and everything" (17:24–25). These sentences overturn the whole of Athenian religion: God doesn't need our temples, sacrifices or service; we need him. It also echoes Stephen (7:48).

**3. "Not far from each one of us."** "He made from one man every nation of mankind," determining their times and boundaries, "that they should seek God, in the hope that they might feel their way toward him and find him. Yet he is actually not far from each one of us" (17:26–27). All humanity comes from one ancestor; no people is innately nobler than another. God orders human history so that people will seek him.

**4. Quoting their poets.** "'In him we live and move and have our being'; as even some of your own poets have said, 'For we are indeed his offspring'" (17:28). The first line is usually attributed to the Cretan poet Epimenides, the second to the Cilician poet Aratus. Paul borrows pagan poets' words but fills them with biblical meaning: we are God's creatures, so we shouldn't think God is like an image of gold, silver or stone made by human hands (17:29).

**5. Repentance and judgment.** The speech peaks here: "The times of ignorance God overlooked, but now he commands all people everywhere to repent, because he has fixed a day on which he will judge the world in righteousness by a man whom he has appointed; and of this he has given assurance to all by raising him from the dead" (17:30–31). Paul doesn't stop at philosophical discussion; he moves on to repentance, judgment and resurrection. Jesus' resurrection is God's "assurance" to all humanity.

**6. Three responses.** "Now when they heard of the resurrection of the dead, some mocked. But others said, 'We will hear you again about this'" (17:32). But "some men joined him and believed," among them Dionysius the Areopagite and a woman named Damaris (17:34). Mockery, delay, belief: the three responses are the same today.

> The point: Paul began with what the Athenians knew, but didn't stay there. He spoke of creation and of all people coming from one man, and finally of repentance, judgment and the man God raised. The gospel can begin anywhere, but it always arrives at Christ's cross and resurrection.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The Bereans "received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily." When I listen to sermons, read Christian books or watch short videos online, do I swallow everything, or habitually find fault? Can I, like them, open my heart and also open my Bible to check? Seeing Athens full of idols, Paul's "spirit was provoked within him." My city has its idols too: success, top schools, property, health, security. Have I grown used to them, or am I, like Paul, stirred for the people who give their lives to them? The God who gives me life and breath and everything lacks nothing; all I have is his free gift. Finally, some heard of the resurrection and said, "We will hear you again about this." Have I said "later" to something God is calling me to? God "now commands all people everywhere to repent," and he has given assurance by raising Jesus from the dead. Today is the day to respond.
