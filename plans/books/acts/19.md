## 本章脉络

第十九章记载保罗第三次宣教旅程的中心：以弗所。以弗所是罗马亚细亚省的大城，有著名的亚底米神庙（古代七大奇观之一），也是一座以法术和邪术闻名的城市。保罗在这里前后住了将近三年（20:31），是他停留最久的地方。

本章有五段：**约翰的门徒**（1-7节）；**推喇奴的学房**（8-10节），福音传遍亚细亚；**士基瓦的儿子**（11-20节），擅用耶稣的名反被恶鬼所伤，众人焚烧邪术的书；**保罗的计划**（21-22节）；**银匠的暴动**（23-41节），整座城的人在戏园里喊了两个小时「大哉，以弗所人的亚底米啊」。

## 深读 19:1-7 约翰的门徒

**1. 「你们信的时候受了圣灵没有？」** 保罗在以弗所遇见几个门徒，问他们：「你们信的时候受了圣灵没有？」他们说：「没有，也未曾听见有圣灵赐下来。」（19:2）原来他们只受过「约翰的洗」（19:3）。他们很可能是施洗约翰的跟随者，知道约翰所预备的那一位要来，却还不知道这一位就是耶稣，也不知道五旬节圣灵已经浇灌下来。

**2. 从约翰到耶稣。** 「保罗说：『约翰所行的是悔改的洗，告诉百姓当信那在他以后要来的，就是耶稣。』」（19:4）约翰的洗礼本身就指向耶稣；约翰的工作是预备，耶稣才是完成。他们听见这话，「就奉主耶稣的名受洗」（19:5）。

**3. 一个特别的情形。** 「保罗按手在他们头上，圣灵便降在他们身上，他们就说方言，又说预言。」（19:6）这和撒玛利亚（第8章）、哥尼流家（第10章）一样，是救恩历史转折中的特殊情形：神要清楚地显明，施洗约翰的门徒也被接进同一个教会、领受同一位圣灵。路加并没有把这当作每个信徒都要经历的模式；保罗的问题本身就假定，人信的时候就领受圣灵（参罗8:9；弗1:13）。

> 要点：约翰的洗是预备，耶稣才是目标。这几个门徒的信不是错的，只是不完全；他们一听见耶稣，就接受了更完全的福音。

## 深读 19:8-10 推喇奴的学房

**1. 三个月，然后离开。** 「保罗进会堂，放胆讲道，一连三个月，辩论神国的事，劝化众人。」（19:8）这是他在会堂停留最久的一次。后来有些人「心里刚硬不信，在众人面前毁谤这道」，保罗就离开会堂，「便在推喇奴的学房天天辩论」（19:9）。

**2. 每天的讲论。** 推喇奴可能是一位哲学教师，学房是他讲学的地方。有一份古抄本补充说，保罗是从第五时到第十时（约上午十一点到下午四点）在那里讲论，正是当地人午休、学房空着的时候。若是这样，保罗很可能是早上做工，中午讲道（参20:34）。

**3. 传遍亚细亚。** 「这样有两年之久，叫一切住在亚细亚的，无论是犹太人，是希腊人，都听见主的道。」（19:10）保罗自己没有走遍亚细亚，可是从以弗所出去的信徒，把福音带到了周围的城市。启示录二至三章的七个教会，以及歌罗西、老底嘉、希拉坡里的教会（西4:13），很可能都是在这段时期建立的。

## 深读 19:11-20 士基瓦的儿子与焚烧的书

**1. 非常的奇事。** 「神藉保罗的手行了些非常的奇事；甚至有人从保罗身上拿手巾或围裙放在病人身上，病就退了，恶鬼也出去了。」（19:11-12）路加说得很清楚：行奇事的是神，不是保罗，更不是手巾。在一座迷信法术的城市里，神用他们能明白的方式显明，他的能力胜过一切法术。

**2. 「你们却是谁呢？」** 有几个游行各处、念咒赶鬼的犹太人，是祭司长士基瓦的七个儿子，他们「擅自称主耶稣的名」，说：「我奉保罗所传的耶稣敕令你们出来！」（19:13）恶鬼回答：「耶稣我认识，保罗我也知道。你们却是谁呢？」（19:15）被恶鬼所附的人就跳在他们身上，把他们打得赤身受伤逃走。耶稣的名不是一句咒语；只有属于耶稣的人，才能奉他的名说话。

**3. 主耶稣的名尊大了。** 「凡住在以弗所的……都知道这事，也都惧怕；主耶稣的名从此就尊大了。」（19:17）人想利用耶稣的名，结果反而显出耶稣的名是不能被人利用的。

**4. 焚烧邪术的书。** 「那已经信的，多有人来承认诉说自己所行的事。平素行邪术的，也有许多人把书拿来，堆积在众人面前焚烧。他们算计书价，便知道共合五万块钱。」（19:18-19）「块钱」原文是银币德拉克马，一块约等于一个工人一天的工资；五万块，就是一百多个工人一整年的工资。他们没有把书卖掉，而是烧掉，因为不愿意让这些东西再去捆绑别人。真悔改是要付代价的。

**5. 道大大兴旺。** 「主的道大大兴旺，而且得胜，就是这样。」（19:20）这是使徒行传的又一个「进展报告」，用的字特别强：不只是「兴旺」，而且「得胜」。

> 要点：耶稣的名不是可以借用的咒语，而是一位活着的主。认识他的人，会甘心付上代价，与旧日的黑暗一刀两断。

## 深读 19:21-22 「也必须往罗马去看看」

「这些事完了，保罗心里定意经过了马其顿、亚该亚，就往耶路撒冷去；又说：『我到了那里以后，也必须往罗马去看看。』」（19:21）「心里定意」也可以译作「在圣灵里定意」。「必须」又是路加用来表示神的计划的字。从这里开始，使徒行传的后半部，就朝着耶路撒冷和罗马推进，正如耶稣当年「定意向耶路撒冷去」一样（路9:51）。

## 深读 19:23-41 「大哉，以弗所人的亚底米」

**1. 银匠的生意。** 有一个银匠底米丢，「是制造亚底米神银龛的」，他聚集同行的工人说：「众位，你们知道我们是倚靠这生意发财。」（19:24-25）他抱怨保罗说「人手所做的，不是神」（19:26），使他们的生意受了威胁。他先讲钱，然后才讲女神的「威荣」（19:27）。当福音改变人心，靠偶像赚钱的行业就会感到威胁。

**2. 两个小时的口号。** 众人怒气填胸，喊着说：「大哉，以弗所人的亚底米啊！」全城都乱了，众人拥进戏园。「聚集的人纷纷乱乱，有喊叫这个的，有喊叫那个的；大半不知道是为什么聚集。」（19:32）路加带着讽刺记下这一幕：大多数人根本不知道为什么来，却跟着喊了两个小时（19:34）。

**3. 书记的安抚。** 城里的书记安抚众人，说这些人「并没有偷窃庙中之物，也没有谤我们的女神」，若有争讼，可以按法律去告；「今日的扰乱本是无缘无故，我们难免被查问」（19:37-40），就叫众人散去。路加又一次让我们看见，罗马的官员找不到基督徒违法的证据。

> 要点：福音没有用暴力推翻亚底米的庙，却改变了人心，使人不再买银龛。偶像的势力最怕的，不是辩论，而是人心归向那位又真又活的神。

## 默想

**默想：** 以弗所的信徒把价值五万块钱的邪术书堆起来烧掉，没有卖掉，也没有留着「以防万一」。我的生活里，有没有一样东西，我知道它把我拉离基督，却舍不得丢掉：也许是一本算命的书、一个手机里的应用程序、一段关系，或者一种隐秘的习惯？他们能这样做，是因为他们已经找到一位比这一切更宝贵的主。士基瓦的儿子借用「保罗所传的耶稣」，恶鬼却问：「你们却是谁呢？」我认识的耶稣，是不是也只是「我父母所信的耶稣」「我牧师所传的耶稣」？他邀请我不只是知道他的名，而是亲自认识他，因为他早已认识我、为我舍命。最后，戏园里的众人「大半不知道是为什么聚集」。我每个主日走进教会，是因为习惯、因为人情，还是因为我知道自己是来敬拜那位救我的主？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 19 centers Paul's third missionary journey on Ephesus, a great city of the Roman province of Asia, home to the famous temple of Artemis (one of the seven wonders of the ancient world) and well known for magic and sorcery. Paul stayed there nearly three years (20:31), his longest stay anywhere.

The chapter has five parts: **the disciples of John** (vv. 1–7); **the hall of Tyrannus** (vv. 8–10), from which the gospel spreads through Asia; **the sons of Sceva** (vv. 11–20), who misuse Jesus' name and are beaten by an evil spirit, after which many burn their books of magic; **Paul's plans** (vv. 21–22); and **the silversmiths' riot** (vv. 23–41), in which the whole city shouts "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" in the theater for two hours.

## Going Deeper: 19:1–7 The Disciples of John

**1. "Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?"** At Ephesus Paul met some disciples and asked them, "Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?" They said, "No, we have not even heard that there is a Holy Spirit" (19:2). They had received only "John's baptism" (19:3). They were probably followers of John the Baptist who knew that the one John prepared for was coming, but didn't yet know that this one was Jesus, or that the Spirit had been poured out at Pentecost.

**2. From John to Jesus.** "And Paul said, 'John baptized with the baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the one who was to come after him, that is, Jesus'" (19:4). John's baptism itself pointed to Jesus; John prepared, and Jesus fulfilled. On hearing this, "they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus" (19:5).

**3. A special case.** "And when Paul had laid his hands on them, the Holy Spirit came on them, and they began speaking in tongues and prophesying" (19:6). Like Samaria (ch. 8) and Cornelius's house (ch. 10), this is a special case at a turning point in salvation history: God makes plain that John's disciples too are brought into the one church and receive the one Spirit. Luke doesn't present it as a pattern for every believer; Paul's very question assumes that people receive the Spirit when they believe (cf. Rom 8:9; Eph 1:13).

> The point: John's baptism was preparation; Jesus is the goal. These disciples' faith wasn't wrong, just incomplete; as soon as they heard of Jesus, they embraced the fuller gospel.

## Going Deeper: 19:8–10 The Hall of Tyrannus

**1. Three months, then a move.** "And he entered the synagogue and for three months spoke boldly, reasoning and persuading them about the kingdom of God" (19:8). This was his longest stay in a synagogue. When some "became stubborn and continued in unbelief, speaking evil of the Way before the congregation," Paul left the synagogue and was "reasoning daily in the hall of Tyrannus" (19:9).

**2. Daily teaching.** Tyrannus may have been a philosophy teacher whose lecture hall this was. One ancient manuscript adds that Paul taught there from the fifth to the tenth hour (roughly 11 a.m. to 4 p.m.), when locals rested and the hall stood empty. If so, Paul very likely worked in the mornings and taught at midday (cf. 20:34).

**3. All Asia heard.** "This continued for two years, so that all the residents of Asia heard the word of the Lord, both Jews and Greeks" (19:10). Paul didn't travel all of Asia himself, but believers going out from Ephesus carried the gospel to the surrounding cities. The seven churches of Revelation 2–3, and the churches of Colossae, Laodicea and Hierapolis (Col 4:13), were probably founded in this period.

## Going Deeper: 19:11–20 The Sons of Sceva and the Burned Books

**1. Extraordinary miracles.** "And God was doing extraordinary miracles by the hands of Paul, so that even handkerchiefs or aprons that had touched his skin were carried away to the sick, and their diseases left them and the evil spirits came out of them" (19:11–12). Luke is clear: the one working miracles was God, not Paul, still less the cloths. In a city steeped in magic, God showed in a way they could understand that his power is greater than all sorcery.

**2. "But who are you?"** Some itinerant Jewish exorcists, the seven sons of a chief priest named Sceva, "undertook to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus," saying, "I adjure you by the Jesus whom Paul proclaims" (19:13). The evil spirit answered, "Jesus I know, and Paul I recognize, but who are you?" (19:15). The possessed man leaped on them and overpowered them, and they fled naked and wounded. Jesus' name is not a spell; only those who belong to Jesus can speak in his name.

**3. The name of the Lord Jesus extolled.** "And this became known to all the residents of Ephesus... And fear fell upon them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was extolled" (19:17). People tried to use Jesus' name, and the result showed that his name cannot be used.

**4. Burning the books of magic.** "Also many of those who were now believers came, confessing and divulging their practices. And a number of those who had practiced magic arts brought their books together and burned them in the sight of all. And they counted the value of them and found it came to fifty thousand pieces of silver" (19:18–19). The coins were drachmas, each about a laborer's daily wage; fifty thousand was more than a hundred workers' wages for a whole year. They didn't sell the books but burned them, refusing to let them bind anyone else. Real repentance costs something.

**5. The word prevailed mightily.** "So the word of the Lord continued to increase and prevail mightily" (19:20). This is another of Acts' "progress reports," and its wording is especially strong: not only "increased" but "prevailed."

> The point: Jesus' name is not a spell to borrow, but a living Lord. Those who know him gladly pay the cost of breaking cleanly with the old darkness.

## Going Deeper: 19:21–22 "I Must Also See Rome"

"Now after these events Paul resolved in the Spirit to pass through Macedonia and Achaia and go to Jerusalem, saying, 'After I have been there, I must also see Rome'" (19:21). "Must" is again Luke's word for God's plan. From here on, the second half of Acts moves toward Jerusalem and Rome, just as Jesus once "set his face to go to Jerusalem" (Luke 9:51).

## Going Deeper: 19:23–41 "Great Is Artemis of the Ephesians"

**1. The silversmiths' trade.** A silversmith named Demetrius, "who made silver shrines of Artemis," gathered the craftsmen and said, "Men, you know that from this business we have our wealth" (19:24–25). He complained that Paul was saying "that gods made with hands are not gods" (19:26), threatening their trade. He spoke of money first, and only then of the goddess's "magnificence" (19:27). When the gospel changes hearts, industries that profit from idols feel threatened.

**2. Two hours of chanting.** Enraged, they cried, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" The whole city was in confusion and the crowd rushed into the theater. "Now some cried out one thing, some another, for the assembly was in confusion, and most of them did not know why they had come together" (19:32). Luke records the scene with irony: most of them didn't know why they had come, yet they chanted for two hours (19:34).

**3. The town clerk quiets them.** The town clerk calmed the crowd, saying these men were "neither sacrilegious nor blasphemers of our goddess," that complaints should go through the courts, and "we really are in danger of being charged with rioting today" (19:37–40); then he dismissed the assembly. Once again Luke shows that Roman officials found no evidence that the Christians had broken the law.

> The point: The gospel didn't overthrow the temple of Artemis by force; it changed hearts, so that people stopped buying silver shrines. What idolatrous power fears most isn't argument but hearts turning to the true and living God.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The believers at Ephesus heaped up books of magic worth fifty thousand pieces of silver and burned them; they didn't sell them or keep them "just in case." In my life, is there something I know pulls me away from Christ but can't bring myself to throw away: a fortune-telling book, an app on my phone, a relationship, a secret habit? They could do it because they had found a Lord more precious than all of it. The sons of Sceva borrowed "the Jesus whom Paul proclaims," and the spirit asked, "But who are you?" Is the Jesus I know only "the Jesus my parents believe in" or "the Jesus my pastor preaches"? He invites me not just to know his name but to know him myself, because he already knows me and gave his life for me. Finally, most of the crowd in the theater "did not know why they had come together." When I walk into church each Sunday, is it out of habit, out of social obligation, or because I know I've come to worship the Lord who saved me?
