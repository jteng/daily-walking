## 本章脉络

第二十章是保罗第三次宣教旅程的尾声，也是他走向耶路撒冷的开始。**马其顿与希腊**（1-6节）：保罗探望各地教会，与一群来自各地的同工同行。**特罗亚**（7-12节）：七日的第一日，众人聚会擘饼，少年人犹推古从窗台上掉下去死了，保罗使他复活。**米利都的告别**（13-38节）：保罗请以弗所教会的长老来，对他们讲了一篇满有眼泪的临别赠言，然后跪下同众人祷告。

在使徒行传所记保罗的讲论中，只有米利都这一篇是对信徒讲的，而且是对教会的领袖讲的。在这里，我们看见的不是辩论的保罗，而是牧人的保罗。

## 深读 20:1-6 同行的人

**1. 劝勉与辞别。** 「乱定之后，保罗请门徒来，劝勉他们，就辞别起行，往马其顿去。」（20:1）保罗离开以弗所以后，又走遍马其顿，「用许多话劝勉门徒」，然后到希腊住了三个月（20:2-3）。他很可能就是在这段时间，在哥林多写了罗马书。

**2. 一份名单。** 同行的有庇哩亚人所巴特、帖撒罗尼迦人亚里达古和西公都、特庇人该犹、提摩太，还有亚细亚人推基古和特罗非摩（20:4）。他们来自不同的城市和教会。许多人认为，他们是各地教会派出的代表，一同护送外邦教会给耶路撒冷穷苦圣徒的捐项（参罗15:25-26；林后8:19-21）。这个捐项，正是外邦人和犹太人在基督里合一的具体表现。

**3. 「我们」再次出现。** 「这些人先走，在特罗亚等候我们。」（20:5）叙述又从「他们」变回「我们」，表示路加在腓立比重新加入了保罗的行程。

## 深读 20:7-12 七日的第一日

**1. 聚会擘饼。** 「七日的第一日，我们聚会擘饼的时候，保罗因为要次日起行，就与他们讲论，直讲到半夜。」（20:7）这是新约中最早清楚提到信徒在「七日的第一日」聚会的地方之一（参林前16:2）。主复活的那一天，成了教会聚集敬拜、擘饼记念主的日子，这就是我们今天所说的「主日」。

**2. 窗台上的少年人。** 「有一个少年人，名叫犹推古，坐在窗台上，困倦沉睡。保罗讲了多时，少年人睡熟了，就从三层楼上掉下去；扶起他来，已经死了。」（20:9）楼上点着许多灯（20:8），屋里闷热，又是深夜，这个少年人很可能白天还要做工。路加在路司得记保罗被石头打时说「以为他是死了」（14:19），这里却直接说「已经死了」。

**3. 「你们不要发慌」。** 「保罗下去，伏在他身上，抱着他，说：『你们不要发慌，他的灵魂还在身上。』」（20:10）保罗说「他的灵魂还在身上」，意思是生命已经回来（参王上17:21「使这孩子的灵魂仍入他的身体」），不是说他根本没有死。这令人想起以利亚和以利沙使孩子复活时，伏在孩子身上的情景（王上17:21；王下4:34）。保罗上楼以后，继续擘饼、吃饭，又讲论到天亮（20:11）。「有人把那童子活活地领来，得的安慰不小。」（20:12）在主复活的日子，一个死了的少年人活过来，正是复活之主在他教会中同在的记号。

## 深读 20:13-27 保罗的回顾

**1. 请长老来。** 保罗急着赶在五旬节以前到耶路撒冷，就没有进以弗所，而是「从米利都打发人往以弗所去，请教会的长老来」（20:16-17）。

**2. 怎样服事。** 「你们知道，自从我到亚细亚的日子以来，在你们中间始终为人如何，服事主，凡事谦卑，眼中流泪，又因犹太人的谋害，经历试炼。」（20:18-19）保罗没有先讲他的成就，而是讲他的为人：谦卑、眼泪、试炼。他的事奉不是在讲台上表演出来的，而是在长期的生活中被人看见的。

**3. 公开的，也在各人家里。** 「凡与你们有益的，我没有一样避讳不说的，或在众人面前，或在各人家里，我都教导你们；又对犹太人和希腊人证明当向神悔改，信靠我主耶稣基督。」（20:20-21）他的福音有两面：「向神悔改」和「信靠我主耶稣基督」。他的教导也有两种场合：公开的聚会和信徒的家中。

**4. 「不以性命为念」。** 「现在我往耶路撒冷去，心甚迫切，不知道在那里要遇见什么事；但知道圣灵在各城里向我指证，说有捆锁与患难等待我。我却不以性命为念，也不看为宝贵，只要行完我的路程，成就我从主耶稣所领受的职事，证明神恩惠的福音。」（20:22-24）「心甚迫切」原文直译是「在灵里被捆绑」；许多译本理解为被圣灵约束，这与下一节「圣灵在各城里向我指证」相合。保罗还没有被人捆绑，就已经被圣灵「捆绑」，甘心走向前面的捆锁。他看重的不是性命的长短，而是能否「行完」主交给他的路程。

**5. 神的全备旨意。** 「所以我今日向你们证明，你们中间无论何人死亡，罪不在我身上。因为神的旨意，我并没有一样避讳不传给你们的。」（20:26-27）保罗再次用以西结书守望者的语言（结33:1-9）。原文是「神一切的旨意」，指神在基督里完整的救恩计划：保罗没有只挑人爱听的讲，而是把神所启示的全部真理都教导了他们。

> 要点：保罗看轻性命，是因为他把性命交给了一件更宝贵的事：证明神恩惠的福音。他谦卑服事，把神全备的旨意都讲了；只有自己领受了恩典的人，才能这样服事。

## 深读 20:28-38 牧养神的教会

**1. 用自己血所买来的。** 「圣灵立你们作全群的监督，你们就当为自己谨慎，也为全群谨慎，牧养神的教会，就是他用自己血所买来的。」（20:28）这一节是新约中关于教会和牧养最重要的经文之一。监督（长老）是圣灵所立的；他们要先为「自己」谨慎，然后为「全群」谨慎；他们所牧养的，不是他们自己的教会，而是「神的教会」。「他用自己血所买来的」：这里有两个问题。第一，有些古抄本作「主的教会」，但「神的教会」有更早、更好的抄本支持（如西乃抄本、梵蒂冈抄本），也是保罗常用的说法（林前1:2），多数学者认为是原文。第二，「用自己血」一语，原文也可以理解为「用他自己那一位的血」，「那一位」就是他的爱子（参罗8:32「自己的儿子」）。若译作「自己的血」，这节经文把基督的血直接称为神的血，因为流血的那一位就是道成肉身的神子；这并不是说父神受苦流血。若译作「他自己那一位的血」，是父神用他爱子的血买了教会。无论哪一种，这节经文都显明三一神的工作：圣灵设立监督，父神拥有教会，圣子流血买赎；教会是基督用他替我们所流的血买回来的。

**2. 豺狼与悖谬的话。** 「我知道，我去之后必有凶暴的豺狼进入你们中间，不爱惜羊群。就是你们中间，也必有人起来说悖谬的话，要引诱门徒跟从他们。」（20:29-30）危险来自两方面：外面的豺狼，和里面的假教师。保罗后来写信给在以弗所的提摩太，信中就显出这个预言已经应验（提前1:3-7）；启示录也记载，以弗所教会后来能「试验那自称为使徒却不是使徒的」，却离弃了起初的爱心（启2:2-4）。

**3. 交托神和他恩惠的道。** 「如今我把你们交托神和他恩惠的道；这道能建立你们，叫你们和一切成圣的人同得基业。」（20:32）保罗要离开了，但教会不会被丢下。保护教会的，不是某一位伟大的使徒，而是神自己和他的话。

**4. 「施比受更为有福」。** 保罗说他「未曾贪图一个人的金、银、衣服」，用自己的手供给自己和同人的需用，又说：「当记念主耶稣的话，说：『施比受更为有福。』」（20:33-35）这句主耶稣的话，四福音里没有记载，是早期教会口传下来的。

**5. 跪下祷告，痛哭送别。** 「保罗说完了这话，就跪下同众人祷告。众人痛哭，抱着保罗的颈项，和他亲嘴。叫他们最伤心的，就是他说『以后不能再见我的面』那句话。」（20:36-38）这一幕让我们看见，保罗不只是一位神学家和宣教士，更是一位深受众人爱戴的牧人。三年的眼泪，换来了这样的眼泪。

> 要点：教会是神的，是他用自己的血买来的。所以牧养教会的人要先谨慎自己，忠心地传讲神的道，并且把羊群交托给那位比任何牧人都更爱他们的神。

## 默想

**默想：** 保罗说：「我却不以性命为念，也不看为宝贵，只要行完我的路程……证明神恩惠的福音。」他看轻性命，不是因为性命没有价值，而是因为神的恩惠在他眼中更宝贵。我手里抓得最紧的是什么：健康、存款、孩子的前途，还是退休以后的打算？如果我像保罗一样相信，神的恩惠才是我真正的珍宝，那位接纳我的主也必带我走完全程，我的日子会有什么不同？保罗又说，教会是神「用自己血所买来的」。我是用这个代价买回来的；那位常让我心烦的弟兄、那位我不太喜欢的姊妹，也是。我平常怎样谈论我的教会、牧者和弟兄姊妹？诚实的忧虑不是问题，教会有时正需要；可是我说起他们的时候，口气像是在给属于我的人打分数，还是记得他们属于那位用血买他们的主？最后，以弗所的长老抱着保罗痛哭，因为他三年「昼夜不住地流泪」劝戒他们。有没有一个人曾经为我流泪、为我祷告？我今天能不能写一封信、打一个电话向他道谢，也感谢差他来的那位大牧人？若一时想不起有谁，我仍可以记得：主耶稣自己「是长远活着，替他们祈求」（来7:25），其中也有我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 20 closes Paul's third missionary journey and begins his road to Jerusalem. **Macedonia and Greece** (vv. 1–6): Paul visits the churches, traveling with a group of co-workers from many places. **Troas** (vv. 7–12): on the first day of the week, as the believers gather to break bread, a young man named Eutychus falls from a window and dies, and Paul raises him. **The farewell at Miletus** (vv. 13–38): Paul calls the elders of the Ephesian church and gives them a tearful farewell address, then kneels and prays with them all.

The Miletus speech is the only address in Acts that Paul gives to believers, to church leaders. Here we see not Paul the debater but Paul the shepherd.

## Going Deeper: 20:1–6 Traveling Companions

**1. Encouragement and farewell.** "After the uproar ceased, Paul sent for the disciples, and after encouraging them, he said farewell and departed for Macedonia" (20:1). After Ephesus he went through Macedonia, encouraging the believers "with many words," then spent three months in Greece (20:2–3). This is very likely when he wrote Romans, from Corinth.

**2. A list of names.** With him were Sopater of Berea, Aristarchus and Secundus of Thessalonica, Gaius of Derbe, Timothy, and Tychicus and Trophimus of Asia (20:4). They came from different cities and churches. Many think they were delegates from the churches, escorting the Gentile churches' gift for the poor believers in Jerusalem (cf. Rom 15:25–26; 2 Cor 8:19–21). That gift was a concrete expression of Gentiles and Jews being one in Christ.

**3. "We" again.** "These went on ahead and were waiting for us at Troas" (20:5). The narrative shifts from "they" back to "we," indicating that Luke rejoined Paul at Philippi.

## Going Deeper: 20:7–12 The First Day of the Week

**1. Gathering to break bread.** "On the first day of the week, when we were gathered together to break bread, Paul talked with them, intending to depart on the next day, and he prolonged his speech until midnight" (20:7). This is one of the earliest New Testament references to believers gathering on "the first day of the week" (cf. 1 Cor 16:2). The day the Lord rose became the day the church gathered to worship and break bread in his memory.

**2. A young man at the window.** "And a young man named Eutychus, sitting at the window, sank into a deep sleep as Paul talked still longer. And being overcome by sleep, he fell down from the third story and was taken up dead" (20:9). There were many lamps in the upper room (20:8), the room was close, it was late at night, and the young man may well have worked all day. At Lystra Luke wrote "supposing that he was dead" (14:19); here he says plainly that Eutychus "was taken up dead."

**3. "Do not be alarmed."** "But Paul went down and bent over him, and taking him in his arms, said, 'Do not be alarmed, for his life is in him'" (20:10). Paul's "his life is in him" means life had returned (cf. 1 Kgs 17:21, "let this child's life come into him again"), not that he had never died. It recalls Elijah and Elisha stretching themselves over children they raised (1 Kgs 17:21; 2 Kgs 4:34). Paul went back up, broke bread, ate, and talked until daybreak (20:11). "And they took the youth away alive, and were not a little comforted" (20:12). On the day of the Lord's resurrection, a dead young man came back to life, a sign of the risen Lord present with his church.

## Going Deeper: 20:13–27 Paul Looks Back

**1. Sending for the elders.** Hurrying to reach Jerusalem by Pentecost, Paul didn't go into Ephesus; instead, "from Miletus he sent to Ephesus and called the elders of the church to come to him" (20:16–17).

**2. How he served.** "You yourselves know how I lived among you the whole time from the first day that I set foot in Asia, serving the Lord with all humility and with tears and with trials that happened to me through the plots of the Jews" (20:18–19). Paul doesn't begin with his achievements but with how he lived: humility, tears, trials. His ministry wasn't performed in a pulpit; it was seen in a life lived among them over years.

**3. In public and house to house.** "I did not shrink from declaring to you anything that was profitable, and teaching you in public and from house to house, testifying both to Jews and to Greeks of repentance toward God and of faith in our Lord Jesus Christ" (20:20–21). His gospel had two sides: "repentance toward God" and "faith in our Lord Jesus Christ." His teaching had two settings: public gatherings and private homes.

**4. "I do not account my life of any value."** "And now, behold, I am going to Jerusalem, constrained by the Spirit, not knowing what will happen to me there, except that the Holy Spirit testifies to me in every city that imprisonment and afflictions await me. But I do not account my life of any value nor as precious to myself, if only I may finish my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus, to testify to the gospel of the grace of God" (20:22–24). "Constrained by the Spirit" is literally "bound in/by the spirit"; it could mean Paul's own spirit, but most take it as the Holy Spirit, in line with verse 23. Before anyone bound Paul with chains, he was already "bound" by the Spirit, willingly walking toward the chains ahead. What mattered to him was not the length of his life but whether he would "finish" the course the Lord gave him.

**5. The whole counsel of God.** "Therefore I testify to you this day that I am innocent of the blood of all of you, for I did not shrink from declaring to you the whole counsel of God" (20:26–27). Paul again uses the watchman language of Ezekiel (Ezek 33:1–9). He didn't pick only what people wanted to hear; he taught them all the truth God has revealed.

> The point: Paul held his life cheap because he had given it to something more precious: testifying to the gospel of the grace of God. He served humbly and declared the whole counsel of God; only someone who has received grace can serve like that.

## Going Deeper: 20:28–38 Shepherding the Church of God

**1. Bought with his own blood.** "Pay careful attention to yourselves and to all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to care for the church of God, which he obtained with his own blood" (20:28). This is one of the New Testament's most important verses on the church and pastoral care. Overseers (elders) are appointed by the Spirit; they must watch first over "yourselves," then over "all the flock"; and what they shepherd is not their own church but "the church of God." "With his own blood": two questions arise. First, some manuscripts read "church of the Lord," but "church of God" has the earlier and better support (e.g. Codex Sinaiticus and Vaticanus), matches Paul's usual phrase (1 Cor 1:2), and is generally taken as original. Second, the Greek can mean "his own blood" or "the blood of his own [one]," that is, his Son (cf. Rom 8:32, "his own Son"). On the reading "his own blood," Christ's blood is called God's blood, because the one who shed it is God the Son incarnate; this does not mean the Father suffered. On the reading "the blood of his own [one]," the Father purchased the church with his beloved Son's blood. Either way the verse shows the triune God at work: the Spirit appoints overseers, the church belongs to God, and the Son's blood, shed in our place, bought it.

**2. Wolves and twisted words.** "I know that after my departure fierce wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock; and from among your own selves will arise men speaking twisted things, to draw away the disciples after them" (20:29–30). The danger comes from two directions: wolves from outside and false teachers from within. Paul's later letters to Timothy, then in Ephesus, show this prophecy coming true (1 Tim 1:3–7); and Revelation records that the Ephesian church could later "test those who call themselves apostles and are not," yet had abandoned the love it had at first (Rev 2:2–4).

**3. Commended to God and the word of his grace.** "And now I commend you to God and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up and to give you the inheritance among all those who are sanctified" (20:32). Paul was leaving, but the church would not be abandoned. What protects the church is not any great apostle but God himself and his word.

**4. "More blessed to give."** Paul said he had "coveted no one's silver or gold or apparel," had supported himself and his companions with his own hands, and told them to "remember the words of the Lord Jesus, how he himself said, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive'" (20:33–35). This saying of Jesus isn't recorded in the four Gospels; it was handed down in the early church.

**5. Kneeling in prayer, weeping farewell.** "And when he had said these things, he knelt down and prayed with them all. And there was much weeping on the part of all; they embraced Paul and kissed him, being sorrowful most of all because of the word he had spoken, that they would not see his face again" (20:36–38). This scene shows us that Paul was not only a theologian and missionary, but a deeply loved shepherd. Three years of his tears were answered with theirs.

> The point: The church belongs to God, who bought it with his own blood. So those who shepherd it must first watch over themselves, faithfully teach God's word, and entrust the flock to the God who loves them more than any shepherd does.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Paul said he did not count his life precious "if only I may finish my course... to testify to the gospel of the grace of God." He held his life loosely not because it was worthless, but because God's grace had become worth more to him. What do I hold most tightly: my health, my savings, my children's future, my retirement plans? What would change if I believed, as Paul did, that God's grace is my real treasure, and that the Lord who received me will carry me to the end of my course? Paul also said the church is what God "obtained with his own blood." I was bought at that price, and so were the brother who irritates me and the sister I find hard to like. How do I usually talk about my church, my pastor, my brothers and sisters? Honest concern isn't the problem; sometimes the church needs it. But when I speak of them, do I sound as if they're mine to grade, or do I remember they belong to the Lord who bought them? Finally, the Ephesian elders wept and embraced Paul, because for three years he had "not ceased night or day to admonish everyone with tears." Has someone wept and prayed for me? Could I write or call today to thank them, and thank the Chief Shepherd who sent them? If no one comes to mind, I can still remember that Jesus himself "always lives to make intercession for them" (Heb 7:25), and that includes me.
