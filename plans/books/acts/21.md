## 本章脉络

第二十一章，保罗终于到了耶路撒冷。**一路上的警告**（1-16节）：在推罗和凯撒利亚，门徒和先知一再预告他在耶路撒冷要被捆绑，众人苦劝他不要去，保罗却说：「我为主耶稣的名，不但被人捆绑，就是死在耶路撒冷也是愿意的。」**与雅各和长老相会**（17-26节）：为了消除犹太信徒的误会，保罗同意与四个许愿的人一同行洁净礼。**在殿里被捉拿**（27-40节）：从亚细亚来的犹太人诬告保罗带外邦人进殿，众人要打死他，罗马千夫长把他救下来，用两条铁链捆锁。

这一章有许多地方让人想起耶稣最后上耶路撒冷的路：一样「定意」前往，一样被预言要被「交在外邦人手里」，众人一样喊着「除掉他」。

## 深读 21:1-16 一路上的警告

**1. 推罗的门徒。** 在推罗，门徒「被圣灵感动，对保罗说：『不要上耶路撒冷去。』」（21:4）这看起来和保罗「被圣灵捆绑」要去耶路撒冷（20:22）互相矛盾。较好的理解是：圣灵向他们显明保罗将要受苦，这是从圣灵来的；他们因为爱保罗，就推论说他不应该去，这是他们自己的结论。圣灵启示的是前面的苦难，不是禁止保罗走这条路（参20:23）。

**2. 岸上的祷告。** 「他们众人同妻子儿女，送我们到城外，我们都跪在岸上祷告，彼此辞别。」（21:5）保罗在推罗只住了七天，却已经和那里的信徒，连同他们的孩子，建立了这样的感情。

**3. 腓利的家。** 在凯撒利亚，他们住在「传福音的腓利家里」，「他是那七个执事里的一个」（21:8）。二十多年前，腓利是第六章所选的七人之一；如今他已经在凯撒利亚安家，有「四个女儿，都是处女，是说预言的」（21:9）。保罗当年曾逼迫腓利所属的教会；如今，他住在腓利的家里。

**4. 亚迦布的腰带。** 先知亚迦布「拿保罗的腰带捆上自己的手脚，说：『圣灵说：犹太人在耶路撒冷，要如此捆绑这腰带的主人，把他交在外邦人手里。』」（21:11）这像旧约先知用行动来表达预言（参耶13:1-11）。这和耶稣预言自己「将要被交给外邦人」（路18:32）一样。

**5. 「愿主的旨意成就」。** 众人都苦劝保罗不要去。保罗说：「你们为什么这样痛哭，使我心碎呢？我为主耶稣的名，不但被人捆绑，就是死在耶路撒冷也是愿意的。」（21:13）「保罗既不听劝，我们便住了口，只说：『愿主的旨意成就』，便了。」（21:14）这句话令人想起耶稣在客西马尼园的祷告：「不要成就我的意思，只要成就你的意思。」（路22:42）

> 要点：圣灵预告了苦难，爱保罗的人想拦住他，保罗却甘心前往。最后，他们放下自己的意愿，说：「愿主的旨意成就。」

## 深读 21:17-26 雅各的建议

**1. 归荣耀与神。** 到了耶路撒冷，保罗把神在外邦人中所行的事「一一地述说了」，雅各和众长老「听见，就归荣耀与神」（21:19-20）。耶路撒冷的领袖为外邦人的归主欢喜。

**2. 一个谣言。** 他们又说：「犹太人中信主的有多少万，并且都为律法热心。他们听见人说，你教训一切在外邦的犹太人离弃摩西，对他们说，不要给孩子行割礼，也不要遵行条规。」（21:20-21）这是一个谣言。保罗教导外邦人不必受割礼，却从来没有叫犹太信徒放弃他们的习俗；他自己就给提摩太行了割礼（16:3）。

**3. 洁净的礼。** 雅各建议保罗带着四个许愿的人，「与他们一同行洁净的礼，替他们拿出规费」，好让众人知道他「为人，循规蹈矩，遵行律法」（21:23-24）。雅各也重申，外邦信徒只需要遵守耶路撒冷会议所定的几件事（21:25）。保罗照着做了（21:26）。

**4. 保罗是否妥协了？** 有人认为保罗在这里让步太多。但这正符合他自己说的原则：「向犹太人，我就作犹太人，为要得犹太人。」（林前9:20）遵守洁净礼，并不是靠律法得救，而是一个犹太基督徒可以自由选择的敬虔习俗。保罗愿意为了合一、为了不叫弟兄跌倒，付上时间和钱财。

> 要点：耶路撒冷的长老为外邦人归主而归荣耀与神，保罗也为了犹太弟兄而甘心遵守他们的习俗。福音的合一，需要双方都愿意为对方让一步。

## 深读 21:27-40 在殿里被捉拿

**1. 诬告。** 七日将完，从亚细亚来的犹太人看见保罗在殿里，就耸动众人，喊叫说：「这就是在各处教训众人糟践我们百姓和律法，并这地方的。他又带着希腊人进殿，污秽了这圣地。」（21:28）他们曾看见以弗所人特罗非摩同保罗在城里，「以为保罗带他进了殿」（21:29）。圣殿的外院和内院之间有一道矮墙，墙上刻着警告：外邦人进入内院，死罪自负。这个指控足以叫人置他于死地。

**2. 和司提反、和耶稣一样的控告。** 「糟践我们百姓和律法，并这地方」：这几乎和控告司提反的话一模一样（6:13）。保罗当年站在旁边看守打死司提反之人的衣裳，如今他自己站在同样的位置上。

**3. 殿门关了。** 「合城都震动，百姓一齐跑来，拿住保罗，拉他出殿，殿门立刻都关了。」（21:30）路加记下这个细节，或许带着深意：神的殿向神的使徒关上了门。

**4. 被罗马人救出来。** 他们正想要杀他，罗马千夫长带着兵丁跑下来，众人才止住不打。千夫长「吩咐用两条铁链捆锁」（21:33），亚迦布的预言应验了。众人跟在后面喊着说：「除掉他！」（21:36）众人在彼拉多面前也曾这样喊着要除掉耶稣：「除掉这个人！」（路23:18）

**5. 「求你准我对百姓说话」。** 千夫长以为保罗是那个曾带领四千凶徒作乱的埃及人（21:38）。保罗说：「我本是犹太人，生在基利家的大数，并不是无名小城的人。求你准我对百姓说话。」（21:39）刚刚被打、被锁的保罗，第一个要求不是保护自己，而是向那些要杀他的人讲话。他「站在台阶上，向百姓摆手」，用希伯来话对他们说话（21:40）。

> 要点：保罗走在他的主走过的路上：被诬告、被捉拿、被交在外邦人手里，听见众人喊「除掉他」。可是他心里想的，仍然是向这些人传福音。

## 默想

**默想：** 推罗和凯撒利亚的信徒爱保罗，所以苦劝他不要去；保罗也爱他们，所以说：「你们为什么这样痛哭，使我心碎呢？」最后他们说：「愿主的旨意成就。」我身边有没有一个我所爱的人，正走向一条我很担心的路：一个要去宣教工场的孩子，一个要辞去高薪去读神学的配偶，一个在困难中仍要为主站立的朋友？我的爱是想保护他免于一切苦难，还是愿意把他交在主的旨意里？我能这样放手，是因为父自己曾把他的爱子交出来，走向十字架。耶路撒冷的信徒「听见人说」保罗的坏话，就信了。我是否也曾因为「听见人说」，就对一位弟兄姊妹下了判断，而没有直接去问他，也没有先为他祷告？最后，保罗说，为主耶稣的名，「就是死在耶路撒冷也是愿意的」。我愿意为主付上什么？也许不是性命，而是一份安全、一点名声、一段舒适的日子。今天，我能不能像他一样说：「愿主的旨意成就」？

## The Shape of the Chapter

In chapter 21 Paul finally reaches Jerusalem. **Warnings along the way** (vv. 1–16): at Tyre and Caesarea, disciples and a prophet repeatedly foretell that he will be bound in Jerusalem; everyone urges him not to go, but Paul says, "I am ready not only to be imprisoned but even to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus." **Meeting James and the elders** (vv. 17–26): to clear up a misunderstanding among Jewish believers, Paul agrees to undergo purification with four men under a vow. **Seized in the temple** (vv. 27–40): Jews from Asia falsely accuse Paul of bringing Gentiles into the temple, the crowd tries to kill him, and a Roman tribune rescues him and binds him with two chains.

Much in this chapter recalls Jesus' last journey to Jerusalem: the same resolve to go, the same prophecy of being "delivered into the hands of the Gentiles," the same crowd crying "Away with him!"

## Going Deeper: 21:1–16 Warnings Along the Way

**1. The disciples at Tyre.** At Tyre, "through the Spirit they were telling Paul not to go on to Jerusalem" (21:4). This seems to contradict Paul being "constrained by the Spirit" to go (20:22). The better reading is that the Spirit revealed to them that Paul would suffer, and that came from the Spirit; out of love for Paul, they concluded he shouldn't go, and that was their own conclusion. The Spirit revealed the suffering ahead, not a prohibition against the road (cf. 20:23).

**2. Prayer on the beach.** "They all, with wives and children, accompanied us until we were outside the city. And kneeling down on the beach, we prayed and said farewell to one another" (21:5). Paul stayed only seven days in Tyre, yet he had already formed such bonds with the believers there, children included.

**3. Philip's house.** In Caesarea they stayed in "the house of Philip the evangelist, who was one of the seven" (21:8). More than twenty years earlier Philip had been one of the Seven chosen in chapter 6; now he was settled in Caesarea with "four unmarried daughters, who prophesied" (21:9). Paul had once persecuted the church Philip belonged to; now he was a guest in Philip's home.

**4. Agabus's belt.** The prophet Agabus "took Paul's belt and bound his own feet and hands and said, 'Thus says the Holy Spirit, "This is how the Jews at Jerusalem will bind the man who owns this belt and deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles"'" (21:11). It recalls Old Testament prophets acting out their message (cf. Jer 13:1–11). "Deliver him into the hands of the Gentiles" is exactly what Jesus said of himself (Luke 18:32).

**5. "Let the will of the Lord be done."** Everyone urged Paul not to go. Paul answered, "What are you doing, weeping and breaking my heart? For I am ready not only to be imprisoned but even to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus" (21:13). "And since he would not be persuaded, we ceased and said, 'Let the will of the Lord be done'" (21:14). It recalls Jesus' prayer in Gethsemane: "Not my will, but yours, be done" (Luke 22:42).

> The point: The Spirit foretold suffering, those who loved Paul tried to stop him, and Paul went willingly. In the end they laid down their own wishes and said, "Let the will of the Lord be done."

## Going Deeper: 21:17–26 James's Proposal

**1. They glorified God.** In Jerusalem Paul "related one by one the things that God had done among the Gentiles," and James and the elders, "when they heard it, glorified God" (21:19–20). The Jerusalem leaders rejoiced over the Gentiles coming to faith.

**2. A rumor.** But they added, "You see, brother, how many thousands there are among the Jews of those who have believed. They are all zealous for the law, and they have been told about you that you teach all the Jews who are among the Gentiles to forsake Moses, telling them not to circumcise their children or walk according to our customs" (21:20–21). It was a rumor. Paul taught that Gentiles need not be circumcised, but he never told Jewish believers to abandon their customs; he had circumcised Timothy himself (16:3).

**3. The rite of purification.** James proposed that Paul take four men under a vow, "purify yourself along with them and pay their expenses," so that everyone would know he himself lived "in observance of the law" (21:23–24). James also restated that Gentile believers were asked only to keep the few things decided at the Jerusalem council (21:25). Paul did as they suggested (21:26).

**4. Did Paul compromise?** Some think Paul gave way too far here. But it fits his own principle: "To the Jews I became as a Jew, in order to win Jews" (1 Cor 9:20). Keeping a purification rite was not seeking salvation by the law but a devout custom a Jewish Christian was free to observe. Paul was willing to spend time and money for unity and to avoid causing brothers to stumble.

> The point: The Jerusalem elders glorified God for the Gentiles' faith, and Paul willingly kept their customs for the sake of Jewish brothers. Gospel unity needs both sides willing to give way for the other.

## Going Deeper: 21:27–40 Seized in the Temple

**1. False charges.** When the seven days were nearly over, Jews from Asia saw Paul in the temple, stirred up the crowd, and shouted, "This is the man who is teaching everyone everywhere against the people and the law and this place. Moreover, he even brought Greeks into the temple and has defiled this holy place" (21:28). They had seen Trophimus the Ephesian with Paul in the city "and they supposed that Paul had brought him into the temple" (21:29). A low wall separated the temple's outer court from the inner courts, with inscriptions warning that any Gentile who went beyond it would be responsible for his own death. The charge was enough to get him killed.

**2. The same charges as against Stephen and Jesus.** "Against the people and the law and this place": almost exactly the charge against Stephen (6:13). Paul had once stood by guarding the garments of those who stoned Stephen; now he stood where Stephen had stood.

**3. The gates were shut.** "Then all the city was stirred up, and the people ran together. They seized Paul and dragged him out of the temple, and at once the gates were shut" (21:30). Luke may intend this detail: God's house shut its doors on God's apostle.

**4. Rescued by the Romans.** As they were trying to kill him, the Roman tribune ran down with soldiers, and they stopped beating him. The tribune "ordered him to be bound with two chains" (21:33), and Agabus's prophecy came true. The crowd followed, crying, "Away with him!" (21:36), just as the crowd before Pilate cried about Jesus, "Away with this man!" (Luke 23:18).

**5. "Permit me to speak to the people."** The tribune thought Paul was the Egyptian who had led four thousand Assassins in revolt (21:38). Paul said, "I am a Jew, from Tarsus in Cilicia, a citizen of no obscure city. I beg you, permit me to speak to the people" (21:39). Just beaten and chained, Paul's first request was not for his own protection but to speak to the people who wanted to kill him. "Standing on the steps, [he] motioned with his hand to the people," and addressed them in Hebrew (21:40).

> The point: Paul walked the road his Lord had walked: falsely accused, seized, handed to the Gentiles, hearing the crowd cry "Away with him!" And still what filled his heart was preaching the gospel to these very people.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The believers at Tyre and Caesarea loved Paul, so they pleaded with him not to go; Paul loved them too, and said, "You are breaking my heart." In the end they said, "Let the will of the Lord be done." Is someone I love heading down a road that worries me: a child going to the mission field, a spouse leaving a good salary to study at seminary, a friend standing for the Lord in hard circumstances? Does my love want to shield them from every hardship, or am I willing to place them in the Lord's will? I can let go because the Father himself gave up his beloved Son to the cross. The believers in Jerusalem "had been told" bad things about Paul and believed them. Have I judged a brother or sister because of what I "had been told," without asking them directly or first praying for them? Finally, Paul said he was ready "even to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus." What am I willing to give up for the Lord? Perhaps not my life, but some security, some reputation, some comfortable days. Today, can I say with him, "Let the will of the Lord be done"?
