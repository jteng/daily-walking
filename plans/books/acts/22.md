## 本章脉络

第二十二章是保罗在圣殿台阶上的讲话。刚刚要打死他的群众，听见他用希伯来话说话，「就更加安静了」（22:2）。保罗没有为自己辩护，而是讲述自己归主的经过：**从前的我**（3-5节），**大马士革路上**（6-11节），**亚拿尼亚**（12-16节），**圣殿里的异象**（17-21节）。当他说到主差他「往外邦人那里去」，众人又暴怒起来（22-23节）。千夫长要用鞭子拷问他，保罗才说出自己是生来的罗马人（24-30节）。

这是使徒行传第二次记载保罗归主的事（第一次在第9章，第三次在第26章）。路加三次讲同一件事，可见它在福音历史中的分量。每一次的讲法都配合听众：这一次，保罗面对的是热心律法的犹太人，所以他特别强调自己的犹太背景，和亚拿尼亚「按着律法是虔诚人」。

## 深读 22:1-5 从前的我

**1. 「诸位父兄请听」。** 「诸位父兄请听，我现在对你们分诉。」（22:1）保罗称这群刚刚要打死他的人为「父兄」。这和司提反开始讲论时的称呼一模一样（7:2）。他没有把他们当作仇敌，而是当作同胞。

**2. 「像你们众人今日一样」。** 「我原是犹太人，生在基利家的大数，长在这城里，在迦玛列门下，按着我们祖宗严紧的律法受教，热心事奉神，像你们众人今日一样。」（22:3）保罗明白他们的热心，因为他曾经比他们更热心。迦玛列是当时最受尊敬的拉比之一（参5:34）。

**3. 「我也曾逼迫奉这道的人」。** 「我也曾逼迫奉这道的人，直到死地，无论男女都锁拿下监。」（22:4）保罗没有隐藏自己的过去。他后来写信说，他「就热心说，我是逼迫教会的；就律法上的义说，我是无可指摘的」（腓3:6）。一个在宗教上无可指摘的人，却在逼迫神的教会；这正显出，单靠宗教热心不能使人认识神。

## 深读 22:6-16 大马士革路上与亚拿尼亚

**1. 晌午的大光。** 「约在晌午，忽然从天上发大光，四面照着我。」（22:6）这一次保罗加上了「晌午」这个细节：正午的太阳最亮，那光却比太阳更亮。9:7说同行的人「听见声音」，这里却说他们「没有听明那位对我说话的声音」（22:9）。这并不矛盾：他们听见了声响，却没有听明话语的内容，正如约12:29众人听见天上的声音，却以为是打雷。三处记载合起来看是一致的：只有保罗听懂了主的话，也只有他被差遣。

**2. 「拿撒勒人耶稣」。** 「我回答说：『主啊，你是谁？』他说：『我就是你所逼迫的拿撒勒人耶稣。』」（22:8）在这一次的讲述里，主自称「拿撒勒人耶稣」。对听众来说，这是一个被钉十字架、被看为受咒诅的名字；保罗却说，这位拿撒勒人从天上向他说话。

**3. 「主啊，我当做什么？」** 「我说：『主啊，我当做什么？』」（22:10）这是一个真正被主拦住的人的问题。他不再问「我要怎样为神发热心」，而是问「主啊，我当做什么」。

**4. 按着律法是虔诚人。** 「那里有一个人，名叫亚拿尼亚，按着律法是虔诚人，为一切住在那里的犹太人所称赞。」（22:12）第9章说亚拿尼亚是「一个门徒」；这里保罗特别提到他在犹太人中的好名声，因为听众是犹太人。同一件事，可以从不同的角度说，只要都是真实的。

**5. 「那义者」。** 亚拿尼亚说：「我们祖宗的神拣选了你，叫你明白他的旨意，又得见那义者，听他口中所出的声音。因为你要将所看见的，所听见的，对着万人为他作见证。」（22:14-15）亚拿尼亚称耶稣为「那义者」，这正是彼得（3:14）和司提反临死前（7:52）所用的称呼。

**6. 「求告他的名受洗，洗去你的罪」。** 「现在你为什么耽延呢？起来，求告他的名受洗，洗去你的罪。」（22:16）这句话不是说洗礼的水本身能除罪。句末的「求告他的名」说明了洗净的根源：罪得赦免，是因为人呼求、信靠主耶稣（参2:21；罗10:13）。新约常把洗礼和它所表明的洗净连在一起说（林前6:11；多3:5），彼得也说，洗礼「本不在乎除掉肉体的污秽，只求在神面前有无亏的良心」（彼前3:21）。洗礼是神所设立、表明并印证这洗净的记号；正因如此，亚拿尼亚说：「现在你为什么耽延呢？」信主的人不应轻看或拖延洗礼。

> 要点：保罗的见证，是一个热心宗教的人被耶稣拦住、被恩典改变的故事。他没有夸自己的过去，也没有隐藏它；他只是如实讲出主为他做了什么。

## 深读 22:17-21 「我要差你远远地往外邦人那里去」

**1. 在殿里的异象。** 保罗说，他回到耶路撒冷，「在殿里祷告的时候，魂游象外，看见主向我说：『你赶紧地离开耶路撒冷，不可迟延；因你为我作的见证，这里的人必不领受。』」（22:17-18）保罗特意提到这是「在殿里」：他并没有轻看圣殿，甚至主就是在圣殿里差遣他的。

**2. 保罗的理由。** 保罗回答主：「他们知道我从前把信你的人收在监里，又在各会堂里鞭打他们。并且你的见证人司提反被害流血的时候，我也站在旁边欢喜；又看守害死他之人的衣裳。」（22:19-20）保罗似乎以为，正因为耶路撒冷的人知道他的过去，他的见证就会特别有力量：昔日的逼迫者如今成了信徒。他想留在自己最能被人理解的地方。

**3. 「你去吧！」** 「主向我说：『你去吧！我要差你远远地往外邦人那里去。』」（22:21）主的计划和保罗的想法不同。保罗认为最合理的地方，主却说「这里的人必不领受」；主要差他到他从来没有想过要去的地方。

**4. 一个词引爆的愤怒。** 「众人听他说到这句话，就高声说：『这样的人，从世上除掉他吧！他是不当活着的。』」（22:22）他们安静地听保罗讲大马士革的光、讲亚拿尼亚，可是一听到「外邦人」，就暴怒起来。他们不能接受的，是神的恩典竟然也临到外邦人，而且不需要他们先成为犹太人。

> 要点：保罗以为自己最适合留在耶路撒冷，主却差他到远方的外邦人中去。神的恩典常常越过我们所设定的界线，也常常把我们带到我们没有想过的地方。

## 深读 22:22-30 罗马的民籍

**1. 鞭子拷问。** 千夫长大概听不懂保罗用的希伯来话（亚兰话），不明白众人为什么这样喧嚷，「就吩咐将保罗带进营楼去，叫人用鞭子拷问他」（22:24）。罗马的鞭刑是在皮鞭上缀着骨头或金属，常常使人重伤甚至死亡。

**2. 「有这个例吗？」** 「刚用皮条捆上，保罗对旁边站着的百夫长说：『人是罗马人，又没有定罪，你们就鞭打他，有这个例吗？』」（22:25）按罗马法律，不可以未经审判就鞭打罗马公民；西塞罗说，捆绑罗马公民已是罪行，鞭打他更是恶行。所以千夫长连「捆绑了他」也害怕（22:29）。保罗在腓立比曾先受了鞭打，事后才说明身份（16:37）；这一次，他在受刑之前就说出来了。

**3. 「我生来就是」。** 千夫长说：「我用许多银子才入了罗马的民籍。」保罗说：「我生来就是。」（22:28）千夫长的民籍是花钱买的，保罗的民籍是与生俱来的。这个对比也让我们想到另一种民籍：保罗后来写信说，「我们却是天上的国民」（腓3:20）。这个民籍，人不能用钱买，只能藉着重生，从神那里白白领受。

**4. 合法地使用权利。** 保罗没有为了显得属灵，就放弃法律给他的保护。他在必要的时候，合法地使用公民的权利，为的是保全性命，继续传福音。基督徒可以感恩地使用社会给他的正当权利，只是不倚靠它们。

## 默想

**默想：** 保罗面对一群要打死他的人，没有辩论神学，而是讲述「主为我做了什么」：从前的我怎样，主怎样拦住我，怎样差人来，怎样改变我。如果今天有人问我为什么信耶稣，我能不能用几分钟，诚实又清楚地说出我的故事？我的故事不需要像保罗那样戏剧化，因为它的中心不是我，而是那位找到我的主。保罗曾以为，耶路撒冷最能明白他的过去，所以他留在那里最有用；主却说：「你去吧！我要差你远远地往外邦人那里去。」我是否也只想在自己熟悉、容易被理解的地方服事？主是否正在把我带到一个我没有想过的地方或人群中？最后，千夫长的民籍是花许多银子买来的，保罗却是「生来就是」。我在天上的国籍，不是我用好行为买来的，而是基督用他自己的血为我付上了代价，又藉着重生白白赐给我的。今天，当我面对地上的不安和动荡时，能不能记得自己真正的家乡在哪里？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 22 is Paul's speech from the temple steps. The crowd that had just been trying to kill him heard him speak in Hebrew and "became even more quiet" (22:2). Paul didn't defend himself; he told the story of his conversion: **who I was** (vv. 3–5), **the Damascus road** (vv. 6–11), **Ananias** (vv. 12–16), and **a vision in the temple** (vv. 17–21). When he said the Lord had sent him "to the Gentiles," the crowd erupted again (vv. 22–23). The tribune ordered him flogged, and only then did Paul reveal that he was a Roman citizen by birth (vv. 24–30).

This is the second of Acts' three accounts of Paul's conversion (the first in ch. 9, the third in ch. 26). That Luke tells the same event three times shows its weight in gospel history. Each telling suits its audience: here Paul faces Jews zealous for the law, so he stresses his own Jewish background and that Ananias was "a devout man according to the law."

## Going Deeper: 22:1–5 Who I Was

**1. "Brothers and fathers, hear me."** "Brothers and fathers, hear the defense that I now make before you" (22:1). Paul called the people who had just tried to kill him "brothers and fathers," exactly how Stephen began his speech (7:2). He didn't treat them as enemies but as his own people.

**2. "As all of you are this day."** "I am a Jew, born in Tarsus in Cilicia, but brought up in this city, educated at the feet of Gamaliel according to the strict manner of the law of our fathers, being zealous for God as all of you are this day" (22:3). Paul understood their zeal because he had once been more zealous still. Gamaliel was one of the most respected rabbis of the day (cf. 5:34).

**3. "I persecuted this Way."** "I persecuted this Way to the death, binding and delivering to prison both men and women" (22:4). Paul didn't hide his past. He later wrote that he had been, "as to zeal, a persecutor of the church; as to righteousness, under the law blameless" (Phil 3:6). A man blameless in religion was persecuting God's church; religious zeal alone does not bring a person to know God.

## Going Deeper: 22:6–16 The Damascus Road and Ananias

**1. A great light at noon.** "About noon a great light from heaven suddenly shone around me" (22:6). This time Paul adds "about noon": when the sun is brightest, this light was brighter still. In 9:7 the men "heard the voice"; here they "did not understand the voice of the one who was speaking to me" (22:9). There is no contradiction: they heard a sound but did not make out the words, much as the crowd in John 12:29 heard the voice from heaven and thought it was thunder. Taken together, the accounts agree: only Paul understood the Lord's words, and only Paul was commissioned.

**2. "Jesus of Nazareth."** "And I answered, 'Who are you, Lord?' And he said to me, 'I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you are persecuting'" (22:8). In this telling the Lord calls himself "Jesus of Nazareth." To this audience, it was the name of a crucified man, thought to be under God's curse; yet Paul says this Nazarene spoke to him from heaven.

**3. "What shall I do, Lord?"** "And I said, 'What shall I do, Lord?'" (22:10). It is the question of someone truly stopped by the Lord. He no longer asks how to be zealous for God, but, "Lord, what shall I do?"

**4. Devout according to the law.** "And one Ananias, a devout man according to the law, well spoken of by all the Jews who lived there" (22:12). Chapter 9 called Ananias "a disciple"; here Paul highlights his good name among Jews, because his hearers are Jews. The same event can be told from different angles, so long as each is true.

**5. "The Righteous One."** Ananias said, "The God of our fathers appointed you to know his will, to see the Righteous One and to hear a voice from his mouth; for you will be a witness for him to everyone of what you have seen and heard" (22:14–15). Ananias calls Jesus "the Righteous One," the title Peter (3:14) and Stephen just before his death (7:52) had used.

**6. "Be baptized and wash away your sins, calling on his name."** "And now why do you wait? Rise and be baptized and wash away your sins, calling on his name" (22:16). This doesn't mean the water of baptism itself removes sin. The closing phrase, "calling on his name," points to the source of the cleansing: sins are forgiven as a person calls on and trusts the Lord Jesus (cf. 2:21; Rom 10:13). The New Testament often speaks of baptism together with the washing it signifies (1 Cor 6:11; Titus 3:5), and Peter says baptism is "not as a removal of dirt from the body but as an appeal to God for a good conscience" (1 Pet 3:21). Baptism is the sign and seal God appointed for that washing; that is why Ananias asks, "Why do you wait?" A believer should neither despise nor delay it.

> The point: Paul's testimony is the story of a religious zealot stopped by Jesus and changed by grace. He neither boasted of his past nor hid it; he simply told what the Lord had done for him.

## Going Deeper: 22:17–21 "I Will Send You Far Away to the Gentiles"

**1. A vision in the temple.** Paul said that when he returned to Jerusalem "and was praying in the temple, I fell into a trance and saw him saying to me, 'Make haste and get out of Jerusalem quickly, because they will not accept your testimony about me'" (22:17–18). Paul stresses that this was "in the temple": far from despising the temple, he was commissioned there by the Lord.

**2. Paul's reasoning.** Paul answered the Lord, "Lord, they themselves know that in one synagogue after another I imprisoned and beat those who believed in you. And when the blood of Stephen your witness was being shed, I myself was standing by and approving and watching over the garments of those who killed him" (22:19–20). Paul seems to have thought that because Jerusalem knew his past, his testimony would be especially powerful there: the former persecutor now a believer. He wanted to stay where he would be best understood.

**3. "Go."** "And he said to me, 'Go, for I will send you far away to the Gentiles'" (22:21). The Lord's plan differed from Paul's. Where Paul thought it most sensible to stay, the Lord said "they will not accept your testimony"; he would send him where he had never thought of going.

**4. Rage at one word.** "Up to this word they listened to him. Then they raised their voices and said, 'Away with such a fellow from the earth! For he should not be allowed to live'" (22:22). They listened quietly to the light on the Damascus road and to Ananias, but at the word "Gentiles" they erupted. What they could not accept was that God's grace had reached the Gentiles too, without their first becoming Jews.

> The point: Paul thought he was best suited to stay in Jerusalem, but the Lord sent him far away to the Gentiles. God's grace often crosses the boundaries we draw, and often takes us where we never thought of going.

## Going Deeper: 22:22–30 Roman Citizenship

**1. Examined by flogging.** The tribune, who probably couldn't follow Paul's Hebrew (Aramaic) or why the crowd was shouting, "ordered him to be brought into the barracks, saying that he should be examined by flogging" (22:24). Roman scourging used whips weighted with bone or metal and often caused severe injury or death.

**2. "Is it lawful?"** "But when they had stretched him out for the whips, Paul said to the centurion who was standing by, 'Is it lawful for you to flog a man who is a Roman citizen and uncondemned?'" (22:25). Under Roman law, a citizen could not be flogged without trial; Cicero wrote that binding a Roman citizen is a crime and flogging him a wickedness, which is why the tribune feared even that "he had bound him" (22:29). At Philippi Paul was beaten first and only afterward revealed his status (16:37); this time he spoke before the whip fell.

**3. "I am a citizen by birth."** The tribune said, "I bought this citizenship for a large sum." Paul said, "But I am a citizen by birth" (22:28). The tribune's citizenship was purchased; Paul's was his from birth. The contrast brings another citizenship to mind: Paul later wrote, "our citizenship is in heaven" (Phil 3:20). That citizenship can't be bought with money; it is received freely from God through new birth.

**4. Using rights lawfully.** Paul didn't give up the protection the law gave him in order to look more spiritual. When necessary, he used his rights as a citizen lawfully, to preserve his life and keep preaching the gospel. Christians may gratefully use the legitimate rights society gives them, without relying on them.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Facing a crowd that wanted to kill him, Paul didn't argue theology; he told "what the Lord did for me": who I was, how the Lord stopped me, whom he sent, how he changed me. If someone asked me today why I believe in Jesus, could I tell my story honestly and clearly in a few minutes? My story doesn't have to be dramatic like Paul's, because its center isn't me but the Lord who found me. Paul thought Jerusalem would understand his past best, so he would be most useful there; but the Lord said, "Go, for I will send you far away to the Gentiles." Do I only want to serve where I'm familiar and easily understood? Is the Lord leading me toward a place or a group of people I never thought of? Finally, the tribune bought his citizenship "for a large sum," but Paul was "a citizen by birth." My citizenship in heaven wasn't bought by my good works; Christ paid its price with his own blood and gave it to me freely through new birth. Today, facing the unrest and uncertainty of this world, can I remember where my true home is?
