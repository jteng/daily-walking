## 本章脉络

第二十七章是古代文学中描写航海和船难最详细的记载之一。路加用「我们」叙述，表示他亲自在船上。**启航**（1-8节）：保罗和别的囚犯交给百夫长犹流，从凯撒利亚沿着海岸航行，到了克里特的佳澳。**保罗的劝告被拒**（9-12节）：已过了禁食的节期，保罗警告继续航行会有危险，百夫长却信从掌船的和船主。**风暴**（13-20节）：狂风「友拉革罗」扑来，船任风刮去，多日不见日月星辰，「我们得救的指望就都绝了」。**保罗站起来**（21-38节）：保罗说神的使者向他显现，应许全船的人性命一个也不失丧；他劝众人吃饭，自己拿饼祝谢。**搁浅**（39-44节）：船在马耳他附近撞毁，兵丁要杀囚犯，百夫长为了救保罗拦住他们，二百七十六个人全都上了岸。

整章的主角表面上是风浪和水手，实际上是神的应许：保罗「必定站在凯撒面前」（27:24）。风暴不能拦阻这应许，反而成了显明这应许的舞台。

## 深读 27:1-12 被拒绝的劝告

**1. 百夫长犹流。** 「便将保罗和别的囚犯交给御营里的一个百夫长，名叫犹流。」（27:1）在西顿，「犹流宽待保罗，准他往朋友那里去，受他们的照应」（27:3）。这位罗马军官对保罗的尊重，在这次航程中会越来越明显。

**2. 同行的人。** 同行的有帖撒罗尼迦人亚里达古（27:2），还有路加自己（「我们」）。保罗虽然是囚犯，却没有孤单上路。

**3. 过了禁食的节期。** 「走的日子多了，已经过了禁食的节期，行船又危险。」（27:9）「禁食的节期」指赎罪日，大约在九月底到十月初。古代地中海航行，过了九月中旬就危险，十一月以后几乎停航。

**4. 保罗的警告。** 「众位，我看这次行船，不但货物和船要受损伤，大遭破坏，连我们的性命也难保。」（27:10）保罗是一个有经验的旅行者，他已经经历过三次船难（林后11:25），都发生在写哥林多后书以前，使徒行传都没有记载；马耳他是第四次。「但百夫长信从掌船的和船主，不信从保罗所说的。」（27:11）从人的角度看，这很合理：船主和舵手是专家，保罗只是一个囚犯。大多数人想到一个更好的港口去过冬（27:12）。

## 深读 27:13-20 得救的指望都绝了

**1. 微微的南风。** 「这时，微微起了南风，他们以为得意，就起了锚，贴近克里特行去。」（27:13）看起来一切顺利，好像保罗的警告是多余的。

**2. 友拉革罗。** 「不多几时，狂风从岛上扑下来；那风名叫『友拉革罗』。船被风抓住，敌不住风，我们就任风刮去。」（27:14-15）「友拉革罗」是东北风。他们用缆索捆住船底，怕撞上非洲北岸的沙滩，就落下篷来任船飘去（27:16-17）；第二天把货物抛进海里，第三天连船上的器具也抛掉了（27:18-19）。

**3. 指望都绝了。** 「太阳和星辰多日不显露，又有狂风大浪催逼，我们得救的指望就都绝了。」（27:20）在没有指南针的时代，看不见太阳和星辰，就无法辨认方向。这句话包括路加自己：「我们」得救的指望都绝了。圣经没有隐藏信徒也会有绝望的时刻。

> 要点：人以为「微微起了南风」就是顺利，却不知道风暴就在前面。当人的一切方法都用尽，连信徒也失去盼望的时候，正是神的话要显出力量的时候。

## 深读 27:21-38 保罗站起来

**1. 「你们本该听我的话」。** 「众人多日没有吃什么，保罗就出来站在他们中间，说：『众位，你们本该听我的话，不离开克里特，免得遭这样的伤损破坏。』」（27:21）这不是「我早就说过了」的得意，而是为接下来的话建立信任：我之前说的应验了，现在我要说的，你们也可以相信。

**2. 「我所属所事奉的神」。** 「现在我还劝你们放心，你们的性命一个也不失丧，惟独失丧这船。因我所属所事奉的神，他的使者昨夜站在我旁边，说：『保罗，不要害怕，你必定站在凯撒面前，并且与你同船的人，神都赐给你了。』」（27:22-24）「我所属」：保罗先说他是属于神的，然后才说他事奉神。在一船异教徒面前，他清楚地承认自己的身份。

**3. 「神都赐给你了」。** 「与你同船的人，神都赐给你了。」「赐给」原文有「施恩赐予」的意思，可能暗示保罗曾为全船的人祷告。二百七十多个水手、兵丁和囚犯得以保全，是因为神向他的仆人施恩，并要成就他的计划（参创18:32）。

**4. 「我信神」。** 「所以众位可以放心，我信神他怎样对我说，事情也要怎样成就。只是我们必要撞在一个岛上。」（27:25-26）保罗的信心不是乐观的猜测，而是对神的话的信靠。他知道船会毁坏，也知道会撞在岛上；但他更知道，神说了，就必成就。

**5. 神的应许与人的责任。** 水手们想放下小船偷偷逃走。保罗对百夫长和兵丁说：「这些人若不等在船上，你们必不能得救。」（27:31）神已经应许全船的人性命都必保全，可是保罗仍然说，人必须留在船上。神不但定了结局，也定了达成结局的途径；神的主权并不取消人的责任，神的应许常常是藉着人的顺服来成就的。兵丁就砍断小船的绳子，叫它漂去（27:32）。

**6. 拿饼祝谢。** 「保罗说了这话，就拿着饼，在众人面前祝谢了神，擘开吃。于是他们都放下心，也就吃了。」（27:35-36）这几个动词（拿、祝谢、擘开）和耶稣设立圣餐时一样（路22:19），也和喂饱五千人的记载相似（路9:16）。这不是在船上守圣餐，却是一个在风暴中向众人见证神的举动。一个囚犯在两百多个绝望的人面前感谢神，叫他们都「放下心」。

> 要点：在风暴中，保罗的平安不是来自环境，而是来自他所属、所事奉的神，和神亲口说的应许。他的信心也带给全船的人盼望。

## 深读 27:39-44 都得了救

**1. 搁浅。** 天亮时，他们看见一个有沙滩的海湾，想把船拢进去，「但遇着两水夹流的地方，就把船搁了浅；船头胶住不动，船尾被浪的猛力冲坏」（27:41）。保罗说「惟独失丧这船」（27:22），果然应验了。

**2. 兵丁的意思。** 「兵丁的意思要把囚犯杀了，恐怕有洑水脱逃的。」（27:42）按罗马的规矩，看守若让囚犯逃走，自己要受重罚（参12:19希律处死看守；16:27腓立比的禁卒）。在这一刻，保罗的性命又一次面临危险。

**3. 百夫长要救保罗。** 「但百夫长要救保罗，不准他们任意而行，就吩咐会洑水的，跳下水去先上岸；其余的人可以用板子或船上的零碎东西上岸。这样，众人都得了救，上了岸。」（27:43-44）因为百夫长要救保罗，其他囚犯也都得救了。神的应许，一个字也没有落空：「你们的性命一个也不失丧。」

> 要点：船毁了，货物没了，可是神的话一句也没有落空。二百七十六个人都上了岸，因为神说过「与你同船的人，神都赐给你了」。

## 默想

**默想：** 「太阳和星辰多日不显露……我们得救的指望就都绝了。」路加没有假装他们一直很有信心。我生命中有没有一段「多日不见日月星辰」的日子，连方向都看不清，盼望也一点一点地熄灭？就在那样的时候，保罗站起来说：「我信神他怎样对我说，事情也要怎样成就。」他所抓住的，不是自己的感觉，而是神说过的话。我今天能抓住神的哪一句应许？我所信的那位主，曾经平静风浪；他也亲自进入了死亡最深的黑暗，又复活出来，所以他的话是可靠的。神对保罗说：「与你同船的人，神都赐给你了。」我所在的公司、学校、家族或小区，就是我的「船」。我是否为同船的人祷告？我在风暴中的样子，是叫他们更惊慌，还是像保罗拿饼祝谢那样，叫他们「放下心」？最后，神已经应许全船的人得救，保罗却仍说：「这些人若不等在船上，你们必不能得救。」我是否以为，既然神掌权，我就可以不必认真地照他的话行？神的主权，正是我放心顺服的理由。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 27 is one of the most detailed accounts of a voyage and shipwreck in ancient literature. Luke tells it as "we," meaning he was on board. **Setting sail** (vv. 1–8): Paul and other prisoners are handed to a centurion named Julius and sail up the coast from Caesarea to Fair Havens on Crete. **Paul's advice rejected** (vv. 9–12): with the Fast already past, Paul warns that sailing on will be dangerous, but the centurion listens to the pilot and the ship's owner. **The storm** (vv. 13–20): the northeaster strikes, the ship is driven before the wind, and for many days neither sun nor stars appear; "all hope of our being saved was at last abandoned." **Paul stands up** (vv. 21–38): Paul says an angel of God appeared to him and promised that no one aboard would lose his life; he urges them to eat and himself gives thanks over bread. **Aground** (vv. 39–44): the ship breaks up off Malta, the soldiers want to kill the prisoners, the centurion stops them to save Paul, and all 276 reach land.

On the surface the chapter's main characters are the wind, the waves and the sailors; in reality it is God's promise: Paul "must stand before Caesar" (27:24). The storm couldn't stop that promise; it became the stage on which the promise was displayed.

## Going Deeper: 27:1–12 Advice Rejected

**1. The centurion Julius.** "They delivered Paul and some other prisoners to a centurion of the Augustan Cohort named Julius" (27:1). At Sidon, "Julius treated Paul kindly and gave him leave to go to his friends and be cared for" (27:3). This Roman officer's respect for Paul will grow more evident as the voyage goes on.

**2. Companions.** With them was Aristarchus of Thessalonica (27:2), and Luke himself ("we"). Though a prisoner, Paul didn't travel alone.

**3. After the Fast.** "Since much time had passed, and the voyage was now dangerous because even the Fast was already over" (27:9). "The Fast" was the Day of Atonement, around late September or early October. Ancient Mediterranean sailing became dangerous after mid-September and nearly stopped after November.

**4. Paul's warning.** "Sirs, I perceive that the voyage will be with injury and much loss, not only of the cargo and the ship, but also of our lives" (27:10). Paul was an experienced traveler who had already been shipwrecked three times (2 Cor 11:25), all before 2 Corinthians was written and none recorded in Acts; Malta would be the fourth. "But the centurion paid more attention to the pilot and to the owner of the ship than to what Paul said" (27:11). Humanly, that was reasonable: the owner and helmsman were experts, Paul only a prisoner. The majority wanted to reach a better harbor for the winter (27:12).

## Going Deeper: 27:13–20 All Hope Abandoned

**1. A gentle south wind.** "Now when the south wind blew gently, supposing that they had obtained their purpose, they weighed anchor and sailed along Crete, close to the shore" (27:13). Everything seemed fine, as if Paul's warning had been needless.

**2. The northeaster.** "But soon a tempestuous wind, called the northeaster, struck down from the land. And when the ship was caught and could not face the wind, we gave way to it and were driven along" (27:14–15). They undergirded the ship with ropes, afraid of running onto the sandbars off North Africa, lowered the gear and let the ship drift (27:16–17); the next day they jettisoned cargo, and the third day threw the ship's tackle overboard (27:18–19).

**3. All hope abandoned.** "When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and no small tempest lay on us, all hope of our being saved was at last abandoned" (27:20). In an age without compasses, with no sun or stars there was no way to know direction. The sentence includes Luke himself: "our" hope was abandoned. Scripture doesn't hide that believers too have moments of despair.

> The point: People took the "gentle south wind" for success, not knowing the storm lay ahead. When every human means is exhausted, and even believers lose hope, that is when God's word shows its power.

## Going Deeper: 27:21–38 Paul Stands Up

**1. "You should have listened to me."** "Since they had been without food for a long time, Paul stood up among them and said, 'Men, you should have listened to me and not have set sail from Crete and incurred this injury and loss'" (27:21). This isn't "I told you so"; it builds trust for what comes next: what I said before came true, so you can believe what I say now.

**2. "The God to whom I belong."** "Yet now I urge you to take heart, for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship. For this very night there stood before me an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I worship, and he said, 'Do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar. And behold, God has granted you all those who sail with you'" (27:22–24). "To whom I belong": Paul first says he belongs to God, and only then that he serves him. Before a shipful of pagans, he plainly confesses who he is.

**3. "God has granted you all."** "God has granted you all those who sail with you." "Granted" carries the sense of a gracious gift, and may imply that Paul had been praying for everyone aboard. More than two hundred seventy sailors, soldiers and prisoners were spared because God showed favor to his servant and was carrying out his purpose (cf. Gen 18:32).

**4. "I have faith in God."** "So take heart, men, for I have faith in God that it will be exactly as I have been told. But we must run aground on some island" (27:25–26). Paul's faith isn't optimistic guesswork; it is trust in God's word. He knows the ship will be lost and that they will run aground; but he knows even more surely that what God has said will happen.

**5. God's promise and human responsibility.** The sailors tried to slip away in the ship's boat. Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, "Unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved" (27:31). God had promised that every life aboard would be spared, yet Paul still said people had to stay on board. God ordains not only the end but also the means; his sovereignty doesn't cancel human responsibility, and God often fulfills his promises through human obedience. So the soldiers cut away the ropes of the boat and let it go (27:32).

**6. Giving thanks for bread.** "And when he had said these things, he took bread, and giving thanks to God in the presence of all he broke it and began to eat. Then they all were encouraged and ate some food themselves" (27:35–36). The verbs (took, gave thanks, broke) match Jesus at the Last Supper (Luke 22:19) and resemble the feeding of the five thousand (Luke 9:16). This wasn't the Lord's Supper on shipboard, but it was a witness to God before everyone in the storm. A prisoner gave thanks to God in front of more than two hundred despairing people, and they were all "encouraged."

> The point: In the storm, Paul's peace came not from circumstances but from the God to whom he belonged and whom he served, and from the promise God had spoken. His faith gave hope to everyone on the ship.

## Going Deeper: 27:39–44 All Brought Safely to Land

**1. Aground.** At daybreak they saw a bay with a beach and tried to run the ship ashore, "but striking a reef, they ran the vessel aground. The bow stuck and remained immovable, and the stern was being broken up by the surf" (27:41). Paul had said "only of the ship" (27:22), and so it was.

**2. The soldiers' plan.** "The soldiers' plan was to kill the prisoners, lest any should swim away and escape" (27:42). Under Roman practice, guards who let prisoners escape faced severe punishment (cf. 12:19, under Herod; 16:27, the Philippian jailer). At that moment Paul's life was in danger once more.

**3. The centurion saves Paul.** "But the centurion, wishing to save Paul, kept them from carrying out their plan. He ordered those who could swim to jump overboard first and make for the land, and the rest on planks or on pieces of the ship. And so it was that all were brought safely to land" (27:43–44). Because the centurion wanted to save Paul, the other prisoners were saved too. Not one word of God's promise failed: "there will be no loss of life among you."

> The point: The ship was lost and the cargo gone, but not one word of God failed. All 276 reached land, because God had said, "God has granted you all those who sail with you."

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days... all hope of our being saved was at last abandoned." Luke doesn't pretend they were full of faith the whole time. Has there been a stretch in my life when for "many days" I couldn't see sun or stars, couldn't tell direction, and hope went out bit by bit? In just such a time Paul stood up and said, "I have faith in God that it will be exactly as I have been told." What he held onto was not his feelings but what God had said. Which of God's promises can I hold onto today? The Lord I trust once calmed the storm; he also went into the deepest darkness of death and rose again, so his word can be trusted. God told Paul, "God has granted you all those who sail with you." My company, school, extended family or neighborhood is my "ship." Do I pray for those who sail with me? In the storm, does my presence make them more alarmed, or, like Paul giving thanks for bread, does it leave them "encouraged"? Finally, God had promised to save everyone aboard, yet Paul still said, "Unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved." Do I think that because God is sovereign, I needn't take obeying his word seriously? God's sovereignty is exactly what lets me obey without fear.
