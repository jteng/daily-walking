## 这卷书在讲什么

歌罗西书是保罗写给一个他从来没有见过面的教会的信（2:1）。这个教会在一个不起眼的小城里，人数大概不多，却面对一种听起来很属灵、很有智慧的教导。那种教导没有叫人离开基督，只是暗示：单有基督还不够，你还需要更深的经历、更严的规条、更高的知识，才能得着「完全」。

保罗的回答贯穿全信：基督已经是一切。「爱子是那不能看见之神的像」，「万有都是靠他造的」，「他也是教会全体之首」（1:15-18）；「因为神本性一切的丰盛都有形有体地居住在基督里面，你们在他里面也得了丰盛」（2:9-10）。信徒不是缺了什么，要到基督以外去补；而是要在已经得着的基督里面「生根建造」（2:7）。

这封信有几条线交织在一起：
- **基督超乎万有：** 他是创造的主，也是新创造的头；万有藉着他造，也藉着他十字架的血与神和好（1:15-20）。
- **基督足够：** 一切智慧知识都在他里面藏着（2:3）；神赦免了我们一切过犯，把攻击我们的字据钉在十字架上，因为基督在那里替我们担当了罪的刑罚（2:13-14）。所以信徒不必被「理学和虚空的妄言」掳去（2:8），也不必让人在饮食、节期上论断自己（2:16）。
- **与基督同死同复活：** 信徒已经与基督一同埋葬、一同复活（2:12；3:1），所以要「求在上面的事」，脱去旧人，穿上新人（3:1-10）。
- **在家里和日常中活出来：** 夫妻、父母儿女、主人仆人，都要「无论做什么」都「奉主耶稣的名」（3:17–4:1）。

保罗在3:11用一句话总结：「惟有基督是包括一切，又住在各人之内。」

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 城市和教会。** 歌罗西在小亚细亚西部的吕卡斯河谷，离老底嘉和希拉坡里不远（4:13）。这个城在早期曾经兴旺，到了保罗的时候已经远远比不上它的两个邻城。十九世纪的英国学者莱特福特说，歌罗西是保罗写信给的教会中最不重要的一个城。可是，新约里一些最崇高的基督论，正是写给这个小城的。罗马史家塔西佗记载老底嘉在主后60年左右遭遇大地震；有学者认为歌罗西也在那段时期受到重创，但具体年份和受灾程度，古代资料说法不一。

**2. 教会是怎样开始的。** 保罗没有亲自到过歌罗西（2:1）。歌罗西人是从以巴弗学到福音的（1:7）。以巴弗是「你们那里的人」（4:12），可能是在保罗住在以弗所的那两年多里信了主，然后把福音带回自己的家乡（参徒19:10，「叫一切住在亚细亚的……都听见主的道」）。这是推断，但很合理。后来以巴弗来到保罗那里，报告了歌罗西人的信心和爱心（1:8），大概也报告了那种新教导带来的困扰。保罗就写了这封信，由推基古和阿尼西谋送去（4:7-9）。

**3. 作者。** 书信开头写着「奉神旨意，作基督耶稣使徒的保罗和兄弟提摩太」（1:1），结尾又说「我－保罗亲笔问你们安」（4:18）。初期教会一致接受这是保罗的书信，今天许多学者（如弗雷德里克·布鲁斯、道格拉斯·穆尔、汤姆·赖特）也这样认为。另有一些学者（如爱德华·洛泽）认为这是保罗去世后，他的门徒用他的名义写的。他们的理由主要有三点：句子长而繁复，和保罗其他书信的文风不同；基督论更宏大，特别是论到基督和万有的关系；而且信徒「已经」与基督一同复活（2:12；3:1），似乎比保罗其他书信更强调「已经」。詹姆斯·邓恩提出一个居中的看法：这封信也许是提摩太在保罗还活着的时候、经保罗同意写成的。
- **主张保罗所写的学者回应说：** 文风会随着题目和代笔的人而改变；这样崇高的基督论，在腓2:6-11和林前8:6里已经可以看到；罗马书也说，信徒如今就要「一举一动有新生的样式」，「向神……当看自己是活的」（罗6:4、11），虽然它把身体的复活留在将来；歌罗西书更强调「已经」，是因为要回应那种「还不够」的教导。
- **歌罗西书和腓利门书：** 这两封信提到的人名几乎完全相同（亚里达古、马可、以巴弗、路加、底马、亚基布；参门23-24；门2），阿尼西谋也同时出现（4:9；门10）。把这样一封信说成后人托名所写，很难解释这么多细节为什么会和腓利门书吻合。这套研读按书信自己的说法（1:1；4:18），以保罗为作者。

**4. 写作地点和时间。** 保罗写信时正在坐监（4:3、10、18）。传统认为是在罗马的监禁（徒28:16、30），大约在主后60-62年，许多学者（如道格拉斯·穆尔）仍持这个看法。也有学者（如汤姆·赖特）认为是在以弗所，大约在主后50年代中期，理由是以弗所离歌罗西只有约两百公里，阿尼西谋去那里比去罗马容易，保罗也在腓利门书里请腓利门给他预备住处（门22）；不过使徒行传没有记载保罗在以弗所坐监，这是推断出来的（参林后11:23）。还有少数学者（如博·赖克）认为是在该撒利亚（徒23:35；24:27）。这几种看法都有一定的理由，问题没有定论。

**5. 和以弗所书的关系。** 歌罗西书和以弗所书有许多相似的句子，例如论到推基古的话几乎一字不差（4:7-8；参弗6:21-22），讲家庭的部分也很接近（3:18–4:1；参弗5:22–6:9）。许多人认为这两封信是在同一段时期写成，由推基古一同带去。4:16提到「从老底嘉来的书信」，有人认为是一封已经失传的信，也有人认为就是以弗所书（它可能是一封在亚细亚各教会传阅的信）。

**6. 那种「理学」是什么？** 保罗没有把那种教导的内容完整地写出来，我们只能从他的回应里拼凑。信中提到：饮食、节期、月朔、安息日（2:16）；「故意谦虚和敬拜天使」，「拘泥在所见过的」（2:18）；「不可拿、不可尝、不可摸」的规条（2:20-21）；「世上的小学」（2:8、20）；也许还有割礼（2:11）。学者的看法大致有三种：
- **犹太教的神秘主义和禁欲操练：** 有人（如弗雷德·弗朗西斯）认为这是一种犹太人的属灵操练，靠禁食和守节期预备自己，好在异象中升到天上，与天使一同敬拜（「敬拜天使」也可以理解为参与天使所献上的敬拜）。
- **希腊哲学的影响：** 有人（如爱德华·施魏策尔）认为它带着希腊哲学的色彩，例如新毕达哥拉斯派那种追求脱离物质、苦待身体的学说。
- **混合的民间宗教：** 也有人（如克林顿·阿诺德）认为它把当地弗吕家的民间信仰、犹太教的成分和求天使保护的巫术混在一起，人求告天使或灵界的势力，好得着保护。

也有学者（如詹姆斯·邓恩）认为它纯粹是犹太教的教导。这些看法不必互相排斥。可以确定的是：这种教导不照着基督（2:8），并且暗示信徒还不够「丰盛」。保罗的回答不是逐条辩论，而是把基督高举出来。

## 钥匙经文：2:9-10

**1. 「神本性一切的丰盛」「有形有体地」住在基督里。** 不是一部分神性，也不是神的能力暂时住在他身上：基督是完完全全的神，又真是成了肉身的人。这不是说有两位神：父、子、圣灵是三个位格，每一位都是完全的神，却只有一位神。（详见第二章。）

**2. 「你们在他里面也得了丰盛」。** 这话的意思不是信徒也变成了神，或者得着了神自己的本体，而是：信徒在基督里面，在得救和敬虔的事上一无所缺。这是神已经做成的事，不是信徒要努力去达到的目标。

**3. 「他是各样执政掌权者的元首」。** 那些叫人惧怕的灵界势力，都是基督所造的（1:16），又被他在十字架上胜过（2:15）。信徒属于他们的元首，就不必再惧怕它们。

## 全书结构

歌罗西书是一封书信，大致可以这样分：
- **问安、感谢和祷告**（1:1-14）：保罗为歌罗西人的信心、爱心和盼望感谢神，又求神叫他们满心知道神的旨意，行事为人对得起主。
- **基督超乎万有**（1:15-23）：爱子是神的像，是创造的主，是教会的头；神藉着他十字架的血叫万有与自己和好，也叫从前与神隔绝的歌罗西人与神和好。
- **保罗的劳苦**（1:24–2:5）：保罗为教会受苦，传扬那「历世历代所隐藏的奥秘」，就是「基督在你们心里成了有荣耀的盼望」（1:26-27）。
- **在基督里生根，不被掳去**（2:6-23）：信徒在基督里已经得了丰盛，受了割礼，与他同埋葬、同复活；所以不要被人间的遗传、规条和自高的「谦虚」夺去奖赏。
- **新的生命**（3:1-17）：求在上面的事；治死地上的肢体；脱去旧人，穿上新人；彼此包容、饶恕；凡事奉主耶稣的名感谢父神。
- **家庭和工作**（3:18–4:1）：妻子、丈夫、儿女、父亲、仆人、主人，各人都在主面前生活。
- **祷告、见证和问安**（4:2-18）：恒切祷告，向外人有智慧；一长串同工的名字；「你们要记念我的捆锁。愿恩惠常与你们同在」（4:18）。

全信可以分成两大部分：前两章讲基督是谁、他为我们做了什么；后两章讲在基督里的人怎样生活。两部分之间的枢纽是2:6-7：「你们既然接受了主基督耶稣，就当遵他而行，在他里面生根建造。」

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 留意「丰盛」。** 「一切的丰盛在他里面居住」（1:19）、「神本性一切的丰盛」（2:9）、「你们在他里面也得了丰盛」（2:10）。保罗求神叫歌罗西人「满心知道神的旨意」（1:9），他自己也要「把神的道理传得全备」（1:25）。这几处原文用的是同一个词根。歌罗西书一再告诉我们：丰盛在基督里面，不在基督以外。

**2. 留意「在他里面」和「与基督一同」。** 信徒「在爱子里得蒙救赎」（1:13-14），在他里面生根建造（2:7），与他「一同埋葬」「一同复活」（2:12），生命「与基督一同藏在神里面」（3:3）。读的时候，可以把这些短语画出来，看看自己在基督里已经得着了什么。

**3. 留意「上面」和「地上」。** 「你们要思念上面的事，不要思念地上的事」（3:2）。这不是叫人轻看身体和日常生活；3:18–4:1讲的都是家里和工作上最平常的事。「上面的事」是指基督坐在神右边所掌管的一切，信徒要从那里出发来过地上的日子。

**4. 读的时候想想今天的「理学」。** 今天的教会里，也会有人说：信了耶稣还要加上一点什么，才算真属灵。也许是一套更严的饮食和节期的规条，也许是某种特别的经历或异象，也许是一种「秘诀」或操练方法，也许是在信耶稣之外，还偷偷戴着护身符、求签问卦，好多一层保险。歌罗西书没有轻看操练和敬虔，却一再问：这是照着基督，还是在基督以外另找丰盛？

**5. 和腓利门书一起读。** 送这封信的阿尼西谋，就是腓利门书里那个奴仆（4:9；门10-16）。传统认为他是逃走的；也有人认为他是特意去找保罗，请保罗为他向主人说情。读3:11「为奴的、自主的」和4:1论主人的话时，可以想到他和腓利门在同一个聚会里坐在一起的情景。

## 默想

**默想：** 「正如你们从我们所亲爱、一同作仆人的以巴弗所学的」（1:7）。歌罗西人信主，不是因为一位著名的使徒来布道，而是因为一个本地人以巴弗把福音带了回来。后来他离开家乡，到了保罗那里，仍然「在祷告之间，常为你们竭力地祈求」（4:12）。「竭力」这个词，和保罗说自己为教会「尽心竭力」（1:29；2:1）是同一个词根，原意是在赛场上用尽全力地拼搏。我是从谁那里第一次听到福音的？也许是一位同学、一位邻居、一位亲戚，名字不会被任何人记得。也许他后来自己也软弱了，甚至离开了教会；可是他带给我的福音不是他的，是基督的，所以不会因为他跌倒就失效。今天，有没有人一直在为我竭力地祈求？也许是一位年长的姊妹，也许是远方的父母，也许有我根本不知道的人。如果我想不起有谁在为我祷告，我也不是孤单的：「有基督耶稣已经死了，而且从死里复活，现今在神的右边，也替我们祈求」（罗8:34）。他「是长远活着，替他们祈求」（来7:25）。今天，我能不能为那个把福音带给我的人感谢神？然后安静下来，想一想：那位比以巴弗更可靠地为我祈求的基督，此刻正在父的右边记念我的名字。我得着福音不是靠自己，持守信心也不是只靠自己；我在他里面，已经得了丰盛。

## What This Book Is About

Colossians is Paul's letter to a church he had never seen face to face (2:1). The church was in an unremarkable small town, probably not large, and it faced a teaching that sounded very spiritual and very wise. That teaching did not tell people to leave Christ. It only hinted that Christ alone was not enough: you also needed deeper experiences, stricter rules and higher knowledge before you could be "complete."

Paul's answer runs through the whole letter: Christ is already everything. "He is the image of the invisible God… by him all things were created… he is the head of the body, the church" (1:15–18); "For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, and you have been filled in him" (2:9–10). Believers are not lacking something that they must go outside Christ to supply. They are to be "rooted and built up in him" (2:7), the Christ they have already received.

Several threads run through the letter:
- **Christ above all:** he is the Lord of creation and the head of the new creation; all things were made through him, and through the blood of his cross all things are reconciled to God (1:15–20).
- **Christ is enough:** all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden in him (2:3); God forgave all our trespasses and nailed the record of debt to the cross, where Christ bore sin's penalty in our place (2:13–14). So believers need not be taken captive "by philosophy and empty deceit" (2:8), and need not let anyone pass judgment on them over food or festivals (2:16).
- **Died and raised with Christ:** believers have been buried and raised with Christ (2:12; 3:1), so they are to "seek the things that are above," putting off the old self and putting on the new (3:1–10).
- **Lived out at home and in daily life:** wives and husbands, children and parents, slaves and masters are to do "whatever you do… in the name of the Lord Jesus" (3:17–4:1).

Paul sums it up in a single line in 3:11: "Christ is all, and in all."

## Author and Setting

**1. The city and the church.** Colossae lay in the Lycus valley in the west of Asia Minor, not far from Laodicea and Hierapolis (4:13). The town had once prospered, but by Paul's day it was far behind its two neighbors. The nineteenth-century English scholar J. B. Lightfoot called Colossae the least important town to which Paul ever wrote a letter. Yet some of the most exalted teaching about Christ in the New Testament was written to this small town. The Roman historian Tacitus records a great earthquake at Laodicea around AD 60. Some scholars think Colossae was badly damaged in the same period, but ancient sources differ on the exact year and how much damage was done.

**2. How the church began.** Paul had never visited Colossae (2:1). The Colossians learned the gospel from Epaphras (1:7), who was "one of you" (4:12). He may have come to faith during Paul's two-plus years in Ephesus and then carried the gospel back to his hometown (see Acts 19:10, "all the residents of Asia heard the word of the Lord"). This is an inference, but a reasonable one. Later Epaphras came to Paul and reported the Colossians' faith and love (1:8), and probably also the trouble the new teaching was causing. So Paul wrote this letter and sent it with Tychicus and Onesimus (4:7–9).

**3. Author.** The letter opens, "Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by the will of God, and Timothy our brother" (1:1), and closes, "I, Paul, write this greeting with my own hand" (4:18). The early church received it unanimously as Paul's letter, and many scholars today (such as F. F. Bruce, Douglas Moo and N. T. Wright) agree. Other scholars (such as Eduard Lohse) think a disciple of Paul wrote it in his name after his death. They give three main reasons: the sentences are long and elaborate, unlike the style of Paul's other letters; the teaching about Christ is grander, especially about Christ's relation to all things; and believers have "already" been raised with Christ (2:12; 3:1), which seems to stress the "already" more than Paul's other letters do. James Dunn proposed a middle view: Timothy may have written the letter while Paul was still alive, with Paul's approval.
- **Those who hold that Paul wrote it reply:** style changes with the subject and with the secretary who writes it down; the high view of Christ is already present in Phil 2:6–11 and 1 Cor 8:6; Romans already calls believers to "walk in newness of life" and to be "alive to God" now (Rom 6:4, 11), though it keeps the bodily resurrection future; Colossians presses the "already" because it is answering a "not yet enough" teaching.
- **Colossians and Philemon:** the two letters name almost exactly the same people (Aristarchus, Mark, Epaphras, Luke, Demas, Archippus; see Phlm 23–24; Phlm 2), and Onesimus appears in both (4:9; Phlm 10). If Colossians were written later under Paul's name, it would be hard to explain why so many details fit Philemon. This study, following the letter's own claim (1:1; 4:18), takes Paul as the author.

**4. Place and date.** Paul wrote while in prison (4:3, 10, 18). Tradition places the letter in his Roman imprisonment (Acts 28:16, 30), around AD 60–62, and many scholars (such as Douglas Moo) still hold this view. Others (such as N. T. Wright) suggest Ephesus in the mid-50s: Ephesus is only about 120 miles (some 190 km) from Colossae, Onesimus could reach it more easily than Rome, and in Philemon Paul asks for a guest room to be prepared for him (Phlm 22). Acts, however, records no imprisonment in Ephesus; it is inferred (see 2 Cor 11:23). A few scholars (such as Bo Reicke) suggest Caesarea (Acts 23:35; 24:27). Each view has some support, and the question remains open.

**5. Relation to Ephesians.** Colossians and Ephesians share many similar sentences. What they say about Tychicus is almost word for word the same (4:7–8; see Eph 6:21–22), and the sections on the household are very close (3:18–4:1; see Eph 5:22–6:9). Many think the two letters were written in the same period and carried together by Tychicus. "The letter from Laodicea" in 4:16 may be a letter now lost, or it may be Ephesians itself (which may have been a letter circulated among the churches of Asia).

**6. What was the "philosophy"?** Paul never sets out the teaching in full; we can only piece it together from his answer. The letter mentions food and drink, festivals, new moons and Sabbaths (2:16); "asceticism and worship of angels," "going on in detail about visions" (2:18); rules such as "Do not handle, Do not taste, Do not touch" (2:21); "the elemental spirits of the world" (2:8, 20); and perhaps circumcision (2:11). Scholars' views fall roughly into three groups:
- **Jewish mysticism and ascetic practice:** some (e.g. Fred Francis) think it was a Jewish spiritual discipline that used fasting and the keeping of holy days to prepare for visionary ascent to heaven, to join the angels in worship ("worship of angels" can also be read as "the worship the angels offer").
- **Greek philosophy:** some (e.g. Eduard Schweizer) see in it the color of Greek philosophy, such as the Neo-Pythagorean teaching that sought escape from the material world and harsh treatment of the body.
- **A mixed folk religion:** others (e.g. Clinton Arnold) think it blended local Phrygian folk belief, Jewish elements and magic that called on angels for protection, so that people invoked angels or spiritual powers to keep themselves safe.

Some (e.g. James Dunn) think it was a purely Jewish teaching. These views need not exclude one another. What is clear is that this teaching was "not according to Christ" (2:8) and implied that believers were not yet "filled." Paul does not answer it point by point. He lifts up Christ.

## Key Verse: 2:9–10

**1. "The whole fullness of deity dwells bodily" in Christ.** Not part of deity, nor God's power resting on him for a time: Christ is fully God, and truly became man in the flesh. This does not mean there are two gods: Father, Son and Holy Spirit are three persons, each fully God, and there is only one God. (See chapter 2 for more.)

**2. "You have been filled in him."** This does not mean believers become God or receive God's own being. It means that in Christ believers lack nothing for salvation and godliness. This is something God has already done, not a goal believers must strive to reach.

**3. "The head of all rule and authority."** The spiritual powers people feared were created by Christ (1:16) and defeated by him at the cross (2:15). Believers belong to their head, so they need not fear them any longer.

## The Shape of the Book

Colossians is a letter, and it can be roughly divided like this:
- **Greeting, thanksgiving and prayer** (1:1–14): Paul thanks God for the Colossians' faith, love and hope, and prays that they may be filled with the knowledge of God's will and walk in a manner worthy of the Lord.
- **Christ above all** (1:15–23): the Son is the image of God, the Lord of creation and the head of the church; through the blood of his cross God reconciles all things to himself, including the Colossians, who were once alienated.
- **Paul's labor** (1:24–2:5): Paul suffers for the church and proclaims "the mystery hidden for ages and generations," which is "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (1:26–27).
- **Rooted in Christ, not taken captive** (2:6–23): in Christ believers have been filled, circumcised, buried and raised with him, so they must not let human traditions, rules and puffed-up "humility" disqualify them.
- **The new life** (3:1–17): seek the things above; put to death what is earthly; put off the old self and put on the new; bear with and forgive one another; do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father.
- **Home and work** (3:18–4:1): wives, husbands, children, fathers, slaves and masters all live before the Lord.
- **Prayer, witness and greetings** (4:2–18): continue steadfastly in prayer, walk in wisdom toward outsiders; a long list of fellow workers; "Remember my chains. Grace be with you" (4:18).

The letter falls into two main parts: the first two chapters tell who Christ is and what he has done for us, and the last two tell how those in Christ are to live. The hinge between them is 2:6–7: "Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him, rooted and built up in him."

## How to Read This Book

**1. Watch for "fullness."** "In him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell" (1:19); "the whole fullness of deity" (2:9); "you have been filled in him" (2:10). Paul prays that the Colossians "may be filled with the knowledge of his will" (1:9), and his own task is "to make the word of God fully known" (1:25). In Greek these all come from the same root. Colossians tells us again and again that fullness is in Christ, not outside him.

**2. Watch for "in him" and "with Christ."** Believers have redemption in the beloved Son (1:13–14), are rooted and built up in him (2:7), were "buried with him" and "raised with him" (2:12), and their life "is hidden with Christ in God" (3:3). As you read, you might underline these phrases and see what you already have in Christ.

**3. Watch for "above" and "on earth."** "Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth" (3:2). This does not tell us to despise the body or ordinary life; 3:18–4:1 is all about the most ordinary things at home and at work. "The things above" are everything Christ rules as he sits at God's right hand, and believers are to live their days on earth from there.

**4. Think about today's "philosophy."** In today's church, too, some say that after believing in Jesus you must add something more before you are truly spiritual. It may be a stricter set of rules about food and holy days; it may be a special experience or vision; it may be a "secret" or a technique of practice; or it may be quietly keeping an amulet or consulting fortune-telling sticks on the side, as extra insurance alongside faith in Jesus. Colossians does not despise spiritual discipline or godliness, but it keeps asking: is this according to Christ, or is it seeking fullness somewhere outside him?

**5. Read it with Philemon.** Onesimus, who carried this letter, is the slave of the letter to Philemon (4:9; Phlm 10–16). He is traditionally understood to have run away; some think he went to Paul to ask him to plead his case. When you read "slave, free" in 3:11 and Paul's word to masters in 4:1, you can picture Onesimus and Philemon sitting in the same meeting.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Just as you learned it from Epaphras our beloved fellow servant" (1:7). The Colossians did not believe because a famous apostle came to preach, but because a local man, Epaphras, brought the gospel home. Later he left his hometown to be with Paul, and he was still "always struggling on your behalf in his prayers" (4:12). The word "struggling" shares its root with Paul's own "struggling" for the church (1:29; 2:1); it first meant straining every muscle in a contest. From whom did I first hear the gospel? Perhaps a classmate, a neighbor or a relative whose name no one else will remember. Perhaps that person later grew weak, or even left the church; yet the gospel they brought me was not theirs but Christ's, so it does not lose its power because they fell. Is anyone struggling in prayer for me today? Perhaps an older sister in the church, parents far away, or people I know nothing about. And if I cannot think of anyone praying for me, I am still not alone: "Christ Jesus is the one who died—more than that, who was raised—who is at the right hand of God, who indeed is interceding for us" (Rom 8:34). "He always lives to make intercession for them" (Heb 7:25). Today, can I thank God for the person who brought me the gospel? Then can I be still and remember that Christ, who intercedes for me more surely than Epaphras ever could, is at the Father's right hand remembering my name right now? I did not receive the gospel by myself, and I do not hold on to faith by myself alone; in him I have already been filled.
