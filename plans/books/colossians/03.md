## 本章脉络

第二章揭露了歌罗西那些「不可拿、不可尝、不可摸」的规条（2:20-23），第三章就正面说明：真正的圣洁生活是从哪里来的。本章可以分为五段：**与基督一同复活**（1-4节）；**治死与脱去**（5-11节）；**穿上基督的样式**（12-17节）；**家中的关系**（18-21节）；**仆人与主人**（3:22-4:1）。4:1和上文是同一段，所以放在本章一起读。

全章的次序很重要：先说「你们已经……」，再说「所以要……」。先有神在基督里为我们做成的事，然后才有我们的顺服。本章最常出现的名字是「基督」和「主」：基督坐在神的右边，基督是我们的生命，基督是包括一切，基督的平安，基督的道理，事奉主基督。

## 深读 3:1-4 求在上面的事

**1. 「你们若真与基督一同复活」。** 「所以，你们若真与基督一同复活，就当求在上面的事；那里有基督坐在神的右边」（3:1）。这里的「若」不是怀疑，而是「既然」的意思：保罗在2:12已经说过，「你们既受洗与他一同埋葬，也就在此与他一同复活」。信徒与基督联合，所以基督的死就是我们的死，基督的复活就是我们的复活（参罗6:4-11）。保罗先说我们是谁，再说我们该怎样活。

**2. 那里有基督。** 「上面的事」不是指离开生活、只想天堂的玄想，而是指基督所在的地方，和他所看重的事。「坐在神的右边」引自诗篇第一百一十篇第1节，表示基督已经得胜，正在掌权。歌罗西的假教师要人靠规条、节期和天使的异象去「往上爬」（2:16-18）；保罗却说，不必爬，因为你们已经在基督里，而基督已经在上面了。

**3. 思念上面的事。** 「你们要思念上面的事，不要思念地上的事」（3:2）。「思念」是指一个人心思的方向，他常常想什么、看重什么、追求什么。「地上的事」不是说吃饭、工作、成家这些事本身不好；下面保罗就要谈家庭和工作。「地上的事」是指那些属于旧人、与基督无关的心思和欲望（3:5）。

**4. 已经死了，生命藏着。** 「因为你们已经死了，你们的生命与基督一同藏在神里面」（3:3）。「已经死了」是指那个属于旧世界的我已经与基督同死了（2:20）。「藏」是隐藏，现在还看不出来；而藏在神里面，也就是被安稳地保守着（参2:3）。我们真正的生命不在别人的评价里，也不在自己的成就里，而是在基督里，并且「在神里面」，没有人能夺走（参约10:28-29）。

**5. 他显现的时候。** 「基督是我们的生命，他显现的时候，你们也要与他一同显现在荣耀里」（3:4）。保罗不只说基督「给」我们生命，而是说基督「是」我们的生命。现在这生命是藏着的，将来基督再来的时候，神要把他在基督里为我们所做的，完全显明出来（参约一3:2；罗8:18-19）。

> 要点：信徒已经与基督同死、同复活，真正的生命与基督一同藏在神里面。所以我们思念上面的事，不是要逃离地上的生活，而是因为我们的生命就在那位坐在神右边的基督那里。

## 深读 3:5-11 治死与脱去

**1. 「所以，要治死」。** 「所以，要治死你们在地上的肢体，就如淫乱、污秽、邪情、恶欲，和贪婪（贪婪就与拜偶像一样）」（3:5）。注意开头的「所以」：因为你们已经死了（3:3），所以要治死。这听起来好像矛盾，其实不然：神已经使我们与基督一同死了，旧人确实已经死了（罗6:6），我们现在就照着这个事实去活，不再顺从那些属于旧人的欲望（参罗6:11）。清教徒约翰·欧文在《论治死罪》里说：「你若不天天治死罪，罪就要治死你。」但他也强调，治死罪是靠圣灵（罗8:13），仰望基督的十字架；我们治死罪，是因为已经被神接纳，而不是为了被神接纳。「在地上的肢体」不是叫人伤害自己的身体；保罗刚刚说过，「苦待己身」的规条对克制肉体的情欲毫无功效（2:23）。他说的是在我们里面那些还属于地上的欲望和行为。

**2. 贪婪就是拜偶像。** 保罗列出的五样罪，前四样多半和性方面的罪有关，最后一样是贪婪。他特别说「贪婪就与拜偶像一样」（参太6:24）。拜偶像的本质，就是把神以外的东西当作安全、满足和意义的来源。在一个常常说「有钱才有安全感」的文化里，我们可以一面在教会里事奉，一面心里真正倚靠的却是存款和房子。贪婪很少被当作罪来认，所以更需要被点出来。

**3. 神的忿怒。** 「因这些事，神的忿怒必临到那悖逆之子」（3:6）。（「那悖逆之子」这几个字，有几份很早的抄本没有，但大多数抄本都有；和合本有，有些英文译本没有。）保罗提到忿怒，不是要叫信徒活在惧怕中，而是要我们看清罪的严重：基督已经为这些罪受了忿怒，我们就不能再把它们当作小事；一个人若安心继续活在这些罪中，就不可自欺，以为自己属于基督（参弗5:5-6；林前6:9-11）。「当你们在这些事中活着的时候，也曾这样行过」（3:7）：「曾」字说明，那是从前的事，不是现在的身份。

**4. 口中的罪。** 「但现在你们要弃绝这一切的事，以及恼恨、忿怒、恶毒、毁谤，并口中污秽的言语。不要彼此说谎」（3:8-9）。第二个清单多半和话语有关。教会里的罪常常不是淫乱，而是闲话、讽刺、背后批评和不诚实。这些罪拆毁的，正是基督的身体。

**5. 已经脱去旧人，穿上新人。** 「因你们已经脱去旧人和旧人的行为，穿上了新人。这新人在知识上渐渐更新，正如造他主的形象」（3:9-10）。这里的「脱去」「穿上」原文是过去时态的分词，许多解经家（如约翰·默里）认为是说明原因，指信徒归信、与基督联合时已经脱去旧人、穿上新人（和合本译作「已经」）；也有人因3:12「就要存」（原文是穿上）的命令和弗4:22-24，认为这里也带着劝勉的意思。无论哪一种，新人都在「渐渐更新」：新人已经穿上，但还在成长。神起初照着自己的形象造人（创1:27），罪使这形象受了亏损；而基督就是「那不能看见之神的像」（1:15），神要使我们「效法他儿子的模样」（罗8:29）。

**6. 惟有基督是包括一切。** 「在此并不分希腊人、犹太人，受割礼的、未受割礼的，化外人、西古提人，为奴的、自主的，惟有基督是包括一切，又住在各人之内」（3:11）。「化外人」是希腊人对不讲希腊话的人的称呼；「西古提人」住在黑海北边，在当时被看作最野蛮的民族。在新人里面，这些分别不再决定一个人的价值和地位（参加3:28）。今天的教会里，也可能有看不起人的分别：早来的移民和新来的移民，学历高的和学历低的。这些不同不会消失，但在基督里，没有一样可以叫人高人一等，因为「基督是包括一切」。

> 要点：治死罪和脱去旧人，不是叫人靠自己的努力变好，而是照着神在基督里已经做成的事去活。新人正在照着基督的形象更新，在新人里面，惟有基督是一切。

## 深读 3:12-17 穿上基督的样式

**1. 先是身份，再是衣服。** 「所以，你们既是神的选民，圣洁蒙爱的人，就要存怜悯、恩慈、谦虚、温柔、忍耐的心」（3:12）。在保罗吩咐我们「穿上」什么之前，他先说我们是谁：「神的选民，圣洁蒙爱的人」。这三个称呼原本是用在以色列身上的（参申7:6-8），现在用在歌罗西这些外邦信徒身上。我们不是为了被拣选、被爱而去存怜悯的心；我们是因为已经蒙拣选、蒙爱，才能这样待人。这五样美德，每一样都可以在耶稣身上看见（参太11:29）。

**2. 主怎样饶恕了你们。** 「倘若这人与那人有嫌隙，总要彼此包容，彼此饶恕；主怎样饶恕了你们，你们也要怎样饶恕人」（3:13）。保罗很实际：教会里一定会有「嫌隙」。他没有说「没关系，忘了吧」，而是把饶恕建立在主的饶恕上（参弗4:32；太18:33）。主饶恕我们，不是因为我们的罪不严重，而是因为他自己担当了这罪。我们饶恕人，也不是说那件事不严重，而是把讨债的权利交给神（罗12:19）。

**3. 饶恕不等于掩盖伤害。** 这节经文常常被误用来叫受伤的人沉默，所以必须说清楚。如果伤害牵涉到家暴、性侵、虐待儿童或其他犯罪，向警察或法院举报是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必和仍然不安全的人和好；饶恕一个人，不等于恢复他在教会里的职分，也不等于让他重新接近受害者或孩子；教会更不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而劝人忍耐、替伤害人的人遮掩。饶恕是在神面前放下报复，不是叫受害的人再一次受害。

**4. 爱心和平安。** 「在这一切之外，要存着爱心，爱心就是联络全德的」（3:14）。前面的美德像一件件衣服，爱心就像把它们束在一起的带子。「又要叫基督的平安在你们心里作主」（3:15）：「作主」这个词，许多解经家认为原本是指比赛中的裁判。当教会里意见不合的时候，最后作决定的，不是谁的声音大，而是基督所赐的平安。这平安也是肢体之间的和睦，因为「你们也为此蒙召，归为一体」。

**5. 基督的道理丰丰富富地存在心里。** 「当用各样的智慧，把基督的道理丰丰富富地存在心里，用诗章、颂词、灵歌，彼此教导，互相劝戒，心被恩感，歌颂神」（3:16）。「基督的道理」可以指基督所说的话，也可以指关于基督的信息；这两层意思在这里分不开。这道理不只要住在每一个人心里，也要住在会众中间，因为下面说的是「彼此」教导。「诗章、颂词、灵歌」很难截然分开，但可以看出教会的诗歌是教导的一部分：我们唱的，就是我们所信的。

**6. 无论做什么。** 「无论做什么，或说话或行事，都要奉主耶稣的名，藉着他感谢父神」（3:17）。这节经文打破了属灵的事和属世的事之间的界限。家里和工作上的平常事，都可以「奉主耶稣的名」来做；这句话也是下面家庭和工作那一段的总纲。3:15「存感谢的心」、3:17「感谢父神」，3:16的「心被恩感」也可以理解为感恩：感恩是穿上新人的人最自然的呼吸。

> 要点：我们是神所拣选、所爱的人，所以能穿上基督的样式，彼此包容、饶恕，像主饶恕我们一样。基督的平安作主，基督的道理住在会众中间，生活的每一件事都可以奉主的名、带着感恩去做。

## 深读 3:18-21 家中的关系

**1. 「在主里面」的家。** 从3:18到4:1，保罗对家中的六种人说话：妻子和丈夫，儿女和父亲，仆人和主人。当时的「治家」规条通常只对一家之主说话；保罗却直接对妻子、儿女和仆人说话，把他们当作在神面前有责任的人，并把每一对关系都放在「主」面前（参弗5:22-6:9）。

**2. 妻子顺服，「这在主里面是相宜的」。** 「你们作妻子的，当顺服自己的丈夫，这在主里面是相宜的」（3:18）。对这节经文，福音派里有不同的理解：
- **互补派**（如韦恩·古德恩）认为，丈夫在家中有带领的责任，妻子在主里自愿顺服；他们常把这个次序连到创造（参创2:18-23；林前11:8-9；提前2:13；弗5:23）。
- **平等派**（如克雷格·基纳）认为，保罗是在当时的社会结构里教导信徒怎样活出见证；「在主里面是相宜的」表示这教导要按主的标准来衡量，原则在于彼此顺服（参弗5:21），而不是永远固定的等级。
无论哪一种理解，顺服都是「在主里面」的：妻子是向主负责，不是向丈夫交出良心；顺服也绝不包括顺从丈夫去犯罪，或忍受虐待。

**3. 丈夫要爱，不可苦待。** 「你们作丈夫的，要爱你们的妻子，不可苦待她们」（3:19）。「爱」是用基督舍己的爱（弗5:25）；「苦待」原文的意思是使人苦毒、对人尖刻。保罗很清楚丈夫常犯的罪：冷言冷语，在别人面前贬低妻子。彼得也说，丈夫要敬重妻子，因为她们是「与你一同承受生命之恩的」（彼前3:7）。一个丈夫若用这段经文来控制、恐吓或伤害妻子，就完全违背了经文的意思。

**4. 安全的提醒。** 如果婚姻中有家暴、性暴力、经济控制或其他虐待，向警察或法院求助是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人可以离开，到安全的地方去，不必为了「顺服」而留在危险中，也不必与仍然不安全的人和好或复合；教会的责任是保护受害者，不是劝她回去忍耐，更不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而替施暴者遮掩。施暴的人即使后来悔改、蒙了饶恕，也不等于他就可以恢复教会里的职分，或重新接近受害者和孩子。丈夫也可能是受害者，同样应当得到保护。

**5. 儿女听从，父亲不要惹儿女的气。** 「你们作儿女的，要凡事听从父母，因为这是主所喜悦的。你们作父亲的，不要惹儿女的气，恐怕他们失了志气」（3:20-21）。「这是主所喜悦的」，原文直译是在主里是可喜悦的，儿女的听从也放在主面前，所以「凡事」不包括听从父母去犯罪，也不包括默默忍受殴打或性侵；孩子若在家中受到伤害，告诉老师、可信的大人或警察是对的（罗13:4）。「父亲」这个词有时也泛指父母（参来11:23），所以作母亲的也要听。保罗对父亲只说一件事：不要惹他们，免得他们「失了志气」，就是灰心、放弃，觉得怎么做都不够好。在一个讲究严父的文化里，许多孩子从来没有听过父亲说「我以你为荣」，只听过「为什么不是第一名」。作父亲的若在这件事上已经失败，福音给他的不是更重的定罪，而是回头的路：天父已经在基督里饶恕了他，他也可以向孩子认错，重新开始。

> 要点：保罗把家中的每一个人都放在主面前：妻子在主里顺服，丈夫像基督一样爱、不可苦待，儿女在主里听从，父亲不可叫儿女灰心。「在主里」既是动机，也是界限，绝不是任何人欺压人的借口。

## 深读 3:22-4:1 仆人与主人

**1. 当时的奴隶制度。** 保罗对仆人说的话，比对其他人都长。当时的奴隶制度和后来建立在拐卖人口之上的种族奴隶制度不完全一样，但仍然是不公义的制度：奴隶在法律上是主人的财产。保罗在这里没有直接呼吁废除奴隶制度，可是他说「若能以自由，就求自由更好」（林前7:21；许多译本，包括和合本，这样理解），又把拐卖人口的人列在罪人当中（提前1:10；参出21:16）。后世有奴隶主拿这段经文为奴隶制度辩护，这是严重的误用。这段经文不是为奴隶制度辩护；今天也不是叫人默默忍受拖欠工资、强迫劳动、人口贩卖，或职场上的欺凌和性骚扰。向劳动部门、警察或法院求助是对的（罗13:4）。

**2. 阿尼西谋在场。** 腓利门的奴隶阿尼西谋离开了主人（是逃跑还是特意来找保罗求情，学者看法不一；见下一章），信主以后要回到他那里去（4:9；门10-16）。这段话在教会里宣读的时候，腓利门和阿尼西谋可能都坐在会众当中。

**3. 凡事听从，却有界限。** 「你们作仆人的，要凡事听从你们肉身的主人」（3:22）：「肉身的」主人是暂时的、有限的权柄，在他之上还有「主」；所以「凡事」和3:20一样，不包括听从人去犯罪（参徒5:29），也不是叫人默默忍受虐待。

**4. 像是给主做的。** 「不要只在眼前事奉，像是讨人喜欢的，总要存心诚实敬畏主。无论做什么，都要从心里做，像是给主做的，不是给人做的」（3:22-23）。今天我们大多数人不是奴隶，但这原则可以用在工作上。「只在眼前事奉」就是做给人看；而信徒知道，自己真正服事的是「主基督」（3:24）。这样，就是一份在人眼中卑微的工作，例如洗碗、送货、清洁、看护，都有了尊贵的意义，因为是为主做的。

**5. 奴隶也得基业。** 「因你们知道从主那里必得着基业为赏赐；你们所事奉的乃是主基督」（3:24）。按罗马法，奴隶通常不能继承产业（除非在同一份遗嘱里被释放）。保罗却说，这些奴隶要从主那里得着「基业」，就是神为他儿女所预备的产业（参1:12）。这是真实的赏赐，却是恩典的赏赐：不是工钱，而是儿女的身份所带来的。

**6. 主并不偏待人。** 「那行不义的必受不义的报应；主并不偏待人」（3:25）。这句话是对谁说的？有人认为是警告仆人，不要以为可以偷懒或欺骗（这节以「因为」接上文，又在对仆人说的话里面；参门18）；也有人认为是安慰受欺压的仆人：欺负他们的主人也逃不过神的审判，因为以弗所书6:9正是对主人说「他并不偏待人」。保罗也许有意两边都包括：在主面前，不论地位高低，谁都不能靠身份免责。

**7. 主人也有一位主。** 「你们作主人的，要公公平平地待仆人，因为知道你们也有一位主在天上」（4:1）。今天作老板、主管、家里请了保姆或工人的人，也要记住：我也是仆人，我也有一位主在天上。这位主就是那位「取了奴仆的形象」（腓2:7），为门徒洗脚的基督（约13:5）。

> 要点：保罗在一个不公义的制度里，把仆人和主人都放在同一位主面前：仆人从心里做工，像是给主做的，并要从主那里得着基业；主人要公平待人，因为自己也是仆人。在基督里，谁都不是别人的工具。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们的生命与基督一同藏在神里面。基督是我们的生命，他显现的时候，你们也要与他一同显现在荣耀里」（3:3-4）。我的生活，有没有一部分是没有人看见的？也许我每天下班后照顾一个有特殊需要的孩子，许多年来没有一个晚上是自己的；也许我在餐馆后厨或工厂的流水线上做了二十年，从来没有人问过我的名字。有时我会想：我的一生，大概不会有人记得。更难承认的是，在这些看不见的日子里，我也常常不耐烦、发怨言，甚至心里苦毒，只是没有人知道。保罗说，我的生命「藏」着，不是丢了，也不是白费了，而是与基督一同被珍藏在神里面。我那些没有人看见的罪，基督已经在十字架上担当了，旧的我已经与他同死（3:3）；我那些没有人看见的日子，也没有一天是在神眼前漏掉的。我的生命不是我做了多少、别人怎样看我，而是基督自己。有一天他要显现，那时我不必再证明什么，因为我要与他「一同」显现在荣耀里。今天，当我又一次在没有人看见的地方做那件平凡的事，或者又一次因为自己的软弱而灰心的时候，我能不能对主说：主啊，我的生命藏在你里面；你看见，你记得，你就是我的生命？那位坐在神右边的基督，不会忘记任何一个属于他的人。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 exposed the "Do not handle, Do not taste, Do not touch" regulations of the false teaching at Colossae (2:20–23). Chapter 3 now shows where a truly holy life comes from. It falls into five parts: **Raised with Christ** (vv. 1–4); **Putting to death and putting off** (vv. 5–11); **Putting on the likeness of Christ** (vv. 12–17); **Relationships at home** (vv. 18–21); **Slaves and masters** (3:22–4:1). Since 4:1 belongs to the same paragraph, we read it with this chapter.

The order of the chapter matters. First Paul says, "you have…", then, "therefore…". What God has done for us in Christ comes first, and our obedience follows. The names that recur most often are "Christ" and "the Lord": Christ seated at God's right hand, Christ who is our life, Christ who is all, the peace of Christ, the word of Christ, serving the Lord Christ.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–4 Seek the Things That Are Above

**1. "If then you have been raised with Christ."** "If then you have been raised with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God" (3:1). The "if" expresses no doubt; it means "since." Paul has already said in 2:12 that you were "buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him." Believers are united to Christ, so his death is our death and his resurrection our resurrection (cf. Rom 6:4–11). Paul first tells us who we are, then how to live.

**2. Where Christ is.** "The things that are above" do not mean otherworldly daydreams detached from life. They mean the place where Christ is and the things he values. "Seated at the right hand of God" echoes Psalm 110:1 and means that Christ has won and now reigns. The false teachers at Colossae wanted people to "climb up" by rules, festivals and visions of angels (2:16–18). Paul says there is no need to climb: you are already in Christ, and Christ is already above.

**3. Set your minds above.** "Set your minds on things that are above, not on things that are on earth" (3:2). To "set the mind" is the direction of a person's thoughts: what he keeps thinking about, values and pursues. "Things that are on earth" does not mean that eating, working or raising a family are bad in themselves; Paul is about to speak of home and work. It means the thoughts and desires that belong to the old self and have nothing to do with Christ (3:5).

**4. Dead, and hidden.** "For you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God" (3:3). The self that belonged to the old world has died with Christ (2:20). "Hidden" means concealed, not yet visible; and hidden *in God*, it is also kept safe (cf. 2:3). Our true life does not lie in others' opinions or our own achievements. It is in Christ, and "in God," where no one can snatch it away (cf. John 10:28–29).

**5. When he appears.** "When Christ who is your life appears, then you also will appear with him in glory" (3:4). Paul does not only say that Christ "gives" us life. He says Christ "is" our life. That life is hidden now; when Christ returns, God will display in full what he has done for us in Christ (cf. 1 John 3:2; Rom 8:18–19).

> The point: Believers have died and been raised with Christ, and their true life is hidden with Christ in God. We set our minds above not to escape life on earth, but because our life is with Christ, seated at God's right hand.

## Going Deeper: 3:5–11 Put to Death, Put Off

**1. "Put to death therefore."** "Put to death therefore what is earthly in you: sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry" (3:5). Notice the "therefore": because you have died (3:3), put to death. That sounds like a contradiction, but it is not. God has joined us to Christ in his death, so the old self really has died (Rom 6:6), and now we live in line with that fact, no longer obeying the desires that belong to the old self (cf. Rom 6:11). The Puritan John Owen, in *Of the Mortification of Sin in Believers*, wrote: "Be killing sin or it will be killing you." Yet he insisted that sin is put to death by the Spirit (Rom 8:13), looking to the cross of Christ; we put sin to death because we have already been accepted by God, not in order to be accepted. "What is earthly in you" (literally "your members that are on earth") is not a call to harm the body. Paul has just said that "severity to the body" is of no value in stopping the indulgence of the flesh (2:23). He means the desires and deeds within us that still belong to the earth.

**2. Covetousness is idolatry.** Of the five sins Paul lists, the first four mostly concern sexual sin; the last is covetousness. He adds that covetousness "is idolatry" (cf. Matt 6:24). Idolatry at its core is treating something other than God as the source of security, satisfaction and meaning. In a culture that often says "money is what makes you feel safe," we can serve in church while what we truly rely on is savings and property. Covetousness is rarely confessed as sin, which is why it needs to be named.

**3. The wrath of God.** "On account of these the wrath of God is coming" (3:6). (The words "upon the sons of disobedience" are absent from a few very early manuscripts but present in most; the CUV includes them, the ESV leaves them out.) Paul mentions wrath not to make believers live in fear, but so that we see how serious sin is: Christ bore wrath for these sins, so we cannot treat them as small. And anyone content to go on living in them must not deceive himself that he belongs to Christ (cf. Eph 5:5–6; 1 Cor 6:9–11). "In these you too once walked" (3:7): "once" marks this as the past, not their present identity.

**4. Sins of the mouth.** "But now you must put them all away: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and obscene talk from your mouth. Do not lie to one another" (3:8–9). The second list is mostly about speech. Sin in the church is often not sexual immorality but gossip, sarcasm, criticism behind someone's back and dishonesty. These are the sins that tear down the body of Christ.

**5. You have put off the old, put on the new.** "Seeing that you have put off the old self with its practices and have put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator" (3:9–10). The verbs are aorist participles. Many interpreters (e.g., John Murray) take them as giving the reason, meaning something done at conversion and union with Christ (ESV "seeing that"; CUV 「已经」). Others, noting the command "put on" in 3:12 and Eph 4:22–24, hear a note of exhortation here too. Either way, the new self "is being renewed": it has been put on, but it is still growing. God first made humankind in his own image (Gen 1:27); sin marred that image; Christ is "the image of the invisible God" (1:15), and God means for us "to be conformed to the image of his Son" (Rom 8:29).

**6. Christ is all.** "Here there is not Greek and Jew, circumcised and uncircumcised, barbarian, Scythian, slave, free; but Christ is all, and in all" (3:11). "Barbarian" was the Greek word for anyone who did not speak Greek; the Scythians lived north of the Black Sea and were thought of as the most savage of peoples. In the new self these distinctions no longer decide a person's worth or standing (cf. Gal 3:28). Churches today can have their own looking-down: earlier immigrants and newcomers, the highly educated and the less educated. These differences do not disappear, but in Christ none of them can make anyone superior, because "Christ is all."

> The point: Putting sin to death and putting off the old self do not mean making ourselves better by our own effort. They mean living out what God has already done in Christ. The new self is being renewed after Christ's image, and in it Christ is everything.

## Going Deeper: 3:12–17 Putting On the Likeness of Christ

**1. Identity first, then clothing.** "Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience" (3:12). Before Paul tells us what to "put on," he says who we are: "God's chosen ones, holy and beloved." These titles once belonged to Israel (cf. Deut 7:6–8); now they are given to these Gentile believers at Colossae. We do not show compassion in order to be chosen and loved. We are able to treat others this way because we already are chosen and loved. Each of these five qualities can be seen in Jesus (cf. Matt 11:29).

**2. As the Lord has forgiven you.** "Bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive" (3:13). Paul is realistic: there will be "complaints" in the church. He does not say, "Never mind, just forget it." He builds forgiveness on the Lord's forgiveness (cf. Eph 4:32; Matt 18:33). The Lord forgives us not because our sin was not serious, but because he himself bore it. When we forgive, we are not saying the wrong was not serious; we are handing the right to repayment over to God (Rom 12:19).

**3. Forgiveness does not cover up harm.** This verse is often misused to silence the wounded, so it must be said plainly. Where harm involves domestic violence, sexual assault, child abuse or other crime, reporting it to the police or courts is right (Rom 13:4). Victims need not stay in danger, nor reconcile with someone who is still unsafe. Forgiving someone does not restore his office in the church or give him access again to victims or children. And the church must never urge victims to endure, or cover for the one who did harm, in order to keep "family shame" from getting out (家丑不可外扬). Forgiveness lets go of revenge before God; it does not make the victim a victim again.

**4. Love and peace.** "And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony" (3:14). If the earlier qualities are garments, love is the belt that holds them together. "And let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts" (3:15). Many interpreters note that the word for "rule" was used of an umpire at the games. When the church disagrees, the final decision belongs not to whoever is loudest but to the peace Christ gives. This peace is also harmony between members, "to which indeed you were called in one body."

**5. The word of Christ dwelling richly.** "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly, teaching and admonishing one another in all wisdom, singing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, with thankfulness in your hearts to God" (3:16). "The word of Christ" may mean the words Christ spoke or the message about Christ; here the two cannot be separated. This word is to dwell not only in each heart but among the congregation, since Paul speaks of teaching "one another." "Psalms and hymns and spiritual songs" are hard to tell apart, but it is clear that the church's singing is part of its teaching: what we sing is what we believe.

**6. Whatever you do.** "And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him" (3:17). This verse breaks down the line between "spiritual" and "secular" things. The ordinary tasks of home and work can all be done "in the name of the Lord Jesus"; the verse also heads the section on home and work that follows. Thanksgiving appears three times in this passage (3:15, 16, 17): it is the natural breathing of those who have put on the new self.

> The point: We are chosen and loved by God, so we can put on the likeness of Christ, bearing with and forgiving one another as the Lord forgave us. Christ's peace rules, Christ's word dwells among the congregation, and every part of life can be done in the Lord's name and with thanksgiving.

## Going Deeper: 3:18–21 Relationships at Home

**1. A home "in the Lord."** From 3:18 to 4:1 Paul addresses six kinds of people in the household: wives and husbands, children and fathers, slaves and masters. Household rules of the day usually spoke only to the head of the house; Paul speaks directly to wives, children and slaves, treating them as people responsible before God, and sets every relationship before "the Lord" (cf. Eph 5:22–6:9).

**2. Wives submit, "as is fitting in the Lord."** "Wives, submit to your husbands, as is fitting in the Lord" (3:18). Evangelicals read this verse in different ways:
- **Complementarians** (e.g., Wayne Grudem) hold that husbands bear a responsibility to lead at home and wives submit willingly in the Lord; they often connect this order to creation (cf. Gen 2:18–23; 1 Cor 11:8–9; 1 Tim 2:13; Eph 5:23).
- **Egalitarians** (e.g., Craig Keener) hold that Paul is teaching believers how to bear witness within the social structures of their day; "as is fitting in the Lord" means the teaching is measured by the Lord's standard, and the principle is mutual submission (cf. Eph 5:21) rather than a permanent hierarchy.
On either reading, submission is "in the Lord": a wife answers to the Lord and does not hand her conscience over to her husband; submission never includes following a husband into sin or enduring abuse.

**3. Husbands love and are not harsh.** "Husbands, love your wives, and do not be harsh with them" (3:19). The love is Christ's self-giving love (Eph 5:25). "Be harsh" means to embitter, to be sharp with someone. Paul knows the sins husbands often commit: cold words, belittling a wife in front of others. Peter too tells husbands to honor their wives, "since they are heirs with you of the grace of life" (1 Pet 3:7). A husband who uses this passage to control, intimidate or harm his wife has completely violated its meaning.

**4. A word on safety.** If a marriage involves domestic violence, sexual violence, financial control or other abuse, seeking help from the police or courts is right (Rom 13:4). The victim may leave and go somewhere safe, and need not stay in danger for the sake of "submission," nor reconcile with someone who is still unsafe. The church's duty is to protect the victim, not to urge her to go back and endure, and never to cover for the abuser in order to keep "family shame" from getting out. Even if an abuser later repents and is forgiven, that does not restore him to office in the church or give him access again to the victim or the children. Husbands can be victims too, and they also deserve protection.

**5. Children obey; fathers do not provoke.** "Children, obey your parents in everything, for this pleases the Lord. Fathers, do not provoke your children, lest they become discouraged" (3:20–21). "This pleases the Lord" is literally "this is pleasing in the Lord": children's obedience is also set before the Lord, so "everything" does not include obeying parents into sin, nor silently enduring beatings or sexual abuse. A child harmed at home is right to tell a teacher, a trusted adult or the police (Rom 13:4). The word "fathers" can sometimes mean parents (cf. Heb 11:23), so mothers should hear this too. To fathers Paul says one thing: do not provoke your children, lest they "become discouraged," lose heart, give up, feel that nothing they do is ever enough. In a culture that prizes the "strict father," many children have never heard their father say, "I'm proud of you," only, "Why weren't you first?" A father who has already failed here is offered by the gospel not heavier condemnation but a way back: the Father has forgiven him in Christ, and he can confess his wrong to his child and begin again.

> The point: Paul sets everyone in the home before the Lord. Wives submit in the Lord; husbands love as Christ loves and are never harsh; children obey in the Lord; fathers must not discourage their children. "In the Lord" is both the motive and the limit, never an excuse for anyone to oppress another.

## Going Deeper: 3:22–4:1 Slaves and Masters

**1. Slavery in that day.** Paul says more to slaves than to anyone else. Slavery then was not identical to the later race-based slavery built on kidnapping, but it was still an unjust system: in law a slave was the master's property. Paul does not directly call here for slavery to be abolished, but he says, "if you can gain your freedom, avail yourself of the opportunity" (1 Cor 7:21, as most translations, including the ESV and CUV, take it), and he lists "enslavers" among sinners (1 Tim 1:10; cf. Exod 21:16). Slaveholders in later centuries used these verses to defend slavery, a grave misuse. This passage is no defense of slavery. Nor does it ask anyone today to endure wage theft, forced labor, trafficking, or bullying and sexual harassment at work; seeking help from labor authorities, the police or the courts is right (Rom 13:4).

**2. Onesimus in the room.** Philemon's slave Onesimus, who had left him (whether as a runaway or to seek Paul's intercession is debated; see the next chapter), was now a believer and returning to him (4:9; Phlm 10–16). When these words were read aloud to the church, Philemon and Onesimus may both have been sitting in the congregation.

**3. Obey in everything, within limits.** "Slaves, obey in everything those who are your earthly masters" (3:22): an "earthly" master (literally "according to the flesh") holds a temporary, limited authority under the Lord. So "everything" here, as in 3:20, never includes obeying into sin (cf. Acts 5:29) or silently enduring abuse.

**4. As for the Lord.** "Not by way of eye-service, as people-pleasers, but with sincerity of heart, fearing the Lord. Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men" (3:22–23). Most of us today are not slaves, but the principle applies to work. "Eye-service" is work done for show; believers know that the one they truly serve is "the Lord Christ" (3:24). So even work that people regard as lowly, such as washing dishes, making deliveries, cleaning or caring for the sick, has dignity, because it is done for the Lord.

**5. Slaves receive the inheritance.** "Knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward. You are serving the Lord Christ" (3:24). Under Roman law, slaves generally could not inherit (unless freed in the same will). Yet Paul says these slaves will receive from the Lord "the inheritance," the portion God has prepared for his children (cf. 1:12). It is a real reward, yet a reward of grace: not a wage, but what comes with being a child.

**6. No partiality.** "For the wrongdoer will be paid back for the wrong he has done, and there is no partiality" (3:25). To whom is this said? Some take it as a warning to slaves not to think they can shirk or cheat (it is joined to v. 24 by "for" and stands in the slaves' paragraph; cf. Phlm 18); others as comfort to oppressed slaves, since in Eph 6:9 "no partiality" is said directly to masters, and masters who mistreat slaves will not escape God's judgment. Paul may intend both: before the Lord no one, high or low, can rely on status to escape accountability.

**7. Masters have a Master.** "Masters, treat your slaves justly and fairly, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven" (4:1). Bosses and managers today, and anyone who employs a nanny or a worker at home, should remember: I am a servant too; I too have a Master in heaven. That Master is the Christ who took "the form of a servant" (Phil 2:7) and washed his disciples' feet (John 13:5).

> The point: Within an unjust system Paul sets slaves and masters before the same Lord. Slaves work heartily, as for the Lord, and will receive the inheritance from him; masters treat others fairly, because they too are servants. In Christ no one is merely a tool for someone else.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ who is your life appears, then you also will appear with him in glory" (3:3–4). Is there a part of my life that no one sees? Perhaps every evening after work I care for a child with special needs, and for years not one evening has been my own. Perhaps I have worked for twenty years in a restaurant kitchen or on a factory line, and no one has ever asked my name. Sometimes I think: no one will remember my life. Harder to admit, in these unseen days I am often impatient, I complain, my heart even turns bitter, and no one knows. Paul says my life is "hidden": not lost, not wasted, but treasured with Christ in God. The sins no one saw, Christ has already borne on the cross, and the old me has died with him (3:3). And the days no one saw, not one of them has slipped past God's eyes. My life is not how much I accomplish or what others think of me; my life is Christ himself. One day he will appear, and then I will have nothing left to prove, because I will appear "with him" in glory. Today, when once again I do that ordinary thing where no one sees, or once again lose heart over my own weakness, can I say to the Lord: Lord, my life is hidden in you; you see, you remember, you are my life? The Christ seated at the right hand of God will not forget a single one who belongs to him.
