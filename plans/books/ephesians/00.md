## 这卷书在讲什么

以弗所书一开口就是赞美：「愿颂赞归与我们主耶稣基督的父神！他在基督里曾赐给我们天上各样属灵的福气」（1:3）。加拉太书是一封急信，要把走偏的教会拉回来；以弗所书却没有处理某一个具体的争端，而是把镜头拉远，让读者看见神在永恒里的计划：神「从创立世界以前」就在基督里拣选了我们（1:4），要「使天上、地上、一切所有的都在基督里面同归于一」（1:10）。

这个计划在地上最清楚的样子，就是教会。从前彼此敌对的犹太人和外邦人，如今在基督里「合而为一」（2:14），「同为后嗣，同为一体，同蒙应许」（3:6）。所以，以弗所书前半部讲神在基督里为我们成就了什么，后半部讲这样蒙恩的人当怎样「行事为人」（4:1）：在教会里彼此相爱，在家庭和工作中活出新人的样式，并且靠着主的大能，站立得住（6:10-13）。

历代许多信徒把以弗所书看作保罗书信中最丰富的一卷。它不是一本难懂的教义手册，而是一首由祷告、赞美和劝勉交织成的长诗。

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 作者。** 书信两次自称是保罗写的（1:1；3:1）。初期教会一致接受这是保罗的书信。近代有不少学者（例如许多批判学派的学者）认为是保罗的门徒托他的名写的，理由是用词和文风与其他书信不同，又与歌罗西书非常相似。也有许多学者（如哈罗德·霍纳、彼得·奥白恩、克林顿·阿诺德、弗兰克·锡尔曼）详细回应了这些理由，认为风格的差异可以从题目、体裁和代笔人来解释，作者仍是保罗。本书按书信自己的说法，把作者看作保罗。

**2. 「在以弗所」。** 1:1「写信给在以弗所的圣徒」中的「在以弗所」几个字，在几份很早、很重要的抄本（如P46，以及西乃抄本、梵蒂冈抄本原来的抄写）里没有。书信里也几乎没有个人问候，这有点奇怪，因为保罗在以弗所住了将近三年（徒20:31）。所以许多学者认为，这原是一封传阅的书信，写给以弗所和附近一带的众教会，以弗所是其中最主要的一个。

**3. 写作时间与地点。** 保罗写这封信的时候正在坐监，他称自己是「为主被囚的」（4:1），是「带锁链的使者」（6:20）。传统认为这是在罗马的监禁（徒28:30-31），大约在主后60-62年。推基古带着这封信（6:21），也带着歌罗西书（西4:7-8），所以两封信大概是同时写的。也有学者认为是在该撒利亚的监禁（徒24:27），或在以弗所的一次监禁时写的。

**4. 以弗所城。** 以弗所是罗马亚细亚省最重要的城市之一，有亚底米大庙（徒19:27），也是行邪术、拜偶像的中心。保罗在那里传道的时候，许多信的人把邪术的书拿来焚烧，书价约合五万块钱（徒19:19）。难怪以弗所书特别强调：基督「远超过一切执政的、掌权的、有能的、主治的」（1:21），而信徒的争战「并不是与属血气的争战」（6:12）。

## 钥匙经文：1:3

**1. 「愿颂赞归与……父神」。** 这卷书从赞美开始。信徒的生命不是从「我该做什么」开始，而是从「神已经做了什么」开始。

**2. 「他……曾赐给我们」。** 这是已经完成的事。神不是说「如果你够好，将来会赐给你」，而是说他已经赐下了。我们不是在神面前讨饭的人，而是已经承受丰富产业的儿女。

**3. 「在基督里」。** 「在基督里」「在他里面」这类说法在这卷书中反复出现。一切的福气都在基督里，离开基督就没有一样；在基督里却一样也不缺。

**4. 「天上各样属灵的福气」。** 「在天上」这个说法在以弗所书出现了五次（1:3、20；2:6；3:10；6:12）。它指的不只是将来的天堂，而是基督如今掌权的那个属灵的领域。信徒虽然活在地上，却已经「一同坐在天上」（2:6）。

## 全书结构

以弗所书可以大致分成两大部分：
- **神在基督里为我们成就的**（1–3章）：父神的拣选，圣子的救赎，圣灵的印记（1:3-14）；保罗求神开启信徒的眼睛（1:15-23）；我们从死里被救活，「得救是本乎恩，也因着信」（2:1-10）；犹太人与外邦人在基督里成为一个新人，同建成神的殿（2:11-22）；福音的奥秘向外邦人显明（3:1-13）；保罗再次祷告，愿信徒知道基督测不透的爱（3:14-21）。
- **蒙恩的人当怎样行事为人**（4–6章）：保守圣灵所赐合而为一的心，各尽其职，建立基督的身体（4:1-16）；脱去旧人，穿上新人（4:17-32）；效法神，在爱中、在光明中、在智慧中行事，被圣灵充满（5:1-21）；在家庭和工作中彼此顺服（5:22–6:9）；穿戴神所赐的全副军装（6:10-20）。

第四章开头的「所以」（4:1）是全书的转折。前三章没有一句命令；后三章的命令都建立在前三章的恩典上。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 先看神，再看自己。** 读以弗所书，不要急着跳到第四至六章去找「该做什么」。先在前三章停下来，看神为你做了什么，然后再读那些命令，就会知道它们不是重担，而是蒙恩之人的回应。

**2. 留意「在基督里」。** 每次看见「在基督里」「在他里面」「与基督一同」，都可以停一下：这句话说我在基督里得着了什么？

**3. 留意「行事为人」。** 「行事为人」（原文是「行走」）是这卷书的另一个关键词（2:2、10；4:1、17；5:2、8、15）。信徒从前随着今世的风俗行，如今要在神所预备的善行中行，在爱中行，在光明中行，在智慧中行。

**4. 留意保罗的祷告。** 这卷书里有两段很长的祷告（1:15-23；3:14-21）。可以学着用保罗的祷告来为自己，也为别人祷告。

## 默想

**默想：** 「愿颂赞归与我们主耶稣基督的父神！他在基督里曾赐给我们天上各样属灵的福气」（1:3）。中国人有一句话叫「捧着金饭碗讨饭」。我在神面前，是不是也常常这样活着？明明在基督里已经蒙了「各样属灵的福气」，却仍然像一个讨饭的人：觉得神对我只是勉强忍耐，觉得自己总是比别的信徒少了一点什么，觉得要先做出一点成绩，才配得着神的爱；祷告的时候只剩下一张清单，没有一句赞美。保罗写这封信的时候，身上带着锁链（6:20），可是他一开口就是颂赞，因为他知道自己在基督里有多富足。这份富足不是我赚来的，而是基督用他的血为我买来的：「我们藉这爱子的血得蒙救赎，过犯得以赦免，乃是照他丰富的恩典」（1:7）。今天开始读以弗所书的时候，我能不能先不急着向神要什么，而是慢慢读1:3-14，每看见一样福气，就停下来，为这一样感谢神？最后，我能不能用保罗的第一句话，对神说一声：「愿颂赞归与我们主耶稣基督的父神！」

## What This Book Is About

Ephesians opens with praise: "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places" (1:3). Galatians is an urgent letter written to pull a wandering church back. Ephesians does not deal with one particular dispute. Instead it pulls the camera back and shows God's eternal plan: God "chose us in him before the foundation of the world" (1:4), "to unite all things in him, things in heaven and things on earth" (1:10).

On earth that plan is seen most clearly in the church. Jews and Gentiles, once enemies, have been made "one" in Christ (2:14); they are "fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise" (3:6). So the first half of Ephesians tells what God has done for us in Christ, and the second half tells how people who have received such grace are to "walk" (4:1): loving one another in the church, living out the new self at home and at work, and standing firm in the strength of the Lord (6:10–13).

Through the centuries many believers have counted Ephesians the richest of Paul's letters. It is not a hard doctrinal manual but a long poem woven of prayer, praise and exhortation.

## Author and Setting

**1. Author.** The letter twice names Paul as its author (1:1; 3:1), and the early church received it as Paul's without dispute. In modern times many scholars (especially in the critical tradition) have held that a disciple of Paul wrote it in his name, pointing to differences of vocabulary and style and to its close likeness to Colossians. Many other scholars (such as Harold Hoehner, Peter O'Brien, Clinton Arnold and Frank Thielman) have answered these arguments in detail, holding that differences of style can be explained by subject, genre and the use of a scribe, and that Paul is the author. This study takes the letter at its word and treats Paul as the author.

**2. "In Ephesus."** The words "in Ephesus" in 1:1 are missing from several very early and important manuscripts (such as P46, and the original hands of Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus). The letter also has almost no personal greetings, which is surprising, since Paul had lived in Ephesus for nearly three years (Acts 20:31). So many scholars think it was first a circular letter to Ephesus and the churches around it, with Ephesus as the chief among them.

**3. Date and place.** Paul wrote as a prisoner: "a prisoner for the Lord" (4:1), "an ambassador in chains" (6:20). Tradition places this in his Roman imprisonment (Acts 28:30–31), around AD 60–62. Tychicus carried this letter (6:21) and also Colossians (Col 4:7–8), so the two were probably written at the same time. Some scholars instead suggest the imprisonment in Caesarea (Acts 24:27), or an imprisonment in Ephesus.

**4. The city of Ephesus.** Ephesus was one of the chief cities of the Roman province of Asia. It was home to the great temple of Artemis (Acts 19:27) and was a center of magic and idolatry. When Paul preached there, many believers burned their books of magic, worth fifty thousand pieces of silver (Acts 19:19). No wonder Ephesians stresses that Christ is "far above all rule and authority and power and dominion" (1:21), and that believers "do not wrestle against flesh and blood" (6:12).

## Key Verse: 1:3

**1. "Blessed be the God and Father."** The letter begins with praise. The Christian life does not begin with "what must I do?" but with "what has God done?"

**2. "Who has blessed us."** This is already done. God does not say, "If you are good enough, I will bless you someday," but that he has already blessed us. We are not beggars before God but children who have received a rich inheritance.

**3. "In Christ."** "In Christ," "in him" and similar phrases recur throughout this letter. Every blessing is in Christ; apart from him there is none, and in him nothing is lacking.

**4. "Every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places."** The phrase "in the heavenly places" occurs five times in Ephesians (1:3, 20; 2:6; 3:10; 6:12). It does not mean only heaven in the future but the spiritual realm where Christ now reigns. Believers still live on earth, yet God has already "seated us with him in the heavenly places" (2:6).

## The Shape of the Book

Ephesians falls into two main parts:
- **What God has done for us in Christ** (chs. 1–3): the Father chooses, the Son redeems, the Spirit seals (1:3–14); Paul prays that God would open believers' eyes (1:15–23); we are raised from death, "by grace you have been saved through faith" (2:1–10); Jews and Gentiles become one new man in Christ, built together into God's temple (2:11–22); the mystery of the gospel is revealed to the Gentiles (3:1–13); Paul prays again that believers would know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge (3:14–21).
- **How those who have received grace are to walk** (chs. 4–6): maintaining the unity of the Spirit, each part working to build up the body of Christ (4:1–16); putting off the old self and putting on the new (4:17–32); imitating God, walking in love, in light and in wisdom, filled with the Spirit (5:1–21); mutual submission at home and at work (5:22–6:9); putting on the whole armor of God (6:10–20).

The "therefore" at the start of chapter 4 (4:1) is the hinge of the letter. Chapters 1–3 contain no commands; every command in chapters 4–6 rests on the grace of chapters 1–3.

## How to Read This Book

**1. Look at God first, then at yourself.** In reading Ephesians, do not rush ahead to chapters 4–6 to find "what to do." Stay in the first three chapters and see what God has done for you. Then the commands will read not as burdens but as the response of people who have received grace.

**2. Watch for "in Christ."** Each time you see "in Christ," "in him" or "with Christ," pause: what does this say I have received in Christ?

**3. Watch for "walk."** "Walk" is another key word in this letter (2:2, 10; 4:1, 17; 5:2, 8, 15). Believers once walked "following the course of this world"; now they walk in the good works God prepared, in love, in the light and in wisdom.

**4. Watch Paul's prayers.** The letter contains two long prayers (1:15–23; 3:14–21). Learn to pray Paul's prayers for yourself and for others.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places" (1:3). Chinese has a saying about someone "begging for food while holding a golden rice bowl." Do I often live before God like that? I have already been blessed in Christ "with every spiritual blessing," yet I live like a beggar: I feel that God only barely puts up with me, that I am always missing something other believers have, that I must first show some results before I deserve God's love; my prayers have become a list of requests without a word of praise. When Paul wrote this letter he was in chains (6:20), yet the first thing out of his mouth was praise, because he knew how rich he was in Christ. These riches were not earned by me; Christ bought them for me with his blood: "In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace" (1:7). As I begin Ephesians today, can I hold off asking God for anything and read 1:3–14 slowly, stopping at each blessing to thank God for that one? And at the end, can I say to God Paul's first words: "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ"?
