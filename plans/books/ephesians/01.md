## 本章脉络

第一章可以分成三段。**问安**（1-2节）：保罗「奉神旨意」作使徒，写信给「在基督耶稣里有忠心的人」。**一首赞美诗**（3-14节）：在希腊原文中，这是一个很长的句子，从头到尾在颂赞三一神：父神在基督里拣选了我们，预定我们得儿子的名分（3-6节）；我们在爱子里藉着他的血得蒙救赎（7-12节）；我们信了基督，就受了所应许的圣灵为印记（13-14节）。这一段三次说到神的荣耀（或荣耀的恩典）「得着称赞」（6、12、14节）。**一段祷告**（15-23节）：保罗求神照明信徒「心中的眼睛」，使他们知道神的恩召有何等指望、神的基业有何等丰盛的荣耀，又知道那叫基督从死里复活、坐在神右边的大能，如今也向着我们运行。

这一章几乎没有一句话是叫我们去做什么的；它只是一句接一句地告诉我们，神已经在基督里为我们做了什么。

## 深读 1:3-6 父神在基督里拣选了我们

**1. 从创立世界以前。** 「就如神从创立世界以前，在基督里拣选了我们，使我们在他面前成为圣洁，无有瑕疵」（1:4）。神拣选我们，不是在我们出生以后，看我们表现得好不好，而是在世界还没有造成以前。拣选的目的也很清楚：不是叫我们可以随便活，而是要我们「成为圣洁，无有瑕疵」。

**2. 怎样理解拣选？** 教会历史上对拣选有几种主要的看法，都承认神的拣选是出于恩典，不是出于人的功劳：
- **改革宗的看法**（如奥古斯丁、约翰·加尔文、《威斯敏斯德信条》第三章）：神照着自己的美意，无条件地拣选了某些人得救；人得救的根源完全在神。
- **阿民念派和卫斯理宗的看法**（如雅各·阿民念、约翰·卫斯理）：神预先知道谁会信，就拣选那些人（参《抗辩书》第一条，1610年）。
- **群体拣选的看法**（如罗伯特·尚克、威廉·克莱因）：神拣选的首先是基督，以及「在基督里」的群体；人藉着信进入这个群体。
保罗在这里的重点不是要解答所有的问题，而是要人敬拜：我得救的源头，在神永恒的爱里，比我的信心、我的决定都更早。

**3. 因爱预定我们得儿子的名分。** 「又因爱我们，就按着自己的意旨所喜悦的，预定我们藉着耶稣基督得儿子的名分」（1:5）。和合本把「因爱」放在第5节，有些译本则放在第4节末尾；原文两种读法都有可能：若连于第4节，「在爱中」是说我们在神面前成为圣洁、无有瑕疵的样式；若连于第5节，就是说神因爱预定了我们。多数解经家取后一种。无论如何，1:4-6清楚说明，拣选的根源是神「所喜悦的」美意和他的恩典。「得儿子的名分」是被收养进神的家，成为他的儿女，可以称他为父（参加4:5-6）。

**4. 在爱子里所赐的。** 「使他荣耀的恩典得着称赞；这恩典是他在爱子里所赐给我们的」（1:6）。神所爱的「爱子」就是基督（参太3:17）。父神爱他的儿子，也在他里面爱我们；我们被接纳，是因为我们在他所爱的那一位里面。

> 要点：神在创立世界以前，就在基督里拣选了我们，因爱预定我们作他的儿女。这一切的目的，是要他荣耀的恩典得着称赞。

## 深读 1:7-12 在爱子里得蒙救赎

**1. 藉这爱子的血。** 「我们藉这爱子的血得蒙救赎，过犯得以赦免，乃是照他丰富的恩典」（1:7）。「救赎」是付上赎价，把人从捆绑中买回来，让人想起神把以色列人从埃及为奴之家救出来（出6:6）。我们的赎价，就是基督的血。他在十字架上替我们死，承担了我们过犯的刑罚，所以我们的过犯得以赦免。

**2. 充充足足赏给我们。** 「这恩典是神用诸般智慧聪明，充充足足赏给我们的」（1:8）。神赐恩典不是一点一点地挤出来，而是满溢的。他的恩典「丰富」（1:7），而且「充充足足」。

**3. 旨意的奥秘。** 「叫我们知道他旨意的奥秘」（1:9）。保罗说的「奥秘」，不是神秘难懂的东西，也不是只有少数人才知道的秘诀，而是从前隐藏、如今神亲自启示出来的计划（参3:3-6）。

**4. 在基督里面同归于一。** 「要照所安排的，在日期满足的时候，使天上、地上、一切所有的都在基督里面同归于一」（1:10）。「同归于一」这个词原意是总结、归总；许多解经家把它和1:22「作万有之首」连起来看。今天的世界四分五裂，人与神隔绝，人与人对立，连受造之物也「叹息劳苦」（罗8:22）。神的计划是要叫万有在基督这位元首之下重新得着秩序与和谐。这不是说每一个人最后都必得救（参5:5-6），而是说，到那日，万有都要承认基督是主（腓2:10-11）。

**5. 首先在基督里有盼望的人。** 「叫他的荣耀从我们这首先在基督里有盼望的人可以得着称赞」（1:12）。许多解经家认为「我们」指像保罗这样先信主的犹太信徒，下一节的「你们」（1:13）指外邦信徒；这正预备了第二章犹太人和外邦人在基督里合一的信息。也有人认为「我们」泛指所有信徒。

> 要点：我们得蒙救赎、过犯得以赦免，完全是藉着基督的血，照着神丰富的恩典。神的计划不只是救一些人，更是要叫天上地上的万有都在基督里同归于一。

## 深读 1:13-14 受了所应许的圣灵为印记

**1. 听见、信了、受了印记。** 「你们既听见真理的道，就是那叫你们得救的福音，也信了基督，既然信他，就受了所应许的圣灵为印记」（1:13）。外邦信徒得着圣灵，是因为听见福音、信了基督，不是因为别的条件（参加3:2）。

**2. 印记。** 古时的印记表示归属和保护：盖了印的东西，表示是属于某人的，别人不能随便动。神把圣灵赐给信徒，就是在他们身上盖上了自己的印，表示「这是我的」（参4:30；林后1:22）。

**3. 得基业的凭据。** 「这圣灵是我们得基业的凭据，直等到神之民被赎，使他的荣耀得着称赞」（1:14）。「凭据」这个词在当时的买卖中指定金或头期款：先付一部分，保证以后一定付清全部。「直等到神之民被赎」这句话，原文可以理解为神赎回他自己的子民，也可以理解为我们得着所应许的基业。圣灵如今住在我们里面，就是神给我们的保证：我们将来所要得的全部基业，他必定赐给我们。

> 要点：圣灵是神盖在我们身上的印记，表示我们属于他，也是神的定金，保证他必把将来的基业完全赐给我们。从拣选到救赎到印记，三一神亲自担保了我们的救恩。

## 深读 1:15-23 照明你们心中的眼睛

**1. 为他们感谢，也为他们祈求。** 「因此，我既听见你们信从主耶稣，亲爱众圣徒，就为你们不住地感谢神」（1:15-16）。保罗没有求神改变他们的环境，也没有求神挪去他们的难处；他为他们求的，是更深地认识神。

**2. 心中的眼睛。** 「求我们主耶稣基督的神，荣耀的父，将那赐人智慧和启示的灵赏给你们，使你们真知道他，并且照明你们心中的眼睛」（1:17-18）。在圣经里，「心」不只是情感，也是人思想、决定和爱慕的中心。人可能头脑知道很多，心里的眼睛却是瞎的。只有圣灵能开启我们，叫我们不只是知道关于神的事，而是「真知道他」。

**3. 要知道三件事。** 保罗求神叫他们知道：
- **「他的恩召有何等指望」**（1:18）：神呼召我们，是要带我们到一个确定的将来；
- **「他在圣徒中得的基业有何等丰盛的荣耀」**（1:18）：这句话可以理解为神赐给圣徒的基业，也可以理解为神把圣徒当作他自己的基业（参申32:9）；许多解经家倾向后一种。想想看，神把他的百姓看为他宝贵的产业；
- **「他向我们这信的人所显的能力是何等浩大」**（1:19）。

**4. 叫基督从死里复活的大能。** 这能力是什么样的？「就是照他在基督身上所运行的大能大力，使他从死里复活，叫他在天上坐在自己的右边，远超过一切执政的、掌权的、有能的、主治的，和一切有名的」（1:20-21）。以弗所人从前生活在对邪术和灵界势力的恐惧中（徒19:19）。保罗告诉他们：不论天上地下有什么权势，基督都远在它们之上。而那叫基督复活的同样的大能，如今也向着信他的人运行。

**5. 为教会作万有之首。** 「又将万有服在他的脚下，使他为教会作万有之首。教会是他的身体，是那充满万有者所充满的」（1:22-23）。这里引用了诗篇8:6。「那充满万有者所充满的」有几种理解：
- 许多近代解经家认为，教会是被基督所充满的；
- 也有人（如约翰·屈梭多模、约翰·加尔文）认为，教会是基督的「丰满」，好像头需要身体才算完整；加尔文说这是教会最高的尊荣。这不是说永恒的圣子本身有什么缺欠；加尔文的意思是，基督作为元首和中保，甘愿把教会当作他的「丰满」。
无论哪一种，都说出教会与基督之间极亲密的关系：那位掌管万有的主，把自己赐给教会作元首。

> 要点：保罗最迫切地为信徒求的，是叫他们心中的眼睛被照明，真认识神：知道神呼召的指望、神基业的荣耀，以及叫基督复活、坐在万有之上的大能，如今也在我们身上运行。

## 默想

**默想：** 「并且照明你们心中的眼睛，使你们知道他的恩召有何等指望」（1:18）。我为别人祷告的时候，最常求什么？多半是身体健康、考试顺利、找到工作、孩子听话、签证批下来。这些都可以求，神也乐意听。可是保罗坐在监里为以弗所的信徒祷告，求的却是：叫他们「真知道他」，叫他们心中的眼睛被照明。他知道，一个人如果看见了神的荣耀，看见那叫基督复活的大能在自己身上，他就能面对任何环境；如果看不见，环境再顺利，心里还是贫穷。我自己呢？我是不是也常常只想神改变我的环境，却很少求神开我的眼睛，叫我认识他？今天，我能不能想一个我常常为他祷告的人，用1:17-19的话为他祷告一次，把他的名字放进去？然后也为自己这样求：主啊，开我的眼睛，叫我看见你在基督里为我预备的指望。那位从死里复活、坐在万有之上的基督，不只是我祷告的对象，也是我祷告的保证；他是教会的元首，也是我的主，他必不撇下我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 falls into three parts. **Greeting** (vv. 1–2): Paul, an apostle "by the will of God," writes to those who "are faithful in Christ Jesus." **A hymn of praise** (vv. 3–14): in Greek this is one long sentence, praising the triune God from beginning to end. The Father chose us in Christ and predestined us for adoption (vv. 3–6); in the Beloved we have redemption through his blood (vv. 7–12); when we believed in Christ we were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit (vv. 13–14). Three times the passage says "to the praise of his (glorious grace / his) glory" (vv. 6, 12, 14). **A prayer** (vv. 15–23): Paul asks God to enlighten "the eyes of your hearts," so that believers may know the hope of God's calling and the riches of his glorious inheritance, and may know that the power that raised Christ from the dead and seated him at God's right hand is now at work toward us.

Almost nothing in this chapter tells us to do anything. Sentence after sentence, it tells us what God has already done for us in Christ.

## Going Deeper 1:3–6 The Father Chose Us in Christ

**1. Before the foundation of the world.** "Even as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him" (1:4). God did not choose us after we were born, by watching how well we did, but before the world was made. The purpose of his choice is also clear: not that we may live as we please, but "that we should be holy and blameless."

**2. How should election be understood?** In the history of the church there have been several main views of election. All agree that God's choice is by grace, not by human merit:
- **The Reformed view** (e.g., Augustine, John Calvin, Westminster Confession ch. 3): God, according to his good pleasure, unconditionally chose certain people for salvation; the source of salvation lies wholly in God.
- **The Arminian and Wesleyan view** (e.g., Jacobus Arminius, John Wesley): God foreknew who would believe and chose them (cf. the Remonstrance of 1610, Article 1).
- **The corporate view** (e.g., Robert Shank, William Klein): God chose Christ and the body "in Christ"; people enter that body by faith.
Paul's aim here is not to answer every question but to lead us to worship: the source of my salvation lies in God's eternal love, which is earlier than my faith and my decision.

**3. In love he predestined us for adoption.** "In love he predestined us for adoption through Jesus Christ, according to the purpose of his will" (1:4–5). The ESV places "in love" at the end of verse 4 but reads it with verse 5; the Chinese Union Version places it in verse 5. The Greek allows either reading. Joined to verse 4, "in love" describes the holy and blameless life before God; joined to verse 5, it names God's love as the motive of predestination. Most interpreters take the latter. Either way, 1:4–6 makes plain that election springs from God's good pleasure and grace. "Adoption" means being taken into God's family as his children, able to call him Father (cf. Gal 4:5–6).

**4. Blessed in the Beloved.** "To the praise of his glorious grace, with which he has blessed us in the Beloved" (1:6). "The Beloved" is Christ (cf. Matt 3:17). The Father loves his Son, and in him he loves us; we are accepted because we are in the One he loves.

> The point: Before the foundation of the world God chose us in Christ, and in love he predestined us to be his children. The purpose of it all is the praise of his glorious grace.

## Going Deeper 1:7–12 Redeemed in the Beloved

**1. Through his blood.** "In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace" (1:7). "Redemption" means paying a price to buy someone back out of bondage, and recalls God's rescue of Israel from slavery in Egypt (Exod 6:6). Our price was the blood of Christ. He died on the cross in our place, bearing the penalty of our trespasses, and so our trespasses are forgiven.

**2. Lavished upon us.** "Which he lavished upon us, in all wisdom and insight" (1:8). God does not squeeze out his grace a little at a time; it overflows. His grace is "rich" (1:7), and he "lavished" it.

**3. The mystery of his will.** "Making known to us the mystery of his will" (1:9). By "mystery" Paul does not mean something obscure, or a secret known only to a few, but a plan once hidden and now revealed by God himself (cf. 3:3–6).

**4. To unite all things in him.** "As a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in him, things in heaven and things on earth" (1:10). The verb means "to sum up"; many link it with "head" in 1:22. Today the world is broken apart: people are cut off from God and set against one another, and even creation "has been groaning" (Rom 8:22). God's plan is to bring all things back into order and harmony under Christ as head. This does not mean every person will finally be saved (cf. 5:5–6), but that in that day all things will confess that Christ is Lord (Phil 2:10–11).

**5. The first to hope in Christ.** "So that we who were the first to hope in Christ might be to the praise of his glory" (1:12). Many interpreters take "we" as Jewish believers like Paul who believed first, and the "you also" of the next verse (1:13) as Gentile believers; this prepares for the message of chapter 2, that Jews and Gentiles are one in Christ. Others take "we" as all believers.

> The point: Our redemption and the forgiveness of our trespasses come wholly through the blood of Christ, according to the riches of God's grace. God's plan is not only to save some people but to unite all things in heaven and on earth in Christ.

## Going Deeper 1:13–14 Sealed with the Promised Holy Spirit

**1. Heard, believed, sealed.** "In him you also, when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and believed in him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit" (1:13). Gentile believers received the Spirit because they heard the gospel and believed in Christ, not on any other condition (cf. Gal 3:2).

**2. A seal.** In the ancient world a seal showed ownership and protection: a sealed item belonged to someone and could not be tampered with. In giving the Spirit to believers, God puts his own seal on them, saying, "This one is mine" (cf. 4:30; 2 Cor 1:22).

**3. The guarantee of our inheritance.** "Who is the guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it, to the praise of his glory" (1:14). In the commerce of the day, the word translated "guarantee" meant a deposit or first installment: part is paid now, guaranteeing that the whole will be paid later. The phrase "until we acquire possession of it" can also be read as God taking full possession of his own people (as the Chinese Union Version reads it). The Spirit now living in us is God's pledge that he will surely give us the whole inheritance to come.

> The point: The Spirit is God's seal on us, showing that we belong to him, and God's deposit, guaranteeing that he will give us the full inheritance to come. From election to redemption to sealing, the triune God himself guarantees our salvation.

## Going Deeper 1:15–23 The Eyes of Your Hearts Enlightened

**1. Thanks for them, prayer for them.** "For this reason, because I have heard of your faith in the Lord Jesus and your love toward all the saints, I do not cease to give thanks for you" (1:15–16). Paul does not ask God to change their circumstances or to remove their troubles; what he asks for them is a deeper knowledge of God.

**2. The eyes of the heart.** "That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give you a spirit of wisdom and of revelation in the knowledge of him, having the eyes of your hearts enlightened" (1:17–18). In Scripture the "heart" is not only feeling but the center of a person's thinking, choosing and desiring. A person can know a great deal in the head while the eyes of the heart remain blind. Only the Spirit can open us, so that we do not merely know things about God but truly know him.

**3. Three things to know.** Paul prays that they may know:
- **"what is the hope to which he has called you"** (1:18): God calls us toward a certain future;
- **"what are the riches of his glorious inheritance in the saints"** (1:18): this can be read as the inheritance God gives to the saints, or as God taking the saints as his own inheritance (cf. Deut 32:9); many interpreters lean toward the latter. Think of it: God counts his people his treasured possession;
- **"what is the immeasurable greatness of his power toward us who believe"** (1:19).

**4. The power that raised Christ.** What is this power like? It is "according to the working of his great might that he worked in Christ when he raised him from the dead and seated him at his right hand in the heavenly places, far above all rule and authority and power and dominion, and above every name that is named" (1:19–21). The Ephesians had once lived in fear of magic and spiritual powers (Acts 19:19). Paul tells them that whatever powers there are in heaven or on earth, Christ is far above them all. And the same power that raised Christ is now at work toward those who believe.

**5. Head over all things to the church.** "And he put all things under his feet and gave him as head over all things to the church, which is his body, the fullness of him who fills all in all" (1:22–23). Paul draws on Psalm 8:6. "The fullness of him who fills all in all" is read in several ways:
- many modern interpreters take it to mean the church is filled by Christ;
- others (such as John Chrysostom and John Calvin) take the church to be Christ's "fullness," as a head needs a body to be complete; Calvin calls this the highest honor of the church. This does not mean the eternal Son lacks anything in himself; Calvin's point is that Christ, as Head and Mediator, is pleased to count the church his "fullness."
Either way, it speaks of the closest bond between Christ and the church: the Lord who rules all things has given himself to the church as its head.

> The point: What Paul asks most urgently for believers is that the eyes of their hearts be enlightened to truly know God: the hope of his calling, the glory of his inheritance, and the power that raised Christ and seated him above all things, now at work in us.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Having the eyes of your hearts enlightened, that you may know what is the hope to which he has called you" (1:18). When I pray for others, what do I ask for most? Usually good health, passing exams, finding a job, obedient children, an approved visa. These may all be asked, and God gladly hears. Yet Paul, praying in prison for the believers in Ephesus, asked that they would truly know him, that the eyes of their hearts would be enlightened. He knew that someone who sees God's glory, and sees the power that raised Christ at work in his own life, can face any circumstance; and someone who does not see it remains poor at heart however smooth life becomes. What about me? Do I often want God only to change my circumstances, and rarely ask him to open my eyes to know him? Today, can I think of one person I often pray for and pray for them once in the words of 1:17–19, putting their name in? Then I can ask the same for myself: Lord, open my eyes to see the hope you have prepared for me in Christ. The Christ who rose from the dead and sits above all things is not only the one I pray to but the guarantee that my prayer is heard; he is head of the church and my Lord, and he will not leave me.
