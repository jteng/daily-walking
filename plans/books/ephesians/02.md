## 本章脉络

第二章有两大段，都是从「从前」讲到「如今」。**从死里活过来**（1-10节）：我们从前「死在过犯罪恶之中」，随从今世、魔鬼和肉体的私欲，「本为可怒之子」（1-3节）；「然而，神既有丰富的怜悯」，叫我们与基督一同活过来，一同坐在天上（4-7节）；「你们得救是本乎恩，也因着信」，不是出于行为，却是为要叫我们行善（8-10节）。**远人得亲近**（11-22节）：外邦人从前与基督无关，「没有指望，没有神」（11-12节）；如今靠着基督的血得以亲近，基督拆毁了中间隔断的墙，使犹太人和外邦人成为一个新人，一同进到父面前（13-18节）；他们不再是外人，是神家里的人，被建造成为「神藉着圣灵居住的所在」（19-22节）。

第一段讲人与神和好，第二段讲人与人和好；两件事都是藉着同一个十字架成就的。

## 深读 2:1-3 死在过犯罪恶之中

**1. 不是病了，是死了。** 「你们死在过犯罪恶之中，他叫你们活过来」（2:1）。保罗没有说人是软弱的、生了病的，需要一点帮助；他说人在属灵上是死的。死人不能自己活过来，也不能配合医生把自己救活。这是福音最坏的消息，也是我们需要好消息的原因。

**2. 三个辖制。** 「那时，你们在其中行事为人，随从今世的风俗，顺服空中掌权者的首领，就是现今在悖逆之子心中运行的邪灵。我们从前也都在他们中间，放纵肉体的私欲」（2:2-3）。这里说到三种辖制人的力量：
- **「今世的风俗」：** 一个远离神的世界所看重的价值和潮流；
- **「空中掌权者的首领」：** 魔鬼在悖逆的人心中运行（参6:12）；
- **「肉体的私欲」：** 人里面背离神的欲望，「随着肉体和心中所喜好的去行」。
人不只是做错了一些事，而是被外面的潮流、灵界的势力和自己里面的欲望一同捆绑。

**3. 本为可怒之子。** 「本为可怒之子，和别人一样」（2:3）。「本为」就是按着本性。人不是因为犯了一两次罪才落在神的忿怒之下，而是从亚当以来，人的本性就已经败坏了（参罗5:12）。「和别人一样」：保罗把自己这样的犹太人也包括在内。神的忿怒不是喜怒无常的脾气，而是圣洁的神对罪公义的反应。

> 要点：没有基督的人，在神面前不是病了，而是死了，被今世、魔鬼和自己的私欲捆绑，按本性落在神的忿怒之下。这是我们最需要听的坏消息。

## 深读 2:4-10 然而，神……

**1. 「然而，神」。** 「然而，神既有丰富的怜悯，因他爱我们的大爱，当我们死在过犯中的时候，便叫我们与基督一同活过来」（2:4-5）。全章的转折就在这里。人没有办法改变的处境，神亲自介入了。他这样做，不是因为我们有什么可爱，而是因为他「有丰富的怜悯」，因为他「爱我们的大爱」；而且是在我们「死在过犯中的时候」。

**2. 与基督一同。** 「他又叫我们与基督耶稣一同复活，一同坐在天上」（2:6）。第一章说神叫基督复活，坐在天上（1:20）；第二章说神也叫我们与他「一同」活过来、「一同」复活、「一同」坐在天上。信徒与基督联合，所以基督所经历的，也算是我们的。

**3. 显明给后来的世代看。** 「要将他极丰富的恩典，就是他在基督耶稣里向我们所施的恩慈，显明给后来的世代看」（2:7）。神救我们，是要在永世里叫我们成为他恩典的展览。将来的世代看见我们，就会说：神的恩典何等丰富！

**4. 本乎恩，也因着信。** 「你们得救是本乎恩，也因着信；这并不是出于自己，乃是神所赐的；也不是出于行为，免得有人自夸」（2:8-9）。
- **恩典是根源：** 得救完全是神白白的恩典。
- **信心是途径：** 信心不是我们献给神的功劳，而是空空的手，接受神所赐的。
- **「这」指什么？** 在原文中，「这」是中性的，而「信」和「恩」都是阴性名词，所以许多解经家（包括约翰·加尔文）认为「这」是指整个本乎恩、因着信而得救的事，而不是单单指信心。不过，圣经在别处也说信心是神所赐的（参腓1:29；徒13:48）。
- **「免得有人自夸」：** 在得救的事上，人没有一点可以夸口的地方。

**5. 为要叫我们行善。** 「我们原是他的工作，在基督耶稣里造成的，为要叫我们行善，就是神所预备叫我们行的」（2:10）。善行不是得救的根，而是得救的果子。人不是因为行善而得救，而是得救以后才能真正行善。「他的工作」原文有作品的意思：我们是神亲手的作品。神不只预备了我们的救恩，也预备了我们要行的善事；我们只要在其中「行」（参4:1）。

> 要点：我们得救完全是本乎神的恩典，藉着信心接受，不是出于自己，也不是出于行为，所以没有人可以自夸。但神救我们，是要我们成为他的作品，在他所预备的善行中行走。

## 深读 2:11-13 从前远离，如今亲近

**1. 应当记念。** 「所以你们应当记念：你们从前按肉体是外邦人」（2:11）。保罗要外邦信徒回想自己从前的光景。记得自己从哪里被救出来，才会知道恩典有多大。

**2. 五样缺乏。** 「那时，你们与基督无关，在以色列国民以外，在所应许的诸约上是局外人，并且活在世上没有指望，没有神」（2:12）。外邦人可能有很多神明，以弗所就有亚底米大庙，但他们不认识那位真神；他们可能有很多计划，却没有真正的盼望。

**3. 靠着他的血。** 「你们从前远离神的人，如今却在基督耶稣里，靠着他的血，已经得亲近了」（2:13）。「远」和「近」是旧约的说法：以色列人是「近」神的百姓（诗148:14），列国是「远」的。外邦人得以亲近神，不是因为受了割礼、守了律法，而是「靠着他的血」。

> 要点：外邦人从前与神隔绝，没有指望。如今他们得以亲近神，唯一的根据是基督的血。

## 深读 2:14-18 他使我们和睦

**1. 他就是我们的和睦。** 「因他使我们和睦，将两下合而为一，拆毁了中间隔断的墙」（2:14）。原文直译是：他自己就是我们的和平（参赛9:6；弥5:5）。基督不只是带来和平的方法，他自己就是和平。

**2. 中间隔断的墙。** 当时耶路撒冷的圣殿有一道石栏，把外邦人院和里面的院子隔开，栏上刻着警告，说外邦人若越过这栏，就要被处死。考古学家已经找到这种刻文。保罗自己就曾被人诬告带着以弗所人特罗非摩进入殿里（徒21:28-29）。许多解经家认为保罗在这里想到了这道墙；也有人认为「墙」是比喻，指律法把犹太人和外邦人分开。无论哪一种，基督都已经把这墙拆毁了。

**3. 废掉那记在律法上的规条。** 「而且以自己的身体废掉冤仇，就是那记在律法上的规条」（2:15）。这是什么意思？保罗不是说神的道德律从此作废，因为他在6:2还引用十诫劝勉儿女。解经家有几种理解：
- 许多改革宗解经家（如加尔文）认为，这里主要指礼仪的律法，像割礼、饮食和洁净的条例，这些曾把犹太人和外邦人分开；
- 也有学者（如哈罗德·霍纳、弗兰克·锡尔曼）认为，这是指摩西律法作为一整个旧约的制度已经在基督里成全，信徒不再在这个约之下；但律法所表达的神的道德旨意，在基督里仍然有效（参罗13:8-10）。

**4. 一个新人，与神和好。** 「为要将两下藉着自己造成一个新人，如此便成就了和睦。既在十字架上灭了冤仇，便藉这十字架使两下归为一体，与神和好了」（2:15-16）。基督不是叫外邦人变成犹太人，也不是叫犹太人变成外邦人，而是在他里面造出「一个新人」。而且人与人和好，是建立在人与神和好上：两下「归为一体，与神和好了」。十字架上，基督担当了神对罪的忿怒，也拆毁了人与人之间的敌意。

**5. 被一个圣灵所感，进到父面前。** 「因为我们两下藉着他被一个圣灵所感，得以进到父面前」（2:18）。这里又看见三一神：藉着圣子，靠着圣灵，进到圣父面前。从前只有大祭司一年一次能进入至圣所；如今，无论犹太人还是外邦人，都可以靠着基督坦然进到父面前。

> 要点：基督自己就是我们的和平。他在十字架上使我们与神和好，也拆毁了人与人之间的墙，把从前敌对的两群人造成一个新人，一同进到父面前。

## 深读 2:19-22 神藉着圣灵居住的所在

**1. 不再作外人和客旅。** 「这样，你们不再作外人和客旅，是与圣徒同国，是神家里的人了」（2:19）。保罗用了三个比喻：国家（同国的公民）、家庭（神家里的人）、圣殿（下面几节）。外邦信徒在神的百姓中，不是二等公民，也不是寄居的客人。

**2. 使徒和先知的根基，基督为房角石。** 「并且被建造在使徒和先知的根基上，有基督耶稣自己为房角石」（2:20）。这里的「先知」，许多解经家认为是新约的先知，因为保罗在3:5和4:11也把使徒和先知并列。使徒和先知所传的福音，就是教会的根基；根基只立一次，以后的教会都建造在上面。「房角石」（参赛28:16）是整座建筑定位、对齐所依据的那块石头。

**3. 成为主的圣殿。** 「各房靠他联络得合式，渐渐成为主的圣殿。你们也靠他同被建造，成为神藉着圣灵居住的所在」（2:21-22）。旧约时代，神的同在住在耶路撒冷的圣殿里；如今，神藉着圣灵住在他的百姓中间。教会不是一座建筑，而是一群人；而这群人是神的居所。

> 要点：在基督里，从前的外人如今是神家里的人。整个教会被建造在使徒和先知的根基上，以基督为房角石，成为神藉着圣灵居住的殿。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因他使我们和睦，将两下合而为一，拆毁了中间隔断的墙」（2:14）。在我所在的教会，或在我心里，还有哪些「中间隔断的墙」？也许是国语堂、粤语堂和英文堂之间互不来往；也许是第一代和第二代彼此看不惯；也许是从不同地方来的人，私下里互相看轻；也许是在政治立场或疫苗这些事上意见不同，就不再和那位弟兄姊妹说话。我嘴上说「我们在主里是一家人」，心里却悄悄筑起一道墙，把某一类人放在墙外。保罗说，那道墙不是用劝勉拆掉的，而是基督「以自己的身体」在十字架上拆毁的（2:14-16）。我和那个人能够一同进到父面前，不是因为我们谈得来、背景相近，而是因为我们都是「从前远离神的人」，都是「靠着他的血」才得以亲近（2:13）。我在神面前也曾是外人，基督却为我拆毁了那墙。今天，我能不能承认心里那道墙是骄傲，求主赦免；然后想一个在墙那一边的人，为他祷告，再找一个小小的机会，向他走近一步，问候一声，或请他吃一顿饭？基督自己就是我们的和平；墙已经倒了，我不必再去守着它。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 has two main parts, both moving from "once" to "now." **Made alive from death** (vv. 1–10): we were once "dead in the trespasses and sins," following the world, the devil and the passions of the flesh, "by nature children of wrath" (vv. 1–3); "but God, being rich in mercy," made us alive with Christ and seated us with him in the heavenly places (vv. 4–7); "by grace you have been saved through faith," not as a result of works, yet for good works (vv. 8–10). **The far brought near** (vv. 11–22): Gentiles were once separated from Christ, "having no hope and without God" (vv. 11–12); now they have been brought near by the blood of Christ, who broke down the dividing wall and made Jews and Gentiles one new man with access to the Father (vv. 13–18); they are no longer strangers but members of God's household, built into "a dwelling place for God by the Spirit" (vv. 19–22).

The first part speaks of people reconciled to God, the second of people reconciled to one another; both are accomplished through the same cross.

## Going Deeper 2:1–3 Dead in Trespasses and Sins

**1. Not sick but dead.** "And you were dead in the trespasses and sins" (2:1). Paul does not say people are weak or sick and need a little help; he says they are spiritually dead. The dead cannot bring themselves back to life, nor cooperate with a doctor to revive themselves. This is the worst news in the gospel, and the reason we need good news.

**2. Three masters.** "In which you once walked, following the course of this world, following the prince of the power of the air, the spirit that is now at work in the sons of disobedience- among whom we all once lived in the passions of our flesh" (2:2–3). Three powers hold people captive:
- **"the course of this world":** the values and currents of a world turned away from God;
- **"the prince of the power of the air":** the devil at work in the hearts of the disobedient (cf. 6:12);
- **"the passions of our flesh":** desires within us that turn from God, "carrying out the desires of the body and the mind."
People have not merely done some wrong things; they are bound together by the currents outside them, by spiritual powers and by their own desires.

**3. By nature children of wrath.** "And were by nature children of wrath, like the rest of mankind" (2:3). "By nature" means in our very nature. We do not fall under God's wrath only because of a sin or two; since Adam, human nature itself has been corrupted (cf. Rom 5:12). "Like the rest of mankind": Paul includes Jews like himself. God's wrath is not a fickle temper but the holy God's just response to sin.

> The point: Apart from Christ, people are not sick before God but dead, held captive by the world, the devil and their own desires, and by nature under God's wrath. This is the bad news we most need to hear.

## Going Deeper 2:4–10 But God…

**1. "But God."** "But God, being rich in mercy, because of the great love with which he loved us, even when we were dead in our trespasses, made us alive together with Christ" (2:4–5). Here the chapter turns. Into a condition we could not change, God himself stepped in. He did it not because we were lovable but because he is "rich in mercy," "because of the great love with which he loved us," and "even when we were dead in our trespasses."

**2. Together with Christ.** "And raised us up with him and seated us with him in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus" (2:6). Chapter 1 said God raised Christ and seated him in the heavenly places (1:20); chapter 2 says God has also made us alive, raised us and seated us "with him." Believers are united to Christ, so what Christ has gone through is counted as ours.

**3. Shown in the coming ages.** "So that in the coming ages he might show the immeasurable riches of his grace in kindness toward us in Christ Jesus" (2:7). God saved us in order to make us, through eternity, a display of his grace. The coming ages will look at us and say: how rich is the grace of God!

**4. By grace, through faith.** "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast" (2:8–9).
- **Grace is the source:** salvation is wholly God's free grace.
- **Faith is the means:** faith is not a merit we offer God but an empty hand receiving what he gives.
- **What does "this" refer to?** In Greek "this" is neuter, while "grace" and "faith" are both feminine nouns, so many interpreters (including John Calvin) take "this" to mean the whole event of being saved by grace through faith, not faith alone. Yet Scripture elsewhere also speaks of faith as God's gift (cf. Phil 1:29; Acts 13:48).
- **"So that no one may boast":** in salvation there is nothing at all for us to boast of.

**5. Created for good works.** "For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them" (2:10). Good works are not the root of salvation but its fruit. We are not saved because we do good; only once saved can we truly do good. "Workmanship" carries the sense of something made: we are God's own handiwork. God prepared not only our salvation but also the good works we are to do; we have only to "walk in them" (cf. 4:1).

> The point: We are saved wholly by God's grace, received through faith, not our own doing and not a result of works, so no one may boast. Yet God saved us to make us his workmanship, walking in the good works he prepared.

## Going Deeper 2:11–13 Once Far, Now Near

**1. Remember.** "Therefore remember that at one time you Gentiles in the flesh…" (2:11). Paul wants Gentile believers to recall what they once were. Only those who remember where they were rescued from know how great grace is.

**2. Five lacks.** "Remember that you were at that time separated from Christ, alienated from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers to the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God in the world" (2:12). Gentiles may have had many gods (Ephesus had the great temple of Artemis), but they did not know the true God; they may have had many plans, but no real hope.

**3. By his blood.** "But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ" (2:13). "Far" and "near" are Old Testament terms: Israel was the people "near" to God (Ps 148:14), the nations were "far." Gentiles are brought near to God not by being circumcised or keeping the law but "by the blood of Christ."

> The point: Gentiles were once cut off from God, without hope. Now they are brought near to God, and the only ground is the blood of Christ.

## Going Deeper 2:14–18 He Himself Is Our Peace

**1. He himself is our peace.** "For he himself is our peace, who has made us both one and has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility" (2:14; cf. Isa 9:6; Mic 5:5). Christ is not only the means of bringing peace; he himself is peace.

**2. The dividing wall.** The temple in Jerusalem had a stone balustrade separating the Court of the Gentiles from the inner courts. Inscriptions on it warned that any foreigner who went beyond it would be put to death, and archaeologists have found such inscriptions. Paul himself had been falsely accused of bringing Trophimus the Ephesian into the temple (Acts 21:28–29). Many interpreters think Paul has this wall in mind; others take the "wall" as a figure for the law that separated Jews from Gentiles. Either way, Christ has broken it down.

**3. Abolishing the law of commandments and ordinances.** "By abolishing the law of commandments and ordinances" (2:15). What does this mean? Paul does not mean God's moral law is now void, for in 6:2 he quotes the Ten Commandments to children. Interpreters read it in several ways:
- many Reformed interpreters (such as Calvin) take it mainly as the ceremonial law, such as circumcision and the food and purity laws, which had divided Jews from Gentiles;
- other scholars (such as Harold Hoehner and Frank Thielman) take it as the Mosaic law as a whole covenant order, fulfilled in Christ, so that believers are no longer under that covenant; yet God's moral will expressed in the law remains in force in Christ (cf. Rom 13:8–10).

**4. One new man, reconciled to God.** "That he might create in himself one new man in place of the two, so making peace, and might reconcile us both to God in one body through the cross, thereby killing the hostility" (2:15–16). Christ does not turn Gentiles into Jews or Jews into Gentiles; he creates in himself "one new man." And the reconciliation of people with one another rests on their reconciliation with God: he reconciles "us both to God in one body." On the cross Christ bore God's wrath against sin and also tore down the hostility between people.

**5. Access in one Spirit to the Father.** "For through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father" (2:18). Again we see the triune God: through the Son, in the Spirit, to the Father. Once only the high priest could enter the Most Holy Place, once a year; now Jew and Gentile alike may come boldly to the Father through Christ.

> The point: Christ himself is our peace. On the cross he reconciled us to God and broke down the wall between people, creating one new man from two hostile groups, who now come together to the Father.

## Going Deeper 2:19–22 A Dwelling Place for God by the Spirit

**1. No longer strangers and aliens.** "So then you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God" (2:19). Paul uses three pictures: a nation (fellow citizens), a family (God's household) and a temple (the next verses). Gentile believers are not second-class citizens among God's people, nor guests passing through.

**2. The foundation of apostles and prophets, Christ the cornerstone.** "Built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the cornerstone" (2:20). Many interpreters take these "prophets" to be New Testament prophets, since Paul pairs apostles and prophets again in 3:5 and 4:11. The gospel the apostles and prophets proclaimed is the church's foundation; a foundation is laid once, and every later generation of the church is built on it. The "cornerstone" (cf. Isa 28:16) is the stone by which the whole building is set and aligned.

**3. A holy temple in the Lord.** "In whom the whole structure, being joined together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord. In him you also are being built together into a dwelling place for God by the Spirit" (2:21–22). In the Old Testament, God's presence dwelt in the temple in Jerusalem; now God dwells by the Spirit among his people. The church is not a building but a people, and this people is God's dwelling.

> The point: In Christ, those who were once outsiders are now members of God's household. The whole church is built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ as cornerstone, into a temple where God dwells by the Spirit.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For he himself is our peace, who has made us both one and has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility" (2:14). In my church, or in my own heart, what "dividing walls" remain? Perhaps the Mandarin, Cantonese and English congregations never mix; perhaps the first and second generations cannot stand each other's ways; perhaps people from different places quietly look down on one another; perhaps, after disagreeing over politics or vaccines, I no longer speak to a certain brother or sister. With my lips I say, "We are one family in the Lord," yet in my heart I quietly build a wall and put a certain kind of person outside it. Paul says that wall is not torn down by exhortation; Christ broke it down "in his flesh" on the cross (2:14–16). That person and I can come together to the Father not because we get along or share a background, but because we were both "far off" and were both "brought near by the blood of Christ" (2:13). I too was once a stranger before God, and Christ tore down the wall for me. Today, can I confess that wall in my heart as pride and ask the Lord's forgiveness, then think of one person on the other side of it, pray for them, and look for one small chance to step toward them: a greeting, or a meal together? Christ himself is our peace; the wall has already fallen, and I no longer need to guard it.
