## 本章脉络

第三章有两段。**福音的奥秘与保罗的职分**（1-13节）：保罗刚开口要祷告（「因此，我－保罗……」，1节），就停下来，先说明神托付给他的职分：神用启示叫他知道基督的奥秘，就是「外邦人在基督耶稣里，藉着福音，得以同为后嗣，同为一体，同蒙应许」（6节）；他这个「比众圣徒中最小的还小」的人，蒙恩把「基督那测不透的丰富」传给外邦人（8节）；神要「藉着教会」使天上执政的、掌权的得知神百般的智慧（10节）。**保罗的第二个祷告**（14-21节）：他接着第1节说「因此，我在父面前屈膝」，求神叫信徒心里刚强，叫基督住在他们心里，使他们能明白基督的爱是「何等长阔高深」（18节）；最后以颂赞结束：「神能……充充足足地成就一切，超过我们所求所想的」（20节）。

这一章是全书前半部的结束。前三章从赞美开始（1:3），也以赞美结束（3:20-21）。

## 深读 3:1-7 基督的奥秘

**1. 为外邦人作了被囚的。** 「因此，我－保罗为你们外邦人作了基督耶稣被囚的」（3:1）。保罗坐监，正是因为他把福音传给外邦人（参徒21:28；22:21-22）。他没有说自己是罗马的囚犯，而是「基督耶稣被囚的」：连他的锁链也在主的手中。

**2. 奥秘是启示出来的。** 「用启示使我知道福音的奥秘」（3:3）。保罗的福音不是他自己想出来的，而是神启示给他的（参加1:12）。「这奥秘在以前的世代没有叫人知道，像如今藉着圣灵启示他的圣使徒和先知一样」（3:5）。注意「像……一样」：旧约已经预告万国要因亚伯拉罕得福（创12:3；参赛49:6），但从前没有像如今这样清楚地显明。

**3. 三个「同」。** 「这奥秘就是外邦人在基督耶稣里，藉着福音，得以同为后嗣，同为一体，同蒙应许」（3:6）。原文用了三个以「同」开头的词：同为后嗣、同为一个身体、同享应许。外邦人不是附属在以色列旁边的客人，而是和犹太信徒完全平等地在同一个身体里。这就是第二章所说的「一个新人」（2:15）。

**4. 照神的恩赐作执事。** 「我作了这福音的执事，是照神的恩赐」（3:7）。保罗的职分本身就是恩典，不是他争取来的地位。

> 要点：福音的奥秘，就是外邦人和犹太人在基督里同为后嗣、同为一体、同蒙应许。这是神从前隐藏、如今启示的计划，保罗为这个福音甘心作基督耶稣被囚的。

## 深读 3:8-13 测不透的丰富，百般的智慧

**1. 最小的还小。** 「我本来比众圣徒中最小的还小，然而他还赐我这恩典，叫我把基督那测不透的丰富传给外邦人」（3:8）。「最小的还小」原文是在「最小」这个最高级上再加上比较级的字尾，好像说：比最小的更小。这不是假谦虚。保罗记得自己曾逼迫教会（林前15:9；提前1:13-15）。正因为他知道自己不配，他才看见恩典何等大。

**2. 测不透的丰富。** 「测不透」的意思是找不到尽头，好像追踪足迹却追不到底。基督的丰富不是一个讲完就没有的题目，人一生也探不完。

**3. 藉着教会显明神百般的智慧。** 「为要藉着教会使天上执政的、掌权的，现在得知神百般的智慧」（3:10）。「百般的」原文有多彩、多样的意思。神把从前敌对的犹太人和外邦人，不同语言、不同文化、不同阶层的人，合成一个身体；连天上的灵界势力看见了，也要惊叹神的智慧。教会是神向全宇宙展示的作品。一个四分五裂的教会，是在遮掩这份智慧。

**4. 放胆无惧来到神面前。** 「我们因信耶稣，就在他里面放胆无惧，笃信不疑地来到神面前」（3:12）。这句话也可以译为：因着他的信实；许多解经家仍然认为是指我们信他。不论怎样，我们能坦然来到神面前，根据完全在基督，不在我们自己。

**5. 患难是你们的荣耀。** 「所以，我求你们不要因我为你们所受的患难丧胆，这原是你们的荣耀」（3:13）。保罗的锁链不是福音失败的证据，而是外邦人蒙爱的证据：有人为了把福音带给他们，甘心受苦。

> 要点：保罗看自己比最小的还小，却蒙恩传扬基督测不透的丰富。神要藉着由各样人组成的教会，向天上的权势显明他百般的智慧；我们因信基督，可以放胆来到神面前。

## 深读 3:14-19 叫你们能以明白基督的爱

**1. 在父面前屈膝。** 「因此，我在父面前屈膝，（天上地上的各家，都是从他得名。）」（3:14-15）犹太人通常站着祷告（参路18:11、13），屈膝表示格外迫切和谦卑（参路22:41）。保罗与以弗所的长老分别时，也曾跪下和他们一同祷告（徒20:36）。「父」和「各家」在原文中是同字根的词，好像说：一切的「家」都是从那位「父」得名的。

**2. 一连串的祈求。** 这段祷告像一层一层往上走的台阶：
- **里面刚强：** 「求他按着他丰盛的荣耀，藉着他的灵，叫你们心里的力量刚强起来」（3:16）；
- **基督安家：** 「使基督因你们的信，住在你们心里」（3:17）。这些人已经是信徒，基督已经在他们里面。保罗求的是基督在他们心里更深地「安家」，掌管他们生命的每一个房间；
- **有根有基：** 「叫你们的爱心有根有基」（3:17）。「根」是植物的比喻，「基」是建筑的比喻；
- **明白基督的爱：** 「能以和众圣徒一同明白基督的爱是何等长阔高深」（3:18）；
- **被神充满：** 「便叫神一切所充满的，充满了你们」（3:19）。

**3. 长阔高深。** 保罗没有说明「长阔高深」各指什么。古时有人（如奥古斯丁）把这四个方向看作十字架的形状；也有人把它们分别解作爱的不同方面。多数解经家认为，保罗只是用这四个方向说明基督的爱无边无际，向四面八方都测不到尽头。注意「和众圣徒一同」：基督的爱太大了，没有一个人能独自明白，我们需要在神的家中一同领会。

**4. 知道那过于人所能测度的爱。** 「并知道这爱是过于人所能测度的」（3:19）。这是一个奇妙的说法：要「知道」那「过于人所能测度」的爱。我们永远不能把基督的爱完全测透，却可以越来越深地经历它、认识它。这不只是头脑的知识，而是像孩子认识父母的爱那样的认识。

> 要点：保罗最深的祷告，是求基督在信徒心里安家，叫他们在爱中扎根，和众圣徒一同明白基督的爱是何等长阔高深。这爱测不透，却可以越来越深地认识。

## 深读 3:20-21 超过我们所求所想的

**1. 神能。** 「神能照着运行在我们心里的大力充充足足地成就一切，超过我们所求所想的」（3:20）。保罗刚刚求了极大的事，基督住在心里，被神一切的丰盛充满；他好像担心读者会想：这也求得太多了吧？所以他说，神所能成就的，不只是我们所求的，还超过我们所想的。

**2. 照着运行在我们心里的大力。** 这大力就是1:19-20所说，叫基督从死里复活的大能。神的大能不只在天上，也在我们心里运行。

**3. 在教会中，在基督耶稣里，得着荣耀。** 「但愿他在教会中，并在基督耶稣里，得着荣耀，直到世世代代，永永远远。阿们！」（3:21）一切的目的都是神的荣耀。教会和基督并列，因为教会是基督的身体；神在基督身上得荣耀，也在他所救赎的子民身上得荣耀，直到永远。

> 要点：神所能成就的，超过我们所求所想的。他的大能如今就在我们里面运行。一切都归向一个目的：愿神在教会中、在基督耶稣里得着荣耀，直到永永远远。

## 默想

**默想：** 「能以和众圣徒一同明白基督的爱是何等长阔高深，并知道这爱是过于人所能测度的」（3:18-19）。我是不是早就「知道」基督爱我？从小在主日学唱过「耶稣爱我，我知道」，讲台上也听过无数次。可是在我心里最暗的时候，例如重复跌倒在同一个罪里，或者被人伤害、觉得被神忘记的时候，那句话好像只是一个教义，碰不到我。也许我心里偷偷想：神爱世人，也许爱那些属灵的人，可是这样的我呢？保罗为以弗所的信徒祷告，不是求他们多知道一个道理，而是求他们「明白」那「过于人所能测度」的爱。这爱有多长？从「创立世界以前」（1:4）一直到「世世代代，永永远远」（3:21）。有多阔？连从前「没有指望，没有神」的外邦人也包括在内（2:12）。有多深？深到基督降到十字架上，替我担当神的忿怒（2:16）。有多高？高到他叫我「一同坐在天上」（2:6）。我最低、最坏的那一刻，也没有落在这爱的外面。今天，我能不能把3:16-19慢慢读几遍，当作保罗也在为我祷告，求圣灵叫我不只知道，而是真正尝到这爱？也许，我还可以去找一位弟兄姊妹，告诉他我在哪件事上难以相信神爱我，请他和我一同祷告，因为这爱是要「和众圣徒一同」明白的。即使今天我仍然感觉不到，基督的爱也不会因此少一分；那位在十字架上为我舍命的主，比我更迫切地要我认识他的爱。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 has two parts. **The mystery of the gospel and Paul's stewardship** (vv. 1–13): Paul starts to pray ("For this reason I, Paul…," v. 1) and then breaks off to explain the stewardship God gave him. By revelation God made known to him the mystery of Christ: "that the Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel" (v. 6). He, "the very least of all the saints," was given grace to preach to the Gentiles "the unsearchable riches of Christ" (v. 8), and "through the church" God makes known his manifold wisdom to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places (v. 10). **Paul's second prayer** (vv. 14–21): picking up from verse 1, "For this reason I bow my knees before the Father," he prays that believers would be strengthened within, that Christ would dwell in their hearts, and that they would comprehend "the breadth and length and height and depth" of Christ's love (v. 18). He closes in praise: God "is able to do far more abundantly than all that we ask or think" (v. 20).

This chapter ends the first half of the letter. Chapters 1–3 begin with praise (1:3) and end with praise (3:20–21).

## Going Deeper 3:1–7 The Mystery of Christ

**1. A prisoner on behalf of the Gentiles.** "For this reason I, Paul, a prisoner for Christ Jesus on behalf of you Gentiles" (3:1). Paul was in prison precisely because he took the gospel to the Gentiles (cf. Acts 21:28; 22:21–22). He calls himself not Rome's prisoner but "a prisoner for Christ Jesus": even his chains are in the Lord's hand.

**2. A mystery revealed.** "How the mystery was made known to me by revelation" (3:3). Paul's gospel was not his own invention but God's revelation to him (cf. Gal 1:12). It "was not made known to the sons of men in other generations as it has now been revealed to his holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit" (3:5). Note the word "as": the Old Testament had already foretold that the nations would be blessed through Abraham (Gen 12:3; cf. Isa 49:6), but it had never been revealed as clearly as now.

**3. Three "fellow" words.** "This mystery is that the Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel" (3:6). The Greek uses three words beginning with "together-": co-heirs, co-members of one body, co-sharers in the promise. Gentiles are not guests attached to Israel's side but stand fully equal with Jewish believers in the same body. This is the "one new man" of chapter 2 (2:15).

**4. A minister by God's gift.** "Of this gospel I was made a minister according to the gift of God's grace" (3:7). Paul's office was itself grace, not a position he won.

> The point: The mystery of the gospel is that Gentiles and Jews are fellow heirs, members of one body and sharers in the promise in Christ. This is God's plan, once hidden and now revealed, and for this gospel Paul gladly became a prisoner of Christ Jesus.

## Going Deeper 3:8–13 Unsearchable Riches, Manifold Wisdom

**1. The very least.** "To me, though I am the very least of all the saints, this grace was given, to preach to the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ" (3:8). "The very least" in Greek adds a comparative ending to the superlative "least," as if to say "leaster than the least." This is not false modesty. Paul remembered that he had persecuted the church (1 Cor 15:9; 1 Tim 1:13–15). Precisely because he knew he was unworthy, he saw how great grace is.

**2. Unsearchable riches.** "Unsearchable" means impossible to trace to the end, like following tracks that never run out. The riches of Christ are not a subject one finishes; a lifetime cannot exhaust them.

**3. God's manifold wisdom shown through the church.** "So that through the church the manifold wisdom of God might now be made known to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places" (3:10). "Manifold" carries the sense of many-colored, richly varied. God has joined into one body Jews and Gentiles who were once enemies, people of different languages, cultures and classes; even the spiritual powers in the heavenly places see it and marvel at God's wisdom. The church is God's display before the whole universe. A church torn apart hides that wisdom.

**4. Boldness and access.** "In whom we have boldness and access with confidence through our faith in him" (3:12). The phrase can also be rendered "through his faithfulness"; many interpreters still take it as our faith in him. Either way, the ground of our confident access to God lies wholly in Christ, not in ourselves.

**5. Sufferings that are your glory.** "So I ask you not to lose heart over what I am suffering for you, which is your glory" (3:13). Paul's chains are not evidence that the gospel has failed but evidence that the Gentiles are loved: someone was willing to suffer to bring them the gospel.

> The point: Paul saw himself as less than the least, yet was given grace to preach the unsearchable riches of Christ. Through a church made of every kind of people God displays his manifold wisdom to the powers in the heavenly places, and through faith in Christ we may come boldly to God.

## Going Deeper 3:14–19 To Comprehend the Love of Christ

**1. Bowing before the Father.** "For this reason I bow my knees before the Father, from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named" (3:14–15). Jews usually stood to pray (cf. Luke 18:11, 13); kneeling showed special urgency and humility (cf. Luke 22:41). When Paul parted from the Ephesian elders, he knelt and prayed with them (Acts 20:36). "Father" and "family" share a root in Greek, as if to say that every "family" takes its name from the "Father."

**2. A chain of requests.** The prayer climbs like a staircase:
- **strength within:** "that according to the riches of his glory he may grant you to be strengthened with power through his Spirit in your inner being" (3:16);
- **Christ at home:** "so that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith" (3:17). These people are already believers; Christ is already in them. Paul asks that Christ would settle more deeply "at home" in their hearts, ruling every room of their lives;
- **rooted and grounded:** "that you, being rooted and grounded in love" (3:17). "Rooted" is a picture from plants, "grounded" from building;
- **comprehending Christ's love:** "may have strength to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth" (3:18);
- **filled by God:** "that you may be filled with all the fullness of God" (3:19).

**3. Breadth, length, height and depth.** Paul does not explain what each dimension refers to. Some in the early church (such as Augustine) saw in the four directions the shape of the cross; others have assigned each to a different aspect of love. Most interpreters think Paul simply uses four directions to say that Christ's love has no bounds; in every direction there is no end to it. Note "with all the saints": Christ's love is too great for anyone to grasp alone; we need to take it in together in God's household.

**4. Knowing a love beyond knowledge.** "And to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge" (3:19). This is a wonderful paradox: to "know" a love that "surpasses knowledge." We will never fully fathom Christ's love, yet we can experience it and know it ever more deeply. This is not merely head knowledge but the kind of knowing a child has of a parent's love.

> The point: Paul's deepest prayer is that Christ would make his home in believers' hearts, that they would be rooted in love and comprehend with all the saints the breadth and length and height and depth of Christ's love. This love cannot be fathomed, yet it can be known ever more deeply.

## Going Deeper 3:20–21 Far More Than We Ask or Think

**1. God is able.** "Now to him who is able to do far more abundantly than all that we ask or think, according to the power at work within us" (3:20). Paul has just asked for enormous things: Christ dwelling in the heart, being filled with all the fullness of God. As if readers might think, "Isn't that asking too much?", he says that what God can do is not only what we ask but more than we can imagine.

**2. According to the power at work within us.** This is the power of 1:19–20, the power that raised Christ from the dead. God's power is not only in heaven; it is at work in our hearts.

**3. Glory in the church and in Christ Jesus.** "To him be glory in the church and in Christ Jesus throughout all generations, forever and ever. Amen" (3:21). The goal of everything is God's glory. The church stands beside Christ because the church is Christ's body; God is glorified in Christ and in the people he has redeemed, forever.

> The point: What God can do is beyond all we ask or think, and his power is at work in us now. Everything moves toward one goal: glory to God in the church and in Christ Jesus, forever and ever.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "May have strength to comprehend with all the saints what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge" (3:18–19). Have I "known" for a long time that Christ loves me? I sang "Jesus loves me, this I know" in Sunday school and have heard it from the pulpit countless times. Yet in my darkest moments, when I fall again into the same sin, or when I am hurt by others and feel forgotten by God, those words seem only a doctrine that never reaches me. Perhaps I secretly think: God loves the world, perhaps he loves the spiritual people, but someone like me? Paul prayed for the Ephesians not that they would learn one more truth, but that they would "comprehend" a love that "surpasses knowledge." How long is this love? From "before the foundation of the world" (1:4) to "all generations, forever and ever" (3:21). How broad? Wide enough to take in Gentiles once "having no hope and without God" (2:12). How deep? Deep enough that Christ went down to the cross and bore God's wrath in my place (2:16). How high? High enough that he has "seated us with him in the heavenly places" (2:6). Even at my lowest and worst, I have not fallen outside this love. Today, can I read 3:16–19 slowly several times, as though Paul were praying it for me, and ask the Spirit to let me not only know this love but truly taste it? Perhaps I could also go to a brother or sister, tell them where I find it hard to believe God loves me, and ask them to pray with me, since this love is to be grasped "with all the saints." Even if today I still cannot feel it, Christ's love is not one bit smaller for that; the Lord who gave his life for me on the cross wants me to know his love more urgently than I do.
