## 本章脉络

第四章是全书的转折。前三章讲神在基督里为我们成就了什么；从这一章起，保罗说：「所以」，你们要这样行事为人。本章可以分成四段。**保守合一**（1-6节）：用谦虚、温柔、忍耐和爱心，「竭力保守圣灵所赐合而为一的心」，因为只有一个身体、一位圣灵、一主、一信、一洗、一神。**各样的恩赐**（7-16节）：升天的基督把恩赐赏给人，有使徒、先知、传福音的、牧师和教师，「为要成全圣徒，各尽其职，建立基督的身体」，叫我们「用爱心说诚实话」，长大成人，连于元首基督。**脱去旧人，穿上新人**（17-24节）：不要再像外邦人存虚妄的心行事，要脱去旧人，心志改换一新，穿上照着神形象造的新人。**新人的样式**（25-32节）：说实话，生气却不犯罪，不再偷窃，说造就人的好话，不叫圣灵担忧，「彼此饶恕，正如神在基督里饶恕了你们一样」。

注意这一章的命令都有理由：「因为我们是互相为肢体」（25节），「你们原是受了他的印记」（30节），「正如神在基督里饶恕了你们」（32节）。恩典在先，命令在后。

## 深读 4:1-6 竭力保守合而为一的心

**1. 与蒙召的恩相称。** 「我为主被囚的劝你们：既然蒙召，行事为人就当与蒙召的恩相称」（4:1）。「相称」这个词，许多人解释为天平两边重量相当。神的呼召在先，这呼召是恩典（1:18；2:8-10）；我们的生活要与这份恩典相称，像衣服合身一样。这不是要我们去赚取呼召，而是要我们活出已经蒙召的身份。

**2. 合一的品格。** 「凡事谦虚、温柔、忍耐，用爱心互相宽容」（4:2）。在希腊罗马的世界里，谦卑常被看作奴仆的卑贱，不是美德；是基督自己「虚己」「存心顺服」（腓2:7-8），才使谦卑成了神百姓的荣美。合一不是靠组织或口号维持的，而是靠这样的品格。「互相宽容」原文是彼此忍耐、担待：合一不是因为没有摩擦，而是因为在摩擦中仍然彼此担待。

**3. 保守，不是制造。** 「用和平彼此联络，竭力保守圣灵所赐合而为一的心」（4:3）。合一是「圣灵所赐」的，基督已经在十字架上把我们合而为一（2:14-16）；我们的责任是「保守」它，而且要「竭力」，因为合一很容易破裂。

**4. 七个「一」。** 「身体只有一个，圣灵只有一个，正如你们蒙召同有一个指望。一主，一信，一洗，一神，就是众人的父」（4:4-6）。这里又看见三一神：一位圣灵，一位主（基督），一位神和父。教会的合一建立在神自己的合一上。合一不在于大家意见完全相同，而在于我们同有一位神。

> 要点：神已经藉着基督和圣灵使我们合而为一。我们的责任是用谦虚、温柔、忍耐和爱心，竭力保守这份合一，因为我们同有一主、一信、一洗、一神。

## 深读 4:7-16 各尽其职，建立基督的身体

**1. 照基督所量给的恩赐。** 「我们各人蒙恩，都是照基督所量给各人的恩赐」（4:7）。合一不是人人都一样。每一个信徒都从基督领受了恩赐，没有一个人是多余的。

**2. 升上高天，赏赐给人。** 「所以经上说：他升上高天的时候，掳掠了仇敌，将各样的恩赐赏给人」（4:8；引诗68:18）。诗篇68:18说的是「受了供献」，保罗却说「赏给人」。解经家有几种解释。一种是：犹太人的亚兰文意译本（他尔根）在这里也作「赐给」，并把这节用在摩西上西奈山领受律法的事上。这译本成书虽晚于保罗，却可能保存了更早的解释传统。另一种是：保罗在总结整首诗的意思，得胜的王接受了贡物，就分给他的百姓（参徒2:33，基督从父受了圣灵，就浇灌下来）。

**3. 先降在地下。** 「既说升上，岂不是先降在地下吗？」（4:9）「地下」的意思，解经家有几种看法：
- 许多人认为是指基督道成肉身，降到地上这卑微之处来；
- 有人认为是指基督死后降到阴间（参彼前3:19）；
- 也有人（如霍尔·哈里斯）认为是指基督升天以后，在五旬节藉着圣灵降下来，把恩赐赏给人。
第一种看法最常见。无论哪一种，重点都是：那降卑的，就是那「远升诸天之上要充满万有的」（4:10）。

**4. 成全圣徒，各尽其职。** 「他所赐的，有使徒，有先知，有传福音的，有牧师和教师，为要成全圣徒，各尽其职，建立基督的身体」（4:11-12）。「牧师和教师」可能指同一群人的两方面工作，也可能指两种相近的职分。「成全」这个词，同一字根的动词也用来指补网（太4:21），有装备、修整的意思。这一节的三个短语怎样连接，解经家有不同看法：许多人（如哈罗德·霍纳）认为「各尽其职」是圣徒的工作，和合本也这样翻译；也有人（如大卫·戈登）认为三个短语都在描写牧者和教师自己的工作。无论哪一种，4:7和4:16都清楚说明，每一个肢体都有恩赐，都要发挥功用。牧者和教师的工作，不是代替圣徒做一切的事工，而是装备每一个圣徒去尽他的职分。教会不是少数人表演、多数人观看的地方。

**5. 长大成人。** 「直等到我们众人在真道上同归于一，认识神的儿子，得以长大成人，满有基督长成的身量」（4:13）。目标不是教会人数的增长，而是像基督。不成熟的人像小孩子，「被一切异教之风摇动，飘来飘去」（4:14）；成熟的人站稳在真道上。

**6. 用爱心说诚实话。** 「惟用爱心说诚实话，凡事长进，连于元首基督」（4:15）。原文直译是：在爱中持守（或说出）真理。只有真理没有爱，会变得尖刻；只有爱没有真理，会变得软弱、不敢说话。成长需要两样都有。

**7. 全身彼此相助。** 「全身都靠他联络得合式，百节各按各职，照着各体的功用彼此相助，便叫身体渐渐增长，在爱中建立自己」（4:16）。生命从元首基督流出来，经过每一个关节，叫全身长大。身体的健康，是元首基督的生命流经每一个肢体；连软弱、只能祷告的肢体，也在其中有分。

> 要点：升天的基督把恩赐赏给每一个信徒，又把牧者和教师赐给教会，装备圣徒各尽其职。教会的目标是众人在真道上合一，用爱心说诚实话，长成基督的身量。

## 深读 4:17-24 脱去旧人，穿上新人

**1. 外邦人存虚妄的心。** 「你们行事不要再像外邦人存虚妄的心行事。他们心地昏昧，与神所赐的生命隔绝了，都因自己无知，心里刚硬」（4:17-18）。许多解经家认为这里有先后次序：心里刚硬，导致无知；无知导致心地昏昧，与神的生命隔绝。人的问题不只在头脑，更在心里。

**2. 良心丧尽，放纵私欲。** 「良心既然丧尽，就放纵私欲，贪行种种的污秽」（4:19）。「良心既然丧尽」的「丧尽」，原文有失去感觉的意思，好像皮肤结了厚茧，不再觉得痛。罪一开始叫人不安，后来叫人麻木，最后叫人贪得无厌。

**3. 你们学了基督。** 「你们学了基督，却不是这样」（4:20）。保罗没有说你们学了基督的道理，而是说「你们学了基督」。基督徒的成长不只是学一套规矩，而是认识一个人，就是基督自己，「学了他的真理」（4:21）。

**4. 脱去，改换，穿上。** 「就要脱去你们从前行为上的旧人……又要将你们的心志改换一新，并且穿上新人；这新人是照着神的形象造的，有真理的仁义和圣洁」（4:22-24）。这几句话怎样理解，解经家有两种主要的看法：
- 有人（如约翰·默里）认为，这是描述信徒在归信、与基督联合的时候，已经脱去了旧人，穿上了新人（参罗6:6；西3:9-10）；所以现在要照着这个新身份生活。默里的根据之一是，本章25节「弃绝」一词，原文也是表示已完成的形式（西3:9和合本译作「已经脱去」）；
- 也有人认为，这几个动词带着命令的意思，是信徒每天都要做的事：天天脱去旧的习惯，穿上新的样式。和合本译作「就要脱去」，许多注释书也这样理解。
两种看法其实可以连在一起：在基督里，我们已经是新人；所以我们天天要活出新人的样子。「新人」是「照着神的形象造的」，神在基督里重新创造我们，恢复我们起初被造的样式。

> 要点：人离开神的根源在于心里刚硬，罪使人越来越麻木。信徒「学了基督」，在他里面已经是照神形象重新被造的新人，所以要脱去旧人的样式，心志更新，活出新人的仁义和圣洁。

## 深读 4:25-32 新人的样式

**1. 说实话，因为互为肢体。** 「所以，你们要弃绝谎言，各人与邻舍说实话，因为我们是互相为肢体」（4:25；参亚8:16）。说谎伤害的不只是别人，也是自己的身体：手骗了眼睛，最后伤的是整个人。

**2. 生气却不要犯罪。** 「生气却不要犯罪；不可含怒到日落，也不可给魔鬼留地步」（4:26-27）。前半句引自诗4:4的七十士译本（旧约希腊文译本）；和合本诗4:4按希伯来文译作「应当畏惧」。圣经没有说一切的怒气都是罪；对罪和不公义发怒，有时是对的（参可3:5）。但怒气很容易变成罪：报复、记恨、苦毒。「不可含怒到日落」是说要及早处理，不要让怒气过夜，变成仇恨，给魔鬼留下破口。

**3. 从偷窃到分享。** 「从前偷窃的，不要再偷；总要劳力，亲手做正经事，就可有余分给那缺少的人」（4:28）。福音的改变不只是「不再偷」，而是从拿走别人的东西，变成为了给别人而工作。

**4. 叫听见的人得益处。** 「污秽的言语一句不可出口，只要随事说造就人的好话，叫听见的人得益处」（4:29）。「得益处」原文是：得着恩典。我们的话可以成为恩典的管道。

**5. 不要叫圣灵担忧。** 「不要叫神的圣灵担忧；你们原是受了他的印记，等候得赎的日子来到」（4:30）。圣灵是有位格的，他就是神自己，不是一种力量（参赛63:10「使主的圣灵担忧」）；他会「担忧」。注意这句话夹在论说话之罪的经文中间：苦毒、伤人的话，使住在教会中的圣灵担忧。但保罗也提醒：我们已经「受了他的印记」，直到得赎的日子（参1:13-14）。叫他担忧的信徒，并没有因此失去他的印记。

**6. 正如神在基督里饶恕了你们。** 「一切苦毒、恼恨、忿怒、嚷闹、毁谤，并一切的恶毒，都当从你们中间除掉；并要以恩慈相待，存怜悯的心，彼此饶恕，正如神在基督里饶恕了你们一样」（4:31-32）。饶恕的根据和样式，都是神在基督里对我们的饶恕。我们欠神的，远比别人欠我们的多（参太18:21-35）。
- 饶恕不是假装没有发生，也不是说罪不严重。饶恕是把报复的权柄交给神（罗12:19），不再拿对方的罪来辖制他。
- 饶恕也不等于马上恢复信任或关系。如果遇到虐待、家暴或犯罪，向警察或法院举报是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人不必继续留在危险中，也不必与不安全的人和好；饶恕不会恢复施暴者的职分，也不会让他重新接近受害者或孩子。教会绝不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而隐瞒虐待。

> 要点：新人的生活从新的身份流出来：因为我们互为肢体，所以说实话；因为我们受了圣灵的印记，所以不叫他担忧；因为神在基督里饶恕了我们，所以我们彼此饶恕。

## 默想

**默想：** 「惟用爱心说诚实话，凡事长进，连于元首基督」（4:15）。在我和别人的关系中，我比较容易缺少哪一样：爱心，还是诚实？也许我有一句早就该说的话一直没有说：对一位走偏的弟兄，对一个在小组里常常伤人的同工，对一位在某件事上需要被提醒的长辈。我告诉自己「以和为贵」「不好意思开口」，其实是怕关系变僵，怕丢面子，怕别人不喜欢我。又或者，我常常说实话，却说得又快又重，伤了人还觉得自己只是「直」。保罗说，这两种都不能叫身体长进。可是我怎么能做到呢？我先要看见，基督就是这样待我的。他「充充满满地有恩典有真理」（约1:14）：他没有假装我的罪不存在，却为这罪舍了自己；他对我说的最重的实话，是我死在过犯中（2:1），他对我说的最温柔的话，是「你们得救是本乎恩」（2:5）。我能不能先在他面前承认，我的沉默常常是爱自己多过爱对方，我的直言常常是骄傲多过爱心？然后求主给我智慧和勇气，找一个合适的时候，带着祷告，把那句话温柔地说出来。（如果对方是会伤害人、不安全的人，不要独自去面对，要寻求牧者或专业人士的帮助。）如果我曾经说得太重、伤了人，我也可以先去向他认错。我说话的时候，不是站在对方上面，而是站在他旁边，因为我们同是蒙了饶恕的人，都要一同长进，连于元首基督。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 is the turning point of the letter. Chapters 1–3 tell what God has done for us in Christ; from here Paul says, "therefore," walk like this. The chapter falls into four parts. **Maintaining unity** (vv. 1–6): with humility, gentleness, patience and love, be "eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit," for there is one body, one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God. **Gifts of grace** (vv. 7–16): the ascended Christ gave gifts, the apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors and teachers, "to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ," so that "speaking the truth in love" we grow up into Christ the head. **Putting off the old self, putting on the new** (vv. 17–24): no longer walk as the Gentiles do, in the futility of their minds; put off the old self, be renewed in the spirit of your minds, and put on the new self, created after the likeness of God. **The new self in practice** (vv. 25–32): speak the truth; be angry and do not sin; no more stealing; speak words that build up; do not grieve the Holy Spirit; forgive "one another, as God in Christ forgave you."

Notice that the commands in this chapter come with reasons: "for we are members one of another" (v. 25), "by whom you were sealed" (v. 30), "as God in Christ forgave you" (v. 32). Grace comes first, commands follow.

## Going Deeper 4:1–6 Eager to Maintain the Unity of the Spirit

**1. Worthy of the calling.** "I therefore, a prisoner for the Lord, urge you to walk in a manner worthy of the calling to which you have been called" (4:1). The word for "worthy" is often said to carry the image of a balance with equal weight on both sides. God's call comes first, and it is grace (1:18; 2:8–10); our life is to fit that grace, as clothes fit the one who wears them. Paul is not telling us to earn the calling but to live out who we already are as those called.

**2. The character of unity.** "With all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love" (4:2). In the Greco-Roman world "humility" was often seen as the lowliness of a slave, not a virtue. It was Christ himself, who "made himself nothing" and "became obedient" (Phil 2:7–8), who made humility the beauty of God's people. Unity is kept not by organization or slogans but by this kind of character. "Bearing with one another" means putting up with each other: unity is not the absence of friction but bearing with one another in the middle of it.

**3. Maintaining, not manufacturing.** "Eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace" (4:3). Unity is "of the Spirit"; Christ has already made us one at the cross (2:14–16). Our task is to "maintain" it, and to be "eager" to, because it is easily broken.

**4. Seven "ones."** "There is one body and one Spirit- just as you were called to the one hope that belongs to your call- one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all" (4:4–6). Again the triune God appears: one Spirit, one Lord (Christ), one God and Father. The church's unity rests on the unity of God himself. Our unity does not mean everyone agrees on everything, but that we share one God.

> The point: God has already made us one through Christ and the Spirit. Our task is to be eager to maintain that unity with humility, gentleness, patience and love, because we share one Lord, one faith, one baptism and one God.

## Going Deeper 4:7–16 Building Up the Body of Christ

**1. According to the measure of Christ's gift.** "But grace was given to each one of us according to the measure of Christ's gift" (4:7). Unity does not mean sameness. Every believer has received a gift from Christ; no one is superfluous.

**2. He ascended and gave gifts.** "Therefore it says, 'When he ascended on high he led a host of captives, and he gave gifts to men'" (4:8; quoting Ps 68:18). Psalm 68:18 says "receiving gifts," but Paul says "gave gifts." Interpreters explain this in several ways: an ancient Jewish Aramaic paraphrase (the Targum) also reads "gave" here; though written down later than Paul, it may preserve an older Jewish reading that applied the verse to Moses going up Sinai; others think Paul sums up the sense of the whole psalm, where the victorious king receives tribute and distributes it to his people (cf. Acts 2:33, where Christ receives the Spirit from the Father and pours him out).

**3. He had also descended.** "In saying, 'He ascended,' what does it mean but that he had also descended into the lower parts of the earth?" (4:9). Interpreters read "the lower parts of the earth" in several ways:
- many take it as Christ's incarnation, his coming down to the lowly earth;
- some take it as Christ's descent to the realm of the dead after his death (cf. 1 Pet 3:19);
- others (such as W. Hall Harris) take it as Christ, after his ascension, coming down by the Spirit at Pentecost to give gifts.
The first view is the most common. Either way, the point is that the One who humbled himself is the one "who also ascended far above all the heavens, that he might fill all things" (4:10).

**4. Equipping the saints.** "And he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the pastors and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ" (4:11–12). "Pastors and teachers" may be two sides of one group's work, or two closely related offices. A related verb is used for mending nets (Matt 4:21); it carries the sense of preparing and restoring. Interpreters differ on how the three phrases of 4:12 connect. Many (e.g., Harold Hoehner) take "the work of ministry" as the saints' work, as the CUV does; others (e.g., T. David Gordon) take all three phrases as describing the leaders' own work. Either way, 4:7 and 4:16 make plain that every member is gifted and has a part to play. The work of pastors and teachers is not to do all the ministry in place of the saints but to equip every saint for his or her work. The church is not a place where a few perform and the many watch.

**5. Growing to maturity.** "Until we all attain to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to mature manhood, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ" (4:13). The goal is not numerical growth but likeness to Christ. The immature are like children, "tossed to and fro by the waves and carried about by every wind of doctrine" (4:14); the mature stand firm in the truth.

**6. Speaking the truth in love.** "Rather, speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ" (4:15). The Greek can be rendered "truthing in love": holding to, or speaking, the truth in love. Truth without love turns harsh; love without truth turns weak and afraid to speak. Growth needs both.

**7. Every part working.** "From whom the whole body, joined and held together by every joint with which it is equipped, when each part is working properly, makes the body grow so that it builds itself up in love" (4:16). Life flows from Christ the head through every joint and makes the whole body grow. The body's health is Christ's life flowing through every member, and even weak members who can only pray have their part.

> The point: The ascended Christ has given gifts to every believer, and has given pastors and teachers to the church to equip the saints for ministry. The goal of the church is unity in the faith, speaking the truth in love, and growing to the stature of Christ.

## Going Deeper 4:17–24 Put Off the Old Self, Put On the New

**1. The futility of the Gentiles' minds.** "You must no longer walk as the Gentiles do, in the futility of their minds. They are darkened in their understanding, alienated from the life of God because of the ignorance that is in them, due to their hardness of heart" (4:17–18). Many interpreters see an order here: hardness of heart leads to ignorance, and ignorance to a darkened understanding cut off from the life of God. The human problem lies not only in the head but in the heart.

**2. Callous and given over.** "They have become callous and have given themselves up to sensuality, greedy to practice every kind of impurity" (4:19). "Callous" means losing feeling, like skin grown thick and no longer feeling pain. Sin first troubles a person, then numbs him, and finally makes him insatiable.

**3. You learned Christ.** "But that is not the way you learned Christ!" (4:20). Paul does not say "you learned Christ's teaching" but "you learned Christ." Christian growth is not only learning a set of rules but knowing a person, Christ himself, "as the truth is in Jesus" (4:21).

**4. Put off, be renewed, put on.** "To put off your old self, which belongs to your former manner of life… and to be renewed in the spirit of your minds, and to put on the new self, created after the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness" (4:22–24). Interpreters read these lines in two main ways:
- some (such as John Murray) take them to describe what believers have already done at conversion, in union with Christ: they put off the old self and put on the new (cf. Rom 6:6; Col 3:9–10); so they are now to live by this new identity. Murray points to the past-tense participles in Col 3:9–10 and in 4:25 ("having put away falsehood");
- others take the infinitives as carrying the force of commands, as the CUV's 就要脱去 and many commentaries do, describing what believers must do every day: daily putting off old habits and putting on new ways.
The two readings belong together: in Christ we are already new; therefore every day we live out the new self. The new self is "created after the likeness of God"; in Christ God creates us anew, restoring the likeness for which we were first made.

> The point: The root of turning from God is a hardened heart, and sin makes people ever more numb. Believers have "learned Christ"; in him they are already a new self, created anew in God's likeness, so they put off the old ways, are renewed in mind, and live out the righteousness and holiness of the new self.

## Going Deeper 4:25–32 The New Self in Practice

**1. Speak the truth, for we are members of one another.** "Therefore, having put away falsehood, let each one of you speak the truth with his neighbor, for we are members one of another" (4:25; cf. Zech 8:16). Lying harms not only others but one's own body: if the hand deceives the eye, the whole person is hurt.

**2. Be angry and do not sin.** "Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger, and give no opportunity to the devil" (4:26–27; quoting Ps 4:4 in the Greek Old Testament). Scripture does not say all anger is sin; anger at sin and injustice is sometimes right (cf. Mark 3:5). But anger easily turns into sin: revenge, grudges, bitterness. "Do not let the sun go down on your anger" means dealing with it early, not letting it stay overnight and harden into hatred, opening a door for the devil.

**3. From stealing to sharing.** "Let the thief no longer steal, but rather let him labor, doing honest work with his own hands, so that he may have something to share with anyone in need" (4:28). The gospel's change is not only "no more stealing" but turning from taking what belongs to others to working in order to give to others.

**4. Giving grace to those who hear.** "Let no corrupting talk come out of your mouths, but only such as is good for building up, as fits the occasion, that it may give grace to those who hear" (4:29). Our words can become channels of grace.

**5. Do not grieve the Holy Spirit.** "And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption" (4:30). The Spirit is personal, God himself, not a force (cf. Isa 63:10, "grieved his Holy Spirit"); he can be "grieved." Notice that Paul says this in the middle of sins of speech: bitterness and hurtful words grieve the Spirit who dwells in the church. Yet Paul also reminds us that the Spirit has "sealed" us for the day of redemption (cf. 1:13–14). A believer who grieves him has not thereby lost his seal.

**6. As God in Christ forgave you.** "Let all bitterness and wrath and anger and clamor and slander be put away from you, along with all malice. Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you" (4:31–32). Both the ground and the pattern of forgiveness are God's forgiveness of us in Christ. What we owed God far exceeds what anyone owes us (cf. Matt 18:21–35).
- Forgiveness is not pretending nothing happened, nor saying the sin is not serious. Forgiveness hands the right of vengeance over to God (Rom 12:19) and no longer uses the other person's sin to control them.
- Forgiveness also does not mean trust or relationship is immediately restored. Where there is abuse, domestic violence or crime, reporting it to the police or the courts is right (Rom 13:4). Victims need not stay in danger or reconcile with someone unsafe. Forgiveness does not restore an abuser's office or give him access again to victims or children. The church must never hide abuse to avoid "airing the family's dirty laundry."

> The point: The new life flows from a new identity: because we are members of one another, we speak the truth; because the Spirit has sealed us, we do not grieve him; because God in Christ forgave us, we forgive one another.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Rather, speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ" (4:15). In my relationships, which do I more easily lack: love, or truth? Perhaps there is something I should have said long ago and never have: to a brother who is drifting, to a co-worker in my small group who often hurts others, to an older believer who needs a gentle reminder about something. I tell myself "keeping the peace matters most," or "it's awkward to bring it up," but in truth I am afraid the relationship will sour, afraid of losing face, afraid of not being liked. Or perhaps I often speak the truth, but quickly and heavily, wounding people and telling myself I am just being "direct." Paul says neither helps the body grow. But how can I do this? First I need to see that this is how Christ treats me. He is "full of grace and truth" (John 1:14). He did not pretend my sin was not there, yet he gave himself for that sin. The hardest truth he told me is that I was dead in my trespasses (2:1); the gentlest word he spoke to me is "by grace you have been saved" (2:5). Can I first admit before him that my silence is often love of self more than love of the other, and my bluntness often pride more than love? Then I can ask the Lord for wisdom and courage to find a fitting time and, with prayer, say that word gently. (If the other person is harmful or unsafe, do not face them alone; seek the help of a pastor or a professional.) And if I have already spoken too harshly and hurt someone, I can go to him first and own it. When I speak, I do not stand above the other person but beside him, because we are both forgiven people, growing up together into Christ our head.
