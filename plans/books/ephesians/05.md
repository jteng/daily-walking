## 本章脉络

第五章继续讲新人怎样「行事为人」，可以分成四段。**凭爱心行事**（1-2节）：「你们该效法神，好像蒙慈爱的儿女一样」，「正如基督爱我们，为我们舍了自己」。**像光明的子女**（3-14节）：淫乱、污秽和贪婪「连提都不可」；「从前你们是暗昧的，但如今在主里面是光明的」，所以要察验何为主所喜悦的，揭露暗昧的事。**像智慧人，被圣灵充满**（15-21节）：爱惜光阴，明白主的旨意；「不要醉酒……乃要被圣灵充满」，彼此对说，歌唱，感谢，「存敬畏基督的心，彼此顺服」。**丈夫与妻子**（22-33节）：妻子顺服丈夫，如同教会顺服基督；丈夫爱妻子，「正如基督爱教会，为教会舍己」；保罗说，婚姻是「极大的奥秘」，是指着基督和教会说的。

「行事为人」在这一章出现了三次：凭爱心行事（2节）、像光明的子女行事（8节）、谨慎行事（15节）。每一次的根据都是基督已经为我们做成的事。

## 深读 5:1-2 效法神，凭爱心行事

**1. 好像蒙慈爱的儿女。** 「所以，你们该效法神，好像蒙慈爱的儿女一样」（5:1）。新约中，「效法神」这个说法只出现在这里。孩子模仿父亲，不是为了赢得父亲的爱，而是因为已经被爱。「所以」连到上一节：「正如神在基督里饶恕了你们」（4:32）。被饶恕的人，学着像饶恕他的父那样去饶恕人。

**2. 正如基督为我们舍了自己。** 「也要凭爱心行事，正如基督爱我们，为我们舍了自己，当作馨香的供物和祭物，献与神」（5:2）。爱的标准不是我们的感觉，而是十字架。「馨香的供物和祭物」是旧约献祭的语言（参出29:18；利1:9）：基督把自己献上，替我们承担了罪的刑罚，这祭物蒙神悦纳。我们所有的爱，都是从这个爱流出来的。

> 要点：我们效法神，不是为了得着他的爱，而是因为我们已经是他所爱的儿女。爱的样式就是基督：他爱我们，为我们舍己，作了蒙神悦纳的祭物。

## 深读 5:3-14 如今在主里面是光明的

**1. 连提都不可。** 「至于淫乱并一切污秽，或是贪婪，在你们中间连提都不可，方合圣徒的体统」（5:3）。在希腊罗马世界，异教的节期和宴席常常伴随着性的放纵。保罗把性的罪和「贪婪」放在一起，因为两样都是把别人或东西当作满足自己的工具。「淫词、妄语，和戏笑的话都不相宜；总要说感谢的话」（5:4）：对付不洁之言最好的，不只是闭口，更是感恩。

**2. 贪心就是拜偶像。** 「有贪心的，就与拜偶像的一样」（5:5）。贪心为什么是拜偶像？因为人把本该从神那里寻求的满足、安全和价值，寄托在钱财或别的东西上。偶像不一定是庙里的神像；凡是人心里离不开、取代了神的东西，都可能成为偶像。

**3. 不要被虚浮的话欺哄。** 「不要被人虚浮的话欺哄；因这些事，神的忿怒必临到那悖逆之子」（5:6）。当时可能有人说：身体的事无关紧要，神不会在意。保罗说，这是虚浮的话。神是圣洁的，他必审判罪。

**4. 从暗昧到光明。** 「从前你们是暗昧的，但如今在主里面是光明的，行事为人就当像光明的子女」（5:8）。注意保罗不是说你们从前在黑暗中，而是说你们从前「是」黑暗，如今「是」光明。这是身份的改变。「光明所结的果子就是一切良善、公义、诚实」（5:9）：光明的子女要「察验何为主所喜悦的事」（5:10）。

**5. 揭露暗昧的事。** 「那暗昧无益的事，不要与人同行，倒要责备行这事的人」（5:11）。「责备」也可以译作揭露。光明的子女不只是自己不做，也不替黑暗遮掩。在教会里，这也包括不可为了面子而遮掩性侵、虐待这样的事。

**6. 睡着的人当醒过来。** 「所以主说：你这睡着的人当醒过来，从死里复活！基督就要光照你了」（5:14）。这句话不是直接引自旧约某一节，许多解经家认为是初期教会的一首诗歌，可能在洗礼时唱，用了以赛亚书的意象（参赛26:19；60:1）。被光照的人，自己也成了光。

> 要点：信徒从前是暗昧的，如今在主里是光明的。所以要远离性的不洁和贪心这种拜偶像的罪，察验主所喜悦的，并且揭露暗昧的事。基督的光照亮了我们，也叫我们发光。

## 深读 5:15-21 不要醉酒，乃要被圣灵充满

**1. 爱惜光阴。** 「你们要谨慎行事，不要像愚昧人，当像智慧人。要爱惜光阴，因为现今的世代邪恶」（5:15-16）。「爱惜光阴」原文有赎回时间、把握机会的意思。时日邪恶，所以每一个行善的机会都很宝贵。

**2. 醉酒与圣灵充满。** 「不要醉酒，酒能使人放荡；乃要被圣灵充满」（5:18）。保罗把两种「充满」作对比。喝醉的人被酒控制，失去自制；被圣灵充满的人，被神的灵引导，结出圣灵的果子，其中也包括节制（加5:23）。有些人认为保罗可能也想到当时酒神的崇拜，在醉酒中寻求宗教的狂热；但这只是可能的背景。
- **「被……充满」：** 原文是现在式的被动命令，许多人认为这强调持续不断：不是一次的经历，而是一直不断地让圣灵来充满。我们不能自己充满自己，只能敞开自己，降服在他的引导之下。

**3. 被圣灵充满的样子。** 原文接下来的几个分词，描写被圣灵充满的人：
- **彼此对说：** 「当用诗章、颂词、灵歌彼此对说，口唱心和地赞美主」（5:19）。圣灵充满首先表现在敬拜和彼此造就上，不是个人的神秘经验；
- **常常感谢：** 「凡事要奉我们主耶稣基督的名常常感谢父神」（5:20）。这里又看见三一神：被圣灵充满，奉圣子的名，感谢圣父；
- **彼此顺服：** 「又当存敬畏基督的心，彼此顺服」（5:21）。

**4. 存敬畏基督的心，彼此顺服。** 这一节是下面讲家庭关系（5:22–6:9）的引言。怎样理解「彼此顺服」，福音派学者有不同的看法：
- **有人认为**（如克雷格·基纳等平等派学者）：这是所有信徒之间的彼此顺服，丈夫和妻子都要彼此顺服；下面的经文是这个原则在家里的应用。
- **也有人认为**（如韦恩·古德恩等互补派学者）：「彼此」在这里是一些人对另一些人，就是在神所设立的各种关系中，各人照着自己的位分顺服当顺服的人；下面列出的就是这些关系。
- **还有许多学者认为**：5:21是所有信徒之间一般性的彼此谦让，但5:22-33仍然给丈夫和妻子不同的责任。
平等派和互补派真正的分歧，主要在于怎样理解下面5:22-33的「头」和「顺服」。无论哪一种，顺服的动机都是「敬畏基督」；在基督的群体里，没有人可以骄傲地只要求别人，自己却不谦卑。

> 要点：在邪恶的世代中，智慧人爱惜光阴，明白主的旨意。他们不是被酒或别的东西控制，而是持续地被圣灵充满，表现在一同敬拜、常常感谢和存敬畏基督的心彼此顺服上。

## 深读 5:22-33 极大的奥秘：基督和教会

**1. 妻子当顺服，如同顺服主。** 「你们作妻子的，当顺服自己的丈夫，如同顺服主」（5:22）。在最早、最好的几份抄本里，这一节没有动词（后来的抄本补上了「顺服」），「顺服」是从上一节借来的：妻子的顺服是被圣灵充满、彼此顺服的一种表现。保罗直接对妻子说话，把她看作有责任、有尊严的人，而不是丈夫的财产。「如同顺服主」：妻子顺服丈夫，是出于对主的顺服。
- **顺服不是什么：** 顺服不是说妻子比丈夫低一等（参彼前3:7，夫妻「一同承受生命之恩」），不是说她要顺从丈夫去犯罪（参徒5:29），也绝不是说她要忍受虐待。

**2. 丈夫当爱妻子，正如基督为教会舍己。** 「你们作丈夫的，要爱你们的妻子，正如基督爱教会，为教会舍己」（5:25）。在当时的社会里，叫妻子顺服并不稀奇；叫丈夫像基督那样为妻子舍己，才是革命性的。「头」的意思（5:23），学者有不同看法：互补派（如古德恩）认为主要指带领和权柄，有些平等派学者认为指「源头」。无论哪一种，都要从基督来看：基督作教会的头，又是「教会全体的救主」（5:23），他的权柄是用为教会舍己、洗净她、保养顾惜她来施行的（5:25-29），从来不是作威作福。丈夫若用「头」来压制、控制或伤害妻子，就完全违背了这段经文。

**3. 一个荣耀的教会。** 「要用水藉着道把教会洗净，成为圣洁，可以献给自己，作个荣耀的教会，毫无玷污、皱纹等类的病，乃是圣洁没有瑕疵的」（5:26-27）。这里的「水」可能指洗礼，也可能是用当时新娘出嫁前沐浴的风俗作比喻；「道」是福音的话。基督的爱不只是接纳我们本来的样子，而是要把我们变得荣美。我们满身的玷污和皱纹，基督都亲自洗净，好在那日把我们当作荣耀的新妇献给自己（参启19:7-8；21:2）。

**4. 极大的奥秘。** 「为这个缘故，人要离开父母，与妻子连合，二人成为一体。这是极大的奥秘，但我是指着基督和教会说的」（5:31-32；引创2:24）。神设立婚姻的时候，心里就想着基督和教会。婚姻是一幅图画，指向一个更大的实体：基督对他的子民永不改变的爱。所以每一个基督徒，无论已婚、单身、离婚还是丧偶，都在这个最深的「婚姻」里，是基督所爱、所洗净的新妇的一部分。

**5. 安全的提醒。** 这段经文常常被误用，所以必须说清楚：如果婚姻中有家暴、性暴力或其他虐待，向警察或法院求助是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人可以离开，到安全的地方去，不必为了「顺服」而留在危险中，也不必与不安全的人和好；教会的责任是保护受害者，绝不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而劝她忍耐或替施暴者遮掩。饶恕也不会恢复施暴者在教会中的职分，或让他重新接近受害者和孩子。丈夫也可能是受害者，同样应当得到保护。

> 要点：妻子顺服丈夫，如同顺服主；丈夫爱妻子，正如基督为教会舍己。婚姻最深的意义，是指向基督和教会：基督为他的新妇舍命，洗净她，要把她作为荣耀的教会献给自己。

## 默想

**默想：** 「不要醉酒，酒能使人放荡；乃要被圣灵充满」（5:18）。一天结束的时候，我心里空了、累了、烦了，我会拿什么来把自己「填满」？也许不是酒，而是手机，一个短视频接一个短视频，一直滑到深夜；也许是游戏、追剧、网购，或者一包零食；也许是工作，好叫我不必面对心里的空虚。这些东西本身不一定是罪，可是当我靠它们来逃避、来麻醉自己的时候，它们就在控制我，而且填得越多，心里越空。保罗没有只说「不要」，他说「乃要被圣灵充满」。那位「充满万有者」（1:23），就是基督，愿意藉着他的圣灵充满我。他不是要我更努力地管住自己，而是要我把空空的心敞开给他，让他来充满。我能不能今天晚上，在伸手拿手机或别的东西以前，先停下来，承认：主啊，我心里空了，我常常拿别的东西来填，求你赦免；求你的灵充满我？然后读一首诗篇，或者唱一首诗歌，为今天的一件事感谢父神（5:19-20）。如果我已经被某样东西辖制得很深，例如酒、赌博或色情，我不必独自挣扎，可以找一位可信的弟兄姊妹或牧者，请他陪我一同走。那位为我舍了自己的基督（5:2），不是要夺走我的喜乐，而是要把他自己赐给我，叫我的心真正被填满。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 continues showing how the new self is to "walk," in four parts. **Walk in love** (vv. 1–2): "be imitators of God, as beloved children," "as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us." **Walk as children of light** (vv. 3–14): sexual immorality, impurity and covetousness "must not even be named among you"; "at one time you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord," so discern what pleases the Lord and expose the works of darkness. **Walk as wise, filled with the Spirit** (vv. 15–21): make the best use of the time and understand the Lord's will; "do not get drunk with wine… but be filled with the Spirit," speaking to one another, singing, giving thanks, "submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ." **Husbands and wives** (vv. 22–33): wives submit to their husbands as the church submits to Christ; husbands love their wives "as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her"; and Paul says marriage is a "profound" mystery that "refers to Christ and the church."

"Walk" appears three times in this chapter: walk in love (v. 2), walk as children of light (v. 8), look carefully how you walk (v. 15). Each time the ground is what Christ has already done for us.

## Going Deeper 5:1–2 Imitators of God, Walking in Love

**1. As beloved children.** "Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children" (5:1). Only here does the New Testament use the phrase "imitators of God." A child imitates his father not to win his father's love but because he is already loved. "Therefore" ties back to the previous verse: "as God in Christ forgave you" (4:32). Those who have been forgiven learn to forgive as the Father who forgave them.

**2. As Christ gave himself up for us.** "And walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God" (5:2). The measure of love is not our feelings but the cross. "A fragrant offering and sacrifice" is the language of Old Testament sacrifice (cf. Exod 29:18; Lev 1:9): Christ offered himself, bearing the penalty of sin in our place, and God accepted the sacrifice. All our love flows from this love.

> The point: We imitate God not to gain his love but because we are already his beloved children. The pattern of love is Christ, who loved us, gave himself up for us, and became a sacrifice pleasing to God.

## Going Deeper 5:3–14 Now You Are Light in the Lord

**1. Not even named.** "But sexual immorality and all impurity or covetousness must not even be named among you, as is proper among saints" (5:3). In the Greco-Roman world pagan festivals and banquets were often occasions of sexual license. Paul puts sexual sin and "covetousness" side by side because both treat people or things as tools for satisfying oneself. "Let there be no filthiness nor foolish talk nor crude joking, which are out of place, but instead let there be thanksgiving" (5:4): the best answer to unclean speech is not only silence but gratitude.

**2. Covetousness is idolatry.** "Everyone… who is covetous (that is, an idolater)" (5:5). Why is covetousness idolatry? Because a person rests on money or other things for the satisfaction, security and worth that should be sought from God. An idol need not be a statue in a temple; anything the heart cannot do without, anything that takes God's place, can become an idol.

**3. Do not be deceived by empty words.** "Let no one deceive you with empty words, for because of these things the wrath of God comes upon the sons of disobedience" (5:6). Some may have been saying that what we do with the body does not matter and God does not care. Paul calls these empty words. God is holy, and he will judge sin.

**4. From darkness to light.** "For at one time you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord. Walk as children of light" (5:8). Notice that Paul does not say "you were once in darkness" but that you once "were" darkness and now "are" light. This is a change of identity. "For the fruit of light is found in all that is good and right and true" (5:9); children of light "try to discern what is pleasing to the Lord" (5:10).

**5. Exposing the works of darkness.** "Take no part in the unfruitful works of darkness, but instead expose them" (5:11). Children of light not only refrain from these things themselves; they also do not cover for darkness. In the church this includes never covering up sexual abuse or other abuse to save face.

**6. Awake, O sleeper.** "Therefore it says, 'Awake, O sleeper, and arise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you'" (5:14). These words are not a direct quotation of any one Old Testament verse. Many interpreters think they come from an early Christian hymn, perhaps sung at baptism, drawing on images from Isaiah (cf. Isa 26:19; 60:1). Those whom Christ shines on become light themselves.

> The point: Believers were once darkness and are now light in the Lord. So they turn from sexual impurity and from covetousness, which is idolatry, discern what pleases the Lord, and expose the works of darkness. Christ's light shines on us and makes us shine.

## Going Deeper 5:15–21 Not Drunk with Wine, but Filled with the Spirit

**1. Making the best use of the time.** "Look carefully then how you walk, not as unwise but as wise, making the best use of the time, because the days are evil" (5:15–16). "Making the best use of the time" carries the sense of buying back time, seizing the opportunity. Because the days are evil, every chance to do good is precious.

**2. Drunkenness and the Spirit's filling.** "And do not get drunk with wine, for that is debauchery, but be filled with the Spirit" (5:18). Paul contrasts two kinds of "filling." The drunk person is controlled by wine and loses self-control; the person filled with the Spirit is led by God's Spirit and bears the Spirit's fruit, including self-control (Gal 5:23). Some think Paul may also have had in mind the worship of the wine-god, where people sought religious frenzy in drunkenness; but this is only a possible background.
- **"Be filled":** the Greek is a present passive imperative, which many take to stress an ongoing filling: not a one-time experience but a continual being filled by the Spirit. We cannot fill ourselves; we can only open ourselves and yield to his leading.

**3. What the Spirit's filling looks like.** Several participles in the Greek describe the Spirit-filled person:
- **speaking to one another:** "addressing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing and making melody to the Lord with all your heart" (5:19). The Spirit's filling shows first in worship and mutual upbuilding, not in private mystical experience;
- **always giving thanks:** "giving thanks always and for everything to God the Father in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ" (5:20). Again the triune God: filled by the Spirit, in the name of the Son, giving thanks to the Father;
- **submitting to one another:** "submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ" (5:21).

**4. Submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ.** This verse introduces the household relationships that follow (5:22–6:9). Evangelical scholars understand "submitting to one another" differently:
- **Some** (such as Craig Keener and other egalitarian scholars) take it as mutual submission among all believers, husbands and wives submitting to each other; the verses that follow apply this principle at home.
- **Others** (such as Wayne Grudem and other complementarian scholars) take "to one another" here to mean "some to others": within the relationships God has set up, each submits to those to whom submission is due according to his or her place; the verses that follow list those relationships.
- **Many others** hold that 5:21 calls all believers to a general mutual deference, while 5:22–33 still gives husbands and wives distinct responsibilities.
The main egalitarian–complementarian disagreement concerns how to read "head" and "submit" in 5:22–33. Either way, the motive for submission is "reverence for Christ"; in Christ's community no one may proudly demand from others while refusing humility himself.

> The point: In evil days the wise make the best use of the time and understand the Lord's will. They are controlled not by wine or anything else but continually filled with the Spirit, which shows in worship together, constant thanksgiving and mutual submission out of reverence for Christ.

## Going Deeper 5:22–33 A Profound Mystery: Christ and the Church

**1. Wives submit, as to the Lord.** "Wives, submit to your own husbands, as to the Lord" (5:22). In the earliest and best manuscripts this verse has no verb (later copies supply "submit"); "submit" is borrowed from the previous verse: a wife's submission is one expression of being filled with the Spirit and submitting to one another. Paul addresses wives directly, treating them as responsible persons with dignity, not as their husbands' property. "As to the Lord": a wife submits to her husband out of her submission to the Lord.
- **What submission is not:** submission does not mean a wife is inferior to her husband (cf. 1 Pet 3:7, husband and wife are "heirs with you of the grace of life"). It does not mean following her husband into sin (cf. Acts 5:29). And it never means enduring abuse.

**2. Husbands love, as Christ gave himself up for the church.** "Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her" (5:25). In that society, telling wives to submit was nothing unusual; telling husbands to give themselves up for their wives as Christ did was revolutionary. Scholars differ on "head" (5:23): complementarians (e.g., Grudem) see chiefly leadership and authority, some egalitarians "source." Either way it must be read from Christ, who is head as "himself its Savior" (5:23) and exercises his authority by giving himself up for the church, cleansing, nourishing and cherishing her (5:25–29), never by lording it over her. A husband who uses "headship" to dominate, control or harm his wife has completely violated this passage.

**3. A church in splendor.** "That he might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of water with the word, so that he might present the church to himself in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or any such thing, that she might be holy and without blemish" (5:26–27). "Water" may refer to baptism, or may draw on the custom of a bride's bath before her wedding; "the word" is the word of the gospel. Christ's love does not only accept us as we are; it makes us beautiful. Christ himself washes away all our spots and wrinkles, so that on that day he may present us to himself as his glorious bride (cf. Rev 19:7–8; 21:2).

**4. A profound mystery.** "'Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.' This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church" (5:31–32; quoting Gen 2:24). When God instituted marriage, he had Christ and the church in mind. Marriage is a picture pointing to a greater reality: Christ's unchanging love for his people. So every Christian, whether married, single, divorced or widowed, belongs to this deepest "marriage," part of the bride Christ loves and cleanses.

**5. A word about safety.** This passage is often misused, so it must be said plainly: if there is domestic violence, sexual violence or other abuse in a marriage, seeking help from the police or the courts is right (Rom 13:4). The victim may leave for a safe place; she need not stay in danger for the sake of "submission," nor reconcile with someone unsafe. The church's duty is to protect the victim, never to urge her to endure or to cover for the abuser to avoid "airing the family's dirty laundry." Forgiveness does not restore an abuser's church office or give him access again to the victim or children. Husbands, too, can be victims and deserve the same protection.

> The point: Wives submit to their husbands as to the Lord; husbands love their wives as Christ gave himself up for the church. The deepest meaning of marriage points to Christ and the church: Christ gave his life for his bride and cleanses her, to present her to himself as a church in splendor.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And do not get drunk with wine, for that is debauchery, but be filled with the Spirit" (5:18). At the end of the day, when I feel empty, tired or restless, what do I use to "fill" myself? Perhaps not wine, but my phone, one short video after another until late at night; perhaps games, binge-watching, online shopping, or a bag of snacks; perhaps work, so I never have to face the emptiness inside. These things are not necessarily sins in themselves, but when I use them to escape and numb myself, they begin to control me, and the more I fill up with them, the emptier I feel. Paul does not only say "do not"; he says "but be filled with the Spirit." Christ, the One "who fills all in all" (1:23), is willing to fill me by his Spirit. He does not ask me to try harder to control myself, but to open my empty heart to him and let him fill it. Tonight, before I reach for my phone or anything else, can I stop and admit: Lord, my heart is empty, and I often fill it with other things; forgive me; fill me with your Spirit? Then I can read a psalm or sing a hymn, and thank the Father for one thing from today (5:19–20). If something already has a deep hold on me, such as alcohol, gambling or pornography, I need not struggle alone; I can go to a trusted brother or sister or a pastor and ask them to walk with me. The Christ who gave himself up for me (5:2) does not want to take away my joy but to give me himself, so that my heart is truly filled.
