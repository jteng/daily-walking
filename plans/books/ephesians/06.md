## 本章脉络

第六章可以分成三段。**家中和工作中的关系**（1-9节）：接着第五章的夫妻关系，保罗对儿女和父亲说话（1-4节），又对仆人和主人说话（5-9节）；每一组关系都放在「主」的面前：儿女「要在主里听从父母」，父亲要照着「主的教训和警戒」养育儿女，仆人要「好像听从基督一般」，主人要记得「他们和你们同有一位主在天上」。**神所赐的全副军装**（10-20节）：「要靠着主，倚赖他的大能大力作刚强的人」；我们的争战不是与属血气的，而是与灵界的恶势力；所以要穿戴神的全副军装，站立得住，并且「随时多方祷告祈求」，也为保罗祈求，使他能放胆讲明福音的奥秘。**结尾的问安**（21-24节）：推基古要把保罗的景况告诉他们；最后以平安、仁爱、信心和恩惠祝福他们。

全书开头说，神叫基督坐在天上，「远超过一切执政的、掌权的」（1:20-21）；结尾说，信徒要与「那些执政的、掌权的」争战（6:12）。我们争战，是站在基督已经得胜的地位上。

## 深读 6:1-4 儿女与父亲

**1. 在主里听从父母。** 「你们作儿女的，要在主里听从父母，这是理所当然的」（6:1）。保罗直接对儿女说话，表示他们也是教会的一分子，在聚会中一同听这封信。「在主里」说明了顺服的动机和界限：儿女听从父母，是因为他们属于主；但这不是说儿女要顺从父母去犯罪。

**2. 第一条带应许的诫命。** 「『要孝敬父母，使你得福，在世长寿。』这是第一条带应许的诫命」（6:2-3；引出20:12；申5:16）。中国文化很看重孝道，圣经也看重，但根基不同：孝敬父母是因为神吩咐，不是因为父母永远是对的，也不只是出于报恩的人情；圣经的孝敬也不是把父母或祖先放在神的位置上，所以不包括祭拜祖先。成年以后，我们仍要孝敬父母，表现在尊重、感恩和照顾上，却不等于在一切事上都要听从父母，特别是在信仰和婚姻的事上（参创2:24；太10:37）。父母若一直伤害、控制或虐待成年儿女，儿女设立界限、保持安全的距离，并不是不孝；孝敬可以用祷告和合宜的方式来表达，不必把自己重新放在伤害之下。

**3. 不要惹儿女的气。** 「你们作父亲的，不要惹儿女的气，只要照着主的教训和警戒养育他们」（6:4）。在罗马社会，父亲对儿女有极大的权柄。保罗却限制这权柄：不要苛刻，不要偏心，不要冷嘲热讽，不要总拿孩子和「别人家的孩子」比，也不要永远不满意，叫孩子灰心、愤怒（参西3:21）。父亲的责任是「养育」，就是喂养、照顾，并且用「主的」教训来教导。作父母的若在这些事上已经失败，可以先回到天父那里：他待我们从来不是这样。我们蒙了他的赦免，就能向孩子说一声「对不起，是爸爸错了」；这样的认错，本身就是在教孩子认识「主的教训」。
- **安全的提醒：** 「听从父母」绝不是说儿女要默默忍受虐待。孩子若在家中受到殴打、性侵或其他伤害，告诉一位可信的大人、老师或警察是对的（罗13:4）；教会必须保护孩子，绝不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而隐瞒。

> 要点：儿女在主里听从、孝敬父母，因为这是神的吩咐；父亲不可惹儿女的气，而要照着主的教训和警戒养育他们。家里的每一个关系都在主的面前。

## 深读 6:5-9 仆人与主人

**1. 当时的奴隶制度。** 罗马帝国中有大量的奴隶，奴隶在有些城市可能占人口很大的比例。保罗没有在这里直接呼吁废除奴隶制度，但他在一个给主人极大权柄的世界里，说出了颠覆性的话：奴隶是有道德责任的人，主人也要向一位更高的主交账。这些原则后来成了许多基督徒反对奴隶制度的根据（参门16）。历史上曾有奴隶主滥用这段经文，为奴隶制度辩护，特别是建立在拐卖人口之上的奴隶制度；但圣经明明把拐卖人口定为罪（出21:16；提前1:10）。这段经文绝不是在为奴隶制度辩护。保罗也说「若能以自由，就求自由更好」（林前7:21）。今天，这段经文也不是叫人默默忍受被拖欠工资、强迫劳动、人口贩卖，或职场上的虐待和性骚扰；向劳动部门、警察或法院求助是对的（罗13:4）。

**2. 好像听从基督一般。** 「你们作仆人的，要惧怕战兢，用诚实的心听从你们肉身的主人，好像听从基督一般。不要只在眼前事奉，像是讨人喜欢的，要像基督的仆人，从心里遵行神的旨意」（6:5-6）。「只在眼前事奉」就是老板在的时候才认真。保罗叫他们看见，在地上的主人背后，还有一位真正的主。今天我们大多数人不是奴隶，但这原则仍可以应用在工作上：我们是为主做工；他看见，也记念。这叫我们不必再靠讨老板喜欢来证明自己，也不必因为没人看见就灰心。

**3. 必得主的赏赐。** 「因为晓得各人所行的善事，不论是为奴的，是自主的，都必按所行的得主的赏赐」（6:8）。在人眼中最没有价值的工作，在主眼中都被看见。这赏赐不是叫人赚取救恩，而是一位慷慨的主对他仆人的记念。

**4. 不要威吓他们。** 「你们作主人的，待仆人也是一理，不要威吓他们。因为知道，他们和你们同有一位主在天上；他并不偏待人」（6:9）。主人和奴隶在社会上地位天差地别，在主面前却同样是仆人。今天作老板、主管的人，也要记得这一点：不可用威吓管人，因为自己也要向主交账。

> 要点：保罗在一个不公平的制度中，把仆人和主人都放在同一位主面前：仆人从心里做工，好像服事主；主人不可威吓人，因为他们同有一位不偏待人的主在天上。

## 深读 6:10-17 神所赐的全副军装

**1. 靠着主作刚强的人。** 「我还有末了的话：你们要靠着主，倚赖他的大能大力作刚强的人」（6:10）。力量的来源不是我们自己，而是那叫基督从死里复活的「大能大力」（1:19-20）。

**2. 不是与属血气的争战。** 「因我们并不是与属血气的争战，乃是与那些执政的、掌权的、管辖这幽暗世界的，以及天空属灵气的恶魔争战」（6:12）。我们真正的敌人不是那个伤害我们的人、那个反对福音的官员，或者那个难相处的同工，而是背后的属灵势力。这样，我们就不会把人当作仇敌，也不会小看属灵的争战。但这不是说伤害人的人就没有责任。属灵争战的真实，不能拿来替罪遮掩：伤害、虐待或犯罪，仍然要向牧者、警察或法院说出来（罗13:4），受害的人也仍然需要保护。有些人从小在怕鬼、看风水、戴平安符的环境中长大；基督徒不必靠这些保护自己，也不必活在惧怕里，因为基督已经胜过一切执政掌权的（西2:15）。C·S·路易斯在《地狱来鸿》的序言中提醒，人对魔鬼常犯两种相反的错误：一种是不相信它们存在，另一种是对它们有过分、不健康的兴趣。

**3. 站立得住。** 「要穿戴神所赐的全副军装，就能抵挡魔鬼的诡计」（6:11）。这段经文中，「站立」「抵挡」反复出现（6:11、13、14）。保罗没有叫信徒去攻占新的地盘，而是要他们站稳。基督已经得胜（1:20-22；参西2:15），我们的责任是在他的得胜中站立得住，不被打倒。

**4. 这是神自己的军装。** 保罗可能想到罗马兵丁的装备，但他更多是在用以赛亚书的话：
- **真理的带子：** 论到弥赛亚：「公义必当他的腰带；信实必当他胁下的带子」（赛11:5）；
- **公义的护心镜，救恩的头盔：** 耶和华「以公义为铠甲，以拯救为头盔」（赛59:17）；
- **平安的福音当作鞋：** 那报佳音、传平安的人，「这人的脚登山何等佳美」（赛52:7）；
- **圣灵的宝剑，就是神的道：** 参赛49:2「他使我的口如快刀」；又参赛11:4，他「以口中的杖击打世界，以嘴里的气杀戮恶人」。
在以赛亚书中，这些军装是神自己和弥赛亚穿戴的。神看见没有人能拯救，「就用自己的膀臂施行拯救」（赛59:16）。所以这「全副军装」不只是神所赐的，也是神自己穿戴过的；许多解经家因此把穿戴这军装和「披戴主耶稣基督」（罗13:14）连在一起。

**5. 每一件军装。**
- **真理：** 可能指福音的真理，也可能指为人的诚实正直；我们两样都需要。
- **公义：** 可能指神在基督里所赐给我们的义（称义），也可能指正直的生活（成圣）。许多解经家认为两样都包括在内：我们站在基督的义里，所以能面对仇敌的控告。
- **平安的福音：** 「预备」可能是预备好去传福音，也可能是福音所赐的平安，叫人脚步稳固。
- **信德的盾牌：** 罗马兵丁有一种又长又大的盾牌，像一扇门；有人认为（但证据不多），士兵作战前会把盾牌外面的皮革浸湿，用来扑灭带火的箭。「那恶者一切的火箭」可能指控告、怀疑、试探和恐惧。信心的力量不在于信心本身有多大，而在于所信的对象是谁。
- **救恩的头盔：** 保护头的，是神在基督里所赐的救恩本身，以及对这救恩确实的盼望（参帖前5:8「得救的盼望」）。
- **圣灵的宝剑：** 这是唯一可以用来进攻的兵器。「神的道」这里用的词常指神所说的话。主耶稣在旷野受试探时，三次用「经上记着说」回答魔鬼（太4:1-11）。

> 要点：我们的争战不是与人，而是与灵界的恶势力。但我们不是靠自己的力量，而是穿上神自己的军装，也就是在基督里所得的真理、公义、福音、信心、救恩和神的道，在他已经得胜的地位上站立得住。

## 深读 6:18-24 随时多方祷告，带锁链的使者

**1. 随时多方祷告。** 「靠着圣灵，随时多方祷告祈求；并要在此警醒不倦，为众圣徒祈求」（6:18）。保罗没有把祷告列为另一件军装，但它贯穿了全部的争战。原文这一节连用了四个「一切」（随时、多方、不倦、众圣徒）。争战不是单打独斗，而是全体圣徒彼此代祷。

**2. 带锁链的使者。** 「也为我祈求，使我得着口才，能以放胆开口讲明福音的奥秘，（我为这福音的奥秘作了带锁链的使者，）并使我照着当尽的本分放胆讲论」（6:19-20）。古时的使者代表一个国家，通常受到保护，不会被捆锁。保罗却是「带锁链的使者」。他请人为他代祷，不是求早日出狱，而是求能放胆传福音。

**3. 推基古。** 「今有所亲爱、忠心事奉主的兄弟推基古，他要把我的事情，并我的景况如何全告诉你们」（6:21）。推基古是亚细亚人（徒20:4），带过保罗的书信（6:21；西4:7），也常被保罗差派到各处（提后4:12；多3:12）。保罗的事工中，有许多像推基古这样忠心却不显眼的同工。

**4. 平安、仁爱、信心、恩惠。** 「愿平安、仁爱、信心从父神和主耶稣基督归与弟兄们！并愿所有诚心爱我们主耶稣基督的人都蒙恩惠！」（6:23-24）书信以「恩惠、平安」开始（1:2），也以平安和恩惠结束。「诚心」原文是「不朽坏」的意思（参林前15:42、53）：愿那些以不朽坏的爱来爱主的人都蒙恩惠。

> 要点：属灵的争战离不开祷告：靠着圣灵随时祷告，为众圣徒和传福音的人代求。保罗身带锁链，所求的不是自由，而是放胆传福音。全书以恩惠开始，也以恩惠结束。

## 默想

**默想：** 「所以，要拿起神所赐的全副军装，好在磨难的日子抵挡仇敌，并且成就了一切，还能站立得住」（6:13）。我生命中有没有这样的「磨难的日子」？也许是一个老试探，每到深夜、出差、和配偶吵完架的时候，就准时回来；也许是一件压了很久的事，我一直咬着牙自己扛，不跟任何人说。我习惯了靠意志力硬撑：再发一次誓，再定一条规矩，失败了就躲起来，等自己好一点再回到神面前。这些就像「那恶者一切的火箭」。保罗却没有叫我靠意志力打赢这场仗，他只说：「站立得住」。而且他叫我穿上的，不是我自己做的盔甲，而是神自己穿戴过的军装。以赛亚说，神看见没有人能拯救，「就用自己的膀臂施行拯救」（赛59:16）。基督已经穿着这军装上了战场，藉着十字架胜过了一切执政掌权的（西2:15），升上高天的时候「掳掠了仇敌」（4:8），如今坐在一切执政掌权者之上（1:21）。试探说我离不开它，基督的义却遮盖着我的胸膛；恐惧说我会被打倒，救恩的头盔却护着我的头。今天，如果我正在「磨难的日子」里，我能不能拿起「神的道」，找一节经文，像主耶稣那样说「经上记着说」？例如：「我们因信耶稣，就在他里面放胆无惧，笃信不疑地来到神面前」（3:12）。我不必等到自己好一点，现在就可以来。我还要请几位弟兄姊妹为我祷告（6:18），把那件一直独自扛着的事告诉他们，因为神从来没有叫我独自站立。（如果我想伤害自己，要立刻告诉身边的人，或打当地的危机求助热线。）我能站住，不是因为我够刚强，也不是因为我的爱够坚定，而是因为基督已经得胜；是他的恩惠托住我，也保守我对他的爱，直到永远（参6:24）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 falls into three parts. **Relationships at home and at work** (vv. 1–9): following the husbands and wives of chapter 5, Paul speaks to children and fathers (vv. 1–4), then to slaves and masters (vv. 5–9). Each relationship is placed before "the Lord": children are to "obey your parents in the Lord," fathers are to bring up their children "in the discipline and instruction of the Lord," slaves are to obey "as you would Christ," and masters are to remember that "he who is both their Master and yours is in heaven." **The whole armor of God** (vv. 10–20): "be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might"; we do not wrestle against flesh and blood but against spiritual forces of evil; so put on the whole armor of God, stand firm, and pray "at all times in the Spirit, with all prayer and supplication," also for Paul, that he may boldly proclaim the mystery of the gospel. **Closing greetings** (vv. 21–24): Tychicus will tell them how Paul is; and Paul blesses them with peace, love, faith and grace.

The letter began by saying God seated Christ in the heavenly places "far above all rule and authority" (1:20–21); it ends by saying believers wrestle "against the rulers, against the authorities" (6:12). We fight from the position of Christ's victory.

## Going Deeper 6:1–4 Children and Fathers

**1. Obey your parents in the Lord.** "Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right" (6:1). Paul speaks directly to children, showing that they too are part of the church, listening as the letter is read in the gathering. "In the Lord" sets both the motive and the limit of obedience: children obey their parents because they belong to the Lord; this does not mean children must follow their parents into sin.

**2. The first commandment with a promise.** "'Honor your father and mother' (this is the first commandment with a promise), 'that it may go well with you and that you may live long in the land'" (6:2–3; quoting Exod 20:12; Deut 5:16). Chinese culture highly values filial piety, and so does Scripture, but on a different foundation: we honor our parents because God commands it, not because parents are always right, nor merely to repay a debt of gratitude; and biblical honor does not put parents or ancestors in God's place, so it does not include ancestor worship. Honoring parents continues into adulthood, in respect, gratitude and care, but it does not mean obeying them in everything, especially in matters of faith and marriage (cf. Gen 2:24; Matt 10:37). If parents keep harming, controlling or abusing their adult children, setting boundaries and keeping a safe distance is not a failure to honor them. Honor can be shown through prayer and fitting means without putting oneself back under harm.

**3. Do not provoke your children.** "Fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord" (6:4). In Roman society a father had immense power over his children. Paul limits that power: do not discourage or anger children by harshness, favoritism, sarcasm, comparing them with others, or never being satisfied (cf. Col 3:21). A father's task is to "bring them up," to nourish and care for them, and to teach them with the instruction "of the Lord." Parents who have already failed here can first go back to the Father, who has never treated us this way. Forgiven by him, we can say to our children, "I'm sorry, Dad was wrong." Such a confession itself teaches them "the instruction of the Lord."
- **A word about safety:** "obey your parents" never means children must silently endure abuse. A child who is beaten, sexually abused or otherwise harmed at home is right to tell a trusted adult, a teacher or the police (Rom 13:4). The church must protect children and never hide abuse to avoid "airing the family's dirty laundry."

> The point: Children obey and honor their parents in the Lord because God commands it; fathers must not provoke their children but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord. Every relationship in the home stands before the Lord.

## Going Deeper 6:5–9 Slaves and Masters

**1. Slavery in that day.** The Roman Empire held a vast number of slaves; in some cities they may have made up a large part of the population. Paul does not here directly call for the abolition of slavery, but in a world that gave masters sweeping power over slaves, he says subversive things: slaves are morally responsible persons, and masters too must answer to a higher Master. These principles later became grounds on which many Christians opposed slavery (cf. Phlm 16). This passage is in no way a defense of slavery. Slaveholders in later centuries misused these verses to defend slavery, especially slavery built on kidnapping, which Scripture condemns (Exod 21:16; 1 Tim 1:10). Paul also says, "if you can gain your freedom, avail yourself of the opportunity" (1 Cor 7:21). Nor does this passage ask anyone today to endure wage theft, forced labor, trafficking, or abuse and harassment at work; seeking help from labor authorities, the police or the courts is right (Rom 13:4).

**2. As you would Christ.** "Slaves, obey your earthly masters with fear and trembling, with a sincere heart, as you would Christ, not by the way of eye-service, as people-pleasers, but as servants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart" (6:5–6). "Eye-service" means working hard only when the boss is watching. Paul has them see that behind their earthly master stands the true Master. Though most of us are not slaves, the principle still applies to work: we work for the Lord, who sees and remembers. That frees us from proving ourselves by pleasing the boss, and from losing heart when no one notices.

**3. Receiving back from the Lord.** "Knowing that whatever good anyone does, this he will receive back from the Lord, whether he is a slave or free" (6:8). Work that seems worthless in human eyes is seen by the Lord. This reward is not earning salvation but a generous Master's remembrance of his servants.

**4. Stop your threatening.** "Masters, do the same to them, and stop your threatening, knowing that he who is both their Master and yours is in heaven, and that there is no partiality with him" (6:9). In society master and slave stood worlds apart; before the Lord both were servants. Bosses and managers today should remember this too: do not manage by threats, for you too will give account to the Lord.

> The point: Within an unjust system Paul places slave and master before the same Lord: slaves work from the heart as serving the Lord; masters must not threaten, for they share one impartial Master in heaven.

## Going Deeper 6:10–17 The Whole Armor of God

**1. Strong in the Lord.** "Finally, be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might" (6:10). The source of strength is not ourselves but the "great might" that raised Christ from the dead (1:19–20).

**2. Not against flesh and blood.** "For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers over this present darkness, against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places" (6:12). Our real enemy is not the person who hurt us, the official who opposes the gospel, or the difficult co-worker, but the spiritual powers behind them. So we will neither treat people as enemies nor make light of spiritual warfare. This does not mean the person who did harm bears no responsibility. The reality of spiritual warfare must never be used to cover sin: harm, abuse or crime should still be brought to pastors, the police or the courts (Rom 13:4), and those harmed still need protection. Some grew up fearing ghosts, consulting feng shui and wearing amulets; Christians need neither these charms nor the fear behind them, for Christ has triumphed over every rule and authority (Col 2:15). In the preface to *The Screwtape Letters*, C. S. Lewis warned that people fall into two opposite errors about devils: disbelieving in their existence, or feeling an excessive and unhealthy interest in them.

**3. To stand.** "Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the schemes of the devil" (6:11). "Stand" and "withstand" recur through this passage (6:11, 13, 14). Paul does not send believers out to take new ground but tells them to stand firm. Christ has already won (1:20–22; cf. Col 2:15); our task is to stand firm in his victory and not be knocked down.

**4. God's own armor.** Paul may have had a Roman soldier's equipment in view, but he draws even more on Isaiah:
- **the belt of truth:** of the Messiah: "Righteousness shall be the belt of his waist, and faithfulness the belt of his loins" (Isa 11:5);
- **the breastplate of righteousness, the helmet of salvation:** the LORD "put on righteousness as a breastplate, and a helmet of salvation on his head" (Isa 59:17);
- **shoes of the gospel of peace:** "How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news, who publishes peace" (Isa 52:7);
- **the sword of the Spirit, the word of God:** cf. Isa 49:2, "He made my mouth like a sharp sword"; and Isa 11:4, "the rod of his mouth… the breath of his lips."
In Isaiah this armor is worn by God himself and by the Messiah. God saw there was no one to save, "then his own arm brought him salvation" (Isa 59:16). So "the whole armor of God" is both supplied by God and worn by God himself; many interpreters therefore link putting it on with "put on the Lord Jesus Christ" (Rom 13:14).

**5. Each piece.**
- **Truth:** may mean the truth of the gospel, or personal honesty and integrity; both are needed.
- **Righteousness:** may mean the righteousness God gives us in Christ (justification), or upright living (sanctification). Many interpreters think both are included: we stand in Christ's righteousness, and so we can face the enemy's accusations.
- **The gospel of peace:** "readiness" may mean being prepared to preach the gospel, or the firm footing the gospel gives, so that we stand steady.
- **The shield of faith:** Roman soldiers had a long, large shield shaped like a door; some commentators suggest, on limited evidence, that its leather covering could be soaked in water before battle to put out flaming arrows. "All the flaming darts of the evil one" may mean accusations, doubts, temptations and fears. The strength of faith lies not in how great our faith is but in whom we trust.
- **The helmet of salvation:** what guards the head is salvation itself, given in Christ, and its sure hope (cf. 1 Thess 5:8, "the hope of salvation").
- **The sword of the Spirit:** the only weapon for attack. The word used here for "word of God" often means what God has spoken. When the Lord Jesus was tempted in the wilderness, three times he answered the devil, "It is written" (Matt 4:1–11).

> The point: Our battle is not against people but against spiritual forces of evil. Yet we do not fight in our own strength; we put on God's own armor, the truth, righteousness, gospel, faith, salvation and word of God that are ours in Christ, and stand firm in the victory he has already won.

## Going Deeper 6:18–24 Praying at All Times; an Ambassador in Chains

**1. Praying at all times.** "Praying at all times in the Spirit, with all prayer and supplication. To that end keep alert with all perseverance, making supplication for all the saints" (6:18). Paul does not list prayer as another piece of armor, but it runs through the whole battle. In Greek this verse uses "all" four times (at all times, all prayer, all perseverance, all the saints). The battle is not fought alone; all the saints pray for one another.

**2. An ambassador in chains.** "And also for me, that words may be given to me in opening my mouth boldly to proclaim the mystery of the gospel, for which I am an ambassador in chains, that I may declare it boldly, as I ought to speak" (6:19–20). An ancient ambassador represented a nation and was normally protected, not bound. Paul is "an ambassador in chains." He asks for prayer not for early release but for boldness to preach the gospel.

**3. Tychicus.** "Tychicus the beloved brother and faithful minister in the Lord will tell you everything" (6:21). Tychicus came from Asia (Acts 20:4) and carried Paul's letters (6:21; Col 4:7) and was often sent on his errands (2 Tim 4:12; Titus 3:12). Paul's ministry depended on many faithful, unnoticed co-workers like Tychicus.

**4. Peace, love, faith, grace.** "Peace be to the brothers, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Grace be with all who love our Lord Jesus Christ with love incorruptible" (6:23–24). The letter began with "grace and peace" (1:2) and ends with peace and grace. "Incorruptible" is the word for imperishable (cf. 1 Cor 15:42, 53): grace be with all who love the Lord with an undying love.

> The point: Spiritual warfare cannot be separated from prayer: praying at all times in the Spirit, for all the saints and for those who preach the gospel. Paul in chains asks not for freedom but for boldness to proclaim the gospel. The letter begins with grace and ends with grace.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Therefore take up the whole armor of God, that you may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand firm" (6:13). Is there an "evil day" in my life? Perhaps an old temptation that comes back right on time: late at night, on a business trip, after a quarrel with my spouse. Perhaps a weight I have carried for a long time with gritted teeth, telling no one. I am used to fighting by willpower: one more vow, one more rule, and when I fail, hiding until I feel better before coming back to God. These are like "the flaming darts of the evil one." But Paul does not tell me to win this battle by willpower; he says only, "stand firm." And what he tells me to put on is not armor I have made but the armor God himself has worn. Isaiah says God saw there was no one to save, "then his own arm brought him salvation" (Isa 59:16). Christ has already gone into battle in this armor; by the cross he triumphed over the rulers and authorities (Col 2:15), and when he ascended on high he "led a host of captives" (4:8), and now he is seated above every rule and authority (1:21). The temptation says I cannot live without it, but Christ's righteousness covers my chest; fear says I will be knocked down, but the helmet of salvation guards my head. Today, if I am in an "evil day," can I take up "the word of God," find one verse, and say as the Lord Jesus did, "It is written"? For example: "in whom we have boldness and access with confidence through our faith in him" (3:12). I do not have to wait until I feel better; I can come now. I also need to ask a few brothers and sisters to pray for me (6:18) and tell them about the thing I have been carrying alone, because God never asked me to stand alone. (If I find myself wanting to harm myself, I should tell someone near me at once or call a local crisis helpline.) I can stand not because I am strong enough, nor because my love is steady enough, but because Christ has already won; it is his grace that holds me up and keeps my love for him, forever (cf. 6:24).
