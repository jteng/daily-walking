## 这卷书在讲什么

加拉太书是保罗最急切、最激动的一封信。别的书信开头多半有感恩的话，这封信却没有；保罗问候完，立刻就说：「我希奇你们这么快离开那藉着基督之恩召你们的，去从别的福音。」（1:6）

发生了什么事？保罗在加拉太一带传了福音，许多外邦人信了主，领受了圣灵。保罗离开以后，有一些人来到这些教会，教导说：单单信耶稣还不够；外邦信徒还要受割礼、守摩西的律法，才算真正属于神的百姓（参5:2-4；6:12-13）。

在保罗看来，这不是一个小小的补充，而是把福音整个改掉了。如果人要靠「基督加上律法」才能被神接纳，那么「基督就是徒然死了」（2:21）。所以他用整封信说明：人称义「不是因行律法，乃是因信耶稣基督」（2:16）；信徒是神的儿子，不是奴仆（4:7）；「基督释放了我们，叫我们得以自由」（5:1），而这自由不是放纵，而是「用爱心互相服事」（5:13）。

宗教改革时，马丁·路德特别喜爱这卷书。他说加拉太书是他自己的书信，好像他所娶的妻子一样宝贵。因信称义的福音，就是在这卷书和罗马书中最清楚地显明出来的。

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 作者。** 「作使徒的保罗（不是由于人，也不是藉着人，乃是藉着耶稣基督，与叫他从死里复活的父神）」（1:1）。保罗是这封信的作者，几乎没有人怀疑。

**2. 「加拉太的各教会」在哪里？** 这个问题学者有两种主要的看法：
- **南加拉太说：** 指罗马加拉太省南部的城市，就是保罗第一次宣教旅程所建立的彼西底的安提阿、以哥念、路司得和特庇（徒13–14章）。采取这种看法的有威廉·兰赛、弗雷德里克·布鲁斯、理查德·朗格内克等学者。
- **北加拉太说：** 指小亚细亚中部、原来由加拉太族人居住的地区，保罗可能在第二、第三次宣教旅程时经过那里（徒16:6；18:23）。采取这种看法的有约瑟·莱特福特、汉斯·贝茨等学者。

今天福音派学者多倾向南加拉太说，但两种看法都有理由，也都不影响这封信的信息。

**3. 写作时间。** 许多持南加拉太说的学者（如布鲁斯、朗格内克）认为这封信写于主后48-49年，在耶路撒冷会议（徒15章）以前，是保罗最早的书信之一；但也有持南加拉太说的学者（如兰赛）认为写于会议以后。北加拉太说则多定在主后50年代中期。这也牵涉到2:1-10所记的那次上耶路撒冷：认为是徒11:30送捐项那次的，理由是保罗在信中没有提到会议的决定，而且2:2「奉启示」可能与亚迦布的预言（徒11:28）有关；认为是徒15章的（如莱特福特），理由是两处都讨论外邦人受割礼的问题，人物也相同，而2:2的「私下」可能是指大会以前的私下会谈。

**4. 搅扰的人。** 保罗没有说出他们的名字，只称他们是「搅扰你们」的人（1:7；5:10）。从信的内容看，他们很可能是来自犹太地的犹太基督徒（参徒15:1，24），坚持外邦信徒必须受割礼（6:12），大概也要他们守犹太的节期（4:10）。他们大概不否认耶稣是基督，却认为单单信耶稣不够。今天许多人称他们为「犹太化派」；不过保罗在2:14用「随犹太人」这个说法，是指外邦人照犹太人的规矩生活，用这个名称称呼那些教师，是后来的习惯。

## 钥匙经文：5:1

**1. 「基督释放了我们」。** 自由不是我们自己争取来的，而是基督为我们成就的。他「为我们的罪舍己」（1:4），又「为我们受了咒诅」（3:13），把我们从罪的咒诅和律法的定罪中赎出来。

**2. 「叫我们得以自由」。** 自由是目的。神救我们，不是为了把我们从一种奴役换到另一种奴役，而是要我们作他的儿女（4:7）。

**3. 「所以要站立得稳，不要再被奴仆的轭挟制」。** 已经得了自由的人，仍然可能被拉回去：回到靠自己的表现、靠宗教的规条、靠别人的认可来证明自己的生活。保罗呼召加拉太人站稳在恩典里。

## 全书结构

加拉太书可以大致分成三个部分：
- **保罗的福音从神而来**（1–2章）：保罗讲述他怎样蒙召，怎样与耶路撒冷的使徒相交，又怎样在安提阿当面抵挡彼得；最后说出全书的中心：「我已经与基督同钉十字架，现在活着的不再是我，乃是基督在我里面活着。」（2:20）
- **因信称义，不因行律法**（3–4章）：保罗从加拉太人自己的经历、亚伯拉罕的故事、律法的作用、儿子与奴仆的对比，以及夏甲与撒拉的比喻，证明人是因信称义，是神的儿子，承受应许。
- **在自由中凭着圣灵而行**（5–6章）：自由不是放纵，而是用爱心互相服事；顺着圣灵而行，结出圣灵的果子；彼此担当重担；最后保罗说，他只夸「我们主耶稣基督的十字架」（6:14）。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 留意「福音的真理」。** 保罗两次说到「福音的真理」（2:5、14）。对他来说，有些事可以让步，但福音的核心不能让步：人在神面前被接纳，完全是因着基督，不是因着基督加上别的什么。

**2. 留意「律法」的意思。** 保罗在这卷书中有时说律法的「咒诅」，有时又说「全律法都包在『爱人如己』这一句话之内了」（5:14）。他反对的不是神的律法本身，而是把遵守律法当作在神面前称义的根据。

**3. 留意福音怎样改变生活。** 加拉太书不只讲教义，也讲生活。因信称义的人，不是可以随便活的人，而是被圣灵带领、彼此相爱、彼此担当的人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「基督释放了我们，叫我们得以自由。所以要站立得稳，不要再被奴仆的轭挟制。」（5:1）我心里是否也有一个「基督加上……」？基督加上我每天读经祷告的记录，加上我在教会服事的年数，加上我从小在基督徒家庭长大，加上我比别人守规矩，我才觉得自己在神面前站得住？每当我做得不够好，我是否就觉得神不太接纳我了？加拉太人起初是靠着恩典开始的，后来却想靠自己的表现来完成。保罗说，这样做，等于说「基督就是徒然死了」（2:21）。可是基督没有白死。他「为我们的罪舍己」（1:4），他「是爱我，为我舍己」（2:20）。我在神面前被接纳，从头到尾都是因为他，不是因为我加上去的东西。读经、祷告、服事仍然宝贵，却不是我的「门票」，而是一个已经被爱的儿女对父亲的回应。在开始读加拉太书的时候，我能不能把我心里那个「加上……」说出来，交给神，然后安静地再读一次：「他是爱我，为我舍己」？

## What This Book Is About

Galatians is Paul's most urgent and impassioned letter. Most of his letters open with thanksgiving; this one does not. After his greeting Paul says at once, "I am astonished that you are so quickly deserting him who called you in the grace of Christ and are turning to a different gospel" (1:6).

What had happened? Paul had preached the gospel in Galatia, and many Gentiles had believed and received the Holy Spirit. After he left, some people came to these churches teaching that believing in Jesus was not enough; Gentile believers must also be circumcised and keep the law of Moses to belong fully to God's people (cf. 5:2–4; 6:12–13).

To Paul this was not a small addition but a different gospel altogether. If people need "Christ plus the law" to be accepted by God, then "Christ died for no purpose" (2:21). So the whole letter argues that "a person is not justified by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ" (2:16); that believers are God's sons, not slaves (4:7); and that "for freedom Christ has set us free" (5:1), a freedom that is not license but "through love serve one another" (5:13).

Luther especially loved this letter; in his Table Talk he called it his own epistle, as dear to him as his wife. The gospel of justification by faith is set out most clearly here and in Romans.

## Author and Setting

**1. Author.** "Paul, an apostle- not from men nor through man, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father, who raised him from the dead-" (1:1). Paul's authorship is almost universally accepted.

**2. Where were "the churches of Galatia"?** Scholars hold two main views:
- **The South Galatian view:** the cities in the southern part of the Roman province of Galatia that Paul founded on his first missionary journey: Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra and Derbe (Acts 13–14). Scholars such as William Ramsay, F. F. Bruce and Richard Longenecker hold this view.
- **The North Galatian view:** the region of central Asia Minor originally settled by the ethnic Galatians, which Paul may have passed through on his second and third journeys (Acts 16:6; 18:23). Scholars such as J. B. Lightfoot and Hans Dieter Betz hold this view.

Most evangelical scholars today lean toward the South Galatian view, but both have reasons, and neither changes the letter's message.

**3. Date.** Many South Galatian scholars (e.g., Bruce, Longenecker) date the letter to AD 48–49, before the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), which would make it one of Paul's earliest letters. Some South Galatian advocates (e.g., Ramsay) date it after the council. North Galatian scholars usually place it in the mid-50s. This also bears on the visit of 2:1–10. Those who identify it with the famine visit of Acts 11:30 note that Paul never mentions the council's decree, and that "because of a revelation" (2:2) may echo Agabus (Acts 11:28). Those who identify it with Acts 15 (e.g., Lightfoot) note the same issue, circumcision of Gentiles, and the same people, and read the "private" meeting of 2:2 as preceding the public council.

**4. The agitators.** Paul does not name them; he calls them those who "trouble you" (1:7; 5:10). From the letter they were probably Jewish Christians from Judea (cf. Acts 15:1, 24) who insisted that Gentile believers be circumcised (6:12) and probably to keep Jewish festivals (4:10). They probably did not deny that Jesus was the Christ, but held that believing in Jesus was not enough. Many today call them "Judaizers," though when Paul uses the related verb (2:14, "live like Jews") he means Gentiles adopting Jewish practice; the label for the teachers themselves is later.

## Key Verse: 5:1

**1. "Christ has set us free."** Freedom is not something we win for ourselves but something Christ has accomplished for us. He "gave himself for our sins" (1:4) and became "a curse for us" (3:13), redeeming us from the curse of sin and the condemnation of the law.

**2. "For freedom."** Freedom is the goal. God did not save us to move us from one slavery to another, but to make us his children (4:7).

**3. "Stand firm therefore, and do not submit again to a yoke of slavery."** People who have been set free can still be pulled back: back into proving themselves by performance, religious rules or other people's approval. Paul calls the Galatians to stand firm in grace.

## Structure

Galatians falls broadly into three parts:
- **Paul's gospel comes from God** (chs. 1–2): Paul tells how he was called, how he related to the apostles in Jerusalem, and how he opposed Peter to his face at Antioch, ending with the center of the letter: "I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me" (2:19–20).
- **Justified by faith, not by works of the law** (chs. 3–4): from the Galatians' own experience, the story of Abraham, the purpose of the law, the contrast of sons and slaves, and the allegory of Hagar and Sarah, Paul shows that people are justified by faith, are God's sons and inherit the promise.
- **Walking in freedom by the Spirit** (chs. 5–6): freedom is not license but serving one another in love; walking by the Spirit bears the fruit of the Spirit; bearing one another's burdens; and finally Paul boasts only "in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ" (6:14).

## How to Read This Book

**1. Watch for "the truth of the gospel."** Paul twice speaks of "the truth of the gospel" (2:5, 14). For him some things can be conceded, but not the heart of the gospel: a person is accepted by God wholly because of Christ, not because of Christ plus anything else.

**2. Watch what "law" means.** In this letter Paul sometimes speaks of the law's "curse," and sometimes says, "the whole law is fulfilled in one word: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself'" (5:14). What he opposes is not God's law itself but treating law-keeping as the ground of being justified before God.

**3. Watch how the gospel changes life.** Galatians is not only doctrine but life. Those justified by faith are not people who may live as they please, but people led by the Spirit, who love one another and bear one another's burdens.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For freedom Christ has set us free; stand firm therefore, and do not submit again to a yoke of slavery" (5:1). Is there a "Christ plus..." in my heart? Christ plus my record of daily devotions, plus my years of serving at church, plus having grown up in a Christian home, plus being better behaved than others, and only then do I feel I can stand before God? Whenever I fall short, do I feel God accepts me a little less? The Galatians began by grace and then tried to finish by their own performance. Paul says that amounts to saying "Christ died for no purpose" (2:21). But Christ did not die for nothing. He "gave himself for our sins" (1:4); he "loved me and gave himself for me" (2:20). I am accepted by God from beginning to end because of him, not because of anything I add. Scripture, prayer and service are still precious, but they are not my "ticket"; they are a beloved child's response to the Father. As I begin Galatians, can I name the "plus" in my heart, hand it over to God, and then quietly read once more: "who loved me and gave himself for me"?
