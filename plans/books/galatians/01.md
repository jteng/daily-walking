## 本章脉络

第一章有三段。**问安**（1-5节）：保罗说他作使徒「不是由于人，也不是藉着人」；又说基督「为我们的罪舍己，要救我们脱离这罪恶的世代」。**没有别的福音**（6-10节）：保罗没有感恩，而是「希奇」加拉太人这么快就转向「别的福音」；他说，无论是谁，传另一个福音，「他就应当被咒诅」。**保罗的福音从哪里来**（11-24节）：这福音「不是从人领受的……乃是从耶稣基督启示来的」；保罗讲到他从前怎样逼迫教会，神怎样「施恩召我」，以及他信主以后多年的经历。

这一章要回答一个问题：保罗所传的福音，是他自己想出来的、从别人那里学来的，还是从神来的？如果是从神来的，那就没有人可以更改它，连保罗自己也不能。

## 深读 1:1-5 为我们的罪舍己

**1. 不是由于人。** 「作使徒的保罗（不是由于人，也不是藉着人，乃是藉着耶稣基督，与叫他从死里复活的父神）」（1:1）。那些搅扰的人大概说，保罗的使徒身份是从耶路撒冷的使徒那里得来的，是二手的。保罗一开头就说：我的使徒职分直接来自耶稣基督和父神。

**2. 基督和父神并列。** 保罗把「耶稣基督」和「父神」并列，作为他使徒职分的来源，又说恩惠、平安「从父神与我们的主耶稣基督」而来（1:3）。在一位犹太人的口中，这是对基督神性很自然、却又很惊人的见证。

**3. 为我们的罪舍己。** 「基督照我们父神的旨意，为我们的罪舍己，要救我们脱离这罪恶的世代。」（1:4）这短短一句话，已经是全书的福音：
- **「为我们的罪」：** 基督的死是为罪，是代替我们承担罪的刑罚（参3:13）。
- **「舍己」：** 他不是被迫的，而是甘心把自己献上（2:20）。
- **「脱离这罪恶的世代」：** 犹太人把历史分为「今世」和「来世」。保罗说，基督的死和复活已经把信徒从这个被罪辖制的世代中救出来，叫他们在今天就尝到来世的能力（参6:15「新造的人」）。
- **「照我们父神的旨意」：** 救恩出于父神的计划，不是一场意外。

**4. 荣耀归于神。** 「但愿荣耀归于神，直到永永远远。阿们！」（1:5）既然救恩完全是神的作为，荣耀也就完全归于神，没有人可以分享。

> 要点：福音的中心是基督照父神的旨意，为我们的罪舍己。救恩既然完全是神的作为，荣耀也就完全归于神。

## 深读 1:6-10 没有别的福音

**1. 我希奇。** 「我希奇你们这么快离开那藉着基督之恩召你们的，去从别的福音。」（1:6）保罗没有说「你们离开了一套教义」，而是说「离开那……召你们的」，就是离开神自己。离开恩典的福音，就是离开那位施恩的神。

**2. 那并不是福音。** 「那并不是福音，不过有些人搅扰你们，要把基督的福音更改了。」（1:7）「福音」是好消息。如果人还要靠自己的行为去补足基督所作的，那就不再是好消息了，而是一个新的重担。

**3. 应当被咒诅。** 「但无论是我们，是天上来的使者，若传福音给你们，与我们所传给你们的不同，他就应当被咒诅。」（1:8）「被咒诅」原文就是林前16:22的那个词，意思是交给神审判。这句话很重。但要留意：
- 保罗咒诅的对象是**传**另一个福音的人，不是信心软弱、一时被迷惑的信徒；对于那些被迷惑的加拉太人，他仍然称他们为「弟兄们」（1:11）。
- 他连自己也包括在内：「无论是我们……」。衡量的标准不是传道人的名气、恩赐或经历（甚至是「天上来的使者」），而是福音本身。

**4. 讨人的喜欢吗？** 「我现在是要得人的心呢？还是要得神的心呢？我岂是讨人的喜欢吗？若仍旧讨人的喜欢，我就不是基督的仆人了。」（1:10）有人可能批评保罗不叫外邦人受割礼，是为了讨好外邦人，让福音更容易被接受。保罗说，他说这么重的话，显然不是在讨人喜欢。一个人若要作基督的仆人，就不能以讨人喜欢为目标。

> 要点：福音只有一个，就是神在基督里白白的恩典。任何在基督以外加上条件的「福音」，都不是好消息。衡量传道人的标准，不是他的名气或经历，而是他所传的是不是这个福音。

## 深读 1:11-17 从耶稣基督启示来的

**1. 不是出于人的意思。** 「弟兄们，我告诉你们，我素来所传的福音不是出于人的意思。因为我不是从人领受的，也不是人教导我的，乃是从耶稣基督启示来的。」（1:11-12）保罗不是说他从来没有从别人学过任何关于耶稣的事（参林前15:3）；他是说，福音的核心和他的使命，是复活的基督亲自向他显明的。

**2. 我从前的生活。** 「你们听见我从前在犹太教中所行的事，怎样极力逼迫残害神的教会。我又在犹太教中，比我本国许多同岁的人更有长进，为我祖宗的遗传更加热心。」（1:13-14）保罗以前不是一个对宗教冷淡的人，而是最热心、最有成就的人之一。他的热心使他逼迫教会。这说明，一个人可以非常虔诚、非常热心，却仍然与神为敌。

**3. 然而，神。** 「然而，那把我从母腹里分别出来、又施恩召我的神，既然乐意将他儿子启示在我心里，叫我把他传在外邦人中……」（1:15-16）转折的关键是「然而」和「神」。保罗的改变不是他自己找到了真理，而是神主动作工：
- **「从母腹里分别出来」：** 这句话让人想起耶利米和以赛亚书中的仆人（耶1:5；赛49:1）。神在保罗出生以前就有计划，连他逼迫教会的那些年，也在神的手中。
- **「施恩召我」：** 呼召是恩典，不是奖赏。神召的正是一个逼迫他教会的人。
- **「将他儿子启示在我心里」：** 原文可以译作「在我里面」，也可以理解为「藉着我」显明；无论哪一种，都是神亲自让保罗认识基督。

**4. 往阿拉伯去。** 「我就没有与属血气的人商量，也没有上耶路撒冷去见那些比我先作使徒的，惟独往阿拉伯去，后又回到大马士革。」（1:16-17）「阿拉伯」大概是指大马士革以南、以东的拿巴提王国（参林后11:32）。保罗在那里做了什么，他没有说：有人认为他在那里传福音，所以亚哩达王的人要捉拿他；也有人认为那是一段安静、默想圣经的日子。

> 要点：保罗是一位热心却逼迫教会的人，神却「施恩召」了他，把他儿子启示在他心里。保罗的改变不是他找到了神，而是神找到了他；连他过去的年日，也在神的手中。

## 深读 1:18-24 他们为我的缘故归荣耀给神

**1. 去见矶法。** 「过了三年，才上耶路撒冷去见矶法，和他同住了十五天。至于别的使徒，除了主的兄弟雅各，我都没有看见。」（1:18-19）「见」原文有拜访、请教的意思。保罗不是否认与使徒的交通，而是说明他的福音不是从他们那里领受的。「主的兄弟雅各」，大多数新教学者认为是马利亚和约瑟后来所生的儿子（参可6:3）；天主教传统认为是耶稣的表亲；东正教传统认为是约瑟前妻所生的儿子。

**2. 我不是说谎。** 「我写给你们的不是谎话，这是我在神面前说的。」（1:20）保罗在这里起誓，可见那些人在攻击他的诚实。

**3. 叙利亚和基利家。** 「以后我到了叙利亚和基利家境内。」（1:21）保罗的家乡大数就在基利家（徒21:39）。这是一段很长、新约记载很少的日子。

**4. 他们就为我的缘故，归荣耀给神。** 「不过听说那从前逼迫我们的，现在传扬他原先所残害的真道。他们就为我的缘故，归荣耀给神。」（1:23-24）犹太地的教会还没有见过保罗，却已经听说了这件事。他们没有说「这个人不可信」，而是为他归荣耀给神。一个蒙恩罪人的生命改变，本身就是对神恩典的见证。

> 要点：从前逼迫教会的人，现在传扬他所残害的真道；教会为此归荣耀给神。一个人被改变的生命，最能显明神的恩典。

## 默想

**默想：** 「然而，那把我从母腹里分别出来、又施恩召我的神，既然乐意将他儿子启示在我心里……」（1:15-16）我是否常常为过去的年日感到可惜，甚至懊悔：信主以前那些离神很远、甚至嘲笑基督徒的日子；信主以后长期冷淡、在教会边上的日子；或者那些默默无闻、看不出有什么果效的年日？保罗曾经逼迫教会，信主以后又有好几年在阿拉伯、叙利亚和基利家，没有人知道他在做什么。可是他说，神「从母腹里」就把他分别出来了。神没有等他变好才开始写他的故事，连那些他以为浪费了的年日，也都在神的手中。神「施恩召我」：保罗蒙召不是因为他配得，而是因为基督「为我们的罪舍己」（1:4）。这不是说过去的罪不算什么，而是说，基督已经为那些罪舍了自己，神的恩典比我的过去更大。今天，我能不能把那段我最想抹掉的年日带到神面前，承认其中的罪，然后相信：那位「施恩召我」的神，在那时也没有放下我？也许将来有一天，别人会因为我的故事，「为我的缘故，归荣耀给神」。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 has three parts. **Greeting** (vv. 1–5): Paul says he is an apostle "not from men nor through man," and that Christ "gave himself for our sins to deliver us from the present evil age." **No other gospel** (vv. 6–10): instead of thanksgiving, Paul is "astonished" that the Galatians have so quickly turned to "a different gospel"; anyone who preaches another gospel, he says, "let him be accursed." **Where Paul's gospel came from** (vv. 11–24): "I did not receive it from any man... but I received it through a revelation of Jesus Christ"; Paul tells how he once persecuted the church, how God "called me by his grace," and what happened in the years after his conversion.

This chapter answers one question: did Paul invent his gospel, learn it from others, or receive it from God? If it came from God, no one may change it, not even Paul himself.

## Going Deeper 1:1–5 He Gave Himself for Our Sins

**1. Not from men.** "Paul, an apostle- not from men nor through man, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father, who raised him from the dead-" (1:1). The agitators probably said Paul's apostleship came secondhand, from the apostles in Jerusalem. Paul says from the start: my apostleship comes directly from Jesus Christ and God the Father.

**2. Christ and the Father side by side.** Paul names "Jesus Christ" and "God the Father" together as the source of his apostleship, and says grace and peace come "from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ" (1:3). From the mouth of a Jew, this is a natural yet astonishing witness to Christ's deity.

**3. He gave himself for our sins.** "...who gave himself for our sins to deliver us from the present evil age, according to the will of our God and Father" (1:4). This one sentence already holds the gospel of the whole letter:
- **"For our sins":** Christ's death was for sin, bearing its penalty in our place (cf. 3:13).
- **"Gave himself":** he was not forced; he offered himself willingly (2:20).
- **"To deliver us from the present evil age":** Jews divided history into "this age" and "the age to come." Paul says Christ's death and resurrection have already rescued believers from this age ruled by sin, so that they taste the power of the age to come today (cf. 6:15, "a new creation").
- **"According to the will of our God and Father":** salvation comes from the Father's plan, not by accident.

**4. Glory to God.** "...to whom be the glory forever and ever. Amen" (1:5). Since salvation is wholly God's work, the glory belongs wholly to God; no one may share it.

> The point: The heart of the gospel is that Christ gave himself for our sins according to the Father's will. Since salvation is wholly God's work, the glory belongs wholly to God.

## Going Deeper 1:6–10 No Other Gospel

**1. I am astonished.** "I am astonished that you are so quickly deserting him who called you in the grace of Christ and are turning to a different gospel-" (1:6). Paul does not say "you have left a set of doctrines" but "deserting him who called you," God himself. To leave the gospel of grace is to leave the God of grace.

**2. Not another gospel.** "...not that there is another one, but there are some who trouble you and want to distort the gospel of Christ" (1:7). "Gospel" means good news. If people must still make up by their own works what Christ has done, it is no longer good news but a new burden.

**3. Let him be accursed.** "But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed" (1:8). "Accursed" (*anathema*) is the word of 1 Corinthians 16:22, meaning handed over to God's judgment. These are heavy words. But notice:
- Paul's curse falls on those who **preach** another gospel, not on weak believers who have been confused for a time; the confused Galatians he still calls "brothers" (1:11).
- He includes himself: "even if we..." The measure is not a preacher's fame, gifts or experiences (not even "an angel from heaven") but the gospel itself.

**4. Pleasing man?** "For am I now seeking the approval of man, or of God? Or am I trying to please man? If I were still trying to please man, I would not be a servant of Christ" (1:10). Some may have charged that Paul dropped circumcision for Gentiles to please them and make the gospel easier to accept. Paul says that words as strong as these are plainly not the words of a man-pleaser. Whoever would serve Christ cannot make pleasing people his aim.

> The point: There is only one gospel: God's free grace in Christ. Any "gospel" that adds conditions to Christ is not good news. The measure of a preacher is not his fame or experience but whether he preaches this gospel.

## Going Deeper 1:11–17 Through a Revelation of Jesus Christ

**1. Not man's gospel.** "For I would have you know, brothers, that the gospel that was preached by me is not man's gospel. For I did not receive it from any man, nor was I taught it, but I received it through a revelation of Jesus Christ" (1:11–12). Paul does not mean he never learned anything about Jesus from others (cf. 1 Cor 15:3). He means the heart of the gospel and his commission were revealed to him by the risen Christ himself.

**2. My former life.** "For you have heard of my former life in Judaism, how I persecuted the church of God violently and tried to destroy it. And I was advancing in Judaism beyond many of my own age among my people, so extremely zealous was I for the traditions of my fathers" (1:13–14). Paul was not religiously indifferent; he was among the most zealous and accomplished. His zeal drove him to persecute the church. A person can be very devout and very zealous and still be God's enemy.

**3. But God.** "But when he who had set me apart before I was born, and who called me by his grace, was pleased to reveal his Son to me, in order that I might preach him among the Gentiles..." (1:15–16). The turning point is "but" and "he." Paul's change was not his finding the truth but God taking the initiative:
- **"Set me apart before I was born":** this echoes Jeremiah and the Servant of Isaiah (Jer 1:5; Isa 49:1). God had a plan for Paul before his birth, and even the years he persecuted the church were in God's hands.
- **"Called me by his grace":** the call is grace, not reward. God called a man who was persecuting his church.
- **"To reveal his Son to me":** literally "in me," which may also mean "through me"; either way, God himself made Christ known to Paul.

**4. Into Arabia.** "I did not immediately consult with anyone; nor did I go up to Jerusalem to those who were apostles before me, but I went away into Arabia, and returned again to Damascus" (1:16–17). "Arabia" probably means the Nabataean kingdom south and east of Damascus (cf. 2 Cor 11:32). Paul does not say what he did there. Some think he preached, which is why King Aretas's men sought to seize him; others think it was a quiet time of reflecting on Scripture.

> The point: Paul was a zealous persecutor of the church, yet God "called me by his grace" and revealed his Son to him. Paul's change was not his finding God but God finding him; even his past years were in God's hands.

## Going Deeper 1:18–24 They Glorified God Because of Me

**1. Visiting Cephas.** "Then after three years I went up to Jerusalem to visit Cephas and remained with him fifteen days. But I saw none of the other apostles except James the Lord's brother" (1:18–19). "Visit" can mean to get to know or consult. Paul is not denying fellowship with the apostles but showing his gospel did not come from them. "James the Lord's brother" is taken by most Protestant scholars as a son later born to Mary and Joseph (cf. Mark 6:3); Catholic tradition takes him as a cousin of Jesus; Orthodox tradition as a son of Joseph by an earlier marriage.

**2. I do not lie.** "(In what I am writing to you, before God, I do not lie!)" (1:20). Paul takes an oath here, which shows the agitators were attacking his honesty.

**3. Syria and Cilicia.** "Then I went into the regions of Syria and Cilicia" (1:21). Paul's hometown, Tarsus, was in Cilicia (Acts 21:39). These were long years about which the New Testament says little.

**4. They glorified God because of me.** "They only were hearing it said, 'He who used to persecute us is now preaching the faith he once tried to destroy.' And they glorified God because of me" (1:23–24). The churches of Judea had never seen Paul, but they had heard. They did not say "this man can't be trusted"; they glorified God because of him. A forgiven sinner's changed life is itself a witness to God's grace.

> The point: The man who once persecuted the church now preached the faith he had tried to destroy, and the church glorified God for it. A changed life shows God's grace most clearly.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But when he who had set me apart before I was born, and who called me by his grace, was pleased to reveal his Son to me..." (1:15–16). Do I often look back on years with regret: the years before I believed, far from God, perhaps mocking Christians; the years after I believed, cold and on the edge of church life; or years of obscurity that seemed to bear no fruit? Paul once persecuted the church, and after his conversion spent several years in Arabia, Syria and Cilicia, with no one knowing what he was doing. Yet he says God had set him apart "before I was born." God did not wait until he was better to begin writing his story; even the years he thought wasted were in God's hands. God "called me by his grace": Paul was called not because he deserved it but because Christ "gave himself for our sins" (1:4). This does not make past sins nothing; it means Christ has already given himself for those sins, and God's grace is greater than my past. Today, can I bring before God the years I most wish I could erase, confess the sin in them, and believe that the God who "called me by his grace" did not let go of me even then? Perhaps one day others will "glorify God because of me."
