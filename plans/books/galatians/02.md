## 本章脉络

第二章有三段。**在耶路撒冷**（1-10节）：保罗带着巴拿巴和提多上耶路撒冷，把他所传的福音向「那有名望之人」陈说；外邦人提多「也没有勉强他受割礼」；教会的柱石雅各、矶法、约翰「向我和巴拿巴用右手行相交之礼」。**在安提阿**（11-14节）：彼得本来和外邦信徒一同吃饭，后来「因怕奉割礼的人」就退去了；保罗「当面抵挡他」，因为这「与福音的真理不合」。**因信称义**（15-21节）：「人称义不是因行律法，乃是因信耶稣基督」；「我已经与基督同钉十字架，现在活着的不再是我，乃是基督在我里面活着」。

第一章说明保罗的福音不是从人来的；第二章说明这福音得到了耶路撒冷使徒的认可，并且保罗为了这福音，连彼得也敢当面抵挡。最后，保罗用几句话说出了这福音的核心。

## 深读 2:1-10 右手行相交之礼

**1. 过了十四年。** 「过了十四年，我同巴拿巴又上耶路撒冷去，并带着提多同去。」（2:1）这十四年是从他信主算起，还是从第一次上耶路撒冷（1:18）算起，学者有不同的看法。这次上耶路撒冷，是使徒行传11:30送捐项的那次，还是使徒行传15章的会议，也有两种看法（见导读）。

**2. 提多没有受割礼。** 「但与我同去的提多，虽是希腊人，也没有勉强他受割礼。」（2:3）提多是一个活生生的测试案例：一个没有受割礼的外邦信徒，被耶路撒冷的使徒接纳为弟兄。这证明，外邦人不必先成为犹太人，才能成为基督徒。

**3. 一刻也没有容让。** 「因为有偷着引进来的假弟兄，私下窥探我们在基督耶稣里的自由，要叫我们作奴仆。我们就是一刻的工夫也没有容让顺服他们，为要叫福音的真理仍存在你们中间。」（2:4-5）保罗在别的事上可以很有弹性（林前9:20-22），但在福音的真理上，他「一刻的工夫也没有容让」。在不涉及福音的事上，他可以为了爱人而让步；一旦有人把守律法当作得救的条件，他就一步也不让。

**4. 神不以外貌取人。** 「至于那些有名望的，不论他是何等人，都与我无干。神不以外貌取人。」（2:6）保罗尊重耶路撒冷的使徒，却不以他们的地位为福音的根据。福音的权威在神，不在人的名望。

**5. 同一位神在两人身上作工。** 「那感动彼得、叫他为受割礼之人作使徒的，也感动我，叫我为外邦人作使徒……就向我和巴拿巴用右手行相交之礼。」（2:8-9）这是彼此承认、彼此合作的记号：同一个福音，同一位神，不同的工场。

**6. 记念穷人。** 「只是愿意我们记念穷人；这也是我本来热心去行的。」（2:10）福音的真理和爱穷人的心，从一开始就是连在一起的（参林后8–9章）。

> 要点：保罗在不涉及福音的事上可以让步，在福音的真理上却一刻也不容让。耶路撒冷的使徒承认，同一位神藉着同一个福音，在不同的人中间作工。

## 深读 2:11-14 当面抵挡矶法

**1. 彼得的转变。** 「从雅各那里来的人未到以先，他和外邦人一同吃饭，及至他们来到，他因怕奉割礼的人，就退去与外邦人隔开了。」（2:12）彼得早就知道「无论什么人都不可看作俗而不洁净的」（徒10:28），也曾因为和外邦人一同吃饭被人责难（徒11:2-3）。他不是不懂，而是「怕」。「从雅各那里来的人」是否真的代表雅各的意思，经文没有说；使徒行传15:24记载，有人从耶路撒冷出去搅扰人，使徒们「并没有吩咐他们」。

**2. 一同吃饭为什么这么重要？** 在当时，一同吃饭表示彼此接纳为一家人。主耶稣常常「和税吏并罪人一同吃饭」（太9:11），引起法利赛人的不满。彼得退去不和外邦信徒吃饭，等于告诉他们：你们还不够资格作我们的弟兄，除非你们先成为犹太人。这不只是礼仪的问题，而是在行动上否认了福音。

**3. 连巴拿巴也随伙装假。** 「其余的犹太人也都随着他装假，甚至连巴拿巴也随伙装假。」（2:13）「装假」原文就是「假冒为善」，指演员戴着面具演戏。彼得心里知道真理，行为上却装作另一回事。一位领袖的妥协，会带着许多人一起偏离，连巴拿巴这位「劝慰子」（徒4:36）也被带走了。

**4. 当面抵挡。** 「但我一看见他们行的不正，与福音的真理不合，就在众人面前对矶法说：『你既是犹太人，若随外邦人行事，不随犹太人行事，怎么还勉强外邦人随犹太人呢？』」（2:14）彼得的错是公开的，影响整个教会，并且关系到福音的真理，所以保罗公开地处理。这不是叫我们随便在众人面前羞辱人；私人的过犯，应当先私下劝诫（太18:15）。后来彼得称保罗为「我们所亲爱的兄弟保罗」（彼后3:15），可见这次冲突并没有毁掉他们的关系。

> 要点：彼得不是不懂福音，而是因为怕人，在行动上否认了福音。在基督里，信徒彼此接纳的根据只有基督，不能加上任何别的条件；一位领袖的妥协，也会带走许多人。

## 深读 2:15-21 基督在我里面活着

**1. 不是因行律法，乃是因信。** 「既知道人称义不是因行律法，乃是因信耶稣基督，连我们也信了基督耶稣，使我们因信基督称义，不因行律法称义；因为凡有血气的，没有一人因行律法称义。」（2:16）这一节三次说「不因行律法」，三次说「因信」。「凡有血气的，没有一人……称义」很可能是引用诗篇143:2「在你面前，凡活着的人没有一个是义的」。
- **「称义」：** 是法庭用语，指神宣告一个人在他面前为义，不是指人变得在道德上完全。
- **「行律法」：** 宗教改革以来的传统理解是，指人想靠遵守律法赚得神的接纳；也有学者（如詹姆斯·邓恩等「保罗新观」学者）认为，这里特别指割礼、饮食、守安息日等把犹太人和外邦人分开的「身份标记」。许多改革宗学者回应说，在安提阿的事件中确实牵涉到这些标记，但保罗在3:10和5:3都把「行律法」扩展到遵行全部律法，所以问题最终是人能不能靠自己的行为称义。
- **「信耶稣基督」：** 多数译本译作信靠耶稣基督；也有学者（如理查德·海斯）认为可以译作耶稣基督的信实，强调基督忠心的顺服。两种理解都承认，称义的根据是基督，不是我们的行为。

**2. 基督是叫人犯罪的吗？** 「我们若求在基督里称义，却仍旧是罪人，难道基督是叫人犯罪的吗？断乎不是！」（2:17）有人反对说：如果犹太人和外邦人一同吃饭，就是把自己和「外邦的罪人」放在一起，这样基督岂不是叫人犯罪吗？保罗说「断乎不是」：真正叫人成为违犯者的，是重新建立那已经拆毁的墙（2:18）。

**3. 向律法死了。** 「我因律法，就向律法死了，叫我可以向神活着。」（2:19）律法定了我死罪；在基督里，我已经和他一同承受了那刑罚（参罗7:4）。律法对我的要求已经在基督的死里满足了，所以我不再是在律法之下求称义，而是「向神活着」。

**4. 与基督同钉十字架。** 「我已经与基督同钉十字架，现在活着的不再是我，乃是基督在我里面活着；并且我如今在肉身活着，是因信神的儿子而活；他是爱我，为我舍己。」（2:20）这一节是全书的中心：
- **「与基督同钉十字架」：** 原文是完成时，表示这是已经发生、持续有效的事实。基督死的时候，信徒在他里面也死了：旧我的定罪已经结束。
- **「基督在我里面活着」：** 这不是说我的个性消失了，而是说我生命的中心和能力，现在是基督。
- **「因信神的儿子而活」：** 每一天的生活，仍然是靠信，不是靠律法。
- **「他是爱我，为我舍己」：** 马丁·路德特别留意这里的「我」：基督不只是爱世人，也是爱我，为我舍己。

**5. 不废掉神的恩。** 「我不废掉神的恩；义若是藉着律法得的，基督就是徒然死了。」（2:21）如果人可以靠律法称义，十字架就没有必要了。在基督之外加上任何条件，等于说基督白死了。

> 要点：人称义不是因行律法，乃是因信耶稣基督。我已经与基督同钉十字架，现在活着的是基督在我里面；我每天的生活，都是靠着信那位爱我、为我舍己的神的儿子。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他因怕奉割礼的人，就退去与外邦人隔开了。」（2:12）我是否也像彼得一样，在不同的人面前「装假」？在教会里，我是一个热心的基督徒；在家族聚餐、公司饭局、同学聚会里，我却不敢说自己信主，或者随着大家说话、做事，好像我和他们没有两样？或者在教会里，因为怕某一群有影响力的人，我就不敢接近那些被他们看不起的弟兄姊妹？彼得不是不懂福音，他只是怕。可是那位他曾经三次否认的主，没有离弃他。保罗说：「他是爱我，为我舍己。」（2:20）当我还在两边讨好、怕失去别人认可的时候，基督已经为我舍了自己。我在神面前的身份，已经由他确定了；我不需要再靠任何一群人的接纳，才知道自己是谁。今天，我能不能向神承认，我在哪一个场合、哪一群人面前，因为怕而「隔开了」？然后，我能不能在那个地方，安静地活出一个被基督所爱的人的样子？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 has three parts. **In Jerusalem** (vv. 1–10): Paul went up to Jerusalem with Barnabas and Titus and set before "those who seemed influential" the gospel he preached; Titus, a Gentile, "was not forced to be circumcised"; and the pillars James, Cephas and John "gave the right hand of fellowship to Barnabas and me." **At Antioch** (vv. 11–14): Peter had been eating with Gentile believers, but drew back "fearing the circumcision party"; Paul "opposed him to his face," because this was "not in step with the truth of the gospel." **Justified by faith** (vv. 15–21): "a person is not justified by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ"; "I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me."

Chapter 1 showed that Paul's gospel did not come from men; chapter 2 shows that the Jerusalem apostles recognized it, and that for this gospel Paul would oppose even Peter to his face. Finally Paul sums up its heart in a few sentences.

## Going Deeper 2:1–10 The Right Hand of Fellowship

**1. After fourteen years.** "Then after fourteen years I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus along with me" (2:1). Scholars differ on whether the fourteen years count from his conversion or from his first visit (1:18). They also differ on whether this visit is the famine-relief visit of Acts 11:30 or the council of Acts 15 (see the Introduction).

**2. Titus was not circumcised.** "But even Titus, who was with me, was not forced to be circumcised, though he was a Greek" (2:3). Titus was a living test case: an uncircumcised Gentile believer accepted as a brother by the Jerusalem apostles. This shows that Gentiles need not become Jews first in order to become Christians.

**3. Not for a moment.** "Yet because of false brothers secretly brought in- who slipped in to spy out our freedom that we have in Christ Jesus, so that they might bring us into slavery- to them we did not yield in submission even for a moment, so that the truth of the gospel might be preserved for you" (2:4–5). In other matters Paul could be very flexible (1 Cor 9:20–22), but on the truth of the gospel he did "not yield... even for a moment." Where the gospel was not at stake, he could give way out of love; once anyone made law-keeping a condition of salvation, he would not give an inch.

**4. God shows no partiality.** "And from those who seemed to be influential (what they were makes no difference to me; God shows no partiality)" (2:6). Paul respected the Jerusalem apostles but did not ground the gospel in their standing. The gospel's authority is in God, not in human reputation.

**5. The same God at work in both.** "(for he who worked through Peter for his apostolic ministry to the circumcised worked also through me for mine to the Gentiles), and when James and Cephas and John... perceived the grace that was given to me, they gave the right hand of fellowship to Barnabas and me" (2:8–9). This was a sign of mutual recognition and partnership: one gospel, one God, different fields.

**6. Remember the poor.** "Only, they asked us to remember the poor, the very thing I was eager to do" (2:10). The truth of the gospel and love for the poor belonged together from the start (cf. 2 Cor 8–9).

> The point: Paul could give way where the gospel was not at stake, but on the truth of the gospel he would not yield for a moment. The Jerusalem apostles recognized that the same God was at work through the same gospel among different peoples.

## Going Deeper 2:11–14 Opposing Cephas to His Face

**1. Peter's change.** "For before certain men came from James, he was eating with the Gentiles; but when they came he drew back and separated himself, fearing the circumcision party" (2:12). Peter already knew that he "should not call any person common or unclean" (Acts 10:28), and had been criticized for eating with Gentiles (Acts 11:2–3). He did not lack understanding; he was afraid. Whether "certain men... from James" truly represented James's view, the text does not say; Acts 15:24 records that some went out from Jerusalem and troubled people, "although we gave them no instructions."

**2. Why did eating together matter so much?** In that world, sharing a meal meant accepting one another as family. Jesus often ate "with tax collectors and sinners" (Matt 9:11), to the Pharisees' displeasure. When Peter withdrew from Gentile believers' tables, he was telling them: you are not yet qualified to be our brothers unless you first become Jews. This was not merely a matter of custom; it denied the gospel in practice.

**3. Even Barnabas.** "And the rest of the Jews acted hypocritically along with him, so that even Barnabas was led astray by their hypocrisy" (2:13). "Hypocrisy" is the word for an actor playing behind a mask. Peter knew the truth in his heart but acted out something else. One leader's compromise can carry many with him; even Barnabas, the "son of encouragement," was swept along.

**4. Opposed to his face.** "But when I saw that their conduct was not in step with the truth of the gospel, I said to Cephas before them all, 'If you, though a Jew, live like a Gentile and not like a Jew, how can you force the Gentiles to live like Jews?'" (2:14). Peter's wrong was public, affected the whole church and touched the truth of the gospel, so Paul dealt with it publicly. This is no license to humiliate people in front of others; private faults should first be addressed privately (Matt 18:15). Later Peter calls Paul "our beloved brother Paul" (2 Pet 3:15), so this clash did not destroy their relationship.

> The point: Peter did understand the gospel, but out of fear of people he denied it in practice. In Christ the only ground on which believers welcome one another is Christ himself, with no conditions added; and a leader's compromise can carry many away.

## Going Deeper 2:15–21 Christ Lives in Me

**1. Not by works of the law, but through faith.** "...yet we know that a person is not justified by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ, so we also have believed in Christ Jesus, in order to be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of the law, because by works of the law no one will be justified" (2:16). This verse says "not by works of the law" three times and "faith" three times. "No one will be justified" probably echoes Psalm 143:2, "no one living is righteous before you."
- **"Justified":** a law-court word for God's declaring a person righteous before him, not for a person becoming morally perfect.
- **"Works of the law":** the traditional understanding since the Reformation is that this means trying to earn God's acceptance by keeping the law. Some scholars (such as James Dunn and others of the "New Perspective on Paul") think it refers especially to circumcision, food laws and the Sabbath, the "identity markers" that set Jews apart from Gentiles. Many Reformed scholars reply that such markers were indeed at stake at Antioch, but that in 3:10 and 5:3 Paul extends "works of the law" to keeping the whole law, so the question finally is whether anyone can be justified by his own works.
- **"Faith in Jesus Christ":** most translations render it this way; some scholars (such as Richard Hays) think it can be translated "the faithfulness of Jesus Christ," stressing Christ's faithful obedience. Both readings agree that the ground of justification is Christ, not our works.

**2. Is Christ a servant of sin?** "But if, in our endeavor to be justified in Christ, we too were found to be sinners, is Christ then a servant of sin? Certainly not!" (2:17). Someone objected: if Jews eat with Gentiles, they put themselves among "Gentile sinners," so doesn't Christ lead people into sin? Paul says, "Certainly not!" What really makes someone a transgressor is rebuilding the wall that was torn down (2:18).

**3. Dead to the law.** "For through the law I died to the law, so that I might live to God" (2:19). The law sentenced me to death; in Christ I have already undergone that penalty with him (cf. Rom 7:4). The law's demand on me has been met in Christ's death, so I am no longer seeking justification under the law; I "live to God."

**4. Crucified with Christ.** "I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me" (2:19–20). This is the center of the whole letter:
- **"Crucified with Christ":** the Greek perfect tense marks a completed fact with lasting effect. When Christ died, believers died in him; the condemnation of the old self is over.
- **"Christ who lives in me":** not that my personality disappears, but that the center and power of my life is now Christ.
- **"By faith in the Son of God":** daily life is still lived by faith, not by law.
- **"Who loved me and gave himself for me":** Martin Luther paid special attention to the "me" here: Christ loved not only the world but me, and gave himself for me.

**5. Not nullifying grace.** "I do not nullify the grace of God, for if justification were through the law, then Christ died for no purpose" (2:21). If people could be justified by the law, the cross would be unnecessary. To add any condition to Christ is to say Christ died for nothing.

> The point: A person is justified not by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ. I have been crucified with Christ, and now Christ lives in me; every day I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "...he drew back and separated himself, fearing the circumcision party" (2:12). Do I, like Peter, wear different masks before different people? At church I am a keen Christian; at family banquets, company dinners or school reunions I dare not say I believe, or I talk and act just like everyone else, as if I were no different from them. Or at church, because I fear an influential group, do I keep away from brothers and sisters they look down on? Peter understood the gospel; he was simply afraid. Yet the Lord he had denied three times did not abandon him. Paul says, "who loved me and gave himself for me" (2:20). While I am still trying to please both sides, afraid of losing people's approval, Christ has already given himself for me. My standing before God is settled by him; I no longer need any group's acceptance to know who I am. Today, can I confess to God in which setting, before which group, I have "drawn back" out of fear? And then, in that very place, can I quietly live as someone loved by Christ?
