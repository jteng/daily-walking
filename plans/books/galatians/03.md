## 本章脉络

第三章是全书论证的中心，可以分成几段。**你们怎样受了圣灵？**（1-5节）：「你们受了圣灵，是因行律法呢？是因听信福音呢？」**亚伯拉罕的子孙**（6-9节）：「亚伯拉罕信神，这就算为他的义」；「那以信为本的人，就是亚伯拉罕的子孙」。**咒诅与祝福**（10-14节）：「凡以行律法为本的，都是被咒诅的」；但「基督既为我们受了咒诅，就赎出我们脱离律法的咒诅」。**应许在律法以先**（15-22节）：神与亚伯拉罕所立的约，不能被「四百三十年以后的律法」废掉；那么，「律法是为什么有的呢？」**训蒙的师傅**（23-29节）：律法是「训蒙的师傅，引我们到基督那里」；如今「你们因信基督耶稣都是神的儿子」，「在基督耶稣里都成为一了」。

保罗用加拉太人自己的经历和旧约圣经，证明人得着圣灵、称义、成为亚伯拉罕的子孙，都是因着信，不是因着行律法。

## 深读 3:1-5 是因听信福音

**1. 谁迷惑了你们？** 「无知的加拉太人哪，耶稣基督钉十字架，已经活画在你们眼前，谁又迷惑了你们呢？」（3:1）「活画」原文有公开张贴告示的意思。保罗传福音时，把钉十字架的基督清清楚楚地「贴」在他们眼前。看见了十字架的人，怎么还会以为需要别的？

**2. 圣灵是怎样来的？** 「我只要问你们这一件：你们受了圣灵，是因行律法呢？是因听信福音呢？」（3:2）保罗请他们回想自己的经历：当他们领受圣灵的时候，他们还没有受割礼，也没有守摩西的律法，只是听了福音，信了。圣灵的同在就是神接纳他们的证据（参徒15:8-9）。

**3. 靠圣灵入门，靠肉身成全？** 「你们既靠圣灵入门，如今还靠肉身成全吗？你们是这样的无知吗？」（3:3）这是许多信徒的试探：觉得得救是靠恩典，成长却要靠自己。可是基督徒的生命从开始到完成，都是圣灵的工作。

> 要点：我们领受圣灵，是因为听信福音，不是因为行律法。靠恩典开始的生命，也要靠恩典走下去。

## 深读 3:6-9 亚伯拉罕信神

**1. 这就算为他的义。** 「正如『亚伯拉罕信神，这就算为他的义』。」（3:6；引创15:6）那些搅扰的人大概很喜欢讲亚伯拉罕，因为他是第一个受割礼的人（创17章）。保罗却指出，神算亚伯拉罕为义，是在他受割礼以前，是因为他信（参罗4:9-11）。

**2. 亚伯拉罕的子孙。** 「所以，你们要知道：那以信为本的人，就是亚伯拉罕的子孙。」（3:7）真正的亚伯拉罕的子孙，不是看血统，也不是看割礼，而是看有没有和亚伯拉罕一样的信心。

**3. 预先传福音给亚伯拉罕。** 「并且圣经既然预先看明，神要叫外邦人因信称义，就早已传福音给亚伯拉罕，说：『万国都必因你得福。』」（3:8；引创12:3；18:18）神呼召亚伯拉罕的时候，就已经计划要藉着他祝福万国。外邦人因信得救，不是神后来临时加上去的，而是从一开始就在他的应许里。

> 要点：亚伯拉罕是因信被算为义的，那时他还没有受割礼。凡和亚伯拉罕一样相信神的人，无论是犹太人还是外邦人，都是他的子孙，都与他一同得福。

## 深读 3:10-14 基督为我们受了咒诅

**1. 律法的咒诅。** 「凡以行律法为本的，都是被咒诅的；因为经上记着：『凡不常照律法书上所记一切之事去行的，就被咒诅。』」（3:10；引申27:26）律法要求的是完全的顺服，不是大部分。没有人能够常常遵行律法上所记的一切，所以凡是靠律法在神面前站立的人，都在咒诅之下。

**2. 义人必因信得生。** 「没有一个人靠着律法在神面前称义，这是明显的；因为经上说：『义人必因信得生。』律法原不本乎信，只说：『行这些事的，就必因此活着。』」（3:11-12；引哈2:4；利18:5）律法的原则是「做到就活」；福音的原则是「信就得生」。保罗不是说律法不好，而是说，罪人不能靠「做到」来得生命。

**3. 基督成了咒诅。** 「基督既为我们受了咒诅，就赎出我们脱离律法的咒诅；因为经上记着：『凡挂在木头上都是被咒诅的。』」（3:13；引申21:23）
- **「赎出」：** 原文是付上代价把人买回来，像从奴隶市场赎出奴隶。
- **「为我们受了咒诅」：** 原文直译是为我们成了咒诅。基督自己没有罪，却站在我们的位置上，承受了律法对罪人的咒诅。这就是代替的意思。
- **「挂在木头上」：** 申命记说，被处死的人若挂在木头上，就是在神面前被咒诅的。犹太人看十字架，以为这正证明耶稣是被神弃绝的；保罗却说，是的，他在十字架上确实承受了咒诅，但那是我们的咒诅。

**4. 亚伯拉罕的福临到外邦人。** 「这便叫亚伯拉罕的福，因基督耶稣可以临到外邦人，使我们因信得着所应许的圣灵。」（3:14）基督承受了咒诅，祝福就流向万国。这祝福的中心，就是「所应许的圣灵」。

> 要点：律法对每一个罪人宣告咒诅，因为没有人能常常遵行一切。但基督为我们成了咒诅，把我们赎出来；于是亚伯拉罕的福藉着他临到我们，我们因信得着所应许的圣灵。

## 深读 3:15-22 律法是为什么有的呢？

**1. 约一经立定。** 「弟兄们，我且照着人的常话说：虽然是人的文约，若已经立定了，就没有能废弃或加增的。」（3:15）连人立的约，一经正式确认，也不能随意更改，何况神的约。

**2. 那一个子孙。** 「所应许的原是向亚伯拉罕和他子孙说的。神并不是说『众子孙』，指着许多人，乃是说『你那一个子孙』，指着一个人，就是基督。」（3:16）希伯来文和希腊文的「子孙」（种子）是一个集合名词，可以指一个人，也可以指许多后代。保罗当然知道这一点（他在3:29就说信徒都是「亚伯拉罕的后裔」）。他的意思是，神的应许最终集中在一位后裔身上，就是基督；凡在基督里的人，也都因他成为亚伯拉罕的后裔。

**3. 四百三十年以后的律法。** 「神预先所立的约，不能被那四百三十年以后的律法废掉，叫应许归于虚空。」（3:17）「四百三十年」是根据出埃及记12:40（创15:13说的是约数「四百年」）。律法在应许以后很久才来，不能把应许变成有条件的交易。

**4. 律法为什么有？** 「这样说来，律法是为什么有的呢？原是为过犯添上的，等候那蒙应许的子孙来到，并且是藉天使经中保之手设立的。」（3:19）
- **「为过犯添上的」：** 解经家有几种理解：律法使人认识过犯、显明罪就是违背神（参罗3:20；4:15）；律法限制罪，像篱笆一样拦阻；律法甚至激起罪，使人看见自己需要救主（参罗5:20）。这几种理解并不互相排斥。
- **「等候那蒙应许的子孙来到」：** 律法有一个时间的界限，是直到基督来。
- **「藉天使经中保之手」：** 犹太传统认为天使参与了颁布律法（参申33:2；徒7:53），「中保」就是摩西。律法是间接颁布的；应许却是神亲自对亚伯拉罕说的。

**5. 律法与应许反对吗？** 「这样，律法是与神的应许反对吗？断乎不是！若曾传一个能叫人得生的律法，义就诚然本乎律法了。但圣经把众人都圈在罪里，使所应许的福因信耶稣基督，归给那信的人。」（3:21-22）律法不是神的敌人，它只是不能叫人得生命。它的作用是把所有人「圈在罪里」，叫人无路可走，只能投靠基督。

> 要点：律法在应许以后很久才来，不能把神白白的应许变成交易。律法是好的，却不能叫人得生命；它把所有人圈在罪里，好叫人只能因信基督得着应许。

## 深读 3:23-29 你们都是神的儿子

**1. 训蒙的师傅。** 「这样，律法是我们训蒙的师傅，引我们到基督那里，使我们因信称义。」（3:24）「训蒙的师傅」原文是希腊罗马家庭中负责看管孩子的奴仆，他带孩子上学、管他的行为，直到孩子长大。他不是老师，而是监护人。律法看守神的百姓，直到基督来；基督来了，监护的时期就结束了（3:25）。宗教改革的传统常说律法有三种用处：约束社会的罪恶、叫人认罪而归向基督、作信徒生活的准则（加尔文称后者为律法的第三种功用，路德宗则较强调前两种）。这里讲的主要是第二种。

**2. 都是神的儿子。** 「所以，你们因信基督耶稣都是神的儿子。你们受洗归入基督的都是披戴基督了。」（3:26-27）「儿子」在当时是承受产业的身份，女信徒也包括在内。「披戴基督」好像穿上一件新衣服：神看我们，是看我们在基督里。洗礼是这个联合的记号，但得救的根据仍然是信（3:26）。

**3. 在基督耶稣里都成为一。** 「并不分犹太人、希腊人，自主的、为奴的，或男或女，因为你们在基督耶稣里都成为一了。」（3:28）后来的拉比文献记载了一句犹太男人的祷告，感谢神没有使他成为外邦人、奴隶（或无知的人）或女人；许多学者认为保罗时代已有类似的说法。保罗说，在基督里，这三种分别都不能决定人在神面前的地位。这不是说民族、身份、性别的分别消失了（保罗在别处仍然分别对丈夫和妻子、主人和仆人说话），而是说这些分别不能再成为高低贵贱的根据。这节经文在教会关于女性事奉的讨论中常被引用：平等派认为它支持在教会职分上不分男女；互补派认为它讲的是在基督里的地位和产业，不直接决定教会中的职分。两派都同意，在神面前，男女同样尊贵、同样蒙恩。

**4. 承受产业的。** 「你们既属乎基督，就是亚伯拉罕的后裔，是照着应许承受产业的了。」（3:29）这里回到3:16：基督是那一个后裔，凡属基督的，也都是后裔。外邦信徒不必受割礼，已经完全是亚伯拉罕的家人。

> 要点：律法像看管孩子的监护人，引我们到基督那里。如今我们因信都是神的儿女，披戴了基督；在他里面，民族、身份和性别都不能决定人的高低。凡属基督的，都是亚伯拉罕的后裔，承受神的应许。

## 默想

**默想：** 「基督既为我们受了咒诅，就赎出我们脱离律法的咒诅……这便叫亚伯拉罕的福，因基督耶稣可以临到外邦人。」（3:13-14）我心里是否常常有一种「被咒诅」的感觉？也许是从小听人说我「命不好」「八字太硬」；也许是家里接连出事，有人说是祖坟风水不好，或者是有人「下了咒」；也许连在教会里，也有人说我身上有「世代的咒诅」，要我一再地去断开。也许更深的，是我自己心里知道，在神圣洁的律法面前，我确实是一个该受咒诅的罪人。福音告诉我：真正的咒诅，是罪人在神面前的定罪；而这咒诅，基督已经在十字架上替我承受了。他被挂在木头上，为我们「受了咒诅」，好叫我得着亚伯拉罕的福。凡在基督里的人，不再在咒诅之下，「如今，那些在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了」（罗8:1）。生病、失业、家中的不幸都是真实的痛苦，但不是神对我的咒诅。今天，我能不能把那句压在我心上的「你就是命苦」带到十字架前，然后对自己说：我的咒诅，基督已经担当了；我现在是神的儿女，「照着应许承受产业」（3:29）？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 is the center of the letter's argument, and falls into several parts. **How did you receive the Spirit?** (vv. 1–5): "Did you receive the Spirit by works of the law or by hearing with faith?" **Sons of Abraham** (vv. 6–9): Abraham "believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness"; "it is those of faith who are the sons of Abraham." **Curse and blessing** (vv. 10–14): "all who rely on works of the law are under a curse," but "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us." **The promise came before the law** (vv. 15–22): God's covenant with Abraham cannot be annulled by "the law, which came 430 years afterward"; "Why then the law?" **The guardian** (vv. 23–29): the law "was our guardian until Christ came"; now "in Christ Jesus you are all sons of God, through faith," "all one in Christ Jesus."

From the Galatians' own experience and from the Old Testament, Paul shows that receiving the Spirit, being justified and becoming Abraham's children all come by faith, not by works of the law.

## Going Deeper 3:1–5 By Hearing with Faith

**1. Who has bewitched you?** "O foolish Galatians! Who has bewitched you? It was before your eyes that Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed as crucified" (3:1). "Publicly portrayed" is the word for posting a public notice. When Paul preached, he "posted" Christ crucified plainly before their eyes. How could anyone who had seen the cross think something more was needed?

**2. How did the Spirit come?** "Let me ask you only this: Did you receive the Spirit by works of the law or by hearing with faith?" (3:2). Paul asks them to recall their own experience: when they received the Spirit, they had not been circumcised or kept the law of Moses; they had simply heard the gospel and believed. The Spirit's presence was the proof that God had accepted them (cf. Acts 15:8–9).

**3. Begun by the Spirit, perfected by the flesh?** "Are you so foolish? Having begun by the Spirit, are you now being perfected by the flesh?" (3:3). This is the temptation of many believers: to think salvation is by grace but growth is up to us. Yet the Christian life, from beginning to end, is the work of the Spirit.

> The point: We received the Spirit by hearing with faith, not by works of the law. A life begun by grace must go on by grace.

## Going Deeper 3:6–9 Abraham Believed God

**1. Counted as righteousness.** "...just as Abraham 'believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness'?" (3:6; quoting Gen 15:6). The agitators probably liked to talk about Abraham, the first man to be circumcised (Gen 17). Paul points out that God counted Abraham righteous before he was circumcised, because he believed (cf. Rom 4:9–11).

**2. Sons of Abraham.** "Know then that it is those of faith who are the sons of Abraham" (3:7). Abraham's true children are known not by bloodline or circumcision but by sharing Abraham's faith.

**3. The gospel preached beforehand to Abraham.** "And the Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, preached the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, 'In you shall all the nations be blessed'" (3:8; quoting Gen 12:3; 18:18). When God called Abraham he already planned to bless the nations through him. Gentiles being saved by faith was not an afterthought; it was in the promise from the start.

> The point: Abraham was counted righteous by faith before he was circumcised. All who believe God as Abraham did, Jew or Gentile, are his children and are blessed with him.

## Going Deeper 3:10–14 Christ Became a Curse for Us

**1. The curse of the law.** "For all who rely on works of the law are under a curse; for it is written, 'Cursed be everyone who does not abide by all things written in the Book of the Law, and do them'" (3:10; quoting Deut 27:26). The law demands complete obedience, not most of it. No one keeps all that is written in it, so all who rely on the law to stand before God are under a curse.

**2. The righteous shall live by faith.** "Now it is evident that no one is justified before God by the law, for 'The righteous shall live by faith.' But the law is not of faith, rather 'The one who does them shall live by them'" (3:11–12; quoting Hab 2:4; Lev 18:5). The law's principle is "do this and live"; the gospel's is "believe and live." Paul does not say the law is bad; he says sinners cannot gain life by "doing."

**3. Christ became a curse.** "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us- for it is written, 'Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree'-" (3:13; quoting Deut 21:23).
- **"Redeemed":** to buy back at a price, like freeing a slave from the market.
- **"Becoming a curse for us":** Christ had no sin of his own, but took our place and bore the curse the law pronounces on sinners. This is substitution.
- **"Hanged on a tree":** Deuteronomy says that an executed man hung on a tree is cursed by God. Jews looked at the cross as proof that God had rejected Jesus; Paul says, yes, on the cross he truly bore the curse, but it was our curse.

**4. Abraham's blessing comes to the Gentiles.** "...so that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles, so that we might receive the promised Spirit through faith" (3:14). Christ bore the curse, and the blessing flows to the nations. At the heart of that blessing is "the promised Spirit."

> The point: The law pronounces a curse on every sinner, because no one keeps all of it. But Christ became a curse for us and redeemed us; so Abraham's blessing comes to us through him, and we receive the promised Spirit by faith.

## Going Deeper 3:15–22 Why Then the Law?

**1. A ratified covenant.** "To give a human example, brothers: even with a man-made covenant, no one annuls it or adds to it once it has been ratified" (3:15). Even a human covenant, once formally ratified, cannot be changed at will; how much less God's.

**2. The one offspring.** "Now the promises were made to Abraham and to his offspring. It does not say, 'And to offsprings,' referring to many, but referring to one, 'And to your offspring,' who is Christ" (3:16). The Hebrew and Greek word for "offspring" (seed) is a collective noun; it can mean one person or many descendants. Paul knows this well (in 3:29 he calls all believers "Abraham's offspring"). He means that God's promise finally centers on one offspring, Christ, and all who are in Christ become Abraham's offspring through him.

**3. The law 430 years later.** "This is what I mean: the law, which came 430 years afterward, does not annul a covenant previously ratified by God, so as to make the promise void" (3:17). "430 years" follows Exodus 12:40 (Gen 15:13 gives the round figure "four hundred years"). The law came long after the promise and cannot turn it into a conditional bargain.

**4. Why the law?** "Why then the law? It was added because of transgressions, until the offspring should come to whom the promise had been made, and it was put in place through angels by an intermediary" (3:19).
- **"Because of transgressions":** interpreters read this several ways: the law makes sin known as transgression against God (cf. Rom 3:20; 4:15); it restrains sin like a fence; it even provokes sin, so that people see their need of a Savior (cf. Rom 5:20). These readings are not mutually exclusive.
- **"Until the offspring should come":** the law had a time limit, until Christ came.
- **"Through angels by an intermediary":** Jewish tradition held that angels took part in giving the law (cf. Deut 33:2; Acts 7:53), and the "intermediary" was Moses. The law was given indirectly; the promise God spoke to Abraham himself.

**5. Is the law against the promises?** "Is the law then contrary to the promises of God? Certainly not! For if a law had been given that could give life, then righteousness would indeed be by the law. But the Scripture imprisoned everything under sin, so that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe" (3:21–22). The law is not God's enemy; it simply cannot give life. Its work is to "imprison everything under sin," leaving no way out but to trust Christ.

> The point: The law came long after the promise and cannot turn God's free promise into a bargain. The law is good but cannot give life; it shuts everyone up under sin so that the promise may be received only by faith in Christ.

## Going Deeper 3:23–29 You Are All Sons of God

**1. The guardian.** "So then, the law was our guardian until Christ came, in order that we might be justified by faith" (3:24). The "guardian" (*paidagōgos*) was the household slave in a Greco-Roman family who watched over a child, took him to school and supervised his behavior until he came of age. He was not a teacher but a custodian. The law watched over God's people until Christ came; now that Christ has come, the time of custody is over (3:25). The Reformation tradition often speaks of three uses of the law: restraining evil in society, driving people to Christ by showing their sin, and guiding believers' lives (Calvin called the last its "third use," while Lutherans stressed the first two). Here Paul mainly speaks of the second.

**2. All sons of God.** "...for in Christ Jesus you are all sons of God, through faith. For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ" (3:26–27). "Sons" was the status of heirs, and women believers are included. "Put on Christ" is like putting on new clothes: God sees us in Christ. Baptism is the sign of this union, but the ground of salvation is still faith (3:26).

**3. All one in Christ Jesus.** "There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is neither male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus" (3:28). A prayer recorded in later rabbinic sources has a Jewish man thank God for not making him a Gentile, a slave (or an ignorant man) or a woman, and many scholars think similar sayings were current in Paul's day. Paul says that in Christ none of these distinctions determines a person's standing before God. This does not mean ethnicity, social status and sex disappear (elsewhere Paul still addresses husbands and wives, masters and slaves separately), but that these differences can no longer rank people higher or lower. This verse is often cited in church discussions of women's ministry: egalitarians take it to support opening church offices without regard to sex; complementarians take it as speaking of standing and inheritance in Christ, not directly settling roles in church office. Both agree that before God men and women are equally honored and equally recipients of grace.

**4. Heirs.** "And if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to promise" (3:29). This returns to 3:16: Christ is the one offspring, and all who belong to Christ are offspring too. Gentile believers need not be circumcised; they are already fully members of Abraham's family.

> The point: The law was like a guardian over a child, leading us to Christ. Now by faith we are all God's children and have put on Christ; in him, ethnicity, status and sex cannot rank people higher or lower. All who belong to Christ are Abraham's offspring and heirs of God's promise.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us... so that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the Gentiles" (3:13–14). Do I often feel somehow "cursed"? Perhaps since childhood I have been told I was born under a bad fate, or that my birth chart was unlucky; perhaps after one family misfortune after another, someone blamed the feng shui of the ancestors' graves, or said someone had put a curse on us; perhaps even at church someone told me I carry a "generational curse" that I must keep breaking. Perhaps, deeper still, I know in my own heart that before God's holy law I truly am a sinner who deserves the curse. The gospel tells me that the real curse is a sinner's condemnation before God, and Christ has already borne that curse for me on the cross. He was hung on a tree, "becoming a curse for us," so that I might receive Abraham's blessing. Those who are in Christ are no longer under the curse: "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Rom 8:1). Illness, job loss and family misfortune are real pain, but they are not God's curse on me. Today, can I bring that sentence weighing on my heart, "you were just born unlucky," to the cross, and tell myself: my curse Christ has already borne; I am now God's child, an heir "according to promise" (3:29)?
