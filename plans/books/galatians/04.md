## 本章脉络

第四章有三段。**从奴仆到儿子**（1-11节）：承受产业的人在孩童的时候，和奴仆没有分别；「及至时候满足，神就差遣他的儿子」，「叫我们得着儿子的名分」，又「差他儿子的灵进入你们的心，呼叫：『阿爸！父！』」。保罗问加拉太人：既然认识了神，怎么还要「归回那懦弱无用的小学」？**保罗的恳求**（12-20节）：保罗提醒他们当初怎样接待他，又说：「我小子啊，我为你们再受生产之苦，直等到基督成形在你们心里。」**两个妇人，两个约**（21-31节）：夏甲和撒拉代表两个约；「我们不是使女的儿女，乃是自主妇人的儿女了。」

第三章讲到我们因信都是神的儿子（3:26）；第四章进一步说明，作儿子是什么意思，回到奴仆的样式又意味着什么。

## 深读 4:1-7 时候满足，神差遣他的儿子

**1. 孩童与奴仆。** 「我说那承受产业的，虽然是全业的主人，但为孩童的时候却与奴仆毫无分别，乃在师傅和管家的手下，直等他父亲预定的时候来到。」（4:1-2）一个富家的继承人，年幼时也要受管家管束，好像没有自由的奴仆；但到了父亲定的日子，他就成年，可以承受产业。

**2. 世俗小学。** 「我们为孩童的时候，受管于世俗小学之下，也是如此。」（4:3）「小学」原文是基本的元素、初阶的原则。解经家有几种理解：
- 指宗教上初阶的规条，包括犹太人的律法礼仪和外邦人的宗教习俗（参4:9-10）；
- 指当时人以为掌管世界的灵界势力或星宿（有些译本译作属灵势力）；
- 也有人认为指构成世界的基本元素（地、水、风、火），与4:10的历法相连；
- 也有人认为，宗教规条和灵界势力这两层意思都包括在内：人在基督以外的宗教生活，无论是犹太的还是外邦的，都受这些「小学」辖制（参西2:8、20）。

**3. 时候满足。** 「及至时候满足，神就差遣他的儿子，为女子所生，且生在律法以下，要把律法以下的人赎出来，叫我们得着儿子的名分。」（4:4-5）这两节是新约中对道成肉身最精简的叙述之一：
- **「差遣他的儿子」：** 许多解经家认为这里含有先存的意思：4:6同样说神「差」他儿子的灵，而圣灵本来就存在；保罗在别处也明说基督本有神的形像（腓2:6-7；罗8:3；林后8:9）。子受父差遣，并不表示他比父卑微；他是父永远的儿子。
- **「为女子所生」：** 他是真正的人，有真实的身体。
- **「生在律法以下」：** 他亲自活在律法之下，完全遵守律法，好把律法以下的人赎出来（参3:13）。
- **「儿子的名分」：** 原文是收养为儿子的意思。基督是神本来的儿子；我们是因着他被收养为儿子。在当时，「儿子」是承受产业的人；在基督里，女信徒也同样有这儿子的名分（参3:28）。

**4. 阿爸！父！** 「你们既为儿子，神就差他儿子的灵进入你们的心，呼叫：『阿爸！父！』」（4:6）这一节里有父神、儿子和圣灵。「阿爸」是亚兰文，是主耶稣在客西马尼园祷告时对父神的称呼（可14:36）。圣灵使我们能用耶稣自己的称呼来称呼神。次序也很重要：不是我们先呼叫「阿爸」，神才接纳我们为儿子；而是「你们既为儿子」，神就把圣灵赐下。这是逻辑上的次序，不是时间上先后的两个阶段：人一信，就得着儿子的名分，也领受了圣灵（3:2、14）。

**5. 不是奴仆，乃是儿子。** 「可见，从此以后，你不是奴仆，乃是儿子了；既是儿子，就靠着神为后嗣。」（4:7）这里的「你」是单数：保罗对每一个读者说话。奴仆做工是被迫的，怕受罚、怕被卖；儿子住在家里，是因为他是儿子。

> 要点：时候满足，神差遣他的儿子成为人，生在律法以下，把我们赎出来，叫我们被收养为儿子，又差他儿子的灵进入我们心里，呼叫「阿爸！父！」。我们不再是奴仆，乃是儿子，靠着神为后嗣。

## 深读 4:8-11 怎么还要归回？

**1. 本来不是神的。** 「但从前你们不认识神的时候，是给那些本来不是神的作奴仆。」（4:8）加拉太的外邦信徒从前拜偶像。

**2. 被神所认识。** 「现在你们既然认识神，更可说是被神所认识的，怎么还要归回那懦弱无用的小学，情愿再给他作奴仆呢？」（4:9）保罗特意改口：与其说「你们认识神」，不如说你们是被神所认识的。我们与神的关系，最根本的不是我们找到了神，而是神先认识了我们、拣选了我们（参摩3:2；林前8:3）。

**3. 谨守日子。** 「你们谨守日子、月份、节期、年份。」（4:10）多数解经家认为这里指犹太历法（安息日、月朔、节期、安息年）。保罗说加拉太人这样做是「归回」（4:9），把它和他们从前拜偶像放在同一类辖制之下。这不是说旧约的节期等于拜偶像（参罗14:5-6；西2:16-17：这些是影儿，那形体却是基督），而是说：一个已经在基督里得着儿子名分的人，若把守节期当作得神接纳的条件，就等于回到奴仆的样式。

**4. 我为你们害怕。** 「我为你们害怕，惟恐我在你们身上是枉费了工夫。」（4:11）保罗不是冷冷地纠正教义，而是真的害怕失去他们。

> 要点：我们认识神，是因为神先认识了我们。已经作了儿子的人，若再把宗教规条当作被神接纳的条件，不论那规条看起来多么属灵，都是回到奴仆的样式。

## 深读 4:12-20 直等到基督成形在你们心里

**1. 要像我一样。** 「弟兄们，我劝你们要像我一样，因为我也像你们一样。」（4:12）保罗是犹太人，却为了福音「像」外邦人一样生活，不再靠律法称义（参2:14；林前9:21）。他请加拉太人也像他一样，在基督的自由里站立。

**2. 身体的疾病。** 「你们知道我头一次传福音给你们，是因为身体有疾病。你们为我身体的缘故受试炼，没有轻看我，也没有厌弃我，反倒接待我，如同神的使者，如同基督耶稣。」（4:13-14）保罗大概是因为生病，才停留在加拉太，而有机会向他们传福音。这病是什么，我们不知道；从4:15「把自己的眼睛剜出来给我」来看，有人推测是眼疾，但这只是推测。在当时，有人会把疾病看作被神明咒诅的记号，加拉太人却没有因此轻看他。

**3. 成了你们的仇敌吗？** 「如今我将真理告诉你们，就成了你们的仇敌吗？」（4:16）那些搅扰的人对加拉太人很热心，「却不是好意」，是要他们依附自己（4:17）。保罗却冒着失去他们友谊的危险，告诉他们真理。

**4. 再受生产之苦。** 「我小子啊，我为你们再受生产之苦，直等到基督成形在你们心里。」（4:19）保罗用母亲生产的痛苦来形容自己对他们的爱。他要的不是他们跟随自己，而是「基督成形在你们心里」：基督的样式在他们生命中显出来。这是一切牧养的目标。

> 要点：保罗冒着失去友谊的危险，向加拉太人说真理，因为他像母亲一样爱他们。无论是牧者、父母还是小组长，我们关心人的目标，都不是叫人跟随自己，而是叫基督成形在人心里；这是神的工作，我们只是为此劳苦、祷告。

## 深读 4:21-31 自主妇人的儿女

**1. 两个儿子。** 「因为律法上记着，亚伯拉罕有两个儿子，一个是使女生的，一个是自主之妇人生的。然而，那使女所生的是按着血气生的；那自主之妇人所生的是凭着应许生的。」（4:22-23）以实玛利是亚伯拉罕和撒拉想用人的方法成就神应许时所生的（创16章）；以撒是神照着应许，在不可能的时候赐下的（创21章）。

**2. 这都是比方。** 「这都是比方：那两个妇人就是两约。」（4:24）「比方」原文的意思是用寓意来解释。保罗在这里用了一种当时犹太人熟悉的解经方式，把历史事件看作属灵真理的图画。但他不是随意发挥：他把夏甲和撒拉的故事，放在神整个救恩历史中看。这也不是叫我们随意用寓意解经，因为保罗是使徒，他的解释本身就是神所默示的。
- **夏甲：** 代表西奈山的约，「与现在的耶路撒冷同类」。解经家多认为这是指当时不信基督、靠律法称义的犹太教，也有人认为特别指以耶路撒冷为根据地、要外邦人受割礼的那一派。无论哪一种，这都不是种族的分别：保罗和众使徒都是犹太人，却是自主妇人的儿女；外邦教会若靠律法，也会落入夏甲的样式。
- **撒拉：** 代表应许的约，「那在上的耶路撒冷是自主的，她是我们的母」（4:26；参腓3:20；启21:2）。

**3. 不怀孕的要欢乐。** 「因为经上记着：不怀孕、不生养的，你要欢乐……因为没有丈夫的，比有丈夫的儿女更多。」（4:27；引赛54:1）以赛亚原来是对被掳、荒凉的耶路撒冷说的。以赛亚书54章紧接在53章受苦仆人的诗歌之后：荒凉者的儿女，是藉着仆人的死而来的。保罗说，这应许在福音中成就了：神藉着应许，生出许多儿女，包括外邦人在内。

**4. 把使女赶出去。** 「然而经上是怎么说的呢？是说：『把使女和她儿子赶出去！因为使女的儿子不可与自主妇人的儿子一同承受产业。』」（4:30；引创21:10）撒拉的话在创21:12得到神的认可。保罗的意思是：靠律法称义的道路，和凭应许称义的道路，不能并存；加拉太人要拒绝那些要他们回到律法之下的教导；也有人认为这是要教会把搅扰的人赶出去。这不是说要轻看夏甲这个人，或轻看阿拉伯人。在创世记中，神看顾被赶出去的夏甲，她称神为「看顾人的神」（创16:13；参21:17）；神也应许使以实玛利成为大国（创21:13、18）。这也不是对犹太人的敌意；保罗自己就是犹太人，他是在对比两种与神相处的方式。

> 要点：靠人的力量、靠律法与神相处，生出的是奴仆；凭神的应许，生出的是自由的儿女。我们像以撒一样，是凭着应许作儿女；我们的母是那在上自主的耶路撒冷。

## 默想

**默想：** 「可见，从此以后，你不是奴仆，乃是儿子了；既是儿子，就靠着神为后嗣。」（4:7）我和神的关系，是像一个儿女和父亲，还是像一个员工和老板？我是否常常觉得要「表现好」才能保住自己的位置：服事做得不好就觉得神要把我「开除」，就像小时候，只有考得好才得到父母的笑脸；生活一顺利就觉得是自己表现好，一不顺就怀疑是不是做错了什么，要被扣分了？也许我从小认识的「父亲」，就是一位只看成绩、很少夸奖、我总怕让他失望的人，所以连对神，我也只会交成绩单。可是天父不是放大了的我的父亲；他是那位先认识了我（4:9），又差遣自己儿子来寻找我的父。保罗在罗马书说：「你们所受的，不是奴仆的心，仍旧害怕；所受的，乃是儿子的心，因此我们呼叫：『阿爸！父！』」（罗8:15）主耶稣也对门徒说：「以后我不再称你们为仆人……我乃称你们为朋友。」（约15:15）神差遣他的儿子「生在律法以下」，替我承担了律法的要求和咒诅，「叫我们得着儿子的名分」（4:5）。我的位置不是我赚来的，是基督为我买来的；也不会因为我这个月表现不好就被收回。今天，我能不能把心里那张绩效考核表放下，用圣灵赐给我的那个称呼，单单地叫一声：「阿爸！父！」？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 has three parts. **From slaves to sons** (vv. 1–11): an heir, while a child, is no different from a slave; "when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son," "so that we might receive adoption as sons," and "sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, 'Abba! Father!'" Paul asks the Galatians: now that you know God, how can you "turn back again to the weak and worthless elementary principles"? **Paul's appeal** (vv. 12–20): Paul reminds them how they first received him, and says, "my little children, for whom I am again in the anguish of childbirth until Christ is formed in you!" **Two women, two covenants** (vv. 21–31): Hagar and Sarah stand for two covenants; "we are not children of the slave but of the free woman."

Chapter 3 said that by faith we are all sons of God (3:26); chapter 4 goes on to show what being a son means, and what it means to go back to being a slave.

## Going Deeper 4:1–7 In the Fullness of Time God Sent His Son

**1. Child and slave.** "I mean that the heir, as long as he is a child, is no different from a slave, though he is the owner of everything, but he is under guardians and managers until the date set by his father" (4:1–2). The heir of a wealthy house, while young, is under stewards like a slave with no freedom; but on the date his father sets, he comes of age and enters his inheritance.

**2. The elementary principles of the world.** "In the same way we also, when we were children, were enslaved to the elementary principles of the world" (4:3). The word means basic elements or first principles. Interpreters read it several ways:
- the elementary rules of religion, both Jewish law-observance and Gentile religious customs (cf. 4:9–10);
- the spiritual powers or stars that people then believed governed the world (some translations have "elemental spirits");
- some take it as the physical elements of the world (earth, water, air, fire), linked with the calendar of 4:10;
- some think the first two senses (religious rules and spiritual powers) are both included: all religious life apart from Christ, Jewish or pagan, is in bondage to these "principles" (cf. Col 2:8, 20).

**3. The fullness of time.** "But when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son, born of woman, born under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, so that we might receive adoption as sons" (4:4–5). These two verses are among the most compact statements of the incarnation in the New Testament:
- **"Sent forth his Son":** many interpreters see preexistence here: in 4:6 God likewise "sends" the Spirit, who already exists, and Paul elsewhere says plainly that Christ was in the form of God (Phil 2:6–7; Rom 8:3; 2 Cor 8:9). Being sent by the Father does not make the Son less than the Father; he is the Father's eternal Son.
- **"Born of woman":** he was truly human, with a real body.
- **"Born under the law":** he lived under the law himself and kept it perfectly, in order to redeem those under the law (cf. 3:13).
- **"Adoption as sons":** Christ is God's Son by nature; we are adopted as sons through him. In that world sons were the heirs; in Christ, women believers receive this same full status as heirs (cf. 3:28).

**4. Abba! Father!** "And because you are sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, 'Abba! Father!'" (4:6). The Father, the Son and the Spirit are all in this verse. "Abba" is Aramaic, the word Jesus used for his Father in Gethsemane (Mark 14:36). The Spirit enables us to address God with Jesus' own word. The order matters too: it is not that we first cry "Abba" and then God accepts us as sons, but "because you are sons," God has sent the Spirit. This is a logical order, not two stages in time: the moment we believe, we are adopted and receive the Spirit (3:2, 14).

**5. No longer a slave, but a son.** "So you are no longer a slave, but a son, and if a son, then an heir through God" (4:7). "You" here is singular: Paul speaks to each reader. A slave works under compulsion, fearing punishment or being sold; a son lives in the house because he is a son.

> The point: In the fullness of time God sent his Son, born under the law, to redeem us so that we might be adopted as sons, and sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, "Abba! Father!" We are no longer slaves but sons, and heirs through God.

## Going Deeper 4:8–11 How Can You Turn Back?

**1. Those that are not gods.** "Formerly, when you did not know God, you were enslaved to those that by nature are not gods" (4:8). The Gentile believers of Galatia had once worshiped idols.

**2. Known by God.** "But now that you have come to know God, or rather to be known by God, how can you turn back again to the weak and worthless elementary principles of the world, whose slaves you want to be once more?" (4:9). Paul deliberately corrects himself: rather than "you have come to know God," "you have been known by God." At bottom our relationship with God is not that we found him, but that he first knew and chose us (cf. Amos 3:2; 1 Cor 8:3).

**3. Observing days.** "You observe days and months and seasons and years!" (4:10). Most interpreters take these as the Jewish calendar (Sabbaths, new moons, feasts, sabbatical years). Paul calls this "turning back" (4:9), putting it in the same kind of bondage as their former idolatry. He is not saying the Old Testament festivals are idolatry (cf. Rom 14:5–6; Col 2:16–17: a shadow, whose substance is Christ). It means that someone who already has sonship in Christ, if he makes observing days a condition of God's acceptance, has gone back to a slave's way of life.

**4. I am afraid for you.** "I am afraid I may have labored over you in vain" (4:11). Paul is not coldly correcting doctrine; he is truly afraid of losing them.

> The point: We know God because God first knew us. Anyone who is already a son and again makes religious rules, however spiritual they look, a condition of God's acceptance has gone back to a slave's way of life.

## Going Deeper 4:12–20 Until Christ Is Formed in You

**1. Become as I am.** "Brothers, I entreat you, become as I am, for I also have become as you are" (4:12). Paul, a Jew, had for the gospel's sake become "like" a Gentile, no longer relying on the law for justification (cf. 2:14; 1 Cor 9:21). He asks the Galatians to become like him, standing in the freedom of Christ.

**2. A bodily ailment.** "You know it was because of a bodily ailment that I preached the gospel to you at first, and though my condition was a trial to you, you did not scorn or despise me, but received me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus" (4:13–14). Paul probably stayed in Galatia because of illness, and so had the opportunity to preach. We do not know what the illness was; from 4:15, "you would have gouged out your eyes and given them to me," some guess an eye disease, but this is only a guess. In that world some took illness as a sign of a god's curse, yet the Galatians did not despise him.

**3. Your enemy?** "Have I then become your enemy by telling you the truth?" (4:16). The agitators made much of the Galatians, "but for no good purpose"; they wanted the Galatians dependent on them (4:17). Paul risked losing their friendship to tell them the truth.

**4. The anguish of childbirth.** "...my little children, for whom I am again in the anguish of childbirth until Christ is formed in you!" (4:19). Paul uses a mother's labor pains to describe his love for them. He wants not that they follow him but that "Christ is formed in you," that Christ's likeness appear in their lives. This is the goal of all pastoral care.

> The point: Paul risked losing their friendship to tell the Galatians the truth, because he loved them like a mother. Whether as pastors, parents or small-group leaders, our aim in caring for others is not that they follow us but that Christ be formed in them; that is God's work, and we labor and pray toward it.

## Going Deeper 4:21–31 Children of the Free Woman

**1. Two sons.** "For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by a slave woman and one by a free woman. But the son of the slave was born according to the flesh, while the son of the free woman was born through promise" (4:22–23). Ishmael was born when Abraham and Sarah tried to fulfill God's promise by human means (Gen 16); Isaac was given by God according to his promise, when it was impossible (Gen 21).

**2. Interpreted allegorically.** "Now this may be interpreted allegorically: these women are two covenants" (4:24). Paul uses a way of reading Scripture familiar to Jews of his day, treating historical events as pictures of spiritual truth. But he is not free-associating: he reads the story of Hagar and Sarah within the whole history of God's salvation. Nor is this a license for us to allegorize at will; Paul is an apostle, and his interpretation is itself inspired by God.
- **Hagar:** stands for the covenant from Sinai, which "corresponds to the present Jerusalem." Many take this as the Judaism of Paul's day that did not believe in Christ and sought justification by the law; others take it more narrowly as the Jerusalem-based party pressing circumcision on Gentiles. Either way this is not about ethnicity: Paul and the apostles were Jews and children of the free woman, and a Gentile church that relies on law falls into Hagar's pattern.
- **Sarah:** stands for the covenant of promise; "the Jerusalem above is free, and she is our mother" (4:26; cf. Phil 3:20; Rev 21:2).

**3. Rejoice, O barren one.** "For it is written, 'Rejoice, O barren one who does not bear... For the children of the desolate one will be more than those of the one who has a husband'" (4:27; quoting Isa 54:1). Isaiah originally spoke to Jerusalem, exiled and desolate. Isaiah 54 follows the suffering Servant of Isaiah 53: the barren one's children come through the Servant's death. Paul says the promise is fulfilled in the gospel: through the promise God is bringing forth many children, including Gentiles.

**4. Cast out the slave woman.** "But what does the Scripture say? 'Cast out the slave woman and her son, for the son of the slave woman shall not inherit with the son of the free woman'" (4:30; quoting Gen 21:10). Sarah's words were endorsed by God in Genesis 21:12. Paul means that the way of justification by law and the way of justification by promise cannot coexist; the Galatians must reject the teaching that would put them back under the law; some read it as a call to expel the agitators. This is no reason to despise Hagar herself, or Arab peoples. In Genesis God cared for Hagar when she was driven out, and she called him "a God of seeing" (Gen 16:13; cf. 21:17); God also promised to make Ishmael a nation (Gen 21:13, 18). Nor is this hostility toward Jews; Paul himself was a Jew, contrasting two ways of relating to God.

> The point: Relating to God by human effort and law produces slaves; relating to him by his promise produces free children. Like Isaac we are children of promise, and our mother is the Jerusalem above, which is free.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "So you are no longer a slave, but a son, and if a son, then an heir through God" (4:7). Is my relationship with God like a child's with a father, or like an employee's with a boss? Do I often feel I must "perform" to keep my position: afraid God will "fire" me when my service goes badly, sure I did well when life goes smoothly, and when it doesn't, wondering what I did wrong and whether I am losing points? Perhaps the "father" I grew up with mostly checked my grades, rarely praised me, and was always about to be disappointed, so even with God all I know how to do is hand in a report card. But the heavenly Father is not my own father writ large; he is the Father who knew me first (4:9) and sent his own Son to find me. Paul writes in Romans, "you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!'" (Rom 8:15). And Jesus told his disciples, "No longer do I call you servants... but I have called you friends" (John 15:15). God sent his Son, "born under the law," to bear the law's demands and curse for me, "so that we might receive adoption as sons" (4:5). My place in the family is not something I earned; Christ bought it for me, and it will not be taken back because of a bad month. Today, can I put down the "performance review" in my heart, and simply call God by the name the Spirit has given me: "Abba! Father!"?
