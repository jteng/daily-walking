## 本章脉络

第五章有三段。**站立得稳**（1-12节）：「基督释放了我们，叫我们得以自由」；若靠受割礼称义，就是「与基督隔绝，从恩典中坠落了」；「在基督耶稣里……惟独使人生发仁爱的信心才有功效」。**自由是为了服事**（13-15节）：「不可将你们的自由当作放纵情欲的机会，总要用爱心互相服事」；「若相咬相吞，只怕要彼此消灭了」。**圣灵与情欲**（16-26节）：「你们当顺着圣灵而行，就不放纵肉体的情欲了」；保罗列出「情欲的事」和「圣灵所结的果子」，又说「凡属基督耶稣的人，是已经把肉体……同钉在十字架上了」。

前四章说明我们怎样得自由；第五章说明自由的人怎样生活。保罗要避免两种错误：一种是回到律法的轭下，另一种是把自由当作放纵的机会。两种的答案都是圣灵。

## 深读 5:1-6 惟独使人生发仁爱的信心

**1. 站立得稳。** 「基督释放了我们，叫我们得以自由。所以要站立得稳，不要再被奴仆的轭挟制。」（5:1）这一节总结了前四章，也开启了后两章。自由是基督为我们成就的，我们的责任是站稳。

**2. 基督就与你们无益了。** 「我－保罗告诉你们，若受割礼，基督就与你们无益了。我再指着凡受割礼的人确实地说，他是欠着行全律法的债。」（5:2-3）保罗自己并不反对犹太人按习俗受割礼（参徒16:3）。他反对的是把割礼当作称义的条件。一旦人想靠一条律法得救，他就必须守全部律法（参3:10）；而他也就不再单单信靠基督了。

**3. 从恩典中坠落。** 「你们这要靠律法称义的，是与基督隔绝，从恩典中坠落了。」（5:4）这句话很严厉。对它的理解，福音派中有不同的看法：
- 改革宗传统认为，保罗是说他们离开了靠恩典称义这条路，转向靠律法称义；真正重生的人，神必保守他到底（参约一2:19），但这样的警告正是神保守他百姓的方法之一。
- 卫斯理宗和其他阿民念派传统认为，这节经文表示一个真信徒可能因离弃福音而失去救恩。

两方都同意，这是一个严肃的警告：任何把律法加在基督之上的人，等于把基督推开了。

**4. 等候所盼望的义。** 「我们靠着圣灵，凭着信心，等候所盼望的义。」（5:5）信徒今天已经被神称为义（2:16），却仍然等候末日神公开宣告的那一天。这盼望不是靠我们的行为，而是「靠着圣灵，凭着信心」。

**5. 使人生发仁爱的信心。** 「原来在基督耶稣里，受割礼不受割礼全无功效，惟独使人生发仁爱的信心才有功效。」（5:6）这句话常被讨论：
- 天主教传统认为，这里说明称义的信心是由爱成形的信心，爱是称义的一部分。
- 宗教改革的传统认为，人称义是单单因着信，但这信心从来不是孤单的，它必然藉着爱活出来。加尔文说：叫人称义的只是信心，但叫人称义的信心不是孤单的。这与雅各书「信心没有行为也是死的」（雅2:26）是一致的。

> 要点：基督已经释放了我们，我们要站立得稳，不再被律法的轭挟制。叫人称义的只有信心，但真信心从来不是孤单的，它必然生发仁爱。

## 深读 5:7-12 十字架讨厌的地方

**1. 你们向来跑得好。** 「你们向来跑得好，有谁拦阻你们，叫你们不顺从真理呢？这样的劝导不是出于那召你们的。」（5:7-8）加拉太人起初跑得很好，后来被人拦阻了。那些人的劝导听起来很属灵，却不是从神来的。

**2. 一点面酵。** 「一点面酵能使全团都发起来。」（5:9）这是当时的俗语（参林前5:6）。一点点错误的教导，如果不处理，会影响整个教会。在福音的核心上，没有「小问题」。

**3. 十字架讨厌的地方。** 「弟兄们，我若仍旧传割礼，为什么还受逼迫呢？若是这样，那十字架讨厌的地方就没有了。」（5:11）「讨厌的地方」原文就是「绊脚石」（参林前1:23）。十字架叫人讨厌，因为它宣告人什么都不能带来，只能白白领受。如果加上割礼，人就有东西可以夸了，十字架也就不再叫人跌倒；但那样的福音也不再能救人。

**4. 恨不得……把自己割绝了。** 「恨不得那搅乱你们的人把自己割绝了。」（5:12）这是全书最尖刻的一句话，带着强烈的讽刺：既然他们这么看重割礼，不如干脆割得更彻底一点。保罗的怒气，是因为他们正在伤害神的群羊、破坏福音。这不是叫我们在争论中可以随意用尖刻的话攻击人；保罗是使徒，是为了保护福音，而且这是对假教师说的，不是对软弱的信徒说的。

> 要点：十字架叫人讨厌，因为它不让人有任何可以自夸的。一旦在福音上加上人的条件，十字架就不再绊倒人，却也不再能救人。

## 深读 5:13-15 用爱心互相服事

**1. 自由不是放纵。** 「弟兄们，你们蒙召是要得自由，只是不可将你们的自由当作放纵情欲的机会，总要用爱心互相服事。」（5:13）「机会」原文是军事用语，指一个据点、一个可以发动攻击的基地。肉体总是在找一个据点。福音的自由不是「想做什么就做什么」，而是从罪和律法的辖制中被释放出来，可以甘心乐意地服事人。「互相服事」原文是彼此作奴仆：我们不再作律法的奴仆，却甘心因爱作彼此的仆人，像主耶稣一样（可10:45）。

**2. 全律法都包在这一句话之内。** 「因为全律法都包在『爱人如己』这一句话之内了。」（5:14；引利19:18）律法的目的是爱。靠圣灵活出爱的人，就成全了律法的要求（参罗13:8-10）。

**3. 相咬相吞。** 「你们要谨慎，若相咬相吞，只怕要彼此消灭了。」（5:15）这是一幅野兽互相撕咬的图画。加拉太教会大概正在为割礼的问题争吵。一个为「真理」争吵到彼此撕咬的教会，最后会把自己毁掉。

> 要点：福音的自由不是放纵，而是甘心因爱彼此服事。若是彼此撕咬，就算赢了争论，也会毁了教会。

## 深读 5:16-26 圣灵所结的果子

**1. 顺着圣灵而行。** 「我说，你们当顺着圣灵而行，就不放纵肉体的情欲了。」（5:16）保罗的答案不是「更努力地守规矩」，而是「顺着圣灵而行」。「行」是一步一步地走：每天依靠圣灵，听从他的带领。

**2. 情欲和圣灵相争。** 「因为情欲和圣灵相争，圣灵和情欲相争，这两个是彼此相敌，使你们不能做所愿意做的。」（5:17）这里的「情欲」原文是「肉体」，不是只指身体，而是指人在罪中、离开神的整个倾向。多数解经家认为，这节描写的是信徒心里真实的争战：圣灵和肉体彼此相敌。有人理解为，肉体拦阻我们做圣灵所愿意的；也有人理解为，圣灵拦阻我们做肉体所想要的。无论哪一种，信徒都不会在今生没有挣扎；挣扎本身，正说明圣灵在我们里面。

**3. 情欲的事。** 「情欲的事都是显而易见的，就如奸淫、污秽、邪荡、拜偶像、邪术、仇恨、争竞、忌恨、恼怒、结党、纷争、异端、嫉妒、醉酒、荒宴等类。」（5:19-21）这份清单有四类：性方面的罪、宗教方面的罪（拜偶像、邪术）、人际关系的罪（从仇恨到嫉妒，共八项，占了一半），以及放纵的罪（醉酒、荒宴）。人际关系的罪最多，很可能正反映了加拉太教会的光景（5:15、26）。

**4. 必不能承受神的国。** 「我从前告诉你们，现在又告诉你们，行这样事的人必不能承受神的国。」（5:21）「行」在原文有习惯性地做、以此为生活方式的意思。这不是说信徒偶然跌倒就失去救恩，而是警告：一个人若一直活在这些事里，毫无悔改，就显明他并不属于神的国。保罗在别处也说：「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今……已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」（林前6:11）

**5. 圣灵所结的果子。** 「圣灵所结的果子，就是仁爱、喜乐、和平、忍耐、恩慈、良善、信实、温柔、节制。这样的事没有律法禁止。」（5:22-23）
- **「果子」：** 原文是单数。这不是九个可以挑选的项目，而是一个完整的品格，就是基督的品格在我们身上长出来。
- **「所结的」：** 果子不是做出来的，而是长出来的。我们的责任是常在主里面（约15:5），圣灵会使果子长出来。
- **「仁爱」排在第一：** 其他的果子，都可以看作爱的不同面貌（参林前13:4-7）。

**6. 已经钉在十字架上了。** 「凡属基督耶稣的人，是已经把肉体连肉体的邪情私欲同钉在十字架上了。」（5:24）这是已经发生的事：在信主、与基督联合的时候，肉体的权势已经被判了死刑（参2:20；罗6:6）。所以我们不是在为一场还没有开始的战争努力，而是在活出基督已经得胜的事实。

**7. 靠圣灵行事。** 「我们若是靠圣灵得生，就当靠圣灵行事。不要贪图虚名，彼此惹气，互相嫉妒。」（5:25-26）「行事」原文是排成一行、跟着步伐前进的意思，像军队跟着领队的步伐。我们既然靠圣灵得了生命，就跟着他的步伐走。

> 要点：胜过肉体的方法，不是更努力地守规矩，而是顺着圣灵而行。圣灵所结的果子是一个完整的品格，就是基督的样式；它是长出来的，不是做出来的。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们要谨慎，若相咬相吞，只怕要彼此消灭了……圣灵所结的果子，就是仁爱、喜乐、和平、忍耐、恩慈、良善、信实、温柔、节制。」（5:15、22-23）我是否也在「相咬相吞」？也许是在教会的微信群里，为了一个神学问题、一次教会的决定、一位讲员的观点，和人争得面红耳赤；也许是在网上转发、评论，用很尖刻的话批评别的信徒，心里还觉得自己在「维护真理」；也许是在家里，和配偶、父母为了信仰的事说出伤人的话。我可能在道理上是对的，可是我身上结出来的，是「仇恨、争竞、忌恨、恼怒、结党、纷争」，还是「温柔」「忍耐」「恩慈」？主耶稣是真理本身，他「被骂不还口；受害不说威吓的话」（彼前2:23）；他来「并不是要受人的服事，乃是要服事人，并且要舍命作多人的赎价」（可10:45）。他为我这个常常咬人的人舍了命。我的骄傲和尖刻，他已经在十字架上担当了；他的灵现在住在我里面，要在我身上结出他自己的温柔。今天，我能不能在下一次按下「发送」以前，先停一停，向神承认我心里的怒气，求圣灵叫我说话像一个被基督服事过的人？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 has three parts. **Stand firm** (vv. 1–12): "For freedom Christ has set us free"; those who would be justified by circumcision "are severed from Christ... fallen away from grace"; "in Christ Jesus... only faith working through love" counts. **Freedom for serving** (vv. 13–15): "do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh, but through love serve one another"; "if you bite and devour one another, watch out that you are not consumed by one another." **Spirit and flesh** (vv. 16–26): "walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh"; Paul lists "the works of the flesh" and "the fruit of the Spirit," and says, "those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh."

The first four chapters show how we are made free; chapter 5 shows how free people live. Paul guards against two errors: going back under the yoke of the law, and using freedom as license. The answer to both is the Spirit.

## Going Deeper 5:1–6 Faith Working Through Love

**1. Stand firm.** "For freedom Christ has set us free; stand firm therefore, and do not submit again to a yoke of slavery" (5:1). This verse sums up the first four chapters and opens the last two. Christ has won our freedom; our part is to stand firm in it.

**2. Christ of no advantage.** "Look: I, Paul, say to you that if you accept circumcision, Christ will be of no advantage to you. I testify again to every man who accepts circumcision that he is obligated to keep the whole law" (5:2–3). Paul himself did not object to Jews being circumcised as a matter of custom (cf. Acts 16:3). What he opposed was making circumcision a condition of justification. Once someone seeks salvation by one law, he must keep the whole law (cf. 3:10), and he is no longer trusting Christ alone.

**3. Fallen away from grace.** "You are severed from Christ, you who would be justified by the law; you have fallen away from grace" (5:4). These are severe words, and evangelicals read them differently:
- The Reformed tradition holds that Paul means they have left the way of "justification by grace" for "justification by law"; God will keep those truly born again to the end (cf. 1 John 2:19), and warnings like this are one of the means by which he keeps them.
- The Wesleyan and other Arminian traditions hold that this verse shows a true believer can lose salvation by abandoning the gospel.

Both agree it is a solemn warning: anyone who adds the law to Christ is pushing Christ away.

**4. Waiting for the hope of righteousness.** "For through the Spirit, by faith, we ourselves eagerly wait for the hope of righteousness" (5:5). Believers are already justified by God today (2:16), yet they still await the day when God declares it publicly at the end. This hope rests not on our works but "through the Spirit, by faith."

**5. Faith working through love.** "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything, but only faith working through love" (5:6). This verse is much discussed:
- Catholic tradition takes it to show that justifying faith is faith "formed by love," love being part of justification.
- The Reformation tradition holds that we are justified by faith alone, but that this faith is never alone; it always works itself out in love. Calvin said that it is faith alone that justifies, but the faith that justifies is not alone. This agrees with James: "faith apart from works is dead" (Jas 2:26).

> The point: Christ has set us free; we must stand firm and not be yoked again to the law. Faith alone justifies, but true faith is never alone; it always works through love.

## Going Deeper 5:7–12 The Offense of the Cross

**1. You were running well.** "You were running well. Who hindered you from obeying the truth? This persuasion is not from him who calls you" (5:7–8). The Galatians started well, then were cut in on. The agitators' persuasion sounded spiritual, but it was not from God.

**2. A little leaven.** "A little leaven leavens the whole lump" (5:9). This was a proverb (cf. 1 Cor 5:6). A little false teaching, left alone, affects the whole church. At the heart of the gospel there are no "small issues."

**3. The offense of the cross.** "But if I, brothers, still preach circumcision, why am I still being persecuted? In that case the offense of the cross has been removed" (5:11). "Offense" is literally "stumbling block" (cf. 1 Cor 1:23). The cross offends because it declares that we can bring nothing and can only receive freely. Add circumcision, and people have something to boast of, and the cross stops making them stumble; but such a gospel no longer saves.

**4. Emasculate themselves.** "I wish those who unsettle you would emasculate themselves!" (5:12). This is the sharpest sentence in the letter, biting irony: since they think so highly of cutting, let them go all the way. Paul's anger is because they are harming God's flock and destroying the gospel. This is no license for us to use cutting words freely in arguments; Paul is an apostle protecting the gospel, and he says this of false teachers, not of weak believers.

> The point: The cross offends because it leaves us nothing to boast of. Once human conditions are added to the gospel, the cross no longer makes people stumble, but neither can it save.

## Going Deeper 5:13–15 Through Love Serve One Another

**1. Freedom is not license.** "For you were called to freedom, brothers. Only do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh, but through love serve one another" (5:13). "Opportunity" is a military word for a base of operations, a foothold from which to attack. The flesh is always looking for a foothold. Gospel freedom is not "doing whatever I like"; it is being released from the grip of sin and law so that we can gladly serve others. "Serve one another" is literally "be slaves to one another": no longer slaves of the law, we freely become one another's servants in love, as Jesus did (Mark 10:45).

**2. The whole law in one word.** "For the whole law is fulfilled in one word: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself'" (5:14; quoting Lev 19:18). The law's aim is love. Those who love by the Spirit fulfill what the law requires (cf. Rom 13:8–10).

**3. Biting and devouring.** "But if you bite and devour one another, watch out that you are not consumed by one another" (5:15). This is a picture of wild animals tearing each other apart. The Galatian church was probably quarreling over circumcision. A church that fights over "truth" until its members tear one another apart will in the end destroy itself.

> The point: Gospel freedom is not license but the freedom to serve one another gladly in love. If we bite and devour one another, we may win the argument and destroy the church.

## Going Deeper 5:16–26 The Fruit of the Spirit

**1. Walk by the Spirit.** "But I say, walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh" (5:16). Paul's answer is not "try harder to keep the rules" but "walk by the Spirit." "Walk" means step by step: relying on the Spirit each day and following his lead.

**2. Flesh against Spirit.** "For the desires of the flesh are against the Spirit, and the desires of the Spirit are against the flesh, for these are opposed to each other, to keep you from doing the things you want to do" (5:17). "Flesh" here does not mean just the body, but the whole bent of human nature in sin, turned away from God. Most interpreters take this verse to describe a real conflict in the believer: Spirit and flesh opposed. Some read it as the flesh keeping us from doing what the Spirit desires; others as the Spirit keeping us from doing what the flesh wants. Either way, believers will not be without struggle in this life, and the struggle itself shows that the Spirit is in us.

**3. The works of the flesh.** "Now the works of the flesh are evident: sexual immorality, impurity, sensuality, idolatry, sorcery, enmity, strife, jealousy, fits of anger, rivalries, dissensions, divisions, envy, drunkenness, orgies, and things like these" (5:19–21). The list has four groups: sexual sins, religious sins (idolatry, sorcery), relational sins (eight of them, from enmity to envy, half the list) and sins of excess (drunkenness, orgies). That relational sins are most numerous probably reflects the state of the Galatian churches (5:15, 26).

**4. Will not inherit the kingdom.** "I warn you, as I warned you before, that those who do such things will not inherit the kingdom of God" (5:21). "Do" carries the sense of practicing habitually, as a way of life. This does not mean a believer who stumbles loses salvation; it warns that someone who goes on living in these things without repentance shows he does not belong to God's kingdom. Paul says elsewhere, "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified" (1 Cor 6:11).

**5. The fruit of the Spirit.** "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such things there is no law" (5:22–23).
- **"Fruit":** singular. Not nine items to pick from, but one whole character: the character of Christ growing in us.
- **"Of the Spirit":** fruit is not manufactured but grown. Our part is to abide in the Lord (John 15:5); the Spirit makes the fruit grow.
- **"Love" comes first:** the others can be seen as different faces of love (cf. 1 Cor 13:4–7).

**6. Already crucified.** "And those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires" (5:24). This has already happened: when we came to faith and were united to Christ, the flesh's dominion was sentenced to death (cf. 2:20; Rom 6:6). So we are not struggling toward a battle not yet begun; we are living out a victory Christ has already won.

**7. Keep in step with the Spirit.** "If we live by the Spirit, let us also walk by the Spirit. Let us not become conceited, provoking one another, envying one another" (5:25–26). "Walk" here is a different word, meaning to keep in line, to march in step, like soldiers following the leader's pace. Since we have life by the Spirit, we keep in step with him.

> The point: The way to overcome the flesh is not to try harder at the rules but to walk by the Spirit. The fruit of the Spirit is one whole character, the likeness of Christ; it is grown, not manufactured.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But if you bite and devour one another, watch out that you are not consumed by one another... But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control" (5:15, 22–23). Am I "biting and devouring" too? Perhaps in the church group chat, red-faced over a theological question, a church decision or a speaker's views; perhaps online, sharing and commenting, criticizing other believers in cutting words while feeling I am "defending the truth"; perhaps at home, saying wounding things to my spouse or parents over matters of faith. I may be right in doctrine, but what is growing in me: "enmity, strife, jealousy, fits of anger, rivalries, dissensions," or "gentleness, patience, kindness"? The Lord Jesus is the truth itself, and "when he was reviled, he did not revile in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten" (1 Pet 2:23); he "came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45). He gave his life for me, who so often bite. My pride and sharpness he has already borne on the cross; his Spirit now lives in me to grow his own gentleness in me. Today, before I next press "send," can I stop, confess the anger in my heart to God, and ask the Spirit to make me speak like someone Christ has served?
