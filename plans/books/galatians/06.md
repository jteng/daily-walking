## 本章脉络

第六章有三段。**彼此担当**（1-5节）：若有人被过犯所胜，「属灵的人就当用温柔的心把他挽回过来」；「你们各人的重担要互相担当，如此，就完全了基督的律法」。**撒种与收割**（6-10节）：「人种的是什么，收的也是什么」；「我们行善，不可丧志；若不灰心，到了时候就要收成」。**保罗亲笔的结语**（11-18节）：那些人勉强人受割礼，是「怕自己为基督的十字架受逼迫」；保罗却说：「我断不以别的夸口，只夸我们主耶稣基督的十字架」；「要紧的就是作新造的人」。

第五章讲到「顺着圣灵而行」；第六章把它落实在教会生活中：怎样对待跌倒的人，怎样彼此担当，怎样支持教导的人，怎样不灰心地行善。最后，保罗亲手写下全书的总结：十字架和新造。

## 深读 6:1-5 彼此担当重担

**1. 用温柔的心挽回。** 「弟兄们，若有人偶然被过犯所胜，你们属灵的人就当用温柔的心把他挽回过来；又当自己小心，恐怕也被引诱。」（6:1）
- **「被过犯所胜」：** 原文有被抓住、被绊住的意思，像一个人突然被罪绊倒。
- **「属灵的人」：** 不是一群自以为高人一等的人，而是顺着圣灵而行、结出圣灵果子的人（5:22-25）。
- **「挽回」：** 原文是修补的意思，补网、接骨用的也是这个词（参太4:21）。
- **「温柔」：** 正是圣灵所结的果子之一（5:23）。
- **「自己小心」：** 挽回别人的人，要记得自己也可能跌倒。

这节经文讲的是在弟兄姊妹之间的挽回。如果所说的过犯是家暴、虐待、性侵或其他罪行，用温柔的心挽回，并不等于私下了结。受害人需要保护；报警、交由司法处理是对的（罗13:4）。挽回也不等于让犯罪的人恢复职分，或再接触受害人和儿童。长期、有计划地伤害人，不是偶然被绊倒；这里所说的温柔，也不是要受害人去温柔地对待伤害自己的人，更不是叫受害人不要声张。受害人不必留在危险中，也不必勉强与仍会伤害自己的人和好；教会要担当的，首先是受害人的重担（6:2），也不可因为「家丑不可外扬」而隐瞒虐待。

**2. 互相担当。** 「你们各人的重担要互相担当，如此，就完全了基督的律法。」（6:2）那些搅扰的人要加拉太人背上律法的重担；保罗说，基督的律法是彼此担当重担。「基督的律法」很可能是指基督所活出、所教导的爱的律法（参5:14；约13:34）。基督自己就是那位担当我们重担的（参赛53:4）。

**3. 各人担当自己的担子。** 「各人应当察验自己的行为；这样，他所夸的就专在自己，不在别人了，因为各人必担当自己的担子。」（6:4-5）这和6:2矛盾吗？不矛盾。两个词的意思有重叠（参太23:4），但许多解经家指出，6:2讲的是压得人喘不过气的重负，6:5讲的是各人当负的责任；6:5用将来式「必担当」，很可能指末日各人在神面前交账。我们要帮助别人背重担，但每个人也要在神面前为自己交账，不可拿别人来比较（参林后10:12）。

> 要点：挽回跌倒的人，要用圣灵所赐的温柔，并且记得自己也可能跌倒。彼此担当重担，就成全了基督爱的律法；同时，每个人也要在神面前为自己负责。

## 深读 6:6-10 不可丧志

**1. 供给施教的人。** 「在道理上受教的，当把一切需用的供给施教的人。」（6:6）教导神话语的人，应当得到教会的供应（参林前9:14）。「一切需用的」原文是一切好处，所以也可能包括彼此分享属灵和物质上的好处。

**2. 神是轻慢不得的。** 「不要自欺，神是轻慢不得的。人种的是什么，收的也是什么。顺着情欲撒种的，必从情欲收败坏；顺着圣灵撒种的，必从圣灵收永生。」（6:7-8）保罗不是说我们靠行善赚得永生；永生是神白白的恩赐（参2:16；罗6:23）。他是说：一个人的生活方向，显明他将来会有哪一种收成。一个人若天天在肉体上撒种，却以为可以收到圣灵的果子，就是自欺。

**3. 行善不可丧志。** 「我们行善，不可丧志；若不灰心，到了时候就要收成。」（6:9）行善常常是一件很累的事：照顾生病的家人、长期的服事、在不诚实的环境中坚持诚实。保罗承认人会疲倦，所以才说「不可丧志」。「到了时候」是神所定的时候，不一定是我们期望的时候。

**4. 向众人行善。** 「所以，有了机会就当向众人行善，向信徒一家的人更当这样。」（6:10）信徒的爱不只限于教会里面，也要临到所有人；但教会是一家人，彼此照顾更是应当的。

> 要点：神是轻慢不得的，人种的是什么，收的也是什么；但永生是神白白的恩赐，不是我们种出来的工价。行善会叫人疲倦，我们却可以不丧志，因为收成在神手中，到了他所定的时候，必有收成。

## 深读 6:11-18 只夸基督的十字架

**1. 亲手写的大字。** 「请看我亲手写给你们的字是何等的大呢！」（6:11）保罗通常请人代笔（参罗16:22），最后亲手写几句话作为签名（林前16:21；帖后3:17）。这里他用大字，可能是为了强调，也可能和他的视力有关（参4:15），学者看法不一。

**2. 希图外貌体面的人。** 「凡希图外貌体面的人都勉强你们受割礼，无非是怕自己为基督的十字架受逼迫。」（6:12）保罗揭开那些人的真正动机：他们要在犹太社群面前有面子，可以夸口自己叫多少外邦人受了割礼，也免得自己因传十字架而受逼迫（参5:11）。他们「愿意你们受割礼，不过要藉着你们的肉体夸口」（6:13）。

**3. 只夸十字架。** 「但我断不以别的夸口，只夸我们主耶稣基督的十字架；因这十字架，就我而论，世界已经钉在十字架上；就世界而论，我已经钉在十字架上。」（6:14）在罗马世界，钉十字架是最羞耻的刑罚，没有人会拿它来夸口。保罗却说，那是他唯一可夸的。因着十字架，世界对他已经失去了吸引力和统治力，他对世界也已经「死了」；世人看重的面子、成就和认可，不再能决定他是谁。

**4. 新造的人。** 「受割礼不受割礼都无关紧要，要紧的就是作新造的人。」（6:15）这呼应5:6，也呼应林后5:17。神在基督里开始了一个新的创造，在这新造里，受不受割礼都不再决定人的地位。

**5. 神的以色列民。** 「凡照此理而行的，愿平安、怜悯加给他们，和神的以色列民。」（6:16）「神的以色列民」指谁，解经家有不同的看法；关键在于「和」这个词：它是指同一群人（也就是神的以色列民），还是在前面那群人以外另加一群人？
- 许多解经家（如加尔文和许多改革宗学者）认为，这是指包括犹太人和外邦人在内、所有在基督里的神的百姓（参3:29；罗2:28-29；腓3:3）；
- 另一些解经家（如刘易斯·约翰逊，以及许多时代论者）认为，这是指信主的犹太人，或者指神对以色列民族仍有的怜悯（参罗11:26）。

**6. 耶稣的印记。** 「从今以后，人都不要搅扰我，因为我身上带着耶稣的印记。」（6:17）「印记」原文是当时烙在奴仆或牲口身上、表明主人的记号。那些人以割礼为记号；保罗身上的记号，却是他为耶稣受鞭打、被石头打留下的伤痕（参林后11:24-25；在加拉太南部的路司得被石头打，徒14:19）。这些伤痕证明他属于谁。

**7. 愿恩常在你们心里。** 「弟兄们，愿我主耶稣基督的恩常在你们心里。阿们！」（6:18）这封信没有开头的感恩，也没有结尾的问安，却以「恩」和「弟兄们」结束。保罗说了那么多严厉的话，最后的一句仍然是恩典。

> 要点：保罗唯一可夸的，是世人看为最羞耻的十字架。因着十字架，世界不再能决定他是谁；要紧的不是外面的记号，而是在基督里作新造的人。全书以恩典开始，也以恩典结束。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我们行善，不可丧志；若不灰心，到了时候就要收成。」（6:9）我是否正在一件长期的「行善」里感到疲倦？也许是照顾一位生病或年老的家人，日复一日，没有人看见；也许是一份做了很多年的服事，越来越像例行公事；也许是在一个人人都走捷径的工作环境里，坚持诚实，却总是吃亏；也许是为一位离开主的孩子祷告了很多年。我可能已经开始灰心，甚至在心里想：做这些到底有什么用？主耶稣知道这种疲倦。他「忍受罪人这样顶撞」，一直走到十字架（来12:3）；在那里，他为我这个常常灰心、常常想放弃的人舍了自己。十字架在人看来，是最失败、最没有果效的一件事；可是到了神所定的时候，那里长出了全世界的收成。所以保罗只夸这十字架（6:14）。我的行善不是为了赚得神的爱，因为在十字架上，他已经把最好的给了我。收成在神所定的「时候」，不在我的时间表上；他也说：「凡劳苦担重担的人可以到我这里来，我就使你们得安息。」（太11:28）歇一歇也包括让弟兄姊妹来帮我担一点（6:2），这不是软弱，而是基督的身体本来的样子。如果我已经放下了，他也没有放下我。今天，就先把这份疲倦带到他面前，在他那里歇一歇，再把那件善事交托给他：主啊，收成在你手中，我只求今天不灰心。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 has three parts. **Bearing one another** (vv. 1–5): if anyone is caught in a transgression, "you who are spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness"; "Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ." **Sowing and reaping** (vv. 6–10): "whatever one sows, that will he also reap"; "let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up." **Paul's own closing words** (vv. 11–18): those who force circumcision do so "in order that they may not be persecuted for the cross of Christ"; but Paul says, "far be it from me to boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ"; what counts is "a new creation."

Chapter 5 spoke of walking by the Spirit; chapter 6 works it out in church life: how to treat those who fall, how to bear one another's burdens, how to support those who teach, how to keep doing good without giving up. Finally Paul writes the summary of the whole letter in his own hand: the cross and the new creation.

## Going Deeper 6:1–5 Bearing One Another's Burdens

**1. Restoring with gentleness.** "Brothers, if anyone is caught in any transgression, you who are spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness. Keep watch on yourself, lest you too be tempted" (6:1).
- **"Caught":** the word suggests being overtaken or tripped up, as someone suddenly stumbling into sin.
- **"You who are spiritual":** not a superior class, but those who walk by the Spirit and bear the Spirit's fruit (5:22–25).
- **"Restore":** the word means to mend, the same word used of mending nets or setting bones (cf. Matt 4:21).
- **"Gentleness":** one of the fruits of the Spirit (5:23).
- **"Keep watch on yourself":** those who restore others must remember they too can fall.

This verse is about restoration among brothers and sisters. Where the "transgression" is domestic violence, abuse, sexual assault or another crime, restoring gently does not mean settling it privately: victims need protection, reporting to the police and the courts is right (Rom 13:4), and restoration does not mean returning the person to office or giving him access again to victims or children. A pattern of harm is not being suddenly "caught" in a sin, and the gentleness of this verse is never something a victim owes the one who hurt them, still less a reason to keep quiet. Victims need not remain in danger or be reconciled to someone unsafe; the burden the church must carry first is the victim's (6:2), and the church must never hide abuse to save face.

**2. Bearing one another's burdens.** "Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ" (6:2). The agitators wanted to load the Galatians with the burden of the law; Paul says the law of Christ is bearing one another's burdens. "The law of Christ" probably means the law of love that Christ lived and taught (cf. 5:14; John 13:34). Christ himself is the one who bears our burdens (cf. Isa 53:4).

**3. Each will bear his own load.** "But let each one test his own work, and then his reason to boast will be in himself alone and not in his neighbor. For each will have to bear his own load" (6:4–5). Does this contradict 6:2? No. The two words overlap (cf. Matt 23:4), but many note that 6:2 speaks of crushing weights and 6:5 of each person's own responsibility; the future tense "will bear" in 6:5 probably points to each giving account to God at the last day. We help others carry their crushing weights, and each of us also gives account to God for himself, without comparing ourselves with others (cf. 2 Cor 10:12).

> The point: Restoring those who fall calls for the gentleness the Spirit gives, and the humility to remember we too may fall. Bearing one another's burdens fulfills Christ's law of love; and each of us is still responsible to God for himself.

## Going Deeper 6:6–10 Do Not Grow Weary

**1. Sharing with teachers.** "One who is taught the word must share all good things with the one who teaches" (6:6). Those who teach God's word should be supported by the church (cf. 1 Cor 9:14). "All good things" may also include mutual sharing of spiritual and material good.

**2. God is not mocked.** "Do not be deceived: God is not mocked, for whatever one sows, that will he also reap. For the one who sows to his own flesh will from the flesh reap corruption, but the one who sows to the Spirit will from the Spirit reap eternal life" (6:7–8). This does not mean we earn eternal life by doing good; eternal life is God's free gift (cf. 2:16; Rom 6:23). It means the direction of a person's life shows which harvest he belongs to. Whoever sows to the flesh every day yet expects to reap the Spirit's fruit deceives himself.

**3. Not growing weary in doing good.** "And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up" (6:9). Doing good is often exhausting: caring for a sick family member, years of ministry, staying honest in a dishonest workplace. Paul knows people grow weary; that is why he says, "do not grow weary." "In due season" is God's appointed time, not necessarily ours.

**4. Doing good to everyone.** "So then, as we have opportunity, let us do good to everyone, and especially to those who are of the household of faith" (6:10). Believers' love is not only for those inside the church but for all; yet the church is a family, and caring for one another there is all the more fitting.

> The point: God is not mocked; whatever one sows, that he will reap. Yet eternal life is God's free gift, not wages we grow for ourselves. Doing good can wear us out, but we need not give up, because the harvest is in God's hands and will come in his appointed time.

## Going Deeper 6:11–18 Boasting Only in the Cross

**1. Large letters in his own hand.** "See with what large letters I am writing to you with my own hand" (6:11). Paul usually dictated to a scribe (cf. Rom 16:22) and then added a few lines in his own hand as a signature (1 Cor 16:21; 2 Thess 3:17). The large letters may be for emphasis, or may relate to his eyesight (cf. 4:15); scholars differ.

**2. A good showing in the flesh.** "It is those who want to make a good showing in the flesh who would force you to be circumcised, and only in order that they may not be persecuted for the cross of Christ" (6:12). Paul exposes their real motive: they wanted to look good before the Jewish community, to boast of how many Gentiles they had circumcised, and to avoid persecution for preaching the cross (cf. 5:11). They "desire to have you circumcised that they may boast in your flesh" (6:13).

**3. Boasting only in the cross.** "But far be it from me to boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world" (6:14). In the Roman world the cross was the most shameful of things; no one would boast of it. Paul says it is the only thing he will boast of. Through the cross the world has lost its attraction and its rule over him, and he has "died" to the world; the world's face, achievements and approval no longer define him.

**4. A new creation.** "For neither circumcision counts for anything, nor uncircumcision, but a new creation" (6:15). This echoes 5:6 and 2 Corinthians 5:17. In Christ God has begun a new creation, in which neither circumcision nor uncircumcision determines anyone's standing.

**5. The Israel of God.** "And as for all who walk by this rule, peace and mercy be upon them, and upon the Israel of God" (6:16). Interpreters differ on who "the Israel of God" are. The key is the little word "and" (*kai*): it either explains ("that is, the Israel of God") or adds a further group.
- many (such as Calvin and many Reformed scholars) take it as all God's people in Christ, Jews and Gentiles together (cf. 3:29; Rom 2:28–29; Phil 3:3);
- others (e.g., S. Lewis Johnson Jr., including many dispensationalists and some other scholars) take it as Jewish believers, or as God's continuing mercy toward the people of Israel (cf. Rom 11:26).

**6. The marks of Jesus.** "From now on let no one cause me trouble, for I bear on my body the marks of Jesus" (6:17). "Marks" (*stigmata*) is the word for brands burned on slaves or livestock to show the owner. The agitators prized circumcision as a mark; Paul's marks were the scars of the beatings and stoning he suffered for Jesus (cf. 2 Cor 11:24–25; stoned at Lystra in southern Galatia, Acts 14:19). These scars showed to whom he belonged.

**7. Grace be with your spirit.** "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, brothers. Amen" (6:18). This letter has no opening thanksgiving and no closing greetings, yet it ends with "grace" and "brothers." After so many hard words, Paul's last word is still grace.

> The point: The only thing Paul would boast of was the cross the world counted most shameful. Through the cross the world no longer defines him; what matters is not outward marks but being a new creation in Christ. The letter begins with grace and ends with grace.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And let us not grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap, if we do not give up" (6:9). Am I worn out by some long work of "doing good"? Perhaps caring for a sick or aging family member, day after day, unseen; perhaps a ministry I have carried for years that now feels like routine; perhaps staying honest in a workplace where everyone cuts corners, and always losing out; perhaps praying for years for a child who has left the Lord. I may already be losing heart, even thinking: what is the point of all this? The Lord Jesus knows this weariness. He "endured from sinners such hostility against himself," all the way to the cross (Heb 12:3), and there he gave himself for me, who so often lose heart and want to give up. To every human eye the cross was the most fruitless, failed thing ever done; yet in God's due season it became the harvest of the world. That is why Paul will boast only in this cross (6:14). My doing good is not to earn God's love, for at the cross he has already given me his best. The harvest comes "in due season," God's time, not my schedule; and he also says, "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matt 11:28). Resting may also mean letting brothers and sisters carry part of the load (6:2); that is not weakness but how Christ's body is meant to work. And if I have already given up, he has not given up on me. Today, I bring this weariness to him first, rest a while with him, and then entrust that good work to him: Lord, the harvest is in your hands; I ask only not to give up today.
