## 本章脉络

第一章分成两段。**序言**（1-4节）：神从前藉着众先知说话，如今藉着他儿子说话；这位儿子承受万有，创造诸世界，是神荣耀的光辉、神本体的真像，用他权能的命令托住万有；他洗净了人的罪，就坐在高天至大者的右边，远超过天使。**儿子比天使更尊贵**（5-14节）：作者一连引了七处旧约经文，把儿子和天使对比：神称他为儿子，叫天使都拜他；称他为神，说他的宝座永永远远；天地都要像衣服渐渐旧了，他却永不改变；神请他坐在自己的右边，天使却都是服役的灵，奉差遣为承受救恩的人效力。

这一章几乎全是讲基督是谁，直到第二章才有第一句劝勉。作者要疲倦的读者先停下来，好好看一看这位儿子；他们要怎样生活，以后再讲。

## 深读 1:1-2上 神藉着他儿子说话

**1. 「神既在古时……晓谕列祖」。** 「神既在古时藉着众先知多次多方地晓谕列祖。」（1:1）
- **神是说话的神：** 全书的第一个主语就是「神」。人能认识神，不是因为人找到了神，而是因为神先开口说话。
- **「多次多方」：** 神从前说话，是一段一段的，用不同的方法：藉着异梦、异象、律法、诗歌、先知的预言，也藉着历史中的作为。这些话都是真实的，却还不是全部。
- **作者尊重旧约：** 他没有说旧约的话错了，反而在这卷书里不断引用旧约，并且说那是「圣灵」的话（3:7；10:15）。

**2. 「就在这末世藉着他儿子晓谕我们」。** 「就在这末世藉着他儿子晓谕我们。」（1:2上）
- **「这末世」：** 在旧约里，「末后的日子」是神要成就他一切应许的时候。作者说，这个时候已经来到了，因为儿子已经来了。
- **「藉着他儿子」：** 原文没有「他」字，直译是：藉着一位儿子。许多解经家认为，这个说法强调的是说话者是什么样的身份：从前神藉着仆人说话，如今藉着一位儿子说话。先知传的是神的话，儿子自己就是神的话（约1:1、14）。
- **最后的话：** 儿子不只是众先知中最大的一位，而是神最终、最完全的启示。「从来没有人看见神，只有在父怀里的独生子将他表明出来。」（约1:18）所以，人若想认识神，不必再到别处去找新的启示；神要说的，都已经在他儿子身上说了。

> 要点：神没有沉默。他从前藉着先知一段一段地说话，如今藉着他儿子把话说完全了。要认识神，就要听他的儿子。

## 深读 1:2下-4 这位儿子是谁

**1. 七个描写。** 作者用一句很长的话（原文1-4节是一个句子），讲出儿子是谁、做了什么：
- **「立他为承受万有的」：** 万有最终都要归给他。
- **「藉着他创造诸世界」：** 他在万有之先，万物是藉着他造的（约1:3；西1:16）。
- **「神荣耀所发的光辉」：** 好像太阳和它的光，光从太阳发出，却和太阳一同存在。许多人认为，这个词也可以译作「反照」；无论哪一种译法，意思都是：人在儿子身上看见神的荣耀。
- **「神本体的真像」：** 「真像」这个词，原指印章盖下的印纹，和印章一模一样。神是什么样的，儿子就是什么样的。耶稣说：「人看见了我，就是看见了父。」（约14:9）
- **「常用他权能的命令托住万有」：** 他不只在起初创造了世界，如今还在托住世界，带领它走向神所定的结局。
- **「他洗净了人的罪」：** 在这一串描写中间，作者讲到十字架。创造万有的那一位，亲自来洗净我们的罪。这是全书的中心，第9-10章会详细讲。
- **「就坐在高天至大者的右边」：** 旧约的祭司献祭时总是「站着」，因为工作永远做不完（10:11）；基督「坐下了」，因为洗罪的工作已经完成了（10:12）。

**2. 儿子的神性。** 这几节把创造、托住万有这些只有神才能做的事归给儿子，又说他是「神本体的真像」。四世纪的教会为基督的神性争辩时，亚他那修常常引用这节经文，反驳阿里乌说儿子是被造的。儿子和父不是同一位，却同有神的本体：他是「神荣耀所发的光辉」，不是另一个次等的神。

**3. 「远超过天使」。** 「他所承受的名，既比天使的名更尊贵，就远超过天使。」（1:4）
- **「所承受的名」：** 接下来的经文显示，这个名就是「儿子」（5节）。儿子本来就是儿子（2节）；但他完成了洗罪的工作、升到父的右边以后，就以人子和弥赛亚的身份承受了这个名所当得的尊荣（参腓2:9-11；罗1:4）。
- **为什么讲天使？** 犹太人相信，律法是藉着天使传下来的（2:2；徒7:53；加3:19）。作者要说明：儿子比传旧约的天使更大，所以儿子所带来的救恩也更大（2:2-3）。也有学者认为，读者中有人敬拜天使，或者把基督看作天使中的一位（参西2:18）。无论是哪一种情况，作者的意思都很清楚：基督不是天使，而是天使所敬拜的主。

> 要点：这位儿子是创造者，是神荣耀的光辉，是托住万有的主；他亲自洗净我们的罪，然后坐下。他的工作已经完成了。

## 深读 1:5-9 你是我的儿子

**1. 「你是我的儿子，我今日生你」。** 「所有的天使，神从来对哪一个说：你是我的儿子，我今日生你？又指着哪一个说：我要作他的父，他要作我的子？」（1:5）
- **两处经文：** 第一处引自诗篇2:7，第二处引自撒母耳记下7:14，都是神对大卫的后裔、受膏的君王所说的话，最终在基督身上应验。
- **「今日」是哪一天？** 历代有不同的理解。许多解经家（如加尔文）认为，「今日」是指基督复活升天、被立为王的日子；保罗在使徒行传13:33就把诗篇2:7和耶稣复活连在一起。这不是说耶稣从那天才成为神的儿子（本章2节说，神早已藉着儿子创造诸世界），而是说他以弥赛亚君王的身份被公开立为王。也有一些教父（如奥古斯丁）把「今日」理解为永恒的今日，指父永远生子。两种看法都同意：儿子不是被造的，而是永远的儿子。

**2. 「神的使者都要拜他」。** 「再者，神使长子到世上来的时候，就说：神的使者都要拜他。」（1:6）
- **「长子」：** 这个词在这里不是说他是第一个被造的，而是讲地位和产业。神论到大卫的后裔说：「我也要立他为长子，为世上最高的君王。」（诗89:27）
- **「到世上来的时候」：** 解经家有三种看法：一是指道成肉身，天使在伯利恒的夜里赞美神；二是指基督升天，进入「将来的世界」（2:5），天使都拜他；三是指基督再来。原文「再者」的位置也可以读成：当他再使长子到世上来；所以有人认为是指再来。无论是哪一个时候，天使都要敬拜他，可见他不是天使中的一位。
- **引文的出处：** 这句话很可能引自七十士译本的申命记32:43；和合本所根据的马所拉希伯来文本没有这一句，但死海古卷中的一份希伯来文抄本有相近的句子；诗篇97:7也有相近的话：「万神哪，你们都当拜他。」

**3. 「神啊，你的宝座是永永远远的」。** 「论到使者，又说：神以风为使者，以火焰为仆役；论到子却说：神啊，你的宝座是永永远远的；你的国权是正直的。你喜爱公义，恨恶罪恶；所以神，就是你的神，用喜乐油膏你，胜过膏你的同伴。」（1:7-9）
- **天使像风和火：** 天使大有能力，却是被差遣的「仆役」；许多人认为，这也是说他们像风和火焰一样会变化。
- **儿子的宝座是永远的：** 诗篇45篇原是一首君王婚礼的诗，作者却看见这首诗最终指向基督。
- **「神啊」：** 多数解经家认为，这里的「神」是称呼语，儿子被称为「神」（也有人译作「神是你的宝座」，但第10节同样直接称呼儿子「主啊」，又有第8节「论到子却说」的对比，所以大多数人不取这个译法）。新约其他地方也这样称呼基督（约1:1；20:28；罗9:5；多2:13）。
- **「神，就是你的神，用喜乐油膏你」：** 同一处经文既称儿子为「神」，又说「神，就是你的神」膏他。这显明父和子同是神，却是两个不同的位格。儿子以人的身份，在顺服中被父所膏。教会所承认的三位一体之中父与子位格的分别，在这里已经可以看见。

> 要点：天使是被差遣的仆人，儿子却是天使所敬拜的主。父称他为儿子，又称他为神；他的宝座永远长存。

## 深读 1:10-14 你却要长存

**1. 「天地都要灭没，你却要长存」。** 「又说：主啊，你起初立了地的根基；天也是你手所造的。天地都要灭没，你却要长存。天地都要像衣服渐渐旧了；你要将天地卷起来，像一件外衣，天地就都改变了。惟有你永不改变；你的年数没有穷尽。」（1:10-12）
- **原是对耶和华说的话：** 这段引自诗篇102:25-27，在诗篇中是一个受苦的人向耶和华祷告。作者却把它用在儿子身上。旧约讲耶和华的话，新约用来讲基督，可见作者把基督和耶和华放在同一个地位上。
- **像衣服一样：** 天地看起来是最稳固、最长久的，在儿子面前却像一件会穿旧的衣服。他要把它「卷起来」，天地就都改变了（参12:26-27；彼后3:13）。
- **「惟有你永不改变」：** 这句话在全书的结尾又回响了一次：「耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的。」（13:8）
- **给读者的安慰：** 这些信徒曾经失去家业（10:34），也许正看着自己熟悉的一切在改变。作者告诉他们：一切被造的都会旧、会变，惟有儿子永不改变（参12:27-28）。

**2. 「你坐在我的右边」。** 「所有的天使，神从来对哪一个说：你坐在我的右边，等我使你仇敌作你的脚凳？」（1:13）
- **诗篇110:1：** 这是新约引用最多的一处旧约经文。耶稣自己也引用过（太22:44），用来说明弥赛亚不只是大卫的子孙，也是大卫的主。
- **「等」：** 基督已经坐在宝座上，但他的仇敌还没有全部降服。我们活在「已经」和「还没有」之间（参2:8）。作者在第10章又讲到这一点：基督「就在神的右边坐下了。从此，等候他仇敌成了他的脚凳」（10:12-13）。

**3. 「服役的灵」。** 「天使岂不都是服役的灵、奉差遣为那将要承受救恩的人效力吗？」（1:14）
- **天使服事信徒：** 这些大有能力的灵，是神差来「为那将要承受救恩的人效力」的。以利沙的仆人看见「满山有火车火马」围绕他们（王下6:17），信徒常常看不见，却并不孤单。
- **不要敬拜天使：** 天使是仆人，不是我们敬拜的对象。约翰要拜天使时，天使说：「千万不可！……你要敬拜神。」（启22:8-9）
- **「将要承受」：** 儿子承受万有（2节），信靠他的人也要「承受救恩」。这救恩现在已经开始，却还要在将来完全显明。

> 要点：天地会旧，世上的一切都会改变，惟有儿子永不改变。他已经坐在宝座上，连天使都奉差遣来服事那些属于他的人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「天地都要灭没，你却要长存。天地都要像衣服渐渐旧了……惟有你永不改变；你的年数没有穷尽。」（1:11-12）我曾经以为有些东西是不会变的。我长大的那条老街，后来拆迁了，回去时连路都认不出来；我服事多年的教会，后来分裂了，那个礼堂也卖掉了；我在一家公司做了二十年，以为可以做到退休，公司却倒闭了。这些事发生的时候，我好像失去了一部分的自己。希伯来书的读者也是这样，他们的「家业被人抢去」（10:34），熟悉的生活一样一样地被拿走。作者没有对他们说：「不要难过，再建一个就是了。」他把他们的眼睛转向儿子：天地都会像衣服一样旧了，「惟有你永不改变」。这位永不改变的儿子，不是远远地看着一切会旧的人。他「常用他权能的命令托住万有」（1:3），也托住我这个会旧、会软弱的人。他亲自取了会疲倦、会死的身体，为我「洗净了人的罪」，然后坐在宝座上（1:3）。我所失去的那些东西，也许永远回不来了，我可以在他面前为它们哀伤。但我心里最深的根，不必再扎在那些会旧的地方；我可以扎在他身上，因为我们「得了不能震动的国」（12:28）。今天我可以把一件已经失去、至今还让我难过的事，告诉这位永不改变的主，然后感谢他：主啊，一切都在改变，但你没有变；你昨日救了我，今日托住我，一直到永远，你都是一样的（13:8）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 has two parts. **The prologue** (vv. 1–4): God, who once spoke through the prophets, has now spoken by his Son; this Son is heir of all things and maker of the world, the radiance of God's glory and the exact imprint of his nature, upholding the universe by the word of his power; after making purification for sins he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high, far superior to the angels. **The Son is greater than the angels** (vv. 5–14): the author quotes seven Old Testament passages in a row to set the Son beside the angels. God calls him Son and commands the angels to worship him; calls him God and says his throne is forever; the heavens and earth will wear out like a garment, but he remains the same; God invites him to sit at his right hand, while the angels are all ministering spirits sent to serve those who will inherit salvation.

Almost the whole chapter is about who Christ is; the first word of exhortation does not come until chapter 2. The author wants his tired readers first to stop and look closely at this Son. How they should live comes later.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–2a God Has Spoken by His Son

**1. "God spoke to our fathers."** "Long ago, at many times and in many ways, God spoke to our fathers by the prophets" (1:1).
- **God is a God who speaks:** the first subject in the book is "God." We can know God not because we found him, but because he spoke first.
- **"At many times and in many ways":** God spoke in parts and by different means: through dreams, visions, the law, songs and the prophets' oracles, and through his acts in history. All these words were true, but they were not yet the whole.
- **The author honors the Old Testament:** he never says its words were wrong. Instead he quotes it throughout this book and calls it the speech of "the Holy Spirit" (3:7; 10:15).

**2. "In these last days he has spoken to us by his Son."** "But in these last days he has spoken to us by his Son" (1:2a).
- **"These last days":** in the Old Testament, "the latter days" are the time when God fulfills all his promises. The author says that time has come, because the Son has come.
- **"By his Son":** the Greek has no "his"; it reads literally "by a Son," and many take this to stress what kind of speaker this is. Once God spoke through servants; now he has spoken through one who is a Son. The prophets carried God's word; the Son himself is God's Word (John 1:1, 14).
- **The final word:** the Son is not just the greatest of the prophets; he is God's final and fullest revelation. "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (John 1:18). So no one who wants to know God needs to look elsewhere for new revelation. All that God means to say he has said in his Son.

> The point: God has not been silent. Once he spoke through the prophets in parts; now he has spoken fully in his Son. To know God, listen to his Son.

## Going Deeper: 1:2b–4 Who This Son Is

**1. Seven descriptions.** In one long sentence (in Greek, vv. 1–4 are a single sentence), the author says who the Son is and what he has done:
- **"Whom he appointed the heir of all things":** all things will finally belong to him.
- **"Through whom also he created the world":** he is before all things, and all things were made through him (John 1:3; Col 1:16).
- **"The radiance of the glory of God":** like the sun and its light: the light comes from the sun yet exists together with it. Many note that the word can also mean "reflection"; either way, the meaning is that in the Son we see the glory of God.
- **"The exact imprint of his nature":** the word for "imprint" was used for the mark a seal leaves, identical to the seal itself. What God is, the Son is. Jesus said, "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9).
- **"He upholds the universe by the word of his power":** he did not only create the world in the beginning; he holds it up now and carries it toward the end God has appointed.
- **"After making purification for sins":** in the middle of this list, the author speaks of the cross. The one who made all things came himself to cleanse our sins. This is the center of the whole book, and chapters 9–10 will unfold it.
- **"He sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high":** the Old Testament priests always "stood" when they offered sacrifices, because their work was never finished (10:11). Christ "sat down," because the work of cleansing sin is done (10:12).

**2. The Son's deity.** These verses ascribe to the Son what only God does, creating and upholding all things, and call him "the exact imprint of [God's] nature." When the fourth-century church debated Christ's deity, Athanasius often cited this verse against Arius's claim that the Son was created. The Son is not the same person as the Father, yet he shares the Father's very nature. He is "the radiance of the glory of God," not a second, lesser god.

**3. "Superior to angels."** "Having become as much superior to angels as the name he has inherited is more excellent than theirs" (1:4).
- **"The name he has inherited":** the verses that follow show that this name is "Son" (v. 5). The Son was always the Son (v. 2); but when he had finished the work of cleansing and ascended to the Father's right hand, he received, as the Son of Man and Messiah, the honor that name deserves (see Phil 2:9–11; Rom 1:4).
- **Why angels?** Jews believed the law had been delivered through angels (2:2; Acts 7:53; Gal 3:19). The author wants to show that the Son is greater than the angels who brought the old covenant, so the salvation the Son brings is greater too (2:2–3). Some scholars also think some of the readers were venerating angels, or thought of Christ as one of the angels (see Col 2:18). Either way, the author's point is clear: Christ is not an angel but the Lord whom angels worship.

> The point: This Son is the Creator, the radiance of God's glory, the Lord who upholds all things. He himself made purification for our sins, and then he sat down. His work is finished.

## Going Deeper: 1:5–9 "You Are My Son"

**1. "You are my Son, today I have begotten you."** "For to which of the angels did God ever say, 'You are my Son, today I have begotten you'? Or again, 'I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son'?" (1:5).
- **Two passages:** the first is from Psalm 2:7 and the second from 2 Samuel 7:14. Both are God's words to David's descendant, the anointed king, and both are finally fulfilled in Christ.
- **Which day is "today"?** Interpreters have understood this in different ways. Many (such as Calvin) take "today" to be the day of Christ's resurrection, ascension and enthronement; in Acts 13:33 Paul links Psalm 2:7 with Jesus' resurrection. This does not mean Jesus became God's Son on that day (v. 2 says God made the world through the Son long before); it means he was publicly installed as King in his role as Messiah. Some church fathers, such as Augustine, took "today" as the eternal today, referring to the Father's eternal begetting of the Son. Both views agree that the Son is not created but is the eternal Son.

**2. "Let all God's angels worship him."** "And again, when he brings the firstborn into the world, he says, 'Let all God's angels worship him'" (1:6).
- **"Firstborn":** here the word does not mean the first one created; it speaks of rank and inheritance. God says of David's descendant, "I will make him the firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth" (Ps 89:27).
- **"When he brings [him] into the world":** interpreters hold three views: the incarnation, when angels praised God on the night in Bethlehem; the ascension, when Christ entered "the world to come" (2:5) and the angels worshiped him; or the second coming. The word "again" can also be read with "brings" ("when he again brings the firstborn into the world"), which is why some think of the second coming. Whichever moment is meant, the angels worship him, so he cannot be one of them.
- **The source of the quotation:** it most likely comes from the Septuagint of Deuteronomy 32:43. The Masoretic Hebrew text lacks this line, but a Dead Sea Scrolls Hebrew manuscript has a similar one (the ESV follows it: "bow down to him, all gods"); Psalm 97:7 has a similar line: "worship him, all you gods!"

**3. "Your throne, O God, is forever and ever."** "Of the angels he says, 'He makes his angels winds, and his ministers a flame of fire.' But of the Son he says, 'Your throne, O God, is forever and ever, the scepter of uprightness is the scepter of your kingdom. You have loved righteousness and hated wickedness; therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness beyond your companions'" (1:7–9).
- **Angels like wind and fire:** angels are mighty, yet they are "ministers" who are sent; many take this to mean that, like wind and flame, they change.
- **The Son's throne is forever:** Psalm 45 was first a song for a royal wedding, but the author sees that it finally points to Christ.
- **"O God":** most interpreters take "God" here as a form of address, so the Son is addressed as "God." (Some render it "God is your throne," but the direct address "You, Lord" in v. 10 and the contrast "of the Son he says" make the address much more likely.) Other New Testament passages call Christ God as well (John 1:1; 20:28; Rom 9:5; Titus 2:13).
- **"God, your God, has anointed you":** the same passage calls the Son "God" and says that "God, your God," anointed him. This shows that the Father and the Son are both God, yet distinct persons. As man, the Son was anointed by the Father in his obedience. The distinction of Father and Son, which the church confesses within the Trinity, is already visible here.

> The point: The angels are servants who are sent; the Son is the Lord whom angels worship. The Father calls him Son and calls him God, and his throne stands forever.

## Going Deeper: 1:10–14 "But You Remain"

**1. "They will perish, but you remain."** "And, 'You, Lord, laid the foundation of the earth in the beginning, and the heavens are the work of your hands; they will perish, but you remain; they will all wear out like a garment, like a robe you will roll them up, like a garment they will be changed. But you are the same, and your years will have no end'" (1:10–12).
- **Words first spoken to the LORD:** this comes from Psalm 102:25–27, where a suffering man prays to the LORD. The author applies it to the Son. Words the Old Testament speaks of the LORD, the New Testament speaks of Christ, so the author puts Christ on the same footing as the LORD.
- **Like a garment:** the heavens and earth seem the most solid and lasting things there are, yet before the Son they are like a garment that wears out. He will "roll them up," and they "will be changed" (see 12:26–27; 2 Pet 3:13).
- **"But you are the same":** this line echoes again at the end of the book: "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:8).
- **Comfort for the readers:** these believers had lost property (10:34) and may have been watching everything familiar change. The author tells them that everything created grows old and changes, but the Son never changes (see 12:27–28).

**2. "Sit at my right hand."** "And to which of the angels has he ever said, 'Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet'?" (1:13).
- **Psalm 110:1:** this is the Old Testament text the New Testament quotes most. Jesus himself quoted it (Matt 22:44) to show that the Messiah is not only David's son but David's Lord.
- **"Until":** Christ is already seated on the throne, but his enemies have not all been subdued. We live between "already" and "not yet" (see 2:8). The author returns to this in chapter 10: Christ "sat down at the right hand of God, waiting from that time until his enemies should be made a footstool for his feet" (10:12–13).

**3. "Ministering spirits."** "Are they not all ministering spirits sent out to serve for the sake of those who are to inherit salvation?" (1:14).
- **Angels serve believers:** these mighty spirits are sent by God "to serve for the sake of those who are to inherit salvation." Elisha's servant saw "the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire" all around them (2 Kgs 6:17). Believers often cannot see it, but they are not alone.
- **Do not worship angels:** angels are servants, not objects of worship. When John fell down to worship an angel, the angel said, "You must not do that!... Worship God" (Rev 22:8–9).
- **"Are to inherit":** the Son is heir of all things (v. 2), and those who trust him will "inherit salvation" too. That salvation has begun now, but it will be fully revealed in the future.

> The point: The heavens and earth will grow old, and everything in the world will change, but the Son never changes. He is already on the throne, and even the angels are sent to serve those who belong to him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "They will perish, but you remain; they will all wear out like a garment... But you are the same, and your years will have no end" (1:11–12). I once thought some things would never change. The old street I grew up on was later torn down for redevelopment; when I went back I could not even recognize the roads. The church I served for years later split, and its building was sold. I worked at one company for twenty years and thought I would stay until retirement, but the company went under. When these things happened, it felt as if I had lost a part of myself. The readers of Hebrews knew this too: "the plundering of [their] property" (10:34), as the life they knew was taken away piece by piece. The author does not tell them, "Don't be sad; just build another one." He turns their eyes to the Son: the heavens and earth will wear out like a garment, "but you are the same." This unchanging Son does not watch from far away as people wear out. He "upholds the universe by the word of his power" (1:3), and he upholds me, someone who also grows old and weak. He himself took a body that could tire and die, and made "purification for sins" for me, and then sat down on the throne (1:3). Some of what I lost may never come back, and I can grieve it before him. But the deepest roots of my heart no longer need to sink into what wears out; they can sink into him, for we are "receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken" (12:28). Today I can tell this unchanging Lord about one thing I have lost that still grieves me, and then thank him: Lord, everything is changing, but you have not changed. You saved me yesterday, you uphold me today, and you will be the same forever (13:8).
