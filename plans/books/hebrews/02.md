## 本章脉络

第二章分成三段。**第一次警告**（1-4节）：藉着天使所传的话尚且是确定的，我们若忽略这么大的救恩，怎能逃罪呢？所以我们当越发郑重所听见的道理，恐怕我们随流失去。**人算什么**（5-9节）：神按诗篇第8篇所说，要把万物都交给人管理；如今我们还不见万物都服人，惟独看见耶稣，他成为比天使小一点，为人人尝了死味，就得了荣耀尊贵为冠冕。**儿子成为我们的弟兄**（10-18节）：神要领许多的儿子进荣耀里去，就使救他们的元帅因受苦难得以完全；他称他们为弟兄也不以为耻；他成了血肉之体，要藉着死败坏掌死权的魔鬼，释放一生因怕死而为奴仆的人；他凡事与弟兄相同，作慈悲忠信的大祭司，为百姓的罪献上挽回祭，能搭救被试探的人。

第一章讲儿子比天使更高，第二章却讲他「成为比天使小一点」。他从那么高的地方降下来，是为了到我们中间，成为我们的弟兄。

## 深读 2:1-4 不要随流失去

**1. 「恐怕我们随流失去」。** 「所以，我们当越发郑重所听见的道理，恐怕我们随流失去。」（2:1）
- **「所以」：** 劝勉是从第一章来的。既然说话的是这样一位儿子，我们就要格外认真地听。
- **「随流失去」：** 这个词的意思是流过去、滑走；许多解经家用一条没有系好的船，被水流慢慢带离港口来比喻。离开信仰常常不是一下子转身，而是不知不觉地漂走：今天少读一次经，下个月少去一次聚会，心里渐渐不在意了。漂走不需要用力，只需要什么都不做。
- **「我们」：** 作者没有把自己放在外面。他知道自己也会漂走。

**2. 「我们若忽略这么大的救恩，怎能逃罪呢」。** 「那藉着天使所传的话既是确定的；凡干犯悖逆的都受了该受的报应。我们若忽略这么大的救恩，怎能逃罪呢？」（2:2-3上）
- **「藉着天使所传的话」：** 指西乃山上颁布的律法（参徒7:53；加3:19）。干犯律法的人尚且受了报应，何况轻忽神儿子所带来的救恩呢？这种由小推到大的论证，作者在书中用了好几次（9:13-14；10:28-29；12:25）。
- **「忽略」：** 作者说的不是公开反对，而是「忽略」，好像对一份贵重的礼物不闻不问。对神的救恩冷淡，本身就是很严重的事。
- **「这么大的救恩」：** 救恩之所以大，是因为那位拯救的是神的儿子（第一章），他所付的代价是他自己的死（9节）。

**3. 「起先是主亲自讲的」。** 「这救恩起先是主亲自讲的，后来是听见的人给我们证实了。神又按自己的旨意，用神迹、奇事和百般的异能，并圣灵的恩赐，同他们作见证。」（2:3下-4）
- **三重的见证：** 主耶稣亲自讲了这救恩；听见的使徒们传下来；神又用神迹和圣灵的恩赐为他们作见证。我们所信的，不是一个人想出来的道理，而是有根有据的福音。
- **「按自己的旨意」：** 圣灵的恩赐怎样分配，是神按他的旨意决定的（参林前12:11），不是人能控制的。

> 要点：离开基督，常常不是一下子转身，而是慢慢漂走。救恩越大，忽略它就越危险；所以我们要牢牢地系在主所说的话上。

## 深读 2:5-9 我们惟独看见耶稣

**1. 「人算什么，你竟顾念他」。** 「我们所说将来的世界，神原没有交给天使管辖。但有人在经上某处证明说：人算什么，你竟顾念他？世人算什么，你竟眷顾他？你叫他比天使微小一点，赐他荣耀尊贵为冠冕，并将你手所造的都派他管理，叫万物都服在他的脚下。」（2:5-8上）
- **「将来的世界」：** 神没有把将来的世界交给天使，而是交给「人」。
- **诗篇第8篇：** 这首诗赞美神创造人的尊荣：人虽然渺小，神却让他管理万物（参创1:26-28）。诗篇8:5的「天使」，希伯来文原是「神」或「属天的」，七十士译本译作「天使」，作者照七十士译本引用。
- **「人」是指谁？** 许多解经家认为，诗篇说的是全人类，作者的意思是：神给人的这个呼召，最终在耶稣这位真正的人身上实现。也有人认为，作者直接把这首诗读作指着基督说的（「世人」原文是「人子」，也是耶稣常用来称自己的名号）。两种读法都同意：神起初给人的尊荣，要在耶稣身上成全。

**2. 「只是如今我们还不见万物都服他」。** 「既叫万物都服他，就没有剩下一样不服他的。只是如今我们还不见万物都服他。」（2:8下）
- **作者很诚实：** 他没有假装一切都很好。疾病、死亡、不公义、逼迫，都还在这个世界上；信徒的家业被人抢去，也还没有讨回来。
- **人失去了什么：** 神要人管理万物，人却因罪失去了这份尊荣，反而被死亡和惧怕辖制（14-15节）。

**3. 「惟独见……耶稣」。** 「惟独见那成为比天使小一点的耶稣；因为受死的苦，就得了尊贵荣耀为冠冕，叫他因着神的恩，为人人尝了死味。」（2:9）
- **「惟独见」：** 我们还看不见万物都服在人的脚下，却看见了耶稣。他是第一个已经戴上冠冕的人；他在哪里，属他的人将来也要在那里。
- **「比天使小一点」：** 第一章讲他远超过天使，这里讲他为我们降卑，成为比天使小一点（参腓2:6-8）。「一点」原文也可以译作「暂时」，英文标准译本就译作「暂时」。
- **「尝了死味」：** 不是只尝一点点，而是完完全全经历了死。他代替我们，承受了罪的工价（罗6:23）。
- **「为人人」：** 历代信徒对「人人」的范围有不同的理解。阿米念传统（如卫斯理）认为，「人人」就是每一个人，基督为每一个人死（参约一2:2），救恩向每一个人敞开。改革宗传统（如欧文）则按上下文来读，认为基督特别为神所拣选的人而死（参10节的「许多的儿子」，13节「神所给我的儿女」），他的死也足够拯救所有人。两种看法都同意：福音要向所有的人传讲，凡信的人都必得救。
- **「因着神的恩」：** 有少数抄本作「离了神」，俄利根已经知道这个异文；但最早的和大多数的抄本（包括P46、西奈抄本和梵蒂冈抄本）和译本都作「因着神的恩」。耶稣为我们死，是出于神的恩典。

> 要点：我们还看不见一切都已经得胜，却已经看见了耶稣。他为我们尝了死味，又得了荣耀的冠冕；他所在的地方，就是我们将来要到的地方。

## 深读 2:10-13 领许多的儿子进荣耀里去

**1. 「因受苦难得以完全」。** 「原来那为万物所属、为万物所本的，要领许多的儿子进荣耀里去，使救他们的元帅，因受苦难得以完全，本是合宜的。」（2:10）
- **「为万物所属、为万物所本的」：** 就是父神。拯救的计划是从父来的。
- **「领许多的儿子进荣耀里去」：** 神的目的不只是赦免我们，而是把我们带进荣耀里，作他的儿女。
- **「元帅」：** 这个词也可以译作「开路的」「领袖」「创始者」（参徒3:15；5:31；来12:2）。他走在前面，开出一条路，又带着我们走这条路。
- **「得以完全」：** 这不是说耶稣在道德上曾经有缺欠。他从来没有犯过罪（4:15；7:26）；在神性上，儿子本来就是完全的。「完全」在这卷书里常常是指完成、合格：他经过受苦和死亡，完成了作救主所需的一切，成了完全合格的救主和大祭司（5:8-9）。这里讲的是：他成为人以后，要作救主和大祭司，这个职分是藉着受苦完成的。

**2. 「都是出于一」。** 「因那使人成圣的和那些得以成圣的，都是出于一。所以，他称他们为弟兄也不以为耻。」（2:11）
- **「出于一」：** 解经家有不同的理解：多数认为是指同出于一位父神；也有人认为是指同出于亚当，或同属亚伯拉罕的后裔（16节），也就是说，耶稣和我们有同样的人性。
- **「不以为耻」：** 这位使人成圣的主，称我们这些软弱、有罪的人为「弟兄」，并不觉得丢脸。复活的主也对马利亚说：「你往我弟兄那里去，告诉他们说，我要升上去见我的父，也是你们的父。」（约20:17）

**3. 三处引文。** 「说：我要将你的名传与我的弟兄，在会中我要颂扬你；又说：我要倚赖他；又说：看哪，我与神所给我的儿女。」（2:12-13）
- **诗篇22:22：** 这首诗以「我的神，我的神！为什么离弃我？」开始（诗22:1），耶稣在十字架上就是这样呼喊的；诗的后半却转为在会中赞美神。受苦的弥赛亚，最后要在他的弟兄中间颂扬父的名。
- **以赛亚书8:17-18：** 「我要倚赖他」显出耶稣作为人，是怎样完全信靠父的。「我与神所给我的儿女」显出信徒是父交给子的，子不会丢失他们（参约6:37-39）。

> 要点：神要把许多的儿女带进荣耀里。耶稣走在前面开路，经过受苦完成了救恩；他称我们为弟兄，并不以我们为耻。

## 深读 2:14-18 他凡事与他的弟兄相同

**1. 「他也照样亲自成了血肉之体」。** 「儿女既同有血肉之体，他也照样亲自成了血肉之体，特要藉着死败坏那掌死权的，就是魔鬼，并要释放那些一生因怕死而为奴仆的人。」（2:14-15）
- **真正的人：** 儿子不是装成人的样子，而是「亲自成了血肉之体」，会饿、会累、会流泪、会死。
- **「藉着死」：** 耶稣胜过死亡的方法，不是避开死亡，而是亲自进入死亡。
- **「败坏」：** 原文的意思是使他失去作用、解除他的权势，不是说魔鬼已经不存在了。魔鬼怎样「掌死权」？许多解经家认为，他引诱人犯罪，又在神面前控告罪人（参启12:10），而「死的毒钩就是罪」（林前15:56）。基督担当了我们的罪，魔鬼就失去了控告我们的把柄（参西2:14-15；约一3:8）。
- **「一生因怕死而为奴仆」：** 怕死不只是在临终的时候。人一辈子都活在它的阴影下，许多焦虑、贪婪、讨好人，追到底都和怕死有关。基督要释放的，正是这样的人。

**2. 「他并不救拔天使，乃是救拔亚伯拉罕的后裔」。** 「他并不救拔天使，乃是救拔亚伯拉罕的后裔。」（2:16）
- **「救拔」：** 原文有伸手抓住、扶起的意思。
- **为什么不是天使：** 儿子没有成为天使，而是成为人。他来，是为了救人。
- **「亚伯拉罕的后裔」：** 这是对犹太读者很亲切的说法；在新约中，凡信基督的人，不分犹太人或外邦人，都是亚伯拉罕的后裔（加3:29）。

**3. 「慈悲忠信的大祭司」。** 「所以，他凡事该与他的弟兄相同，为要在神的事上成为慈悲忠信的大祭司，为百姓的罪献上挽回祭。」（2:17）
- **第一次提到「大祭司」：** 这是全书的主题，后面要一再展开（4:14-10:18）。
- **「慈悲」：** 他向软弱的人存怜悯。
- **「忠信」：** 他在神面前尽忠，把百姓的事办到底。
- **「挽回祭」：** 学者对这个词的意思有讨论。有人（如多德）强调它是除去罪、洁净罪，因为这里的受词是「罪」；许多人（如莫理斯）指出，它也包括平息神对罪公义的忿怒。这两方面不必对立：神是公义的，罪必须受审判；基督却代替我们，担当了罪的刑罚，使神的忿怒不再临到信他的人。这挽回祭也是神自己预备的：「不是我们爱神，乃是神爱我们，差他的儿子为我们的罪作了挽回祭。」（约一4:10）

**4. 「就能搭救被试探的人」。** 「他自己既然被试探而受苦，就能搭救被试探的人。」（2:18）
- **他知道试探有多重：** 耶稣在旷野受魔鬼的试探（太4:1），在客西马尼园说：「我心里甚是忧伤，几乎要死。」（太26:38）他没有犯罪（4:15），却比任何人都更知道抵挡试探有多难，因为他从来没有向试探让步。
- **「搭救」：** 这个词就是帮助、援助的意思；七十士译本常用它讲神帮助呼求他的人。我们在试探中呼求他，他不是远远地看着，而是跑过来帮助我们。

> 要点：儿子成了和我们一样的血肉之体，藉着死胜过了死亡的权势，释放怕死的人。他作了我们慈悲忠信的大祭司，为我们的罪献上挽回祭，也在试探中帮助我们。

## 默想

**默想：** 「并要释放那些一生因怕死而为奴仆的人。」（2:15）我们中国人很忌讳谈死。有的人家过年不许说「死」字，医院和楼房常常没有「4」楼；家里的老人病重了，子女不敢告诉他实情，大家都装作没事；我自己也从来不愿意想遗嘱的事，好像一想就会不吉利。可是，不谈不等于不怕。我心里许多的焦虑，拼命存钱、拼命保养，怕被人忘记，追到底都和怕死有关。希伯来书说，人「一生因怕死而为奴仆」。耶稣没有叫我假装不怕。他自己「亲自成了血肉之体」，在客西马尼园「大声哀哭，流泪祷告」（5:7），然后真的走进了死亡，「为人人尝了死味」（2:9）。死亡最可怕的，不是身体的痛苦，而是死后要站在神面前，我的罪要被审判（9:27）。可是他已经为我的罪献上了挽回祭（2:17），死的毒钩就被拔去了。他是第一个从死里复活、永不再死、戴上冠冕的人（2:9；参罗6:9），现在拿着死亡和阴间的钥匙（启1:18）。他也不以我为耻，称我为弟兄（2:11）。所以我不必再用忌讳来躲避死亡；我可以看着它，说：「死啊！你的毒钩在哪里？」（林前15:55）如果我从来没有信靠过他，今天就可以把我的罪和对死亡的恐惧一同交给他。也许我心里仍然会怕，我可以把这份惧怕带到那位曾经流泪祷告的大祭司面前。今天，我可以想一想自己最怕的是什么，然后向他祷告：主耶稣，你为我尝了死味，又活过来了；求你释放我，不再作惧怕的奴仆。我死的那一天，你必领我进荣耀里去（2:10）。感谢神，使我们藉着我们的主耶稣基督得胜（林前15:57）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 has three parts. **The first warning** (vv. 1–4): if the message declared by angels proved reliable, how shall we escape if we neglect such a great salvation? So we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, lest we drift away. **What is man?** (vv. 5–9): according to Psalm 8, God means to put all things under man's rule; we do not yet see everything in subjection to him, but we see Jesus, made for a little while lower than the angels, who tasted death for everyone and is crowned with glory and honor. **The Son becomes our brother** (vv. 10–18): in bringing many sons to glory, God made the founder of their salvation perfect through suffering; he is not ashamed to call them brothers; he took on flesh and blood to destroy through death the devil, who has the power of death, and to deliver those who all their lives were enslaved by the fear of death; made like his brothers in every respect, he became a merciful and faithful high priest who made propitiation for the people's sins and is able to help those who are tempted.

Chapter 1 says the Son is higher than the angels; chapter 2 says he "was made lower than the angels." He came down from so high a place to come among us and become our brother.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–4 Do Not Drift Away

**1. "Lest we drift away."** "Therefore we must pay much closer attention to what we have heard, lest we drift away from it" (2:1).
- **"Therefore":** the exhortation flows from chapter 1. Since the one speaking is such a Son, we must listen with special care.
- **"Drift away":** the word means to flow past or slip away; many commentators picture a boat not tied fast, slowly carried off by the current. Leaving the faith is usually not a sudden turn but an unnoticed drift: one less time reading Scripture today, one less meeting next month, a heart that slowly stops caring. Drifting takes no effort; it only takes doing nothing.
- **"We":** the author does not exclude himself. He knows he too could drift.

**2. "How shall we escape if we neglect such a great salvation?"** "For since the message declared by angels proved to be reliable and every transgression or disobedience received a just retribution, how shall we escape if we neglect such a great salvation?" (2:2–3a).
- **"The message declared by angels":** this means the law given at Sinai (see Acts 7:53; Gal 3:19). If those who broke the law were punished, how much more those who treat lightly the salvation brought by God's Son? The author uses this "lesser to greater" argument several times in the book (9:13–14; 10:28–29; 12:25).
- **"Neglect":** the author is not talking about open opposition but about neglect, like ignoring a precious gift. Indifference to God's salvation is itself a serious thing.
- **"Such a great salvation":** the salvation is great because the one who saves is God's Son (chapter 1), and the price he paid was his own death (v. 9).

**3. "Declared at first by the Lord."** "It was declared at first by the Lord, and it was attested to us by those who heard, while God also bore witness by signs and wonders and various miracles and by gifts of the Holy Spirit distributed according to his will" (2:3b–4).
- **A threefold witness:** the Lord Jesus himself declared this salvation; the apostles who heard him passed it on; and God bore witness to them with miracles and gifts of the Spirit. What we believe is not a teaching someone invented but a gospel with firm grounds.
- **"According to his will":** how the gifts of the Spirit are given is God's decision, according to his will (see 1 Cor 12:11), not something people control.

> The point: Leaving Christ usually happens not by a sudden turn but by a slow drift. The greater the salvation, the more dangerous it is to neglect it, so we must hold fast to what the Lord has spoken.

## Going Deeper: 2:5–9 We See Jesus

**1. "What is man, that you are mindful of him?"** "Now it was not to angels that God subjected the world to come, of which we are speaking. It has been testified somewhere, 'What is man, that you are mindful of him, or the son of man, that you care for him? You made him for a little while lower than the angels; you have crowned him with glory and honor, putting everything in subjection under his feet'" (2:5–8a).
- **"The world to come":** God did not put the world to come under angels, but under "man."
- **Psalm 8:** this psalm praises God for the honor he gave humanity at creation: small as we are, God gave us rule over his works (see Gen 1:26–28). In Psalm 8:5 the Hebrew has *elohim* (the ESV there has "heavenly beings"); the Septuagint has "angels," and the author quotes the Septuagint.
- **Who is "man"?** Many interpreters think the psalm speaks of all humanity, and the author's point is that God's calling for humanity is finally fulfilled in Jesus, the true man. Others think the author reads the psalm directly as speaking of Christ ("son of man" is also the title Jesus most often used for himself). Both readings agree that the honor God first gave to humanity is fulfilled in Jesus.

**2. "At present, we do not yet see."** "Now in putting everything in subjection to him, he left nothing outside his control. At present, we do not yet see everything in subjection to him" (2:8b).
- **The author is honest:** he does not pretend everything is fine. Sickness, death, injustice and persecution are still in the world; the believers' plundered property has not been returned.
- **What humanity lost:** God meant us to rule over his works, but through sin we lost that honor and instead fell under the rule of death and fear (vv. 14–15).

**3. "But we see him... Jesus."** "But we see him who for a little while was made lower than the angels, namely Jesus, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death, so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone" (2:9).
- **"But we see":** we do not yet see all things under humanity's feet, but we do see Jesus. He is the first man already crowned; where he is, those who belong to him will one day be.
- **"Lower than the angels":** chapter 1 says he is far above the angels; here he humbles himself for us and is made lower than they (see Phil 2:6–8). The Greek word can mean "a little" or "for a little while"; the ESV takes it as "for a little while."
- **"Taste death":** not a small taste, but the full experience of death. In our place he bore the wages of sin (Rom 6:23).
- **"For everyone":** believers have understood the scope of "everyone" in different ways. The Arminian tradition (such as Wesley) takes "everyone" at face value: Christ died for every person (see 1 John 2:2), and salvation is open to every person. The Reformed tradition (such as Owen) reads it in the light of the context and holds that Christ died especially for those God has chosen (see "many sons" in v. 10 and "the children God has given me" in v. 13), and that his death is sufficient to save all. Both agree that the gospel is to be preached to all, and that all who believe will be saved.
- **"By the grace of God":** a few manuscripts read "apart from God," a reading Origen already knew; but the earliest and most manuscripts (including P46, Sinaiticus and Vaticanus) and translations read "by the grace of God." Jesus' death for us comes from God's grace.

> The point: We do not yet see everything won, but we do see Jesus. He tasted death for us and is crowned with glory; where he is now is where we are going.

## Going Deeper: 2:10–13 Bringing Many Sons to Glory

**1. "Perfect through suffering."** "For it was fitting that he, for whom and by whom all things exist, in bringing many sons to glory, should make the founder of their salvation perfect through suffering" (2:10).
- **"He, for whom and by whom all things exist":** this is God the Father. The plan of salvation comes from the Father.
- **"Bringing many sons to glory":** God's purpose is not only to forgive us but to bring us into glory as his children.
- **"Founder":** the word can also mean "pioneer," "leader" or "originator" (see Acts 3:15; 5:31; Heb 12:2). He goes ahead, opens a road, and leads us along it.
- **"Perfect":** this does not mean Jesus was once morally lacking. He never sinned (4:15; 7:26), and in his deity the Son lacked nothing. In this book "perfect" often means completed or fully qualified: through suffering and death he completed all that a Savior must do and became a fully qualified Savior and high priest (5:8–9). What is perfected is his office as man, Savior and high priest, completed through suffering.

**2. "All have one origin."** "For he who sanctifies and those who are sanctified all have one origin. That is why he is not ashamed to call them brothers" (2:11).
- **"One origin":** interpreters differ. Most take it to mean one Father, God; others think of one father Adam, or of belonging to Abraham's offspring (v. 16), meaning that Jesus shares our human nature.
- **"Not ashamed":** the Lord who sanctifies calls us, weak and sinful as we are, his "brothers," and he does not count it a disgrace. The risen Lord said to Mary, "Go to my brothers and say to them, 'I am ascending to my Father and your Father'" (John 20:17).

**3. Three quotations.** "Saying, 'I will tell of your name to my brothers; in the midst of the congregation I will sing your praise.' And again, 'I will put my trust in him.' And again, 'Behold, I and the children God has given me'" (2:12–13).
- **Psalm 22:22:** this psalm begins, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Ps 22:1), the cry Jesus uttered on the cross; but its second half turns to praising God in the congregation. The suffering Messiah will in the end praise the Father's name among his brothers.
- **Isaiah 8:17–18:** "I will put my trust in him" shows how Jesus, as a man, trusted his Father completely. "I and the children God has given me" shows that believers are the Father's gift to the Son, and the Son will not lose them (see John 6:37–39).

> The point: God means to bring many children into glory. Jesus goes ahead to open the way and completed salvation through suffering; he calls us his brothers and is not ashamed of us.

## Going Deeper: 2:14–18 Made Like His Brothers in Every Respect

**1. "He himself likewise partook of the same things."** "Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself likewise partook of the same things, that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil, and deliver all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery" (2:14–15).
- **A real man:** the Son did not merely appear to be human; he "partook" of flesh and blood. He could be hungry, tired and in tears, and he could die.
- **"Through death":** Jesus overcame death not by avoiding it but by entering it himself.
- **"Destroy":** the word means to render powerless, to break someone's power; it does not mean the devil no longer exists. How does the devil "have the power of death"? Many explain that he lures people into sin and accuses sinners before God (see Rev 12:10), and "the sting of death is sin" (1 Cor 15:56). Since Christ bore our sins, the devil has lost his grounds to accuse us (see Col 2:14–15; 1 John 3:8).
- **"Through fear of death... lifelong slavery":** fear of death is not only for the deathbed. People live their whole lives in its shadow; much anxiety, greed and people-pleasing comes, at bottom, from the fear of death. These are the very people Christ came to set free.

**2. "It is not angels that he helps."** "For surely it is not angels that he helps, but he helps the offspring of Abraham" (2:16).
- **"Helps":** the Greek has the sense of reaching out to take hold of someone and lift them up.
- **Why not angels:** the Son did not become an angel; he became a man. He came to save human beings.
- **"The offspring of Abraham":** a warm phrase for Jewish readers; in the New Testament, everyone who belongs to Christ, Jew or Gentile, is Abraham's offspring (Gal 3:29).

**3. "A merciful and faithful high priest."** "Therefore he had to be made like his brothers in every respect, so that he might become a merciful and faithful high priest in the service of God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people" (2:17).
- **The first mention of "high priest":** this is the theme of the whole book, unfolded at length later (4:14–10:18).
- **"Merciful":** he has compassion on the weak.
- **"Faithful":** he is loyal before God and carries his people's cause through to the end.
- **"Propitiation":** scholars have discussed the meaning of this word. Some (such as C. H. Dodd) stress that it means removing or cleansing sin, since the object here is "sins"; many (such as Leon Morris) point out that it also includes turning away God's righteous wrath against sin. The two need not be set against each other: God is just, and sin must be judged; but Christ took our place and bore the penalty of sin, so that God's wrath no longer falls on those who trust him. And God himself provided this propitiation: "In this is love, not that we have loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins" (1 John 4:10).

**4. "He is able to help those who are being tempted."** "For because he himself has suffered when tempted, he is able to help those who are being tempted" (2:18).
- **He knows how heavy temptation is:** Jesus was tempted by the devil in the wilderness (Matt 4:1), and in Gethsemane he said, "My soul is very sorrowful, even to death" (Matt 26:38). He never sinned (4:15), yet he knows better than anyone how hard resisting temptation is, because he never gave in to it.
- **"Help":** the word simply means to help or come to someone's aid; the Septuagint often uses it of God helping those who cry to him. When we cry to him in temptation, he does not watch from afar; he runs to help us.

> The point: The Son took on flesh and blood like ours, broke the power of death through his death, and set free those who fear it. He became our merciful and faithful high priest, made propitiation for our sins, and helps us when we are tempted.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And deliver all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery" (2:15). Many Chinese families avoid talking about death. Some families forbid the word "die" at New Year; hospitals and apartment buildings often skip a fourth floor; when an elderly parent is gravely ill, the children dare not tell them the truth, and everyone pretends nothing is wrong. I myself never want to think about a will, as if thinking about it would bring bad luck. But not talking about death is not the same as not fearing it. Much of my anxiety, the frantic saving and the anxious care of my health, the fear of being forgotten, comes at bottom from the fear of death. Hebrews says people are "subject to lifelong slavery" through fear of death. Jesus does not ask me to pretend I am not afraid. He himself "partook of" flesh and blood; in Gethsemane he "offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears" (5:7); and then he really did enter death and "taste death for everyone" (2:9). The most fearful thing about death is not the pain of the body but standing before God afterward, with my sins under judgment (9:27). But he has already made propitiation for my sins (2:17), and death's sting has been drawn. He is the first to rise from death never to die again, and be crowned (2:9; see Rom 6:9), and he now holds "the keys of Death and Hades" (Rev 1:18). And he is not ashamed of me; he calls me his brother (2:11). So I no longer need taboos to hide from death; I can look at it and say, "O death, where is your sting?" (1 Cor 15:55). If I have never trusted him, today I can hand him both my sins and my fear of death. My heart may still be afraid at times, and I can bring that fear to the high priest who once prayed with tears. Today I can think about what I fear most, and then pray: Lord Jesus, you tasted death for me and came back to life; set me free so that I am no longer a slave to fear. On the day I die, you will bring me into glory (2:10). "Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Cor 15:57).
