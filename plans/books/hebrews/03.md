## 本章脉络

第三章分成三段。**耶稣比摩西更配得荣耀**（1-6节）：我们要思想耶稣，他是我们所承认的使者和大祭司；摩西在神的全家尽忠，是仆人，基督却是儿子，治理神的家；我们若坚持到底，便是他的家了。**诗篇95篇的警告**（7-11节）：圣灵说，你们今日若听他的话，就不可硬着心，像以色列人在旷野一样；神起誓说，他们断不可进入他的安息。**不要被罪迷惑**（12-19节）：要谨慎，免得有不信的恶心，离弃永生神；要趁着还有今日，天天彼此相劝；从埃及出来的那一代人，因为不信，尸首倒在旷野。

第一、二章讲耶稣比天使更大，第三章讲他比摩西更大。在犹太读者心中，摩西是神最伟大的仆人，是带领以色列人出埃及、颁布律法的人。作者没有贬低摩西，反而称赞他「尽忠」；但他要读者看见，摩西所指向的，是一位更大的。

## 深读 3:1-2 思想耶稣

**1. 「同蒙天召的圣洁弟兄啊」。** 「同蒙天召的圣洁弟兄啊，你们应当思想我们所认为使者、为大祭司的耶稣。」（3:1）
- **「圣洁弟兄」：** 读者是软弱、疲倦的人，作者却称他们为「圣洁」。这不是因为他们自己有多圣洁，而是因为耶稣「使人成圣」，又称他们为弟兄（2:11）。
- **「同蒙天召」：** 他们在地上失去了家业，受人羞辱，神却从天上呼召了他们。他们真正的家乡在天上（11:16；13:14）。

**2. 「思想……耶稣」。**
- **「思想」：** 这个词的意思是定睛、仔细地看、用心去想。作者给疲倦信徒的第一个吩咐，不是叫他们更努力，而是叫他们好好地看耶稣。
- **「使者」：** 原文就是「使徒」，意思是被差遣的。整本新约只有这里称耶稣为「使徒」。父差他来，把神的话带给我们（1:2）。
- **「大祭司」：** 他又代表我们到神面前。使者从神那里来到我们这里，大祭司从我们这里到神面前去；这两样职分，耶稣一身担当。
- **「我们所认为」：** 原文的意思是我们所承认的，可能指信徒在洗礼和聚会中公开承认的信仰（参4:14；10:23）。

**3. 「他为那设立他的尽忠」。** 「他为那设立他的尽忠，如同摩西在神的全家尽忠一样。」（3:2）
- **引自民数记12:7：** 神说：「我的仆人摩西不是这样；他是在我全家尽忠的。」那时米利暗和亚伦毁谤摩西，神亲自为他作证。
- **耶稣也尽忠：** 2:17说他是「慈悲忠信的大祭司」。他对父忠心，把父交给他的事做到底（约17:4）；他对我们也忠心，不会半途把我们丢下。

> 要点：我们疲倦的时候，最需要的不是先看自己，而是先看耶稣。他是父差来的使者，又是为我们到父面前的大祭司，他对父和对我们都忠心到底。

## 深读 3:3-6 建造房屋的和房屋

**1. 「好像建造房屋的比房屋更尊荣」。** 「他比摩西算是更配多得荣耀，好像建造房屋的比房屋更尊荣；因为房屋都必有人建造，但建造万物的就是神。」（3:3-4）
- **摩西是房屋的一部分：** 摩西虽然伟大，也是神百姓中的一员，是神「家」里的人。
- **耶稣是建造的那一位：** 「建造万物的就是神」，而第一章已经说，神「藉着他创造诸世界」（1:2）。许多解经家认为，作者在这里再一次把耶稣放在创造者的位置上（参1:2、10）。

**2. 「摩西为仆人……基督为儿子」。** 「摩西为仆人，在神的全家诚然尽忠，为要证明将来必传说的事。但基督为儿子，治理神的家。」（3:5-6上）
- **仆人和儿子：** 两个人都忠心，地位却不一样。仆人在家里服事，儿子却是家的继承人和主人（1:2）。
- **「为要证明将来必传说的事」：** 摩西的一生和他所立的会幕、祭司、献祭，都在指向将来要来的那一位。耶稣说：「你们如果信摩西，也必信我，因为他书上有指着我写的话。」（约5:46）

**3. 「我们……便是他的家了」。** 「我们若将可夸的盼望和胆量坚持到底，便是他的家了。」（3:6下）
- **神的家就是神的百姓：** 神的家不是一座建筑，而是信靠基督的人。
- **「若……坚持到底」：** 这节和3:14都带着一个「若」字。历代信徒对此有不同的理解。改革宗传统（如加尔文、欧文）一般认为，坚持到底是一个人真正属于基督的凭据，不是叫人靠坚持去赚得这个身份；神必保守真正属他的人到底（参腓1:6）。这个传统里也有人（如施莱纳）强调，这样的警告本身就是神保守他百姓的方法之一。阿米念传统（如卫斯理）一般认为，坚持到底是继续留在基督里的条件，真信徒若不坚持，就有可能失落。这几种看法都同意：真信心不是一时的热情，而是把盼望一直持守到底；而持守的力量，来自那位忠心的儿子。
- **「可夸的盼望和胆量」：** 读者正在受羞辱，作者却说他们可以有「胆量」，可以「夸」。这不是夸自己，而是夸他们所盼望的那一位。

> 要点：摩西是忠心的仆人，耶稣是治理神家的儿子。我们能在他的家中，不是因为自己站得稳，而是因为这位儿子忠心；所以要倚靠他，把所盼望的持守到底。

## 深读 3:7-11 不可硬着心

**1. 「圣灵有话说」。** 「圣灵有话说：你们今日若听他的话，就不可硬着心，像在旷野惹他发怒、试探他的时候一样。」（3:7-8）
- **诗篇95篇：** 作者引用的是诗篇95:7-11。他没有说「大卫说」或「诗人说」，而是说「圣灵有话说」，并且用现在式。到了4:7，他又说这是神「在大卫的书上」说的。可见圣灵是藉着人说话的。圣经是圣灵所默示的，圣灵今天仍然藉着圣经对我们说话。
- **「今日」：** 诗篇写于以色列人出埃及几百年以后，却仍然说「今日」。神的话对每一代人都直接说话；每一次听见，都是必须回应的「今日」。

**2. 旷野的那一代人。** 「在那里，你们的祖宗试我探我，并且观看我的作为有四十年之久。所以，我厌烦那世代的人，说：他们心里常常迷糊，竟不晓得我的作为！我就在怒中起誓说：他们断不可进入我的安息。」（3:9-11）
- **「惹他发怒、试探他」：** 诗篇95:8提到「米利巴」和「玛撒」，就是以色列人因为没有水而和摩西争闹的地方，他们说：「耶和华是在我们中间不是？」（出17:7）
- **加低斯的不信：** 后来在加低斯，探子回来报信，百姓不信神能带他们进迦南，反而要回埃及。神说：「这百姓藐视我要到几时呢？我在他们中间行了这一切神迹，他们还不信我要到几时呢？」（民14:11）
- **「观看我的作为」：** 他们亲眼看见十灾、过红海、吗哪、磐石出水，却仍然不信。看见神迹，不等于有信心。
- **「心里常常迷糊」：** 问题不在头脑，而在心里。他们「不晓得」神的作为，不是缺少证据，而是不肯信靠。
- **「我的安息」：** 在诗篇中，这首先是指迦南地。第四章会说明，这安息最终指向一个更大的安息。

> 要点：圣灵今天仍然在说话。以色列人看见了许多神迹，心却硬了；问题不在于他们看见的不够多，而在于他们不肯信。神的话每一次临到我们，都是必须回应的「今日」。

## 深读 3:12-19 免得被罪迷惑

**1. 「不信的恶心」。** 「弟兄们，你们要谨慎，免得你们中间或有人存着不信的恶心，把永生神离弃了。」（3:12）
- **不信是恶：** 作者把「不信」和「恶」连在一起。不信不只是理智上的疑问，而是心里不肯信靠神，这是得罪神的罪。
- **「把永生神离弃了」：** 犹太读者若退回旧约的制度，好像是回到祖宗的神那里；作者却说，离开基督，就是离开永生神。

**2. 「天天彼此相劝」。** 「总要趁着还有今日，天天彼此相劝，免得你们中间有人被罪迷惑，心里就刚硬了。」（3:13）
- **「天天」：** 不是一年一次的特会，而是每天。信心需要每天的提醒。
- **「彼此」：** 神保守信徒的方法之一，是藉着其他的信徒。一个人独自走信仰的路，很容易漂走（参10:24-25）。
- **「被罪迷惑」：** 罪很少直接说「我是罪」。它会骗人，说这不算什么、人人都这样、你是例外、以后再说。人心是诡诈的（耶17:9），自己常常看不清自己；所以需要别人的提醒。
- **「趁着还有今日」：** 今天还是回应的日子，神的门还开着。但这个「今日」不会永远延续，所以不要拖延。

**3. 「就在基督里有分了」。** 「我们若将起初确实的信心坚持到底，就在基督里有分了。」（3:14）
- **「有分」：** 原文的意思是同伴、有分的人（1:9的「同伴」也是这个词）。信徒和基督同享他所承受的一切。
- **原文的时态：** 这里的时态表示我们已经在基督里有分了。许多解经家因此认为，坚持到底是显明我们早已真正有分于基督。不同的看法可参看上面3:6的讨论。

**4. 「是因为不信的缘故了」。** 「那时，听见他话惹他发怒的是谁呢？岂不是跟着摩西从埃及出来的众人吗？……这样看来，他们不能进入安息是因为不信的缘故了。」（3:16-19）
- **蒙了救赎的人：** 倒在旷野的，不是埃及人，而是蒙神拯救、走出埃及的以色列人。经历过神大能的人，也会硬心。保罗也用这件事警戒哥林多的信徒：「自己以为站得稳的，须要谨慎，免得跌倒。」（林前10:12）
- **例外：** 但也有迦勒，「因他另有一个心志，专一跟从我」（民14:24），还有约书亚（民14:30、38）。同样的旷野，同样的困难，有人硬心，有人信靠。
- **根源是不信：** 作者在最后用一个词总结：「不信」。他们的悖逆、抱怨、试探神，根源都在于不信神的应许。

> 要点：罪会骗人，硬心常常是不知不觉的。神赐给我们弟兄姊妹，天天彼此提醒；趁着还有今日，就要听他的声音，信靠那位治理神家、忠心到底的儿子。

## 默想

**默想：** 「总要趁着还有今日，天天彼此相劝，免得你们中间有人被罪迷惑，心里就刚硬了。」（3:13）罪很少用它自己的名字出现在我心里。它常常换一个好听的名字：我对孩子发脾气，说是「严格管教」；我在背后讲人，说是「分享代祷事项」；我拼命赚钱、顾不上神，说是「为家庭负责」；我在网上看不该看的东西，说是「放松一下，又没有伤害谁」。名字换了，良心就不再刺痛了；日子久了，心就硬了。大卫犯了大罪以后，也是这样过了一段日子，直到先知拿单对他说：「你就是那人！」（撒下12:7），他才承认：「我得罪耶和华了！」这正是作者要信徒「天天彼此相劝」的原因：我自己看不清的罪，神常常藉着身边的弟兄姊妹让我看见。我不必怕别人指出我的罪。罪一旦显露、我向神承认的时候，我所面对的不是一位等着定我罪的主，而是那位「为百姓的罪献上挽回祭」的大祭司（2:17）。拿单接着对大卫说：「耶和华已经除掉你的罪，你必不至于死。」（撒下12:13）今天，我可以求圣灵指出一件我已经换了名字的罪，用它真正的名字向神承认；我也可以请一位信得过的弟兄姊妹为我祷告，必要的时候提醒我。轮到我提醒别人的时候，我也要记得拿单是先听了神的话才去的；我要用温柔的心，记得自己也是蒙怜悯的人（加6:1）。这个「今日」不只是对信主多年的人说的。还从来没有真正悔改、信靠基督的人，今天听见他的声音，就不要再硬着心，也不必先把自己收拾好才来。「我们若认自己的罪，神是信实的，是公义的，必要赦免我们的罪，洗净我们一切的不义。」（约一1:9）那位治理神家的儿子，没有因为我曾经硬心就把我赶出他的家；他是忠心的，他要保守我到底。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 has three parts. **Jesus is worthy of more glory than Moses** (vv. 1–6): we are to consider Jesus, the apostle and high priest of our confession; Moses was faithful in all God's house as a servant, but Christ is faithful over God's house as a son, and we are his house if we hold fast to the end. **The warning of Psalm 95** (vv. 7–11): the Holy Spirit says, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts," as Israel did in the wilderness; God swore that they would not enter his rest. **Do not be deceived by sin** (vv. 12–19): take care lest an evil, unbelieving heart lead you to fall away from the living God; exhort one another every day, as long as it is called "today"; the generation that came out of Egypt fell in the wilderness because of unbelief.

Chapters 1–2 say Jesus is greater than the angels; chapter 3 says he is greater than Moses. To Jewish readers Moses was God's greatest servant, the one who led Israel out of Egypt and gave the law. The author does not belittle Moses; he praises him as "faithful." But he wants his readers to see that Moses pointed to someone greater.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–2 Consider Jesus

**1. "Holy brothers."** "Therefore, holy brothers, you who share in a heavenly calling, consider Jesus, the apostle and high priest of our confession" (3:1).
- **"Holy brothers":** the readers are weak and tired, yet the author calls them "holy." This is not because they are so holy in themselves, but because Jesus is the one "who sanctifies" and calls them brothers (2:11).
- **"A heavenly calling":** they had lost their property on earth and were being shamed, but God had called them from heaven. Their true homeland is in heaven (11:16; 13:14).

**2. "Consider Jesus."**
- **"Consider":** the word means to fix one's eyes on, to look carefully, to think hard about. The author's first command to tired believers is not "try harder" but "look closely at Jesus."
- **"Apostle":** the word means one who is sent. This is the only place in the New Testament where Jesus is called an "apostle." The Father sent him to bring God's word to us (1:2).
- **"High priest":** he also represents us before God. An apostle comes from God to us; a high priest goes from us to God. Jesus alone does both.
- **"Of our confession":** this probably means the faith believers openly confess, at baptism and in worship (see 4:14; 10:23).

**3. "Faithful to him who appointed him."** "Who was faithful to him who appointed him, just as Moses also was faithful in all God's house" (3:2).
- **From Numbers 12:7:** God said, "Not so with my servant Moses. He is faithful in all my house." Miriam and Aaron had spoken against Moses, and God himself defended him.
- **Jesus is faithful too:** 2:17 calls him "a merciful and faithful high priest." He is faithful to the Father, finishing the work the Father gave him (John 17:4); and he is faithful to us, and will not drop us halfway.

> The point: When we are tired, what we need first is not to look at ourselves but to look at Jesus. He is the apostle sent from the Father and the high priest who goes to the Father for us, faithful to the Father and to us to the end.

## Going Deeper: 3:3–6 The Builder and the House

**1. "As the builder of a house has more honor than the house."** "For Jesus has been counted worthy of more glory than Moses—as much more glory as the builder of a house has more honor than the house itself. (For every house is built by someone, but the builder of all things is God.)" (3:3–4).
- **Moses is part of the house:** great as he was, Moses was one of God's people, a member of God's "house."
- **Jesus is the builder:** "The builder of all things is God," and chapter 1 has already said that God "created the world" through the Son (1:2). Many take the author here to place Jesus again on the Creator's side (see 1:2, 10).

**2. "Moses... as a servant... Christ... as a son."** "Now Moses was faithful in all God's house as a servant, to testify to the things that were to be spoken later, but Christ is faithful over God's house as a son" (3:5–6a).
- **Servant and son:** both were faithful, but their places differ. A servant serves in the house; a son is the heir and master of the house (1:2).
- **"To testify to the things that were to be spoken later":** Moses' life, and the tabernacle, priests and sacrifices he set up, all pointed to the one who was to come. Jesus said, "If you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of me" (John 5:46).

**3. "We are his house."** "And we are his house if indeed we hold fast our confidence and our boasting in our hope" (3:6b).
- **God's house is God's people:** God's house is not a building but the people who trust Christ.
- **"If indeed we hold fast":** this verse and 3:14 both contain an "if." Believers have understood it in different ways. The Reformed tradition (such as Calvin and Owen) generally holds that holding fast to the end is the evidence that someone truly belongs to Christ, not a way of earning a place with him; God will keep to the end those who are truly his (see Phil 1:6). Some in this tradition (such as Thomas Schreiner) stress that warnings like this are themselves one of the means by which God keeps his people. The Arminian tradition (such as Wesley) generally holds that holding fast to the end is the condition for remaining in Christ, and that true believers who do not hold fast may be lost. These views all agree that faith is not a passing enthusiasm but a hope held to the end, and that the strength to hold on comes from the faithful Son.
- **"Our confidence and our boasting":** the readers are being shamed, yet the author says they may have "confidence" and may "boast." This is not boasting in themselves, but in the one they hope in.

> The point: Moses was a faithful servant; Jesus is the Son over God's house. We belong to his house not because we stand firm by ourselves, but because of this faithful Son; so let us, leaning on him, hold fast our hope to the end.

## Going Deeper: 3:7–11 Do Not Harden Your Hearts

**1. "As the Holy Spirit says."** "Therefore, as the Holy Spirit says, 'Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as in the rebellion, on the day of testing in the wilderness'" (3:7–8).
- **Psalm 95:** the author quotes Psalm 95:7–11. He does not say "David says" or "the psalmist says," but "the Holy Spirit says," in the present tense. In 4:7 he says God spoke "through David": the Spirit speaks through human authors. Scripture is inspired by the Spirit, and the Spirit still speaks through it today.
- **"Today":** the psalm was written centuries after the exodus, yet it still says "today." God's word speaks afresh to every generation; every time we hear it is a "today" in which to respond.

**2. The wilderness generation.** "Where your fathers put me to the test and saw my works for forty years. Therefore I was provoked with that generation, and said, 'They always go astray in their heart; they have not known my ways.' As I swore in my wrath, 'They shall not enter my rest'" (3:9–11).
- **"The rebellion... the day of testing":** Psalm 95:8 names "Meribah" and "Massah," the place where Israel quarreled with Moses for lack of water and said, "Is the LORD among us or not?" (Exod 17:7).
- **Unbelief at Kadesh:** later, at Kadesh, the spies returned and the people did not believe God could bring them into Canaan; they wanted to go back to Egypt. God said, "How long will this people despise me? And how long will they not believe in me, in spite of all the signs that I have done among them?" (Num 14:11).
- **"Saw my works":** they saw the ten plagues, the crossing of the sea, the manna and the water from the rock with their own eyes, yet they still did not believe. Seeing miracles is not the same as having faith.
- **"They always go astray in their heart":** the problem was not in their heads but in their hearts. They "have not known" God's ways, not for lack of information, but because they would not trust him.
- **"My rest":** in the psalm this first means the land of Canaan. Chapter 4 will show that this rest points finally to a greater rest.

> The point: The Holy Spirit still speaks today. Israel saw many miracles, yet their hearts grew hard; the problem was not that they had seen too little, but that they would not believe. Every time God's word comes to us is a "today" in which to respond.

## Going Deeper: 3:12–19 Lest You Be Hardened by the Deceitfulness of Sin

**1. "An evil, unbelieving heart."** "Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God" (3:12).
- **Unbelief is evil:** the author ties "unbelief" to "evil." Unbelief is not merely an intellectual question; it is a heart that will not trust God, and that is sin against him.
- **"Fall away from the living God":** to Jewish readers, going back to the old system might feel like returning to the God of their fathers; but the author says that leaving Christ is leaving the living God.

**2. "Exhort one another every day."** "But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called 'today,' that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin" (3:13).
- **"Every day":** not a once-a-year conference, but every day. Faith needs daily reminders.
- **"One another":** one of the ways God keeps believers is through other believers. Someone who walks the road of faith alone drifts easily (see 10:24–25).
- **"The deceitfulness of sin":** sin seldom says outright, "I am sin." It deceives: this is nothing, everyone does it, you are the exception, deal with it later. "The heart is deceitful above all things" (Jer 17:9), and we often cannot see ourselves clearly; so we need others to remind us.
- **"As long as it is called 'today'":** today is still the day to respond; God's door is still open. But this "today" will not last forever, so do not put it off.

**3. "We share in Christ."** "For we share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end" (3:14).
- **"Share":** the word means companions or partners (it is the same word as "companions" in 1:9). Believers share in all that Christ inherits.
- **The tense:** the Greek says "we have come to share in Christ," and many interpreters take the author to mean that holding firm to the end shows that we have truly come to share in Christ. On the different views, see 3:6 above.

**4. "Because of unbelief."** "For who were those who heard and yet rebelled? Was it not all those who left Egypt led by Moses?... So we see that they were unable to enter because of unbelief" (3:16–19).
- **A redeemed people:** those who fell in the wilderness were not Egyptians but Israelites whom God had saved and brought out of Egypt. People who have experienced God's power can still harden their hearts. Paul uses the same story to warn the Corinthians: "Therefore let anyone who thinks that he stands take heed lest he fall" (1 Cor 10:12).
- **The exceptions:** there was Caleb, "because he has a different spirit and has followed me fully" (Num 14:24), and Joshua (Num 14:30, 38). The same wilderness, the same hardships: some hardened their hearts, others trusted.
- **The root is unbelief:** at the end the author sums it all up in one word: "unbelief." Their rebellion, their grumbling and their testing of God all came from not believing God's promise.

> The point: Sin deceives, and hardening usually happens without our noticing. God has given us brothers and sisters to remind one another every day; while it is still called "today," let us hear his voice and trust the Son who is faithful over God's house to the end.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called 'today,' that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin" (3:13). Sin seldom shows up in my heart under its own name. It usually takes a nicer one. I lose my temper with my children and call it "strict discipline." I talk about people behind their backs and call it "sharing a prayer request." I pour myself into making money and have no time for God, and call it "providing for my family." I look at things online I should not look at and call it "just unwinding; it doesn't hurt anyone." Once the name changes, my conscience stops stinging; given time, the heart grows hard. After David's great sin he lived like this for a while, until the prophet Nathan said to him, "You are the man!" (2 Sam 12:7), and only then did he admit, "I have sinned against the LORD." This is exactly why the author wants believers to "exhort one another every day": the sins I cannot see in myself, God often shows me through the brothers and sisters around me. I need not be afraid of having my sin pointed out. When sin comes to light and I confess it to God, I do not face a Lord waiting to condemn me, but the high priest who made "propitiation for the sins of the people" (2:17). Nathan went on to tell David, "The LORD also has put away your sin; you shall not die" (2 Sam 12:13). Today I can ask the Holy Spirit to show me one sin I have renamed, and confess it to God by its real name; I can also ask a trusted brother or sister to pray for me and, when needed, to remind me. And when it is my turn to remind someone else, I remember that Nathan went because God had first spoken to him, and I go gently, as one who has also been shown mercy (Gal 6:1). This "today" is not only for those who have believed for years. Anyone who has never truly repented and trusted Christ, and hears his voice today, must not harden their heart any longer, and need not tidy themselves up before coming. "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). The Son who is faithful over God's house has not thrown me out of his house because my heart was once hard; he is faithful, and he will keep me to the end.
