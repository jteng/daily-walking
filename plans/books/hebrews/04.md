## 本章脉络

第四章分成三段。**进入神的安息**（1-10节）：进入安息的应许仍然存留；以色列人听了福音，却因为不信没有进去；神在创世以后就安息了，后来又藉着大卫说「今日」，可见约书亚并没有叫他们得着最终的安息；所以必另有一安息日的安息，为神的子民存留。**神的道是活泼的**（11-13节）：我们务必竭力进入那安息；神的道比两刃的剑更快，连心中的思念和主意都能辨明，万物在他眼前都是赤露敞开的。**施恩的宝座**（14-16节）：我们有一位已经升入高天的大祭司，就是神的儿子耶稣；他能体恤我们的软弱，曾凡事受过试探，只是没有犯罪；所以我们可以坦然无惧地来到施恩的宝座前。

这一章从严厉的警告开始，到温暖的邀请结束。神的道像剑一样剖开我们的心（12-13节），但站在神面前的，不只是赤露敞开的我们，还有一位为我们升入高天的大祭司（14-16节）。

## 深读 4:1-5 进入安息的应许仍然存留

**1. 「就当畏惧」。** 「我们既蒙留下，有进入他安息的应许，就当畏惧，免得我们中间或有人似乎是赶不上了。」（4:1）
- **应许还在：** 旷野那一代人没有进去，但神的应许没有因此作废。
- **「畏惧」：** 这不是怀疑神的信实，而是严肃地看待不信的后果：怕我们轻看了应许，像以色列人一样落在审判之下。

**2. 「有福音传给我们，像传给他们一样」。** 「因为有福音传给我们，像传给他们一样；只是所听见的道与他们无益，因为他们没有信心与所听见的道调和。」（4:2）
- **旷野也有福音：** 以色列人在旷野听见的，是神要带他们进应许之地的好消息。我们听见的，是神要带我们进入他永远安息的好消息。
- **「调和」：** 和合本是照着一部分抄本译的，意思是他们听见了道，却没有用信心接受。最早的许多抄本作没有用信心与那些听见的人联合，有些译本就是这样译的；许多人认为，意思是他们没有和迦勒、约书亚这些真正信的人一同信。两种读法的重点都是：听见不等于得益处，要用信心接受。

**3. 「我们已经相信的人得以进入那安息」。** 「但我们已经相信的人得以进入那安息……其实造物之工，从创世以来已经成全了。论到第七日，有一处说：『到第七日，神就歇了他一切的工。』」（4:3-4）
- **「得以进入」：** 原文是现在式，许多解经家认为，信的人现在已经开始进入这安息，将来要完全进去。
- **神自己的安息：** 作者把诗篇95篇的「我的安息」和创世记2:2神在第七日的安息连在一起。神创造完毕就「歇了他一切的工」，不是因为累了，而是因为工作已经完成。这安息从创世以来就预备好了，等着人进去。

> 要点：神的安息从创世以来就已经预备好了。听见福音不等于进入安息；凡用信心接受神应许的人，现在就开始进入这安息。

## 深读 4:6-10 另有一安息日的安息

**1. 「若是约书亚已叫他们享了安息」。** 「所以过了多年，就在大卫的书上，又限定一日，如以上所引的说：『你们今日若听他的话，就不可硬着心。』若是约书亚已叫他们享了安息，后来神就不再提别的日子了。」（4:7-8）
- **作者的论证：** 约书亚带以色列人进了迦南，经上也说「耶和华……使他们四境平安」（书21:44）。可是几百年后，神又藉着大卫说「今日」，还在邀请人进入他的安息。可见迦南地的安息还不是最终的安息。
- **「约书亚」：** 在希腊文中，「约书亚」和「耶稣」是同一个名字。旧约的约书亚没有能给的安息，新约的耶稣给了。

**2. 「必另有一安息日的安息」。** 「这样看来，必另有一安息日的安息为神的子民存留。因为那进入安息的，乃是歇了自己的工，正如神歇了他的工一样。」（4:9-10）
- **「安息日的安息」：** 原文是一个很少见的词，意思是像守安息日那样的安息。作者用它来讲神子民最终要进入的安息。
- **「歇了自己的工」：** 历代信徒有不同的理解。有人认为，这是指不再靠自己的行为得救，安息在基督已经完成的工作上（参弗2:8-9）；加尔文把它和舍己连在一起：停下自己的私意和肉体的工作，让神在我们里面作工。也有许多解经家认为，这主要是指信徒将来息了地上的劳苦，进入永远的安息（参启14:13）。还有人（如欧文）认为第10节说的是基督自己：他完成了救赎的工作，就进入了他的安息。这几种理解都指向同一件事：安息的根基是神和基督已经完成的工作，而不是我们的努力。
- **和主日的关系：** 这段经文对守安息日或主日有什么意义，信徒也有不同的看法。《威斯敏斯德信条》（21.7-8）等传统认为，安息日的原则在主日继续存在，信徒要分别这一天来敬拜和安息；《海德堡要理问答》第103问也强调主日要殷勤聚会、学习神的话；也有许多福音派信徒认为，安息日是「后事的影儿」，已经在基督里成全（西2:16-17），信徒在守日的事上可以有自由（参罗14:5，虽然这节是否包括每周的安息日也有争论）。两种看法都同意：主日敬拜和安息是神所赐的恩典，最终的安息是基督自己。

> 要点：迦南地不是最终的安息，约书亚没有能给的安息，耶稣给了。信的人可以歇了自己的工，安息在基督已经完成的救恩上，又盼望将来完全的安息。

## 深读 4:11-13 神的道是活泼的

**1. 「务必竭力进入那安息」。** 「所以，我们务必竭力进入那安息，免得有人学那不信从的样子跌倒了。」（4:11）
- **竭力进入安息：** 这听起来好像矛盾。但作者要我们竭力的，不是靠自己的努力赚得安息，而是竭力不让任何东西拦阻我们信靠神。对抗不信，需要认真。

**2. 「神的道……比一切两刃的剑更快」。** 「神的道是活泼的，是有功效的，比一切两刃的剑更快，甚至魂与灵，骨节与骨髓，都能刺入、剖开，连心中的思念和主意都能辨明。」（4:12）
- **「活泼的，是有功效的」：** 神的话不是死的文字，而是活的、有能力的。第三章讲「圣灵有话说」，这里讲神的道刺入人心，两处都说明：神今天仍然藉着圣经说话。
- **「魂与灵，骨节与骨髓」：** 有人用这节经文来支持人有灵、魂、体三部分的看法（三分说）；也有人认为，人只有灵魂和身体两部分，「魂」和「灵」常常是同一件事的两个说法（二分说）。许多解经家认为，作者在这里不是要讲人的构造，而是用几个对比的说法强调：神的话能刺入人最深、最隐密的地方。
- **「心中的思念和主意」：** 人可以骗别人，也可以骗自己（3:13），却骗不了神的话。

**3. 「都是赤露敞开的」。** 「并且被造的没有一样在他面前不显然的；原来万物在那与我们有关系的主眼前，都是赤露敞开的。」（4:13）
- **没有可以躲藏的地方：** 亚当犯罪以后躲在树林中，神仍然看见他（创3:8）。在神面前，我们一切的假装都没有用。
- **「与我们有关系的主」：** 原文的意思是我们要向他交账的那一位。总有一天，我们要站在神面前交账。

> 要点：神的道是活的，能刺透人心最深的地方。在神面前没有可以躲藏的；这是严肃的警告，也为我们预备了下一段的安慰。

## 深读 4:14-16 施恩的宝座

**1. 「已经升入高天尊荣的大祭司」。** 「我们既然有一位已经升入高天尊荣的大祭司，就是神的儿子耶稣，便当持定所承认的道。」（4:14）
- **「升入高天」：** 旧约的大祭司每年一次进入至圣所（利16章）；耶稣却「升入高天」，进到神自己的面前（9:24）。
- **「便当持定」：** 读者想要放弃他们所承认的信仰，作者却说：我们有这样一位大祭司，怎么能放手呢？

**2. 「并非不能体恤我们的软弱」。** 「因我们的大祭司并非不能体恤我们的软弱。他也曾凡事受过试探，与我们一样，只是他没有犯罪。」（4:15）
- **「体恤」：** 他不是从外面同情我们，而是真的和我们一同感受。他知道饥饿、疲倦、被人误解、被朋友出卖的滋味。
- **「凡事受过试探，与我们一样」：** 他所受的试探是真实的，不是演戏。
- **「只是他没有犯罪」：** 他从来没有向试探让步。神学家对一个问题有不同的看法：耶稣有没有犯罪的可能？有人（如谢德）认为，他是神的儿子，所以不可能犯罪，但试探仍然是真实的；也有人（如贺智）认为，试探若是真实的，他既是真正的人，就有犯罪的可能，只是他从来没有犯。教会历史上多数神学家（包括多数改革宗神学家）持前一种看法。两种看法也都同意：耶稣没有堕落的本性，他的试探不是从里面的私欲来的（参雅1:14），而是从外面来的；耶稣完全无罪，他所受的试探也是真实的。
- **无罪反而更能体恤：** 一个人向试探让步，就不知道试探再往后有多重；耶稣从来没有让步，所以他承受了试探的全部重量。

**3. 「坦然无惧地来到施恩的宝座前」。** 「所以，我们只管坦然无惧地来到施恩的宝座前，为要得怜恤，蒙恩惠，作随时的帮助。」（4:16）
- **「施恩的宝座」：** 宝座本来是审判的地方，但因为我们的大祭司坐在宝座的右边，长远活着替我们祈求（8:1；7:25），它成了施恩的地方。旧约的「施恩座」在至圣所里，只有大祭司一年一次可以进去（利16:2、34；来9:7）；如今，凡信靠耶稣的人都可以随时来。
- **「坦然无惧」：** 这个词的意思是有胆量、说话自由。我们不必像犯人一样，战战兢兢地站在门外。
- **「得怜恤，蒙恩惠」：** 怜恤是为我们的失败，恩惠是为我们的需要。
- **「随时的帮助」：** 原文的意思是在需要的时候得帮助。神的帮助不一定提早给，却从不迟到。

> 要点：站在神面前，我们是赤露敞开的；但我们不是孤单地站在那里。我们的大祭司体恤我们的软弱，所以我们可以坦然地来到施恩的宝座前，在需要的时候得着怜恤和恩惠。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因为那进入安息的，乃是歇了自己的工，正如神歇了他的工一样。」（4:10）我很难真正休息。就算放假了，心也停不下来：工作群里的消息要回，孩子的成绩要盯，父母的身体要顾，教会的事工也要我安排。坐下来什么都不做，我会有罪恶感；好像我一停下来，一切就会垮掉。在一个人人都怕落后的社会里，休息好像是一种奢侈。可是神在第七日就「歇了他一切的工」（创2:2），不是因为他累了，而是因为工作已经完成了。耶稣在十字架上说：「成了！」（约19:30）他也在洗净了人的罪以后，「坐在高天至大者的右边」（来1:3）。我最深的不安，也许不是事情太多，而是我以为一切都要靠我撑着：我的价值要靠我的成绩来证明，我在神面前的地位要靠我的服事来维持。无论第10节还包括什么，它至少告诉我：这正是我需要「歇了自己的工」的地方。托住万有的，是他，不是我（1:3）；救我的，是他已经完成的工作，不是我每天做了多少。我在神面前的平安，也不是我挣来的：「我们既因信称义，就藉着我们的主耶稣基督得与神相和。」（罗5:1）如果我还在靠自己的行为想要赚得神的接纳，这位大祭司今天就邀请我停下来，来到施恩的宝座前，承认我做不到，信靠他已经为我做成的。耶稣说：「凡劳苦担重担的人可以到我这里来，我就使你们得安息。」（太11:28）这个星期，我也可以学习在主日真正停下来，把那些放不下的事交在他手里。我的休息，不是向世界认输，而是承认神才是神。他歇了他的工，又邀请我进入他的安息；有一天，那「为神的子民存留」的安息（来4:9），要完完全全地成为我的。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 has three parts. **Entering God's rest** (vv. 1–10): the promise of entering his rest still stands; Israel heard the good news but did not enter because of unbelief; God rested after creation, and later spoke of "today" through David, which shows that Joshua had not given them the final rest; so there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God. **The word of God is living** (vv. 11–13): let us strive to enter that rest; the word of God is sharper than any two-edged sword, discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart, and all are naked and exposed to his eyes. **The throne of grace** (vv. 14–16): we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus, the Son of God; he is able to sympathize with our weaknesses and was tempted in every respect, yet without sin; so we may draw near to the throne of grace with confidence.

The chapter begins with a stern warning and ends with a warm invitation. God's word lays open our hearts like a sword (vv. 12–13), but standing before God is not only ourselves, naked and exposed; there is also a high priest who has passed through the heavens for us (vv. 14–16).

## Going Deeper: 4:1–5 The Promise of Rest Still Stands

**1. "Let us fear."** "Therefore, while the promise of entering his rest still stands, let us fear lest any of you should seem to have failed to reach it" (4:1).
- **The promise remains:** the wilderness generation did not enter, but God's promise was not cancelled.
- **"Fear":** this is not doubting God's faithfulness; it is taking seriously the consequence of unbelief: fear that we might make light of the promise and fall under judgment, as Israel did.

**2. "Good news came to us just as to them."** "For good news came to us just as to them, but the message they heard did not benefit them, because they were not united by faith with those who listened" (4:2).
- **There was good news in the wilderness too:** what Israel heard in the wilderness was the good news that God would bring them into the promised land. What we hear is the good news that God will bring us into his eternal rest.
- **"Not united by faith":** many of the earliest manuscripts read this way, as the ESV does; many take it to mean that they did not join in faith with those, like Caleb and Joshua, who truly believed. Other manuscripts read "the message was not mixed with faith in those who heard it," which the Chinese Union Version follows. On either reading the point is the same: hearing is not benefiting; the word must be received by faith.

**3. "We who have believed enter that rest."** "For we who have believed enter that rest... although his works were finished from the foundation of the world. For he has somewhere spoken of the seventh day in this way: 'And God rested on the seventh day from all his works'" (4:3–4).
- **"Enter":** the Greek is present tense, and many interpreters take it to mean that believers already begin to enter this rest now and will enter it fully later.
- **God's own rest:** the author links "my rest" in Psalm 95 with God's rest on the seventh day in Genesis 2:2. When God finished creating, he "rested... from all his works," not because he was tired but because the work was complete. This rest has been ready since creation, waiting for people to enter it.

> The point: God's rest has been ready since the foundation of the world. Hearing the good news is not the same as entering the rest; all who receive God's promise by faith begin to enter it now.

## Going Deeper: 4:6–10 There Remains a Sabbath Rest

**1. "If Joshua had given them rest."** "Again he appoints a certain day, 'Today,' saying through David so long afterward, in the words already quoted, 'Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts.' For if Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken of another day later on" (4:7–8).
- **The author's argument:** Joshua brought Israel into Canaan, and Scripture says, "The LORD gave them rest on every side" (Josh 21:44). Yet centuries later God spoke through David of "today," still inviting people into his rest. So the rest of Canaan was not the final rest.
- **"Joshua":** in Greek, "Joshua" and "Jesus" are the same name. The rest the Joshua of the Old Testament could not give, the Jesus of the New Testament gives.

**2. "There remains a Sabbath rest."** "So then, there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God, for whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works as God did from his" (4:9–10).
- **"Sabbath rest":** the Greek word is very rare; it means a rest like the keeping of a Sabbath. The author uses it for the rest God's people will finally enter.
- **"Rested from his works":** believers have understood this in different ways. Some take it to mean no longer relying on our own works to be saved, and resting in Christ's finished work (see Eph 2:8–9); Calvin links it with self-denial, ceasing from our own fleshly works so that God may work in us. Many interpreters take it mainly to mean that believers will one day rest from their earthly labors and enter eternal rest (see Rev 14:13). Others (such as John Owen) take verse 10 to speak of Christ himself, who finished the work of redemption and entered his rest. All of these point to one thing: our rest rests on the finished work of God and of Christ, not on our effort.
- **The Lord's Day:** believers also differ on what this passage means for keeping the Sabbath or the Lord's Day. Traditions such as the *Westminster Confession* (21.7–8) hold that the Sabbath principle continues in the Lord's Day, so believers should set that day apart for worship and rest; the *Heidelberg Catechism* (Q. 103) also stresses diligent attendance on the Lord's Day to hear God's word; many other evangelicals hold that the Sabbath is "a shadow of the things to come" fulfilled in Christ (Col 2:16–17), so believers have freedom about observing days (see Rom 14:5, though whether that verse includes the weekly Sabbath is itself debated). Both agree that Lord's Day worship and rest are a gracious gift from God, and that our final rest is Christ himself.

> The point: Canaan was not the final rest. The rest Joshua could not give, Jesus gives. Those who believe may rest from their own works, resting in the salvation Christ has finished, and look forward to the full rest to come.

## Going Deeper: 4:11–13 The Word of God Is Living

**1. "Let us therefore strive to enter that rest."** "Let us therefore strive to enter that rest, so that no one may fall by the same sort of disobedience" (4:11).
- **Striving to rest:** this sounds like a contradiction. But what the author wants us to strive for is not earning rest by our own effort; it is making every effort not to let anything keep us from trusting God. Fighting unbelief takes seriousness.

**2. "Sharper than any two-edged sword."** "For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit, of joints and of marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart" (4:12).
- **"Living and active":** God's word is not dead letters but living and powerful. Chapter 3 says "the Holy Spirit says," and here God's word pierces the heart; both show that God still speaks through Scripture today.
- **"Soul and spirit, joints and marrow":** some use this verse to support the view that a person has three parts, spirit, soul and body (trichotomy); others hold that a person has two parts, soul and body, with "soul" and "spirit" often two ways of speaking of the same thing (dichotomy). Many interpreters think the author is not describing human makeup here, but using a few paired phrases to stress that God's word reaches the deepest, most hidden places in us.
- **"The thoughts and intentions of the heart":** we can deceive others and even ourselves (3:13), but we cannot deceive God's word.

**3. "Naked and exposed."** "And no creature is hidden from his sight, but all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account" (4:13).
- **Nowhere to hide:** after Adam sinned he hid among the trees, yet God still saw him (Gen 3:8). Before God, all our pretending is useless.
- **"To whom we must give account":** one day we will stand before God and give an account.

> The point: God's word is living and pierces to the deepest places of the heart. Before God there is nowhere to hide. This is a sober warning, and it prepares us for the comfort of the next passage.

## Going Deeper: 4:14–16 The Throne of Grace

**1. "A great high priest who has passed through the heavens."** "Since then we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus, the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession" (4:14).
- **"Passed through the heavens":** the Old Testament high priest entered the Most Holy Place once a year (Lev 16); Jesus "passed through the heavens" into God's own presence (9:24).
- **"Let us hold fast":** the readers were tempted to let go of the faith they had confessed. The author says: with such a high priest, how can we let go?

**2. "Not... unable to sympathize with our weaknesses."** "For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin" (4:15).
- **"Sympathize":** he does not pity us from the outside; he truly feels with us. He knows what it is to be hungry and tired, to be misunderstood and betrayed by a friend.
- **"In every respect has been tempted as we are":** the temptations he faced were real, not play-acting.
- **"Yet without sin":** he never gave in to temptation. Theologians differ on one question: could Jesus have sinned? Some (such as W. G. T. Shedd) hold that, being the Son of God, he could not sin, yet his temptations were still real; others (such as Charles Hodge) hold that if the temptations were real, then as a man he could have sinned, though he never did. Most theologians in church history, including most Reformed theologians, have held the first view. Both views also agree that Jesus had no fallen nature: his temptations did not come from inner evil desire (see Jas 1:14) but from outside; and that Jesus was wholly without sin and his temptations were real.
- **Sinlessness makes him more able to sympathize:** someone who gives in to temptation never learns how heavy it would have become; Jesus never gave in, so he bore the full weight of temptation.

**3. "Draw near to the throne of grace with confidence."** "Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace, that we may receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need" (4:16).
- **"The throne of grace":** a throne is the place of judgment, but because our high priest is seated at its right hand and always lives to intercede for us (8:1; 7:25), it has become the place of grace. The Old Testament "mercy seat" was in the Most Holy Place, and only the high priest could approach it, once a year (Lev 16:2, 34; Heb 9:7); now all who trust Jesus may come at any time.
- **"With confidence":** the word means boldness, freedom of speech. We need not stand trembling outside the door like prisoners.
- **"Mercy" and "grace":** mercy for our failures, grace for our needs.
- **"Help in time of need":** God's help may not come early, but it is never late.

> The point: Before God we are naked and exposed, but we do not stand there alone. Our high priest sympathizes with our weaknesses, so we may come with confidence to the throne of grace and receive mercy and grace when we need them.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works as God did from his" (4:10). I find it hard to truly rest. Even on holiday my mind will not stop: there are work-group messages to answer, my children's grades to watch, my parents' health to look after, and church ministries waiting for me to organize. If I sit down and do nothing, I feel guilty, as if everything will collapse the moment I stop. In a society where everyone is afraid of falling behind, rest feels like a luxury. Yet on the seventh day God "rested... from all his work" (Gen 2:2), not because he was tired but because the work was finished. On the cross Jesus said, "It is finished" (John 19:30). And after making purification for sins, he "sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high" (Heb 1:3). Perhaps my deepest unrest is not that I have too much to do, but that I think everything depends on me holding it up: my worth must be proved by my results, and my standing before God must be kept up by my service. Whatever else verse 10 includes, it at least tells me that this is exactly where I need to rest from my works. It is he who upholds the universe, not I (1:3); what saves me is the work he has finished, not how much I get done each day. My peace with God is not something I earned: "Since we have been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ" (Rom 5:1). If I am still trying to earn God's acceptance by my own works, this high priest invites me today to stop, come to the throne of grace, admit that I cannot do it, and trust what he has already done for me. Jesus said, "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matt 11:28). This week I can also learn to truly stop on the Lord's Day, and put into his hands the things I cannot let go. My rest is not giving up to the world; it is confessing that God is God. He rested from his work and invites me into his rest; and one day the rest that "remains... for the people of God" (Heb 4:9) will be wholly mine.
