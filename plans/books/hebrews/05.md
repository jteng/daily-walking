## 本章脉络

第五章分成两段。**大祭司的资格**（1-10节）：凡从人间挑选的大祭司，是替人办理属神的事，能体谅愚蒙和失迷的人，又要蒙神所召，像亚伦一样；基督也不是自取荣耀，乃是神对他说「你是我的儿子」，又说「你是照着麦基洗德的等次永远为祭司」；他在肉体的时候大声哀哭、流泪祷告，因所受的苦难学了顺从，既得以完全，就为凡顺从他的人成了永远得救的根源。**不要作吃奶的**（11-14节）：论到麦基洗德，作者有好些话要说，但读者听不进去；他们本该作师傅，却还要人再教他们神圣言小学的开端；只能吃奶的是婴孩，长大成人的才能吃干粮。

作者在4:14-16刚刚讲到大祭司能体恤我们的软弱，这一章就解释什么样的人可以作大祭司，为什么耶稣完全合格。讲到一半，他停下来，因为他担心读者听不进去更深的道理（11节）。这段劝勉一直延续到第六章结束，第七章才回到麦基洗德。

## 深读 5:1-4 从人间挑选的大祭司

**1. 「替人办理属神的事」。** 「凡从人间挑选的大祭司，是奉派替人办理属神的事，为要献上礼物和赎罪祭。」（5:1）
- **「从人间挑选」：** 大祭司必须是人，才能代表人。这就是为什么儿子要「成了血肉之体」（2:14、17）。
- **「替人」：** 大祭司站在神和人中间，代表百姓到神面前，为他们献祭赎罪。

**2. 「能体谅那愚蒙的和失迷的人」。** 「他能体谅那愚蒙的和失迷的人，因为他自己也是被软弱所困。故此，他理当为百姓和自己献祭赎罪。」（5:2-3）
- **「体谅」：** 这个词的意思是用适度、温和的态度对待人，既不轻看罪，也不严厉地压碎人。
- **「愚蒙的和失迷的人」：** 旧约的献祭主要是为了无知的罪和误犯的罪（利4:2；民15:27-31）。大祭司要温和地对待那些软弱跌倒的人。
- **「他自己也是被软弱所困」：** 亚伦和他的子孙自己也是罪人。在赎罪日，大祭司要先「为自己和本家赎罪」（利16:6），然后才为百姓赎罪。耶稣在这一点上和他们不同：他「没有犯罪」（4:15），不需要为自己献祭（7:27）；但他也「被试探而受苦」（2:18），所以同样能温和地对待软弱的人。

**3. 「这大祭司的尊荣，没有人自取」。** 「这大祭司的尊荣，没有人自取。惟要蒙神所召，像亚伦一样。」（5:4）
- **神所召：** 亚伦作大祭司，是神拣选的（出28:1）。可拉一党想要自取祭司的职分，就受了审判（民16章）；乌西雅王擅自进殿烧香，也受了责罚（代下26:16-21）。
- **服事不是自己争来的：** 在神的事上，人不能靠自己的野心或能力，抢一个位置来坐。

> 要点：大祭司必须从人间挑选，才能代表人；他要温和地对待软弱跌倒的人，又必须是神所召的。旧约的大祭司自己也软弱，需要为自己献祭；我们的大祭司却没有罪，又一样体恤我们。

## 深读 5:5-6 基督不是自取荣耀

**1. 「乃是在乎向他说……的那一位」。** 「如此，基督也不是自取荣耀作大祭司，乃是在乎向他说『你是我的儿子，我今日生你』的那一位；就如经上又有一处说：『你是照着麦基洗德的等次永远为祭司。』」（5:5-6）
- **两处诗篇：** 第一处是诗篇2:7，1:5已经引过；第二处是诗篇110:4。作者把这两处连在一起：那位称他为儿子的，也立他为祭司。
- **耶稣也没有自取：** 他「不以自己与神同等为强夺的」（腓2:6），而是等候父来立他。

**2. 「照着麦基洗德的等次」。**
- **麦基洗德是谁：** 他是撒冷王，又是「至高神的祭司」，在亚伯兰打败诸王回来时，带着饼和酒出来迎接他，为他祝福；亚伯兰把所得的十分之一给了他（创14:18-20）。
- **新的祭司职分：** 旧约的祭司都出于利未支派、亚伦的家族；耶稣却出于犹大支派（7:14）。诗篇110:4预言，将来要有一位祭司，不是照着亚伦的等次，而是照着麦基洗德的等次，并且「永远为祭司」。第七章会详细讲这一点。
- **君王又是祭司：** 诗篇110篇的第1节讲王坐在神的右边（1:13），第4节讲他永远为祭司。在耶稣身上，君王和祭司合而为一。

> 要点：耶稣作大祭司，不是自己抢来的，而是父所立的。他是坐在宝座上的王，又是照着麦基洗德的等次永远为祭司的那一位。

## 深读 5:7-10 因所受的苦难学了顺从

**1. 「大声哀哭，流泪祷告」。** 「基督在肉体的时候，既大声哀哭，流泪祷告，恳求那能救他免死的主，就因他的虔诚蒙了应允。」（5:7）
- **「在肉体的时候」：** 指耶稣在地上的日子。许多解经家认为，这特别是指客西马尼园，耶稣「俯伏在地，祷告说：『我父啊，倘若可行，求你叫这杯离开我。然而，不要照我的意思，只要照你的意思。』」（太26:39）
- **真实的人：** 神的儿子祷告时会哭，会大声呼求。这是大祭司能体恤我们的原因之一。
- **「蒙了应允」：** 耶稣还是死了，怎么说是「蒙了应允」呢？解经家有几种看法：许多人认为，原文的「免死」也可以理解为从死里救出来，神没有让他不死，却叫他从死里复活了（参徒2:24）；也有人认为，神应允的是他「只要照你的意思」的祷告，神的旨意成全了；还有人认为，神赐他力量去面对死亡。无论哪一种，都显出父没有离弃他所爱的儿子。
- **「因他的虔诚」：** 这个词的意思是敬畏、顺服神的心。

**2. 「他虽然为儿子，还是因所受的苦难学了顺从」。** 「他虽然为儿子，还是因所受的苦难学了顺从。」（5:8）
- **不是从不顺从变成顺从：** 耶稣从来没有不顺从过。许多解经家认为，「学了顺从」是说他在受苦中亲身经历了顺从是什么：在每一个新的处境中，在越来越重的苦难中，一直顺服到底，「以至于死，且死在十字架上」（腓2:8）。
- **「虽然为儿子」：** 作者特别说明，他是儿子，本来不需要学什么；他学了顺从，是因为他为我们取了人的样式和奴仆的形象（腓2:7）。
- **子的顺服和父子的同等：** 子在成为人、完成救恩的使命中顺服父，并不表示子在本质上比父低。父和子同是神，同样尊荣（1:3、8）；子甘心顺服，是出于爱，是三位一体的神为拯救我们所定的计划。

**3. 「为凡顺从他的人成了永远得救的根源」。** 「他既得以完全，就为凡顺从他的人成了永远得救的根源，并蒙神照着麦基洗德的等次称他为大祭司。」（5:9-10）
- **「得以完全」：** 和2:10一样，不是说他在道德上有缺欠，而是说他经过受苦，完成了作救主所需的一切。
- **「永远得救的根源」：** 旧约的祭司献的祭要年年重复；耶稣所成就的救恩却是「永远」的。
- **「凡顺从他的人」：** 这不是说人要靠顺从来赚得救恩。在新约里，信靠基督本身就是顺从（罗1:5「信服真道」）；真信心也必然带来顺服的生命。耶稣顺从父，成了救恩的根源；我们顺从耶稣，就是领受这救恩。

> 要点：耶稣在受苦中顺服到底。他流泪祷告，父垂听了他，又叫他从死里复活；他成了凡信靠他、顺从他的人永远得救的根源。

## 深读 5:11-14 吃奶的和吃干粮的

**1. 「因为你们听不进去」。** 「论到麦基洗德，我们有好些话，并且难以解明，因为你们听不进去。」（5:11）
- **问题不在道理太难：** 作者说的不是这道理太深，而是「你们听不进去」。原文的意思是迟钝、懒散，6:12的「懈怠」也是这个词。
- **「听不进去」：** 这正是第三、四章所警告的：听见了，却没有用信心接受。

**2. 「本该作师傅」。** 「看你们学习的工夫，本该作师傅，谁知还得有人将神圣言小学的开端另教导你们，并且成了那必须吃奶、不能吃干粮的人。」（5:12）
- **「本该作师傅」：** 这不是说每个人都要在教会担任教导的职分（参雅3:1），而是说信主这么久，本该能够把信仰讲给别人听，帮助别人了。
- **「小学的开端」：** 指最基本的道理，6:1-2列出了一些，例如悔改、信靠神、洗礼、复活和审判。
- **奶和干粮：** 保罗也用过同样的比喻（林前3:1-2）。奶本身不是坏的，新生的婴孩就该「爱慕那纯净的灵奶」（彼前2:2）；但一个人一直只能吃奶，就不正常了。

**3. 「心窍习练得通达」。** 「凡只能吃奶的都不熟练仁义的道理，因为他是婴孩；惟独长大成人的才能吃干粮；他们的心窍习练得通达，就能分辨好歹了。」（5:13-14）
- **「不熟练仁义的道理」：** 这个说法可以理解为不熟悉讲论公义的道，也就是没有经验去明白和运用神的话。
- **「习练」：** 原文和体操、锻炼是同一个词根。分辨的能力不是一天就有的，而是在生活中一次又一次地运用神的话，慢慢锻炼出来的。
- **「分辨好歹」：** 成熟的标志，不是知道多少知识，而是能在复杂的处境中分辨什么是对的，什么是错的。
- **干粮是什么：** 在上下文中，作者要给读者的「干粮」，就是基督照着麦基洗德等次作大祭司的道理（第7-10章）。成熟不是离开基督去学别的，而是更深地认识基督。

> 要点：信主多年却还只能吃奶，问题常常不在道理太难，而在我们不肯认真去听。成熟就是更深地认识基督，又在生活中一次次运用神的话，学会分辨好歹。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他能体谅那愚蒙的和失迷的人。」（5:2）「看你们学习的工夫，本该作师傅。」（5:12）读到第12节，我有点脸红。我信主已经很多年了，可是孩子问我「为什么神允许坏事发生」，我答不上来；同事问我「你们基督徒到底信什么」，我只会说「信耶稣就能上天堂」；小组里讨论一段经文，我总是等别人先说。我的信仰好像一直停在主日学的程度，讲道听了很多，却很少真正消化。有时候我会想：像我这样的人，还算是基督徒吗？可是希伯来书的作者在责备读者以前，先讲了大祭司是怎样的一位：他「能体谅那愚蒙的和失迷的人」（5:2）。这位大祭司知道我的迟钝和懒散，也知道我为什么总是停在原地。他没有因为我长得慢就放弃我；他自己就是我「永远得救的根源」（5:9），不是我学得多好。我也要承认，我的停滞有时候不是因为能力不够，而是因为我不想付代价，宁可让别人替我读经、替我思想，这是需要悔改的。但作者要给我的「干粮」，不是一套更难的学问，而是基督自己：他是照着麦基洗德的等次永远为祭司的那一位。成长不是靠我一个人咬紧牙关，而是更深地认识他。也许我可以从一件很小的事开始：这个星期，把下一章经文慢慢读两遍，写下我从中看见的基督，再找一位弟兄姊妹一起讨论。慢慢地，我的「心窍」会被他「习练得通达」（5:14）。他是那位温柔的大祭司，他会耐心地带我长大，直到有一天，我也能把他讲给别人听。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 has two parts. **The qualifications of a high priest** (vv. 1–10): every high priest chosen from among men acts on their behalf before God, deals gently with the ignorant and wayward, and must be called by God, as Aaron was; Christ too did not exalt himself, but was appointed by the one who said, "You are my Son," and "You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek"; in the days of his flesh he offered up prayers with loud cries and tears, learned obedience through what he suffered, and being made perfect, became the source of eternal salvation to all who obey him. **Do not stay on milk** (vv. 11–14): about Melchizedek the author has much to say, but the readers have become dull of hearing; though they ought to be teachers, they need someone to teach them again the basic principles of the oracles of God; one who lives on milk is a child, and solid food is for the mature.

In 4:14–16 the author has just said that our high priest sympathizes with our weaknesses; now he explains who may be a high priest and why Jesus is fully qualified. Halfway through he stops, because he fears his readers will not take in deeper teaching (v. 11). This exhortation runs to the end of chapter 6, and only in chapter 7 does he return to Melchizedek.

## Going Deeper: 5:1–4 A High Priest Chosen from Among Men

**1. "To act on behalf of men in relation to God."** "For every high priest chosen from among men is appointed to act on behalf of men in relation to God, to offer gifts and sacrifices for sins" (5:1).
- **"Chosen from among men":** a high priest had to be human in order to represent humans. That is why the Son "partook of" flesh and blood (2:14, 17).
- **"On behalf of men":** the high priest stands between God and the people, representing them before God and offering sacrifices for their sins.

**2. "He can deal gently with the ignorant and wayward."** "He can deal gently with the ignorant and wayward, since he himself is beset with weakness. Because of this he is obligated to offer sacrifice for his own sins just as he does for those of the people" (5:2–3).
- **"Deal gently":** the word means to treat people with measured, gentle feeling, neither making light of sin nor crushing the sinner.
- **"The ignorant and wayward":** the Old Testament sacrifices were mainly for sins done in ignorance or by straying (Lev 4:2; Num 15:27–31). The high priest was to deal gently with those who stumbled in weakness.
- **"He himself is beset with weakness":** Aaron and his sons were sinners too. On the Day of Atonement the high priest first had to "make atonement for himself and for his house" (Lev 16:6), and only then for the people. Here Jesus differs from them: he was "without sin" (4:15) and needs no sacrifice for himself (7:27); yet he too "suffered when tempted" (2:18), so he too can deal gently with the weak.

**3. "No one takes this honor for himself."** "And no one takes this honor for himself, but only when called by God, just as Aaron was" (5:4).
- **Called by God:** Aaron became high priest because God chose him (Exod 28:1). Korah and his company tried to seize the priesthood for themselves and were judged (Num 16); King Uzziah entered the temple to burn incense on his own authority and was punished (2 Chr 26:16–21).
- **Service is not something we grab:** in the things of God, no one can seize a position by ambition or ability.

> The point: A high priest had to be chosen from among men to represent them; he was to deal gently with those who stumbled in weakness, and he had to be called by God. The Old Testament high priests were weak themselves and needed to offer sacrifice for their own sins; our high priest is without sin, and sympathizes with us just the same.

## Going Deeper: 5:5–6 Christ Did Not Exalt Himself

**1. "Appointed by him who said to him..."** "So also Christ did not exalt himself to be made a high priest, but was appointed by him who said to him, 'You are my Son, today I have begotten you'; as he says also in another place, 'You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek'" (5:5–6).
- **Two psalms:** the first is Psalm 2:7, already quoted in 1:5; the second is Psalm 110:4. The author joins them: the one who called him Son also appointed him priest.
- **Jesus did not seize it either:** he "did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped" (Phil 2:6), but waited for the Father to appoint him.

**2. "After the order of Melchizedek."**
- **Who was Melchizedek?** He was king of Salem and "priest of God Most High." When Abram returned from defeating the kings, Melchizedek brought out bread and wine and blessed him, and Abram gave him a tenth of everything (Gen 14:18–20).
- **A new priesthood:** all the Old Testament priests came from the tribe of Levi and the family of Aaron; but Jesus came from the tribe of Judah (7:14). Psalm 110:4 foretold a priest who would be not after the order of Aaron but after the order of Melchizedek, and a priest "forever." Chapter 7 will explain this in detail.
- **King and priest:** Psalm 110:1 speaks of the king seated at God's right hand (1:13), and 110:4 of a priest forever. In Jesus, king and priest are one.

> The point: Jesus did not seize the high priesthood; the Father appointed him. He is the King seated on the throne and the priest forever after the order of Melchizedek.

## Going Deeper: 5:7–10 He Learned Obedience Through What He Suffered

**1. "With loud cries and tears."** "In the days of his flesh, Jesus offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to him who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverence" (5:7).
- **"In the days of his flesh":** this means Jesus' days on earth. Many interpreters think of Gethsemane in particular, where Jesus "fell on his face and prayed, saying, 'My Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as you will'" (Matt 26:39).
- **A real man:** the Son of God wept when he prayed and cried out aloud. This is one reason our high priest can sympathize with us.
- **"He was heard":** Jesus still died, so how was he "heard"? Interpreters hold several views. Many note that "save him from death" can also mean "save him out of death": God did not keep him from dying, but raised him from the dead (see Acts 2:24). Others think God answered his prayer "not as I will, but as you will," and the Father's will was done. Still others think God gave him strength to face death. On any view, the Father did not abandon his beloved Son.
- **"Because of his reverence":** the word means a heart that fears and submits to God.

**2. "Although he was a son, he learned obedience."** "Although he was a son, he learned obedience through what he suffered" (5:8).
- **Not from disobedience to obedience:** Jesus was never disobedient. Many interpreters take "learned obedience" to mean that in suffering he came to know by experience what obedience is: in each new situation, under ever heavier suffering, he obeyed to the end, "to the point of death, even death on a cross" (Phil 2:8).
- **"Although he was a son":** the author stresses that, as the Son, he had nothing he needed to learn; he learned obedience because for our sake he took "the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men" (Phil 2:7).
- **The Son's obedience and the equality of Father and Son:** the Son's obedience to the Father, as he became man and carried out the work of salvation, does not mean that the Son is lesser than the Father in his being. Father and Son are both God and share the same honor (1:3, 8); the Son's willing obedience comes from love, in the plan of the triune God to save us.

**3. "The source of eternal salvation to all who obey him."** "And being made perfect, he became the source of eternal salvation to all who obey him, being designated by God a high priest after the order of Melchizedek" (5:9–10).
- **"Being made perfect":** as in 2:10, this does not mean he was morally lacking, but that through suffering he completed all that a Savior must do.
- **"Eternal salvation":** the sacrifices of the Old Testament priests had to be repeated year after year; the salvation Jesus accomplished is "eternal."
- **"All who obey him":** this does not mean we earn salvation by obedience. In the New Testament, trusting Christ is itself obedience (Rom 1:5, "the obedience of faith"), and true faith always brings a life of obedience. Jesus obeyed the Father and became the source of salvation; we obey Jesus, and so receive that salvation.

> The point: Jesus obeyed to the end through suffering. He prayed with tears, the Father heard him and raised him from the dead, and he became the source of eternal salvation for all who trust and obey him.

## Going Deeper: 5:11–14 Milk and Solid Food

**1. "Since you have become dull of hearing."** "About this we have much to say, and it is hard to explain, since you have become dull of hearing" (5:11).
- **The problem is not that the teaching is too hard:** the author does not say "this is too deep," but "you have become dull of hearing." The word means sluggish or lazy; it is the same word translated "sluggish" in 6:12.
- **"Dull of hearing":** this is exactly what chapters 3–4 warned against: hearing without receiving by faith.

**2. "You ought to be teachers."** "For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the basic principles of the oracles of God. You need milk, not solid food" (5:12).
- **"You ought to be teachers":** this does not mean everyone should hold a teaching office in the church (see Jas 3:1), but that after so long in the faith they should be able to explain it to others and help them.
- **"The basic principles":** the most elementary teachings; 6:1–2 lists some of them, such as repentance, faith toward God, washings, resurrection and judgment.
- **Milk and solid food:** Paul uses the same picture (1 Cor 3:1–2). Milk itself is not bad; newborn infants should "long for the pure spiritual milk" (1 Pet 2:2). But someone who can only ever take milk is not well.

**3. "Powers of discernment trained by constant practice."** "For everyone who lives on milk is unskilled in the word of righteousness, since he is a child. But solid food is for the mature, for those who have their powers of discernment trained by constant practice to distinguish good from evil" (5:13–14).
- **"Unskilled in the word of righteousness":** this can be taken to mean inexperienced in teaching about righteousness, that is, without practice in understanding and using God's word.
- **"Trained":** the Greek word shares a root with "gymnastics" and "exercise." Discernment does not come in a day; it is built up slowly by using God's word again and again in real life.
- **"To distinguish good from evil":** the mark of maturity is not how much one knows, but being able to tell right from wrong in complicated situations.
- **What is the solid food?** In context, the "solid food" the author wants to give his readers is the teaching that Christ is high priest after the order of Melchizedek (chapters 7–10). Maturity is not moving on from Christ to something else, but knowing Christ more deeply.

> The point: When we have believed for years yet still live on milk, the problem is usually not that the teaching is too hard but that we will not listen in earnest. Maturity is knowing Christ more deeply, and learning to tell good from evil by using God's word again and again in life.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "He can deal gently with the ignorant and wayward" (5:2). "For though by this time you ought to be teachers..." (5:12). When I read verse 12, I blush a little. I have been a believer for many years, yet when my child asks, "Why does God let bad things happen?" I cannot answer; when a coworker asks, "What do you Christians actually believe?" all I can say is, "Believe in Jesus and you go to heaven"; when my small group discusses a passage, I always wait for others to speak first. My faith seems stuck at the Sunday-school level; I have heard many sermons but truly digested few. Sometimes I wonder: is someone like me even a Christian? Yet before the author of Hebrews rebukes his readers, he first describes what kind of high priest we have: he "can deal gently with the ignorant and wayward" (5:2). This high priest knows how dull and sluggish I am, and why I keep standing still. He has not given up on me because I grow slowly; he himself is "the source of eternal salvation" for me (5:9), not how well I have learned. I must also admit that my standing still is sometimes not for lack of ability but because I do not want to pay the cost; I would rather let others read the Bible and think for me, and that calls for repentance. But the "solid food" the author wants to give me is not a harder body of knowledge; it is Christ himself, the priest forever after the order of Melchizedek. Growth does not come from gritting my teeth alone; it comes from knowing him more deeply. Perhaps I can begin with something small: this week, read the next chapter slowly twice, write down what I see of Christ in it, and find a brother or sister to talk it over with. Little by little, my "powers of discernment" will be "trained by constant practice" (5:14) under his hand. He is the gentle high priest; he will patiently help me grow, until one day I too can tell others about him.
