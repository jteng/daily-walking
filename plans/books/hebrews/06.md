## 本章脉络

第六章接着5:11-14的责备，分成四段。**竭力进到完全的地步**（1-3节）：我们应当离开基督道理的开端，不必再立根基。**离弃道理的危险**（4-8节）：那些已经蒙了光照、尝过天恩滋味的人，若是离弃道理，就不能叫他们重新懊悔了；吃过雨水的田地若长荆棘和蒺藜，结局就是焚烧。**深信你们有更美的事**（9-12节）：神不会忘记读者伺候圣徒的爱心；作者愿他们殷勤到底，效法那些凭信心和忍耐承受应许的人。**神指着自己起誓**（13-20节）：亚伯拉罕恒久忍耐，就得了所应许的；神藉着应许和誓言这两件不更改的事，叫我们这逃往避难所的人大得勉励；这指望如同灵魂的锚，通入幔内，作先锋的耶稣已经为我们进去了。

作者说完最严厉的警告，紧接着就说「我们虽是这样说，却深信你们的行为强过这些」（9节）；最后他把读者的眼睛带到幔内的耶稣那里，又接上5:10暂时放下的麦基洗德的话题。

## 深读 6:1-3 离开基督道理的开端

**1. 「竭力进到完全的地步」。** 「所以，我们应当离开基督道理的开端，竭力进到完全的地步。」（6:1）
- **不是丢掉根基：** 「离开」不是说这些道理不重要，而是说不要一直停在打根基的阶段。根基打好了，房子就要往上建造。
- **「完全」：** 这个词和5:14「长大成人的」是同一个词根。许多解经家认为，作者说的成熟，就是更深地认识基督照着麦基洗德的等次作大祭司（7-10章）。

**2. 六样根基的道理。** 「不必再立根基，就如那懊悔死行、信靠神、各样洗礼、按手之礼、死人复活，以及永远审判各等教训。」（6:1-2）
- **三对道理：** 许多人把这六样看成三对：悔改和信靠神，讲人怎样归向神；洗礼和按手，讲入门的礼仪；复活和审判，讲末后的事。
- **犹太人也熟悉：** 这些道理在旧约和犹太人的信仰里几乎都能找到。许多解经家认为，读者若只停在和犹太会堂共有的根基上，就可能看不出基督有什么不同，最后退回前约的礼仪里去。

**3. 「神若许我们，我们必如此行」。** 「神若许我们，我们必如此行。」（6:3）
- **成长也在神手中：** 作者要读者竭力往前，又承认往前走要靠神许可；连成熟也是恩典。

> 要点：成熟不是离开根基，而是在根基上往上建造；也不是离开基督去学别的，而是更深地认识他。往前走要竭力，也要靠神许可。

## 深读 6:4-8 若是离弃道理

**1. 五个描述。** 「论到那些已经蒙了光照、尝过天恩的滋味、又于圣灵有分，并尝过神善道的滋味、觉悟来世权能的人，若是离弃道理，就不能叫他们重新懊悔了。因为他们把神的儿子重钉十字架，明明地羞辱他。」（6:4-6）
- **「蒙了光照」：** 作者在10:32说读者「蒙了光照以后」忍受了大争战，用的是同一个说法。
- **「尝过」「有分」：** 许多人认为「尝」是真实的经历，正如耶稣「为人人尝了死味」（2:9）；也有人认为只是浅浅地沾一点。「有分」和3:14「在基督里有分」原文是同一个词。
- **「若是离弃道理」：** 这不是一时软弱跌倒，而是公开、彻底地背弃基督。许多人认为，「把神的儿子重钉十字架」就是站到当年钉耶稣的那些人一边，公开宣告耶稣该死。

**2. 「就不能叫他们重新懊悔了」。**
- **为什么不能：** 不是基督的血不够，而是这样的人弃绝了唯一能叫人悔改、蒙赦免的那一位。离开了他，「赎罪的祭就再没有了」（10:26）。
- **「因为」还是「当……的时候」：** 6节后半句，和合本和多数译本理解为原因；也有人理解为：当他们还在钉他、羞辱他的时候，就不能叫他们重新懊悔。不过许多人认为，这样理解只是说了一句不言自明的话。

**3. 吃过雨水的田地。** 「就如一块田地，吃过屡次下的雨水，生长菜蔬，合乎耕种的人用，就从神得福；若长荆棘和蒺藜，必被废弃，近于咒诅，结局就是焚烧。」（6:7-8）
- **同样的雨，不同的出产：** 雨是神的恩典，两块地都吃过，出产却不同。领受的恩典越多，责任也越重。「荆棘和蒺藜」让人想起始祖犯罪以后，地「受咒诅」的情景（创3:17-18）。

**4. 四种主要的理解。** 这是新约中争论最多的经文之一。下面四种看法，每一种都有严谨的解经家支持：
- **真信徒可能失落（阿米念传统）：** 这五个描述最自然的读法是指真信徒；真信徒若离弃基督，就会失去救恩，不能再回来。持这种看法的有马歇尔、麦克奈特，以及卫斯理传统的柯克里尔等。这种看法要面对的难题是：怎样与「谁也不能从我手里把他们夺去」（约10:28）这类应许相协调。
- **假设的警告：** 作者是在说一件实际上不会发生的事：假如真信徒离弃基督，就再也回不来了，所以不要往那条路上走。持这种看法的有休伊特、肯特等。批评者认为，作者显然把这当作真实的危险（3:12；10:26-27），而且原文「离弃」和前面的描述一样是分词，并不是假设的条件句。
- **看起来信了，其实没有重生（改革宗的一种看法）：** 这些人在教会中真实地经历了圣灵的许多工作，却没有重生，像撒在石头地上的种子，「立刻欢喜领受」，却「心里没有根」（可4:16-17）。加尔文、欧文，以及今天的古德恩持这一类看法。他们指出，两块地吃一样的雨，出产却不同。批评者认为，作者在10:32也用「蒙了光照」来说读者。
- **神保守信徒的方法（改革宗的另一种看法）：** 施莱纳和卡尼迪认为，警告是对真信徒说的，后果也是真的：离弃基督的人必定沉沦；神正是用这样的警告，保守他的百姓不离弃他。就像山路上提醒前方有悬崖的警示牌，是为了叫司机不掉下去。批评者问：既然选民最终不会离弃基督，这警告又怎能算是真实的？
- **其他看法：** 还有人（如格利森、霍奇斯）认为，这里失去的不是救恩，而是赏赐（参贝特曼编《希伯来书警告经文四观》，2007年）；但10:26-27说的是审判和烈火，很难只理解为失去赏赐。
- **共同点：** 这几种看法都同意：离弃基督是极其严重的事；在基督以外再没有别的赎罪祭；作者写这段话，是要叫读者紧紧抓住基督，不是要叫跌倒的人绝望。

**5. 这段经文不是在说什么。**
- **不是说信徒犯了罪就不能回来：** 彼得三次不认主，后来「出去痛哭」（太26:75），复活的主又恢复了他（约21:15-17）。
- **不是说长期冷淡、离开教会多年的人就回不来了：** 浪子在远方很久，父亲却在「相离还远」的时候就看见他，「跑去抱着他的颈项」（路15:20）。希伯来书的读者自己也已经「听不进去」了（5:11），作者却仍然「深信你们的行为强过这些」（6:9）。
- **不是要人整天害怕自己犯了这罪：** 许多牧者指出，一个人若还为自己是否离弃了基督而忧伤，还想回到他那里，这本身就说明他的心还没有这样刚硬。耶稣说：「到我这里来的，我总不丢弃他。」（约6:37）

> 要点：离弃基督是最可怕的事，因为在他以外再没有别的赎罪祭。福音派对这段经文有不同的理解，却都同意：这警告是要我们紧紧抓住基督；凡愿意回到他面前的人，他总不丢弃。

## 深读 6:9-12 神并非不公义

**1. 「我们虽是这样说」。** 「亲爱的弟兄们，我们虽是这样说，却深信你们的行为强过这些，而且近乎得救。」（6:9）
- **严厉的话出于爱：** 这是希伯来书里唯一一次称读者为「亲爱的」：作者刚说完最重的话，就让读者知道他爱他们。
- **「近乎得救」：** 和合本的「近乎」容易被误会成差一点就得救。许多解经家认为，原文的意思是与得救相连的事，也就是真正得救的人身上会有的更美的事。

**2. 「神并非不公义，竟忘记」。** 「因为神并非不公义，竟忘记你们所做的工和你们为他名所显的爱心，就是先前伺候圣徒，如今还是伺候。」（6:10）
- **他们的爱心：** 他们曾经陪伴受苦的人，「体恤了那些被捆锁的人」，家业被抢也甘心忍受（10:33-34）。
- **神不忘记：** 这不是说他们的工作赚得了救恩，而是说神在他们身上的工作结出了真实的果子，神必记念。神应许「不再记念他们的罪愆」（8:12），却不会忘记他们为他的名所做的小事：探监、送饭、接待、陪伴。

**3. 「满足的指望，一直到底」。** 「我们愿你们各人都显出这样的殷勤，使你们有满足的指望，一直到底。并且不懈怠，总要效法那些凭信心和忍耐承受应许的人。」（6:11-12）
- **「满足的指望」：** 指对将来的盼望有完全的把握。
- **「不懈怠」：** 「懈怠」和5:11「听不进去」是同一个词。作者在5:11说他们已经这样了，在这里说愿他们不要这样：他相信他们还能被唤醒。第十一章要列出许多「凭信心和忍耐」的人，这里先讲亚伯拉罕。

> 要点：作者警告得很严厉，却深信读者有与得救相连的更美的事。神没有忘记他们为他名所显的爱心；他要他们对盼望有完全的把握，凭信心和忍耐一直走到底。

## 深读 6:13-18 神指着自己起誓

**1. 「就指着自己起誓」。** 「当初神应许亚伯拉罕的时候，因为没有比自己更大可以指着起誓的，就指着自己起誓，说：『论福，我必赐大福给你；论子孙，我必叫你的子孙多起来。』这样，亚伯拉罕既恒久忍耐，就得了所应许的。」（6:13-15）
- **在摩利亚山上：** 这是亚伯拉罕把以撒献在坛上以后，神对他说的话：「我便指着自己起誓」（创22:16-17）。就在那里，神预备了一只公羊，「代替他的儿子」（创22:13）。
- **漫长的等待：** 他等了二十五年（创12:4；21:5），中间也软弱过，神却没有收回应许。
- **他得了什么：** 那么，他到底得着了没有？希伯来书后面说，这些先祖「都是存着信心死的，并没有得着所应许的」（11:13）。可见亚伯拉罕得着的是应许的开始，完全的成就要和我们一同得着（11:39-40）。

**2. 「两件不更改的事」。** 「照样，神愿意为那承受应许的人格外显明他的旨意是不更改的，就起誓为证。藉这两件不更改的事，神决不能说谎，好叫我们这逃往避难所、持定摆在我们前头指望的人可以大得勉励。」（6:17-18）
- **神为谁起誓：** 人起誓，是指着比自己大的（16节）；神找不到更大的，就指着自己起誓。他起誓是「为那承受应许的人」，因为他知道我们容易疑惑。
- **两件事是什么：** 多数解经家认为是神的应许和神的誓言；也有人认为是神的两次起誓：向亚伯拉罕起的誓（创22:16），和诗篇110:4立基督为祭司的誓：「主起了誓，决不后悔」（7:21）。
- **两种「不能」：** 4-6节说，离弃基督的人「不能」重新懊悔；这里说，神「决不能」说谎，原文是同一个词。离弃者的「不能」叫人警醒，不敢轻看基督；神的「不能」却是我们最大的安慰。

**3. 「逃往避难所」。**
- **逃城：** 许多人认为这里暗指旧约的逃城：误杀人的可以逃到那里，「不至于死」（民35:11-12）。信徒不是靠自己站稳的人，而是逃到神那里去的人。

> 要点：神知道我们容易疑惑，就在应许以外又加上誓言。他决不能说谎；我们这些逃到他那里去的人，可以靠他的话大得勉励。

## 深读 6:19-20 通入幔内

**1. 「如同灵魂的锚」。** 「我们有这指望，如同灵魂的锚，又坚固又牢靠，且通入幔内。」（6:19）
- **锚：** 船在风浪中不被冲走，是因为锚抓住了船以外的东西。一般的锚往下沉，这个锚却往上「通入幔内」：我们的盼望抓住的，不在自己里面，而在神的面前。
- **「幔内」：** 指至圣所的幔子里面。亚伦「不可随时进圣所的幔子内」（利16:2），大祭司一年只能进去一次（9:7）。

**2. 「作先锋的耶稣」。** 「作先锋的耶稣，既照着麦基洗德的等次成了永远的大祭司，就为我们进入幔内。」（6:20）
- **「先锋」：** 这个词指跑在前头、为后面的人开路的人。旧约的大祭司进去以后还要出来，百姓只能站在外面；耶稣却是「为我们」进去的，好叫我们跟着他进去（10:19-20）。
- **用自己的血：** 他「用自己的血，只一次进入圣所，成了永远赎罪的事」（9:12）。他在十字架上替我们担当了罪的刑罚，所以幔子「从上到下裂为两半」（太27:51），路为我们打开了。

> 要点：我们的盼望不是抓住自己里面的什么，而是抓住幔内的那一位。作先锋的耶稣已经用自己的血为我们进去了；他在哪里，他的百姓将来也要在哪里。

## 默想

**默想：** 「这样，亚伯拉罕既恒久忍耐，就得了所应许的。」（6:15）亚伯拉罕（那时还叫亚伯兰）离开哈兰的时候七十五岁，等到以撒出生，他已经一百岁了。二十五年里，帐棚里一直没有听到应许之子的哭声；他也有等不下去的时候，想用自己的办法得一个儿子（创16:2-4），听到应许，心里还会问：一百岁的人还能得孩子吗？（创17:17）我也有一个等了很久的应许。刚信主的时候，我读到「我总不撇下你，也不丢弃你」（13:5），读到「还有一点点时候，那要来的就来」（10:37），心里很火热。后来，身体的病一直没有好，心里的孤单一年比一年重；我嘴上还唱着主必再来的诗歌，心里却早就不再期待了。我所等的，有些其实只是我自己的愿望，神从来没有这样应许过，我可以在他面前为这些哀哭；有些却真是他的应许，我也累得不想再信了。这种疲倦，有时候其实就是不信的罪；我需要向他承认，求他赦免。可是「压伤的芦苇，他不折断」（太12:20），我可以带着这份疲倦，直接到他面前。神怎样对待亚伯拉罕的软弱呢？他没有收回应许。过了这么多年，在摩利亚山上，就在他预备公羊「代替他的儿子」的地方，他又「指着自己起誓」。希伯来书说，这誓不只是为亚伯拉罕起的，也是「为那承受应许的人」，为像我这样累得不想再信的人（6:17）。那只公羊代替了以撒；后来，神「不爱惜自己的儿子，为我们众人舍了」（罗8:32），他的儿子替我们担当了罪。神连儿子都给了，他的应许怎么会落空呢？这应许不是给等得最有耐心的人，而是给逃来投靠他的人。你若还没有投靠他，今天就可以像逃往逃城的人那样，什么也不用带，只带着自己的罪，悔改，逃到他那里去。这盼望的锚已经抛进了幔内，因为作先锋的耶稣已经在那里了。我还在路上，他已经到了；我的信心时冷时热，他却「如今为我们显在神面前」（9:24）。主耶稣，你用自己的血打开了幔子；你照着麦基洗德的等次，作了永远的大祭司，又是为我们先进去的先锋。我等了这么多年的一切，都系在你身上。风浪还没有停，我的心也还会摇晃；可是锚不在我手里，在你那里。你在哪里，我将来也要在哪里。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 continues the rebuke of 5:11–14 and has four parts. **Go on to maturity** (vv. 1–3): let us leave the elementary doctrine of Christ and not lay the foundation again. **The danger of falling away** (vv. 4–8): those who have once been enlightened and have tasted the heavenly gift, if they then fall away, cannot be restored again to repentance; land that has drunk the rain, if it bears thorns and thistles, ends by being burned. **Sure of better things in your case** (vv. 9–12): God is not so unjust as to overlook the readers' love in serving the saints; the author wants them to show earnestness to the end, imitating those who through faith and patience inherit the promises. **God swore by himself** (vv. 13–20): Abraham, having patiently waited, obtained the promise; by two unchangeable things, the promise and the oath, God gives strong encouragement to us who have fled for refuge; this hope is an anchor of the soul that enters behind the curtain, where Jesus has gone as a forerunner on our behalf.

Having given his sternest warning, the author at once adds, "Though we speak in this way, yet in your case, beloved, we feel sure of better things" (v. 9); at last he lifts his readers' eyes to Jesus behind the curtain and comes back to Melchizedek, where he broke off at 5:10.

## Going Deeper: 6:1–3 Leaving the Elementary Doctrine of Christ

**1. "Go on to maturity."** "Therefore let us leave the elementary doctrine of Christ and go on to maturity" (6:1).
- **Not throwing the foundation away:** "leave" does not mean these teachings are unimportant, but that we should not stay forever at the stage of laying a foundation. Once the foundation is laid, the house is to be built up.
- **"Maturity":** this word shares its root with "the mature" in 5:14. Many interpreters take the maturity he means to be a deeper knowledge of Christ as high priest after the order of Melchizedek (chapters 7–10).

**2. Six foundational teachings.** "...not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God, and of instruction about washings, the laying on of hands, the resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgment" (6:1–2).
- **Three pairs:** many see these six as three pairs: repentance and faith toward God, about how a person turns to God; washings and the laying on of hands, about rites of entry; resurrection and judgment, about the last things.
- **Familiar to Jews as well:** nearly all of these can be found in the Old Testament and in Jewish faith. Many interpreters think that if the readers stayed only on a foundation shared with the synagogue, they might not see what was different about Christ, and in the end go back to the old covenant's rites.

**3. "This we will do if God permits."** "And this we will do if God permits" (6:3).
- **Growth is in God's hands too:** the author urges his readers to press on, and confesses that going forward depends on God's permission; even maturity is grace.

> The point: Maturity is not leaving the foundation but building on it; it is not moving on from Christ to something else but knowing him more deeply. Going forward takes earnest effort, and it depends on God's permission.

## Going Deeper: 6:4–8 If They Then Fall Away

**1. Five descriptions.** "For it is impossible to restore again to repentance those who have once been enlightened, who have tasted the heavenly gift, and have shared in the Holy Spirit, and have tasted the goodness of the word of God and the powers of the age to come, if they then fall away, since they are crucifying once again the Son of God to their own harm and holding him up to contempt" (6:4–6).
- **"Enlightened":** in 10:32 the author says his readers endured "a hard struggle with sufferings" "after you were enlightened," using the same expression.
- **"Tasted," "shared":** many take "tasted" as real experience, just as Jesus was to "taste death for everyone" (2:9); others think it means only a slight sampling. "Shared" is the same word as "share" in "we share in Christ" (3:14).
- **"If they then fall away":** this is not stumbling in a moment of weakness, but turning one's back on Christ openly and completely. Many take "crucifying once again the Son of God" to mean siding with those who crucified Jesus and declaring openly that he deserved to die.

**2. "It is impossible to restore again to repentance."**
- **Why impossible?** Not because Christ's blood is not enough, but because such people have rejected the only one through whom anyone can repent and be forgiven. Apart from him, "there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins" (10:26).
- **"Since" or "while"?** The ESV, the CUV and most translations take the second half of verse 6 as giving the reason; others read "while": as long as they go on crucifying and shaming him, they cannot be restored. Many, though, find that reading only states the obvious.

**3. Land that has drunk the rain.** "For land that has drunk the rain that often falls on it, and produces a crop useful to those for whose sake it is cultivated, receives a blessing from God. But if it bears thorns and thistles, it is worthless and near to being cursed, and its end is to be burned" (6:7–8).
- **The same rain, different crops:** the rain is God's grace, and both pieces of land have drunk it, yet their crops differ. The more grace received, the greater the responsibility. "Thorns and thistles" recalls the ground after our first parents sinned: "cursed is the ground because of you" (Gen 3:17–18).

**4. Four of the main readings.** This is one of the most debated passages in the New Testament. Each of these four views is held by rigorous interpreters:
- **True believers can be lost (the Arminian tradition):** the five descriptions most naturally read as describing true believers; a true believer who falls away from Christ loses salvation and cannot come back. This view is held by I. Howard Marshall, Scot McKnight, and Gareth Cockerill in the Wesleyan tradition, among others. The difficulty it must face is how to reconcile it with promises such as "no one will snatch them out of my hand" (John 10:28).
- **A hypothetical warning:** the author describes something that will not actually happen: if a true believer were to fall away from Christ, he could never come back, so do not go down that road. This view is held by Thomas Hewitt and Homer Kent, among others. Critics answer that the author plainly treats this as a real danger (3:12; 10:26–27). Also, in Greek "fall away" is a participle in the same series as the descriptions before it, not a conditional clause.
- **Those who seemed to believe but were never born again (one Reformed view):** these people truly experienced many workings of the Holy Spirit in the church, yet were never regenerate, like seed sown on rocky ground, who "immediately receive it with joy" but "have no root in themselves" (Mark 4:16–17). Calvin, John Owen and, today, Wayne Grudem hold views of this kind. They note that the two pieces of land drink the same rain but bear different crops. Critics answer that in 10:32 the author uses "enlightened" of the readers themselves.
- **God's means of keeping believers (another Reformed view):** Thomas Schreiner and Ardel Caneday hold that the warning is addressed to true believers and that its consequence is real: those who fall away from Christ will perish; and God uses just such warnings to keep his people from falling away. Like a sign warning of a cliff ahead on a mountain road, it is there so that the driver will not go over. Critics ask: if the elect will never finally fall away, how can the warning be real?
- **Other views:** some (such as Randall Gleason and Zane Hodges, in the Free Grace tradition) hold that what is lost here is not salvation but rewards (see Herbert Bateman, ed., *Four Views on the Warning Passages in Hebrews*, 2007); but 10:26–27 speaks of judgment and fire, which is hard to read as a mere loss of rewards.
- **What they share:** all these views agree that falling away from Christ is deadly serious; that outside Christ there is no other sacrifice for sins; and that the author writes this to make his readers hold fast to Christ, not to drive those who have stumbled to despair.

**5. What this passage does not say.**
- **It does not say a believer who sins can never come back:** Peter denied the Lord three times, then "went out and wept bitterly" (Matt 26:75), and the risen Lord restored him (John 21:15–17).
- **It does not say that someone who has been cold for years, or away from church for a long time, can never come back:** the prodigal was far off a long while, yet "while he was still a long way off," his father saw him and "ran and embraced him" (Luke 15:20). The readers of Hebrews had themselves already "become dull of hearing" (5:11), yet the author says, "we feel sure of better things" (6:9).
- **It is not meant to make us live in fear that we have committed this sin:** many pastors note that a person who still grieves over whether he has fallen away from Christ, and still wants to come back to him, shows by that very fact that his heart has not hardened in this way. Jesus said, "whoever comes to me I will never cast out" (John 6:37).

> The point: Falling away from Christ is the most terrible thing, because outside him there is no other sacrifice for sins. Evangelicals read this passage in different ways, but all agree that the warning is meant to make us hold fast to Christ; whoever is willing to come back to him, he will never cast out.

## Going Deeper: 6:9–12 God Is Not Unjust

**1. "Though we speak in this way."** "Though we speak in this way, yet in your case, beloved, we feel sure of better things—things that belong to salvation" (6:9).
- **Hard words spoken in love:** this is the only time in Hebrews the author calls his readers "beloved": having just said the heaviest thing, he lets them know he loves them.
- **"Things that belong to salvation":** the CUV's wording (近乎得救) can be misread as "almost saved." Many interpreters take the Greek to mean things that go with salvation, the better things found in those who are truly saved.

**2. "God is not so unjust as to overlook."** "For God is not so unjust as to overlook your work and the love that you showed for his sake in serving the saints, as you still do" (6:10).
- **Their love:** they had stood with those who suffered, "had compassion on those in prison," and "joyfully accepted the plundering of your property" (10:33–34).
- **God does not overlook it:** this does not mean their work earned salvation, but that God's work in them has borne real fruit, and God will remember it. God promises, "I will remember their sins no more" (8:12), yet he does not forget the small things they did for his name: visiting prisoners, bringing food, giving hospitality, keeping company.

**3. "The full assurance of hope until the end."** "And we desire each one of you to show the same earnestness to have the full assurance of hope until the end, so that you may not be sluggish, but imitators of those who through faith and patience inherit the promises" (6:11–12).
- **"Full assurance of hope":** complete confidence in what lies ahead.
- **"Not be sluggish":** "sluggish" is the same word as "dull" in 5:11. In 5:11 he said they had become so; here he says he wants them not to be: he believes they can still be roused. Chapter 11 will list many who lived "through faith and patience"; here he begins with Abraham.

> The point: The author's warning is stern, yet he is sure of better things in his readers, things that belong to salvation. God has not overlooked the love they showed for his sake; he wants them to be fully assured of their hope and to go on to the end through faith and patience.

## Going Deeper: 6:13–18 God Swore by Himself

**1. "He swore by himself."** "For when God made a promise to Abraham, since he had no one greater by whom to swear, he swore by himself, saying, 'Surely I will bless you and multiply you.' And thus Abraham, having patiently waited, obtained the promise" (6:13–15).
- **On Mount Moriah:** these are the words God spoke to Abraham after he had laid Isaac on the altar: "By myself I have sworn, declares the LORD" (Gen 22:16–17). Right there God had provided a ram, which Abraham offered "instead of his son" (Gen 22:13).
- **A long wait:** he waited twenty-five years (Gen 12:4; 21:5), and was weak along the way, yet God did not take the promise back.
- **What did he obtain?** Did he, then, receive it? Later Hebrews says these patriarchs "all died in faith, not having received the things promised" (11:13). So what Abraham obtained was the beginning of the promise; its full fulfillment he will receive together with us (11:39–40).

**2. "Two unchangeable things."** "So when God desired to show more convincingly to the heirs of the promise the unchangeable character of his purpose, he guaranteed it with an oath, so that by two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled for refuge might have strong encouragement to hold fast to the hope set before us" (6:17–18).
- **For whom did God swear?** People swear by something greater than themselves (v. 16); God could find no one greater, so he swore by himself. He swore for the sake of "the heirs of the promise," because he knows how easily we doubt.
- **What are the two things?** Most interpreters take them to be God's promise and God's oath; others take them to be God's two oaths: the oath to Abraham (Gen 22:16) and the oath of Psalm 110:4 appointing Christ a priest: "The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind" (7:21).
- **Two kinds of "impossible":** verses 4–6 say it is "impossible" to restore those who fall away from Christ; here it is "impossible for God to lie," the same Greek word. The apostate's "impossible" is meant to sober us, so that we never treat Christ lightly; God's "impossible" is our greatest comfort.

**3. "We who have fled for refuge."**
- **The cities of refuge:** many think this alludes to the Old Testament cities of refuge, where a manslayer could flee so that he "may not die" (Num 35:11–12). Believers are not people who stand firm by themselves, but people who have fled to God.

> The point: God knows how easily we doubt, so he added an oath to his promise. It is impossible for him to lie; we who have fled to him may have strong encouragement from his word.

## Going Deeper: 6:19–20 Behind the Curtain

**1. "A sure and steadfast anchor of the soul."** "We have this as a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul, a hope that enters into the inner place behind the curtain" (6:19).
- **The anchor:** a ship is not swept away in a storm because its anchor has taken hold of something outside the ship. An ordinary anchor sinks down; this one goes up and "enters into the inner place behind the curtain": what our hope takes hold of is not in ourselves but in the presence of God.
- **"Behind the curtain":** inside the curtain of the Most Holy Place. Aaron was "not to come at any time into the Holy Place inside the veil" (Lev 16:2); the high priest went in only once a year (9:7).

**2. "Jesus has gone as a forerunner."** "...where Jesus has gone as a forerunner on our behalf, having become a high priest forever after the order of Melchizedek" (6:20).
- **"Forerunner":** the word means one who runs ahead and opens the way for those who follow. The Old Testament high priest went in and had to come out again, and the people could only stand outside; but Jesus has gone in "on our behalf," so that we may follow him in (10:19–20).
- **By his own blood:** he "entered once for all into the holy places... by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption" (9:12). On the cross he bore the penalty of our sin in our place; so "the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom" (Matt 27:51), and the way was opened for us.

> The point: Our hope does not take hold of anything in ourselves, but of the one behind the curtain. Jesus the forerunner has gone in for us by his own blood; where he is, his people too will one day be.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And thus Abraham, having patiently waited, obtained the promise" (6:15). Abraham (then still Abram) was seventy-five when he left Haran; by the time Isaac was born he was a hundred. For twenty-five years the cry of the promised son was never heard in their tent; there were times he could not wait any longer, and they tried to get a son their own way (Gen 16:2–4), and even when he heard the promise, he asked in his heart whether a man of a hundred could have a child (Gen 17:17). I too have a promise I have waited for a long time. When I first believed, I read "I will never leave you nor forsake you" (13:5) and "Yet a little while, and the coming one will come" (10:37), and my heart burned. Later, my illness never got better, and my loneliness grew heavier year by year; I still sing hymns about the Lord's return, but in my heart I stopped expecting it long ago. Some of what I waited for was really only my own wish, which God never promised, and I may weep over those things before him; but some are truly his promises, and I have grown too tired to keep believing them too. That weariness is sometimes simply the sin of unbelief, and I need to confess it to him and ask his forgiveness. Yet "a bruised reed he will not break" (Matt 12:20), and I may come to him with this weariness just as it is. How did God deal with Abraham's weakness? He did not take back the promise. After all those years, on Mount Moriah, the very place where he provided the ram "instead of his son," he confirmed it: "he swore by himself." Hebrews says this oath was not only for Abraham; God meant it for "the heirs of the promise" (6:17), for people like me, too tired to keep believing. The ram took Isaac's place; later God "did not spare his own Son but gave him up for us all" (Rom 8:32), and his Son bore our sins in our place. If God has given even his Son, how could his promise fail? This promise is not for those who wait most patiently, but for those who flee to him for refuge. If you have not yet taken refuge in him, you may come today like one running to the city of refuge, bringing nothing but your sins, and in repentance flee to him. The anchor of this hope has already been cast behind the curtain, because Jesus the forerunner is already there. I am still on the road; he has already arrived. My faith runs hot and cold, but he is "now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf" (9:24). Lord Jesus, by your own blood you opened the curtain; you have become a high priest forever after the order of Melchizedek, the forerunner who has gone in ahead of us. Everything I have waited for all these years is bound up in you. The storm has not stopped, and my heart will still sway; but the anchor is not in my hands. It is with you, and where you are, I too will one day be.
