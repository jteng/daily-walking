## 本章脉络

第七章分成三段。**麦基洗德比亚伯拉罕更大**（1-10节）：亚伯拉罕向他纳十分之一，又从他领受祝福。**祭司职任更改，律法也必更改**（11-19节）：神照无穷之生命的大能另立一位祭司，引进了更美的指望。**永远常存的大祭司**（20-28节）：他是起誓立的，长远活着替我们祈求，只一次将自己献上。

这一章就是作者在5:11-14想给读者的「干粮」，全章都在解释诗篇110:4：「耶和华起了誓，决不后悔，说：你是照着麦基洗德的等次永远为祭司。」

## 深读 7:1-10 麦基洗德是谁

**1. 仁义王，平安王。** 「这麦基洗德就是撒冷王，又是至高神的祭司，本是长远为祭司的。他当亚伯拉罕杀败诸王回来的时候，就迎接他，给他祝福。」（7:1）
- **创世记的记载：** 麦基洗德在旧约只出现两次：创世记14:18-20，他「带着饼和酒出来迎接」亚伯兰，为他祝福；还有诗篇110:4。许多人认为撒冷就是后来的耶路撒冷（诗76:2）。他同时是王和祭司，预表那位坐在神右边、又永远为祭司的基督（诗110:1、4）。和合本把原文第3节末尾的「长远为祭司」移到了第1节。
- **先仁义，后平安：** 作者解释说，他「头一个名翻出来就是仁义王」，又是「平安王」（7:2）。这个次序也提醒我们：在基督里，神的公义得到满足，罪人才得与神相和（罗3:25-26；5:1）。

**2. 「无父，无母，无族谱」。** 「他无父，无母，无族谱，无生之始，无命之终，乃是与神的儿子相似。」（7:3）
- **大多数解经家的读法：圣经的沉默。** 加尔文、欧文，以及近代的布鲁斯、雷恩、施赖纳等人认为，作者是按照创世记的写法来推论：创世记常常记载人的家谱、生年和死年，到了麦基洗德却什么都没有记。作者不是说他真的没有父母、没有生死，而是说圣经的沉默使他在文字上成了一个影像，指向那位真正无始无终的神的儿子。旧约的祭司要靠族谱证明资格（拉2:62），麦基洗德的祭司职分却不靠族谱。
- **基督显现的读法：** 古今也有人认为，麦基洗德就是神的儿子在道成肉身以前的显现。但经文说他「与神的儿子相似」，既说相似，就不是同一位；他又是一座真实城邑的王。所以多数解经家没有采纳这个看法。
- **不要猜测：** 后来有人说他就是挪亚的儿子闪，也有人把他当作天上的人物（如死海古卷11Q13）。希伯来书没有采用这些说法；我们也应该在圣经停下来的地方停下来。

**3. 十分之一和祝福。** 「你们想一想，先祖亚伯拉罕将自己所掳来上等之物取十分之一给他，这人是何等尊贵呢！」（7:4）「从来位分大的给位分小的祝福，这是驳不倒的理。」（7:7）
- **比亚伯拉罕更大：** 利未的子孙照律法向弟兄收十分之一（7:5；民18:21）；麦基洗德「不与他们同谱」，却收了先祖亚伯拉罕的十分之一，又为这蒙应许的人祝福（7:6）。
- **「他是活的」（7:8）：** 许多人认为，这里和第3节的推论一样：创世记没有记载他的死。作者的眼光已经转向那位真正「长远活着」的祭司（7:25）。
- **「利未已经在他先祖的身中」：** 作者先说「可说」（7:9），表明这是一种打比方的说法。圣经常把先祖和后裔看作一体（参罗5:12；创25:23）；连收十分之一的利未，也在亚伯拉罕身中向麦基洗德纳了十分之一（7:9-10）。

> 要点：麦基洗德是真实的历史人物，圣经对他来历的沉默，使他成了神的儿子的影像。连亚伯拉罕和在他身中的利未都向他纳十分之一；照他等次的那位祭司，比旧约一切的祭司都大。

## 深读 7:11-19 照无穷之生命的大能

**1. 「倘若藉这职任能得完全」。** 「从前百姓在利未人祭司职任以下受律法，倘若藉这职任能得完全，又何用另外兴起一位祭司，照麦基洗德的等次，不照亚伦的等次呢？」（7:11）大卫写诗篇110篇时，利未的祭司制度已有几百年；神若仍要另立祭司，可见旧的职任不够。在希伯来书里，「完全」不是指道德上毫无瑕疵，而是指人的良心得到洁净，真正被带到神面前（9:9；10:1、14）。

**2. 「律法也必须更改」。** 「祭司的职任既已更改，律法也必须更改。」（7:12）「我们的主分明是从犹大出来的；但这支派，摩西并没有提到祭司。」（7:14）
- **改变到什么程度：** 摩西律法的制度建立在利未的祭司职任上（7:11）。改革宗的传统（加尔文、《威斯敏斯德信条》第十九章）认为，这里废掉的是关于祭司和献祭的礼仪律，这些都已在基督身上成全；神在道德律中显明的旨意仍然有效。另一些解经家（如施赖纳，以及持新盟约神学的人）认为，作者是说整个摩西之约都已经结束（8:13），神的道德旨意如今藉着基督和「基督的律法」显明（加6:2）。这两种看法都同意：没有人能靠律法进到神面前；基督来「乃是要成全」律法（太5:17）。

**3. 「乃是照无穷之生命的大能」。** 「他成为祭司，并不是照属肉体的条例，乃是照无穷之生命的大能。因为有给他作见证的说：『你是照着麦基洗德的等次永远为祭司。』」（7:16-17）
- **「无穷之生命」：** 这个词的意思是不能毁坏的生命。许多解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为这是指他的复活：他真实地死了，死亡却拘禁不住他（徒2:24），他「直活到永永远远」（启1:18）；也有人认为这是指永远的儿子本有的生命（1:3、8）。这两种理解可以并存：永远的儿子成了真正的人，有血肉之体（2:14），死了，又复活了，如今带着这不能毁坏的生命作我们的祭司。

**4. 「就引进了更美的指望」。** 「先前的条例，因软弱无益，所以废掉了，（律法原来一无所成）就引进了更美的指望；靠这指望，我们便可以进到神面前。」（7:18-19）
- **「软弱无益」：** 不是说律法本身不好，「律法是圣洁的」（罗7:12）。律法能指出罪，却不能除去罪（罗3:20；来10:4、11）。「一无所成」原文的意思是没有使什么得以完全，呼应第11节的「完全」。
- **「我们便可以进到神面前」：** 这是全章的核心。旧约的百姓只能远远站着（出20:18、21）；如今凡藉着耶稣的人，都可以「坦然无惧地来到施恩的宝座前」（4:16；参10:19-22）。这指望就是那「通入幔内」的灵魂之锚（6:19-20）。

> 要点：旧的祭司职任不能叫人得完全，所以神另外兴起一位祭司，他是照无穷之生命的大能作祭司。律法不能做的，基督做成了；靠着他，我们这些罪人可以真正来到神面前。

## 深读 7:20-25 他都能拯救到底

**1. 「主起了誓，决不后悔」。** 「至于那些祭司，原不是起誓立的，只有耶稣是起誓立的；因为那立他的对他说：『主起了誓，决不后悔，你是永远为祭司。』既是起誓立的，耶稣就作了更美之约的中保。」（7:21-22）
- **起誓：** 利未的祭司照律法设立，耶稣作祭司却有神的誓言作保证，显明「他的旨意是不更改的」（6:17）。
- **「中保」：** 和合本在这里和8:6都译作「中保」，原文却是不同的词。这里的词在新约只出现这一次，意思是担保人。许多人（如雷恩）强调，他向我们担保新约的应许必定实现；改革宗的传统（如欧文）还强调，他也在神面前为我们作保，亲自偿还了我们的罪债。这两方面并不冲突。

**2. 「有死阻隔，不能长久」。** 「那些成为祭司的，数目本来多，是因为有死阻隔，不能长久。这位既是永远常存的，他祭司的职任就长久不更换。」（7:23-24）
- **一代一代的祭司：** 据犹太史家约瑟夫的记载，从亚伦到圣殿被毁共有八十三位大祭司，每一位都死了，圣衣穿到了下一位身上（参民20:28）。
- **「长久不更换」：** 这个词通常指永久不变；有人认为也含有不能转让给别人的意思。上下文（23-24节）也说明：他的职任没有期限，也不需要接班人。

**3. 「他都能拯救到底」。** 「凡靠着他进到神面前的人，他都能拯救到底；因为他是长远活着，替他们祈求。」（7:25）
- **「拯救到底」：** 这个说法可以指「完完全全地」，也可以指「直到永远」，许多解经家认为两层意思都包括。
- **「凡靠着他……的人」：** 没有人的罪大到他不能救，也没有人能绕过他到神面前去。
- **代求是什么：** 不是父不愿意施恩，需要儿子去说服，因为「父自己爱你们」（约16:27）；也不是他的祭还没有完成，因为他「献了一次永远的赎罪祭，就在神的右边坐下了」（10:12）。许多改革宗的解经家（如欧文）认为，这位已经把自己献上的大祭司，如今「为我们显在神面前」（9:24）；他在那里，本身就是我们的凭据；他也真实地为属他的人祈求，像他为彼得祈求「叫你不至于失了信心」（路22:32）。所以谁也不能定我们的罪（罗8:34）。

> 要点：从前的祭司都被死亡阻隔，耶稣却是神起誓立的，永远常存，长远活着替我们祈求。凡靠着他来到神面前的人，他都能拯救到底，完完全全，直到永远。

## 深读 7:26-28 只一次将自己献上

**1. 「原是与我们合宜的」。** 「像这样圣洁、无邪恶、无玷污、远离罪人、高过诸天的大祭司，原是与我们合宜的。」（7:26）
- **圣洁、无邪恶、无玷污：** 他全心归于父；「他并没有犯罪，口里也没有诡诈」（彼前2:22）。旧约的大祭司连为父母也不可挨近死尸，免得沾染自己（利21:11）；耶稣摸了长大麻风的人，自己却没有被沾染，反倒叫他洁净了（太8:3）。
- **「远离罪人」：** 不是说他和罪人保持距离，他正是「接待罪人，又同他们吃饭」的那一位（路15:2）。意思是他在品格上与罪人截然不同，如今又升到高天（1:3）。

**2. 「他只一次将自己献上」。** 「他不像那些大祭司，每日必须先为自己的罪，后为百姓的罪献祭；因为他只一次将自己献上，就把这事成全了。」（7:27）
- **「每日」：** 赎罪日的祭一年只献一次（利16:6、34；参来9:7），这里却说「每日」。有人认为，作者是把大祭司和天天献祭的整个祭司制度连在一起说（10:11）；也有人指出，大祭司每天都要献素祭（利6:20）。
- **祭司就是祭物：** 在十字架上，献祭的和被献的是同一位。他「亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24）；「神使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义」（林后5:21）。我们被神称为义，根据不在我们的行为，也不在我们悔改得多深，而在他替我们献上的这一次完全的祭；我们是藉着信，白白地领受这义（罗3:22-25）。
- **「成全了」：** 这件事不需要重复，也不需要我们再烧香、做功德来补足。罗马天主教教导，弥撒不是重复十字架的献祭，而是使那一次的献祭临在，与十字架是同一个祭；弥撒是以不流血的方式献上，却是真实的挽回祭（《天主教教理》1366-1367条）。更正教的传统（如加尔文）则认为，「只一次」（9:12；10:10）排除了把圣餐当作献祭的理解：罪既已赦免，「就不用再为罪献祭了」（10:18）。圣餐是记念并领受那已经成全的祭。

**3. 「成全到永远的」。** 「律法本是立软弱的人为大祭司；但在律法以后起誓的话，是立儿子为大祭司，乃是成全到永远的。」（7:28）和5:9一样，「成全」不是说儿子曾有缺欠，而是说他经过受苦、受死、复活，完成了作大祭司所需的一切。

> 要点：我们需要的，正是这样一位圣洁、无罪、高过诸天的大祭司。他献上的不是别的，而是他自己，替我们担当了罪；这祭只一次，就永远成全了。

## 默想

**默想：** 「那些成为祭司的，数目本来多，是因为有死阻隔，不能长久。这位既是永远常存的，他祭司的职任就长久不更换。」（7:23-24）我信主的路上，曾经有人像祭司一样背着我走。有为我施洗的老牧师，有每天清早跪在床边、为全家人一个一个祷告的外婆，还有在我最迷茫那一年陪我查经的学长。后来，牧师退休了，学长搬走了，外婆也走了。追思礼拜那天我忽然想到：从此再也没有人那样为我祷告了。我心里还有亏欠：外婆病重时，我总说忙，很少回去；牧师退休后，我连一句谢谢都没有说。希伯来书没有叫我假装不难过，反倒叫我要「想念」那些传神之道给我们的人（13:7）。我可以为他们哀伤、感恩，也可以把亏欠带到那位只一次将自己献上的大祭司面前（7:27）。也有人的伤更深：那个带他信主的人，后来跌倒在罪里，甚至利用属灵的权柄伤害了人。这样的事不可遮掩：如果涉及虐待或犯罪，向警方举报是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人不必回到危险中去，教会也不可因为「家丑不可外扬」而替他隐瞒。那位带领者的跌倒，不能使我们从他口中听到的福音变成假的，因为我们得救的根基从来不在他身上，而在那位从不欺哄信靠他之人的大祭司身上。我常常不知不觉地把这些人当作自己的遮盖，好像有他们为我祷告，我才安全。这其实是把人放在了只有他才能站的位置上，也让他们背了他们背不动的担子。可是这一位「永远常存」：他不会退休，不会搬走，不会跌倒，也不会再死。我不必盼望外婆在天上保佑我；外婆的祷告停了，他的祈求没有停，「他是长远活着，替他们祈求」（7:25）。其实外婆当年也是靠着他才能来到神面前；她是把我交托给了一位不会死的祭司。这位祭司今天也在呼召那些还站在远处的人：「凡靠着他进到神面前的人，他都能拯救到底。」你不必等别人替你祷告；你的罪也没有大到他救不了的。带着它来，悔改，信靠他。我只想停下来看看他：他圣洁、无邪恶；他远离罪人，高过诸天，却为罪人舍了自己。「像这样圣洁、无邪恶、无玷污、远离罪人、高过诸天的大祭司，原是与我们合宜的。」（7:26）主耶稣，那些陪我走过一段路的人，都是你所赐的；可是你，死亡永远不能把你从我这里夺走。父已经起了誓，决不后悔：你是永远为祭司。我的心要因你欢喜。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 7 has three parts. **Melchizedek is greater than Abraham** (vv. 1–10): Abraham pays him a tenth and receives his blessing. **A change of priesthood means a change of law** (vv. 11–19): God raises up another priest by the power of an indestructible life and introduces a better hope. **The high priest who continues forever** (vv. 20–28): made priest with an oath, he always lives to intercede for us and offered up himself once for all.

This chapter is the "solid food" the author wanted to give his readers in 5:11–14, and the whole of it explains Psalm 110:4: "The LORD has sworn and will not change his mind, 'You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek.'"

## Going Deeper: 7:1–10 Who Was Melchizedek?

**1. King of righteousness, king of peace.** "For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of the Most High God, met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him" (7:1).
- **The record in Genesis:** Melchizedek appears only twice in the Old Testament: in Genesis 14:18–20, where he "brought out bread and wine" to meet Abram and blessed him, and in Psalm 110:4. Many take Salem to be the later Jerusalem (Ps 76:2). He was both king and priest, a picture of Christ, who sits at God's right hand and is a priest forever (Ps 110:1, 4). The CUV moves "he continues a priest forever" from the end of verse 3 to verse 1.
- **First righteousness, then peace:** the author explains that he is "first, by translation of his name, king of righteousness," and then "king of peace" (7:2). This order also reminds us that in Christ, God's righteousness is satisfied, and then sinners have peace with God (Rom 3:25–26; 5:1).

**2. "Without father or mother or genealogy."** "He is without father or mother or genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, but resembling the Son of God he continues a priest forever" (7:3).
- **The reading of most interpreters: the silence of Scripture.** Calvin and Owen, and in modern times Bruce, Lane, Schreiner and others, hold that the author is reasoning from the way Genesis is written: Genesis often records people's genealogies, births and deaths, but for Melchizedek it records none of these. The author is not saying he really had no parents and no birth or death, but that Scripture's silence makes him, on the page, a likeness pointing to the Son of God, who truly has no beginning and no end. Old Testament priests had to prove their right by genealogy (Ezra 2:62); Melchizedek's priesthood did not depend on one.
- **The christophany reading:** some, ancient and modern, have held that Melchizedek was the Son of God appearing before the incarnation. But the text says he was "resembling the Son of God," and one who resembles is not the same person; he was also king of a real city. So most interpreters have not adopted this view.
- **Do not speculate:** some later said he was Noah's son Shem, others made him a heavenly figure (as in the Dead Sea Scroll 11Q13). Hebrews takes up none of these ideas; we too should stop where Scripture stops.

**3. The tithe and the blessing.** "See how great this man was to whom Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth of the spoils!" (7:4). "It is beyond dispute that the inferior is blessed by the superior" (7:7).
- **Greater than Abraham:** the descendants of Levi took tithes from their brothers under the law (7:5; Num 18:21); Melchizedek, "who does not have his descent from them," received tithes from the patriarch Abraham himself and blessed "him who had the promises" (7:6).
- **"He lives" (7:8):** many take this as the same reasoning as in verse 3: Genesis records no death for him. The author's eyes have already turned to the priest who truly "always lives" (7:25).
- **"He was still in the loins of his ancestor":** the author begins, "One might even say" (7:9), signalling a figurative argument. Scripture often treats an ancestor and his descendants as one (compare Rom 5:12; Gen 25:23); so even Levi, who received tithes, paid tithes to Melchizedek in Abraham (7:9–10).

> The point: Melchizedek was a real person in history, and Scripture's silence about where he came from made him a likeness of the Son of God. Even Abraham, and Levi in him, paid tithes to him; the priest after his order is greater than all the priests of the Old Testament.

## Going Deeper: 7:11–19 By the Power of an Indestructible Life

**1. "If perfection had been attainable through the Levitical priesthood."** "Now if perfection had been attainable through the Levitical priesthood (for under it the people received the law), what further need would there have been for another priest to arise after the order of Melchizedek, rather than one named after the order of Aaron?" (7:11). When David wrote Psalm 110, the Levitical priesthood had stood for centuries; if God still meant to appoint another priest, the old one was not enough. In Hebrews, "perfection" does not mean moral flawlessness; it means that a person's conscience is cleansed and he is truly brought to God (9:9; 10:1, 14).

**2. "A change in the law as well."** "For when there is a change in the priesthood, there is necessarily a change in the law as well" (7:12). "For it is evident that our Lord was descended from Judah, and in connection with that tribe Moses said nothing about priests" (7:14).
- **How far does it change?** The Mosaic system rested on the Levitical priesthood (7:11). The Reformed tradition (Calvin, the Westminster Confession chapter 19) holds that what is set aside is the ceremonial law of priesthood and sacrifice, all fulfilled in Christ, while God's will revealed in the moral law still stands. Other interpreters (such as Schreiner, and New Covenant Theology) hold that the author means the Mosaic covenant as a whole has come to an end (8:13), and that God's moral will is now made known through Christ and "the law of Christ" (Gal 6:2). Both views agree: no one draws near to God by the law, and Christ came "to fulfill" the law (Matt 5:17).

**3. "By the power of an indestructible life."** "who has become a priest, not on the basis of a legal requirement concerning bodily descent, but by the power of an indestructible life. For it is witnessed of him, 'You are a priest forever, after the order of Melchizedek'" (7:16–17).
- **"An indestructible life":** the word means a life that cannot be torn down. Many interpreters (such as Bruce and Lane) take this to refer to his resurrection: he truly died, but death could not hold him (Acts 2:24); he is "alive forevermore" (Rev 1:18). Others take it to mean the life the eternal Son has always had (1:3, 8). These two readings can stand together: the eternal Son became truly human, sharing in flesh and blood (2:14), died and rose again, and now serves as our priest with this indestructible life.

**4. "A better hope is introduced."** "On the one hand, a former commandment is set aside because of its weakness and uselessness (for the law made nothing perfect); but on the other hand, a better hope is introduced, through which we draw near to God" (7:18–19).
- **"Weakness and uselessness":** this does not mean the law itself is bad; "the law is holy" (Rom 7:12). The law can show sin but cannot take it away (Rom 3:20; Heb 10:4, 11). "Made nothing perfect" echoes the "perfection" of verse 11.
- **"Through which we draw near to God":** this is the heart of the chapter. The people of the Old Testament had to stand far off (Exod 20:18, 21); now all who come through Jesus may "with confidence draw near to the throne of grace" (4:16; see 10:19–22). This hope is the anchor of the soul that "enters into the inner place behind the curtain" (6:19–20).

> The point: The old priesthood could not bring anyone to perfection, so God raised up another priest, one who serves by the power of an indestructible life. What the law could not do, Christ has done; through him, sinners like us can truly come near to God.

## Going Deeper: 7:20–25 He Is Able to Save to the Uttermost

**1. "The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind."** "For those who formerly became priests were made such without an oath, but this one was made a priest with an oath by the one who said to him: 'The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind, "You are a priest forever."' This makes Jesus the guarantor of a better covenant" (7:20–22).
- **The oath:** the Levitical priests were appointed under the law; Jesus' priesthood is guaranteed by God's oath, showing "the unchangeable character of his purpose" (6:17).
- **"Guarantor":** the CUV uses the same word, 中保, here and in 8:6, but the Greek words differ. The word here appears only once in the New Testament and means a surety. Many (such as Lane) stress that he guarantees to us that the new covenant's promises will be kept; the Reformed tradition (such as Owen) also stresses that he stands surety for us before God, having paid our debt of sin himself. These two sides do not conflict.

**2. "Prevented by death from continuing in office."** "The former priests were many in number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in office, but he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever" (7:23–24).
- **Priest after priest:** according to the Jewish historian Josephus, there were eighty-three high priests from Aaron to the destruction of the temple; each one died, and the holy garments passed to the next (see Num 20:28).
- **"Permanently":** the word usually means permanent or unchangeable; some think it also carries the sense of non-transferable. The context (vv. 23–24) makes the same point: his priesthood has no end date and needs no successor.

**3. "He is able to save to the uttermost."** "Consequently, he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them" (7:25).
- **"To the uttermost":** the phrase can mean "completely" or "for all time," and many interpreters think it includes both.
- **"Those who draw near to God through him":** no one's sin is too great for him to save, and no one can go around him to reach God.
- **What his intercession is:** it does not mean the Father is unwilling to show grace and must be persuaded by the Son, for "the Father himself loves you" (John 16:27); nor that his sacrifice is unfinished, for "when Christ had offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins, he sat down at the right hand of God" (10:12). Many Reformed interpreters (such as Owen) hold that he appears "in the presence of God on our behalf" (9:24) as the one who has offered himself, and his presence there is itself our plea; and that he truly prays for his own, as he prayed for Peter "that your faith may not fail" (Luke 22:32). So no one can condemn us (Rom 8:34).

> The point: The former priests were all prevented by death, but Jesus, made priest by God's oath, continues forever and always lives to intercede for us. He is able to save to the uttermost all who come to God through him, completely and for all time.

## Going Deeper: 7:26–28 When He Offered Up Himself

**1. "It was indeed fitting that we should have such a high priest."** "For it was indeed fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens" (7:26).
- **Holy, innocent, unstained:** he is wholly devoted to the Father; "He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth" (1 Pet 2:22). The Old Testament high priest could not go near a dead body, even for his father or mother, lest he make himself unclean (Lev 21:11); Jesus touched a leper and was not defiled, but made him clean (Matt 8:3).
- **"Separated from sinners":** this does not mean he kept his distance from sinners; he is the one who "receives sinners and eats with them" (Luke 15:2). It means that in character he is wholly unlike sinners, and he is now exalted on high (1:3).

**2. "He did this once for all when he offered up himself."** "He has no need, like those high priests, to offer sacrifices daily, first for his own sins and then for those of the people, since he did this once for all when he offered up himself" (7:27).
- **"Daily":** the Day of Atonement came once a year (Lev 16:6, 34; see Heb 9:7); some take "daily" to join the whole priestly system of daily sacrifice with the high priest (10:11), and others point out that the high priest offered a grain offering every day (Lev 6:20).
- **The priest is the sacrifice:** on the cross, the one who offered and the one offered are the same person. "He himself bore our sins" (1 Pet 2:24); "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (2 Cor 5:21). The ground on which God declares us righteous is not our works, nor how deep our repentance is, but this one perfect sacrifice he offered in our place; we receive that righteousness freely, by faith (Rom 3:22–25).
- **"He did this":** the work needs no repeating, nor topping up by our incense or merit-making. Roman Catholic teaching holds that the Mass does not repeat the sacrifice of the cross but makes that one sacrifice present, so that they are one single sacrifice; the Mass is offered in an unbloody manner, yet is a truly propitiatory sacrifice (Catechism of the Catholic Church 1366–1367). The Protestant tradition (such as Calvin) holds that "once for all" (9:12; 10:10) rules out understanding the Lord's Supper as a sacrifice: where sins are forgiven, "there is no longer any offering for sin" (10:18). The Supper remembers and receives the sacrifice already completed.

**3. "Made perfect forever."** "For the law appoints men in their weakness as high priests, but the word of the oath, which came later than the law, appoints a Son who has been made perfect forever" (7:28). As in 5:9, "made perfect" does not mean the Son was ever lacking, but that through suffering, death and resurrection he completed all that a high priest must do.

> The point: We need exactly this kind of high priest: holy, without sin, exalted above the heavens. What he offered was not something else but himself, bearing our sins in our place; this sacrifice was made once, and it is complete forever.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "The former priests were many in number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in office, but he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever" (7:23–24). Along my road of faith, there were people who carried me the way a priest carries his people. There was the old pastor who baptized me, my grandmother who knelt by her bed every morning and prayed for each person in the family by name, and the older student who read the Bible with me in my most lost year. Later the pastor retired, the older student moved away, and my grandmother died. On the day of her memorial service it suddenly struck me: no one will ever pray for me like that again. I carry regrets too: when my grandmother was very ill I kept saying I was busy and rarely went back; after the pastor retired I never even said thank you. Hebrews does not ask me to pretend I am not sad; rather, it tells me to "remember" those who spoke to us the word of God (13:7). I can grieve for them and give thanks for them, and I can bring my regrets to the high priest who offered himself "once for all" (7:27). For some the wound is deeper: the one who led them to faith later fell into sin, even using spiritual authority to harm others. This must not be covered up: where there is abuse or crime, reporting it to the police is right (Rom 13:4); those who were harmed need not go back into danger, and the church must not hide it to save face ("family shame must not go outside the house"). That leader's fall cannot make the gospel we heard from him false, because the ground of our salvation was never in him, but in the high priest who has never once deceived anyone who trusted him. Without noticing, I often treat these people as my covering, as if I am safe only while they are praying for me. In truth I was putting them in a place only he can stand, and laying on them a weight they could never carry. But this one "continues forever": he will not retire, will not move away, will not fall, and will never die again. I need not hope that my grandmother is watching over me from heaven; her prayers have stopped, but his intercession has not, for "he always lives to make intercession for them" (7:25). In fact my grandmother, too, could come to God only through him; she was entrusting me to a priest who cannot die. This priest is calling today to those still standing far off: "he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him." You need not wait for someone else to pray for you, and your sin is not too great for him. Bring it, repent and trust him. I only want to stop and look at him: he is holy and innocent; he is separated from sinners and exalted above the heavens, yet he gave himself for sinners. "For it was indeed fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens" (7:26). Lord Jesus, all who walked part of the way with me were your gifts; but you yourself, death can never take from me. The Father has sworn and will not change his mind: you are a priest forever. My heart rejoices in you.
