## 本章脉络

第八章分成两段。**天上的大祭司**（1-6节）：作者说，他所讲的事「第一要紧的」，就是我们有这样一位大祭司，已经坐在天上至大者宝座的右边，在主所支的真帐幕里作执事；地上的祭司所供奉的，不过是天上事的形状和影像，正如神吩咐摩西要照着在山上指示他的样式造帐幕；耶稣所得的职任更美，因为他是更美之约的中保。**新约的应许**（7-13节）：前约若没有瑕疵，就不必寻求后约；所以神藉着耶利米应许要另立新约：把律法写在人心上，作他们的神，叫他们从最小的到至大的都认识他，又不再记念他们的罪愆；既说新约，前约就旧了，渐旧渐衰的必快归无有。

第七章讲耶稣是照着麦基洗德等次的祭司，第八章把这个讲论带到一个总结：这位祭司在哪里事奉（真帐幕），凭着什么约事奉（新约）。耶利米书31:31-34是新约圣经引用最长的一段旧约经文，作者到10:16-17又引了一次。

## 深读 8:1-2 第一要紧的事

**1. 「其中第一要紧的」。** 「我们所讲的事，其中第一要紧的，就是我们有这样的大祭司，已经坐在天上至大者宝座的右边，在圣所，就是真帐幕里，作执事；这帐幕是主所支的，不是人所支的。」（8:1-2）
- **「我们有」：** 前面七章的重点就是这一句：我们有这样一位大祭司。读者想要回到旧的礼仪那里，好像离开了这些就一无所有；作者却说，你们有一位大祭司。

**2. 「已经坐在……宝座的右边」。**
- **祭司坐下了：** 旧约的帐幕里没有给祭司坐的椅子，祭司「天天站着事奉神」（10:11），因为工作永远做不完。基督却「献了一次永远的赎罪祭，就在神的右边坐下了」（10:12）。他坐着，表示赎罪的工作已经完成了（1:3）。
- **君王又是祭司：** 坐在宝座右边的君王（诗110:1），就是为我们代求的祭司（7:25）。

**3. 「真帐幕」。**
- **「真」：** 不是说地上的帐幕是假的，而是说天上的才是本体，地上的只是影像（9:24）。
- **「主所支的」：** 旷野的帐幕是人按神的吩咐亲手做成的；耶稣事奉的地方却「不是人手所造，也不是属乎这世界的」（9:11）。

> 要点：整卷书的重点，就是我们有这样一位大祭司：他已经坐在宝座的右边，因为赎罪的工作成了；他在神亲自预备的真帐幕里，为我们事奉。

## 深读 8:3-5 天上事的形状和影像

**1. 「这位大祭司也必须有所献的」。** 「凡大祭司都是为献礼物和祭物设立的，所以这位大祭司也必须有所献的。他若在地上，必不得为祭司，因为已经有照律法献礼物的祭司。」（8:3-4）
- **他献的是什么：** 作者在7:27已经说了：「他只一次将自己献上」（参9:14）。祭司和祭物是同一位。
- **「他若在地上，必不得为祭司」：** 按律法，只有亚伦的后裔能作祭司，而我们的主是从犹大出来的（7:14）。耶稣的祭司职分在天上。

**2. 「形状和影像」。** 「他们供奉的事本是天上事的形状和影像，正如摩西将要造帐幕的时候，蒙神警戒他，说：『你要谨慎，作各样的物件都要照着在山上指示你的样式。』」（8:5）
- **出埃及记25:40：** 神在西奈山上给摩西看了一个「样式」，帐幕要照着它造（参徒7:44）。作者从这节经文看出：地上的帐幕本身不是终点，它是照着另一样东西造的。
- **「影像」：** 影子有形状，能让人认出是什么东西投下来的，但影子本身不是那东西。旧约的帐幕、祭司和献祭都是真的，是神设立的；但它们是影子，「那形体却是基督」（西2:17）。

**3. 这种说法的背景。** 学者对作者思想的背景有不同的看法。
- **柏拉图哲学：** 犹太哲学家斐洛也用出埃及记25:40讲地上的帐幕是天上原型的仿本，带有柏拉图的色彩：看得见的世界只是永恒理念的影子。有些学者（如斯皮克）认为希伯来书受了这种思想的影响。
- **犹太启示文学：** 另一些学者（如赫斯特、雷恩）认为，背景主要是旧约和犹太启示文学：神在天上有真实的圣所（诗11:4；启11:19）。他们指出，希伯来书的影子不只是上和下的对比，更是先和后的对比：律法是「将来美事的影儿」（10:1），神是「在这末世」藉着儿子说话（1:2）。
- **许多人的看法：** 许多解经家（如布鲁斯）认为，作者可能用了当时熟悉的说法，但他的框架是救恩历史：影子指向的不是永恒的理念，而是一位来到历史中、流了自己的血、又升到天上的人。

> 要点：神设立的旧约礼仪是真的，却是影子；影子要指向本体。地上的帐幕叫人抬头看天上的圣所，也叫人往前看那位要来的基督。

## 深读 8:6-9 更美之约

**1. 「更美之约的中保」。** 「如今耶稣所得的职任是更美的，正如他作更美之约的中保；这约原是凭更美之应许立的。」（8:6）
- **「中保」：** 站在两方中间、使约成立的人。摩西把立约的血洒在百姓身上（出24:8）；耶稣立约用的是自己的血（9:12、15）。作者在7:22用的「中保」一词有担保的意思：这约有他亲自作保。
- **「更美之应许」：** 下面引的耶利米书就是这些应许。旧约也有应许，但新约的应许直接针对人心的问题，并且有罪得赦免作根基。

**2. 「那前约若没有瑕疵」。** 「那前约若没有瑕疵，就无处寻求后约了。所以主指责他的百姓说……」（8:7-8）
- **问题在哪里：** 第8节有些抄本可以读作神指责前约，然后对他们说话；多数译本（包括和合本）读作神指责他们，也就是百姓。两种读法并不冲突：约有缺欠，因为立约的百姓背了约。
- **律法本身是好的：** 「律法是圣洁的，诫命也是圣洁、公义、良善的」（罗7:12）。前约的缺欠不在于神的要求不对，而在于它不能改变人心；律法「因肉体软弱，有所不能行的」（罗8:3）。作者在7:18-19也说，先前的条例「软弱无益」，「律法原来一无所成」。

**3. 「他们不恒心守我的约」。** 「不像我拉着他们祖宗的手，领他们出埃及的时候，与他们所立的约。因为他们不恒心守我的约，我也不理他们。这是主说的。」（8:9）
- **「拉着他们祖宗的手」：** 神像父亲拉着孩子一样领他们出埃及，他们却在西奈山下就造了金牛犊。
- **和耶利米书的文字不同：** 耶利米书31:32在希伯来文是「我虽作他们的丈夫，他们却背了我的约」；希伯来书照着七十士译本，作「我也不理他们」。两种文字都说：百姓背约，就落在约的咒诅之下。
- **神没有放弃：** 百姓背约，神却应许另立新约；新约的根基不是人更忠心，而是神自己作更深的事。

> 要点：前约的问题不在于神的律法，而在于人心；人背了约，神却没有放弃，反而应许一个更美的约。这约有耶稣作中保，用他自己的血立定。

## 深读 8:10-12 新约的应许

**1. 「我要将我的律法……写在他们心上」。** 「主又说：那些日子以后，我与以色列家所立的约乃是这样：我要将我的律法放在他们里面，写在他们心上；我要作他们的神；他们要作我的子民。」（8:10）
- **一连串的「我要」：** 第10-12节里，神一次又一次地说「我要」。西奈山立约的时候，百姓说「耶和华所吩咐的，我们都必遵行」（出24:7）；新约的重点却是神要做什么。
- **从石版到心版：** 以西结也应许：「我也要赐给你们一个新心，将新灵放在你们里面」（结36:26）。律法没有被丢掉，而是圣灵叫人从心里遵行（罗8:4；林后3:6）。
- **「我要作他们的神」：** 这是圣经里立约的核心，从亚伯拉罕（创17:7）到律法（利26:12），一直到新天新地（启21:3）。

**2. 「都必认识我」。** 「他们不用各人教导自己的乡邻和自己的弟兄，说：你该认识主；因为他们从最小的到至大的，都必认识我。」（8:11）
- **不是说不需要教导：** 希伯来书自己就是一篇教导，作者也要读者「本该作师傅」（5:12）。
- **是说每个人都亲自认识神：** 旧约时代，生在立约百姓中的人，常常有整个世代「不知道耶和华」（士2:10）。新约的应许是：每一个属神的人都亲自认识他，不只是听别人说过他（参约6:45）。
- **「认识」：** 不只是知道关于他的事，而是与他有活的关系（约17:3）。

**3. 「不再记念他们的罪愆」。** 「我要宽恕他们的不义，不再记念他们的罪愆。」（8:12）
- **「因为」：** 原文第12节开头有「因为」一词（和合本没有译出）。许多解经家认为，罪得赦免是前面一切应许的根基。
- **「不再记念」：** 不是说全知的神会忘记，而是说他不再追讨这些罪（赛43:25）。
- **赦免从哪里来：** 神是公义的，他不能假装罪不存在。新约能应许完全的赦免，是因为基督替我们受了罪的刑罚：「他为我们的过犯受害，为我们的罪孽压伤」（赛53:5）。作者到第十章会说：「这些罪过既已赦免，就不用再为罪献祭了。」（10:18）罪人不是靠自己的行为进入新约，而是单单靠着信靠这位中保和他所流的血。

**4. 新约和以色列。** 这个应许是给「以色列家和犹大家」的，那么它和教会是什么关系？福音派里主要有两种看法。
- **圣约神学：** 改革宗传统（如加尔文、欧文，《威斯敏斯德信条》7.5-6）认为，新约在基督里已经实现了；在基督里的犹太人和外邦人同是一个神的子民，「你们既属乎基督，就是亚伯拉罕的后裔」（加3:29）。这个传统里也有不少人相信，将来会有许多犹太人归向基督（罗11:26-27）。
- **时代论：** 时代论传统（如司可福、沃尔沃德，以及近年的渐进时代论者如布莱辛、博克）强调，神给以色列民族的应许仍然有效（罗11:29），将来要在以色列身上应验。今天多数时代论者认为，新约已经藉着基督的血开始，教会现在就领受它属灵的福分，给以色列民族的应许还要在将来完全应验。
- **共同的地方：** 两种看法都同意：希伯来书把这段应许直接用在当时的信徒身上（10:15-18）；无论是犹太人还是外邦人，进入新约只有一条路，就是耶稣基督的血。

> 要点：新约的应许几乎每一句都以神的「我要」开头。神要把他的律法写在我们心上，要作我们的神，要叫我们亲自认识他；而这一切的根基，是他因着基督的血，不再记念我们的罪。

## 深读 8:13 渐旧渐衰的必快归无有

**1. 「就以前约为旧了」。** 「既说新约，就以前约为旧了；但那渐旧渐衰的，就必快归无有了。」（8:13）
- **旧约自己的见证：** 早在耶利米的时代，神说要立新约，就已经表示西奈的约是旧的了。
- **「旧」不是「错」：** 基督说：「我来不是要废掉，乃是要成全。」（太5:17）旧约的礼仪不是被丢掉的垃圾，而是完成了任务的影子；本体来了，影子就退下了。

**2. 「必快归无有了」：圣殿那时还在吗？**
- **有人认为是：** 作者在这里和别处（8:4；10:1-2）都用现在式讲献祭，所以许多解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为，写信的时候圣殿可能还在，也就是在主后70年以前；这样，「必快归无有」就更具体了（参太24:2）。
- **也有人认为不一定：** 另一些学者指出，作者讲的始终是旷野的帐幕，是按着旧约经文在讲；而且主后70年以后的作品，例如犹太史家约瑟夫的著作和《革利免一书》，也用现在式描写献祭。所以现在式本身不能决定年代。
- **无论哪一种：** 本体已经来了，再回到影子那里去，就是走回头路。

> 要点：神自己在旧约里就预告了旧约要过去。影子会旧，会衰残；新约却建立在已经坐在宝座右边的大祭司身上，永不过去。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他们不用各人教导自己的乡邻和自己的弟兄，说：你该认识主；因为他们从最小的到至大的，都必认识我。」（8:11）我对神的认识，有多少是我自己的？遇到大事要做决定，我第一个想到的是打电话问牧师，神的旨意是什么；家里谢饭祷告，总是我的丈夫或妻子开口；孩子生病了，我请母亲多为我们祷告，好像她的祷告比我的更算数。别人讲他们怎样经历神，我也会转述，可是一个人安静下来的时候，我却不太知道怎样和他说话。直到有一天，带领我的牧师调走了，或者那位一直为我祷告的长辈离世了，我才发现，原来我的信心一直靠在别人身上；也许我的信心就在那时冷了下来，很久没有回到他面前。撒玛利亚人对那个妇人说：「现在我们信，不是因为你的话，是我们亲自听见了，知道这真是救世主。」（约4:42）神并不轻看那些带我们来到他面前的人，希伯来书也要我们效法从前引导我们的人的信心（13:7）。可是新约的应许是：从最小的到至大的，都要亲自认识他。读这几节，最打动我的不是我要怎样努力去认识他，而是神一连说了好几次「我要」：我要将我的律法写在他们心上，我要作他们的神，我要宽恕，不再记念（8:10-12）。认识他不是我要靠自己爬上去的台阶，而是他亲口应许要给的。这个应许的根基，是耶稣的血：「这杯是用我血所立的新约，是为你们流出来的。」（路22:20）他替我受了罪的刑罚，拦在我和神中间的罪被挪去了，神才能说「不再记念他们的罪愆」（8:12）。我也要承认，我常常宁可让别人替我亲近神，因为自己到他面前，就得放下骄傲，不能再躲在别人后面；这是罪，但这罪也在他宽恕的应许之内。还没有信靠他的人，也不能靠家人或牧师的信心得救；神在新约里呼召你亲自向他认罪悔改，信靠那为罪人流血的中保；他应许不再记念你的罪。那么，我是不是一直在借别人的信心过日子？如果是，我最需要的不是先给自己定一个新计划，而是听一听这位神亲口说的话：「我要作他们的神；他们要作我的子民。」（8:10）他没有说要等我认识他够深了，才作我的神；他说的是「我要」。从对亚伯拉罕的应许（创17:7），到新天新地的那一天（启21:3），他一直这样说；今天，藉着那位坐在宝座右边的中保，他也对我这样说：我要作你的神。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 8 has two parts. **The heavenly high priest** (vv. 1–6): the author says that "the point" of what he is saying is this: we have such a high priest, seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven, a minister in the true tent that the Lord set up; the earthly priests serve only a copy and shadow of the heavenly things, just as God told Moses to build the tent according to the pattern shown him on the mountain; Jesus has obtained a more excellent ministry, because he is the mediator of a better covenant. **The promise of a new covenant** (vv. 7–13): if the first covenant had been faultless, there would have been no occasion to look for a second; so God promised through Jeremiah to make a new covenant: to write his laws on people's hearts, to be their God, to make them all know him from the least to the greatest, and to remember their sins no more; in speaking of a new covenant he makes the first one obsolete, and what is growing old is ready to vanish away.

Chapter 7 showed that Jesus is a priest after the order of Melchizedek; chapter 8 brings that argument to a summary: where this priest serves (the true tent), and under what covenant (the new covenant). Jeremiah 31:31–34 is the longest Old Testament quotation in the New Testament, and the author quotes it again in 10:16–17.

## Going Deeper: 8:1–2 The Point

**1. "Now the point in what we are saying is this."** "Now the point in what we are saying is this: we have such a high priest, one who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven, a minister in the holy places, in the true tent that the Lord set up, not man" (8:1–2).
- **"We have":** the point of the first seven chapters is this one sentence: we have such a high priest. The readers were tempted to go back to the old rituals, as if leaving them meant having nothing; the author says, you have a high priest.

**2. "Seated at the right hand of the throne."**
- **A priest who sits down:** the tabernacle had no chair for the priests. Every priest "stands daily at his service" (10:11), because the work was never finished. But "when Christ had offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins, he sat down at the right hand of God" (10:12). His sitting shows that the work of atonement is complete (1:3).
- **King and priest:** the king seated at the right hand of the throne (Ps 110:1) is the priest who intercedes for us (7:25).

**3. "The true tent."**
- **"True":** not that the earthly tabernacle was false, but that the heavenly one is the reality and the earthly one only a copy (9:24).
- **"That the Lord set up":** the wilderness tabernacle was made by human hands at God's command; the place where Jesus serves is "not made with hands, that is, not of this creation" (9:11).

> The point: The point of the whole book is that we have such a high priest: he is seated at the right hand of the throne because the work of atonement is finished, and he serves for us in the true tent that God himself set up.

## Going Deeper: 8:3–5 A Copy and Shadow of the Heavenly Things

**1. "It is necessary for this priest also to have something to offer."** "For every high priest is appointed to offer gifts and sacrifices; thus it is necessary for this priest also to have something to offer. Now if he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all, since there are priests who offer gifts according to the law" (8:3–4).
- **What does he offer?** The author has already said it in 7:27: "he did this once for all when he offered up himself" (see 9:14). The priest and the sacrifice are the same person.
- **"If he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all":** under the law only Aaron's descendants could be priests, and our Lord was descended from Judah (7:14). Jesus' priesthood is in heaven.

**2. "A copy and shadow."** "They serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things. For when Moses was about to erect the tent, he was instructed by God, saying, 'See that you make everything according to the pattern that was shown you on the mountain'" (8:5).
- **Exodus 25:40:** on Mount Sinai God showed Moses a "pattern," and the tabernacle was to be built according to it (see Acts 7:44). From this verse the author sees that the earthly tabernacle was not an end in itself; it was made after something else.
- **"Shadow":** a shadow has a shape, so you can tell what cast it, but the shadow is not the thing itself. The tabernacle, priests and sacrifices of the Old Testament were real, and God appointed them; but they were shadows, and "the substance belongs to Christ" (Col 2:17).

**3. The background of this language.** Scholars disagree about the background of the author's thought.
- **Platonic philosophy:** the Jewish philosopher Philo also used Exodus 25:40 to speak of the earthly tabernacle as a copy of a heavenly original, with a Platonic coloring: the visible world is only a shadow of eternal ideas. Some scholars (such as Spicq) think Hebrews was influenced by this way of thinking.
- **Jewish apocalyptic:** other scholars (such as Hurst and Lane) think the background is mainly the Old Testament and Jewish apocalyptic writing: God has a real sanctuary in heaven (Ps 11:4; Rev 11:19). They point out that the shadow in Hebrews is not only a contrast of above and below but even more of before and after: the law is "a shadow of the good things to come" (10:1), and God has spoken by his Son "in these last days" (1:2).
- **Where many land:** many interpreters (such as Bruce) think the author may use language familiar in his day, but his framework is the history of salvation: the shadow points not to an eternal idea but to a man who came into history, shed his own blood, and ascended into heaven.

> The point: The Old Testament worship God appointed was real, but it was a shadow, and a shadow points to the reality. The earthly tabernacle taught people to look up to the heavenly sanctuary, and to look ahead to the Christ who was to come.

## Going Deeper: 8:6–9 A Better Covenant

**1. "The covenant he mediates is better."** "But as it is, Christ has obtained a ministry that is as much more excellent than the old as the covenant he mediates is better, since it is enacted on better promises" (8:6).
- **"Mediator":** one who stands between two parties so that a covenant is made. Moses threw the blood of the covenant on the people (Exod 24:8); the blood Jesus uses is his own (9:12, 15). In 7:22 he is called the "guarantor": he himself stands surety for this covenant.
- **"Better promises":** these are the promises in the Jeremiah passage that follows. The old covenant had promises too, but the new covenant's promises go straight to the problem of the human heart, and they rest on the forgiveness of sins.

**2. "If that first covenant had been faultless."** "For if that first covenant had been faultless, there would have been no occasion to look for a second. For he finds fault with them when he says..." (8:7–8).
- **Where was the fault?** Some manuscripts of verse 8 can be read as God finding fault with the first covenant and then speaking to them; most translations (the CUV too) read it as God finding fault with them, that is, the people. The readings do not conflict: the covenant was lacking because the people who entered it broke it.
- **The law itself is good:** "the law is holy, and the commandment is holy and righteous and good" (Rom 7:12). The weakness of the first covenant was not that God's demands were wrong, but that it could not change the heart; it was "weakened by the flesh" (Rom 8:3). In 7:18–19 the author also says the former commandment was set aside "because of its weakness and uselessness (for the law made nothing perfect)."

**3. "They did not continue in my covenant."** "Not like the covenant that I made with their fathers on the day when I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt. For they did not continue in my covenant, and so I showed no concern for them, declares the Lord" (8:9).
- **"I took them by the hand":** God led them out of Egypt as a father leads a child, yet at the foot of Sinai they made the golden calf.
- **A different wording from Jeremiah:** in the Hebrew of Jeremiah 31:32 the line reads, "my covenant that they broke, though I was their husband"; Hebrews follows the Septuagint: "and so I showed no concern for them." Both say that the people broke the covenant and came under its curse.
- **God did not give up:** the people broke the covenant, yet God promised a new one; its foundation is not that people will be more faithful, but that God himself will do something deeper.

> The point: The problem with the first covenant was not God's law but the human heart. The people broke the covenant, but God did not give up on them; he promised a better covenant, with Jesus as its mediator, made in his own blood.

## Going Deeper: 8:10–12 The Promises of the New Covenant

**1. "I will put my laws into their minds, and write them on their hearts."** "For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the Lord: I will put my laws into their minds, and write them on their hearts, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people" (8:10).
- **A string of "I will":** in verses 10–12 God says "I will" again and again. When the covenant was made at Sinai, the people said, "All that the LORD has spoken we will do, and we will be obedient" (Exod 24:7); the new covenant is about what God will do.
- **From tablets of stone to the heart:** Ezekiel promises too: "I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you" (Ezek 36:26). The law is not thrown away; the Spirit makes people keep it from the heart (Rom 8:4; 2 Cor 3:6).
- **"I will be their God":** this is the heart of the covenants in Scripture, from Abraham (Gen 17:7) to the law (Lev 26:12) and on to the new heaven and new earth (Rev 21:3).

**2. "They shall all know me."** "And they shall not teach, each one his neighbor and each one his brother, saying, 'Know the Lord,' for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest" (8:11).
- **Not that teaching is unnecessary:** Hebrews itself is a work of teaching, and the author says his readers "ought to be teachers" (5:12).
- **But that each one knows God personally:** in the Old Testament, whole generations born among the covenant people "did not know the LORD" (Judg 2:10). The new covenant promises that every one of God's people will know him personally, not only have heard about him from others (see John 6:45).
- **"Know":** not only knowing facts about him but having a living relationship with him (John 17:3).

**3. "I will remember their sins no more."** "For I will be merciful toward their iniquities, and I will remember their sins no more" (8:12).
- **"For":** verse 12 begins with "for" (the CUV does not translate it). Many interpreters take forgiveness to be the ground of all the promises before it.
- **"Remember no more":** not that the all-knowing God forgets, but that he no longer holds these sins against us (Isa 43:25).
- **Where forgiveness comes from:** God is just; he cannot pretend sin does not exist. The new covenant can promise full forgiveness because Christ bore the penalty of sin in our place: "he was wounded for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities" (Isa 53:5). In chapter 10 the author will say, "Where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer any offering for sin" (10:18). Sinners do not enter the new covenant by their own works, but only by trusting this mediator and the blood he shed.

**4. The new covenant and Israel.** This promise is made to "the house of Israel and the house of Judah"; how then does it relate to the church? Evangelicals hold two main views.
- **Covenant theology:** the Reformed tradition (such as Calvin, Owen, and the Westminster Confession 7.5–6) holds that the new covenant is already fulfilled in Christ; Jews and Gentiles in Christ are one people of God: "if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring" (Gal 3:29). Many in this tradition also believe that in the future many Jews will turn to Christ (see Rom 11:26–27).
- **Dispensationalism:** the dispensational tradition (such as Scofield and Walvoord, and more recently progressive dispensationalists such as Blaising and Bock) stresses that God's promises to the nation of Israel still stand (Rom 11:29) and will be fulfilled for Israel in the future. Most dispensationalists today hold that the new covenant has already begun through Christ's blood and the church receives its spiritual blessings now, while the promises to the nation of Israel await full fulfillment in the future.
- **What they share:** both views agree that Hebrews applies this promise directly to the believers of its own day (10:15–18), and that for Jew and Gentile alike there is only one way into the new covenant: the blood of Jesus Christ.

> The point: Almost every line of the new covenant promise begins with God's "I will." God will write his law on our hearts, be our God, and make us know him personally; and the ground of it all is that, because of Christ's blood, he will remember our sins no more.

## Going Deeper: 8:13 Ready to Vanish Away

**1. "He makes the first one obsolete."** "In speaking of a new covenant, he makes the first one obsolete. And what is becoming obsolete and growing old is ready to vanish away" (8:13).
- **The Old Testament's own witness:** as early as Jeremiah's day, when God spoke of a new covenant, he was already marking the Sinai covenant as old.
- **"Old" does not mean "wrong":** Christ said, "I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them" (Matt 5:17). The old covenant worship is not rubbish to be thrown out but a shadow that has finished its task; when the reality comes, the shadow steps back.

**2. "Ready to vanish away": was the temple still standing?**
- **Some say yes:** here and elsewhere (8:4; 10:1–2) the author speaks of the sacrifices in the present tense, so many interpreters (such as Bruce and Lane) think the temple may still have been standing when he wrote, that is, before AD 70; if so, "ready to vanish away" is all the more concrete (see Matt 24:2).
- **Others say not necessarily:** other scholars point out that the author always speaks of the wilderness tent, from the text of the Old Testament; and writings from after AD 70, such as those of the Jewish historian Josephus and 1 Clement, also describe the sacrifices in the present tense. So the present tense alone cannot settle the date.
- **Either way:** the reality has come, and going back to the shadow is going backwards.

> The point: God himself foretold in the Old Testament that the old covenant would pass. Shadows grow old and fade; the new covenant rests on the high priest seated at the right hand of the throne, and it will never pass away.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And they shall not teach, each one his neighbor and each one his brother, saying, 'Know the Lord,' for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest" (8:11). How much of what I know of God is my own? When I face a big decision, my first thought is to call the pastor and ask what God's will is; when we give thanks at meals, it is always my husband or wife who prays; when my child is sick, I ask my mother to pray hard for us, as if her prayers counted more than mine. When others tell how they have experienced God, I can pass their stories on; but when I am quiet and alone, I hardly know how to speak with him. Then one day the pastor who led me moves away, or the older believer who always prayed for me dies, and I discover that my faith has been leaning on someone else all along; perhaps my faith grew cold just then, and it has been a long time since I came back to him. The Samaritans said to the woman, "It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is indeed the Savior of the world" (John 4:42). God does not despise those who brought us to him; Hebrews itself tells us to imitate the faith of those who led us (13:7). But the promise of the new covenant is that all, from the least to the greatest, will know him personally. What moves me most in these verses is not how hard I must try to know him, but that God says "I will" again and again: I will write my laws on their hearts, I will be their God, I will be merciful, I will remember their sins no more (8:10–12). Knowing him is not a staircase I must climb by my own strength; it is something he has promised with his own mouth to give. The ground of this promise is the blood of Jesus: "This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20). He bore the penalty of sin in my place; the sin that stood between me and God has been taken away, and so God can say, "I will remember their sins no more" (8:12). I must also admit that I have often preferred to let others draw near to God for me, because coming to him myself means laying down my pride and no longer hiding behind someone else; this is sin, but this sin too lies within his promise of mercy. Anyone who has not yet trusted him cannot be saved by a family member's or a pastor's faith either; in the new covenant God calls you to confess your sins to him yourself, repent, and trust the mediator who shed his blood for sinners; he promises to remember your sins no more. So have I been living on borrowed faith? If I have, what I need most is not first to set myself a new plan, but to listen to what this God has said with his own mouth: "I will be their God, and they shall be my people" (8:10). He did not say he would wait until I knew him well enough before being my God; he said, "I will." From his promise to Abraham (Gen 17:7) to the day of the new heaven and new earth (Rev 21:3), he has kept saying it; and today, through the mediator seated at the right hand of the throne, he says it to me too: I will be your God.
