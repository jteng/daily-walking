## 本章脉络

第八章分成两段。**天上的大祭司**（1-6节）：我们有一位大祭司坐在天上宝座的右边，在主所支的真帐幕里作执事，地上的祭司所供奉的只是天上事的形状和影像；**新约的应许**（7-13节）：神藉着耶利米应许另立新约，要把律法写在人心上，叫人都认识他，又不再记念他们的罪，于是前约就渐旧渐衰，必快归无有。

第八章说明这位祭司在哪里事奉（真帐幕），凭着什么约事奉（新约）。耶利米书31:31-34是新约圣经引用最长的一段旧约经文，作者到10:16-17又引了一次。

## 深读 8:1-2 第一要紧的事

**1. 「其中第一要紧的」。** 「我们所讲的事，其中第一要紧的，就是我们有这样的大祭司，已经坐在天上至大者宝座的右边，在圣所，就是真帐幕里，作执事；这帐幕是主所支的，不是人所支的。」（8:1-2）
- **「我们有」：** 「第一要紧的」原文有要点、总结的意思，这里多半是指要点。作者这一大段讲论，归结到一个核心：我们有这样一位大祭司。读者想要回到旧的礼仪那里，好像离开了这些就一无所有；作者却说，你们有一位大祭司。

**2. 「已经坐在……宝座的右边」。**
- **祭司坐下了：** 地上的帐幕里没有给祭司坐的椅子，祭司「天天站着事奉神」（10:11），因为工作永远做不完。基督却「献了一次永远的赎罪祭，就在神的右边坐下了」（10:12）。他坐着，表示赎罪的工作已经完成了（1:3）。
- **君王又是祭司：** 坐在宝座右边的君王（诗110:1），就是永远为祭司、为我们代求的那一位（诗110:4；来7:25）。

**3. 「真帐幕」。**
- **「真」：** 不是说地上的帐幕是假的，而是说天上的才是本体，地上的只是影像；这帐幕是神亲自支搭的，「不是人手所造」（9:11、24）。

> 要点：我们有这样一位大祭司：他已经坐在宝座的右边，因为赎罪的工作已经完成；他在神亲自预备的真帐幕里，为我们事奉。

## 深读 8:3-5 天上事的形状和影像

**1. 「这位大祭司也必须有所献的」。** 「凡大祭司都是为献礼物和祭物设立的，所以这位大祭司也必须有所献的。他若在地上，必不得为祭司，因为已经有照律法献礼物的祭司。」（8:3-4）
- **他献的是什么：** 「他只一次将自己献上」（7:27）；祭司和祭物是同一位。
- **「他若在地上，必不得为祭司」：** 按律法，只有亚伦的后裔能作祭司，我们的主却是从犹大出来的（7:14）。他不在地上的圣所里按律法供职；他献上自己是在十字架上（9:26-28；13:12），如今在天上的圣所里执行祭司的职分。

**2. 「形状和影像」。** 「他们供奉的事本是天上事的形状和影像，正如摩西将要造帐幕的时候，蒙神警戒他，说：『你要谨慎，作各样的物件都要照着在山上指示你的样式。』」（8:5）
- **出埃及记25:40：** 帐幕要照着神在山上指示的样式造（参徒7:44）；可见地上的帐幕不是终点。
- **「影像」：** 看影子，就知道是什么东西投下的；但影子不是那东西本身。前约的帐幕、祭司和献祭是神设立的，却是影子，「那形体却是基督」（西2:17）。

**3. 这种说法的背景。** 学者对作者思想的背景有不同的看法。
- **柏拉图哲学：** 犹太哲学家斐洛也引用出埃及记25:40，用柏拉图的观念解释：地上的帐幕是天上原型的仿本；有些学者（如斯皮克）认为希伯来书受了这种思想的影响。
- **犹太启示文学：** 另一些学者（如赫斯特、雷恩）认为，背景主要是旧约圣经和犹太启示文学：神在天上有真实的圣所和宝座（诗11:4；赛6:1；王上22:19）。他们指出，希伯来书的影子不只是天上与地上的对比，更是先与后的对比：律法是「将来美事的影儿」（10:1），神「在这末世」才藉着儿子向我们说话（1:2）。
- **救恩历史的框架：** 许多解经家（如布鲁斯）认为，作者也许借用了当时读者熟悉的说法，但他的框架是救恩历史。影子指向的不是永恒的理念，而是神的儿子：他在历史中成为人，流了自己的血，又升到天上。

> 要点：神设立的前约的礼仪是真的，却是影子；影子要指向本体。地上的帐幕叫人抬头看天上的圣所，也叫人往前看那位要来的基督。

## 深读 8:6-9 更美之约

**1. 「更美之约的中保」。** 「如今耶稣所得的职任是更美的，正如他作更美之约的中保；这约原是凭更美之应许立的。」（8:6）
- **「中保」：** 站在两方中间、使约成立的人。摩西把立约的血洒在百姓身上（出24:8；参9:18-20）；耶稣立约用的是自己的血（9:14-15）。7:22的「中保」原文是另一个词，意思是担保人（参第七章）：这约有他亲自作保。
- **「更美之应许」：** 就是下面耶利米书的应许。前约也有应许，但新约的应许直接针对人心的问题，并且有罪得赦免作根基。

**2. 「那前约若没有瑕疵」。** 「那前约若没有瑕疵，就无处寻求后约了。所以主指责他的百姓说……」（8:7-8）
- **问题在哪里：** 第8节有些抄本可以读作神指责前约，然后对他们说话；多数译本（包括和合本）读作神指责他们，也就是百姓。两种读法并不冲突：约本身有缺欠，立约的百姓也背了约。
- **律法本身是好的：** 律法是圣洁的（罗7:12）；可是作者在7:18-19说，先前的条例「软弱无益」，「律法原来一无所成」：它的祭司会死，祭物不能洁净良心，也不能改变人心（罗8:3）。

**3. 「他们不恒心守我的约」。** 「不像我拉着他们祖宗的手，领他们出埃及的时候，与他们所立的约。因为他们不恒心守我的约，我也不理他们。这是主说的。」（8:9）
- **和耶利米书的文字不同：** 耶利米书31:32在希伯来文是「我虽作他们的丈夫，他们却背了我的约」；作者在圣灵的带领下，引用读者所用的希腊文圣经，就是七十士译本。那里作「我也不理他们」。希伯来文强调神虽信实如丈夫，百姓仍然背约；七十士译本则说出背约的后果：神不理他们。两种文字都说，问题出在百姓背约。
- **神却没有就此罢手：** 百姓背约，神却应许另立新约；新约的根基不是人更忠心，而是神亲自在人心里动工。

> 要点：前约的缺欠不在于神的要求不对，而在于：它的祭司会死，它的祭物不能洁净良心（9:9；10:4），它本来就是影子，不能改变背约之百姓的心。神却没有就此罢手，反而应许一个更美的约。这约有耶稣作中保，用他自己的血立定。

## 深读 8:10-12 新约的应许

**1. 「我要将我的律法……写在他们心上」。** 「主又说：那些日子以后，我与以色列家所立的约乃是这样：我要将我的律法放在他们里面，写在他们心上；我要作他们的神；他们要作我的子民。」（8:10）
- **一连串的「我要」：** 第10-12节里，神一次又一次地说「我要」。西奈山立约的时候，百姓说「耶和华所吩咐的，我们都必遵行」（出24:7）；新约的重点却是神要做什么。
- **从石版到心版：** 以西结也应许：「我也要赐给你们一个新心，将新灵放在你们里面」（结36:26）。律法没有作废，而是圣灵叫人从心里遵行（罗8:4；林后3:3、6）。写在心上的，是神圣洁的旨意；礼仪上的影子已经在基督里成全了。
- **「我要作他们的神」：** 这是圣经里立约的核心，从亚伯拉罕（创17:7）到律法（利26:12），一直到新天新地（启21:3）。

**2. 「都必认识我」。** 「他们不用各人教导自己的乡邻和自己的弟兄，说：你该认识主；因为他们从最小的到至大的，都必认识我。」（8:11）
- **每个人都亲自认识神：** 这不是说不需要教导（5:12）。在前约之下，人生在立约的百姓当中，却不一定认识神；甚至有整个世代「不知道耶和华」（士2:10）。新约的应许是：每一个属神的人都亲自认识他，不只是听别人说过他（参约6:45）。

**3. 「不再记念他们的罪愆」。** 「我要宽恕他们的不义，不再记念他们的罪愆。」（8:12）
- **「因为」：** 原文第12节开头有「因为」一词（和合本没有译出）。许多解经家认为，罪得赦免是前面一切应许的根基。
- **「不再记念」：** 不是说全知的神会忘记，而是说他不再追讨这些罪（赛43:25）。
- **赦免从哪里来：** 神是公义的；新约能应许完全的赦免，是因为基督替我们受了罪的刑罚：「他为我们的过犯受害，为我们的罪孽压伤」（赛53:5）。作者到第十章会说：「这些罪过既已赦免，就不用再为罪献祭了。」（10:18）罪人不是靠自己的行为进入新约，而是单单信靠这位中保和他所流的血。

**4. 新约和以色列。** 这个应许是给「以色列家和犹大家」的，那么它和教会是什么关系？福音派里主要有两种看法。
- **圣约神学：** 改革宗传统（如加尔文、欧文）认为，新约已经在基督里开始实现，到他再来时完全成就；在基督里的犹太人和外邦人同是一个神的子民（加3:29）。《威斯敏斯德信条》7.5-6说，恩典之约只有一个，在律法时代和福音时代有不同的施行方式（欧文则认为西奈之约是另一个约）。这个传统中也有不少人相信，将来会有许多犹太人归向基督（罗11:26-27）。
- **时代论：** 时代论传统（如司可福、沃尔沃德，以及渐进时代论者布莱辛、博克）强调，神给以色列民族的应许仍然有效（罗11:29）。今天多数时代论者认为，新约已经藉着基督的血开始，教会现在就领受它属灵的福分。给以色列民族的应许，还要在将来完全应验。
- **共同的地方：** 两种看法都同意，希伯来书把这应许用在当时的信徒身上（10:15-18）；无论犹太人或外邦人，进入新约只有藉着耶稣的血。

> 要点：新约的应许几乎每一句都以神的「我要」开头。神要把他的律法写在我们心上，要作我们的神，要叫我们亲自认识他；而这一切的根基，是他因着基督的血，不再记念我们的罪。

## 深读 8:13 渐旧渐衰的必快归无有

**1. 「就以前约为旧了」。** 「既说新约，就以前约为旧了；但那渐旧渐衰的，就必快归无有了。」（8:13）
- **「旧」不是「错」：** 早在耶利米的时代，神说要立新约，就已经表示西奈山的约是旧的了（这是旧约圣经自己的见证）。基督说：「我来不是要废掉，乃是要成全。」（太5:17）前约的礼仪不是被丢掉的垃圾，而是完成了任务的影子；本体来了，影子就退下了。
- **圣殿那时还在吗：** 这节是否说明圣殿还在，请参看本书简介。无论哪一种看法，本体已经来了，再回到影子那里去，就是走回头路。

> 要点：神自己早在旧约圣经里就预告了：前约要过去。影子会旧，会衰残；新约却建立在已经坐在宝座右边的大祭司身上，是永远的约（13:20），永不过去。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他们不用各人教导自己的乡邻和自己的弟兄，说：你该认识主；因为他们从最小的到至大的，都必认识我。」（8:11）我对神的认识，有多少是我自己的？遇到大事要做决定，我第一个想到的是打电话问牧师：神的旨意是什么？家里谢饭祷告，总是我的丈夫或妻子开口；孩子生病了，我第一个念头是请小组的人代祷。请人代祷本是好事（雅5:16），可是我自己却很少开口。直到那一年，我因为工作搬到一个陌生的城市，附近没有一间有人认识我的教会。一个人坐在租来的房间里，打开圣经，想祷告，却发现不知道该怎样自己开口；原来我的信心一直靠在别人身上。撒玛利亚人对那个妇人说：「现在我们信，不是因为你的话，是我们亲自听见了，知道这真是救世主。」（约4:42）那么，我是不是一直在借别人的信心过日子？神并不轻看那些带我们来到他面前的人（13:7）；可是新约的应许是，人人都要亲自认识他。读这几节，最打动我的不是我要怎样努力去认识神，而是神一连说了好几次「我要」：我要将我的律法写在他们心上，我要作他们的神，我要宽恕，不再记念（8:10-12）。认识神不是我要靠自己爬上去的台阶，而是神亲口应许要给的。这个应许的根基，是耶稣的血：「这杯是用我血所立的新约，是为你们流出来的。」（路22:20）他替我受了罪的刑罚，拦在我和神中间的罪已经被挪去了。我也要承认，我躲在别人后面，有时是因为怕：自己到他面前，就再没有人替我挡着，他会看见我真实的样子。这份不信也是罪。可是我怕见的那一位，正是应许「不再记念他们的罪愆」的神（8:12）。我身边没有人替我挡着，可是在神和我中间，有一位中保（提前2:5），他用自己的血替我担当了罪。如果你还没有信靠他，神今天也亲自向你说话：「你们今日若听他的话，就不可硬着心」（3:7-8）。不要再靠别人的信心，今天就亲自到他面前认罪悔改，信靠那为罪人流血的中保；他应许不再记念你的罪。我最需要的不是先学会怎样自己祷告，而是安静下来，听这位神亲口说的话：「我要作他们的神；他们要作我的子民。」（8:10）他没有说要等我认识他够深了，才作我的神；他说的是「我要」。今天，藉着那位坐在宝座右边的中保，他也对我这样说：我要作你的神。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 8 has two parts. **The heavenly high priest** (vv. 1–6): we have a high priest seated at the right hand of the throne in heaven, a minister in the true tent that the Lord set up, while the earthly priests serve only a copy and shadow of the heavenly things; **the promise of a new covenant** (vv. 7–13): through Jeremiah God promised a new covenant, to write his laws on people's hearts, to make them all know him, and to remember their sins no more, so that the first covenant is growing old and ready to vanish away.

Chapter 8 shows where this priest serves (the true tent) and under what covenant (the new covenant). Jeremiah 31:31–34 is the longest Old Testament quotation in the New Testament, and the author quotes it again in 10:16–17.

## Going Deeper: 8:1–2 The Point

**1. "Now the point in what we are saying is this."** "Now the point in what we are saying is this: we have such a high priest, one who is seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in heaven, a minister in the holy places, in the true tent that the Lord set up, not man" (8:1–2).
- **"We have":** the word for "the point" can mean "summary," but here most likely means "main point." The author's whole long argument comes down to one core: we have such a high priest. The readers were tempted to go back to the old rituals, as if leaving them meant having nothing; the author says, you have a high priest.

**2. "Seated at the right hand of the throne."**
- **A priest who sits down:** the tabernacle had no chair for the priests. Every priest "stands daily at his service" (10:11), because the work was never finished. But "when Christ had offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins, he sat down at the right hand of God" (10:12). His sitting shows that the work of atonement is complete (1:3).
- **King and priest:** the king seated at the right hand of the throne (Ps 110:1) is the priest forever who intercedes for us (Ps 110:4; Heb 7:25).

**3. "The true tent."**
- **"True":** not that the earthly tabernacle was false, but that the heavenly one is the reality and the earthly one only a copy; God set it up, "not made with hands" (9:11, 24).

> The point: We have such a high priest: he is seated at the right hand of the throne because the work of atonement is finished, and he serves for us in the true tent that God himself set up.

## Going Deeper: 8:3–5 A Copy and Shadow of the Heavenly Things

**1. "It is necessary for this priest also to have something to offer."** "For every high priest is appointed to offer gifts and sacrifices; thus it is necessary for this priest also to have something to offer. Now if he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all, since there are priests who offer gifts according to the law" (8:3–4).
- **What does he offer?** "He did this once for all when he offered up himself" (7:27); the priest and the sacrifice are the same person.
- **"If he were on earth, he would not be a priest at all":** under the law only Aaron's descendants could be priests, and our Lord was descended from Judah (7:14). He does not serve in the earthly sanctuary under the law; he offered himself on the cross (9:26–28; 13:12), and he now serves as priest in the heavenly sanctuary.

**2. "A copy and shadow."** "They serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things. For when Moses was about to erect the tent, he was instructed by God, saying, 'See that you make everything according to the pattern that was shown you on the mountain'" (8:5).
- **Exodus 25:40:** the tabernacle was to be built after the pattern God showed on the mountain (see Acts 7:44), so the earthly tabernacle was not an end in itself.
- **"Shadow":** a shadow shows what cast it but is not the thing itself. The tabernacle, priests and sacrifices of the first covenant were appointed by God, yet they were shadows, and "the substance belongs to Christ" (Col 2:17).

**3. The background of this language.** Scholars disagree about the background of the author's thought.
- **Platonic philosophy:** the Jewish philosopher Philo also used Exodus 25:40, with a Platonic coloring, to speak of the earthly tabernacle as a copy of a heavenly original; some scholars (such as Spicq) think Hebrews was influenced by this way of thinking.
- **Jewish apocalyptic:** other scholars (such as Hurst and Lane) think the background is mainly the Old Testament and Jewish apocalyptic writing: God has a real sanctuary and throne in heaven (Ps 11:4; Isa 6:1; 1 Kgs 22:19). They point out that the shadow in Hebrews is not only a contrast of above and below but even more of before and after: the law is "a shadow of the good things to come" (10:1), and God has spoken by his Son "in these last days" (1:2).
- **A salvation-historical frame:** many interpreters (such as Bruce) think the author may borrow language his readers knew, but his framework is the history of salvation. The shadow points not to an eternal idea but to the Son of God, who became man in history, shed his own blood, and ascended into heaven.

> The point: The worship of the first covenant, which God appointed, was real, but it was a shadow, and a shadow points to the reality. The earthly tabernacle taught people to look up to the heavenly sanctuary, and to look ahead to the Christ who was to come.

## Going Deeper: 8:6–9 A Better Covenant

**1. "The covenant he mediates is better."** "But as it is, Christ has obtained a ministry that is as much more excellent than the old as the covenant he mediates is better, since it is enacted on better promises" (8:6).
- **"Mediator":** one who stands between two parties so that a covenant is made. Moses threw the blood of the covenant on the people (Exod 24:8; see 9:18–20); the blood Jesus uses is his own (9:14–15). In 7:22 a different word is used, "guarantor" (see chapter 7): he himself stands surety for this covenant.
- **"Better promises":** the promises of the Jeremiah passage that follows. The first covenant had promises too, but the new covenant's promises go straight to the problem of the human heart, and they rest on the forgiveness of sins.

**2. "If that first covenant had been faultless."** "For if that first covenant had been faultless, there would have been no occasion to look for a second. For he finds fault with them when he says..." (8:7–8).
- **Where was the fault?** Some manuscripts of verse 8 can be read as God finding fault with the first covenant and then speaking to them; most translations (the CUV too) read it as God finding fault with them, that is, the people. The readings do not conflict: the covenant itself was lacking, and the people who entered it broke it.
- **The law itself is good:** the law is holy (Rom 7:12); yet in 7:18–19 the author says the former commandment was set aside "because of its weakness and uselessness (for the law made nothing perfect)": its priests died, its sacrifices could not cleanse the conscience, and it could not change the heart (Rom 8:3).

**3. "They did not continue in my covenant."** "Not like the covenant that I made with their fathers on the day when I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt. For they did not continue in my covenant, and so I showed no concern for them, declares the Lord" (8:9).
- **A different wording from Jeremiah:** in the Hebrew of Jeremiah 31:32 the line reads, "my covenant that they broke, though I was their husband"; the author, under the Spirit's guidance, cites the Greek Bible his readers used (the Septuagint): "and so I showed no concern for them." The Hebrew stresses that although God was faithful as a husband, the people broke the covenant; the Septuagint states the consequence: God disregarded them. Both locate the problem in the people's breach.
- **Yet God did not leave it there:** the people broke the covenant, yet God promised a new one; its foundation is not that people will be more faithful, but God himself working in the heart.

> The point: The first covenant's lack was not that God's demands were wrong, but that its priests died, its sacrifices could not cleanse the conscience (9:9; 10:4), it was by design a shadow, and it could not change the hearts of the people who broke it. Yet God did not leave it there; he promised a better covenant, with Jesus as its mediator, made in his own blood.

## Going Deeper: 8:10–12 The Promises of the New Covenant

**1. "I will put my laws into their minds, and write them on their hearts."** "For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the Lord: I will put my laws into their minds, and write them on their hearts, and I will be their God, and they shall be my people" (8:10).
- **A string of "I will":** in verses 10–12 God says "I will" again and again. When the covenant was made at Sinai, the people said, "All that the LORD has spoken we will do, and we will be obedient" (Exod 24:7); the new covenant is about what God will do.
- **From tablets of stone to the heart:** Ezekiel promises too: "I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you" (Ezek 36:26). The law is not thrown away; the Spirit makes people keep it from the heart (Rom 8:4; 2 Cor 3:3, 6). What is written on the heart is God's holy will; the ceremonial shadows are fulfilled in Christ.
- **"I will be their God":** this is the heart of the covenants in Scripture, from Abraham (Gen 17:7) to the law (Lev 26:12) and on to the new heaven and new earth (Rev 21:3).

**2. "They shall all know me."** "And they shall not teach, each one his neighbor and each one his brother, saying, 'Know the Lord,' for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest" (8:11).
- **Each one knows God personally:** this does not mean teaching is unnecessary (5:12). Under the first covenant, people were born among the covenant people yet did not necessarily know God; even a whole generation arose that "did not know the LORD" (Judg 2:10). The new covenant promises that every one of God's people will know him personally, not only have heard about him from others (see John 6:45).

**3. "I will remember their sins no more."** "For I will be merciful toward their iniquities, and I will remember their sins no more" (8:12).
- **"For":** verse 12 begins with "for" (the CUV does not translate it). Many interpreters take forgiveness to be the ground of all the promises before it.
- **"Remember no more":** not that the all-knowing God forgets, but that he no longer holds these sins against us (Isa 43:25).
- **Where forgiveness comes from:** God is just; the new covenant can promise full forgiveness because Christ bore the penalty of sin in our place: "he was wounded for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities" (Isa 53:5). In chapter 10 the author will say, "Where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer any offering for sin" (10:18). Sinners do not enter the new covenant by their own works, but only by trusting this mediator and the blood he shed.

**4. The new covenant and Israel.** This promise is made to "the house of Israel and the house of Judah"; how then does it relate to the church? Evangelicals hold two main views.
- **Covenant theology:** the Reformed tradition (such as Calvin and Owen) holds that the new covenant is already inaugurated in Christ and will be consummated at his return; Jews and Gentiles in Christ are one people of God (Gal 3:29). The Westminster Confession (7.5–6) speaks of one covenant of grace under different administrations in the time of the law and of the gospel (Owen, by contrast, held Sinai to be a distinct covenant). Many in this tradition also believe that in the future many Jews will turn to Christ (Rom 11:26–27).
- **Dispensationalism:** the dispensational tradition (such as Scofield and Walvoord, and the progressive dispensationalists Blaising and Bock) stresses that God's promises to the nation of Israel still stand (Rom 11:29). Most dispensationalists today hold that the new covenant has already begun through Christ's blood and that the church receives its spiritual blessings now. The promises to the nation of Israel await full fulfillment in the future.
- **What they share:** both agree that Hebrews applies this promise to the believers of its own day (10:15–18), and that Jew and Gentile alike enter the new covenant only through the blood of Jesus.

> The point: Almost every line of the new covenant promise begins with God's "I will." God will write his law on our hearts, be our God, and make us know him personally; and the ground of it all is that, because of Christ's blood, he will remember our sins no more.

## Going Deeper: 8:13 Ready to Vanish Away

**1. "He makes the first one obsolete."** "In speaking of a new covenant, he makes the first one obsolete. And what is becoming obsolete and growing old is ready to vanish away" (8:13).
- **"Old" does not mean "wrong":** as early as Jeremiah's day, when God spoke of a new covenant, he was already marking the Sinai covenant as old (the Old Testament Scriptures' own witness). Christ said, "I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them" (Matt 5:17). The worship of the first covenant is not rubbish to be thrown out but a shadow that has finished its task; when the reality comes, the shadow steps back.
- **Was the temple still standing?** On whether this implies the temple was still standing, see the introduction. Either way, the reality has come, and going back to the shadow is going backwards.

> The point: God himself foretold in the Old Testament Scriptures that the first covenant would pass. Shadows grow old and fade; the new covenant rests on the high priest seated at the right hand of the throne; it is the "eternal covenant" (13:20) and will never pass away.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And they shall not teach, each one his neighbor and each one his brother, saying, 'Know the Lord,' for they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest" (8:11). How much of what I know of God is my own? When I face a big decision, my first thought is to call the pastor: what is God's will? When we give thanks at meals, it is always my husband or wife who prays; when my child is sick, my first thought is to ask my small group to pray. Asking others to pray is good (Jas 5:16), but I myself rarely spoke to him at all. Then one year work took me to a strange city, with no church nearby where anyone knew me. Sitting alone in a rented room with my Bible open, I tried to pray and found I did not know how to begin on my own; my faith had been leaning on someone else all along. The Samaritans said to the woman, "It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is indeed the Savior of the world" (John 4:42). So have I been living on borrowed faith? God does not despise those who brought us to him (13:7); but the promise of the new covenant is that all will know him personally. What moves me most in these verses is not how hard I must try to know God, but that God says "I will" again and again: I will write my laws on their hearts, I will be their God, I will be merciful, I will remember their sins no more (8:10–12). Knowing God is not a staircase I must climb by my own strength; it is something God has promised with his own mouth to give. The ground of this promise is the blood of Jesus: "This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20). He bore the penalty of sin in my place, and the sin that stood between me and God has been taken away. I must also admit that I hide behind others partly out of fear: if I come to him myself, no one stands between us, and he will see me as I really am. That unbelief is sin too. Yet the One I am afraid to face is the God who has promised, "I will remember their sins no more" (8:12). No one beside me shields me, yet between God and me there stands one Mediator (1 Tim 2:5), who bore my sin in his own blood. If you have not yet trusted him, God is speaking to you himself today: "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts" (3:7–8). Stop leaning on someone else's faith; come to him yourself today, repent, and trust the mediator who shed his blood for sinners; he promises to remember your sins no more. What I need most is not first to learn how to pray on my own, but to be still and listen to what this God has said with his own mouth: "I will be their God, and they shall be my people" (8:10). He did not say he would wait until I knew him well enough before being my God; he said, "I will." Today, through the mediator seated at the right hand of the throne, he says it to me too: I will be your God.
