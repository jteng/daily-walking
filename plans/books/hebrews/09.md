## 本章脉络

第九章分成四段。**地上的帐幕**（1-10节）：前约有属世界的圣幕，头一层是圣所，第二幔子后是至圣所；众祭司常进头一层，惟有大祭司一年一次进入第二层，而且必须带着血；圣灵藉此指明，进入至圣所的路还未显明；这些礼物和祭物都不能叫礼拜的人良心得以完全。**基督用自己的血进入**（11-14节）：基督经过更大更全备的帐幕，用自己的血只一次进入圣所，成了永远赎罪的事；他藉着永远的灵将自己无瑕无疵献给神，他的血更能洗净我们的心，使我们事奉永生神。**新约的中保**（15-22节）：他受死作了新约的中保；遗命要等立遗命的人死了才有效；前约也是用血立的；若不流血，罪就不得赦免。**一次显现，第二次显现**（23-28节）：天上的本物要用更美的祭物洁净；基督进了天堂，如今为我们显在神面前；他在末世显现一次，把自己献为祭，好除掉罪；人人都有一死，死后且有审判；基督一次被献，担当了多人的罪，将来要第二次显现，乃是为拯救等候他的人。

第八章讲了地上的帐幕是天上事的影像（8:5），也讲了新约的应许（8:8-12）。第九章接着讲：那影像里到底有什么，大祭司在里面做什么，基督又怎样完成了那影像所指向的事。全章的中心是「血」：没有血，大祭司不能进去；没有血，约不能成立；没有流血，罪不得赦免。而基督所带的，是他自己的血。

## 深读 9:1-10 头一层帐幕仍存的时候

**1. 「属世界的圣幕」。** 「原来前约有礼拜的条例和属世界的圣幕。因为有预备的帐幕，头一层叫作圣所，里面有灯台、桌子，和陈设饼。第二幔子后又有一层帐幕，叫作至圣所，有金香炉，有包金的约柜，柜里有盛吗哪的金罐和亚伦发过芽的杖，并两块约版；柜上面有荣耀基路伯的影罩着施恩座。」（9:1-5）
- **作者讲的是帐幕，不是圣殿：** 他回到旷野的会幕，也就是摩西照着山上的样式造的（8:5）。
- **「金香炉」：** 这个词可以指香坛，也可以指香炉。英文标准译本作香坛；和合本作香炉。按出埃及记，香坛在幔子外面，「对着法柜上的施恩座」（出30:6）。有人认为这里是指大祭司在赎罪日带进幔内的香炉（利16:12-13）；也有人认为是指香坛，作者说至圣所「有」香坛，是说它属于至圣所，和至圣所的事奉连在一起，列王纪也说这坛是「内殿前的坛」（王上6:22），赎罪日也要在它的角上抹血（出30:10）。两种看法都不说作者弄错了位置。
- **约柜里的东西：** 吗哪和亚伦的杖原是放在「耶和华面前」「法柜前」（出16:33；民17:10）；到所罗门的时候，「约柜里惟有两块石版」（王上8:9）。作者描写的是旷野时期的会幕。
- **「这几件我现在不能一一细说」：** 作者不想讲家具，他要讲的是：谁能进去，怎样进去。

**2. 「一年一次独自进去，没有不带着血」。** 「众祭司就常进头一层帐幕，行拜神的礼。至于第二层帐幕，惟有大祭司一年一次独自进去，没有不带着血为自己和百姓的过错献上。」（9:6-7）
- **只有一个人，一年一次：** 在赎罪日，大祭司「不可随时进圣所的幔子内」，免得他死亡（利16:2）；他一年只进去一次（利16:34），先为自己，后为百姓弹血在施恩座上（利16:14-15）。
- **「没有不带着血」：** 罪人不能空手来到圣洁的神面前。要接近神，必须有一条命替人死。这是全章的主线。

**3. 「进入至圣所的路还未显明」。** 「圣灵用此指明，头一层帐幕仍存的时候，进入至圣所的路还未显明。」（9:8）
- **圣灵藉着经文说话：** 和3:7一样，作者相信圣灵藉着旧约的条例本身说话：那道幔子、那「一年一次」，都在说「路还没有开」。
- **「头一层帐幕」：** 许多人认为是指外面的圣所，它挡在百姓和至圣所之间，代表整个前约的敬拜制度；也有人从下一节的「现今的一个表样」看，认为作者已经把「头一层」扩大为整个前约的时代。两种看法的意思相近：只要这个制度还是神和人之间的安排，路就没有完全打开。
- **路开了：** 耶稣死的时候，「殿里的幔子从上到下裂为两半」（可15:38）；他「给我们开了一条又新又活的路，从幔子经过，这幔子就是他的身体」（10:20）。

**4. 「就着良心说，都不能叫礼拜的人得以完全」。** 「那头一层帐幕作现今的一个表样，所献的礼物和祭物，就着良心说，都不能叫礼拜的人得以完全。这些事，连那饮食和诸般洗濯的规矩，都不过是属肉体的条例，命定到振兴的时候为止。」（9:9-10）
- **良心：** 前约的祭物能使人在礼仪上洁净，可以再进会众中敬拜，但不能触到人里面那个知道自己有罪的地方。所以这些祭物年年献，也年年「叫人想起罪来」（10:3）。
- **「属肉体的条例」：** 不是说这些条例不好，它们是神设立的；而是说它们处理的是外面的事：吃什么、怎样洗。
- **「振兴的时候」：** 意思是把一切重新摆正的时候，就是基督来了以后的新约时代。

> 要点：地上的帐幕每一处都在说：神是圣洁的，罪人不能随意进到他面前；就是大祭司，也必须带着血，一年只进去一次。这些祭物能洁净身体，却不能洁净良心；路还没有开，要等一位更大的大祭司来。

## 深读 9:11-14 他的血岂不更能洗净你们的心

**1. 「将来美事的大祭司」。** 「但现在基督已经来到，作了将来美事的大祭司，经过那更大更全备的帐幕，不是人手所造，也不是属乎这世界的。」（9:11）
- **「将来」还是「已经来到」：** 古抄本在这里有两种读法：一种是「将来的美事」（和合本照这种读法），一种是「已经来到的美事」（英文标准译本照这种读法）。许多学者认为后者是较早的读法，但两种读法都合乎全书：美事已经在基督里来到，又要在他第二次显现时完全成就（9:28；10:1）。
- **「更大更全备的帐幕」：** 多数解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为，是指天上真实的圣所，基督「升入高天」（4:14）进到父面前；加尔文和一些古代教父则认为是指基督的身体，他就是神与人相会的真殿（约2:21；10:20）。无论哪一种，重点都是：这帐幕「不是人手所造」，基督的事奉不在地上的会幕里。

**2. 「乃用自己的血，只一次进入圣所」。** 「并且不用山羊和牛犊的血，乃用自己的血，只一次进入圣所，成了永远赎罪的事。」（9:12）
- **「用自己的血」：** 不必理解为他把血带进天上，而是说他进去的凭据是他自己的死，他舍了自己的命。大祭司带的是别的活物的血，基督带的是他自己。
- **「只一次」：** 第七章已经讲过这个词（7:27）；这里作者把它和「进入」连在一起：他进去了，就不需要再出来重新献祭。
- **「永远赎罪的事」：** 「赎」这个词有付赎价、使人得释放的意思。人子来，是「要舍命作多人的赎价」（可10:45）。这赎罪是「永远」的，不像赎罪日的果效只管一年。

**3. 「母牛犊的灰」。** 「若山羊和公牛的血，并母牛犊的灰，洒在不洁的人身上，尚且叫人成圣，身体洁净……」（9:13）
- **红母牛：** 按民数记19章，红母牛烧成灰，调成「除污秽的水」（民19:9），洒在摸了死尸的人身上。摸了死尸的人「必七天不洁净」（民19:11）。
- **「何况」：** 作者用由小推大的论证：神设立的影子尚且有洁净的功用，那本体岂不更能吗？

**4. 「藉着永远的灵」。** 「何况基督藉着永远的灵，将自己无瑕无疵献给神……」（9:14）
- **圣灵：** 许多解经家（如欧文、布鲁斯、雷恩）认为，「永远的灵」是指圣灵。以赛亚书预言的仆人，神「已将我的灵赐给他」（赛42:1；参61:1）；基督在圣灵里一生顺服，也藉着圣灵把自己献上。这样，父、子、圣灵都参与在这献祭中。
- **他自己的灵：** 另一些解经家（如韦斯科特）认为，这是指基督自己永远的灵，就是他属神的生命，或他甘心献上自己的内心，所以他的献祭有永远的价值，不像牲畜的献祭只是身体上的。
- **共同点：** 两种看法都同意，这献祭不是一时的，而是有永远的果效；他是甘心献上自己，不是被迫的。
- **「无瑕无疵」：** 旧约的祭牲要没有残疾；基督是「无瑕疵、无玷污的羔羊」（彼前1:19）。他没有自己的罪要赎，所以他的死是替别人死的。神「使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义」（林后5:21）；他「亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24）。

**5. 「洗净你们的心，除去你们的死行」。** 「……他的血岂不更能洗净你们的心，除去你们的死行，使你们事奉那永生神吗？」（9:14）
- **「心」：** 和合本在这里作「心」，原文是「良心」，和9:9是同一个词。旧约的祭物「就着良心说」不能叫人完全，基督的血却能。有些抄本作「你们的」，有些作「我们的」，意思一样。
- **「死行」：** 和6:1的「懊悔死行」同一个说法。多数解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为是指引向死亡的罪行；在红母牛的背景下，罪像死尸一样叫人污秽，不能来到神面前。另一些人（如欧文）认为，这也包括人还在罪中死着的时候所行的一切，连宗教上的事在内；从9:9-10的上下文看，人想靠礼仪和行为洁净自己，也是不能给人生命的「死行」。两种看法都同意，只有基督的血能洗净良心。
- **「使你们事奉那永生神」：** 洗净的目的是事奉，而且是事奉一位「永生」的神。这里的「事奉」也有敬拜的意思。不是死的行为，而是活人事奉活的神；就像帖撒罗尼迦人「离弃偶像，归向神，要服事那又真又活的神」（帖前1:9）。

> 要点：牲畜的血只能洁净身体，基督的血却能洗净良心。他是无罪的，藉着永远的灵把自己献给神，替我们担当了罪。良心被他洗净的人，就从死行里被释放出来，去事奉永生的神。

## 深读 9:15-22 若不流血，罪就不得赦免

**1. 「他作了新约的中保」。** 「为此，他作了新约的中保，既然受死赎了人在前约之时所犯的罪过，便叫蒙召之人得着所应许永远的产业。」（9:15）
- **「中保」：** 第八章已经讲过（8:6）。这里作者补充：他作中保，是藉着「受死」。
- **「赎了人在前约之时所犯的罪过」：** 前约的祭物不能除罪（10:4），那么旧约的信徒怎样得救呢？基督的死也向后生效。神「用忍耐的心宽容人先时所犯的罪」（罗3:25），因为他看着十字架。旧约的圣徒「若不与我们同得，就不能完全」（11:40）。
- **「永远的产业」：** 新约不只赦罪，也带来产业。迦南地是影子，真正的产业是永远的。

**2. 「遗命」。** 「凡有遗命必须等到留遗命的人死了；因为人死了，遗命才有效力，若留遗命的尚在，那遗命还有用处吗？」（9:16-17）
- **一个词，两个意思：** 「约」和「遗命」在原文是同一个词。前后文都讲「约」（9:15、18），这两节却讲人死了才生效，像遗嘱一样。
- **遗嘱的读法：** 和合本、英文标准译本和多数译本都译作遗命或遗嘱。许多解经家（如布鲁斯、阿特里奇）认为，作者在这里借用了这个词的另一个意思：遗嘱要等立遗嘱的人死了才生效；照样，基督死了，我们才承受产业（9:15）。
- **约的读法：** 另一些解经家（如韦斯科特、雷恩、哈恩）认为，这里仍然是讲「约」：古代立约要宰杀牲畜，表示背约的人当受死亡的咒诅（创15:9-18；耶34:18）；以色列人背了约，基督就替背约的人承受了这咒诅。
- **共同点：** 两种读法都说，新约要藉着死才生效，而那死是基督的死。

**3. 「这血就是神与你们立约的凭据」。** 「所以，前约也不是不用血立的；因为摩西当日照着律法将各样诫命传给众百姓，就拿朱红色绒和牛膝草，把牛犊山羊的血和水洒在书上，又洒在众百姓身上，说：『这血就是神与你们立约的凭据。』他又照样把血洒在帐幕和各样器皿上。」（9:18-21）
- **出埃及记24章：** 摩西将血洒在百姓身上，说：「你看！这是立约的血。」（出24:8）出埃及记那里没有提水、朱红色绒和牛膝草，也没有提洒书和洒帐幕；作者可能把旧约其他的洁净礼（民19:6、18）和当时犹太人的传统合在一起，说明前约的一切都要用血洁净。
- **主的晚餐：** 耶稣设立圣餐时，用的正是这句话：「这是我立约的血，为多人流出来，使罪得赦。」（太26:28）摩西洒的是牲畜的血，主给我们的是他自己的血。

**4. 「若不流血，罪就不得赦免了」。** 「按着律法，凡物差不多都是用血洁净的；若不流血，罪就不得赦免了。」（9:22）
- **「差不多」：** 律法也有例外，例如穷人献不起斑鸠，可以献细面作赎罪祭（利5:11）；但原则是用血。
- **为什么是血：** 「因为活物的生命是在血中。我把这血赐给你们，可以在坛上为你们的生命赎罪。」（利17:11）流血就是交出生命。罪的工价是死；赦罪不是神装作没有看见，而是有一条命替罪人死了。
- **神为什么不能只说「我赦免」：** 神是公义的，不能把罪当作没有发生；神又是慈爱的，愿意赦免。在十字架上，两样都显明了：神设立耶稣作挽回祭，「使人知道他自己为义，也称信耶稣的人为义」（罗3:26）。

> 要点：新约是用血立的，也是用血生效的。赦罪从来不是便宜的：若不流血，罪就不得赦免。神没有降低他的公义，而是让他的儿子替我们流血；所以凡信靠他的，罪就真的得赦免了。

## 深读 9:23-28 第二次显现，并与罪无关

**1. 「那天上的本物」。** 「照着天上样式做的物件必须用这些祭物去洁净；但那天上的本物自然当用更美的祭物去洁净。」（9:23）
- **天上需要洁净吗：** 这是难解的一句，大致有三种看法。加尔文和布鲁斯认为，天上本身没有污秽，要洁净的是神的百姓，就是神的家（3:6），使我们的良心被洒去（10:22），可以进到神面前。另一些人（如雷恩）从上文的立约（9:18-21）来看，认为这是指天上圣所为新约的敬拜被奉献、开启，好像会幕起初被分别为圣一样（出40:9）。还有人从利未记16:16看，赎罪日也为圣所赎罪，因为它在百姓的污秽之中；人的罪在神面前造成的拦阻是真实的，要藉着基督的血除去。
- **共同点：** 不论哪一种看法，都是基督的血打开了罪人到神面前的路。

**2. 「如今为我们显在神面前」。** 「因为基督并不是进了人手所造的圣所（这不过是真圣所的影像），乃是进了天堂，如今为我们显在神面前；也不是多次将自己献上，像那大祭司每年带着牛羊的血进入圣所，如果这样，他从创世以来，就必多次受苦了。但如今在这末世显现一次，把自己献为祭，好除掉罪。」（9:24-26）
- **「进了天堂」：** 不是进到影像里，而是进到神自己面前。
- **「为我们」：** 他在那里不是为他自己，而是代表我们（7:25）。
- **「显现一次」：** 如果他的祭需要重复，他就必须从创世以来一次又一次地受苦。可是他只来了一次，就在「这末世」，也就是神藉着儿子说话的时候（1:2）；他来了，「把自己献为祭，好除掉罪」。「除掉」这个词有废除、使其无效的意思。

**3. 「人人都有一死，死后且有审判」。** 「按着定命，人人都有一死，死后且有审判。」（9:27）
- **只有一次：** 人只死一次，死后不是轮回转世，再来一次机会；人死了，也不能靠活人烧纸、超度或为亡者祷告改变结局。经上讲到的例外，如拉撒路被叫活、主再来时还活着的人「都要改变」（林前15:51），都不改变这个原则：每个人的一生只有一次，然后要站在神面前。
- **对还没有信主的人：** 这句话很严肃，却也是一个邀请。审判是真实的，但那位要审判人的，就是「一次被献，担当了多人的罪」的基督（9:28）。今天还有机会：不要靠功德或善行，而要向神认罪悔改，信靠这位替罪人死的救主。

**4. 「将来要……第二次显现」。** 「像这样，基督既然一次被献，担当了多人的罪，将来要向那等候他的人第二次显现，并与罪无关，乃是为拯救他们。」（9:28）
- **「担当了多人的罪」：** 这句话出自以赛亚书53:12，「他却担当多人的罪，又为罪犯代求」。他担当，是把我们的罪放在自己身上，替我们受了刑罚。人死后要受审判；基督却在十字架上替信靠他的人受了那审判。
- **百姓在外面等：** 赎罪日，大祭司进了至圣所，百姓在外面等他出来（利16:17、24）。撒迦利亚在殿里烧香时，「百姓等候撒迦利亚，诧异他许久在殿里」（路1:21）。我们的大祭司进了天上的圣所，也要从那里出来。
- **「并与罪无关」：** 他第二次来，不是再来处理罪，因为罪的问题在第一次已经解决了；他来是要完成救恩，使我们的「身体得赎」（罗8:23）。
- **「等候他的人」：** 信徒不是害怕他来，而是盼望他来，「等候救主，就是主耶稣基督从天上降临」（腓3:20）。

> 要点：基督只一次被献，就除掉了罪；人只死一次，死后有审判，而他已经替信靠他的人担当了罪。他还要第二次显现，不是为了罪，而是为了拯救等候他的人。我们可以带着盼望，而不是带着惧怕，等候他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「何况基督藉着永远的灵，将自己无瑕无疵献给神，他的血岂不更能洗净你们的心，除去你们的死行，使你们事奉那永生神吗？」（9:14）十几年前，我在公司里出了一个错，账目对不上，我没有承认，让一位同事背了责任。他被辞退了，后来听说他家里因此过得很难。这件事我早就向神认过罪，也读过「若不流血，罪就不得赦免了」（9:22），可是它一直压在那里。我的办法是多做一点。教会缺人，我从不推辞：主日一早来摆椅子，散会后留下来洗碗，又带儿童主日学，又跟着去探访。别人说我「真有爱心」，我心里却明白，我是在补。每多做一件事，那件事好像就轻一点；哪个主日没有服事，它又压回来。我从来没有说出口，心里却一直在问：这样够了吗？希伯来书说，前约的礼物和祭物「就着良心说，都不能叫礼拜的人得以完全」（9:9），只能年年「叫人想起罪来」（10:3）。我的服事也成了这样的祭：天天献上，天天提醒我还欠多少。这样的事奉，做得再多，也是「死行」，因为我服事的不是一位活的神，而是一本还不清的账。我也要承认，我拼命服事，不只是因为愧疚，也是因为骄傲：我宁可自己还，也不愿意白白领受。这也是罪。可是那件事所需要的血，已经流出来了。基督「将自己无瑕无疵献给神」，不是献给我的良心去审查，也不是等我再加上什么；父已经悦纳了这祭，他「只一次进入圣所，成了永远赎罪的事」（9:12）。我的良心不是等我补够了才安静，而是被他的血洗净。还没有信靠他的人，也不必先做许多善事、烧香积功德才敢来；你可以向神认罪悔改，信靠这位替罪人流血的基督。被洗净以后，事奉就变成另一回事。以赛亚的嘴先被坛上的红炭沾过，听见「你的罪孽便除掉，你的罪恶就赦免了」，然后才说「我在这里，请差遣我！」（赛6:7-8）如果还能找到那位同事，主的血也释放我去向他认错，尽力弥补；那不是去买赦免，而是被赦免的人才敢做的事。我仍旧可以摆椅子、洗碗，却不再是还债，而是事奉那位永生的神：他活着，他看见我，他在基督里悦纳我。读到这一章的末了，我看见那件事最后的答案：「将来要向那等候他的人第二次显现，并与罪无关，乃是为拯救他们。」（9:28）他再来的时候，不会翻开那本账，也不会再提那一天的事；他第一次来已经担当了，第二次来是要把我完全救回家。从前百姓站在殿外，等候大祭司出来；如今我也在等。主耶稣，你进到父面前为我显现，有一天你也要从那里出来。我不再用服事去填补你已经成就的，我只等候你，爱你，赞美你，因为「担当了多人的罪」的，就是你。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 9 has four parts. **The earthly tent** (vv. 1–10): the first covenant had an earthly place of holiness, with the Holy Place in front and the Most Holy Place behind the second curtain; the priests go regularly into the first section, but only the high priest goes into the second, once a year and not without blood; by this the Holy Spirit indicates that the way into the holy places is not yet opened; these gifts and sacrifices cannot perfect the conscience of the worshiper. **Christ entered by his own blood** (vv. 11–14): through the greater and more perfect tent, Christ entered once for all into the holy places by means of his own blood, securing an eternal redemption; through the eternal Spirit he offered himself without blemish to God, and his blood will much more purify our conscience to serve the living God. **The mediator of a new covenant** (vv. 15–22): by his death he is the mediator of a new covenant; a will takes effect only at death; the first covenant too was inaugurated with blood; without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins. **Once, and a second time** (vv. 23–28): the heavenly things are purified with better sacrifices; Christ has entered heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf; he appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself; it is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment; Christ, offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time to save those who are eagerly waiting for him.

Chapter 8 said that the earthly tent was a copy and shadow of the heavenly things (8:5) and laid out the promises of the new covenant (8:8–12). Chapter 9 goes on: what was actually inside that copy, what the high priest did there, and how Christ accomplished what the copy pointed to. The center of the whole chapter is "blood": without blood the high priest could not go in; without blood the covenant could not be made; without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. And the blood Christ brings is his own.

## Going Deeper: 9:1–10 While the First Section Is Still Standing

**1. "An earthly place of holiness."** "Now even the first covenant had regulations for worship and an earthly place of holiness. For a tent was prepared, the first section, in which were the lampstand and the table and the bread of the Presence. It is called the Holy Place. Behind the second curtain was a second section called the Most Holy Place, having the golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant covered on all sides with gold, in which was a golden urn holding the manna, and Aaron's staff that budded, and the tablets of the covenant. Above it were the cherubim of glory overshadowing the mercy seat" (9:1–5).
- **The author speaks of the tent, not the temple:** he goes back to the wilderness tabernacle, the one Moses built after the pattern shown on the mountain (8:5).
- **"The golden altar of incense":** the word can mean either an incense altar or a censer. The ESV has "altar of incense"; the CUV has "golden censer." According to Exodus, the incense altar stood outside the curtain, "in front of the mercy seat" (Exod 30:6). Some hold that the author means the censer the high priest carried behind the curtain on the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:12–13). Others hold that he means the incense altar, and that the Most Holy Place is said to be "having" it because it belonged to the Most Holy Place and its service: Kings calls it "the altar that belonged to the inner sanctuary" (1 Kgs 6:22), and on the Day of Atonement blood was put on its horns (Exod 30:10). Neither view says the author got the location wrong.
- **What was in the ark:** the manna and Aaron's staff were first placed "before the LORD" and "before the testimony" (Exod 16:33; Num 17:10); by Solomon's time "there was nothing in the ark except the two tablets of stone" (1 Kgs 8:9). The author is describing the wilderness tabernacle.
- **"Of these things we cannot now speak in detail":** the author does not want to talk about furniture. His subject is who may go in, and how.

**2. "Once a year... and not without taking blood."** "The priests go regularly into the first section, performing their ritual duties, but into the second only the high priest goes, and he but once a year, and not without taking blood, which he offers for himself and for the unintentional sins of the people" (9:6–7).
- **One man, once a year:** on the Day of Atonement the high priest was "not to come at any time into the Holy Place inside the veil," so that he might not die (Lev 16:2); he went in only once a year (Lev 16:34), sprinkling blood on the mercy seat, first for himself and then for the people (Lev 16:14–15).
- **"Not without taking blood":** a sinner cannot come empty-handed before the holy God. To draw near to him, a life must die in the sinner's place. This is the thread of the whole chapter.

**3. "The way into the holy places is not yet opened."** "By this the Holy Spirit indicates that the way into the holy places is not yet opened as long as the first section is still standing" (9:8).
- **The Spirit speaks through Scripture:** as in 3:7, the author believes the Holy Spirit speaks through the Old Testament regulations themselves: the curtain and the "once a year" both say, "The way is not open yet."
- **"The first section":** many take it as the outer Holy Place, which stood between the people and the Most Holy Place and represented the whole worship system of the first covenant; others, reading it with "symbolic for the present age" in the next verse, think the author has widened "the first" to mean the whole age of the first covenant. The two views come to much the same thing: as long as this system is God's arrangement between himself and his people, the way is not fully open.
- **The way opened:** when Jesus died, "the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom" (Mark 15:38); he opened for us "the new and living way... through the curtain, that is, through his flesh" (10:20).

**4. "Cannot perfect the conscience of the worshiper."** "(Which is symbolic for the present age). According to this arrangement, gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot perfect the conscience of the worshiper, but deal only with food and drink and various washings, regulations for the body imposed until the time of reformation" (9:9–10).
- **Conscience:** the sacrifices of the first covenant could make a person ritually clean, so that he could worship with the congregation again, but they could not reach the place within that knows it is guilty. That is why they were offered every year, and every year there was "a reminder of sin" (10:3).
- **"Regulations for the body":** not that these regulations were bad, for God gave them; but they dealt with outward things: what to eat and how to wash.
- **"The time of reformation":** the time when everything is set right, that is, the new covenant age that came with Christ.

> The point: Every part of the earthly tent said that God is holy and sinners cannot come into his presence as they please; even the high priest had to bring blood, and could go in only once a year. These sacrifices could cleanse the body but not the conscience. The way was not yet open; a greater high priest had to come.

## Going Deeper: 9:11–14 How Much More Will the Blood of Christ

**1. "A high priest of the good things that have come."** "But when Christ appeared as a high priest of the good things that have come, then through the greater and more perfect tent (not made with hands, that is, not of this creation)..." (9:11).
- **"To come" or "have come":** the ancient manuscripts have two readings here: "the good things to come" (followed by the CUV) and "the good things that have come" (followed by the ESV). Many scholars take the second as the earlier reading, but both fit the letter: the good things have already come in Christ, and will be completed when he appears a second time (9:28; 10:1).
- **"The greater and more perfect tent":** most interpreters (such as Bruce and Lane) take it as the true sanctuary in heaven, as Christ "passed through the heavens" (4:14) into the Father's presence; Calvin and some early church fathers took it as Christ's body, the true temple where God meets man (John 2:21; 10:20). Either way, the point is that this tent is "not made with hands": Christ's ministry is not in an earthly tabernacle.

**2. "By means of his own blood... once for all."** "He entered once for all into the holy places, not by means of the blood of goats and calves but by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption" (9:12).
- **"By means of his own blood":** this need not mean he carried blood into heaven; it means the ground on which he entered was his own death, his life laid down. The high priest brought the blood of another creature; Christ brought himself.
- **"Once for all":** chapter 7 has already explained this word (7:27); here the author ties it to "entered": he went in, and never needs to come out to offer again.
- **"An eternal redemption":** "redemption" carries the sense of paying a ransom to set someone free. The Son of Man came "to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45). This redemption is "eternal," unlike the Day of Atonement, whose effect lasted only a year.

**3. "The ashes of a heifer."** "For if the sprinkling of defiled persons with the blood of goats and bulls and with the ashes of a heifer sanctifies for the purification of the flesh..." (9:13).
- **The red heifer:** in Numbers 19 a red heifer was burned to ashes, which were kept "for the water for impurity" (Num 19:9) and sprinkled on anyone who had touched a dead body. "Whoever touches the dead body of any person shall be unclean seven days" (Num 19:11).
- **"How much more":** the author argues from the lesser to the greater: if the shadows God set up could cleanse, how much more the reality.

**4. "Through the eternal Spirit."** "How much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God..." (9:14).
- **The Holy Spirit:** many interpreters (such as Owen, Bruce and Lane) take "the eternal Spirit" as the Holy Spirit. Isaiah's Servant is the one on whom God says, "I have put my Spirit upon him" (Isa 42:1; see 61:1); Christ lived his whole life in obedience by the Spirit, and by the Spirit he offered himself. Then the Father, the Son and the Spirit all take part in this sacrifice.
- **His own spirit:** others (such as Westcott) take it as Christ's own eternal spirit, his divine life, or the willing heart with which he offered himself, so that his sacrifice has eternal worth, unlike animal sacrifices that were only bodily.
- **What they share:** both views agree that this sacrifice is not for a moment but has eternal effect, and that he offered himself willingly, not under compulsion.
- **"Without blemish":** Old Testament sacrifices had to be without defect; Christ is "a lamb without blemish or spot" (1 Pet 1:19). He had no sin of his own to atone for, so his death was a death for others. "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (2 Cor 5:21); "he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" (1 Pet 2:24).

**5. "Purify our conscience from dead works."** "...purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God" (9:14).
- **"Conscience":** the CUV has 心 ("heart") here, but the Greek word is "conscience," the same as in 9:9. The sacrifices of the first covenant could not perfect the conscience; Christ's blood can. Some manuscripts read "your," others "our"; the meaning is the same.
- **"Dead works":** the same phrase as "repentance from dead works" in 6:1. Most interpreters (such as Bruce and Lane) take it as sinful deeds that lead to death; against the background of the red heifer, sin defiles like a corpse and keeps a person from coming to God. Others (such as Owen) think it also includes everything people do while still dead in sin, religious acts included; and from the context of 9:9–10, trying to cleanse oneself by rites and deeds is also a "dead work" that cannot give life. Both views agree that only the blood of Christ can cleanse the conscience.
- **"To serve the living God":** the purpose of cleansing is service, and service of a God who is "living." The word "serve" also carries the sense of worship. Not dead works, but living people serving the living God, as the Thessalonians "turned to God from idols to serve the living and true God" (1 Thess 1:9).

> The point: The blood of animals could cleanse only the body; the blood of Christ cleanses the conscience. He was without sin, offered himself to God through the eternal Spirit, and bore our sins in our place. Those whose conscience he has cleansed are set free from dead works to serve the living God.

## Going Deeper: 9:15–22 Without the Shedding of Blood

**1. "The mediator of a new covenant."** "Therefore he is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance, since a death has occurred that redeems them from the transgressions committed under the first covenant" (9:15).
- **"Mediator":** chapter 8 has already explained this (8:6). Here the author adds that he is mediator by means of "a death."
- **"Redeems them from the transgressions committed under the first covenant":** the sacrifices of the first covenant could not take away sins (10:4); how then were Old Testament believers saved? Christ's death also reaches backward. "In his divine forbearance he had passed over former sins" (Rom 3:25), because he was looking to the cross. The Old Testament saints "apart from us... should not be made perfect" (11:40).
- **"The promised eternal inheritance":** the new covenant brings not only forgiveness but an inheritance. Canaan was the shadow; the true inheritance is eternal.

**2. "A will."** "For where a will is involved, the death of the one who made it must be established. For a will takes effect only at death, since it is not in force as long as the one who made it is alive" (9:16–17).
- **One word, two meanings:** "covenant" and "will" are the same word in Greek. The verses on either side speak of a "covenant" (9:15, 18), but these two verses speak of something that takes effect only at death, like a will.
- **The "will" reading:** the CUV, the ESV and most translations render it "will" or "testament." Many interpreters (such as Bruce and Attridge) think the author borrows the word's other meaning here: a will takes effect only when the one who made it dies; in the same way, Christ died so that we might receive the inheritance (9:15).
- **The "covenant" reading:** others (such as Westcott, Lane and Hahn) think the author is still speaking of a "covenant": in the ancient world, making a covenant involved killing animals, showing that the one who broke it deserved the curse of death (Gen 15:9–18; Jer 34:18); Israel broke the covenant, and Christ bore that curse in place of the covenant-breakers.
- **What they share:** both readings say that the new covenant takes effect through a death, and that death is Christ's.

**3. "The blood of the covenant."** "Therefore not even the first covenant was inaugurated without blood. For when every commandment of the law had been declared by Moses to all the people, he took the blood of calves and goats, with water and scarlet wool and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people, saying, 'This is the blood of the covenant that God commanded for you.' And in the same way he sprinkled with the blood both the tent and all the vessels used in worship" (9:18–21).
- **Exodus 24:** Moses threw the blood on the people and said, "Behold the blood of the covenant" (Exod 24:8). Exodus does not mention water, scarlet wool or hyssop, nor the sprinkling of the book and the tent; the author seems to draw together other cleansing rites of the Old Testament (Num 19:6, 18) and Jewish tradition of his day, to show that everything in the first covenant was cleansed with blood.
- **The Lord's Supper:** when Jesus instituted the Supper, he used these very words: "for this is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins" (Matt 26:28). Moses sprinkled the blood of animals; the Lord gives us his own blood.

**4. "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness."** "Indeed, under the law almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins" (9:22).
- **"Almost":** the law had exceptions; a poor person who could not afford two birds could bring fine flour as a sin offering (Lev 5:11). But blood was the rule.
- **Why blood:** "For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it for you on the altar to make atonement for your souls" (Lev 17:11). To shed blood is to give up a life. The wages of sin is death; forgiveness is not God pretending not to see, but a life dying in the sinner's place.
- **Why God cannot simply say "I forgive":** God is just and cannot treat sin as if it never happened; God is also loving and wills to forgive. At the cross both are shown: God put Jesus forward as a propitiation "so that he might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (Rom 3:26).

> The point: The new covenant was made with blood and takes effect through blood. Forgiveness has never been cheap: without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. God did not lower his justice; he let his Son shed his blood in our place, so that everyone who trusts in him is truly forgiven.

## Going Deeper: 9:23–28 A Second Time, Not to Deal with Sin

**1. "The heavenly things themselves."** "Thus it was necessary for the copies of the heavenly things to be purified with these rites, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these" (9:23).
- **Does heaven need cleansing?** This is a hard verse, with roughly three views. Calvin and Bruce hold that heaven itself has no defilement; what is cleansed is God's people, his house (3:6), whose consciences are sprinkled clean (10:22) so that they may come before him. Others (such as Lane), reading it with the covenant-making just described (9:18–21), take it as the heavenly sanctuary being dedicated and opened for new covenant worship, as the tabernacle was first consecrated (Exod 40:9). Still others look to Leviticus 16:16, where the Day of Atonement also made atonement for the sanctuary because it dwelt in the midst of the people's uncleanness; the barrier our sin raises before God is real, and is removed by Christ's blood.
- **What they share:** on every view, it is Christ's blood that opens the way for sinners into God's presence.

**2. "Now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf."** "For Christ has entered, not into holy places made with hands, which are copies of the true things, but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf. Nor was it to offer himself repeatedly, as the high priest enters the holy places every year with blood not his own, for then he would have had to suffer repeatedly since the foundation of the world. But as it is, he has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" (9:24–26).
- **"Into heaven itself":** not into a copy, but into the presence of God himself.
- **"On our behalf":** he is there not for himself but as our representative (7:25).
- **"Once for all":** if his sacrifice had to be repeated, he would have had to suffer again and again since the foundation of the world. But he came once, "at the end of the ages," the time in which God has spoken by his Son (1:2), and he came "to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself." The word "put away" carries the sense of annulling, setting aside.

**3. "Appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment."** "And just as it is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment..." (9:27).
- **Only once:** a person dies once; after death there is no reincarnation and no second round. Nor can the living change the outcome by burning paper offerings, Buddhist rites for the dead, or prayers for the dead. The exceptions Scripture mentions, such as Lazarus raised from the dead and those still alive at the Lord's return who "shall all be changed" (1 Cor 15:51), do not change the rule: each of us has one life, and then we stand before God.
- **To the one who does not yet believe:** this is a sobering word, but also an invitation. The judgment is real, but the one who will judge is the Christ who was "offered once to bear the sins of many" (9:28). There is still time today: not by merit or good works, but by confessing your sin to God, repenting, and trusting this Savior who died for sinners.

**4. "He will appear a second time."** "So Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him" (9:28).
- **"To bear the sins of many":** these words come from Isaiah 53:12, "yet he bore the sin of many, and makes intercession for the transgressors." To bear them is to take our sins upon himself and suffer their penalty in our place. After death comes judgment; but at the cross Christ took that judgment for those who trust in him.
- **The people waiting outside:** on the Day of Atonement, while the high priest was in the Most Holy Place, the people waited outside for him to come out (Lev 16:17, 24). When Zechariah was burning incense, "the people were waiting for Zechariah, and they were wondering at his delay in the temple" (Luke 1:21). Our high priest has entered the heavenly sanctuary, and he will come out from there.
- **"Not to deal with sin":** when he comes a second time, it will not be to deal with sin again, for that was settled the first time; he comes to complete our salvation, "the redemption of our bodies" (Rom 8:23).
- **"Those who are eagerly waiting for him":** believers do not dread his coming but long for it: "we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ" (Phil 3:20).

> The point: Christ was offered once and put away sin; a person dies once and then faces judgment, but he has already borne the sins of those who trust in him. He will appear a second time, not for sin, but to save those who wait for him. We can wait for him with hope, not with dread.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "How much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God, purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God" (9:14). More than ten years ago I made a mistake at work: the accounts did not add up, I did not own it, and I let a coworker take the blame. He was let go, and later I heard his family had a hard time because of it. I confessed this to God long ago; I have read "without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins" (9:22); yet it has stayed there, pressing down. My way of dealing with it is to do more. When the church needs people, I never say no: I come early on Sunday to set up chairs, stay after the service to wash dishes, teach children's Sunday school, go along on visits. People say I am "so loving," but I know in my heart that I am making up for something. Each extra task seems to make that thing a little lighter; any Sunday I do not serve, it presses back down. I have never said it aloud, but inside I keep asking: is this enough yet? Hebrews says the gifts and sacrifices of the first covenant "cannot perfect the conscience of the worshiper" (9:9); they bring only "a reminder of sin every year" (10:3). My service has become that kind of sacrifice: offered every day, reminding me every day how much I still owe. Service like this, however much of it I do, is a "dead work," because I am not serving a living God but an account I can never settle. I must also admit that my driven serving comes not only from guilt but from pride: I would rather pay it back myself than receive anything for free. That too is sin. But the blood that thing required has already been shed. Christ "offered himself without blemish to God": not to my conscience for inspection, and not waiting for me to add something. The Father has accepted this sacrifice; "he entered once for all into the holy places... thus securing an eternal redemption" (9:12). My conscience is not quieted when I have finally paid enough; it is cleansed by his blood. If you have not yet trusted him, you do not need to pile up good deeds, burn incense or store up merit before you dare to come; you can confess your sin to God, repent, and trust this Christ who shed his blood for sinners. Once cleansed, service becomes something else. Isaiah's lips were first touched by the coal from the altar, and he heard, "Your guilt is taken away, and your sin atoned for"; only then did he say, "Here am I! Send me" (Isa 6:7–8). If that coworker can still be found, the Lord's blood also frees me to go and admit my wrong to him and make it right as far as I can; not to buy forgiveness, but because only the forgiven dare to do such a thing. I can still set up chairs and wash dishes, but no longer to pay a debt. I serve the living God: he lives, he sees me, and he accepts me in Christ. As I come to the end of this chapter, I see the last answer to that old thing: "Christ... will appear a second time, not to deal with sin but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him" (9:28). When he comes again, he will not open that account book or bring up that day again; the first time he came, he bore it; the second time he comes, it is to bring me all the way home. Long ago the people stood outside, waiting for the high priest to come out; now I am waiting too. Lord Jesus, you have gone into the Father's presence for me, and one day you will come out from there. I will no longer use my service to fill up what you have already finished. I will only wait for you, love you and praise you, for the one who bore "the sins of many" is you.
