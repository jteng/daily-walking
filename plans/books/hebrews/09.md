## 本章脉络

第九章分成四段。**地上的帐幕**（1-10节）：惟有大祭司一年一次带着血进入至圣所，这些祭物却不能叫良心得以完全。**基督用自己的血进入**（11-14节）：他只一次进入圣所，成了永远赎罪的事，他的血洗净我们的心，使我们事奉永生神。**新约的中保**（15-22节）：他受死作了新约的中保；若不流血，罪就不得赦免。**一次显现，第二次显现**（23-28节）：他在末世显现一次，把自己献为祭；人人都有一死，死后且有审判；他将来要第二次显现，拯救等候他的人。

全章的中心是「血」：没有血，大祭司不能进去，约不能成立，罪也不得赦免；而基督所献的，是他自己的血。

## 深读 9:1-10 头一层帐幕仍存的时候

**1. 「属世界的圣幕」。** 「原来前约有礼拜的条例和属世界的圣幕。」（9:1）作者描写的是旷野的会幕：头一层是圣所，第二幔子后是至圣所（9:2-5）。
- **「有金香炉」（9:4）：** 这个词可以指香炉，也可以指香坛，而香坛是在幔子外面的（出30:6）。有人认为是指大祭司在赎罪日带进幔内的香炉（利16:12-13，和合本照这个意思）；也有人认为是指香坛，作者说至圣所「有」它，是说它属于至圣所的事奉：列王纪说这坛是属内殿的（王上6:22原文；和合本译作内殿前的坛）；它又被安在「法柜前」（出40:5），赎罪日大祭司也要用血在它的角上赎罪（出30:10；参利16:18）。两种看法都不认为作者弄错了位置。

**2. 「没有不带着血」。** 「至于第二层帐幕，惟有大祭司一年一次独自进去，没有不带着血为自己和百姓的过错献上。」（9:7）
- **一个人，一年一次：** 大祭司「不可随时进圣所的幔子内」，免得他死亡（利16:2）；他一年只有一天能进去（利16:34），先为自己，后为百姓，把血弹在施恩座上（利16:14-15）。罪人不能空手来到圣洁的神面前。

**3. 「进入至圣所的路还未显明」。** 「圣灵用此指明，头一层帐幕仍存的时候，进入至圣所的路还未显明。」（9:8）
- **圣灵藉着条例说话：** 和3:7一样，作者相信圣灵藉着旧约圣经说话；那道幔子和那「一年一次」，都在说路还没有开。

**4. 「就着良心说」。** 「所献的礼物和祭物，就着良心说，都不能叫礼拜的人得以完全。这些事，连那饮食和诸般洗濯的规矩，都不过是属肉体的条例，命定到振兴的时候为止。」（9:9-10）
- **洁净外面，摸不到里面：** 前约的祭物能使人在礼仪上洁净，却触不到人里面那个知道自己有罪的地方；所以这些祭物「是叫人每年想起罪来」（10:3）。

> 要点：地上的帐幕处处都在说：神是圣洁的，罪人不能随意来到他面前；就是大祭司，也必须带着血。这些祭物能洁净身体，却不能洁净良心；路还没有开，要等一位更大的大祭司来。

## 深读 9:11-14 他的血岂不更能洗净你们的心

**1. 「更大更全备的帐幕」。** 「但现在基督已经来到，作了将来美事的大祭司，经过那更大更全备的帐幕，不是人手所造，也不是属乎这世界的。」（9:11）
- **这帐幕是什么：** 多数解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为是指天上真实的圣所，他「升入高天」（4:14）；加尔文认为是指基督的身体（约2:21）。无论哪一种，基督的事奉都不在人手所造的会幕里。

**2. 「乃用自己的血，只一次进入圣所」。** 「并且不用山羊和牛犊的血，乃用自己的血，只一次进入圣所，成了永远赎罪的事。」（9:12）
- **献祭在十字架上：** 「用自己的血」不必理解为他把血带进天上，而是说他进去的凭据是他已经舍了的命。他的献祭是在十字架上完成的：他「把自己献为祭」（9:26），「在城门外受苦」（13:12）。十六、十七世纪的苏西尼派否认基督的死满足了神的公义，说他的献祭到了天上才开始；改革宗（如欧文）却坚持，十字架就是那一次的献祭，他是带着已经成全的祭显在神面前（9:24；参1:3「他洗净了人的罪，就坐在高天至大者的右边」）。
- **「永远赎罪」：** 人子来，「要舍命作多人的赎价」（可10:45）；这赎罪是永远的，不像赎罪日只管一年。

**3. 「藉着永远的灵」。** 「若山羊和公牛的血，并母牛犊的灰，洒在不洁的人身上，尚且叫人成圣，身体洁净，何况基督藉着永远的灵，将自己无瑕无疵献给神……」（9:13-14）
- **母牛犊的灰：** 按民数记19章，红母牛的灰调成「除污秽的水」（民19:9），洒在摸了死尸的人身上。影子尚且能洁净身体，何况本体呢？
- **两种理解：** 许多解经家（如欧文、布鲁斯）认为「永远的灵」是指圣灵：神说他的仆人「我已将我的灵赐给他」（赛42:1），他也藉着圣灵献上自己，父、子、圣灵都在这献祭中；另一些人（如韦斯科特）认为是指基督自己永远的灵，就是他自己永远的神性，他是在其中甘心献上自己。两种看法都同意这献祭有永远的果效。
- **「无瑕无疵」：** 他是「无瑕疵、无玷污的羔羊」（彼前1:19），没有自己的罪要赎，所以他的死是替别人死的。神「使那无罪的，替我们成为罪」（林后5:21）；他「亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24）。

**4. 「除去你们的死行」。** 「……他的血岂不更能洗净你们的心，除去你们的死行，使你们事奉那永生神吗？」（9:14）
- **「死行」：** 和合本的「心」原文是良心，和9:9同一个词。「死行」和6:1「懊悔死行」同一个说法：多数人（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为是指引向死亡的罪行，在红母牛的背景下，罪像死尸一样叫人污秽；另一些解经家认为，也包括人离开永生神所做的一切，连想靠礼仪和行为洁净自己的宗教努力在内（9:9-10）。两种看法都同意，只有基督的血能洗净良心。
- **「事奉那永生神」：** 洗净的目的是事奉，这个词也有敬拜的意思。不再是死的行为，而是活人事奉活的神。

> 要点：牲畜的血只能洁净身体，基督的血却能洗净良心。他无罪，在十字架上把自己献给神，替我们担当了罪。良心被他洗净的人，就从死行里被释放出来，去事奉永生的神。

## 深读 9:15-22 若不流血，罪就不得赦免

**1. 「新约的中保」。** 「为此，他作了新约的中保，既然受死赎了人在前约之时所犯的罪过，便叫蒙召之人得着所应许永远的产业。」（9:15）

**2. 「遗命」。** 「凡有遗命必须等到留遗命的人死了；因为人死了，遗命才有效力。」（9:16-17）
- **遗嘱的读法：** 「约」和「遗命」在原文是同一个词；前后文讲的是约（9:15、18），这两节却像在讲遗嘱，多数译本也这样译。许多解经家（如布鲁斯、阿特里奇）认为，作者借用了这个词的另一个意思：立遗嘱的人死了，遗嘱才生效；照样，基督死了，我们才承受产业。
- **约的读法：** 另一些人（如韦斯科特、雷恩、哈恩）认为仍是讲约：古代立约要宰杀牲畜，表示背约的人当受死亡的咒诅（创15:9-18；耶34:18）；以色列人背了约，基督替背约的人承受了这咒诅。
- **共同点：** 新约要藉着死才生效，那死就是基督的死。

**3. 「这血就是神与你们立约的凭据」。** 「所以，前约也不是不用血立的。」（9:18）摩西把血洒在书上和百姓身上，说：「这血就是神与你们立约的凭据。」（9:20；出24:8）耶稣设立圣餐时也说：「这是我立约的血，为多人流出来，使罪得赦。」（太26:28）

**4. 「若不流血」。** 「按着律法，凡物差不多都是用血洁净的；若不流血，罪就不得赦免了。」（9:22）
- **为什么是血：** 律法也有例外（利5:11），但原则是用血。 「因为活物的生命是在血中。」（利17:11）流血就是交出生命。赦罪不是神装作没有看见，而是有一条命替罪人死了。神是公义的，不能把罪当作没有发生；神又是慈爱的，愿意赦免。在十字架上两样都显明了：「使人知道他自己为义，也称信耶稣的人为义。」（罗3:26）

> 要点：赦罪从来不是便宜的：若不流血，罪就不得赦免。神没有降低他的公义，而是让他的儿子替我们流血；所以凡信靠他的，罪就真的得赦免了。

## 深读 9:23-28 第二次显现，并与罪无关

**1. 「那天上的本物」。** 「但那天上的本物自然当用更美的祭物去洁净。」（9:23）天上需要洁净吗？有人（如布鲁斯）认为，天上本身没有污秽，要洁净的是神的家，就是他的百姓（3:6），使我们的良心被洒去（10:22）；有人从上文的立约来看，认为这是天上圣所为新约的敬拜被洁净、分别为圣，像摩西立坛时用血洁净祭坛一样（利8:15）；还有人从赎罪日来看：会幕「在他们污秽之中」，所以要为圣所赎罪（利16:16）；照样，百姓的罪玷污了他们来到神面前的地方，要用更美的血除去。不论哪一种看法，都是基督的血打开了罪人到神面前的路。

**2. 「如今为我们显在神面前」。** 「乃是进了天堂，如今为我们显在神面前……但如今在这末世显现一次，把自己献为祭，好除掉罪。」（9:24-26）他的祭若要重复，他就必须从创世以来多次受苦；可是他只在「这末世」来了一次（1:2）。

**3. 「人人都有一死，死后且有审判」。** 「按着定命，人人都有一死，死后且有审判。」（9:27）
- **只有一次：** 人死后不是轮回转世，再来一次；活人也不能靠烧纸、超度或为亡者祷告改变结局。每个人只有一生，然后要站在神面前。我们把已经去世的人交托给那位「审判全地的主」（创18:25）。
- **对还没有信主的人：** 审判是真实的，但父已将审判的事交给子（约5:22；徒17:31），那位审判人的，就是「一次被献，担当了多人的罪」的基督（9:28）。今天还有机会：不是靠功德或善行，而是向神认罪悔改，信靠这位替罪人死的救主。

**4. 「将来要……第二次显现」。** 「像这样，基督既然一次被献，担当了多人的罪，将来要向那等候他的人第二次显现，并与罪无关，乃是为拯救他们。」（9:28）
- **「担当了多人的罪」：** 出自以赛亚书53:12，「他却担当多人的罪，又为罪犯代求」。他把我们的罪放在自己身上，在十字架上替信靠他的人受了刑罚和审判。
- **百姓在外面等：** 赎罪日，大祭司在至圣所里，百姓在外面等他出来（利16:17、24）（参路1:21）。我们的大祭司进了天上的圣所，也要从那里出来。
- **「并与罪无关」：** 他再来，不是再处理罪，因为罪的问题在第一次已经解决了；他来是要完成救恩，使我们的「身体得赎」（罗8:23）。所以信徒不是怕他来，而是热切地等候他；这里的「等候」和罗马书8:23、腓立比书3:20是同一个词：「等候救主，就是主耶稣基督从天上降临」（腓3:20）。

> 要点：基督只一次被献，就除掉了罪；人只死一次，死后有审判，而他已经替信靠他的人担当了罪。他还要第二次显现，不是为了罪，而是为了拯救。我们可以带着盼望，而不是带着惧怕，等候他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「何况基督藉着永远的灵，将自己无瑕无疵献给神，他的血岂不更能洗净你们的心，除去你们的死行，使你们事奉那永生神吗？」（9:14）十几年前，公司的账目出了问题，错是我的，我却没有承认，让一位同事背了责任。他被辞退了，听说家里因此过得很难。这件事我早就向神认过罪，可是它一直压在那里。我的办法是多做一点。教会缺人，我从不推辞：主日一早来摆椅子，散会后留下来洗碗。别人夸我有爱心，我心里却明白，我是在补。每多做一件，那件事好像就轻一点；哪个主日没有服事，它又压回来。我心里一直在问：这样够了吗？前约的祭物「就着良心说，都不能叫礼拜的人得以完全」（9:9），只「是叫人每年想起罪来」（10:3）。我的服事也成了这样的祭：天天献上，天天提醒我还欠多少。这样的事奉做得再多，也是死行，因为我服事的不是一位活的神，而是一本还不清的账。我也要承认，我拼命服事，不只是因为愧疚，也是因为骄傲：我宁可自己还，也不愿意白白领受。这也是罪。可是那件事所需要的血，已经流了。基督「将自己无瑕无疵献给神」，不是献给我的良心去审查，也不是等我再加上什么；父已经悦纳了这祭，他「只一次进入圣所，成了永远赎罪的事」（9:12）。我的良心不是等我补够了才安静，而是被他的血洗净。被洗净以后，事奉就不一样了。以赛亚的嘴先被坛上的红炭沾过，听见「你的罪孽便除掉，你的罪恶就赦免了」，然后才说「我在这里，请差遣我！」（赛6:7-8）如果还能找到那位同事，主的血也释放我去找他，向他认错，尽力弥补（参太5:23-24）；不是去买赦免，而是蒙了赦免的人才敢这样做。我仍旧可以摆椅子、洗碗，却不再是还债，而是事奉那位永生的神：他活着，他看见我，他在基督里悦纳我。读到这一章的末了，我看见那件事最后的答案：「将来要向那等候他的人第二次显现，并与罪无关，乃是为拯救他们。」（9:28）他再来的时候，不会翻开那本账，也不会再提那一天的事，因为他说过：「我不再记念他们的罪愆和他们的过犯。」（10:17）他第一次来已经担当了，第二次来是要把我完全救回家。主耶稣，我不再用服事去填补你已经成就的，我只等候你，爱你，赞美你，因为「担当了多人的罪」的，就是你。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 9 has four parts. **The earthly tent** (vv. 1–10): only the high priest entered the Most Holy Place, once a year and not without blood, yet these sacrifices cannot perfect the conscience. **Christ entered by his own blood** (vv. 11–14): he entered once for all, securing an eternal redemption, and his blood purifies our conscience to serve the living God. **The mediator of a new covenant** (vv. 15–22): by his death he mediates a new covenant; without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. **Once, and a second time** (vv. 23–28): he appeared once at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself; man dies once, and then comes judgment; he will appear a second time to save those who wait for him.

The center of the whole chapter is "blood": without it the high priest could not go in, the covenant could not be made, and sin could not be forgiven; and the blood Christ offered was his own.

## Going Deeper: 9:1–10 While the First Section Is Still Standing

**1. "An earthly place of holiness."** "Now even the first covenant had regulations for worship and an earthly place of holiness" (9:1). The author describes the wilderness tabernacle: the Holy Place, and behind the second curtain the Most Holy Place (9:2–5).
- **"Having the golden altar of incense" (9:4):** the word can mean a censer or an incense altar, and the incense altar stood outside the curtain (Exod 30:6). Some hold that the author means the censer the high priest carried behind the curtain on the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:12–13; the CUV reads it this way). Others hold that he means the incense altar, said to belong to the Most Holy Place because of its service: Kings speaks of "the whole altar that belonged to the inner sanctuary" (1 Kgs 6:22); it was set "before the ark of the testimony" (Exod 40:5), and on the Day of Atonement its horns were cleansed with blood (Exod 30:10; see Lev 16:18). Neither view thinks the author got the location wrong.

**2. "Not without taking blood."** "But into the second only the high priest goes, and he but once a year, and not without taking blood, which he offers for himself and for the unintentional sins of the people" (9:7).
- **One man, once a year:** the high priest was "not to come at any time into the Holy Place inside the veil," so that he might not die (Lev 16:2); he could go in only on one day a year (Lev 16:34), sprinkling blood on the mercy seat, first for himself and then for the people (Lev 16:14–15). A sinner cannot come empty-handed before the holy God.

**3. "The way into the holy places is not yet opened."** "By this the Holy Spirit indicates that the way into the holy places is not yet opened as long as the first section is still standing" (9:8).
- **The Spirit speaks through the regulations:** as in 3:7, the author believes the Holy Spirit speaks through the Old Testament; the curtain and the "once a year" both say the way is not open yet.

**4. "Cannot perfect the conscience."** "Gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot perfect the conscience of the worshiper, but deal only with food and drink and various washings, regulations for the body imposed until the time of reformation" (9:9–10).
- **Cleansing the outside, not reaching the inside:** the sacrifices of the first covenant could make a person ritually clean, but they could not reach the place within that knows it is guilty; so "in these sacrifices there is a reminder of sin every year" (10:3).

> The point: Everywhere the earthly tent said that God is holy and sinners cannot come into his presence as they please; even the high priest had to bring blood. These sacrifices could cleanse the body but not the conscience. The way was not yet open; a greater high priest had to come.

## Going Deeper: 9:11–14 How Much More Will the Blood of Christ

**1. "The greater and more perfect tent."** "But when Christ appeared as a high priest of the good things that have come, then through the greater and more perfect tent (not made with hands, that is, not of this creation)..." (9:11).
- **What this tent is:** most interpreters (such as Bruce and Lane) take it as the true sanctuary in heaven, as he "passed through the heavens" (4:14); Calvin took it as Christ's body (John 2:21). Either way, Christ's ministry is not in a tent made with hands.

**2. "By means of his own blood... once for all."** "He entered once for all into the holy places, not by means of the blood of goats and calves but by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption" (9:12).
- **The sacrifice was on the cross:** "by means of his own blood" need not mean he carried blood into heaven; it means he entered on the ground of the life he had already laid down. His sacrifice was completed on the cross: he put away sin "by the sacrifice of himself" (9:26), and "suffered outside the gate" (13:12). The Socinians of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, who denied that Christ's death satisfied God's justice, said his offering began only in heaven; Reformed teachers such as Owen insisted that the cross was the one offering, and that he appears before God with a sacrifice already finished (9:24; cf. 1:3, "After making purification for sins, he sat down").
- **"An eternal redemption":** the Son of Man came "to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45); this redemption is eternal, unlike the Day of Atonement, whose effect lasted a year.

**3. "Through the eternal Spirit."** "For if the sprinkling of defiled persons with the blood of goats and bulls and with the ashes of a heifer sanctifies for the purification of the flesh, how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God..." (9:13–14).
- **The ashes of a heifer:** in Numbers 19 the ashes of a red heifer were kept "for the water for impurity" (Num 19:9) and sprinkled on anyone who had touched a corpse. If the shadow could cleanse the body, "how much more" the reality?
- **Two readings:** many interpreters (such as Owen and Bruce) take "the eternal Spirit" as the Holy Spirit: God says of his Servant, "I have put my Spirit upon him" (Isa 42:1), and he offered himself by the Spirit, so that Father, Son and Spirit all take part in this sacrifice; others (such as Westcott) take it as Christ's own eternal spirit, his own divine nature, in which he freely offered himself. Both agree the offering has eternal effect.
- **"Without blemish":** he is "a lamb without blemish or spot" (1 Pet 1:19), with no sin of his own to atone for, so his death was a death for others. God "made him to be sin who knew no sin" (2 Cor 5:21); "he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" (1 Pet 2:24).

**4. "From dead works."** "...purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God" (9:14).
- **"Dead works":** "conscience" is the same word as in 9:9 (the CUV has 心, "heart"). "Dead works" is the phrase of 6:1, "repentance from dead works": most (such as Bruce and Lane) take it as sinful deeds that lead to death, which against the red-heifer background defile like a corpse; others think it also includes everything done apart from the living God, including religious efforts to cleanse oneself by rites and deeds (9:9–10). Both agree that only the blood of Christ cleanses the conscience.
- **"To serve the living God":** the purpose of cleansing is service, a word that also carries the sense of worship. No longer dead works, but living people serving the living God.

> The point: The blood of animals could cleanse only the body; the blood of Christ cleanses the conscience. He was without sin, offered himself to God on the cross, and bore our sins in our place. Those whose conscience he has cleansed are set free from dead works to serve the living God.

## Going Deeper: 9:15–22 Without the Shedding of Blood

**1. "The mediator of a new covenant."** "Therefore he is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance, since a death has occurred that redeems them from the transgressions committed under the first covenant" (9:15).

**2. "A will."** "For where a will is involved, the death of the one who made it must be established. For a will takes effect only at death" (9:16–17).
- **The "will" reading:** "covenant" and "will" are the same word in Greek; the verses around speak of a covenant (9:15, 18), but these two sound like a will, and most translations render it so. Many interpreters (such as Bruce and Attridge) think the author borrows the word's other meaning: a will takes effect when its maker dies; so Christ died, and we receive the inheritance.
- **The "covenant" reading:** others (such as Westcott, Lane and Hahn) think it still means covenant: in the ancient world, making a covenant involved killing animals, showing that a covenant-breaker deserved the curse of death (Gen 15:9–18; Jer 34:18); Israel broke the covenant, and Christ bore that curse for covenant-breakers.
- **What they share:** the new covenant takes effect through a death, and that death is Christ's.

**3. "The blood of the covenant."** "Therefore not even the first covenant was inaugurated without blood" (9:18). Moses sprinkled the book and the people, saying, "This is the blood of the covenant that God commanded for you" (9:20; Exod 24:8). When Jesus instituted the Supper he said, "this is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins" (Matt 26:28).

**4. "Without the shedding of blood."** "Indeed, under the law almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins" (9:22).
- **Why blood:** the law had exceptions (Lev 5:11), but blood was the rule. "For the life of the flesh is in the blood" (Lev 17:11). To shed blood is to give up a life. Forgiveness is not God pretending not to see, but a life dying in the sinner's place. God is just and cannot treat sin as if it never happened; God is loving and wills to forgive. At the cross both are shown, "so that he might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (Rom 3:26).

> The point: Forgiveness has never been cheap: without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. God did not lower his justice; he let his Son shed his blood in our place, so that everyone who trusts in him is truly forgiven.

## Going Deeper: 9:23–28 A Second Time, Not to Deal with Sin

**1. "The heavenly things themselves."** "But the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these" (9:23). Does heaven need cleansing? Some (such as Bruce) hold that heaven itself has no defilement; what is cleansed is God's house, his people (3:6), whose consciences are sprinkled clean (10:22). Some, reading it with the covenant-making just described, take it as the heavenly sanctuary cleansed and consecrated for new covenant worship, as Moses purified the altar with blood (Lev 8:15). Others read it through the Day of Atonement: the tent "dwells with them in the midst of their uncleannesses," so atonement was made for the sanctuary (Lev 16:16); likewise the defilement of the people's sin before God must be purged by a better blood. On every view, Christ's blood opens the way for sinners into God's presence.

**2. "Now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf."** "But into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf... But as it is, he has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" (9:24–26). If his sacrifice had to be repeated, he would have had to suffer again and again since the foundation of the world; but he came once, "at the end of the ages" (1:2).

**3. "Appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment."** (9:27).
- **Only once:** after death there is no reincarnation and no second round; nor can the living change the outcome by burning paper offerings, Buddhist or Daoist rites for the dead, or prayers for the dead. Each of us has one life, and then we stand before God. We entrust those who have died to "the Judge of all the earth" (Gen 18:25).
- **To the one who does not yet believe:** the judgment is real, but the Father has given all judgment to the Son (John 5:22; Acts 17:31), and the one who judges is the Christ who was "offered once to bear the sins of many" (9:28). There is still time today: not by merit or good works, but by confessing your sin to God, repenting, and trusting this Savior who died for sinners.

**4. "Will appear a second time."** "So Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him" (9:28).
- **"To bear the sins of many":** from Isaiah 53:12, "yet he bore the sin of many, and makes intercession for the transgressors." He took our sins upon himself and at the cross bore their penalty and judgment in place of those who trust in him.
- **The people waiting outside:** on the Day of Atonement, while the high priest was in the Most Holy Place, the people waited for him to come out (Lev 16:17, 24) (see Luke 1:21). Our high priest has entered the heavenly sanctuary, and he will come out from there.
- **"Not to deal with sin":** he does not return to deal with sin again, for that was settled the first time; he comes to complete our salvation, "the redemption of our bodies" (Rom 8:23). So believers do not dread his coming but eagerly wait for him; "waiting" here is the same word as in Romans 8:23 and Philippians 3:20: "we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ" (Phil 3:20).

> The point: Christ was offered once and put away sin; a person dies once and then faces judgment, but he has already borne the sins of those who trust in him. He will appear a second time, not for sin, but to save. We can wait for him with hope, not dread.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "How much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without blemish to God, purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God" (9:14). More than ten years ago the accounts at my company went wrong. The mistake was mine, but I did not own it, and I let a coworker take the blame. He was let go, and I heard his family had a hard time because of it. I confessed this to God long ago, yet it has stayed there, pressing down. My way of dealing with it is to do more. When the church needs people, I never say no: I come early on Sunday to set up chairs, stay after the service to wash dishes. People praise me for being loving, but I know I am making up for something. Each extra task seems to make that thing a little lighter; any Sunday I do not serve, it presses back down. Inside I keep asking: is this enough yet? The sacrifices of the first covenant "cannot perfect the conscience of the worshiper" (9:9); "in these sacrifices there is a reminder of sin every year" (10:3). My service has become that kind of sacrifice: offered every day, reminding me every day how much I still owe. However much of it I do, it is a dead work, because I am not serving a living God but an account I can never settle. I must also admit that my driven serving comes not only from guilt but from pride: I would rather pay it back myself than receive anything for free. That too is sin. But the blood that thing required has already been shed. Christ "offered himself without blemish to God": not to my conscience for inspection, and not waiting for me to add something. The Father has accepted this sacrifice; "he entered once for all into the holy places... thus securing an eternal redemption" (9:12). My conscience is not quieted when I have finally paid enough; it is cleansed by his blood. Once cleansed, service is different. Isaiah's lips were first touched by the coal from the altar, and he heard, "your guilt is taken away, and your sin atoned for"; only then did he say, "Here am I! Send me" (Isa 6:7–8). If that coworker can still be found, the Lord's blood also frees me to seek him out, admit my wrong and make it right as far as I can (see Matt 5:23–24); not to buy forgiveness, but because only the forgiven dare to do this. I can still set up chairs and wash dishes, but no longer to pay a debt. I serve the living God: he lives, he sees me, and he accepts me in Christ. At the end of this chapter I see the last answer to that old thing: Christ "will appear a second time, not to deal with sin but to save those who are eagerly waiting for him" (9:28). When he comes again, he will not open that account book or bring up that day again, for he has said, "I will remember their sins and their lawless deeds no more" (10:17); the first time he came, he bore it; the second time, he comes to bring me all the way home. Lord Jesus, I will no longer use my service to fill up what you have already finished. I will only wait for you, love you and praise you, for the one who bore "the sins of many" is you.
