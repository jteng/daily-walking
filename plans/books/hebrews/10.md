## 本章脉络

第十章分成两大段。**一次献上的祭**（1-18节）：律法只是将来美事的影儿，年年献的祭只叫人想起罪来；基督到世上来，说「我来了，为要照你的旨意行」，只一次献上他的身体，我们就得以成圣；他献了一次永远的赎罪祭，就在神的右边坐下了；罪既已赦免，就不用再为罪献祭了。**所以要来到神面前**（19-39节）：我们既因耶稣的血得以坦然进入至圣所，就当来到神面前，坚守指望，彼此相顾，不可停止聚会（19-25节）；故意犯罪的人，赎罪的祭就再没有了（26-31节）；读者要追念往日，不可丢弃勇敢的心，因为「义人必因信得生」（32-39节）。

作者从4:14开始讲大祭司和祭物，到10:18告一段落。19节的「既……就当」把道理变成劝勉：基督既然做成了这一切，我们就该来到神面前，而且一同来。19-39节的次序和6:1-12很像：先劝勉，再警告，然后追念读者从前的爱心。

## 深读 10:1-10 我来了，为要照你的旨意行

**1. 「叫人每年想起罪来」。** 律法只是「将来美事的影儿」（10:1）。「但这些祭物是叫人每年想起罪来；因为公牛和山羊的血，断不能除罪。」（10:3-4）
- **「想起」：** 原文这个词也用在主的晚餐上：「为的是记念我」（路22:19）。许多人注意到这个对比：赎罪日年年叫人记起自己的罪；主的晚餐却叫我们记念那一次为我们舍的身体。
- **那些祭不是毫无意义：** 信靠神的人也真的蒙了赦免，但根基是这些祭所指向的基督，他「受死赎了人在前约之时所犯的罪过」（9:15）。动物不能担当人对神的悖逆；能代替人的，必须是无罪的人（2:17）。

**2. 「你曾给我预备了身体」。** 「所以基督到世上来的时候，就说：神啊，祭物和礼物是你不愿意的；你曾给我预备了身体。」（10:5）
- **谁在说话：** 诗篇40篇是大卫的诗，作者却说这是基督「到世上来的时候」说的，也就是神的儿子成为真正的人的时候（2:14）。
- **身体还是耳朵：** 希伯来文的诗篇40:6直译是你挖通了我的耳朵，和合本译作「你已经开通我的耳朵」。作者引的是读者熟悉的希腊文旧约圣经（七十士译本），那里作「你曾给我预备了身体」；他是在圣灵的引导下引用这个译本的。许多人认为，七十士译本的译者是用整体解释部分：开通耳朵是为了听从，而人是用整个人去顺从。无论如何，作者的论点不只靠一个词，诗篇40:8本身就说：「我乐意照你的旨意行；你的律法在我心里。」
- **不是用顺服代替献祭：** 基督的顺服不是十字架以外的另一条路；他顺服到极处，正是献上自己的身体。所以神「除去在先的，为要立定在后的」（10:9）。

**3. 「我们凭这旨意……就得以成圣」。** 「我们凭这旨意，靠耶稣基督，只一次献上他的身体，就得以成圣。」（10:10）
- **替我们献上：** 他「义的代替不义的，为要引我们到神面前」（彼前3:18）。他替我们担当了罪的刑罚，所以我们在神面前被分别为圣、被接纳，不是靠自己的行为。
- **「得以成圣」：** 原文是完成式。许多解经家（如彼得森）认为，这里的成圣主要是指神藉着基督的祭，一次把我们分别出来归他，好亲近他，慕理称之为确定的成圣。这和信徒一生在圣洁上长进（12:14）紧密相连，却不完全是同一件事。

> 要点：律法下的祭只叫人想起罪来。基督来，是为了照父的旨意行，而父的旨意就是要他献上自己的身体；我们得以成圣，是因为他一次替我们献上了自己。

## 深读 10:11-18 他就在神的右边坐下了

**1. 站着和坐下。** 「凡祭司天天站着事奉神，屡次献上一样的祭物，这祭物永不能除罪。但基督献了一次永远的赎罪祭，就在神的右边坐下了。」（10:11-12）
- **工作做成了：** 祭司站着，因为工作做不完；基督坐下，因为洗罪的工作已经做成了（1:3）。他坐着「等候他仇敌成了他的脚凳」（10:13；诗110:1），是作王掌权，不是闲着。

**2. 「便叫那得以成圣的人永远完全」。** 「因为他一次献祭，便叫那得以成圣的人永远完全。」（10:14）
- **两个时态：** 「叫……完全」原文是完成式，「得以成圣的人」是现在式分词，直译是正被成圣的人。许多人认为，作者有意把两件事并列：在神面前，我们已经一次永远地被成全，可以亲近神；在生活中，我们还在被成圣的路上。也有人认为，这个现在式只是指那些蒙圣洁的人，不特别强调过程。
- **给跌倒的人：** 他在神面前的地位，不在于他今天圣洁到什么程度，而在于那「一次献祭」；而被成全的人，也正是那些还在被成圣的人。

**3. 「就不用再为罪献祭了」。** 「以后就说：我不再记念他们的罪愆和他们的过犯。这些罪过既已赦免，就不用再为罪献祭了。」（10:17-18）
- **圣灵作见证：** 作者又引耶利米书31章（8:8-12），这次重点放在「不再记念」上。
- **结论：** 罪既然真的赦免了，就不需要再献任何赎罪的祭（参7:27），人也不能用善行、苦修或眼泪再加上去。

> 要点：基督已经坐下，因为工作已经成了。我们的盼望不在于自己的进步，而在于那一次献上、永不需要重复的祭。

## 深读 10:19-25 又新又活的路

**1. 我们已经有的两件事。** 「弟兄们，我们既因耶稣的血得以坦然进入至圣所，是藉着他给我们开了一条又新又活的路，从幔子经过，这幔子就是他的身体。又有一位大祭司治理神的家」（10:19-21）。
- **「坦然」：** 原文和10:35的「勇敢的心」是同一个词。从前只有大祭司一年一次进入幔内（9:7）；如今所有信徒都可以坦然进去。
- **「又新又活」：** 新，是因为这条路在前约之下「还未显明」（9:8）；活，是因为这条路就是一位「长远活着」的主（7:25；参约14:6）。
- **「这幔子就是他的身体」：** 多数解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为，作者把幔子和基督的身体连在一起：他的身体在十字架上被撕裂，殿里的幔子也「从上到下裂为两半」（可15:38），路就开了。也有人（如韦斯科特）认为，「他的身体」是说明那条路的。两种理解都说明：没有十字架，就没有路。

**2. 信、望、爱。** 「就当存着诚心和充足的信心来到神面前；也要坚守我们所承认的指望，不至摇动，因为那应许我们的是信实的。又要彼此相顾，激发爱心，勉励行善。」（10:22-24）
- **三个劝勉：** 来到神面前、坚守指望、彼此相顾，依次讲到「信心」「指望」和「爱心」（参林前13:13）。
- **像祭司一样被洁净：** 「心中天良的亏欠已经洒去，身体用清水洗净了」（10:22），让人想起亚伦和他的儿子就职时「用水洗身」，又被血弹洒（出29:4；利8:30）。许多人认为这也指洗礼；洗礼本身不能洗净良心，而是那内在洁净的外在记号（彼前3:21）。
- **「相顾」：** 和3:1「思想」耶稣原文是同一个词，就是用心留意。我们用心思想耶稣，也要用心留意身边的人。

**3. 「不可停止聚会」。** 「你们不可停止聚会，好像那些停止惯了的人，倒要彼此劝勉，既知道那日子临近，就更当如此。」（10:25）
- **为什么不来：** 也许是怕受逼迫（32-34节），也许是等主再来等累了。作者没有细说，只说这已经成了习惯。
- **不是出席的规条：** 这节经文不是要定生病的、照顾家人的、轮班不能来的人的罪，神也不是按出席次数称人为义。作者关心的，是一个人慢慢离开了神的百姓。主再来的日子越近，越需要彼此劝勉。

> 要点：路已经开了，是耶稣用自己的身体开的；治理神家的大祭司已经在那里。所以我们来到神面前，并且一同来，彼此留意，彼此劝勉，直到他再来。

## 深读 10:26-31 落在永生神的手里

**1. 「若故意犯罪」。** 「因为我们得知真道以后，若故意犯罪，赎罪的祭就再没有了；惟有战惧等候审判和那烧灭众敌人的烈火。」（10:26-27）
- **不是任何一次明知故犯：** 「犯罪」原文是现在式分词，许多人认为是指持续、故意地犯罪；29节又用三个动作说明：践踏神的儿子，把立约的血当作平常，亵慢施恩的圣灵。这像律法中「那擅敢行事」的人（民15:30），是公然、彻底地背弃基督。
- **「赎罪的祭就再没有了」：** 不是基督的祭不够，而是18节说的：基督以外再没有别的祭。离开了唯一的祭，就再没有可以投靠的了。

**2. 「使他成圣之约的血」。** 「何况人践踏神的儿子，将那使他成圣之约的血当作平常，又亵慢施恩的圣灵，你们想，他要受的刑罚该怎样加重呢！」（10:29）
- **四种理解：** 6:4-8的深读已经介绍过福音派的四种主要理解，这里只看它们怎样读这句话。阿米念传统（如柯克里尔）认为，这人真的成圣了，后来又失落了。改革宗里，欧文认为这里的「他」可能指基督自己，他藉着立约的血被分别出来（参约17:19）；另一些人认为是指人在立约群体中外在的分别，像西奈山下以色列全体都被立约的血洒过（出24:8），却不都有信心。施莱纳等人则认为，警告是真实地对信徒说的，神正是用这样的警告保守他的百姓。

**3. 「真是可怕的」。** 「又说：『主要审判他的百姓。』落在永生神的手里，真是可怕的！」（10:30-31）
- **大卫的对比：** 大卫犯罪以后说：「我愿落在耶和华的手里，因为他有丰盛的怜悯。」（撒下24:14）同样是落在神的手里，大卫是认罪投靠神的怜悯；10:31说的却是践踏神儿子的人。分别不在罪的大小，而在于是否投靠那一次献上的祭。
- **给害怕的人：** 请再读6:4-8深读的第5点：一个人还为这事忧伤，还想回到基督那里，本身就说明他的心还没有这样刚硬。

> 要点：在基督以外没有别的祭，只有审判。可是投靠那一次献上之祭的人，会发现永生神的手满有怜悯。

## 深读 10:32-39 义人必因信得生

**1. 追念往日。** 「你们要追念往日，蒙了光照以后所忍受大争战的各样苦难」（10:32）；「你们体恤了那些被捆锁的人，并且你们的家业被人抢去，也甘心忍受，知道自己有更美长存的家业。」（10:34）
- **「甘心忍受」：** 原文是喜乐地接受。他们公开探望坐监的人（参13:3）。如果这封信是写给罗马的，许多人会联想到主后49年克劳第驱逐犹太人的事（徒18:2），但作者没有说是哪一次。他们能喜乐，是因为知道有「更美长存的家业」（参13:14）。

**2. 「不可丢弃勇敢的心」。** 「所以，你们不可丢弃勇敢的心；存这样的心必得大赏赐。你们必须忍耐，使你们行完了神的旨意，就可以得着所应许的。」（10:35-36）
- **「勇敢的心」：** 就是19节的「坦然」。丢弃它，就是不再来到神面前。

**3. 哈巴谷的话。** 「只是义人必因信得生。他若退后，我心里就不喜欢他。我们却不是退后入沉沦的那等人，乃是有信心以致灵魂得救的人。」（10:38-39）
- **引文：** 37-38节取自哈巴谷书2:3-4。希伯来文说的是那默示必然临到；作者所用的七十士译本读成一位要来的，作者就用来指基督。他又把两句的次序对调，把「退后」放在后面，好直接接到39节。
- **保罗和希伯来书：** 保罗用哈巴谷书2:4讲因信称义（罗1:17），希伯来书用来讲因信坚持到底。这不是两种信心。人被称为义，不是因为他的忍耐或行为，而是单单因为信靠那位一次献上、替我们担当了罪的基督；这同一个信心，也叫人在苦难中不退后。
- **「我们却不是」：** 作者在警告以后，带着信心说「我们」（参6:9），接着在第十一章列出一长串有信心的人。

> 要点：我们称义是单单因着信靠基督，坚持到底也是靠着同一个信心，仰望同一位基督，因为那要来的必来。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我们既因耶稣的血得以坦然进入至圣所，是藉着他给我们开了一条又新又活的路，从幔子经过，这幔子就是他的身体。又有一位大祭司治理神的家」（10:19-21）。我不是哪一天决定不去教会的。搬到新的城市以后，我想先安顿下来再找教会；后来工作忙了，孩子出生了，在家看直播好像也不错；再后来，连直播也不开了。也可能是另一条路：我跌倒了，也许是婚姻破裂，也许是欠下了债，也许是一件只有几个人知道的事。我怕看见那些认识我的脸，怕有人问起我最近怎么样，就先躲开了。日子一久，不去反而成了习惯，就像「那些停止惯了的人」（10:25）。可是希伯来书没有先叫我回去，而是先告诉我：路已经开了。这条路是耶稣用他自己的身体开的，他的身体在十字架上为我撕裂，替我受了罪的刑罚。我回到神面前，不是靠先把自己收拾好，而是靠「耶稣的血」；那颗因为羞愧而躲着人的心，正是他要洒去的「天良的亏欠」（10:22）。我也要承认：有时候我不去，不只是因为忙，也是因为不想被人认识、被人劝勉，宁可一个人信。这里面有骄傲，我可以把它也带到他面前认罪。何况这位大祭司「治理神的家」。我一直躲着的聚会，不是别人的地方，而是他的家，开门的也是他。他说：「在会中我要颂扬你」（2:12），就在他所救赎的弟兄姊妹中间。当然，如果我离开的是一个隐瞒性侵、纵容暴力或控制人的教会，那不是这里说的停止聚会；我可以去找一个安全的教会，有人受了伤害，向警方举报也是对的（罗13:4）。因为生病、照顾家人或轮班而不能去的人，主也看见。也许有人读到这里，还从来没有走过这条路。这条路不是为好人开的，而是为罪人开的：你可以承认自己的罪，悔改，信靠那位用自己的身体为你开路的耶稣。回去不必一步走完：也许是写几句话给一位从前认识的弟兄姊妹或牧者，说我想回来，却不知道怎样开始；也许是下一个主日早一点到，坐在后排。那位治理神家的大祭司早已知道我这些年的每一步，却仍然用自己的身体为我开了这条又新又活的路，在他的家中等我回来。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 10 has two main parts. **A sacrifice offered once** (vv. 1–18): the law has but a shadow of the good things to come, and its yearly sacrifices are only a reminder of sin; when Christ came into the world he said, "Behold, I have come to do your will," and by the offering of his body once for all we have been sanctified; having offered a single sacrifice for sins, he sat down at the right hand of God; and where sins are forgiven there is no longer any offering for sin. **So let us draw near** (vv. 19–39): since we have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus, let us draw near, hold fast our hope, consider one another, and not neglect meeting together (vv. 19–25); for those who go on sinning deliberately there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins (vv. 26–31); the readers are to recall the former days and not throw away their confidence, for "my righteous one shall live by faith" (vv. 32–39).

From 4:14 on the author has been explaining the high priest and the sacrifice; at 10:18 that exposition comes to rest. The "since we have... let us" of verse 19 turns doctrine into exhortation: since Christ has done all this, we should draw near to God, and draw near together. Verses 19–39 follow the same order as 6:1–12: exhortation, then warning, then a reminder of the readers' earlier love.

## Going Deeper: 10:1–10 "I Have Come to Do Your Will"

**1. "A reminder of sin every year."** The law has "but a shadow of the good things to come" (10:1). "But in these sacrifices there is a reminder of sin every year. For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins" (10:3–4).
- **"Reminder":** the same Greek word is used at the Lord's Supper: "Do this in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19). Many note the contrast: the Day of Atonement reminded people of their sins every year; the Lord's Supper reminds us of the body given for us once.
- **Those sacrifices were not meaningless:** those who trusted God were truly forgiven, but the ground was the Christ to whom the sacrifices pointed, whose death "redeems them from the transgressions committed under the first covenant" (9:15). An animal cannot bear a person's rebellion against God; the one who stands in our place must be a human being without sin (2:17).

**2. "A body have you prepared for me."** "Consequently, when Christ came into the world, he said, 'Sacrifices and offerings you have not desired, but a body have you prepared for me'" (10:5).
- **Who is speaking?** Psalm 40 is a psalm of David, yet the author says these are the words of Christ "when he came into the world," that is, when the Son of God became truly human (2:14).
- **A body or ears?** The Hebrew of Psalm 40:6 reads literally "ears you have dug for me" (the ESV has "you have given me an open ear"). The author quotes the Greek translation of the Old Testament his readers knew (the Septuagint), which reads "a body have you prepared for me"; he cites that version under the Spirit's guidance. Many think the Septuagint translator explained the part by the whole: an opened ear is for obeying, and one obeys with one's whole self. Either way, the argument does not rest on one word; Psalm 40:8 itself says, "I desire to do your will, O my God; your law is within my heart."
- **Not obedience instead of sacrifice:** Christ's obedience is not a path apart from the cross; his obedience reached its height precisely in offering his own body. So "he abolishes the first in order to establish the second" (10:9).

**3. "By that will we have been sanctified."** "And by that will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all" (10:10).
- **Offered for us:** he suffered "once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring us to God" (1 Pet 3:18). He bore the penalty of our sin in our place, so we are set apart and accepted before God, not by our own works.
- **"We have been sanctified":** the Greek is a perfect tense. Many interpreters (such as David Peterson) think sanctification here mainly means that God, through Christ's sacrifice, has set us apart for himself once for all so that we may draw near; John Murray called this definitive sanctification. It is closely bound up with the believer's lifelong growth in holiness (12:14), but is not quite the same thing.

> The point: The sacrifices under the law only reminded people of sin. Christ came to do the Father's will, and the Father's will was that he offer his own body; we are sanctified because he offered himself once in our place.

## Going Deeper: 10:11–18 He Sat Down at the Right Hand of God

**1. Standing and sitting.** "And every priest stands daily at his service, offering repeatedly the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins. But when Christ had offered for all time a single sacrifice for sins, he sat down at the right hand of God" (10:11–12).
- **The work is done:** the priests stood because their work was never finished; Christ sat down because the work of purification is done (1:3). He is seated "waiting from that time until his enemies should be made a footstool for his feet" (10:13; Ps 110:1): reigning as King, not idle.

**2. "He has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified."** "For by a single offering he has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified" (10:14).
- **Two tenses:** "has perfected" is a perfect tense, while "those who are being sanctified" is a present participle. Many take the author to be setting two things side by side on purpose: before God we have already been perfected once for all and may draw near; in life we are still on the road of being made holy. Others think the present tense simply names "the sanctified" without stressing a process.
- **For the one who has fallen:** his standing before God does not depend on how holy he is today, but on that "single offering"; and those who have been perfected are exactly those who are still being sanctified.

**3. "There is no longer any offering for sin."** "Then he adds, 'I will remember their sins and their lawless deeds no more.' Where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer any offering for sin" (10:17–18).
- **The Spirit bears witness:** the author quotes Jeremiah 31 again (8:8–12), this time putting the weight on "remember... no more."
- **The conclusion:** since sins have truly been forgiven, no further sacrifice for sin is needed (see 7:27), and we cannot add to it with good works, penances or tears.

> The point: Christ has sat down because the work is finished. Our hope rests not on our own progress but on the single offering that never needs repeating.

## Going Deeper: 10:19–25 A New and Living Way

**1. Two things we already have.** "Therefore, brothers, since we have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus, by the new and living way that he opened for us through the curtain, that is, through his flesh, and since we have a great priest over the house of God" (10:19–21).
- **"Confidence":** the same Greek word as "confidence" in 10:35. Once only the high priest entered behind the curtain, once a year (9:7); now every believer may enter with confidence.
- **"New and living":** new, because under the first covenant the way was "not yet opened" (9:8); living, because the way is a Lord who "always lives" (7:25; see John 14:6).
- **"Through the curtain, that is, through his flesh":** most interpreters (such as F.F. Bruce and William Lane) think the author links the curtain with Christ's flesh: his body was torn on the cross, the temple curtain "was torn in two, from top to bottom" (Mark 15:38), and the way was opened. Others (such as B.F. Westcott) take "his flesh" to describe the way itself. Both readings say the same thing: without the cross there is no way.

**2. Faith, hope and love.** "Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith... Let us hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering, for he who promised is faithful. And let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works" (10:22–24).
- **Three exhortations:** draw near, hold fast, consider one another, speaking in turn of "faith," "hope" and "love" (see 1 Cor 13:13).
- **Cleansed like priests:** "with our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water" (10:22) recalls the ordination of Aaron and his sons, when they were washed with water and sprinkled with blood (Exod 29:4; Lev 8:30). Many think this also refers to baptism; baptism does not itself cleanse the conscience but is the outward sign of that inward cleansing (1 Pet 3:21).
- **"Consider":** the same Greek word as "consider Jesus" in 3:1, meaning to give careful attention. We pay careful attention to Jesus, and also to the people beside us.

**3. "Not neglecting to meet together."** "Not neglecting to meet together, as is the habit of some, but encouraging one another, and all the more as you see the Day drawing near" (10:25).
- **Why stay away?** Perhaps for fear of persecution (vv. 32–34), perhaps out of weariness in waiting for the Lord's return. The author does not say; he only says it had become a habit.
- **Not an attendance rule:** this verse does not condemn those who are ill, caring for family, or working shifts and cannot come, nor does God justify anyone by counting attendance. The author's concern is the person slowly drifting away from God's people. The nearer the Lord's return, the more we need to encourage one another.

> The point: The way is open, opened by Jesus through his own flesh, and the great priest over God's house is already there. So we draw near to God, and draw near together, paying attention to one another and encouraging one another until he comes.

## Going Deeper: 10:26–31 The Hands of the Living God

**1. "If we go on sinning deliberately."** "For if we go on sinning deliberately after receiving the knowledge of the truth, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins, but a fearful expectation of judgment, and a fury of fire that will consume the adversaries" (10:26–27).
- **Not any single knowing sin:** "go on sinning" is a present participle, and many take it to mean continuing, deliberate sin; verse 29 explains it with three actions: spurning the Son of God, profaning the blood of the covenant, and outraging the Spirit of grace. This is like the one who sins "with a high hand" in the law (Num 15:30): open and complete rejection of Christ.
- **"There no longer remains a sacrifice for sins":** not because Christ's sacrifice is insufficient, but because, as verse 18 said, there is no other sacrifice besides Christ. Turn away from the only sacrifice, and there is nothing left to take refuge in.

**2. "The blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified."** "How much worse punishment, do you think, will be deserved by the one who has spurned the Son of God, and has profaned the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified, and has outraged the Spirit of grace?" (10:29).
- **Four readings:** the section on 6:4–8 has already set out the four main evangelical readings; here we only note how they read this phrase. The Arminian tradition (such as Gareth Cockerill) holds that this person was truly sanctified and was later lost. Among Reformed interpreters, John Owen suggested that "he" may refer to Christ himself, set apart by the blood of the covenant (see John 17:19); others take it as the outward setting apart of a member of the covenant community, as all Israel at Sinai was sprinkled with the blood of the covenant (Exod 24:8) though not all had faith. Thomas Schreiner and others hold that the warning is genuinely addressed to believers and that God uses such warnings to keep his people.

**3. "A fearful thing."** "And again, 'The Lord will judge his people.' It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God" (10:30–31).
- **David's contrast:** after his sin David said, "Let us fall into the hand of the LORD, for his mercy is great" (2 Sam 24:14). In both cases someone falls into God's hands; but David confessed and took refuge in God's mercy, while 10:31 speaks of one who has spurned God's Son. The difference is not the size of the sin, but whether one takes refuge in the single offering.
- **For the one who is afraid:** read again point 5 under 6:4–8: someone who still grieves over this and still longs to come back to Christ shows by that very longing that his heart has not been hardened in this way.

> The point: Apart from Christ there is no other sacrifice, only judgment. But whoever takes refuge in that single offering will find the hands of the living God full of mercy.

## Going Deeper: 10:32–39 My Righteous One Shall Live by Faith

**1. Recall the former days.** "But recall the former days when, after you were enlightened, you endured a hard struggle with sufferings" (10:32); "for you had compassion on those in prison, and you joyfully accepted the plundering of your property, since you knew that you yourselves had a better possession and an abiding one" (10:34).
- **"Joyfully accepted":** they had openly stood with prisoners (see 13:3). If the letter was written to Rome, many connect this with Claudius's expulsion of the Jews in AD 49 (Acts 18:2), but the author does not say which episode he means. They could rejoice because they knew they had "a better possession and an abiding one" (see 13:14).

**2. "Do not throw away your confidence."** "Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. For you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised" (10:35–36).
- **"Confidence":** the same word as in verse 19. To throw it away is to stop drawing near to God.

**3. Habakkuk's words.** "'But my righteous one shall live by faith, and if he shrinks back, my soul has no pleasure in him.' But we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls" (10:38–39).
- **The quotation:** verses 37–38 come from Habakkuk 2:3–4. The Hebrew says the vision will surely come; the Septuagint, which the author uses, reads it as a person who is coming, and the author applies this to Christ. He also reverses the order of the two lines, putting "shrinks back" last so that it leads straight into verse 39.
- **Paul and Hebrews:** Paul uses Habakkuk 2:4 for justification by faith (Rom 1:17); Hebrews uses it for persevering in faith to the end. These are not two kinds of faith. A person is justified not because of his endurance or his works, but solely by trusting Christ, who offered himself once and bore our sins in our place; and that same faith keeps him from shrinking back in suffering.
- **"But we are not":** after the warning, the author says "we" with confidence (see 6:9), and goes on in chapter 11 to list a long line of people of faith.

> The point: We are justified solely by trusting Christ, and we persevere to the end by that same faith, looking to the same Christ, for the coming one will come.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Therefore, brothers, since we have confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus, by the new and living way that he opened for us through the curtain, that is, through his flesh, and since we have a great priest over the house of God" (10:19–21). I never decided one day to stop going to church. After I moved to a new city, I wanted to settle in first and then look for a church; then work got busy and the baby came, and watching the livestream at home seemed good enough; later I stopped turning even that on. Or perhaps it went another way: I fell. Perhaps a marriage broke down, perhaps I ran up debts, perhaps it was something only a few people know about. I was afraid of the faces that knew me, afraid someone would ask how I had been, so I stayed away first. As time went on, staying away became a habit, "as is the habit of some" (10:25). Yet Hebrews does not first tell me to go back; it first tells me that the way is already open. Jesus opened it through his own flesh, his body torn on the cross for me as he bore the penalty of my sin. I come back to God not by first putting myself in order, but "by the blood of Jesus"; the heart that hides from people out of shame is the very "evil conscience" he sprinkles clean (10:22). I must also admit that sometimes I stay away not only because I am busy, but because I do not want to be known or encouraged, and would rather believe alone. There is pride in that, and I can confess it to him as well. And more than that, this great priest is "over the house of God." The gathering I have been avoiding is not someone else's place; it is his house, and he is the one who opened the door. He says, "in the midst of the congregation I will sing your praise" (2:12), there among the brothers and sisters he has redeemed. Of course, if what I left was a church that covered up sexual abuse, tolerated violence, or controlled people, that is not the "neglecting" this verse means; I can look for a safe church, and if someone has been harmed, reporting it to the police is right (Rom 13:4). Those who cannot come because of illness, caring for family, or shift work are seen by the Lord. Perhaps someone reading this has never yet walked this way at all. It was not opened for good people but for sinners: you can confess your sin, repent, and trust Jesus, who opened the way for you through his own flesh. Going back need not be done in one step: perhaps it is writing a few lines to a brother or sister or a pastor I once knew, saying that I want to come back but do not know how to begin; perhaps it is arriving a little early next Sunday and sitting at the back. The great priest over the house of God already knows every step I have taken these years, and still he has opened this new and living way for me through his own flesh, and waits in his house for me to come home.
