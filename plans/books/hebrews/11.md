## 本章脉络

第十章末了说「义人必因信得生」（10:38）。这样的信心是什么样子？作者打开旧约圣经，用一连串「因着信」，带读者看历代神的百姓怎样活、怎样死。全章分四段：**信是什么，从创造到挪亚**（1-7节）；**亚伯拉罕和众先祖**（8-22节）；**摩西和出埃及的百姓**（23-31节）；**说不完的见证**（32-40节），有人因信得胜，有人因信受苦至死，他们都要与我们一同得以完全。

这一章常被称为信心的英雄榜，其实名单上的人多有软弱和过犯。他们被记念，不是因为他们配，而是因为他们投靠了那位应许的神；他们所行的显出了信心，信心却不是功劳。这份名单一直延伸到12:2才停下来：「仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣」。

## 深读 11:1-7 所望之事的实底

**1. 「信就是所望之事的实底」。** 「信就是所望之事的实底，是未见之事的确据。古人在这信上得了美好的证据。」（11:1-2）
- **「实底」：** 这个词在1:3译作「本体」。解经家大致有两种理解。一种重在信的人里面的确信，许多现代译本这样译。另一种重在客观的实在：所盼望的事虽未临到，却是真实的，信心现在就站在上面（如雷恩、柯斯特；加尔文也说信心好像脚下的根基）。这两种看法并不互相排斥：信心的把握不是来自自己的感觉，而是来自神所应许之事的真实。
- **不是完整的定义：** 许多人指出，这一节不是给信心下全面的定义，而是描写信心做什么：把神的应许当作真实的，并照着去活。「未见之事」包括将来的事，也包括看不见的神自己（27节）。

**2. 「诸世界是藉神的话造成的」。** 「我们因着信，就知道诸世界是藉神的话造成的；这样，所看见的，并不是从显然之物造出来的。」（11:3）
这一节直接说的是，看得见的世界不是出于看得见的东西；圣经又说神是「使无变为有的神」（罗4:17）。根据这些经文，教会一直承认神从无中创造了万有。名单上第一件「因着信」的事，不是哪位英雄的事迹，而是「我们」领受神的话。

**3. 亚伯、以诺、挪亚。**
- **亚伯：** 他的祭为什么更美？作者说是「因着信」。耶和华先看中了亚伯，然后才看中他的供物（创4:4）。他的血向神哀告（创4:10），所以「仍旧说话」；耶稣所洒的血「所说的比亚伯的血所说的更美」（12:24），因为它带来的是赦免。
- **以诺：** 「以诺与神同行」（创5:24），七十士译本译作他蒙神喜悦，作者照这个读法，由此得出：「人非有信，就不能得神的喜悦；因为到神面前来的人必须信有神，且信他赏赐那寻求他的人。」（11:6）
- **挪亚：** 他「蒙神指示他未见的事，动了敬畏的心，预备了一只方舟」，就「承受了那从信而来的义」（11:7）。这正接上10:38「义人必因信得生」。

**4. 信心和行为。** 亚伯献祭，挪亚造方舟，都是行动；作者却每次都说「因着信」。真信心总会动起来（雅2:17），信心本身却不是一件赚得神喜悦的善行。「惟有不做工的，只信称罪人为义的神，他的信就算为义。」（罗4:5）信心蒙神喜悦，不是因为它本身有价值，而是因为它投靠了那「只一次献上他的身体」的基督（10:10）。

> 要点：信心把神的话当作真实的，并照着去活；我们不是靠信心这件善行得神喜悦，而是因信投靠那位一次献上自己的基督。

## 深读 11:8-22 要找一个家乡

**1. 「出去的时候，还不知往哪里去」。** 「亚伯拉罕因着信，蒙召的时候就遵命出去，往将来要得为业的地方去；出去的时候，还不知往哪里去。」（11:8）
- **住帐棚：** 他「在所应许之地作客」，三代人都住帐棚（11:9）。到撒拉死的时候，他在那地唯一买下的，是一块坟地（创23:4、19）。他等候的，是「那座有根基的城，就是神所经营所建造的」（11:10；参12:22）。
- **撒拉：** 她曾「心里暗笑」（创18:12）；希伯来书却没有提，只说「她以为那应许她的是可信的」（11:11）。神的应许常常从人已经没有指望的地方开始，「从一个仿佛已死的人就生出子孙」（11:12）。

**2. 「这些人都是存着信心死的」。** 「这些人都是存着信心死的，并没有得着所应许的；却从远处望见，且欢喜迎接，又承认自己在世上是客旅，是寄居的。」（11:13）
- **没有得着，却望见了：** 他们一生没有得着那地，却远远望见，就欢喜迎接（参约8:56）。回吾珥的路一直开着，他们却没有回去：「他们却羡慕一个更美的家乡，就是在天上的。」（11:16）
- **「神被称为他们的神，并不以为耻」：** 这群人住帐棚，也有过谎言和软弱（创12:13；16:2）。神却把自己的名和他们连在一起：「我是亚伯拉罕的神，以撒的神，雅各的神」（出3:6；太22:32）。神不以他们为耻的凭据是：「他已经给他们预备了一座城」（11:16）。

**3. 「他以为神还能叫人从死里复活」。** 「论到这儿子，曾有话说：『从以撒生的才要称为你的后裔。』他以为神还能叫人从死里复活；他也仿佛从死中得回他的儿子来。」（11:18-19）
- **他怎样想：** 第六章已讲过摩利亚山上的起誓；这里要看亚伯拉罕怎样想。应许全系在以撒身上，命令却叫他献上以撒。他推想，神必能叫以撒从死里复活，好成全应许，所以对仆人说：「我与童子往那里去拜一拜，就回到你们这里来」（创22:5）。
- **「仿佛」：** 原文的意思是当作比喻。许多人认为，以撒被「得回」预表复活：在摩利亚山上没有成为事实的，后来在神的儿子身上成了事实，他真的死了，第三天又复活了。

> 要点：信心常常意味着一生住帐棚，到死也没有看见应许成就。可是神不以这些寄居的人为耻；他为他们预备了一座城，他也能叫死人复活。

## 深读 11:23-31 如同看见那不能看见的主

**1. 「看为基督受的凌辱比埃及的财物更宝贵」。** 「他宁可和神的百姓同受苦害，也不愿暂时享受罪中之乐。他看为基督受的凌辱比埃及的财物更宝贵，因他想望所要得的赏赐。」（11:25-26）
- **「基督受的凌辱」：** 摩西在耶稣降生前一千多年，怎么能为「基督」受凌辱？有人认为，作者从基督已经来了的角度回头看，摩西所受的和基督后来所受的是同一类凌辱。有人指出「基督」原意是受膏者（参诗89:51），摩西是和神的百姓、神所膏的一同受辱。也有人像加尔文那样，认为基督和他的百姓本为一体，神的百姓从起初为他所受的羞辱，就是他的羞辱（参林前10:4）。这几种看法都同意：今天的读者也被邀请「出到营外……忍受他所受的凌辱」（13:13）。

**2. 「如同看见那不能看见的主」。** 「他因着信，就离开埃及，不怕王怒；因为他恒心忍耐，如同看见那不能看见的主。」（11:27）
- **哪一次离开：** 摩西逃往米甸时「便惧怕」（出2:14），所以许多人认为这里是指后来的出埃及；也有人认为仍是指逃往米甸，他怕的是杀人的事被人知道，却没有失去信心。
- **看见不能看见的：** 这正是第1节的「未见之事的确据」：摩西眼中有一位比法老更大的。

**3. 逾越节的血。** 「他因着信，就守逾越节，行洒血的礼，免得那灭长子的临近以色列人。」（11:28）那一夜，各家的差别不在于谁更好，而在于门上有没有血：「我一见这血，就越过你们去」（出12:13）。「我们逾越节的羔羊基督已经被杀献祭了。」（林前5:7）

**4. 妓女喇合。** 「妓女喇合因着信，曾和和平平地接待探子，就不与那些不顺从的人一同灭亡。」（11:31）
- **一个外人：** 她是迦南人，又是妓女，却投靠了以色列的神（书2:11）。雅各说她「因行为称义」（雅2:25），希伯来书说她「因着信」，两处并不矛盾：她冒险接待探子，正显出她真的信。
- **恩典的结局：** 她和父家都被救活了（书6:25），后来她还进了耶稣的家谱（太1:5）。这位神今天仍接纳被人看不起、自己也知道有罪的人。你不必先把过去洗白才来，只要承认自己的罪，悔改，投靠那位为罪人流血的基督。

> 要点：摩西看见了不能看见的主，所以宁可和神的百姓一同受辱。逾越节的血和喇合的得救都告诉我们：免于审判，不是因为我们比别人好，而是因为神所预备的拯救。

## 深读 11:32-40 世界不配有的人

**1. 「我又何必再说呢」。** 「若要一一细说，基甸、巴拉、参孙、耶弗他、大卫、撒母耳，和众先知的事，时候就不够了。」（11:32）
- **有缺点的名单：** 基甸造了以弗得，成了他全家的网罗（士8:27）；巴拉说「你若不同我去，我就不去」（士4:8）；参孙一生放纵，最后的祷告是求神让他报剜眼之仇（士16:28）；大卫犯了奸淫，又借刀杀人（撒下11章）。
- **这是恩典：** 旧约圣经没有掩盖他们的罪，神也没有因此把他们从名单上删去。他们被记念，不是因为品格完美，而是因为在关键的时候投靠了神。这也不是说罪不要紧：大卫说「我得罪耶和华了」，拿单才对他说「耶和华已经除掉你的罪」（撒下12:13）。

**2. 耶弗他的誓愿。** 他许愿说，先从家门出来迎接他的，「我也必将他献上为燔祭」（士11:31）；出来的是他的独生女。
- **两种理解：** 许多古今解经家（如布洛克）认为，他真的把女儿献上了；另一些人（如凯尔和德里慈，以及中世纪一些犹太解经家）认为，他是把女儿献给神终身守童身，因为经上接着说「女儿终身没有亲近男子」（士11:39）。
- **不可效法：** 不论哪一种，这誓愿都是轻率的。神明明禁止用儿女献祭（申12:31），也从不需要人用这样的交易换取胜利。希伯来书记念的，是「耶和华的灵降在耶弗他身上」（士11:29）时他信靠神去争战，不是他的誓愿。

**3. 得胜的和受苦的。**
- **得胜和受苦：** 有人「堵了狮子的口」（11:33；但6:22），有人「被锯锯死」（11:37，犹太传说以赛亚就是这样殉道的）；有人「不肯苟且得释放，为要得着更美的复活」（11:35），许多人认为是暗指马加比时期一位母亲和七个儿子殉道的事（《马加比二书》第七章）。
- **同一个信心，两种结局：** 有人因信「脱了刀剑的锋刃」，有人因信「被刀杀」。信心不是成功的公式；说信心够大就一定得医治、发财的，不是这一章的信心。他们是「世界不配有的人」（11:38）：世人把他们赶到旷野和山洞，神的评价却正好相反。

**4. 「若不与我们同得，就不能完全」。** 「这些人都是因信得了美好的证据，却仍未得着所应许的；因为神给我们预备了更美的事，叫他们若不与我们同得，就不能完全。」（11:39-40）
- **「所应许的」：** 33节说他们「得了应许」，是个别的应许；这里「仍未得着」的，是基督和他所带来的完全的救恩。
- **不是两条得救的路：** 前约的信徒不是走了另一条路，他们也是靠同一位基督得救，只是他们向前望（彼前1:10-11），我们回头看。基督「一次献祭，便叫那得以成圣的人永远完全」（10:14）。许多人认为，「被成全之义人的灵魂」（12:23）是说他们的灵魂如今已在神面前被成全，身体的复活还要等到末日与我们一同得着（罗8:23）。
- **一个百姓：** 古今的信徒是同一群人，在同一条路上（12:1）。
- **名单的终点：** 这一长串名字最后停在耶稣身上（12:2）。许多人指出，「创始」这个词2:10译作「元帅」，「成终」和40节的「完全」同一个字根。他信靠父走完全程，忍受了十字架，替我们担当了罪，如今「坐在神宝座的右边」。古人从远处望见的应许，在他身上成就了。

> 要点：这些人有的得胜，有的到死也没有看见应许成就，却没有一个被神遗忘。他们和我们是同一群百姓，要在基督里一同得以完全，因为他就是我们信心创始成终的那一位。

## 默想

**默想：** 「这些人都是存着信心死的，并没有得着所应许的；却从远处望见，且欢喜迎接」（11:13）。我有几个祷告，可能这一生都看不见答案。二十年前，我们七八个人每周三晚上为这座城市的复兴祷告，后来那间小教会关了门。有一个我看着长大的孩子，多年前离开了教会，也离开了家。还有一件我亲眼看见的不公义的事，受害的人至今还在等一个公道。我曾经迫切地为这些事祷告，后来渐渐不提了，心里对神冷淡下来，甚至有点苦毒，好像他把我的祷告放进了一个没人打开的抽屉。诗人可以问「要到几时呢？」（诗13:1），这样的哀告不是不信；可是那份冷淡和苦毒是罪，我需要向他承认，他的血也为这罪流过。亚伯拉罕到死只得着一块坟地，却「从远处望见，且欢喜迎接」，因为他认识那位应许的神。我能不能把这些还没有成就的祷告，当作从远处望见的应许，而不是被神遗忘的档案？

再想一想：「若不与我们同得，就不能完全」（11:40）。当年和我一起祷告的那位老姊妹已经离世了，到死也没有看见这座城市复兴。她的祷告作废了吗？希伯来书说，她和亚伯拉罕、摩西一样，属于一群还在路上的百姓，要和我们一同得以完全；我的祷告也不必在我手里完成。这条路的终点不在于我的信心够不够坚定，而在于耶稣，「为我们信心创始成终的」那一位（12:2）。他忍受了十字架，替我们担当了罪，如今坐在神宝座的右边，替我们这群还在路上的人领受了应许。这群人里没有一个是靠自己的义走进来的；如果你还没有投靠他，今天也可以悔改，信靠他。神既然不以那些带着未成就的祷告死去的人为耻，他会因为我常常灰心，甚至一度不再祷告，就以我为耻吗？「所以神被称为他们的神，并不以为耻，因为他已经给他们预备了一座城。」（11:16）

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 10 ended with "my righteous one shall live by faith" (10:38). What does such faith look like? The author opens the Old Testament and, with a long series of "by faith," shows how God's people in every age lived and died. The chapter has four parts: **what faith is, from creation to Noah** (vv. 1–7); **Abraham and the patriarchs** (vv. 8–22); **Moses and the exodus people** (vv. 23–31); and **more than time allows** (vv. 32–40), where some through faith triumph and some through faith suffer to the point of death, and all are to be made perfect together with us.

This chapter is often called the hall of heroes of faith, yet many on the list were weak and sinful. They are remembered not because they deserved it but because they took refuge in the God who promised; what they did showed their faith, but faith is not a merit. The list runs on until it comes to rest in 12:2: "looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith."

## Going Deeper: 11:1–7 The Assurance of Things Hoped For

**1. "Faith is the assurance of things hoped for."** "Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen. For by it the people of old received their commendation" (11:1–2).
- **"Assurance":** the same word is translated "nature" in 1:3. Interpreters take it in two main ways. One stresses the believer's inner confidence, and many modern translations render it so. The other stresses objective reality: what we hope for has not yet come but is real, and faith stands on it now (Lane and Koester, among others; Calvin likens faith to the ground beneath our feet). The two views do not exclude each other: faith's confidence comes not from our feelings but from the reality of what God has promised.
- **Not a full definition:** many point out that this verse does not give a complete definition of faith but describes what faith does: it treats God's promise as real and lives by it. "Things not seen" include future things and the invisible God himself (v. 27).

**2. "Created by the word of God."** "By faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible" (11:3).
The verse itself says the visible world did not come from visible things; Scripture also calls God the one who "calls into existence the things that do not exist" (Rom 4:17). On the basis of these texts the church has always confessed that God created all things out of nothing. The first "by faith" on the list is not a hero's deed but "we" receiving God's word.

**3. Abel, Enoch and Noah.**
- **Abel:** why was his sacrifice better? The author says "by faith." The LORD had regard first for Abel, then for his offering (Gen 4:4). His blood cried out to God (Gen 4:10), so "he still speaks"; Jesus' sprinkled blood "speaks a better word than the blood of Abel" (12:24), because it brings forgiveness.
- **Enoch:** "Enoch walked with God" (Gen 5:24); the Septuagint renders this "pleased God," and the author follows that reading and draws the lesson: "And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him" (11:6).
- **Noah:** "being warned by God concerning events as yet unseen, in reverent fear constructed an ark," and so "became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith" (11:7). This ties straight back to 10:38, "my righteous one shall live by faith."

**4. Faith and works.** Abel offered a sacrifice and Noah built an ark; these are actions, yet each time the author says "by faith." True faith always moves (Jas 2:17), but faith itself is not a good work that earns God's pleasure. "To the one who does not work but trusts him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is counted as righteousness" (Rom 4:5). Faith pleases God not because it has value in itself, but because it takes refuge in Christ, whose body was offered "once for all" (10:10).

> The point: Faith takes God's word as true and lives by it; we do not please God by the good work of believing, but by faith take refuge in Christ, who offered himself once for all.

## Going Deeper: 11:8–22 Seeking a Homeland

**1. "Not knowing where he was going."** "By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place that he was to receive as an inheritance. And he went out, not knowing where he was going" (11:8).
- **Living in tents:** he lived "in the land of promise, as in a foreign land," and three generations lived in tents (11:9). When Sarah died, the only land he ever bought there was a grave (Gen 23:4, 19). He was waiting for "the city that has foundations, whose designer and builder is God" (11:10; see 12:22).
- **Sarah:** she once "laughed to herself" (Gen 18:12); Hebrews does not mention it, only that "she considered him faithful who had promised" (11:11). God's promises often begin where human hope has run out: "from one man, and him as good as dead, were born descendants" (11:12).

**2. "These all died in faith."** "These all died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar, and having acknowledged that they were strangers and exiles on the earth" (11:13).
- **Not received, yet seen:** in their lifetime they never possessed the land, yet they saw it from afar and greeted it with joy (see John 8:56). The road back to Ur stayed open, but they did not take it, because "they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one" (11:16).
- **"God is not ashamed to be called their God":** these people lived in tents and had their lies and weaknesses (Gen 12:13; 16:2). Yet God tied his name to theirs: "I am... the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" (Exod 3:6; Matt 22:32). The proof that God is not ashamed of them is this: "he has prepared for them a city" (11:16).

**3. "He considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead."** "...of whom it was said, 'Through Isaac shall your offspring be named.' He considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead, from which, figuratively speaking, he did receive him back" (11:18–19).
- **How he reasoned:** chapter 6 looked at the oath on Moriah; here we see how Abraham reasoned. The whole promise hung on Isaac, yet the command was to offer Isaac up. He concluded that God could raise Isaac from the dead to keep his promise, and so he told his servants, "I and the boy will go over there and worship and come again to you" (Gen 22:5).
- **"Figuratively speaking":** the Greek means "as a figure." Many take Isaac's being "received back" as a figure of resurrection: what did not become fact on Moriah became fact in God's Son, who truly died and on the third day rose again.

> The point: Faith often means living in tents all one's life and dying without seeing the promise fulfilled. Yet God is not ashamed of these exiles; he has prepared a city for them, and he is able to raise the dead.

## Going Deeper: 11:23–31 As Seeing Him Who Is Invisible

**1. "The reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt."** "...choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of God than to enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin. He considered the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking to the reward" (11:25–26).
- **"The reproach of Christ":** how could Moses, more than a thousand years before Jesus' birth, bear reproach for "Christ"? Some take it that the author looks back from Christ's coming, and Moses bore the same kind of reproach Christ would later bear. Others note that "Christ" means "anointed one" (see Ps 89:51), so Moses was reproached together with God's people and his anointed. Still others, as Calvin puts it, hold that Christ and his people are one, so the reproach God's people bore for him from the beginning was his (see 1 Cor 10:4). All agree that readers today are invited to "go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured" (13:13).

**2. "As seeing him who is invisible."** "By faith he left Egypt, not being afraid of the anger of the king, for he endured as seeing him who is invisible" (11:27).
- **Which departure?** Before fleeing to Midian, "Moses was afraid" (Exod 2:14), so many think this verse means the later exodus; others think it still means the flight to Midian: he feared that his killing had become known, but did not lose faith.
- **Seeing the invisible:** this is the "conviction of things not seen" of verse 1: Moses had in view one greater than Pharaoh.

**3. The blood of the Passover.** "By faith he kept the Passover and sprinkled the blood, so that the Destroyer of the firstborn might not touch them" (11:28). That night the difference between the houses was not which family was better, but whether there was blood on the door: "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exod 12:13). "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (1 Cor 5:7).

**4. Rahab the prostitute.** "By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient, because she had given a friendly welcome to the spies" (11:31).
- **An outsider:** she was a Canaanite and a prostitute, yet she took refuge in Israel's God (Josh 2:11). James says she was "justified by works" (Jas 2:25), Hebrews says "by faith," and the two do not conflict: her risky welcome of the spies showed that she truly believed.
- **The ending grace wrote:** she and her father's household were saved alive (Josh 6:25), and later she entered Jesus' genealogy (Matt 1:5). This God still receives people whom others despise and who know they have sinned. You do not have to clean up your past before you come; confess your sin, repent, and take refuge in Christ, who shed his blood for sinners.

> The point: Moses saw him who is invisible, and so chose to bear reproach with God's people. The Passover blood and Rahab's rescue both tell us that we escape judgment not because we are better than others, but because of the rescue God has provided.

## Going Deeper: 11:32–40 Of Whom the World Was Not Worthy

**1. "What more shall I say?"** "For time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets" (11:32).
- **A list of flawed people:** Gideon made an ephod that became a snare (Judg 8:27); Barak said, "if you will not go with me, I will not go" (Judg 4:8); Samson lived a self-indulgent life, and his last prayer was to be avenged "for my two eyes" (Judg 16:28); David committed adultery and arranged a murder (2 Sam 11).
- **This is grace:** the Old Testament does not hide their sins, and God did not strike them off the list because of them. They are remembered not for flawless character but because at the decisive moment they took refuge in God. Nor does this make sin a small thing: David said, "I have sinned against the LORD," and then Nathan said, "The LORD also has put away your sin" (2 Sam 12:13).

**2. Jephthah's vow.** He vowed that whatever first came out of his house to meet him, "I will offer it up for a burnt offering" (Judg 11:31); the one who came out was his only daughter.
- **Two readings:** many interpreters ancient and modern (for example Block) think he actually offered her; others (for example Keil and Delitzsch, and some medieval Jewish interpreters) think he dedicated her to God to live unmarried, since the text adds, "She had never known a man" (Judg 11:39).
- **Not to be imitated:** on either reading the vow was rash. God plainly forbids offering children (Deut 12:31), and he never needs such bargains to give victory. What Hebrews remembers is that when "the Spirit of the LORD was upon Jephthah" (Judg 11:29) he trusted God and went to war, not his vow.

**3. Those who triumphed and those who suffered.**
- **Triumph and suffering:** some "stopped the mouths of lions" (11:33; Dan 6:22), others "were sawn in two" (11:37; Jewish tradition says Isaiah was martyred this way); some refused "to accept release, so that they might rise again to a better life" (11:35), which many think alludes to the martyrdom of a mother and her seven sons in the Maccabean period (2 Maccabees chapter 7).
- **One faith, two outcomes:** some through faith "escaped the edge of the sword," others "were killed with the sword." Faith is not a formula for success; the promise that big enough faith brings healing and wealth is not the faith of this chapter. They are those "of whom the world was not worthy" (11:38): the world drove them into deserts and caves, but God's verdict is exactly the reverse.

**4. "Apart from us they should not be made perfect."** "And all these, though commended through their faith, did not receive what was promised, since God had provided something better for us, that apart from us they should not be made perfect" (11:39–40).
- **"What was promised":** verse 33 says they "obtained promises," particular ones; what they "did not receive" here is Christ and the full salvation he brings.
- **Not two ways of salvation:** believers under the old covenant were not saved another way; they too were saved by the same Christ, looking forward (1 Pet 1:10–11) as we look back. "By a single offering he has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified" (10:14). Many take "the spirits of the righteous made perfect" (12:23) to mean that their spirits are now perfected in God's presence, while the resurrection of the body waits for the last day, with us (Rom 8:23).
- **One people:** believers of every age are one people on one road (12:1).
- **Where the list ends:** this long line of names comes to rest on Jesus (12:2). Many note that "founder" is the word in 2:10, and "perfecter" shares a root with "made perfect" in verse 40. He ran the whole course trusting his Father, endured the cross bearing our sins, and now "is seated at the right hand of the throne of God." The promise the ancients greeted from afar is fulfilled in him.

> The point: Some of these people triumphed and some died without seeing the promise fulfilled, but not one was forgotten by God. They and we are one people, to be made perfect together in Christ, for he is the founder and perfecter of our faith.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "These all died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar" (11:13). I have a few prayers whose answers I may never see in this life. Twenty years ago, seven or eight of us met every Wednesday evening to pray for revival in our city; later that small church closed. There is a young person I watched grow up who left the church years ago, and left home too. And there is an injustice I saw with my own eyes; the one who was wronged is still waiting for justice. I once prayed urgently about these things; slowly I stopped mentioning them, and my heart grew cold toward God, even a little bitter, as if he had put my prayers in a drawer no one opens. The psalmist may ask "How long, O LORD?" (Ps 13:1), and such lament is not unbelief; but that coldness and bitterness are sin, and I need to confess them to him, and his blood was shed for this sin too. Abraham died owning only a grave, yet he greeted the promise from afar, because he knew the God who had promised. Can I hold these unfulfilled prayers as promises greeted from afar, rather than as files God has forgotten?

Then think again: "apart from us they should not be made perfect" (11:40). The elderly sister who prayed with me back then has died, and to the end she never saw our city revived. Were her prayers wasted? Hebrews says that she, like Abraham and Moses, belongs to a people still on the way, who will be made perfect together with us; my prayers do not have to be completed in my own hands either. The end of this road does not rest on whether my faith is firm enough, but on Jesus, "the founder and perfecter of our faith" (12:2). He endured the cross and bore our sins, and now he is seated at the right hand of the throne of God, and has received the promise for us, his people still on the road. Not one person in this company walked in by their own righteousness; if you have never yet taken refuge in him, today you too can repent and trust him. If God is not ashamed of those who died with their prayers unfulfilled, will he be ashamed of me because I so often lose heart, and for a time even stopped praying? "Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God, for he has prepared for them a city" (11:16).
