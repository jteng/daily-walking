## 本章脉络

第十一章紧接着10:38-39：「义人必因信得生」，「我们却不是退后入沉沦的那等人，乃是有信心以致灵魂得救的人」。什么是这样的信心？作者没有先下一个抽象的定义，而是打开旧约圣经，用一连串「因着信」，带读者看历代神的百姓是怎样活、怎样死的。全章可以分成四段。**信是什么，从创造到挪亚**（1-7节）：信是所望之事的实底，未见之事的确据；我们因着信，知道诸世界是藉神的话造成的；亚伯、以诺、挪亚都因信得了神的称许。**亚伯拉罕和众先祖**（8-22节）：亚伯拉罕蒙召出去，不知往哪里去；撒拉过了生育的岁数还能怀孕；作者在中间停下来说，这些人都是存着信心死的，神被称为他们的神并不以为耻；亚伯拉罕把以撒献上，以为神能叫人从死里复活；以撒、雅各、约瑟临终时都望着将来。**摩西和出埃及的百姓**（23-31节）：摩西看为基督受的凌辱比埃及的财物更宝贵；百姓守逾越节，过红海，耶利哥城墙倒塌，妓女喇合得救。**说不完的见证**（32-40节）：基甸、巴拉、参孙、耶弗他、大卫、撒母耳和众先知；有人因信得胜，有人因信受苦至死；他们都未得着所应许的，因为神要他们与我们一同得以完全。

这一章常被称为信心的英雄榜，其实名单上的人多有软弱和过犯。他们被记念，不是因为他们配，而是因为他们投靠了那位应许的神。他们所行的显出了信心，信心本身却不是换取神喜悦的功劳。整章的名单一直向前延伸，到12:2才停下来：「仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣」。

## 深读 11:1-7 信就是所望之事的实底

**1. 「信就是所望之事的实底」。** 「信就是所望之事的实底，是未见之事的确据。古人在这信上得了美好的证据。」（11:1-2）
- **「实底」：** 这个词在1:3译作「本体」（「神本体的真像」），在3:14译作「确实的信心」。解经家的理解大致有两种。一种重在信的人里面的确信：信心就是对所盼望之事的把握，许多现代译本都这样译。另一种重在客观的实在：那些还没有临到的事是真实的，信心使人现在就站在这实在上面；雷恩、柯斯特等人强调这一面，加尔文也说信心好像我们脚下的根基，叫我们可以站立。这两种看法并不互相排斥：信心的把握不是来自自己的感觉，而是来自神所应许之事的真实。
- **「未见之事的确据」：** 「确据」的意思是叫人信服的凭据。「未见之事」既包括将来的事（7节挪亚的洪水），也包括现在看不见的事，最终是看不见的神自己（27节）。
- **不是完整的定义：** 许多人指出，第1节不是给信心下一个全面的定义，而是描写信心做什么：它把神的应许当作真实的，并照着去活。全章的「因着信」，都是这一节的注脚。
- **「美好的证据」：** 原文的意思是得了神的称许。第2节和第39节前后呼应，把整章包了起来。

**2. 「诸世界是藉神的话造成的」。** 「我们因着信，就知道诸世界是藉神的话造成的；这样，所看见的，并不是从显然之物造出来的。」（11:3）
- **藉神的话：** 「神说……就有」（创1:3）；「诸天藉耶和华的命而造」（诗33:6）。1:2说神「藉着他儿子」创造诸世界，用的是同一个词。
- **从无中创造：** 这一节直接说的是，看得见的世界不是出于看得见的东西。保罗说神是「使无变为有的神」（罗4:17），约翰说「万物是藉着他造的」（约1:3）。根据这些经文，教会一直承认神从无中创造了万有。
- **信心的第一步：** 名单上第一件「因着信」的事，不是哪一位英雄的作为，而是「我们」明白世界是神造的。信心先是领受神的话，然后才是照着神的话去行。

**3. 亚伯、以诺、挪亚。** 「亚伯因着信，献祭与神，比该隐所献的更美……他虽然死了，却因这信，仍旧说话。」（11:4）
- **亚伯的祭为什么更美：** 有人认为在于祭物本身，他献的是「羊群中头生的和羊的脂油」（创4:4）；也有人认为在于这是流血的祭。作者的答案却是「因着信」。创世记说，「耶和华看中了亚伯和他的供物」（创4:4），先看中人，然后才看中供物。该隐的行为是恶的（约一3:12），问题在心，不只在祭物。
- **「仍旧说话」：** 亚伯的血从地里向神哀告（创4:10）。12:24说，耶稣所洒的血「所说的比亚伯的血所说的更美」：亚伯的血求伸冤，耶稣的血带来赦免。
- **以诺：** 创世记说「以诺与神同行」（创5:24），七十士译本译作他蒙神喜悦，作者照这个读法，由此得出第6节：「人非有信，就不能得神的喜悦；因为到神面前来的人必须信有神，且信他赏赐那寻求他的人。」信心有两面：信神是真实的，也信他不会叫寻求他的人落空。人所寻求的是他自己，所以他给的赏赐，最终也离不开他自己。
- **挪亚：** 他「蒙神指示他未见的事」，就是还没有发生的洪水，「动了敬畏的心，预备了一只方舟」（11:7）。他的顺服显出了那世代的不信；他也「承受了那从信而来的义」。这是本章第一次说到「义」，正好接上10:38「义人必因信得生」。

**4. 信心和行为。** 亚伯献祭，挪亚造方舟，都是行动；可是作者每一次都说「因着信」。
- **行为显出信心：** 真信心总会动起来；没有行动的信心是死的（雅2:17）。
- **信心不是功劳：** 信心本身也不是一件赚得神喜悦的善行。亚伯拉罕「信耶和华，耶和华就以此为他的义」（创15:6）；「惟有不做工的，只信称罪人为义的神，他的信就算为义」（罗4:5）。信心之所以蒙神喜悦，不是因为它本身有什么价值，而是因为它投靠那位神所喜悦的：神的儿子「只一次献上他的身体」，我们「就得以成圣」（10:10）。

> 要点：信心不是对自己的信心，而是把神的话当作真实的，并照着去活。我们不是靠信心这件善行得神的喜悦，而是因信投靠那位一次献上自己的基督。

## 深读 11:8-22 要找一个家乡

**1. 「出去的时候，还不知往哪里去」。** 「亚伯拉罕因着信，蒙召的时候就遵命出去，往将来要得为业的地方去；出去的时候，还不知往哪里去。」（11:8）
- **先听见，后看见：** 神对他说「往我所要指示你的地去」（创12:1），没有先给他一张地图。信心听见神的话就动身。
- **在应许之地作客：** 「他因着信，就在所应许之地作客，好像在异地居住帐棚，与那同蒙一个应许的以撒、雅各一样。」（11:9）三代人都住帐棚。到撒拉死的时候，亚伯拉罕在那地唯一买下的，是一块坟地（创23:4、19）。
- **「有根基的城」：** 「因为他等候那座有根基的城，就是神所经营所建造的。」（11:10）帐棚没有根基；他等的是「永生神的城邑，就是天上的耶路撒冷」（12:22；参13:14）。

**2. 撒拉。** 「因着信，连撒拉自己，虽然过了生育的岁数，还能怀孕，因她以为那应许她的是可信的。」（11:11）
- **主语是谁：** 和合本照多数译本，以撒拉为主语；也有一些现代译本和解经家认为主语仍是亚伯拉罕，撒拉只是附带提到，因为原文「怀孕」的说法通常用在男人身上。不论哪一种读法，这一节都记念了撒拉。
- **神记念的是她的信：** 撒拉听见应许时曾「心里暗笑」（创18:12）。希伯来书却没有提她的笑，只说她「以为那应许她的是可信的」。神看重的，是她最后投靠了那位信实的神。
- **「仿佛已死的人」：** 保罗也说，亚伯拉罕想到「自己的身体如同已死」，他的信心还是不软弱（罗4:19）。神的应许常常从人已经没有指望的地方开始。

**3. 「这些人都是存着信心死的」。** 「这些人都是存着信心死的，并没有得着所应许的；却从远处望见，且欢喜迎接，又承认自己在世上是客旅，是寄居的。」（11:13）
- **没有得着，却望见了：** 他们一生没有见到子孙多如天上的星，也没有得着那地。他们却「从远处望见，且欢喜迎接」，好像远远看见要来的人就向他招手。耶稣说：「你们的祖宗亚伯拉罕欢欢喜喜地仰望我的日子」（约8:56）。
- **客旅，寄居的：** 亚伯拉罕对赫人说：「我在你们中间是外人，是寄居的」（创23:4）。彼得也用这话称呼信徒（彼前2:11）。
- **不回去：** 「他们若想念所离开的家乡，还有可以回去的机会。他们却羡慕一个更美的家乡，就是在天上的。」（11:15-16）回吾珥的路一直开着，他们却没有回去。
- **「神被称为他们的神，并不以为耻」：** 这群人住帐棚，没有自己的地，也有过谎言和软弱（创12:13；16:2）。神却把自己的名字和他们连在一起：在他们死后几百年，他仍对摩西说「我是亚伯拉罕的神，以撒的神，雅各的神」（出3:6）。耶稣就用这句话证明死人复活：「神不是死人的神，乃是活人的神」（太22:32）。神不以他们为耻的凭据是：「他已经给他们预备了一座城」（11:16；参约14:2）。

**4. 「他以为神还能叫人从死里复活」。** 「亚伯拉罕因着信，被试验的时候，就把以撒献上……论到这儿子，曾有话说：『从以撒生的才要称为你的后裔。』他以为神还能叫人从死里复活；他也仿佛从死中得回他的儿子来。」（11:17-19）
- **应许和命令的冲突：** 应许全系在以撒身上（创21:12），命令却叫他献上以撒。第六章已经讲过神在摩利亚山上的起誓和那只代替以撒的公羊；这里作者让我们看亚伯拉罕心里怎样想。
- **他怎样想：** 他没有在两者之间选一样，而是推想神必能叫以撒从死里复活，好成全应许。所以他对仆人说：「我与童子往那里去拜一拜，就回到你们这里来」（创22:5）。
- **「仿佛」：** 原文的意思是作为比喻。许多人认为，以撒被「得回」预表了复活：在摩利亚山上没有成为事实的，后来在各各他成了事实。神的儿子真的死了，第三天又复活了。

**5. 临终的信心。** 以撒「指着将来的事」祝福，雅各临死时祝福约瑟的两个儿子，约瑟「临终的时候，提到以色列族将来要出，并为自己的骸骨留下遗命」（11:20-22）。约瑟一生大半在埃及，位高权重，却要人把他的骸骨带回一块他从未得着的地（创50:25；出13:19）。他们到死都望着前面。

> 要点：信心常常意味着一生住帐棚，到死也没有看见应许成就。可是神不以这些寄居的人为耻；他为他们预备了一座城，他能叫死人复活。

## 深读 11:23-31 如同看见那不能看见的主

**1. 「看为基督受的凌辱比埃及的财物更宝贵」。** 「摩西因着信，长大了就不肯称为法老女儿之子。他宁可和神的百姓同受苦害，也不愿暂时享受罪中之乐。他看为基督受的凌辱比埃及的财物更宝贵，因他想望所要得的赏赐。」（11:24-26）
- **放弃身份：** 摩西「学了埃及人一切的学问」（徒7:22），前途无量。他的父母当年「并不怕王命」把他藏起来（11:23）；如今他自己也不肯留在王宫。
- **「罪中之乐」：** 宫中的享受本身未必是罪；可是对摩西来说，留下就是背弃神的百姓，所以成了「罪中之乐」，而且只是「暂时」的。
- **「基督受的凌辱」：** 摩西在耶稣降生以前一千多年，怎么能为「基督」受凌辱？解经家有几种理解。一种认为，作者从基督已经来了的角度回头看，摩西所受的，和后来基督所受的是同一类凌辱。一种指出，「基督」的原意是受膏者，诗篇说「你的仇敌……羞辱了你受膏者的脚踪」（诗89:51），神的百姓和他所膏立的王一同受辱，摩西就是加入了他们。还有一种，像加尔文所说，基督和他的百姓本为一体，从起初神的百姓为他所受的羞辱，就是基督的羞辱（参林前10:4）。这几种看法都同意：读者今天也被邀请「出到营外……忍受他所受的凌辱」（13:13）。
- **「想望所要得的赏赐」：** 这不是精明的投资，而是看见了更宝贵的东西。

**2. 「他恒心忍耐，如同看见那不能看见的主」。** 「他因着信，就离开埃及，不怕王怒；因为他恒心忍耐，如同看见那不能看见的主。」（11:27）
- **哪一次离开：** 摩西第一次离开埃及是逃往米甸，那时经上说「摩西便惧怕」（出2:14-15）。所以许多人认为这里是指后来带领百姓出埃及；也有人认为仍是指逃往米甸，他怕的是杀人的事被人知道，却不是在王面前失了信心。
- **看见不能看见的：** 这正是第1节说的「未见之事的确据」。摩西能在法老面前站住，因为他眼中有一位比法老更大的。

**3. 逾越节、红海、耶利哥。** 「他因着信，就守逾越节，行洒血的礼，免得那灭长子的临近以色列人。」（11:28）
- **免于审判是因为血：** 那一夜，以色列人的家和埃及人的家，差别不在于谁更好，而在于门上有没有血。神说：「我一见这血，就越过你们去」（出12:13）。保罗说：「我们逾越节的羔羊基督已经被杀献祭了。」（林前5:7）
- **一同走过的百姓：** 「他们因着信，过红海如行干地」（11:29），这些人里也有后来在旷野因不信倒毙的（3:16-19）。耶利哥城墙倒塌，不是靠攻城的器械，而是靠神的话，绕城七日（11:30）。

**4. 妓女喇合。** 「妓女喇合因着信，曾和和平平地接待探子，就不与那些不顺从的人一同灭亡。」（11:31）
- **一个外人：** 她是迦南人，又是妓女。她说：「耶和华－你们的神本是上天下地的神。」（书2:11）在全城的人里面，只有她投靠了以色列的神。
- **信心和行为：** 雅各说她「因行为称义」（雅2:25），希伯来书说她「因着信」。两处并不矛盾：她冒险接待探子，正显出她真的信；雅各要说的是，活的信心必有行为。
- **恩典的结局：** 她把朱红线绳系在窗户上（书2:21），她和她父家都被救活了（书6:25）；后来她还进了耶稣的家谱（太1:5）。今天，这位神仍然接纳被人看不起、自己也知道有罪的人。你不必先把过去洗白才来，只要承认自己的罪，悔改，投靠那位为罪人流血的基督。

> 要点：摩西看见了不能看见的主，所以宁可和神的百姓一同受辱。逾越节的血、喇合的朱红线绳，都告诉我们：免于审判，不是因为我们比别人好，而是因为神所预备的拯救。

## 深读 11:32-40 世界不配有的人

**1. 「我又何必再说呢」。** 「我又何必再说呢？若要一一细说，基甸、巴拉、参孙、耶弗他、大卫、撒母耳，和众先知的事，时候就不够了。」（11:32）
- **有缺点的名单：** 基甸先要羊毛的兆头（士6:37），后来又造了以弗得，「这就作了基甸和他全家的网罗」（士8:27）。巴拉说：「你若同我去，我就去；你若不同我去，我就不去。」（士4:8）参孙一生放纵，最后的祷告是求神让他报剜眼之仇（士16:28）。大卫犯了奸淫，又借刀杀人（撒下11-12章）。
- **这是恩典：** 旧约圣经没有掩盖他们的罪，神也没有因为这些罪把他们从名单上删去。他们被记念，不是因为品格完美，而是因为在关键的时候投靠了神。神的名单上，从来只有蒙恩的罪人。这不是说他们的罪不要紧：大卫认罪说「我得罪耶和华了」，拿单才对他说「耶和华已经除掉你的罪」（撒下12:13）。

**2. 耶弗他的誓愿。** 耶弗他向耶和华许愿：「我从亚扪人那里平平安安回来的时候，无论什么人，先从我家门出来迎接我，就必归你，我也必将他献上为燔祭。」（士11:31）出来的是他的独生女。
- **两种理解：** 许多古今解经家（如布洛克）认为，他真的把女儿献为燔祭了。另一些人（如凯尔和德里慈，以及中世纪一些犹太解经家）认为，他是把女儿献给神，终身守童身事奉，因为经上接着说「女儿终身没有亲近男子」（士11:39）。
- **不可效法：** 不论哪一种，这个誓愿都是轻率的。神明明禁止人用儿女献祭（申12:31），也从来不需要人用这样的交易来换取胜利。希伯来书记念的，是他在「耶和华的灵降在耶弗他身上」（士11:29）时信靠神去争战，不是他的誓愿。

**3. 得胜的和受苦的。**
- **得胜：** 「他们因着信，制伏了敌国，行了公义，得了应许，堵了狮子的口，灭了烈火的猛势」（11:33-34）。但以理说：「我的神差遣使者，封住狮子的口」（但6:22）；三个朋友在火窑中没有被烧（但3章）；「有妇人得自己的死人复活」（11:35），像撒勒法的寡妇和书念的妇人（王上17:22；王下4章）。
- **受苦：** 「又有人忍受严刑，不肯苟且得释放，为要得着更美的复活。」（11:35）许多人认为，这里暗指马加比时期一位母亲和她七个儿子殉道的事（《马加比二书》第七章）。「被石头打死」，像祭司撒迦利亚（代下24:21）；「被刀杀」，像先知乌利亚（耶26:23）；「被锯锯死」，犹太传说以赛亚就是这样殉道的；「披着绵羊山羊的皮」，令人想起以利亚（王下1:8）。和合本37节的「受试探」，有些抄本没有。
- **同一个信心，两种结局：** 有人因信脱了刀剑的锋刃，有人因信被刀杀。信心不是保证成功的公式；凡说「信心够大就一定得医治、发财、平安」的，都不是这一章的信心。神没有向那些到死也没有得救脱的人失信。
- **「本是世界不配有的人」：** 世人把他们当作流浪汉、罪犯，赶到旷野和山洞里（11:38）；神的评价却正好相反。

**4. 「若不与我们同得，就不能完全」。** 「这些人都是因信得了美好的证据，却仍未得着所应许的；因为神给我们预备了更美的事，叫他们若不与我们同得，就不能完全。」（11:39-40）
- **「所应许的」：** 33节说他们「得了应许」，指的是个别的应许；这里说他们「仍未得着所应许的」，指的是最终的应许：基督和他所带来的完全的救恩。
- **「更美的事」：** 基督已经来了，「他一次献祭，便叫那得以成圣的人永远完全」（10:14）。
- **不是两条得救的路：** 这不是说前约的信徒得的救恩比较差，或是走了另一条路。他们也是靠同一位基督得救，只是他们向前望（彼前1:10-11），我们回头看。他们得以完全，要等基督的工作成就。12:23说到「被成全之义人的灵魂」，许多人认为，基督升天以后，他们的灵魂已经在神面前被成全；身体的复活，还要等到末日与我们一同得着（罗8:23）。
- **一个百姓：** 神没有让他们先到终点，也没有让我们单独走。古今的信徒是同一群人，还在同一条路上。所以12:1说：「我们既有这许多的见证人，如同云彩围着我们」。
- **名单的终点：** 这一长串名字最后停在耶稣身上：「仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣」（12:2）。「创始」这个词，2:10译作「元帅」；「成终」和40节的「完全」同一个字根。他自己凭着信靠父走完了全程，「因那摆在前面的喜乐，就轻看羞辱，忍受了十字架的苦难」，替我们担当了罪；如今「坐在神宝座的右边」（12:2）。古人所望见的应许，在他身上成就了。

> 要点：这些人有的得胜，有的到死也没有看见应许成就；可是没有一个被神遗忘。他们和我们是同一群百姓，要在基督里一同得以完全，因为他就是我们信心创始成终的那一位。

**默想：** 「这些人都是存着信心死的，并没有得着所应许的；却从远处望见，且欢喜迎接」（11:13）。我有几个祷告，可能这一生都看不见答案。二十年前，我们七八个人每周三晚上在一间小教会里，为这座城市的复兴祷告；后来教会没有复兴，反倒分裂，最后关了门。有一个我看着长大的孩子，多年前离开了教会，也离开了家，消息越来越少。还有一件我亲眼看见的不公义的事，受害的人至今还在等一个公道，害人的人却过得很好。我曾经为这些事迫切祷告，后来渐渐不提了。有时候，我不是在等候，而是在心里对神冷淡下来，甚至有一点苦毒，好像他把我的祷告放进了一个没人打开的抽屉。诗人可以问「要到几时呢？」（诗13:1），哈巴谷也可以问（哈1:2）；这样的哀告不是不信。可是我心里那份冷淡和苦毒是罪，我需要把它带到他面前认罪；他的血也为这罪流过。亚伯拉罕到死只得着一块坟地，却「从远处望见，且欢喜迎接」；他欢喜，不是因为看见了答案，而是因为认识那位应许的神。我能不能把这些还没有成就的祷告，当作从远处望见的应许，而不是被神遗忘的档案？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 11 follows straight on from 10:38–39: "my righteous one shall live by faith," and "we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls." What does such faith look like? Instead of giving an abstract definition first, the author opens the Old Testament and, with a long series of "by faith," shows how God's people in every age lived and died. The chapter falls into four parts. **What faith is, from creation to Noah** (vv. 1–7): faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen; by faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God; Abel, Enoch and Noah were commended through faith. **Abraham and the patriarchs** (vv. 8–22): Abraham went out when he was called, not knowing where he was going; Sarah conceived when she was past the age; in the middle the author pauses to say that these all died in faith and that God is not ashamed to be called their God; Abraham offered up Isaac, considering that God was able to raise the dead; Isaac, Jacob and Joseph looked ahead to the future as they died. **Moses and the exodus people** (vv. 23–31): Moses considered the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt; the people kept the Passover, crossed the Red Sea, saw the walls of Jericho fall, and Rahab the prostitute was saved. **More than time allows** (vv. 32–40): Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel and the prophets; some through faith won victories, others through faith suffered to the point of death; none of them received what was promised, because God meant them to be made perfect together with us.

This chapter is often called the hall of heroes of faith, yet many on the list were weak and sinful. They are remembered not because they deserved it but because they took refuge in the God who promised. What they did showed their faith, but faith itself is not a merit that buys God's pleasure. The list keeps running forward and comes to rest only in 12:2: "looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith."

## Going Deeper: 11:1–7 The Assurance of Things Hoped For

**1. "Faith is the assurance of things hoped for."** "Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen. For by it the people of old received their commendation" (11:1–2).
- **"Assurance":** this word is translated "nature" in 1:3 ("the exact imprint of his nature") and "confidence" in 3:14. Interpreters take it in two main ways. One stresses the believer's inner confidence: faith is a firm hold on what we hope for, and many modern translations render it so. The other stresses objective reality: the things that have not yet come are real, and faith lets a person stand on that reality now. Lane and Koester, among others, stress this side, and Calvin speaks of faith as the ground beneath our feet on which we can stand. The two views do not exclude each other: faith's confidence does not come from our own feelings but from the reality of what God has promised.
- **"The conviction of things not seen":** "conviction" means evidence that persuades. "Things not seen" include future things (the flood in v. 7) and things invisible now, and in the end the invisible God himself (v. 27).
- **Not a full definition:** many point out that verse 1 does not give a complete definition of faith but describes what faith does: it treats God's promise as real and lives by it. Every "by faith" in the chapter is a footnote to this verse.
- **"Commendation":** the word means that God bore witness in their favor. Verses 2 and 39 echo each other and frame the whole chapter.

**2. "Created by the word of God."** "By faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible" (11:3).
- **By the word of God:** "God said... and there was" (Gen 1:3); "By the word of the LORD the heavens were made" (Ps 33:6). In 1:2 God created the world "through" his Son, using the same word.
- **Creation out of nothing:** the verse itself says that the visible world did not come from visible things. Paul calls God the one who "calls into existence the things that do not exist" (Rom 4:17), and John says "All things were made through him" (John 1:3). On the basis of these texts the church has always confessed that God created all things out of nothing.
- **Faith's first step:** the first "by faith" on the list is not a hero's deed but "we" understanding that God made the world. Faith first receives God's word, and only then acts on it.

**3. Abel, Enoch and Noah.** "By faith Abel offered to God a more acceptable sacrifice than Cain... And through his faith, though he died, he still speaks" (11:4).
- **Why was Abel's sacrifice better?** Some think it lay in the offering itself, "the firstborn of his flock and of their fat portions" (Gen 4:4); others think it was because it was a blood sacrifice. The author's answer is "by faith." Genesis says "the LORD had regard for Abel and his offering" (Gen 4:4): first the man, then the offering. Cain's deeds were evil (1 John 3:12); the problem was in the heart, not only in the offering.
- **"He still speaks":** Abel's blood cried to God from the ground (Gen 4:10). Hebrews 12:24 says Jesus' sprinkled blood "speaks a better word than the blood of Abel": Abel's blood asks for justice, Jesus' blood brings forgiveness.
- **Enoch:** Genesis says "Enoch walked with God" (Gen 5:24); the Septuagint renders this as "pleased God," and the author follows that reading. From it he draws verse 6: "And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him." Faith has two sides: believing that God is real, and believing that he will not disappoint those who seek him. What they seek is God himself, so his reward, in the end, cannot be separated from himself.
- **Noah:** "being warned by God concerning events as yet unseen," that is, the flood that had not yet come, he "in reverent fear constructed an ark" (11:7). His obedience exposed the unbelief of his generation, and he "became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith." This is the first mention of "righteousness" in the chapter, and it ties straight back to 10:38, "my righteous one shall live by faith."

**4. Faith and works.** Abel offered a sacrifice and Noah built an ark: these are actions. Yet each time the author says "by faith."
- **Works show faith:** true faith always moves; faith without action is dead (Jas 2:17).
- **Faith is not a merit:** faith itself is not a good work that earns God's pleasure. Abraham "believed the LORD, and he counted it to him as righteousness" (Gen 15:6); "to the one who does not work but trusts him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is counted as righteousness" (Rom 4:5). Faith pleases God not because it has value in itself, but because it takes refuge in the one in whom God is pleased: "we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all" (10:10).

> The point: Faith is not confidence in ourselves; it takes God's word as true and lives by it. We do not please God by the good work of believing; by faith we take refuge in Christ, who offered himself once for all.

## Going Deeper: 11:8–22 Seeking a Homeland

**1. "Not knowing where he was going."** "By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place that he was to receive as an inheritance. And he went out, not knowing where he was going" (11:8).
- **Hearing first, seeing later:** God told him, "Go... to the land that I will show you" (Gen 12:1), without first giving him a map. Faith hears God's word and sets out.
- **A stranger in the land of promise:** "By faith he went to live in the land of promise, as in a foreign land, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, heirs with him of the same promise" (11:9). Three generations lived in tents. When Sarah died, the only land Abraham ever bought there was a grave (Gen 23:4, 19).
- **"The city that has foundations":** "For he was looking forward to the city that has foundations, whose designer and builder is God" (11:10). A tent has no foundations; he was waiting for "the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem" (12:22; see 13:14).

**2. Sarah.** "By faith Sarah herself received power to conceive, even when she was past the age, since she considered him faithful who had promised" (11:11).
- **Who is the subject?** The ESV, like most translations, makes Sarah the subject; some modern translations and interpreters think Abraham is still the subject and Sarah is mentioned alongside him, because the Greek phrase behind "power to conceive" is normally used of men. On either reading the verse remembers Sarah.
- **God remembers her faith:** when Sarah heard the promise she "laughed to herself" (Gen 18:12). Hebrews does not mention her laughter; it says only that "she considered him faithful who had promised." What God honors is that in the end she trusted the faithful God.
- **"As good as dead":** Paul too says that Abraham "considered his own body, which was as good as dead," yet "did not weaken in faith" (Rom 4:19). God's promises often begin where human hope has already run out.

**3. "These all died in faith."** "These all died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar, and having acknowledged that they were strangers and exiles on the earth" (11:13).
- **Not received, yet seen:** in their lifetime they did not see offspring as many as the stars, nor did they possess the land. Yet they "greeted them from afar," like waving to someone seen coming in the distance. Jesus said, "Your father Abraham rejoiced that he would see my day" (John 8:56).
- **Strangers and exiles:** Abraham told the Hittites, "I am a sojourner and foreigner among you" (Gen 23:4). Peter uses the same words for believers (1 Pet 2:11).
- **Not going back:** "If they had been thinking of that land from which they had gone out, they would have had opportunity to return. But as it is, they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one" (11:15–16). The road back to Ur stayed open, but they did not take it.
- **"God is not ashamed to be called their God":** these people lived in tents, had no land of their own, and had their lies and weaknesses (Gen 12:13; 16:2). Yet God tied his own name to theirs: centuries after their death he still said to Moses, "I am... the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" (Exod 3:6). Jesus used these words to prove the resurrection: "He is not God of the dead, but of the living" (Matt 22:32). The proof that God is not ashamed of them is this: "he has prepared for them a city" (11:16; see John 14:2).

**4. "He considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead."** "By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac... of whom it was said, 'Through Isaac shall your offspring be named.' He considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead, from which, figuratively speaking, he did receive him back" (11:17–19).
- **Promise and command in conflict:** the whole promise hung on Isaac (Gen 21:12), yet the command was to offer Isaac up. Chapter 6 has already looked at God's oath on Mount Moriah and the ram offered in Isaac's place; here the author lets us see how Abraham reasoned.
- **How he reasoned:** he did not choose one over the other; he concluded that God could raise Isaac from the dead in order to keep his promise. So he told his servants, "I and the boy will go over there and worship and come again to you" (Gen 22:5).
- **"Figuratively speaking":** the Greek means "as a figure" or "as a parable." Many take Isaac's being "received back" as a figure of resurrection: what did not become fact on Moriah became fact at Golgotha. God's Son truly died, and on the third day he rose again.

**5. Faith at the point of death.** Isaac "invoked future blessings," Jacob when dying blessed Joseph's two sons, and Joseph "at the end of his life, made mention of the exodus of the Israelites and gave directions concerning his bones" (11:20–22). Joseph spent most of his life in Egypt, high in power, yet he had his bones carried back to a land he never possessed (Gen 50:25; Exod 13:19). To the very end they were looking ahead.

> The point: Faith often means living in tents all one's life and dying without seeing the promise fulfilled. Yet God is not ashamed of these exiles; he has prepared a city for them, and he is able to raise the dead.

## Going Deeper: 11:23–31 As Seeing Him Who Is Invisible

**1. "The reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt."** "By faith Moses, when he was grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of God than to enjoy the fleeting pleasures of sin. He considered the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking to the reward" (11:24–26).
- **Giving up a status:** Moses "was instructed in all the wisdom of the Egyptians" (Acts 7:22) and had a brilliant future. His parents had once hidden him, "not afraid of the king's edict" (11:23); now he himself refused to stay in the palace.
- **"The fleeting pleasures of sin":** the comforts of the palace were not in themselves sin; but for Moses, staying meant turning his back on God's people, and so they became "pleasures of sin," and only "fleeting" ones.
- **"The reproach of Christ":** how could Moses, more than a thousand years before Jesus' birth, suffer reproach for "Christ"? Interpreters take it in several ways. One view: the author looks back from the side of Christ's coming, and the reproach Moses bore was the same kind that Christ would later bear. Another notes that "Christ" means "anointed one," and a psalm speaks of the insults with which God's enemies "mock the footsteps of your anointed" (Ps 89:51); God's people and his anointed king are reproached together, and Moses joined them. A third, as Calvin puts it, holds that Christ and his people are one, so the reproach God's people bore for him from the beginning was the reproach of Christ (see 1 Cor 10:4). All these views agree that readers today are invited to "go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured" (13:13).
- **"Looking to the reward":** this is not a shrewd investment; it is seeing something more precious.

**2. "He endured as seeing him who is invisible."** "By faith he left Egypt, not being afraid of the anger of the king, for he endured as seeing him who is invisible" (11:27).
- **Which departure?** Moses first left Egypt by fleeing to Midian, and the text says that then "Moses was afraid" (Exod 2:14–15). So many think this verse refers to the later exodus with the people; others think it still means the flight to Midian: he feared that his killing had become known, but did not lose faith before the king.
- **Seeing the invisible:** this is the "conviction of things not seen" of verse 1. Moses could stand before Pharaoh because he had in view one greater than Pharaoh.

**3. Passover, the Red Sea and Jericho.** "By faith he kept the Passover and sprinkled the blood, so that the Destroyer of the firstborn might not touch them" (11:28).
- **Spared from judgment because of the blood:** that night, the difference between Israelite and Egyptian houses was not which family was better, but whether there was blood on the door. God said, "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exod 12:13). Paul says, "Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed" (1 Cor 5:7).
- **A people who crossed together:** "By faith the people crossed the Red Sea as if on dry land" (11:29), and among them were some who later fell in the wilderness through unbelief (3:16–19). The walls of Jericho fell not by siege engines but by God's word, after seven days of circling (11:30).

**4. Rahab the prostitute.** "By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient, because she had given a friendly welcome to the spies" (11:31).
- **An outsider:** she was a Canaanite and a prostitute. She said, "the LORD your God, he is God in the heavens above and on the earth beneath" (Josh 2:11). In the whole city she alone took refuge in Israel's God.
- **Faith and works:** James says she was "justified by works" (Jas 2:25); Hebrews says "by faith." The two do not conflict: her risky welcome of the spies showed that she truly believed; James's point is that living faith always has works.
- **The ending grace wrote:** she tied the scarlet cord in her window (Josh 2:21), she and her father's household were saved alive (Josh 6:25), and later she entered Jesus' genealogy (Matt 1:5). This God still receives people whom others despise and who know they have sinned. You do not have to clean up your past before you come; confess your sin, repent, and take refuge in Christ, who shed his blood for sinners.

> The point: Moses saw him who is invisible, and so chose to bear reproach with God's people. The blood of the Passover and Rahab's scarlet cord both tell us that we escape judgment not because we are better than others, but because of the rescue God has provided.

## Going Deeper: 11:32–40 Of Whom the World Was Not Worthy

**1. "What more shall I say?"** "And what more shall I say? For time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets" (11:32).
- **A list of flawed people:** Gideon first asked for a sign with a fleece (Judg 6:37), and later made an ephod that "became a snare to Gideon and to his family" (Judg 8:27). Barak said, "If you will go with me, I will go, but if you will not go with me, I will not go" (Judg 4:8). Samson lived a self-indulgent life, and his last prayer was to be "avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes" (Judg 16:28). David committed adultery and arranged a murder (2 Sam 11–12).
- **This is grace:** the Old Testament does not hide their sins, and God did not strike them off the list because of them. They are remembered not for flawless character but because at the decisive moment they took refuge in God. God's list has only ever held forgiven sinners. That does not mean their sins did not matter: David confessed, "I have sinned against the LORD," and only then did Nathan say, "The LORD also has put away your sin" (2 Sam 12:13).

**2. Jephthah's vow.** Jephthah vowed to the LORD: "whatever comes out from the doors of my house to meet me when I return in peace from the Ammonites shall be the LORD's, and I will offer it up for a burnt offering" (Judg 11:31). The one who came out was his only daughter.
- **Two readings:** many interpreters ancient and modern (for example Block) think he actually offered his daughter as a burnt offering. Others (for example Keil and Delitzsch, and some medieval Jewish interpreters) think he dedicated her to God to serve for life unmarried, since the text goes on to say, "She had never known a man" (Judg 11:39).
- **Not to be imitated:** on either reading, the vow was rash. God plainly forbids offering children (Deut 12:31), and he never needs such bargains to give victory. What Hebrews remembers is that Jephthah trusted God and went to war when "the Spirit of the LORD was upon Jephthah" (Judg 11:29), not his vow.

**3. Those who triumphed and those who suffered.**
- **Triumph:** they "through faith conquered kingdoms, enforced justice, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions, quenched the power of fire" (11:33–34). Daniel said, "My God sent his angel and shut the lions' mouths" (Dan 6:22); his three friends were not burned in the furnace (Dan 3); "Women received back their dead by resurrection" (11:35), like the widow of Zarephath and the Shunammite woman (1 Kgs 17:22; 2 Kgs 4).
- **Suffering:** "Some were tortured, refusing to accept release, so that they might rise again to a better life" (11:35). Many think this alludes to the martyrdom of a mother and her seven sons in the Maccabean period (2 Maccabees chapter 7). "Stoned," like the priest Zechariah (2 Chr 24:21); "killed with the sword," like the prophet Uriah (Jer 26:23); "sawn in two," as Jewish tradition says Isaiah was martyred; "in skins of sheep and goats," which recalls Elijah (2 Kgs 1:8). The CUV's "were tempted" in verse 37 is missing in some manuscripts, and the ESV omits it.
- **One faith, two outcomes:** some through faith "escaped the edge of the sword," others through faith were "killed with the sword." Faith is not a formula that guarantees success; whatever says "if your faith is big enough you will surely be healed, rich and safe" is not the faith of this chapter. God did not break faith with those who were never delivered in this life.
- **"Of whom the world was not worthy":** the world treated them as vagrants and criminals and drove them into deserts and caves (11:38); God's verdict is exactly the reverse.

**4. "Apart from us they should not be made perfect."** "And all these, though commended through their faith, did not receive what was promised, since God had provided something better for us, that apart from us they should not be made perfect" (11:39–40).
- **"What was promised":** verse 33 says they "obtained promises," meaning particular promises; here they "did not receive what was promised," meaning the final promise: Christ and the full salvation he brings.
- **"Something better":** Christ has come, and "by a single offering he has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified" (10:14).
- **Not two ways of salvation:** this does not mean believers under the old covenant received a lesser salvation or were saved another way. They too were saved by the same Christ; they looked forward (1 Pet 1:10–11), and we look back. Their being made perfect had to wait for Christ's work to be finished. Hebrews 12:23 speaks of "the spirits of the righteous made perfect"; many take this to mean that since Christ's ascension their spirits have been made perfect in God's presence, while the resurrection of the body still waits for the last day, to be received together with us (Rom 8:23).
- **One people:** God did not let them reach the finish line first, nor does he let us run alone. Believers of every age are one people, still on the same road. So 12:1 says, "since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses."
- **Where the list ends:** this long line of names comes to rest on Jesus: "looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith" (12:2). The word "founder" is the same one used in 2:10, and many note that "perfecter" shares its root with "made perfect" in verse 40. He himself ran the whole course trusting his Father, and "for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame," bearing our sins; now he "is seated at the right hand of the throne of God" (12:2). The promise the ancients saw from afar has been fulfilled in him.

> The point: Some of these people triumphed and some died without seeing the promise fulfilled, but not one was forgotten by God. They and we are one people, to be made perfect together in Christ, for he is the founder and perfecter of our faith.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "These all died in faith, not having received the things promised, but having seen them and greeted them from afar" (11:13). I have a few prayers whose answers I may never see in this life. Twenty years ago, seven or eight of us met every Wednesday evening in a small church to pray for revival in our city; the church did not revive. It split instead, and in the end it closed. There is a young person I watched grow up who left the church years ago, and left home too, and the news grows rarer every year. And there is an injustice I saw with my own eyes: the one who was wronged is still waiting for justice, while the one who did the harm is doing very well. I once prayed urgently about these things; slowly I stopped mentioning them. Sometimes I am not waiting at all; my heart has grown cold toward God, even a little bitter, as if he had put my prayers in a drawer no one opens. The psalmist may ask "How long?" (Ps 13:1), and so may Habakkuk (Hab 1:2); such lament is not unbelief. But the coldness and bitterness in my heart are sin, and I need to bring them to him and confess them; his blood was shed for this sin too. Abraham died owning only a grave, yet he "greeted them from afar"; he rejoiced not because he saw the answer but because he knew the God who had promised. Can I hold these unfulfilled prayers as promises greeted from afar, rather than as files God has forgotten?

Then think again: "apart from us they should not be made perfect" (11:40). The elderly sister who prayed with me back then has died; to the end she never saw our city revived. Were her prayers wasted? Hebrews says that she, like Abraham and Moses, belongs to a people still on the way, who will be made perfect together with us. My prayers do not have to be completed in my own hands either. The end of this road is not whether my faith is firm enough, but Jesus, "the founder and perfecter of our faith" (12:2). He himself, for the joy set before him, endured the cross and bore our sins; now he is seated at the right hand of the throne of God, and he has received the promise for us, his people still on the road. Perhaps you have never yet taken refuge in him: not one person in this company walked in by their own righteousness, and today you too can repent, trust him, and join this people still on the way. If God is not ashamed of those who died with their prayers unanswered, owning only a grave, will he be ashamed of me, who so often lose heart and for a time even stopped praying? "Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God, for he has prepared for them a city" (11:16).
