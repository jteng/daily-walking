## 本章脉络

第十二章分成四段。**仰望耶稣**（1-3节）：有这许多的见证人围着我们，我们就当放下重担，脱去缠累我们的罪，存心忍耐奔跑，仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣。**主所爱的，他必管教**（4-11节）：读者所忍受的，是父亲管教儿子，为要叫他们在神的圣洁上有分。**不要像以扫**（12-17节）：要把发酸的腿挺起来，追求和睦与圣洁，不要像以扫为一点食物卖了长子的名分。**两座山**（18-29节）：我们不是来到那能摸的西奈山，乃是来到锡安山，来到新约的中保耶稣和他所洒的血面前；所以不可弃绝那从天上说话的，要因得了不能震动的国而感恩。

第十一章讲的是「他们」，第十二章转成「我们」：那些人已经跑完了，现在轮到我们。

## 深读 12:1-3 仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣

**1. 「这许多的见证人」。** 「我们既有这许多的见证人，如同云彩围着我们，就当放下各样的重担，脱去容易缠累我们的罪，存心忍耐，奔那摆在我们前头的路程。」（12:1）
- **观众还是作见证的人：** 这里的画面是赛跑，许多讲道者把这些人看成看台上的观众。布鲁斯和雷恩则强调，他们主要是作见证的人：第十一章的古人「得了美好的证据」（11:2），一生为信心作了见证；不是他们看我们，而是我们看他们。阿特里奇等人认为两层意思都有。无论哪一种，作者都是叫我们从他们学信心，再把眼睛转向耶稣。
- **「重担」和「罪」：** 重担未必是罪，却会拖慢脚步；罪则像缠住双腿的长袍。两样都要放下。

**2. 「创始成终」。** 「仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣。」（12:2上）
- **两层意思：** 「创始」和2:10「救他们的元帅」是同一个词，意思是开路的人。原文没有「我们」，直译是信心的创始者和完成者。许多人认为，耶稣自己先跑完了信心的路，一生倚靠父（2:13）；他也是我们信心的源头，必把我们的信心带到完全。

**3. 「因那摆在前面的喜乐」。** 「他因那摆在前面的喜乐，就轻看羞辱，忍受了十字架的苦难，便坐在神宝座的右边。那忍受罪人这样顶撞的，你们要思想，免得疲倦灰心。」（12:2下-3）
- **「因」的两种理解：** 多数人（如布鲁斯）认为是为了那将来的喜乐；也有人（如加尔文）认为是代替：他本可以享受喜乐，却选择了十字架（参腓2:6-8）。前一种较常见，也和11:26摩西「想望所要得的赏赐」相呼应。
- **「轻看羞辱」：** 十字架是罗马世界最羞耻的死法，耶稣却不让羞耻拦住他。作者给疲倦之人开的药方，不是再加把劲，而是「思想」耶稣所忍受的。

> 要点：古人的见证叫我们知道这条路能跑完，可是我们要看的是耶稣。他为我们忍受了十字架，如今坐在神宝座的右边；我们的信心从他开始，也要由他完成。

## 深读 12:4-11 主所爱的，他必管教

**1. 「待你们如同待儿子」。** 「你们又忘了那劝你们如同劝儿子的话，说：我儿，你不可轻看主的管教，被他责备的时候也不可灰心；因为主所爱的，他必管教，又鞭打凡所收纳的儿子。你们所忍受的，是神管教你们，待你们如同待儿子。」（12:5-7）
- **处境：** 读者受过逼迫，「还没有抵挡到流血的地步」（4节）。他们可能以为受苦说明神离弃了他们；作者引箴言3:11-12告诉他们：正好相反，受管教正表明神待他们如同儿子。
- **不是私子：** 古时的私生子常常没有父亲管教，也没有产业（8节）；神亲自管教我们，是因为我们是他家里的人。
- **「管教」是什么：** 这个词原指教养、训练；11节又用「经练」，那是运动员操练的用语。在上下文中，读者所受的主要是为信仰受的逼迫（10:32-34），不是犯了某件罪的报应。

**2. 「使我们在他的圣洁上有分」。** 「生身的父都是暂随己意管教我们；惟有万灵的父管教我们，是要我们得益处，使我们在他的圣洁上有分。凡管教的事，当时不觉得快乐，反觉得愁苦；后来却为那经练过的人结出平安的果子，就是义。」（12:10-11）
- **地上的父和天上的父：** 再好的父亲也只是「暂随己意」，判断有限，有时还夹着自己的情绪。神的管教总是「要我们得益处」，目的是叫我们像他。
- **管教不是付罪债：** 神的管教有时也是对罪的纠正（林前11:32），但纠正不是还债。我们罪的刑罚，基督已经在十字架上替我们担当了：「因他受的刑罚，我们得平安」（赛53:5）。这里的「刑罚」，七十士译本用的正是本章「管教」这个词：该落在我们身上的惩罚，已经落在他身上；留给我们的，只有父亲的教养。所以信徒所受的苦，没有一样是神在讨罪债。也不要把每一样苦难都和某一件罪连在一起（约9:2-3）。

**3. 神的管教绝不为虐待开脱。** 这段经文常被人误用，必须说清楚：
- 神像父亲一样管教我们，这绝不能成为父母、配偶或教会领袖施暴的理由。圣经叫作父亲的「不要惹儿女的气」（弗6:4）；这里的「鞭打」是比喻神的管教，任何人都不可拿这节经文作打人、伤人的理由。
- 孩子或配偶若正在遭受暴力、性侵或长期的控制，应当尽快到安全的地方；孩子可以告诉老师或一位可信的大人。也应当向警方或有关部门举报；掌权的是「神的用人」，要「刑罚那作恶的」（罗13:4）。
- 因别人的虐待而受苦，不等于神在叫受害的人留下来忍受。离开危险、寻求保护，不是不顺服神。
- 教会不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而隐瞒虐待，也不可催逼受害的人在仍有危险的时候，与施暴的人和好。

> 要点：神管教我们，是父亲在养育儿子，不是审判官在讨债；我们罪的刑罚已经落在基督身上。他的管教总是为我们的益处，要我们在他的圣洁上有分；神的管教也从来不能拿来作虐待人的借口。

## 深读 12:12-17 不要像以扫

**1. 「把下垂的手、发酸的腿挺起来」。** 「所以，你们要把下垂的手、发酸的腿挺起来；也要为自己的脚，把道路修直了，使瘸子不致歪脚，反得痊愈。」（12:12-13）修直道路，是为了群体中最软弱的人不再跌倒（参赛35:3）。

**2. 「追求与众人和睦，并要追求圣洁」。** 「你们要追求与众人和睦，并要追求圣洁；非圣洁没有人能见主。又要谨慎，恐怕有人失了神的恩；恐怕有毒根生出来扰乱你们。」（12:14-15）
- **不是靠圣洁得救：** 我们得以成圣，是因耶稣基督的身体「只一次献上」（10:10、14）。追求圣洁是蒙恩之人生命的方向；完全没有这个方向的人，就当省察自己是否真的属于主（林后13:5）。圣洁也正是神管教要结出的果子（10节）。
- **「毒根」：** 出自申命记29:18，指心里偏离耶和华、去事奉别神的人。毒根起初看不见，长出来就扰乱整个群体；所以要彼此看顾，不让人掉队（3:13）。

**3. 以扫「得不着门路」。** 「恐怕有淫乱的，有贪恋世俗如以扫的，他因一点食物把自己长子的名分卖了。后来想要承受父所祝的福，竟被弃绝，虽然号哭切求，却得不着门路使他父亲的心意回转。」（12:16-17）
- **「贪恋世俗」：** 就是把神所看重的当作平常（创25:34）。
- **谁的心意回转：** 原文直译是：他找不到悔改的余地。有三种主要理解。**以撒的心意：** 和合本这样翻译；以撒已经为雅各祝福，说「他将来也必蒙福」（创27:33）。**以扫无法挽回：** 许多现代解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为，以扫不能把做过的事收回，眼泪也改变不了结果。**以扫没有真悔改：** 加尔文认为，以扫哭的是失去的福分，不是得罪了神（参林后7:10「世俗的忧愁」）。
- **这警告的意思：** 几种理解见6:4-8的深读。各种看法都同意：这不是说真心悔改、回到神面前的人会被拒绝；作者是警告读者，不要为了躲避眼前的苦难，把基督换成「一点食物」。

> 要点：我们在基督里已经被列在「诸长子」之中（12:23），产业是白白得的；所以不要为了眼前的安稳，把它当作平常。真心回到他面前的人，会发现恩典的门是开着的。

## 深读 12:18-24 你们乃是来到锡安山

**1. 那能摸的山。** 「你们原不是来到那能摸的山；此山有火焰、密云、黑暗、暴风、角声与说话的声音。」（12:18-19上）
- **西奈山：** 神颁布律法的时候，「营中的百姓尽都发颤」（出19:16）。连中保摩西也说：「我甚是恐惧战兢」（21节）。这话最接近申命记9:19，是百姓拜金牛犊以后摩西说的。

**2. 你们所来到的。** 「你们乃是来到锡安山，永生神的城邑，就是天上的耶路撒冷。那里有千万的天使，有名录在天上诸长子之会所共聚的总会，有审判众人的神和被成全之义人的灵魂，并新约的中保耶稣，以及所洒的血。」（12:22-24上）
- **「乃是来到」：** 这个动词是完成式：不是将要来到，而是已经来到。信徒现在就藉着基督在敬拜中进到神面前（10:19-22），虽然还在寻求那将来的城（13:14）。
- **欢庆的聚会：** 原文这里有一个词，意思是节日的欢聚。和合本把它连到下一句，译作「共聚的总会」；也有许多译本把它连到天使，译作千万的天使在欢聚。无论连到哪里，西奈山下百姓发颤，锡安山上却是欢庆。
- **「诸长子之会」：** 多数人认为是指神的百姓，名字录在天上（参路10:20）；也有人认为是指天使。长子是承受产业的人，正和卖了长子名分的以扫相对。
- **「被成全之义人的灵魂」：** 多数人认为是指已经离世的信徒，包括第十一章那些古时的圣徒（11:40），如今因基督一次的献祭得以完全（10:14）。

**3. 「比亚伯的血所说的更美」。** 「这血所说的比亚伯的血所说的更美。」（12:24下）多数人认为，亚伯的血是创世记4:10「从地里向我哀告」、呼求伸冤的血；也有人联系11:4，亚伯「虽然死了」，「仍旧说话」。亚伯的血喊着要审判；耶稣的血说的是赦免、洁净、与神和好（9:14；10:22）。耶稣的血洒在我们心上，叫我们进到神面前。

> 要点：我们不是还站在西奈山下发颤，而是已经藉着耶稣的血，来到锡安山欢庆的聚会中。审判众人的神在那里，中保耶稣也在那里，他的血只洒了一次，却永远有效（9:12、26），一直在为我们说更美的话。

## 深读 12:25-29 不能震动的国

**1. 「不可弃绝那向你们说话的」。** 「你们总要谨慎，不可弃绝那向你们说话的。因为，那些弃绝在地上警戒他们的尚且不能逃罪，何况我们违背那从天上警戒我们的呢？」（12:25）
- **谁在地上警戒：** 有人认为是摩西；多数人认为是神自己在西奈山说话（26节）。如今从天上说话的，是藉着儿子说话的神（1:1-2）。这段警告的不同理解，见6:4-8的深读。

**2. 「再一次」。** 「当时他的声音震动了地，但如今他应许说：『再一次我不单要震动地，还要震动天。』」（12:26）
- **哈该书2:6：** 哈该在重建圣殿的时候说，神要「再一次震动天地」。作者把这话应用到末后的审判。
- **「挪去」：** 被震动的都要挪去（27节）。有人认为现在的天地要被除去，也有许多人认为是被更新；两种看法都引用1:11-12和彼后3:10-13。无论哪一种，会被震动的都不能作我们最后的依靠。

**3. 「就当感恩」。** 「所以我们既得了不能震动的国，就当感恩，照神所喜悦的，用虔诚、敬畏的心事奉神。因为我们的神乃是烈火。」（12:28-29）
- **「得了」「感恩」：** 这国不是我们建立的，而是领受的（参但7:18）。「感恩」原文直译是让我们有恩，这个词可指恩典，也可指感谢；许多人认为这里是感谢的意思，和合本也这样译。我们事奉神，是出于感恩，不是为了赚得什么。
- **「烈火」：** 这是申命记4:24的话。锡安山的神和西奈山的神是同一位，他的圣洁没有因为恩典而减少，所以我们要存着「虔诚、敬畏的心」。我们能够亲近这位烈火般的神，是因为审判已经落在替我们受死的基督身上。

> 要点：天地都会震动，可是神赐给我们的国不会。这国既是白白领受的，我们事奉他就是出于感恩；他既是烈火，这感恩里就有敬畏。

## 默想

**默想：** 「主所爱的，他必管教。」（12:6）「你们乃是来到锡安山。」（12:22）那一年，公司裁员的名单上有我的名字；没过多久，母亲住进了医院。我心里冒出来的第一个问题是：是不是神在惩罚我？我到底做了什么，要受这样的报应？其实我很想知道是哪一件事，因为知道了，我就能改，就能保证下次不再出事；我宁可要一个能自己掌握的原因，也不愿意信靠一位我看不透的父亲。我们从小听惯了「善有善报，恶有恶报」，好像每一件不幸，都是从上面落下来的一个判决。小时候父亲的管教也加深了这种感觉：他生气的时候会打人，事后说是为我好，所以读到「鞭打凡所收纳的儿子」，我心里会缩一下。希伯来书说，生身的父「都是暂随己意管教我们」（12:10），连好父亲的判断也有限。可是父亲在气头上打我，那不只是判断有限，那是错的，也不是天父的样子。我可以在天父面前为那些年哀伤，不必替它找理由；天上的父管教我，从来不夹着怒气，「是要我们得益处」。如果有人正在伤害我，不论是父母、配偶还是教会领袖，那不是神的管教，也不是神要我留下来忍受；我可以先到安全的地方，也可以报警（罗13:4）。神的管教也从来不是要我付罪债，我的罪该受的刑罚已经落在基督身上：「因他受的刑罚，我们得平安。」（赛53:5）以赛亚说的「刑罚」，和希伯来书说的「管教」，在希腊文旧约里是同一个词：当作刑罚的那一份，已经整个落在他身上；临到我的，只剩下父亲的管教。神若光照我看见自己的罪，我就当向他认罪悔改；我也可以坦然这样做，因为纠正我的是父亲；审判我的事，在十字架上已经审判过了。他管教我，是因为我是他的儿女，不是私子；他要的是「使我们在他的圣洁上有分」（12:10）。而管教我的这位父亲，就是我已经来到他面前的那一位：我已经来到锡安山，不是将来才到。就在我失业、守在病房外的那些日子，我其实是在千万天使欢庆的聚会中，新约的中保耶稣也在那里。审判众人的神也在那里（12:23），我却不必发颤，因为他面前有那洒过的血。亚伯的血从地里喊着要伸冤，耶稣「所洒的血」为我说的却是「更美」的话（12:24）。锡安山的门，不是为受够了苦的人开的，是为投靠这血的人开的。如果你还从来没有来到他面前，也可以这样来：承认自己的罪，悔改，信靠这位血为你说更美的话的耶稣。那份工作没有回来，母亲的病也还要慢慢熬；可是神已经把「不能震动的国」赐给了我（12:28）。父啊，这国不是我赚来的，是你因着你儿子的血白白赐给我的。所以我要感恩，怀着敬畏和喜乐站在你的锡安山上，感谢你那为我信心创始成终的儿子和他所洒的血。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 12 has four parts. **Looking to Jesus** (vv. 1–3): surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight and sin, and run with endurance, looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith. **The Lord disciplines the one he loves** (vv. 4–11): what the readers endure is a father disciplining his sons, so that they may share God's holiness. **Not like Esau** (vv. 12–17): strengthen weak knees, strive for peace and holiness, and do not be like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal. **Two mountains** (vv. 18–29): we have not come to the mountain that may be touched, Sinai, but to Mount Zion, to Jesus the mediator of a new covenant and his sprinkled blood; so do not refuse him who speaks from heaven, but be grateful for a kingdom that cannot be shaken.

Chapter 11 speaks of "they"; chapter 12 turns to "we": they have finished their race, and now it is our turn.

## Going Deeper: 12:1–3 Looking to Jesus, the Founder and Perfecter of Our Faith

**1. "So great a cloud of witnesses."** "Therefore, since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us also lay aside every weight, and sin which clings so closely, and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us" (12:1).
- **Spectators or witnesses who testify:** the picture is a race, and many preachers see these people as spectators in the stands. Bruce and Lane stress that they are mainly people who bear witness: the people of chapter 11 "received their commendation" (11:2), and their lives testify to faith; it is not they who look at us but we who look at them. Attridge and others think both senses are present. On any view, the author tells us to learn faith from them and then turn our eyes to Jesus.
- **"Weight" and "sin":** a weight need not be sin, but it slows us down; sin is like a long robe tangled around the legs. Both must be laid aside.

**2. "Founder and perfecter."** "Looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith" (12:2a).
- **Two senses:** "founder" is the same word as in 2:10, "founder of their salvation"; it means the one who opens the way. The Greek has no "our"; it reads literally "the founder and perfecter of faith." Many take it this way: Jesus himself ran the whole course of faith first, trusting the Father all his life (2:13); and he is the source of our faith and will bring it to completion.

**3. "For the joy that was set before him."** "Who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God. Consider him who endured from sinners such hostility against himself, so that you may not grow weary or fainthearted" (12:2b–3).
- **Two readings of "for":** most (e.g., Bruce) take it as "for the sake of" the joy ahead; some (e.g., Calvin) take it as "instead of": he could have had joy, but chose the cross (see Phil 2:6–8). The first is more common, and it matches Moses "looking to the reward" in 11:26.
- **"Despising the shame":** the cross was the most shameful death in the Roman world, yet Jesus did not let the shame stop him. The author's remedy for the weary is not to push harder, but to "consider" what Jesus endured.

> The point: The witness of the faithful tells us this race can be finished, but it is Jesus we look to. He endured the cross for us and now sits at the right hand of the throne of God; our faith begins with him and will be completed by him.

## Going Deeper: 12:4–11 The Lord Disciplines the One He Loves

**1. "God is treating you as sons."** "And have you forgotten the exhortation that addresses you as sons? 'My son, do not regard lightly the discipline of the Lord, nor be weary when reproved by him. For the Lord disciplines the one he loves, and chastises every son whom he receives.' It is for discipline that you have to endure. God is treating you as sons" (12:5–7).
- **The setting:** the readers had been persecuted, though they had "not yet resisted to the point of shedding your blood" (v. 4). They may have thought suffering meant God had abandoned them; quoting Proverbs 3:11–12, the author tells them the opposite: being disciplined shows God is treating them as sons.
- **Not illegitimate:** in the ancient world an illegitimate child often had no father to raise him and no inheritance (v. 8); God disciplines us himself because we belong to his household.
- **What "discipline" is:** the word first means upbringing and training; verse 11 adds "trained," a word for an athlete's training. In context, what the readers suffered was mainly persecution for their faith (10:32–34), not retribution for some sin.

**2. "That we may share his holiness."** "For they disciplined us for a short time as it seemed best to them, but he disciplines us for our good, that we may share his holiness. For the moment all discipline seems painful rather than pleasant, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it" (12:10–11).
- **Earthly fathers and the heavenly Father:** even the best father disciplines "as it seemed best to them," with limited judgment and sometimes mixed with his own feelings. God's discipline is always "for our good," aimed at making us like him.
- **Discipline is not paying a debt for sin:** God's discipline is sometimes correction for sin (1 Cor 11:32), but correction is not repayment. The penalty for our sin Christ has already borne in our place on the cross: "upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace" (Isa 53:5). For "chastisement" the Greek Old Testament uses the very word Hebrews 12 uses for "discipline": the punishment that should have fallen on us fell on him; what is left for us is a father's training. So nothing a believer suffers is God collecting a debt for sin. Nor should we tie every hardship to a particular sin (John 9:2–3).

**3. God's discipline never excuses abuse.** This passage is often misused, so it must be said plainly:
- God's fatherly discipline is never a justification for abuse by parents, spouses or church leaders. Scripture tells fathers, "do not provoke your children to anger" (Eph 6:4); "chastises" is a figure for God's discipline; no one may use this verse to justify striking or harming another.
- A child or spouse facing violence, sexual abuse or ongoing coercion should get to safety as soon as possible; a child can tell a teacher or another trusted adult. It should be reported to the police or the proper authorities; the governing authority is "the servant of God, an avenger who carries out God's wrath on the wrongdoer" (Rom 13:4).
- Suffering caused by someone's abuse does not mean God is telling the victim to stay and endure it. Leaving danger and seeking protection is not disobedience to God.
- The church must not hide abuse to save face ("do not air the family's shame"), and must not press victims to reconcile with an abuser while danger remains.

> The point: When God disciplines us, he is a father raising his children, not a judge collecting a debt; the penalty for our sin has already fallen on Christ. His discipline is always for our good, that we may share his holiness; and God's discipline can never be used as an excuse for abuse.

## Going Deeper: 12:12–17 Not Like Esau

**1. "Lift your drooping hands."** "Therefore lift your drooping hands and strengthen your weak knees, and make straight paths for your feet, so that what is lame may not be put out of joint but rather be healed" (12:12–13). Straight paths are for the sake of the weakest in the community, so that they do not stumble further (see Isa 35:3).

**2. "Strive for peace... and for the holiness."** "Strive for peace with everyone, and for the holiness without which no one will see the Lord. See to it that no one fails to obtain the grace of God; that no 'root of bitterness' springs up and causes trouble" (12:14–15).
- **Not saved by holiness:** we "have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all" (10:10; see 10:14). The pursuit of holiness is the direction of a life that has received grace; one with no such direction at all should examine whether he truly belongs to the Lord (2 Cor 13:5). Holiness is also the very fruit God's discipline is meant to bear (v. 10).
- **"Root of bitterness":** from Deuteronomy 29:18, a person whose heart turns away from the Lord to serve other gods. Such a root is unseen at first, but once it grows it troubles the whole community; so we watch over one another, that no one falls behind (3:13).

**3. Esau "found no chance to repent."** "That no one is sexually immoral or unholy like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal. For you know that afterward, when he desired to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, for he found no chance to repent, though he sought it with tears" (12:16–17).
- **"Unholy":** treating as ordinary what God counts precious (Gen 25:34).
- **Whose change of mind?** The Greek reads literally, "he found no place for repentance." There are three main readings. **Isaac's mind:** the Chinese Union Version translates it this way; Isaac had already blessed Jacob and said, "Yes, and he shall be blessed" (Gen 27:33). **Esau could not undo it:** many modern commentators (e.g., Bruce, Lane) take it to mean Esau could not take back what he had done, and tears could not change the outcome. **Esau had no true repentance:** Calvin held that Esau wept for the lost blessing, not because he had sinned against God (compare the "worldly grief" of 2 Cor 7:10).
- **What the warning means:** for the readings, see the Going Deeper section on 6:4–8. All agree this does not say someone who truly repents and returns to God will be turned away; the author warns his readers not to trade Christ for "a single meal" to escape present suffering.

> The point: In Christ we are already counted among "the firstborn" (12:23), heirs by grace; so do not treat that inheritance as ordinary for the sake of present comfort. Whoever truly comes back to him will find the door of grace open.

## Going Deeper: 12:18–24 You Have Come to Mount Zion

**1. The mountain that may be touched.** "For you have not come to what may be touched, a blazing fire and darkness and gloom and a tempest and the sound of a trumpet and a voice" (12:18–19a).
- **Sinai:** when God gave the law, "all the people in the camp trembled" (Exod 19:16). Even Moses the mediator said, "I tremble with fear" (v. 21); these words come closest to Deuteronomy 9:19, spoken after the golden calf.

**2. Where you have come.** "But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to innumerable angels in festal gathering, and to the assembly of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven, and to God, the judge of all, and to the spirits of the righteous made perfect, and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, and to the sprinkled blood" (12:22–24a).
- **"You have come":** the verb is perfect tense: not "you will come" but "you have come." Believers already draw near to God in worship through Christ (10:19–22), though they still seek the city that is to come (13:14).
- **A festival:** the Greek here has a word meaning a festal gathering. The Chinese Union Version joins it to the next phrase, "the assembly of the firstborn"; many translations join it to the angels, "innumerable angels in festal gathering." Either way, at Sinai the people trembled; on Zion there is celebration.
- **"The assembly of the firstborn":** most take this as God's people, whose names are written in heaven (see Luke 10:20); some take it as the angels. The firstborn is the heir, the opposite of Esau, who sold his birthright.
- **"The spirits of the righteous made perfect":** most take this as believers who have died, including the saints of old in chapter 11 (11:40), now made perfect by Christ's single offering (10:14).

**3. "A better word than the blood of Abel."** "The sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel" (12:24b). Most take Abel's blood from Genesis 4:10, "crying to me from the ground" for justice; some connect it with 11:4, where Abel, "though he died, he still speaks." Abel's blood cries for judgment; Jesus' blood speaks forgiveness, cleansing and peace with God (9:14; 10:22). The blood of Jesus is sprinkled on our hearts and brings us near to God.

> The point: We are not still trembling at the foot of Sinai; through the blood of Jesus we have already come to the festal gathering on Mount Zion. God the judge of all is there, and so is Jesus the mediator, whose blood, shed once and effective forever (9:12, 26), keeps speaking a better word for us.

## Going Deeper: 12:25–29 A Kingdom That Cannot Be Shaken

**1. "Do not refuse him who is speaking."** "See that you do not refuse him who is speaking. For if they did not escape when they refused him who warned them on earth, much less will we escape if we reject him who warns from heaven" (12:25).
- **Who warned on earth:** some think Moses; most think it is God himself speaking at Sinai (v. 26). The one who now speaks from heaven is God, who speaks by his Son (1:1–2). For the readings of this warning, see the Going Deeper section on 6:4–8.

**2. "Yet once more."** "At that time his voice shook the earth, but now he has promised, 'Yet once more I will shake not only the earth but also the heavens'" (12:26).
- **Haggai 2:6:** while the temple was being rebuilt, Haggai said, "Yet once more, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth." The author applies this to the final judgment.
- **"Removal":** the things that are shaken will be removed (v. 27). Some think the present heavens and earth will be done away with; many others think they will be renewed; both views appeal to 1:11–12 and 2 Pet 3:10–13. On either view, nothing that can be shaken can be our final security.

**3. "Let us be grateful."** "Therefore let us be grateful for receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, and thus let us offer to God acceptable worship, with reverence and awe, for our God is a consuming fire" (12:28–29).
- **"Receiving" and "be grateful":** we did not build this kingdom; we receive it (see Dan 7:18). "Be grateful" reads literally "let us have grace"; the word can mean grace or thanks, and many take it here to mean "let us be thankful," as the Chinese Union Version also does. We serve God out of thanksgiving, not to earn anything.
- **"A consuming fire":** the words of Deuteronomy 4:24. The God of Zion is the God of Sinai, and grace has not made him less holy, so we come "with reverence and awe." We can draw near to this consuming fire because judgment has fallen on Christ, who died in our place.

> The point: Heaven and earth will be shaken, but the kingdom God gives us will not. Since we received it as a gift, we serve him out of thanksgiving; since he is a consuming fire, that thanksgiving is full of awe.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For the Lord disciplines the one he loves" (12:6). "But you have come to Mount Zion" (12:22). That year my name was on the company's layoff list; not long after, my mother went into the hospital. The first question that rose in me was: Is God punishing me? What did I do to deserve this? I wanted to know which thing it was, because then I could fix it and make sure it never happened again; I would rather have a cause I can control than trust a Father I cannot see through. We grew up hearing that good is repaid with good and evil with evil, as though every misfortune were a sentence handed down from above. My father's discipline deepened that feeling: he hit me when he was angry and said afterward it was for my own good, so when I read that he "chastises every son whom he receives," something in me flinches. Hebrews says earthly fathers disciplined "as it seemed best to them" (12:10); even good fathers judge imperfectly. But my father hitting me in anger was not just poor judgment; it was wrong, and it is not what the heavenly Father is like. I may grieve those years before him without making excuses for them; the Father's discipline is never mixed with temper, and it is always "for our good." If someone is hurting me now, whether a parent, a spouse or a church leader, that is not God's discipline, and it is not God telling me to stay and endure it; I may first get to a safe place, and I may report it (Rom 13:4). Nor is God's discipline ever meant to make me pay for my sin; the penalty my sin deserved has already fallen on Christ: "upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace" (Isa 53:5). In the Greek Old Testament, Isaiah's "chastisement" and Hebrews' "discipline" are the same word: as punishment, it fell on him in full; what reaches me is only a Father's training. When God shows me my sin, I should confess it and repent, and I can do so without fear, because the one correcting me is my Father; the judgment on my sin was already carried out at the cross. He disciplines me because I am his child, not an illegitimate one; his aim is "that we may share his holiness" (12:10). And the Father who disciplines me is the one before whom I already stand: I have come to Mount Zion, not someday. In those very days, out of work and waiting outside the hospital ward, I was in fact among innumerable angels in festal gathering, and Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, is there. God the judge of all is there too (12:23), yet I need not tremble, because the sprinkled blood is before him. Abel's blood cried from the ground for justice, but Jesus' "sprinkled blood" speaks "a better word" for me (12:24). The gate of Zion does not open to those who have suffered enough, but to those who take refuge in this blood. If you have never come to him, you can come the same way: confess your sin, repent, and trust Jesus, whose blood speaks a better word for you. The job did not come back, and my mother's illness is still a long road; but God has given me "a kingdom that cannot be shaken" (12:28). Father, I did not earn this kingdom; you gave it to me freely through the blood of your Son. So I will "be grateful," and stand on your Mount Zion with awe and joy, thanking you for your Son, the founder and perfecter of my faith, and for his sprinkled blood.
