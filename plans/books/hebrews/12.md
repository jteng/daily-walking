## 本章脉络

第十二章接着第十一章那一长串凭信心的人，分成四段。**仰望耶稣**（1-3节）：我们既有这许多的见证人如同云彩围着我们，就当放下重担，脱去缠累我们的罪，存心忍耐，奔那摆在前头的路程，仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣。**主所爱的，他必管教**（4-13节）：读者所忍受的，是父管教儿子；生身的父暂随己意管教我们，万灵的父管教我们，是要我们在他的圣洁上有分；所以要把下垂的手、发酸的腿挺起来。**不要像以扫**（14-17节）：要追求和睦与圣洁，谨慎不要有人失了神的恩，不要像以扫为一点食物卖了长子的名分。**两座山**（18-29节）：我们不是来到那能摸的西奈山，乃是来到锡安山，来到新约的中保耶稣和他所洒的血面前；所以不可弃绝那从天上说话的，要因得了不能震动的国而感恩，用虔诚、敬畏的心事奉神。

第十一章讲的是「他们」，第十二章开头就转成「我们」：那些人已经跑完了，现在轮到我们。全章的高峰在22-24节，作者用一连串的画面告诉读者：你们已经到了哪里。

## 深读 12:1-3 仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣

**1. 「这许多的见证人，如同云彩围着我们」。** 「我们既有这许多的见证人，如同云彩围着我们，就当放下各样的重担，脱去容易缠累我们的罪，存心忍耐，奔那摆在我们前头的路程。」（12:1）
- **观众还是作见证的人：** 这里的画面是运动场上的赛跑，所以许多讲道者把这些人看成看台上的观众，正在看我们怎样跑。布鲁斯和雷恩则强调，「见证人」这个词主要是指作见证的人：第十一章的古人「得了美好的证据」（11:2、39），他们的一生为信心作了见证，与其说是他们看我们，不如说是我们看他们。阿特里奇等人认为两层意思都有。无论哪一种，作者都没有叫我们向这些人祷告，或靠他们帮助，而是叫我们从他们身上学信心，然后把眼睛转向耶稣。
- **「重担」和「罪」：** 「重担」原指赛跑的人身上多余的分量，未必是罪，却会拖慢脚步；「罪」则像缠住腿的长袍。两样都要放下、脱去。
- **「存心忍耐」：** 这不是短跑，而是长跑。读者已经忍受过苦难（10:32-34），10:36说「你们必须忍耐」，这里又接着说。

**2. 「创始成终」。** 「仰望为我们信心创始成终的耶稣。」（12:2上）
- **「创始」：** 和2:10「救他们的元帅」是同一个词，意思是开路的、带头的。
- **「成终」：** 意思是使之完全、完成。原文没有「我们」，直译是「信心的创始者和完成者」。许多人认为这有两层意思：耶稣自己先跑完了信心的路，一生信靠父（2:13「我要倚赖他」）；他也是我们信心的源头，并且必把我们的信心带到完全。第十一章那些人是见证人，耶稣却是那位使信心有起头、有完成的主。

**3. 「因那摆在前面的喜乐」。** 「他因那摆在前面的喜乐，就轻看羞辱，忍受了十字架的苦难，便坐在神宝座的右边。」（12:2下）
- **「因」的两种理解：** 多数人（如布鲁斯）理解为「为了」：他为了将来的喜乐，忍受了十字架。也有人（如加尔文）理解为「代替」：他本可以享受喜乐，却选择了十字架（参腓2:6-8）。前一种理解较常见，也和11:26摩西「想望所要得的赏赐」相呼应。
- **「轻看羞辱」：** 十字架在罗马世界是最羞耻的死法。耶稣不是不觉得羞耻，而是不让羞耻拦住他。他所看重的喜乐，包括领许多的儿子进荣耀里去（2:10）。
- **「坐在神宝座的右边」：** 这是全书反复出现的画面（1:3；8:1；10:12）。他跑完了，坐下了；我们还在跑，终点那里有他。

**4. 「你们要思想」。** 「那忍受罪人这样顶撞的，你们要思想，免得疲倦灰心。」（12:3）作者给疲倦的人开的药方，不是先叫他们更努力，而是叫他们思想耶稣所忍受的。

> 要点：古人的见证叫我们知道这条路能跑完，可是我们要看的是耶稣。他为我们忍受了十字架，现在已经坐在神宝座的右边；我们的信心从他开始，也要由他完成。

## 深读 12:4-11 主所爱的，他必管教

**1. 「你们又忘了」。** 「你们与罪恶相争，还没有抵挡到流血的地步。你们又忘了那劝你们如同劝儿子的话，说：我儿，你不可轻看主的管教，被他责备的时候也不可灰心；因为主所爱的，他必管教，又鞭打凡所收纳的儿子。」（12:4-6）
- **「还没有抵挡到流血的地步」：** 读者受过逼迫，却还没有人殉道。「与罪恶相争」可能是指抵挡逼迫他们的罪人（3节），也可能是指抵挡自己想要退后的试探；许多人认为两样都包括在内。
- **箴言3:11-12：** 作者说这段经文是「劝你们如同劝儿子的话」，旧约圣经的话今天仍然直接对读者说话。两个危险：一是「轻看」，不当一回事；一是「灰心」，被压垮。

**2. 「待你们如同待儿子」。** 「你们所忍受的，是神管教你们，待你们如同待儿子。焉有儿子不被父亲管教的呢？管教原是众子所共受的。你们若不受管教，就是私子，不是儿子了。」（12:7-8）
- **苦难不是神不要我们的证据：** 读者可能以为，受逼迫说明神离弃了他们。作者却说正相反，这表明神待他们如同儿子。古时的私生子常常没有父亲管，也没有产业；神亲自管教，是因为我们是他家里的人。
- **「管教」是什么：** 这个词的意思是教养、训练，和5:14「习练」、本章11节「经练」放在一起，好像运动员的操练。在上下文中，读者所受的主要是为信仰受的逼迫（10:32-34），不是因为犯了什么罪而受的报应。

**3. 「使我们在他的圣洁上有分」。** 「生身的父都是暂随己意管教我们；惟有万灵的父管教我们，是要我们得益处，使我们在他的圣洁上有分。凡管教的事，当时不觉得快乐，反觉得愁苦；后来却为那经练过的人结出平安的果子，就是义。」（12:10-11）
- **地上的父和天上的父：** 再好的父亲，也只是「暂随己意」，凭着有限的判断，有时也夹着自己的情绪。神的管教却总是「要我们得益处」，目的是叫我们像他。
- **管教不是付罪债：** 神的管教有时也是对罪的纠正，「我们受审的时候，乃是被主惩治，免得我们和世人一同定罪」（林前11:32）。但纠正不是还债。我们罪的刑罚，基督已经在十字架上替我们担当了：「因他受的刑罚，我们得平安」（赛53:5）；他「亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24）。所以信徒所受的苦，没有一样是神在向他讨罪债。也不要把每一样苦难都和某一件罪连在一起，耶稣明说那生来瞎眼的人，「也不是这人犯了罪，也不是他父母犯了罪」（约9:3）。
- **「平安的果子」：** 管教当时是愁苦的，作者没有假装它不痛；但神要的结果是义，是与神、与人之间的平安。

**4. 神的管教绝不为虐待开脱。** 这段经文常被人误用，必须说清楚：
- 神作父亲的管教，绝不是父母、配偶或教会领袖施暴的理由。圣经叫作父亲的「不要惹儿女的气」（弗6:4）。「鞭打」是箴言的比喻，不是允许任何人打伤别人。
- 孩子或配偶若正在遭受暴力，应当先到安全的地方去，并且向警方或有关部门举报；神设立掌权的，就是要「刑罚那作恶的」（罗13:4）。
- 因别人的虐待而受苦，不等于神在叫受害的人留下来忍受。离开危险、寻求保护，并不是不顺服神。
- 教会不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而隐瞒虐待，也不可叫受害的人和不安全的人和好。

> 要点：神管教我们，是父亲在养育儿子，不是审判官在讨债；我们罪的刑罚已经落在基督身上。他的管教总是为我们的益处，要我们在他的圣洁上有分。这一点也提醒我们：神的管教从来不是任何人虐待人的借口。

## 深读 12:12-17 不要像以扫

**1. 「把下垂的手、发酸的腿挺起来」。** 「所以，你们要把下垂的手、发酸的腿挺起来；也要为自己的脚，把道路修直了，使瘸子不致歪脚，反得痊愈。」（12:12-13）
- **旧约的话：** 这里用了以赛亚书35:3「你们要使软弱的手坚壮，无力的膝稳固」和箴言4:26「要修平你脚下的路」。以赛亚书35章讲的是神的百姓回归锡安，正好引到本章22节。
- **为了别人：** 「瘸子」是群体中最软弱的人。我们把路修直，不只是为自己，也是为了不让软弱的弟兄姊妹更跌倒。

**2. 「追求与众人和睦，并要追求圣洁」。** 「你们要追求与众人和睦，并要追求圣洁；非圣洁没有人能见主。」（12:14）
- **不是靠圣洁得救：** 希伯来书说，我们得以成圣，是因基督「一次献祭」（10:10、14）。这里说的追求圣洁，是这样的人必然有的生命方向；没有这方向的人，说明他从来没有真正属于主。圣洁是神管教要结出的果子（10节）。
- **和睦与圣洁放在一起：** 和睦不是一团和气、不讲是非；圣洁也不是冷冷地远离众人。

**3. 「恐怕有毒根生出来」。** 「又要谨慎，恐怕有人失了神的恩；恐怕有毒根生出来扰乱你们，因此叫众人沾染污秽。」（12:15）
- **申命记29:18：** 那里的「恶根」是指心里偏离耶和华、去事奉别神的人。毒根起初看不见，长出来就会扰乱整个群体。
- **「失了神的恩」：** 这个词有掉队、落在后面的意思，像长途旅行中跟不上的人。作者要整个群体彼此看顾（3:13）。

**4. 以扫「得不着门路」。** 「恐怕有淫乱的，有贪恋世俗如以扫的，他因一点食物把自己长子的名分卖了。后来想要承受父所祝的福，竟被弃绝，虽然号哭切求，却得不着门路使他父亲的心意回转。这是你们知道的。」（12:16-17）
- **「贪恋世俗」：** 意思是把神所看重的当作平常。创世记说「这就是以扫轻看了他长子的名分」（创25:34）。为了眼前一碗汤，放弃了神所应许的产业。
- **谁的「心意回转」：** 原文直译是「他找不到悔改的余地」。有几种理解：
  - **以撒的心意：** 和合本把它译成以撒的心意不能回转。以撒已经为雅各祝福，并说「他将来也必蒙福」（创27:33）。
  - **以扫无法挽回：** 许多现代解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩）认为，这是说以扫不能把已经做的事收回，后果已定，眼泪也改变不了。
  - **以扫没有真正的悔改：** 加尔文认为，以扫哭的是失去的福分，不是得罪了神；这种忧愁像「世俗的忧愁」（林后7:10），不是归向神的悔改。
- **这警告的意思：** 6:4-8的深读已经介绍过对这几段警告经文的几种主要理解，这里不再重复。各种看法都同意：这里不是说一个真心悔改、回到神面前的人会被拒绝。以扫的问题是看轻了神所给的；作者警告读者，不要为了躲避眼前的苦难，把基督换成「一点食物」。

> 要点：神的管教要结出和睦与圣洁的果子。不要为了眼前的安稳，轻看神所给的产业；可是任何真心回到他面前的人，都会发现恩典的门是开着的。

## 深读 12:18-24 你们乃是来到锡安山

**1. 那能摸的山。** 「你们原不是来到那能摸的山；此山有火焰、密云、黑暗、暴风、角声与说话的声音。」（12:18-19上）
- **西奈山的情景：** 神在西奈山颁布律法的时候，山上有雷轰、闪电和密云，「营中的百姓尽都发颤」（出19:16）。百姓求摩西说：「不要神和我们说话，恐怕我们死亡。」（出20:19）
- **「我甚是恐惧战兢」：** 这句话最接近申命记9:19，那是摩西看见百姓拜金牛犊以后，怕神发怒灭绝他们。许多人认为，作者把这句话放在这里，是要说明连中保摩西也站立不住。

**2. 你们所来到的。** 「你们乃是来到锡安山，永生神的城邑，就是天上的耶路撒冷。那里有千万的天使，有名录在天上诸长子之会所共聚的总会，有审判众人的神和被成全之义人的灵魂，并新约的中保耶稣，以及所洒的血。」（12:22-24上）
- **「乃是来到」：** 这个动词是完成式，不是「将要来到」，而是「已经来到」。许多人认为，这是指信徒藉着基督，现在就在敬拜中进到神面前（10:19-22），虽然还在等候那将来的城（13:14）。
- **「千万的天使」：** 和合本这节没有译出的那个词，原文有节日欢聚的意思，ESV译作festal gathering。西奈山的百姓发颤，锡安山却是一场欢庆的聚会。
- **「诸长子之会」：** 多数人认为是指神的百姓，就是教会；他们的名字录在天上（参路10:20）。长子是承受产业的人，这正和卖了长子名分的以扫相对。也有人认为是指天使。
- **「被成全之义人的灵魂」：** 多数人认为是指已经离世的信徒，包括第十一章那些旧约的圣徒；他们「若不与我们同得，就不能完全」（11:40），如今因基督一次的献祭得以完全（10:14）。
- **「审判众人的神」：** 锡安山上神仍然是审判者，可是我们来到他面前，因为那里也有中保和他的血。

**3. 「比亚伯的血所说的更美」。** 「这血所说的比亚伯的血所说的更美。」（12:24下）
- **亚伯的血说什么：** 多数人认为是指创世记4:10：「你兄弟的血有声音从地里向我哀告」，那是呼求伸冤的血。也有人联系11:4亚伯「虽然死了，却因这信，仍旧说话」。
- **耶稣的血说什么：** 亚伯的血喊着要审判；耶稣的血说的是赦免、洁净、与神和好（9:14；10:22）。前约的血洒在百姓身上，叫他们站在山下；耶稣的血洒在我们心上，叫我们进到神面前。

> 要点：我们不是还站在西奈山下发颤，而是已经藉着耶稣的血，来到锡安山欢庆的聚会中。审判众人的神就在那里，可是中保耶稣也在那里，他的血一直在为我们说更美的话。

## 深读 12:25-29 不能震动的国

**1. 「不可弃绝那向你们说话的」。** 「你们总要谨慎，不可弃绝那向你们说话的。因为，那些弃绝在地上警戒他们的尚且不能逃罪，何况我们违背那从天上警戒我们的呢？」（12:25）
- **「在地上警戒他们的」：** 有人认为是摩西；多数人认为是神自己在西奈山说话，因为下一节说「当时他的声音震动了地」。从天上说话的，是如今藉着儿子说话的神（1:1-2）。
- **又一段警告：** 这和2:1-4的论证一样，从小推到大。6:4-8的深读已经讨论过这几段警告的不同理解，要点是：不可转离那正在向我们说话的神。

**2. 「再一次」。** 「当时他的声音震动了地，但如今他应许说：『再一次我不单要震动地，还要震动天。』这再一次的话，是指明被震动的，就是受造之物都要挪去，使那不被震动的常存。」（12:26-27）
- **哈该书2:6：** 哈该说神要「再一次震动天地」，那是在第二圣殿重建的时候。作者把这话应用到末后的审判。
- **「挪去」：** 有人认为现在的天地要被除去；也有许多人认为是被炼净、更新，好像1:11-12说天地「都要改变」，又如彼得后书3:13的「新天新地」。无论哪一种，一切可以被震动的，都不能作我们最后的依靠。

**3. 「就当感恩」。** 「所以我们既得了不能震动的国，就当感恩，照神所喜悦的，用虔诚、敬畏的心事奉神。因为我们的神乃是烈火。」（12:28-29）
- **「得了」：** 这国不是我们建立的，而是领受的，现在就已经是我们的（参但7:18）。
- **「感恩」：** 原文直译是「让我们有恩」，许多人认为意思是心存感恩，和合本和ESV都这样理解。我们事奉神，是出于感恩，不是为了赚得什么。
- **「烈火」：** 这是申命记4:24的话。锡安山的神和西奈山的神是同一位，他的圣洁没有因为恩典而减少。正因为他是烈火，那些得以亲近他的人才会存着「虔诚、敬畏的心」；而我们能亲近他，是因为烈火般的审判已经落在替我们受死的基督身上。

> 要点：天地都会震动，可是神赐给我们的国不会。因为我们是白白领受的，所以事奉他是感恩；因为他是烈火，所以这感恩里有敬畏。

## 默想

**默想：** 「主所爱的，他必管教。」（12:6）「你们乃是来到锡安山。」（12:22）那一年，公司裁员的名单上有我的名字；没过多久，母亲住进了医院。我心里第一个冒出来的问题，不是「神啊，你在哪里」，而是「是不是神在惩罚我？我到底做了什么，要受这样的报应？」我们从小听惯了「善有善报，恶有恶报」，好像每一件不幸的事，背后都有一笔要还的账。我小时候的管教也加深了这种感觉：父亲很严，生气的时候会打人，事后说这是「为你好」。我分不清哪些是管教，哪些只是他的怒气。所以读到「鞭打凡所收纳的儿子」（12:6），我心里会缩一下。可是希伯来书说，生身的父「都是暂随己意管教我们」（12:10）；地上的父亲会把怒气当成管教，天上的父不会。如果有人正在伤害我，不论是父母、配偶还是教会的领袖，那不是神的管教，更不是神要我留下来忍受；我可以先到安全的地方去，也可以报警（罗13:4）。神的管教还有一个和我想的完全不同的地方：它从来不是要我付罪债。我的罪该受的刑罚，已经落在基督身上了：「因他受的刑罚，我们得平安。」（赛53:5）十字架上该付的已经付清了，没有一笔账留给我用苦难来还。并不是每一件苦事都和我的某一件罪有关（约9:3）；就算我真的看见了自己的罪，我也可以向他承认，因为纠正我的是父亲，不是讨债的人。他管教我，是因为我是他的儿女，不是私子（12:8）；他的目的是「使我们在他的圣洁上有分」（12:10），叫我更像他。然后作者把我的眼睛从眼前的难处抬起来：「你们乃是来到锡安山。」不是将来才到，是已经到了。就在我失业、守在病房外的那段日子，我其实站在千万天使欢庆的聚会中，我的名字录在天上，审判众人的神就在那里，可是新约的中保耶稣也在那里。亚伯的血从地里喊着要伸冤；耶稣「所洒的血」，为我说的是「更美」的话（12:24）。如果你还从来没有来到他面前，你不必先用受苦来赔偿，也赔偿不了；你可以承认自己的罪，悔改，信靠这位为你流血的耶稣。我失去的工作，母亲衰弱的身体，都是会被震动的东西；可是神已经把一个「不能震动的国」放在我手里（12:28）。父啊，这国不是我赚来的，是你因着你儿子的血白白赐给我的。我要「感恩」（12:28），怀着敬畏和喜乐，站在你的锡安山上，感谢你那位为我信心创始成终的儿子，感谢他所洒的血此刻仍在为我说更美的话。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 12 follows the long line of the faithful in chapter 11 and has four parts. **Looking to Jesus** (vv. 1–3): since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight and the sin that clings so closely, and run with endurance the race set before us, looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith. **The Lord disciplines the one he loves** (vv. 4–13): what the readers endure is a father disciplining his sons; earthly fathers disciplined us as it seemed best to them, but the Father of spirits disciplines us that we may share his holiness; so lift your drooping hands and strengthen your weak knees. **Not like Esau** (vv. 14–17): strive for peace and holiness, see that no one fails to obtain the grace of God, and do not be like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal. **Two mountains** (vv. 18–29): we have not come to the mountain that may be touched, Sinai, but to Mount Zion, to Jesus the mediator of a new covenant and his sprinkled blood; so do not refuse him who speaks from heaven, but be grateful for a kingdom that cannot be shaken, and offer God acceptable worship with reverence and awe.

Chapter 11 speaks of "they"; chapter 12 opens with "we": they have finished their race, and now it is our turn. The chapter's high point is verses 22–24, where the author shows his readers, in a series of pictures, where they have already arrived.

## Going Deeper: 12:1–3 Looking to Jesus, the Founder and Perfecter of Our Faith

**1. "So great a cloud of witnesses."** "Therefore, since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us also lay aside every weight, and sin which clings so closely, and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us" (12:1).
- **Spectators or witnesses who testify:** the picture is a race in a stadium, so many preachers see these people as spectators in the stands, watching how we run. Bruce and Lane stress that "witnesses" mainly means people who bear witness: the people of chapter 11 "received their commendation" (11:2, 39), and their lives testify to faith; it is not so much that they look at us as that we look at them. Attridge and others think both senses are present. On any view, the author does not tell us to pray to them or rely on their help, but to learn faith from them and then turn our eyes to Jesus.
- **"Weight" and "sin":** "weight" was the extra load a runner carried; it need not be sin, but it slows us down. "Sin" is like a long robe tangled around the legs. Both must be laid aside.
- **"With endurance":** this is not a sprint but a distance race. The readers have already endured suffering (10:32–34), and 10:36 said, "you have need of endurance"; now he says it again.

**2. "Founder and perfecter."** "Looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith" (12:2a).
- **"Founder":** the same word as "founder of their salvation" in 2:10; it means the one who opens the way and leads.
- **"Perfecter":** the one who brings something to completion. The Greek has no "our"; it reads literally "the founder and perfecter of faith." Many take it in two senses: Jesus himself ran the whole course of faith first, trusting the Father all his life (2:13, "I will put my trust in him"); and he is the source of our faith and will bring it to completion. The people of chapter 11 are witnesses; Jesus is the Lord who gives faith its beginning and its end.

**3. "For the joy that was set before him."** "Who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God" (12:2b).
- **Two readings of "for":** most (e.g., Bruce) take it as "for the sake of": he endured the cross for the joy ahead. Some (e.g., Calvin) take it as "instead of": he could have had joy, but chose the cross (see Phil 2:6–8). The first reading is more common, and it matches Moses "looking to the reward" in 11:26.
- **"Despising the shame":** in the Roman world the cross was the most shameful of deaths. Jesus was not unaware of the shame; he refused to let it stop him. The joy he valued included "bringing many sons to glory" (2:10).
- **"Seated at the right hand of the throne of God":** a picture repeated through the letter (1:3; 8:1; 10:12). He has finished and sat down; we are still running, and he is at the finish.

**4. "Consider him."** "Consider him who endured from sinners such hostility against himself, so that you may not grow weary or fainthearted" (12:3). The author's remedy for weariness is not first to try harder, but to consider what Jesus endured.

> The point: The witness of the faithful tells us this race can be finished, but it is Jesus we look to. He endured the cross for us and now sits at the right hand of the throne of God; our faith begins with him and will be completed by him.

## Going Deeper: 12:4–11 The Lord Disciplines the One He Loves

**1. "Have you forgotten?"** "In your struggle against sin you have not yet resisted to the point of shedding your blood. And have you forgotten the exhortation that addresses you as sons? 'My son, do not regard lightly the discipline of the Lord, nor be weary when reproved by him. For the Lord disciplines the one he loves, and chastises every son whom he receives'" (12:4–6).
- **"Not yet... to the point of shedding your blood":** the readers had been persecuted, but none had yet been martyred. "Struggle against sin" may mean resisting the sinners who opposed them (v. 3), or resisting their own temptation to shrink back; many think both are included.
- **Proverbs 3:11–12:** the author calls this "the exhortation that addresses you as sons"; the words of the Old Testament Scriptures still speak directly to the readers. There are two dangers: to "regard lightly," shrugging it off, and to "be weary," being crushed by it.

**2. "God is treating you as sons."** "It is for discipline that you have to endure. God is treating you as sons. For what son is there whom his father does not discipline? If you are left without discipline, in which all have participated, then you are illegitimate children and not sons" (12:7–8).
- **Suffering is not proof that God has rejected us:** the readers may have thought persecution meant God had abandoned them. The author says the opposite: it shows God is treating them as sons. In the ancient world an illegitimate child often had no father to raise him and no inheritance; God disciplines us himself because we belong to his household.
- **What "discipline" is:** the word means upbringing and training; with "trained" in 5:14 and "trained" in verse 11 of this chapter, it suggests an athlete's training. In context, what the readers suffered was mainly persecution for their faith (10:32–34), not retribution for some sin.

**3. "That we may share his holiness."** "For they disciplined us for a short time as it seemed best to them, but he disciplines us for our good, that we may share his holiness. For the moment all discipline seems painful rather than pleasant, but later it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it" (12:10–11).
- **Earthly fathers and the heavenly Father:** even the best father disciplines "as it seemed best to" him, with limited judgment and sometimes mixed with his own feelings. God's discipline is always "for our good," aimed at making us like him.
- **Discipline is not paying a debt for sin:** God's discipline is sometimes correction for sin: "when we are judged by the Lord, we are disciplined so that we may not be condemned along with the world" (1 Cor 11:32). But correction is not repayment. The penalty for our sin Christ has already borne in our place on the cross: "upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace" (Isa 53:5); "he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" (1 Pet 2:24). So nothing a believer suffers is God collecting a debt for sin. Nor should we tie every hardship to a particular sin; of the man born blind Jesus said plainly, "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents" (John 9:3).
- **"The peaceful fruit of righteousness":** discipline is painful at the time, and the author does not pretend otherwise; but the result God intends is righteousness, and peace with God and with others.

**4. God's discipline never excuses abuse.** This passage is often misused, so it must be said plainly:
- God's fatherly discipline is never a justification for abuse by parents, spouses or church leaders. Scripture tells fathers, "do not provoke your children to anger" (Eph 6:4). "Chastises" in Proverbs is a figure of speech, not permission for anyone to injure another.
- A child or spouse suffering violence should first get to a safe place and report it to the police or the proper authorities; God appointed the governing authorities as "an avenger who carries out God's wrath on the wrongdoer" (Rom 13:4).
- Suffering caused by someone's abuse does not mean God is telling the victim to stay and endure it. Leaving danger and seeking protection is not disobedience to God.
- The church must not hide abuse to save face ("do not air the family's shame"), and must not press victims to reconcile with someone unsafe.

> The point: When God disciplines us, he is a father raising his children, not a judge collecting a debt; the penalty for our sin has already fallen on Christ. His discipline is always for our good, that we may share his holiness. And this is also a reminder that God's discipline is never anyone's excuse for abuse.

## Going Deeper: 12:12–17 Not Like Esau

**1. "Lift your drooping hands."** "Therefore lift your drooping hands and strengthen your weak knees, and make straight paths for your feet, so that what is lame may not be put out of joint but rather be healed" (12:12–13).
- **Old Testament words:** the author draws on Isaiah 35:3, "Strengthen the weak hands, and make firm the feeble knees," and on the Greek Old Testament of Proverbs 4:26 ("make straight paths for your feet"). Isaiah 35 is about God's people returning to Zion, which leads into verse 22 of this chapter.
- **For others' sake:** "what is lame" is the weakest member of the community. We make the path straight not only for ourselves, but so that weak brothers and sisters do not stumble further.

**2. "Strive for peace... and for the holiness."** "Strive for peace with everyone, and for the holiness without which no one will see the Lord" (12:14).
- **Not saved by holiness:** Hebrews says we are sanctified "by a single offering" of Christ (10:10, 14). The pursuit of holiness here is the direction every such person's life will take; one with no such direction shows he never truly belonged to the Lord. Holiness is the fruit God's discipline is meant to bear (v. 10).
- **Peace and holiness together:** peace is not keeping everyone happy while ignoring right and wrong; holiness is not coldly keeping away from everyone.

**3. "That no 'root of bitterness' springs up."** "See to it that no one fails to obtain the grace of God; that no 'root of bitterness' springs up and causes trouble, and by it many become defiled" (12:15).
- **Deuteronomy 29:18:** the "root bearing poisonous and bitter fruit" there is a person whose heart turns away from the Lord to serve other gods. A poisonous root is unseen at first, but once it grows it troubles the whole community.
- **"Fails to obtain":** the word carries the sense of falling behind, like a traveler who cannot keep up on a long journey. The author wants the whole community to watch over one another (3:13).

**4. Esau "found no chance to repent."** "That no one is sexually immoral or unholy like Esau, who sold his birthright for a single meal. For you know that afterward, when he desired to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, for he found no chance to repent, though he sought it with tears" (12:16–17).
- **"Unholy":** treating as ordinary what God counts precious. Genesis says, "Thus Esau despised his birthright" (Gen 25:34). For a bowl of stew he gave up the inheritance God had promised.
- **Whose change of mind?** The Greek reads literally, "he found no place for repentance." There are several readings:
  - **Isaac's mind:** the Chinese Union Version renders it as Isaac's mind not being changed. Isaac had already blessed Jacob and said, "Yes, and he shall be blessed" (Gen 27:33).
  - **Esau could not undo it:** many modern commentators (e.g., Bruce, Lane) take it to mean Esau could not take back what he had done; the outcome was fixed, and tears could not change it.
  - **Esau had no true repentance:** Calvin held that Esau wept for the lost blessing, not because he had sinned against God; his grief was like "worldly grief" (2 Cor 7:10), not repentance toward God.
- **What the warning means:** the Going Deeper section on 6:4–8 set out the main readings of these warning passages, so they are not repeated here. All of them agree that this does not say someone who truly repents and returns to God will be turned away. Esau's problem was that he despised what God had given; the author warns his readers not to trade Christ for "a single meal" to escape present suffering.

> The point: God's discipline is meant to bear the fruit of peace and holiness. Do not despise the inheritance God gives for the sake of present comfort; yet anyone who truly comes back to him will find the door of grace open.

## Going Deeper: 12:18–24 You Have Come to Mount Zion

**1. The mountain that may be touched.** "For you have not come to what may be touched, a blazing fire and darkness and gloom and a tempest and the sound of a trumpet and a voice" (12:18–19a).
- **The scene at Sinai:** when God gave the law at Sinai there were "thunders and lightnings and a thick cloud on the mountain... so that all the people in the camp trembled" (Exod 19:16). The people begged Moses, "do not let God speak to us, lest we die" (Exod 20:19).
- **"I tremble with fear":** these words come closest to Deuteronomy 9:19, where Moses, after the golden calf, was afraid that God in his anger would destroy the people. Many think the author places them here to show that even Moses the mediator could not stand.

**2. Where you have come.** "But you have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to innumerable angels in festal gathering, and to the assembly of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven, and to God, the judge of all, and to the spirits of the righteous made perfect, and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, and to the sprinkled blood" (12:22–24a).
- **"You have come":** the verb is perfect tense: not "you will come" but "you have come." Many take this to mean that believers, through Christ, already draw near to God in worship now (10:19–22), though they still seek the city that is to come (13:14).
- **"In festal gathering":** this word, which the Chinese Union Version does not translate separately, means a festival celebration. At Sinai the people trembled; Zion is a joyful festival.
- **"The assembly of the firstborn":** most take this as God's people, the church, whose names are written in heaven (see Luke 10:20). The firstborn is the heir, the opposite of Esau, who sold his birthright. Some take it as the angels.
- **"The spirits of the righteous made perfect":** most take this as believers who have died, including the Old Testament saints of chapter 11, who were not to "be made perfect" "apart from us" (11:40) and are now made perfect by Christ's single offering (10:14).
- **"God, the judge of all":** on Zion God is still the judge, yet we come to him, because the mediator and his blood are there too.

**3. "A better word than the blood of Abel."** "The sprinkled blood that speaks a better word than the blood of Abel" (12:24b).
- **What Abel's blood says:** most take this from Genesis 4:10, "The voice of your brother's blood is crying to me from the ground," blood crying out for justice. Some connect it with 11:4: "though he died, he still speaks."
- **What Jesus' blood says:** Abel's blood cries for judgment; Jesus' blood speaks forgiveness, cleansing and peace with God (9:14; 10:22). The blood of the old covenant was sprinkled on the people and kept them at the foot of the mountain; the blood of Jesus is sprinkled on our hearts and brings us near to God.

> The point: We are not still trembling at the foot of Sinai; through the blood of Jesus we have already come to the festal gathering on Mount Zion. God the judge of all is there, but so is Jesus the mediator, and his blood keeps speaking a better word for us.

## Going Deeper: 12:25–29 A Kingdom That Cannot Be Shaken

**1. "Do not refuse him who is speaking."** "See that you do not refuse him who is speaking. For if they did not escape when they refused him who warned them on earth, much less will we escape if we reject him who warns from heaven" (12:25).
- **"Him who warned them on earth":** some think this is Moses; most think it is God himself speaking at Sinai, since the next verse says, "At that time his voice shook the earth." The one who speaks from heaven is God, who now speaks by his Son (1:1–2).
- **Another warning:** the argument runs from lesser to greater, as in 2:1–4. The Going Deeper section on 6:4–8 discussed the different readings of these warnings; the point is not to turn away from the God who is speaking to us now.

**2. "Yet once more."** "At that time his voice shook the earth, but now he has promised, 'Yet once more I will shake not only the earth but also the heavens.' This phrase, 'Yet once more,' indicates the removal of things that are shaken" (12:26–27a).
- **Haggai 2:6:** Haggai said, "Yet once more, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth," when the second temple was being rebuilt. The author applies it to the final judgment.
- **"Removal":** some think the present heavens and earth will be done away with; many others think they will be purified and renewed, as 1:11–12 says they "will be changed," and as 2 Peter 3:13 speaks of "new heavens and a new earth." On either view, nothing that can be shaken can be our final security.

**3. "Let us be grateful."** "Therefore let us be grateful for receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken, and thus let us offer to God acceptable worship, with reverence and awe, for our God is a consuming fire" (12:28–29).
- **"Receiving":** we did not build this kingdom; we receive it, and it is already ours (see Dan 7:18).
- **"Be grateful":** the Greek reads literally "let us have grace"; many take it to mean "let us be thankful," as both the Chinese Union Version and the ESV do. We serve God out of thanksgiving, not to earn anything.
- **"A consuming fire":** these are the words of Deuteronomy 4:24. The God of Zion is the God of Sinai, and grace has not made him less holy. Because he is a consuming fire, those who are brought near to him come "with reverence and awe"; and we can come near only because the fire of judgment fell on Christ, who died in our place.

> The point: Heaven and earth will be shaken, but the kingdom God gives us will not. Because we received it as a gift, our service is thanksgiving; because he is a consuming fire, that thanksgiving is full of awe.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For the Lord disciplines the one he loves" (12:6). "But you have come to Mount Zion" (12:22). That year my name was on the company's layoff list; not long after, my mother went into the hospital. The first question that rose in me was not "God, where are you?" but "Is God punishing me? What did I do to deserve this?" We grew up hearing that good is repaid with good and evil with evil, as though behind every misfortune there were a bill to be paid. The discipline of my childhood deepened that feeling: my father was strict, hit us when he was angry, and said afterward it was "for your own good." I could not tell what was discipline and what was only his anger. So when I read that he "chastises every son whom he receives" (12:6), something in me flinches. Yet Hebrews says earthly fathers "disciplined us... as it seemed best to them" (12:10); an earthly father can mistake his anger for discipline; the heavenly Father does not. If someone is hurting me now, whether a parent, a spouse or a church leader, that is not God's discipline, and it is not God telling me to stay and endure it; I may first get to a safe place, and I may report it (Rom 13:4). And God's discipline differs from what I imagined in one more way: it is never meant to make me pay for my sin. The penalty my sin deserved has already fallen on Christ: "upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace" (Isa 53:5). What the cross had to pay has been paid in full, and no bill is left for me to settle through suffering. Not every hardship is tied to some sin of mine (John 9:3); and even when I do see my sin, I can confess it to him, because the one correcting me is a Father, not a debt collector. He disciplines me because I am his child, not an illegitimate one (12:8); his aim is "that we may share his holiness" (12:10), that I may become like him. Then the author lifts my eyes from the trouble in front of me: "But you have come to Mount Zion." Not someday, but already. In those very days, out of work and waiting outside the hospital ward, I was in fact standing among innumerable angels in festal gathering; my name is enrolled in heaven; God, the judge of all, is there, and so is Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant. Abel's blood cried from the ground for justice; Jesus' "sprinkled blood" speaks "a better word" for me (12:24). If you have never come to him, you need not first pay him back by suffering, and you could not; you can confess your sin, repent, and trust this Jesus who shed his blood for you. The job I lost and my mother's failing body are among the things that are shaken; but God has put into my hands "a kingdom that cannot be shaken" (12:28). Father, I did not earn this kingdom; you gave it to me freely through the blood of your Son. So I will "be grateful" (12:28), and stand on your Mount Zion with awe and joy, thanking you for your Son, the founder and perfecter of my faith, and for his sprinkled blood, which even now speaks a better word for me.
