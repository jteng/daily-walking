## 本章脉络

第十三章是全书最后的劝勉和问安。**彼此相爱的生活**（1-6节）：常存弟兄相爱的心，接待客旅，记念被捆绑的人，尊重婚姻，不贪爱钱财，因为主曾说「我总不撇下你，也不丢弃你」。**带领者和不改变的基督**（7-17节）：要想念从前传神之道的人，因为「耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的」；不要被怪异的教训勾引；耶稣在城门外受苦，我们也当出到营外就了他去，以颂赞为祭献给神；也要依从现在引导你们的人。**代祷、祝福和问安**（18-25节）：请为我们祷告；愿赐平安的神在各样善事上成全你们；提摩太的消息；愿恩惠与众人同在。

这一章不是零碎的附录。12:28刚说要「照神所喜悦的」事奉神，这一章就讲这样的事奉在生活中的样子：行善和捐输是「神所喜悦的」（13:16），最后的祝福又求神行「他所喜悦的事」（13:21）。许多解经家也注意到，第7节和第17节都讲到「引导你们的」人，像一个框子，把中间关于基督和祭坛的话围在里面。

## 深读 13:1-6 彼此相爱的生活

**1. 「不知不觉就接待了天使」。** 「你们务要常存弟兄相爱的心。不可忘记用爱心接待客旅；因为曾有接待客旅的，不知不觉就接待了天使。」（13:1-2）
- **「常存」：** 读者从前就「伺候圣徒，如今还是伺候」（6:10）。作者不是叫他们开始去爱，而是不要让爱心在疲倦中冷下来。
- **亚伯拉罕：** 他看见三个人，「就从帐棚门口跑去迎接他们」（创18:2），后来才知道其中一位就是耶和华（创18:10）；罗得也是这样（创19:1-2）。当时出门传道和逃避逼迫的信徒，常要靠弟兄姊妹接待（约叁5-8）。
- **真正的动机：** 不是为了碰见天使，而是因为主说：「我作客旅，你们留我住」（太25:35）。接待人也要有分辨，特别要保护孩子的安全；但不可因为怕麻烦就关上门。

**2. 「好像与他们同受捆绑」。** 「你们要记念被捆绑的人，好像与他们同受捆绑；也要记念遭苦害的人，想到自己也在肉身之内。」（13:3）古时坐监的人常要靠亲友送饭送衣，读者从前就「体恤了那些被捆锁的人」（10:34）。「也在肉身之内」，多数解经家认为是说我们自己也有会受苦的身体，所以要把他们的痛苦当作自己的。今天，这包括为受逼迫的信徒祷告，也包括探访监狱里的人和他们的家人。

**3. 「婚姻，人人都当尊重」。** 「婚姻，人人都当尊重，床也不可污秽；因为苟合行淫的人，神必要审判。」（13:4）
- **两面的错误：** 有人以为禁欲更属灵，轻看婚姻（提前4:3）；有人以为身体的事与信仰无关，轻看婚床。作者两样都拒绝。「苟合」泛指婚姻以外的性关系，「行淫」特指破坏婚约；神的审判是真的。
- **更大的恩典：** 「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今你们奉主耶稣基督的名，并藉着我们神的灵，已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」（林前6:11）在这方面跌倒的人，可以带着羞愧来到基督面前认罪，他必赦免（约一1:9）。
- **不等于留在危险中：** 婚姻的尊荣，绝不是要人留在暴力或虐待的危险里。受害的人可以离开危险，不必和不安全的人和好；向警方举报是对的（罗13:4）。教会不可为了「家丑不可外扬」叫受害者忍着，或替施暴的人隐瞒。

**4. 「我总不撇下你」。** 「你们存心不可贪爱钱财，要以自己所有的为足；因为主曾说：『我总不撇下你，也不丢弃你。』所以我们可以放胆说：主是帮助我的，我必不惧怕；人能把我怎么样呢？」（13:5-6）知足的根据不是钱少一点也没关系，而是我们有主自己。这应许是摩西把带领的担子交给约书亚的时候说的（申31:6；书1:5）：带领的人换了，应许没有换。第6节引的是诗篇118:6。读者的家业曾被人抢去（10:34），可是人拿不走主的同在。

> 要点：彼此相爱、接待客旅、记念受苦的人、尊重婚姻、不贪爱钱财，不是另外加上的规条，而是蒙了恩的人日常的样子。它的根基是主的应许：他总不撇下我们。

## 深读 13:7-9 昨日、今日、一直到永远

**1. 「要想念他们」。** 「从前引导你们、传神之道给你们的人，你们要想念他们，效法他们的信心，留心看他们为人的结局。」（13:7）这些带领者似乎已经离世了。「为人的结局」，许多解经家（如布鲁斯）认为是指他们怎样走完一生，也许有人为主殉道；也有人认为泛指他们一生行事的结果。作者叫读者效法的是他们的「信心」，也就是效法他们所信靠的那一位，不是崇拜人。

**2. 「耶稣基督……是一样的」。** 「耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的。」（13:8）
- **夹在中间：** 这一节放在第7节和第9节中间。带领的人会离世，他们所传的那一位却没有换人；既然基督是一样的，就不必去追求新奇的教训。
- **回到第一章：** 作者在1:12把诗篇102篇讲神的话用在儿子身上：「惟有你永不改变」。这里他用了耶稣在地上的名字：那位曾经受苦的耶稣，就是永不改变的主（参玛3:6）。
- **什么没有改变：** 耶稣成了血肉之体，受苦，复活，升天，他的境况有许多变化；但他是谁，他的信实，他一次成就的救恩，都没有改变。昨日，他在城门外受苦（13:12）；今日，他「长远活着，替他们祈求」（7:25）；直到永远，他是永约的大牧人（13:20）。

**3. 「人心靠恩得坚固才是好的」。** 「你们不要被那诸般怪异的教训勾引了去；因为人心靠恩得坚固才是好的，并不是靠饮食。那在饮食上专心的从来没有得着益处。」（13:9）这里的饮食指什么，解经家有几种看法：犹太人的洁净饮食条例；和圣殿献祭或节期相连的筵席，以为吃了能得属灵的益处（如布鲁斯、雷恩）；或者带有禁欲色彩的规条（参西2:16）。无论哪一种，作者在9:10已经说过，这些都是「属肉体的条例」。今天也有人说吃素能积功德，或某种饮食、某种仪式能叫人更属灵；作者的回答还是一样：坚固人心的是恩典，「神的国不在乎吃喝」（罗14:17）。

> 要点：带领的人会离去，教会的情形也会改变，但我们所信的耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远都是一样的。坚固人心的，是他的恩典。

## 深读 13:10-16 出到营外，就了他去

**1. 「我们有一祭坛」。** 「我们有一祭坛，上面的祭物是那些在帐幕中供职的人不可同吃的。」（13:10）
- **几种看法：** 许多解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩、柯克里尔）认为，这是指基督在十字架上的献祭本身，「吃」是用信心领受这祭带来的一切。有些人，特别是罗马天主教传统中的一些解经家，认为是指圣餐的祭坛。更正教的传统认为，圣餐桌不是再献挽回祭的祭坛（参第七章关于弥撒的讨论）；但我们在圣餐中确实「同领基督的血」「同领基督的身体」（林前10:16），领受那已经成全的祭。
- **「不可同吃」：** 按律法，赎罪祭的血若带进圣所，「那肉都不可吃，必用火焚烧」（利6:30）。前约的祭司在赎罪日最大的祭上一口也不能吃；凡信靠基督的人却可以领受这真正的赎罪祭。仍旧依靠帐幕的人，在这祭上反倒无分。

**2. 「在城门外受苦」。** 「原来牲畜的血被大祭司带入圣所作赎罪祭；牲畜的身子被烧在营外。所以，耶稣要用自己的血叫百姓成圣，也就在城门外受苦。」（13:11-12）
- **赎罪日的影儿：** 赎罪祭的牛羊「要搬到营外，将皮、肉、粪用火焚烧」（利16:27）。耶稣「背着自己的十字架出来」，钉十字架的地方「与城相近」（约19:17、20），在耶路撒冷城外。
- **被咒诅的地方：** 营外是不洁净的人被送去、受咒诅的人被处死的地方（利24:14）。耶稣在那里「为我们受了咒诅」（加3:13），替我们担当了罪的刑罚，所以他的血能叫百姓成圣，而且只一次就成全了（10:10、14）。

**3. 「出到营外，就了他去」。** 「这样，我们也当出到营外，就了他去，忍受他所受的凌辱。我们在这里本没有常存的城，乃是寻求那将来的城。」（13:13-14）
- **离开什么：** 许多解经家（如布鲁斯）认为，作者首先是叫犹太信徒离开以圣殿献祭为中心的体系，宁可被同胞看作背道的人；加尔文等人讲得更宽，指离开世界所给的安全和尊荣，甘心跟随被弃绝的基督。这两种理解并不冲突。「凌辱」和11:26摩西「为基督受的凌辱」是同一个词，10:33的「毁谤」也是。
- **营外也是遇见神的地方：** 摩西曾把会幕支搭在营外，「凡求问耶和华的，就到营外的会幕那里去」（出33:7）。我们出到营外，是到他那里去，因为他已经在那里了。地上的城，连耶路撒冷在内，都不是我们最后的家（11:10、16）。

**4. 「以颂赞为祭」。** 「我们应当靠着耶稣，常常以颂赞为祭献给神，这就是那承认主名之人嘴唇的果子。只是不可忘记行善和捐输的事，因为这样的祭是神所喜悦的。」（13:15-16）赎罪的祭已经成全了，「就不用再为罪献祭了」（10:18）。加尔文区分挽回的祭和感恩的祭：前一种只有基督能献，而且已经献了；后一种是蒙了恩的人一生献给神的。连我们的赞美也要「靠着耶稣」才蒙悦纳（彼前2:5）。这感恩的祭有两样：嘴唇上向神的颂赞，手中向人的行善和捐输。

> 要点：耶稣在城门外替我们担当了咒诅，用自己的血叫我们成圣。所以我们出到营外，不是为了赚得什么，而是到他那里去；我们没有别的祭要献，只献上感恩的颂赞和爱人的行动。

## 深读 13:17-19 依从引导你们的

**1. 「好像那将来交帐的人」。** 「你们要依从那些引导你们的，且要顺服；因他们为你们的灵魂时刻警醒，好像那将来交帐的人。你们要使他们交的时候有快乐，不致忧愁；若忧愁就与你们无益了。」（13:17）
- **「依从」：** 许多人认为，这个词的意思是被说服而听从，不是盲目服从。「警醒」原指不睡觉，像牧人夜里看守羊群。
- **带领者要交帐：** 神说：「我必与牧人为敌，必向他们的手追讨我的羊」（结34:10）；长老要「牧养神的教会，就是他用自己血所买来的」（徒20:28），「也不是辖制所托付你们的」（彼前5:3）。

**2. 这绝不是属灵虐待的许可。** 顺服带领者，是在神的话以下的顺服；带领者若要人做违背圣经的事，就当「顺从神，不顺从人」（徒5:29）。带领者若利用属灵的权柄控制人、性侵或虐待人，这就不是本节所说的顺服。向警方举报是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必和不安全的人和好；即使他认罪，也不等于恢复他的职分，或让他再接近受害者和孩子。教会不可因为「家丑不可外扬」替他隐瞒。

**3. 「请你们为我们祷告」。** 「请你们为我们祷告，因我们自觉良心无亏，愿意凡事按正道而行。我更求你们为我祷告，使我快些回到你们那里去。」（13:18-19）写这卷书的人没有站在会众之上，他也需要代祷。他说「良心无亏」，许多解经家认为也许有人在议论他；至于是什么拦阻他回去，我们并不知道。

> 要点：神把带领者赐给教会，为羊群警醒，他们自己也要向大牧人交帐。我们尊重、跟随忠心的带领者，为他们祷告；但真正的牧人只有一位，任何人都不可借他的名伤害他的羊。

## 深读 13:20-25 赐平安的神

**1. 「使群羊的大牧人……从死里复活」。** 「但愿赐平安的神，就是那凭永约之血、使群羊的大牧人－我主耶稣从死里复活的神」（13:20）
- **明说复活：** 希伯来书常讲耶稣坐在神的右边；许多人注意到，这是全书唯一明说耶稣复活的一处。
- **大牧人：** 许多人认为这里回响以赛亚书63:11：神「将百姓和牧养他全群的人从海里领上来」，那牧人是摩西。耶稣是神从死里领上来的大牧人，是「为羊舍命」的好牧人（约10:11）。地上的带领者都在他以下。
- **「永约之血」：** 先知应许神要立「永约」（赛55:3；耶32:40），又说「我因与你立约的血」释放被囚的人（亚9:11）。第八章所讲的新约，就是这永约；它是用耶稣的血立的，所以永不过去。

**2. 「在你们心里行他所喜悦的事」。** 「在各样善事上成全你们，叫你们遵行他的旨意；又藉着耶稣基督在你们心里行他所喜悦的事。愿荣耀归给他，直到永永远远。阿们！」（13:21）
- **「成全」：** 这个词也用在雅各、约翰「在船上补网」（太4:21）。神修补我们，装备我们，叫我们能遵行他的旨意。
- **神先做工：** 作者全书劝了许多「当」，最后却是求神亲自在读者里面做工（参腓2:13）。有的抄本作你们，有的作我们，意思一样。我们能行神所喜悦的事，是藉着耶稣基督；所行的蒙悦纳，也是藉着他。
- **「愿荣耀归给他」：** 这个「他」可能指神，也可能指耶稣基督（参彼后3:18）；两种读法都合乎圣经，因为荣耀同归于父和子。

**3. 「劝勉的话」和问安。** 「弟兄们，我略略写信给你们，望你们听我劝勉的话。」（13:22）关于「劝勉的话」和「从意大利来的人」，见本研读的导论。新约别处没有记载提摩太坐监（13:23），我们不知道他在哪里被关。作者第三次提到「引导你们的诸位」（13:24），最后说：「愿恩惠常与你们众人同在。」（13:25）第9节说「人心靠恩得坚固」，全书就以恩惠结束。

> 要点：全书最后不是再一个命令，而是一个祝福：那叫大牧人从死里复活的神，要亲自在他的百姓里面做工，直到他们行他所喜悦的事。荣耀都归给他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「从前引导你们、传神之道给你们的人，你们要想念他们……耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的。」（13:7-8）读到希伯来书的最后一章，我想起自己头一次读这卷书的时候。那时我刚信主，在13:8下面画了一道线，觉得这句话说的是理所当然的事。这些年，许多东西都变了。讲台上换过几位传道人，有的被差到别处，有的走得并不愉快；会众里有人去了别的教会，有人不再来了，留下来的几个人，有时候也不知道该怎样彼此相看。变化最大的其实是我自己。起初的火热，后来变成了在教会里有位置、受人尊重的习惯；中间也有过一段日子，我连自己还信不信都说不清。回头看，我的信心一点也不像一条直线。可是第8节正好放在两种带领者中间：第7节是从前传道、如今已经走完了路的人，第17节是现在正在警醒的人。带领的人来了又去，他们所传的那一位却没有换人。我站得住，从来不是靠某一位牧师，也不是靠某一段时期的自己，而是靠这位昨日、今日、一直到永远都一样的耶稣基督。我也要承认，这些年我常常选择留在营里。同事拿信仰开玩笑，我不出声；教会里有让人为难的人，我宁可守住自己的位置和面子，也不愿意去接待他们。我怕被人看不起。可是耶稣是「在城门外受苦」的（13:12）。他被带到不洁净、被咒诅的人被带去的地方，在那里担当了我的罪，忍受了本该临到我的羞辱，「用自己的血」叫我成圣。所以，「出到营外，就了他去」，不是先要我变得勇敢，而是到他那里去；他已经在那里了，那地方因为有他，就不再只是羞辱的地方。如果你还没有信靠他，也不必先把自己变好；你可以承认自己的罪，悔改，信靠这位在城门外为罪人流血的耶稣。在这卷书的末了，作者没有再交给读者一项任务，而是为他们祝福。我常常以为，我的信心能不能走到底，要看我自己。这祝福却说，是那「赐平安的神」要「在各样善事上成全你们」，又「藉着耶稣基督在你们心里行他所喜悦的事」（13:20-21）。他已经叫群羊的大牧人从死里复活；那永约是用血立的，不会因为我这些年的忽冷忽热而作废。教会会变，带领的人会换，我自己也还会变，可是在我里面做工的，一直是同一位神，藉着同一位耶稣基督。读完这一整卷书，我只想用作者自己的话来结束：「愿荣耀归给他，直到永永远远。阿们！」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 13 is the book's closing exhortation and greeting. **A life of mutual love** (vv. 1–6): let brotherly love continue, show hospitality to strangers, remember those in prison, hold marriage in honor, keep free from love of money, for he has said, "I will never leave you nor forsake you." **Leaders and the unchanging Christ** (vv. 7–17): remember those who spoke the word of God, for "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever"; do not be led away by strange teachings; Jesus suffered outside the gate, so let us go to him outside the camp and offer up a sacrifice of praise to God; and obey your present leaders. **Prayer, blessing and greetings** (vv. 18–25): pray for us; may the God of peace equip you with everything good; news of Timothy; grace be with all of you.

This chapter is not a loose appendix. In 12:28 the author has just said, "let us offer to God acceptable worship"; this chapter shows what such worship looks like in life: doing good and sharing are sacrifices "pleasing to God" (13:16), and the final blessing asks God to work in us "that which is pleasing in his sight" (13:21). Many interpreters also notice that verses 7 and 17 both speak of "your leaders," forming a frame around the words about Christ and the altar in between.

## Going Deeper: 13:1–6 A Life of Mutual Love

**1. "Entertained angels unawares."** "Let brotherly love continue. Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for thereby some have entertained angels unawares" (13:1–2).
- **"Continue":** the readers had already served the saints, "as you still do" (6:10). The author is not telling them to begin loving but not to let love cool in their weariness.
- **Abraham:** when he saw three men, "he ran from the tent door to meet them" (Gen 18:2), and only later did it appear that one of them was the LORD (Gen 18:10); Lot did the same (Gen 19:1–2). In those days believers traveling to preach or fleeing persecution often depended on the hospitality of fellow Christians (3 John 5–8).
- **The real motive:** not the hope of meeting angels, but the Lord's word: "I was a stranger and you welcomed me" (Matt 25:35). Hospitality needs discernment too, especially to keep children safe; but fear of inconvenience is no reason to shut the door.

**2. "As though in prison with them."** "Remember those who are in prison, as though in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you also are in the body" (13:3). In the ancient world prisoners often depended on friends and family for food and clothing, and the readers had once "had compassion on those in prison" (10:34). Most interpreters take "you also are in the body" to mean that we too have bodies that can suffer, so we should feel their pain as our own. Today this includes praying for persecuted believers, and visiting those in prison and their families.

**3. "Let marriage be held in honor among all."** "Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the marriage bed be undefiled, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterous" (13:4).
- **Two errors:** some think abstinence more spiritual and look down on marriage (1 Tim 4:3); others think the body has nothing to do with faith and look down on the marriage bed. The author rejects both. "Sexually immoral" covers sexual relationships outside marriage; "adulterous" refers to breaking the marriage covenant. God's judgment is real.
- **Greater grace:** "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God" (1 Cor 6:11). Whoever has fallen here can come to Christ with their shame and confess, and he will forgive (1 John 1:9).
- **Not staying in danger:** the honor of marriage never means remaining in danger from violence or abuse. A victim may leave the danger and need not reconcile with someone unsafe; reporting to the police is right (Rom 13:4). The church must not tell victims to put up with it to keep "family shame" from getting out, or hide the abuser.

**4. "I will never leave you."** "Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have, for he has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you.' So we can confidently say, 'The Lord is my helper; I will not fear; what can man do to me?'" (13:5–6). The ground of contentment is not that a little less money does not matter, but that we have the Lord himself. This promise was spoken when Moses handed the burden of leadership to Joshua (Deut 31:6; Josh 1:5): the leader changed; the promise did not. Verse 6 quotes Psalm 118:6. The readers' property had once been plundered (10:34), but no one can take away the Lord's presence.

> The point: loving one another, welcoming strangers, remembering those who suffer, honoring marriage and keeping free from the love of money are not extra rules added on; they are the everyday shape of people who have received grace. Its foundation is the Lord's promise: he will never leave us.

## Going Deeper: 13:7–9 Yesterday and Today and Forever

**1. "Remember your leaders."** "Remember your leaders, those who spoke to you the word of God. Consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith" (13:7). These leaders seem to have died. Many interpreters (such as Bruce) take "the outcome of their way of life" to mean the way they finished their lives, perhaps including some who died as martyrs; others take it more generally as the fruit of their whole conduct. What the readers are to imitate is their "faith," that is, to follow the One they trusted, not to venerate people.

**2. "Jesus Christ is the same."** "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:8).
- **Set in between:** this verse stands between verses 7 and 9. Leaders die, but the One they preached has not been replaced; since Christ is the same, there is no need to chase novel teachings.
- **Back to chapter 1:** in 1:12 the author applied Psalm 102's words about God to the Son: "But you are the same." Here he uses the name Jesus bore on earth: the Jesus who suffered is the unchanging Lord (see Mal 3:6).
- **What has not changed:** Jesus took flesh and blood, suffered, rose and ascended, and his circumstances went through many changes; but who he is, his faithfulness, and the salvation he accomplished once for all have not changed. Yesterday he suffered outside the gate (13:12); today "he always lives to make intercession" (7:25); and forever he is the great shepherd of the eternal covenant (13:20).

**3. "Strengthened by grace."** "Do not be led away by diverse and strange teachings, for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods, which have not benefited those devoted to them" (13:9). Interpreters offer several views of what these foods are: Jewish food laws; meals connected with temple sacrifice or festivals, eaten in the belief that they brought spiritual benefit (such as Bruce and Lane); or ascetic rules (see Col 2:16). On any view, the author has already called such things "regulations for the body" (9:10). Today too some say vegetarian fasting earns merit, or that a certain diet or ritual makes a person more spiritual; the author's answer is the same: what strengthens the heart is grace, for "the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking" (Rom 14:17).

> The point: leaders will go, and the church's circumstances will change, but the Jesus Christ we trust is the same yesterday and today and forever. What strengthens the heart is his grace.

## Going Deeper: 13:10–16 Go to Him Outside the Camp

**1. "We have an altar."** "We have an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat" (13:10).
- **Several views:** many interpreters (such as Bruce, Lane and Cockerill) take it to be Christ's sacrifice on the cross itself, and to "eat" is to receive by faith all that this sacrifice brings. Some, especially interpreters within the Roman Catholic tradition, take it to be the altar of the Eucharist. The Protestant tradition holds that the Lord's Table is not an altar on which a propitiatory sacrifice is offered again (see the discussion of the Mass in chapter 7); yet at the Supper we truly have "a participation in the blood of Christ" and "a participation in the body of Christ" (1 Cor 10:16), receiving the sacrifice already completed.
- **"No right to eat":** under the law, if a sin offering's blood was brought into the Holy Place, it was not to be eaten; "it shall be burned up with fire" (Lev 6:30). The priests of the first covenant could not eat a bite of the greatest sacrifice of the Day of Atonement; but all who trust Christ may receive this true sin offering. Those who still rely on the tent have, of all people, no share in it.

**2. "Suffered outside the gate."** "For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the holy places by the high priest as a sacrifice for sin are burned outside the camp. So Jesus also suffered outside the gate in order to sanctify the people through his own blood" (13:11–12).
- **The shadow of the Day of Atonement:** the bull and goat of the sin offering "shall be carried outside the camp. Their skin and their flesh and their dung shall be burned up with fire" (Lev 16:27). Jesus "went out, bearing his own cross," and the place where he was crucified "was near the city" (John 19:17, 20), outside Jerusalem.
- **The place of the curse:** outside the camp was where the unclean were sent and the one under a curse was put to death (Lev 24:14). There Jesus became "a curse for us" (Gal 3:13), bearing in our place the penalty of sin; so his blood sanctifies the people, once for all (10:10, 14).

**3. "Let us go to him outside the camp."** "Therefore let us go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured. For here we have no lasting city, but we seek the city that is to come" (13:13–14).
- **Leaving what?** Many interpreters (such as Bruce) think the author first of all calls Jewish believers to leave the system centered on temple sacrifice, even if their own people count them apostates; Calvin and others read it more broadly, as leaving the security and honor the world offers and gladly following the rejected Christ. The two readings do not conflict. "Reproach" is the same word as Moses' "reproach of Christ" in 11:26, and as the readers' being "exposed to reproach" in 10:33.
- **Outside the camp is also where God is met:** Moses once pitched the tent outside the camp, "and everyone who sought the LORD would go out to the tent of meeting, which was outside the camp" (Exod 33:7). Going outside the camp means going to him, because he is already there. No earthly city, Jerusalem included, is our final home (11:10, 16).

**4. "A sacrifice of praise."** "Through him then let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that acknowledge his name. Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God" (13:15–16). The atoning sacrifice is complete; "there is no longer any offering for sin" (10:18). Calvin distinguished sacrifices of propitiation from sacrifices of thanksgiving: the first only Christ could offer, and he has offered it; the second is what people who have received grace offer to God all their lives. Even our praise is acceptable only "through him" (1 Pet 2:5). This thankful sacrifice has two sides: praise toward God on the lips, and doing good and sharing with people in the hands.

> The point: outside the gate Jesus bore the curse in our place and sanctified us through his own blood. So we go outside the camp not to earn anything but to go to him; we have no other sacrifice to offer than thankful praise and acts of love.

## Going Deeper: 13:17–19 Obey Your Leaders

**1. "As those who will have to give an account."** "Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls, as those who will have to give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no advantage to you" (13:17).
- **"Obey":** many take this word to mean being persuaded and following, not blind obedience. "Keeping watch" originally means going without sleep, like a shepherd guarding the flock at night.
- **Leaders give an account:** God says, "Behold, I am against the shepherds, and I will require my sheep at their hand" (Ezek 34:10); elders are "to care for the church of God, which he obtained with his own blood" (Acts 20:28), "not domineering over those in your charge" (1 Pet 5:3).

**2. This is never a license for spiritual abuse.** Submission to leaders is submission under God's word; if a leader demands what Scripture forbids, "we must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29). If a leader uses spiritual authority to control, sexually assault or abuse people, that is not the submission this verse speaks of. Reporting to the police is right (Rom 13:4); victims need not stay in danger or reconcile with someone unsafe; even if he confesses, that does not restore his office or give him access again to victims or children. The church must not hide it to keep "family shame" from getting out.

**3. "Pray for us."** "Pray for us, for we are sure that we have a clear conscience, desiring to act honorably in all things. I urge you the more earnestly to do this in order that I may be restored to you the sooner" (13:18–19). The author does not stand above the congregation; he too needs their prayers. Many interpreters think his "clear conscience" may answer criticism of him; what was keeping him from returning, we do not know.

> The point: God gives leaders to the church to keep watch over the flock, and they themselves must give account to the great Shepherd. We honor and follow faithful leaders and pray for them; but there is only one true Shepherd, and no one may use his name to harm his sheep.

## Going Deeper: 13:20–25 The God of Peace

**1. "Brought again from the dead... the great shepherd of the sheep."** "Now may the God of peace who brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep, by the blood of the eternal covenant" (13:20).
- **The resurrection named:** Hebrews often speaks of Jesus seated at God's right hand; many note that this is the only place in the book that explicitly names his resurrection.
- **The great shepherd:** many take this to echo Isaiah 63:11: "Where is he who brought them up out of the sea with the shepherds of his flock?" The shepherd there is Moses. Jesus is the great shepherd God brought up from the dead, the good shepherd who "lays down his life for the sheep" (John 10:11). All earthly leaders stand under him.
- **"The blood of the eternal covenant":** the prophets promised that God would make "an everlasting covenant" (Isa 55:3; Jer 32:40), and spoke of setting prisoners free "because of the blood of my covenant with you" (Zech 9:11). The new covenant of chapter 8 is this eternal covenant; it was made in the blood of Jesus, and so it will never pass away.

**2. "Working in us that which is pleasing in his sight."** "Equip you with everything good that you may do his will, working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen" (13:21).
- **"Equip":** the same word is used of James and John "mending their nets" (Matt 4:21). God mends us and equips us so that we can do his will.
- **God works first:** all through the book the author has given many "let us" exhortations; at the end he asks God himself to work within his readers (see Phil 2:13). Some manuscripts read "you," others "us"; the sense is the same. It is through Jesus Christ that we can do what pleases God, and through him that what we do is accepted.
- **"To whom be glory":** "whom" may refer to God or to Jesus Christ (see 2 Pet 3:18); both readings fit Scripture, since glory belongs to the Father and the Son alike.

**3. The word of exhortation and greetings.** "I appeal to you, brothers, bear with my word of exhortation, for I have written to you briefly" (13:22). On the "word of exhortation" and "those who come from Italy," see the introduction to this study. Nowhere else does the New Testament record Timothy's imprisonment (13:23), and we do not know where he was held. The author mentions "your leaders" a third time (13:24), and ends: "Grace be with all of you" (13:25). Verse 9 said that "it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace," and the whole book ends in grace.

> The point: the book's last word is not another command but a blessing: the God who brought the great Shepherd back from the dead will himself work within his people until they do what is pleasing in his sight. All glory belongs to him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Remember your leaders, those who spoke to you the word of God... Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:7–8). Reaching the last chapter of Hebrews, I remember the first time I read this book. I had just come to faith; I underlined 13:8 and thought it said something obvious. Over the years since, so much has changed. Several pastors have come and gone from our pulpit, some sent elsewhere, some leaving on unhappy terms; some in the congregation went to other churches, some stopped coming, and those of us who stayed have not always known how to look at one another. The biggest change, though, has been in me. My first zeal turned into the habit of having a place in the church and being respected; there was also a stretch when I could not say whether I still believed at all. Looking back, my faith is nothing like a straight line. Yet verse 8 stands exactly between two kinds of leaders: verse 7, those who preached and have now finished their course; verse 17, those keeping watch now. Leaders come and go, but the One they preached has not been replaced. I have never stood because of a particular pastor, or because of myself in some particular season; I stand because of this Jesus Christ, who is the same yesterday and today and forever. I must also confess that over these years I have often chosen to stay inside the camp. When coworkers joked about faith, I said nothing; when there were difficult people at church, I would rather guard my place and my face than welcome them. I was afraid of being looked down on. But Jesus "suffered outside the gate" (13:12). He was taken to the place where the unclean and the cursed were taken, and there he bore my sin and the shame that should have fallen on me, sanctifying me "through his own blood." So "go to him outside the camp" does not first require me to become brave; it means going to him. He is already there, and because he is there, it is no longer only a place of shame. If you have not yet trusted him, you need not make yourself better first; you can confess your sin, repent, and trust this Jesus who shed his blood for sinners outside the gate. At the end of the book the author gives his readers no further task; he blesses them. I have often thought that whether my faith makes it to the end depends on me. But this blessing says that "the God of peace" will "equip you with everything good," "working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ" (13:20–21). He has already brought the great shepherd of the sheep again from the dead; the eternal covenant was made in blood, and my years of running hot and cold do not cancel it. The church will change, leaders will change, and I will still change; but the One at work in me has always been the same God, through the same Jesus Christ. Having read the whole book, I want only to end with the author's own words: "to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen."
