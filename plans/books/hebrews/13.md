## 本章脉络

第十三章是全书最后的劝勉和问安，大致可以分成四段。**彼此相爱的生活**（1-6节）：常存弟兄相爱的心，接待客旅，记念被捆绑和遭苦害的人，尊重婚姻，不贪爱钱财，因为主曾说「我总不撇下你，也不丢弃你」。**带领者和不改变的基督**（7-17节）：要想念从前传神之道的人，效法他们的信心，因为「耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的」；不要被怪异的教训勾引了去；我们有一祭坛，耶稣在城门外受苦，我们也当出到营外就了他去，又以颂赞为祭献给神；也要依从现在引导你们的人。**代祷和祝福**（18-21节）：请为我们祷告；愿赐平安的神在各样善事上成全你们。**结语**（22-25节）：劝勉的话，提摩太的消息，问安，愿恩惠与众人同在。

有人觉得第十三章像是后来附上去的一串零碎劝勉。其实它和全书紧紧连在一起。12:28刚说要「照神所喜悦的」事奉神，这一章就讲这样的事奉在日常生活中是什么样子：行善和捐输是「神所喜悦的」（13:16），最后的祝福又求神行「他所喜悦的事」（13:21）。全书讲过的祭坛、血、营外、大祭司和永约，在这里都收拢起来。许多解经家也注意到，第7节和第17节都讲到「引导你们的」人，像一个框子，把中间关于基督和祭坛的话围在里面。

## 深读 13:1-6 彼此相爱的生活

**1. 「常存弟兄相爱的心」。** 「你们务要常存弟兄相爱的心。」（13:1）
- **不是从零开始：** 读者从前就有这样的爱心，他们「先前伺候圣徒，如今还是伺候」（6:10），又「陪伴那些受这样苦难的人」（10:33）。作者没有叫他们开始去爱，而是叫他们「常存」，不要让爱心在疲倦中冷下来。
- **爱的根源：** 他们彼此是弟兄，因为那使人成圣的耶稣「称他们为弟兄也不以为耻」（2:11）。

**2. 「不知不觉就接待了天使」。** 「不可忘记用爱心接待客旅；因为曾有接待客旅的，不知不觉就接待了天使。」（13:2）
- **亚伯拉罕和罗得：** 亚伯拉罕看见三个人站在对面，「就从帐棚门口跑去迎接他们」（创18:2）；后来才知道，其中一位就是耶和华自己（创18:10）。罗得在所多玛城门口也请两位天使到家里住（创19:1-2）。
- **当时的需要：** 出门在外的传道人和受逼迫的信徒，常常要靠弟兄姊妹接待（约叁5-8；彼前4:9）。监督也必须「乐意接待远人」（提前3:2）。
- **真正的动机：** 作者不是叫我们为了碰见天使才接待人。主说：「我作客旅，你们留我住」，又说「这些事你们既做在我这弟兄中一个最小的身上，就是做在我身上了」（太25:35、40）。接待人的时候也要有分辨，特别要保护孩子的安全；但不可因为怕麻烦，就关上了门。

**3. 「好像与他们同受捆绑」。** 「你们要记念被捆绑的人，好像与他们同受捆绑；也要记念遭苦害的人，想到自己也在肉身之内。」（13:3）
- **记念就是去看：** 古时坐监的人，常常要靠亲友送饭送衣。读者从前就「体恤了那些被捆锁的人」（10:34），也因此受了牵连。
- **「也在肉身之内」：** 多数解经家认为，这是说我们自己也有会受苦的身体，同样可能落在那样的处境里；所以要把他们的痛苦当作自己的。今天，这包括为各地受逼迫的信徒祷告，也包括探访监狱里的人和他们的家人。

**4. 「婚姻，人人都当尊重」。** 「婚姻，人人都当尊重，床也不可污秽；因为苟合行淫的人，神必要审判。」（13:4）
- **两面的错误：** 有人轻看婚姻，以为禁欲更属灵（提前4:3）；有人轻看婚床，以为身体的事与信仰无关。作者两样都拒绝：婚姻是好的，人人都当尊重；婚姻里的忠诚也必须守住，「神配合的，人不可分开」（太19:6）。
- **清楚的警告：** 「苟合」泛指婚姻以外的各种性关系，「行淫」特指破坏婚约的奸淫。神的审判是真的，这里没有灰色地带。
- **更大的恩典：** 保罗写给哥林多教会时说：「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今你们奉主耶稣基督的名，并藉着我们神的灵，已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」（林前6:11）在这方面跌倒的人，可以带着羞愧来到基督面前，「我们若认自己的罪，神是信实的，是公义的，必要赦免我们的罪」（约一1:9）。
- **尊重婚姻不等于留在危险中：** 婚姻的尊荣，绝不是要人留在暴力或虐待的危险里。受害的人可以离开危险，不必和一个不安全的人和好；向警方举报是对的（罗13:4）。教会不可为了「家丑不可外扬」叫受害者忍着，也不可替施暴的人隐瞒。

**5. 「我总不撇下你，也不丢弃你」。** 「你们存心不可贪爱钱财，要以自己所有的为足；因为主曾说：『我总不撇下你，也不丢弃你。』所以我们可以放胆说：主是帮助我的，我必不惧怕；人能把我怎么样呢？」（13:5-6）
- **知足的根据：** 作者没有说钱是坏的，而是说：你所有的最宝贵的，不是钱，是主自己。这应许在旧约圣经里出现过好几次，摩西把带领的担子交给约书亚的时候，神就这样应许（申31:6；书1:5）。带领的人换了，应许没有换。
- **「人能把我怎么样呢」：** 这是诗篇118:6的话。读者曾经「家业被人抢去」（10:34），作者提醒他们：人能拿走的，拿不走主的同在。

> 要点：彼此相爱、接待客旅、记念受苦的人、尊重婚姻、不贪爱钱财，这些不是另外加上的规条，而是蒙了恩的人在日常生活里的样子。它的根基是主的应许：他总不撇下我们，也不丢弃我们。

## 深读 13:7-9 昨日、今日、一直到永远

**1. 「要想念他们」。** 「从前引导你们、传神之道给你们的人，你们要想念他们，效法他们的信心，留心看他们为人的结局。」（13:7）
- **从前的带领者：** 这些人「传神之道」给读者，现在似乎已经离世了。
- **「为人的结局」：** 许多解经家（如布鲁斯）认为，这是指他们怎样走完一生，其中也许有人为主殉道；也有人认为泛指他们一生行事的结果。无论哪一种，他们都像第十一章那些见证人，「存着信心死」了。
- **效法信心，不是崇拜人：** 作者叫读者效法的，是他们的「信心」，也就是他们所信靠的那一位。

**2. 「耶稣基督……是一样的」。** 「耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的。」（13:8）
- **夹在中间的一句：** 这一节放在第7节和第9节中间。带领的人会离世，他们所传的那一位却没有换人；既然基督是一样的，读者就不必去追求新奇的教训。
- **回到第一章：** 作者在1:12把诗篇102篇讲神的话用在儿子身上：「惟有你永不改变；你的年数没有穷尽。」这里他用了耶稣在地上的名字：这位曾经受苦的耶稣，就是那位永不改变的主。神永不改变的属性（玛3:6），也属于子。
- **什么没有改变：** 耶稣「成了血肉之体」（2:14），受苦，复活，升天，他所经历的境况有许多变化；但他是谁，他的信实，他一次成就的救恩，都没有改变。昨日，他在城门外受苦（13:12）；今日，他「长远活着，替他们祈求」（7:25）；一直到永远，他是永约的大牧人（13:20）。

**3. 「不要被那诸般怪异的教训勾引了去」。** 「你们不要被那诸般怪异的教训勾引了去；因为人心靠恩得坚固才是好的，并不是靠饮食。那在饮食上专心的从来没有得着益处。」（13:9）
- **哪些饮食：** 我们不确定作者指的是什么，解经家有几种看法：一是犹太人的洁净饮食条例；二是和圣殿献祭或犹太节期相连的筵席，以为吃了就能得到属灵的益处（许多解经家，如布鲁斯、雷恩）；三是带有禁欲色彩的饮食规条（参西2:16）。无论哪一种，作者在9:10已经说过，这些都是「属肉体的条例」。
- **靠恩，不靠饮食：** 心要坚固，不是靠吃什么、不吃什么，而是靠神的恩典。耶稣已经「叫各样的食物都是洁净的」（参可7:19）；「神的国不在乎吃喝」（罗14:17）。今天我们也会遇到类似的事：有人说吃素积功德，有人说某种饮食或某种仪式能叫人更属灵。作者的回答还是一样：坚固人心的是恩典。

> 要点：带领的人会离去，教会的情形也会改变，但我们所信的耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远都是一样的。坚固人心的，不是新奇的教训或外面的规矩，而是他的恩典。

## 深读 13:10-16 出到营外，就了他去

**1. 「我们有一祭坛」。** 「我们有一祭坛，上面的祭物是那些在帐幕中供职的人不可同吃的。」（13:10）
- **这祭坛指什么：** 历代有几种看法。许多解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩、柯克里尔）认为，这是指基督在十字架上的献祭本身；「吃」是用信心领受这祭所带来的一切。有些解经家，特别是罗马天主教传统中的一些人，认为是指圣餐的祭坛。也有人认为是指天上的祭坛。更正教的传统认为，圣餐桌不是再献挽回祭的祭坛（参第七章关于弥撒的讨论）；但我们在圣餐中确实是「同领基督的血」「同领基督的身体」（林前10:16），领受那已经成全的祭。
- **「不可同吃」：** 按律法，赎罪祭的血若带进圣所，「那肉都不可吃，必用火焚烧」（利6:30）。前约的祭司在赎罪日最大的那个祭上，一口也不能吃。基督是真正的赎罪祭，凡信靠他的人却可以领受他；仍旧依靠帐幕和献祭的人，在这祭上反倒无分。

**2. 「在城门外受苦」。** 「原来牲畜的血被大祭司带入圣所作赎罪祭；牲畜的身子被烧在营外。所以，耶稣要用自己的血叫百姓成圣，也就在城门外受苦。」（13:11-12）
- **赎罪日的影儿：** 利未记说：「作赎罪祭的公牛和公山羊的血既带入圣所赎罪，这牛羊就要搬到营外，将皮、肉、粪用火焚烧。」（利16:27）
- **各各他：** 耶稣「背着自己的十字架出来」，钉十字架的地方「与城相近」（约19:17、20），是在耶路撒冷城外。
- **营外是什么地方：** 在旷野，营外是不洁净的人被送去的地方，也是被咒诅的人被处死的地方（利24:14）。耶稣在那里被当作被咒诅的，「基督既为我们受了咒诅，就赎出我们脱离律法的咒诅」（加3:13）。他在那里替我们担当了罪的刑罚，所以他的血能叫百姓成圣，而且只一次就成全了（10:10、14）。

**3. 「出到营外，就了他去」。** 「这样，我们也当出到营外，就了他去，忍受他所受的凌辱。我们在这里本没有常存的城，乃是寻求那将来的城。」（13:13-14）
- **离开什么：** 许多解经家（如布鲁斯）认为，作者首先是叫犹太信徒离开以圣殿和献祭为中心的体系，宁可被同胞看作背道的人。加尔文等人把这话讲得更宽：离开世界所给的安全和尊荣，甘心跟随被弃绝的基督。这两种理解并不冲突。
- **「他所受的凌辱」：** 这里的「凌辱」，和11:26摩西「看为基督受的凌辱比埃及的财物更宝贵」是同一个词，10:33的「毁谤」也是这个词。读者从前就是这样走过来的。
- **营外也是遇见神的地方：** 摩西曾经把会幕支搭在营外，「凡求问耶和华的，就到营外的会幕那里去」（出33:7）。我们出到营外，不是去一个空荡荡的地方，而是到他那里去，因为他已经在那里了。
- **「没有常存的城」：** 亚伯拉罕「等候那座有根基的城」（11:10），我们已经「来到锡安山，永生神的城邑」（12:22）。地上的城，连耶路撒冷在内，都不是我们最后的家。

**4. 「以颂赞为祭献给神」。** 「我们应当靠着耶稣，常常以颂赞为祭献给神，这就是那承认主名之人嘴唇的果子。只是不可忘记行善和捐输的事，因为这样的祭是神所喜悦的。」（13:15-16）
- **不再是赎罪的祭：** 赎罪的祭已经成全了，「就不用再为罪献祭了」（10:18）。加尔文在《基督教要义》讨论圣餐时，区分了挽回的祭和感恩的祭：前一种只有基督能献，而且已经献了；后一种是蒙了恩的人一生献给神的。
- **「靠着耶稣」：** 连我们的赞美，也是藉着他才蒙神悦纳（彼前2:5）。「嘴唇的果子」，许多人认为是回响何西阿书14:2（七十士译本）。
- **两样的祭：** 一样是向神的颂赞，一样是向人的行善和捐输。真正的敬拜，既在嘴唇上，也在手上。

> 要点：耶稣在城门外受苦，替我们担当了咒诅，用自己的血叫我们成圣。所以我们出到营外，不是为了赚得什么，而是到他那里去；我们没有别的祭要献，只献上感恩的颂赞和爱人的行动。

## 深读 13:17-19 依从引导你们的

**1. 「要依从……且要顺服」。** 「你们要依从那些引导你们的，且要顺服；因他们为你们的灵魂时刻警醒，好像那将来交帐的人。你们要使他们交的时候有快乐，不致忧愁；若忧愁就与你们无益了。」（13:17）
- **「依从」：** 许多人认为，这个词的意思是被说服而听从，不是盲目服从。
- **为什么要顺服：** 因为他们「为你们的灵魂时刻警醒」。「警醒」这个词原指不睡觉，像牧人夜里看守羊群。
- **带领者要交帐：** 带领的人自己要向神交帐。神曾经说：「我必与牧人为敌，必向他们的手追讨我的羊」（结34:10）；使徒也嘱咐长老「牧养神的教会，就是他用自己血所买来的」（徒20:28），并且「不是辖制所托付你们的」（彼前5:3）。

**2. 这绝不是属灵虐待的许可。**
- **在神的话以下：** 顺服带领者，是在神的话以下的顺服。带领者若要人做违背圣经的事，就当「顺从神，不顺从人」（徒5:29）。主说外邦人的君王操权管束人，「只是在你们中间，不可这样」（太20:26）。
- **虐待不可遮掩：** 带领者若利用属灵的权柄控制人、性侵或虐待人，这就不是本节所说的顺服。向警方举报是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必和不安全的人和好；即使他认罪，也不等于恢复他的职分，或让他再接近受害者和孩子。教会不可因为「家丑不可外扬」替他隐瞒。

**3. 「请你们为我们祷告」。** 「请你们为我们祷告，因我们自觉良心无亏，愿意凡事按正道而行。我更求你们为我祷告，使我快些回到你们那里去。」（13:18-19）
- **带领者也需要代祷：** 写这卷书的人没有站在会众之上，他请他们为他祷告。
- **「良心无亏」：** 许多解经家认为，也许有人在议论他，所以他这样说。至于是什么拦阻他回去，我们并不知道。

> 要点：神把带领者赐给教会，叫他们为羊群警醒，而他们自己也要向大牧人交帐。我们尊重、代祷、跟随忠心的带领者，但真正的牧人只有一位，任何人都不可借他的名伤害他的羊。

## 深读 13:20-25 赐平安的神

**1. 「从死里复活的神」。** 「但愿赐平安的神，就是那凭永约之血、使群羊的大牧人－我主耶稣从死里复活的神」（13:20）
- **「赐平安的神」：** 读者中间未必太平，作者刚刚劝他们「追求与众人和睦」（12:14）。但平安的源头在神。
- **明说复活：** 希伯来书常讲耶稣升天，坐在神的右边；许多人注意到，这是全书唯一明说耶稣复活的一处。
- **「群羊的大牧人」：** 许多人认为，这里回响以赛亚书63:11：神「将百姓和牧养他全群的人从海里领上来」，那牧人就是摩西。摩西是神从红海领上来的牧人；耶稣是神从死里领上来的大牧人。他是「为羊舍命」的好牧人（约10:11），也是我们「灵魂的牧人监督」（彼前2:25）。地上的带领者都在他以下。
- **「凭永约之血」：** 撒迦利亚书说：「我因与你立约的血，将你中间被掳而囚的人从无水的坑中释放出来。」（亚9:11）先知也应许神要立「永约」（赛55:3；耶32:40；结37:26）。第八章所讲的新约，就是这永约；它是用耶稣的血立的，所以永不过去。

**2. 「在你们心里行他所喜悦的事」。** 「在各样善事上成全你们，叫你们遵行他的旨意；又藉着耶稣基督在你们心里行他所喜悦的事。愿荣耀归给他，直到永永远远。阿们！」（13:21）
- **「成全」：** 这个词也用在雅各、约翰「在船上补网」（太4:21）。神修补我们，装备我们，叫我们能遵行他的旨意。
- **神先做工：** 作者全书劝了许多「当」，最后却是为读者祷告，求神亲自在他们里面做工。这和保罗说的一样：「你们立志行事都是神在你们心里运行，为要成就他的美意。」（腓2:13）有的抄本作「你们」，有的作「我们」，意思都一样：神在他的百姓里面做工。
- **「藉着耶稣基督」：** 我们能行神所喜悦的事，是藉着耶稣基督；我们所行的蒙神悦纳，也是藉着他。
- **「愿荣耀归给他」：** 这个「他」可能指神，也可能指耶稣基督（参彼后3:18）。两种读法都合乎圣经，因为荣耀同归于父和子。

**3. 「劝勉的话」。** 「弟兄们，我略略写信给你们，望你们听我劝勉的话。你们该知道，我们的兄弟提摩太已经释放了；他若快来，我必同他去见你们。」（13:22-23）
- **一篇讲章：** 关于「劝勉的话」和这卷书的体裁，见本研读的导论。作者说他写得「略略」，这一卷是要人用心听的。
- **提摩太：** 新约别处没有记载提摩太坐监，我们不知道他在哪里、什么时候被关。作者盼望和他一同去见读者。

**4. 「愿恩惠常与你们众人同在」。** 「请你们问引导你们的诸位和众圣徒安。从意大利来的人也问你们安。愿恩惠常与你们众人同在。阿们！」（13:24-25）
- **第三次提到带领者：** 作者再一次提到「引导你们的诸位」，叫读者彼此问安。关于「从意大利来的人」，见导论。
- **以恩惠结束：** 全书从神藉着他儿子说话开始，到恩惠结束。第9节说「人心靠恩得坚固」，最后作者就把恩惠留给他们众人。

> 要点：全书最后的话，不是再一个命令，而是一个祝福：那叫大牧人从死里复活的神，要亲自在他的百姓里面做工，直到他们行他所喜悦的事。荣耀都归给他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「从前引导你们、传神之道给你们的人，你们要想念他们……耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的。」（13:7-8）读到希伯来书的最后一章，我想起自己头一次读这卷书的时候。那时我刚信主，在13:8下面画了一道线，觉得这句话说的是理所当然的事。这些年，许多东西都变了。讲台上换过几位传道人，有的被差到别处，有的走得并不愉快；会众里有人去了别的教会，有人不再来了，留下来的几个人，有时候也不知道该怎样彼此相看。变化最大的其实是我自己。起初的火热，后来变成了在教会里有位置、受人尊重的习惯；中间也有过一段日子，我连自己还信不信都说不清。回头看，我的信心一点也不像一条直线。可是第8节正好放在两种带领者中间：第7节是从前传道、如今已经走完了路的人，第17节是现在正在警醒的人。带领的人来了又去，他们所传的那一位却没有换人。我站得住，从来不是靠某一位牧师，也不是靠某一段时期的自己，而是靠这位昨日、今日、一直到永远都一样的耶稣基督。我也要承认，这些年我常常选择留在「营」里。同事拿信仰开玩笑，我不出声；教会里有让人为难的人，我宁可守住自己的位置和面子，也不愿意去接待他们。我怕被人看不起。可是耶稣是「在城门外受苦」的（13:12）。他被带到不洁净、被咒诅的人被带去的地方，在那里担当了我的罪，忍受了本该临到我的羞辱，「用自己的血」叫我成圣。所以，「出到营外，就了他去」，不是先要我变得勇敢，而是到他那里去；他已经在那里了，那地方因为有他，就不再只是羞辱的地方。如果你还没有信靠他，也不必先把自己变好；你可以承认自己的罪，悔改，信靠这位在城门外为罪人流血的耶稣。在这卷书的末了，作者没有再交给读者一项任务，而是为他们祝福。我常常以为，我的信心能不能走到底，要看我自己。这祝福却说，是那「赐平安的神」要「在各样善事上成全你们」，又「藉着耶稣基督在你们心里行他所喜悦的事」（13:20-21）。他已经叫群羊的大牧人从死里复活；那永约是用血立的，不会因为我这些年的忽冷忽热而作废。教会会变，带领的人会换，我自己也还会变，可是在我里面做工的，一直是同一位神，藉着同一位耶稣基督。读完这一整卷书，我只想用作者自己的话来结束：「愿荣耀归给他，直到永永远远。阿们！」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 13 is the book's closing exhortation and greeting, and falls roughly into four parts. **A life of mutual love** (vv. 1–6): let brotherly love continue, show hospitality to strangers, remember those in prison and those mistreated, hold marriage in honor, keep free from love of money, for he has said, "I will never leave you nor forsake you." **Leaders and the unchanging Christ** (vv. 7–17): remember those who spoke the word of God and imitate their faith, for "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever"; do not be led away by strange teachings; we have an altar, Jesus suffered outside the gate, so let us go to him outside the camp and offer up a sacrifice of praise to God; and obey your present leaders. **Prayer and blessing** (vv. 18–21): pray for us; may the God of peace equip you with everything good. **Closing** (vv. 22–25): the word of exhortation, news of Timothy, greetings, and grace to all.

Some feel that chapter 13 reads like a string of loose exhortations added on afterward. In fact it is tightly bound to the whole book. In 12:28 the author has just said, "let us offer to God acceptable worship"; this chapter shows what such worship looks like in daily life: doing good and sharing are sacrifices "pleasing to God" (13:16), and the final blessing asks God to work in us "that which is pleasing in his sight" (13:21). The altar, the blood, the camp, the high priest and the eternal covenant all come together here. Many interpreters also notice that verses 7 and 17 both speak of "your leaders," forming a frame around the words about Christ and the altar in between.

## Going Deeper: 13:1–6 A Life of Mutual Love

**1. "Let brotherly love continue."** "Let brotherly love continue" (13:1).
- **Not starting from nothing:** the readers already had this love; they had served the saints, "as you still do" (6:10), and had been "partners with those so treated" (10:33). The author does not tell them to begin loving but to let love "continue," not letting it cool in their weariness.
- **The root of this love:** they are brothers because Jesus, who sanctifies them, "is not ashamed to call them brothers" (2:11).

**2. "Entertained angels unawares."** "Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for thereby some have entertained angels unawares" (13:2).
- **Abraham and Lot:** when Abraham saw three men standing before him, "he ran from the tent door to meet them" (Gen 18:2); only later did it appear that one of them was the LORD himself (Gen 18:10). Lot, at the gate of Sodom, also invited two angels into his house (Gen 19:1–2).
- **The need of the day:** traveling preachers and persecuted believers often depended on the hospitality of fellow Christians (3 John 5–8; 1 Pet 4:9). An overseer, too, must be "hospitable" (1 Tim 3:2).
- **The real motive:** the author is not telling us to welcome people in the hope of meeting angels. The Lord says, "I was a stranger and you welcomed me," and, "as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me" (Matt 25:35, 40). Hospitality also needs discernment, especially to keep children safe; but fear of inconvenience is no reason to shut the door.

**3. "As though in prison with them."** "Remember those who are in prison, as though in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you also are in the body" (13:3).
- **Remembering means going:** in the ancient world prisoners often depended on friends and family for food and clothing. The readers had once "had compassion on those in prison" (10:34), and had suffered for it.
- **"You also are in the body":** most interpreters take this to mean that we too have bodies that can suffer and could end up in the same place, so we should feel their pain as our own. Today this includes praying for persecuted believers around the world, and visiting those in prison and their families.

**4. "Let marriage be held in honor among all."** "Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the marriage bed be undefiled, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterous" (13:4).
- **Two errors:** some look down on marriage, thinking abstinence more spiritual (1 Tim 4:3); others look down on the marriage bed, thinking the body has nothing to do with faith. The author rejects both: marriage is good and should be honored by all; and faithfulness within it must be kept, for "what therefore God has joined together, let not man separate" (Matt 19:6).
- **A clear warning:** "sexually immoral" covers every kind of sexual relationship outside marriage; "adulterous" refers specifically to breaking the marriage covenant. God's judgment is real; there is no gray zone here.
- **Greater grace:** Paul wrote to the Corinthians, "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God" (1 Cor 6:11). Whoever has fallen here can come to Christ with their shame: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins" (1 John 1:9).
- **Honoring marriage does not mean staying in danger:** the honor of marriage never means remaining in danger from violence or abuse. A victim may leave the danger and need not reconcile with someone unsafe; reporting to the police is right (Rom 13:4). The church must not tell victims to put up with it in order to keep "family shame" from getting out, nor hide the abuser.

**5. "I will never leave you nor forsake you."** "Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have, for he has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you.' So we can confidently say, 'The Lord is my helper; I will not fear; what can man do to me?'" (13:5–6).
- **The ground of contentment:** the author does not say money is evil; he says the most precious thing you have is not money but the Lord himself. This promise appears several times in the Old Testament Scriptures; God gave it when Moses was handing the burden of leadership to Joshua (Deut 31:6; Josh 1:5). The leader changed; the promise did not.
- **"What can man do to me?":** these are the words of Psalm 118:6. The readers had once suffered "the plundering of your property" (10:34); the author reminds them that what people can take away does not include the Lord's presence.

> The point: loving one another, welcoming strangers, remembering those who suffer, honoring marriage and keeping free from the love of money are not extra rules added on; they are what life looks like day by day for people who have received grace. Its foundation is the Lord's promise: he will never leave us nor forsake us.

## Going Deeper: 13:7–9 Yesterday and Today and Forever

**1. "Remember your leaders."** "Remember your leaders, those who spoke to you the word of God. Consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith" (13:7).
- **Former leaders:** these people "spoke to you the word of God," and it seems they have now died.
- **"The outcome of their way of life":** many interpreters (such as Bruce) take this to mean the way they finished their lives, perhaps including some who died as martyrs; others take it more generally as the fruit of their whole conduct. Either way, like the witnesses of chapter 11, they "died in faith."
- **Imitating faith, not venerating people:** what the readers are to imitate is their "faith," that is, the One they trusted.

**2. "Jesus Christ is the same."** "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:8).
- **A verse set in between:** this verse stands between verses 7 and 9. Leaders die, but the One they preached has not been replaced; since Christ is the same, the readers need not chase novel teachings.
- **Back to chapter 1:** in 1:12 the author applied Psalm 102's words about God to the Son: "But you are the same, and your years will have no end." Here he uses the name Jesus bore on earth: the Jesus who suffered is the unchanging Lord. God's unchangeableness (Mal 3:6) belongs to the Son as well.
- **What has not changed:** Jesus "partook of the same things," flesh and blood (2:14); he suffered, rose and ascended, and so his circumstances went through many changes. But who he is, his faithfulness, and the salvation he accomplished once for all have not changed. Yesterday he suffered outside the gate (13:12); today "he always lives to make intercession" (7:25); and forever he is the great shepherd of the eternal covenant (13:20).

**3. "Do not be led away by diverse and strange teachings."** "Do not be led away by diverse and strange teachings, for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods, which have not benefited those devoted to them" (13:9).
- **Which foods?** We are not sure what the author means, and interpreters offer several views: Jewish food laws; meals connected with temple sacrifice or Jewish festivals, eaten in the belief that they brought spiritual benefit (many interpreters, such as Bruce and Lane); or ascetic food rules (see Col 2:16). On any view, the author has already said in 9:10 that such things are "regulations for the body."
- **By grace, not foods:** the heart is made firm not by what we eat or refuse to eat but by the grace of God. Jesus "declared all foods clean" (see Mark 7:19); "the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking" (Rom 14:17). We meet similar things today: some say vegetarian fasting earns merit, others that a certain diet or ritual makes a person more spiritual. The author's answer is still the same: what strengthens the heart is grace.

> The point: leaders will go, and the church's circumstances will change, but the Jesus Christ we trust is the same yesterday and today and forever. What strengthens the heart is not novel teaching or outward rules but his grace.

## Going Deeper: 13:10–16 Go to Him Outside the Camp

**1. "We have an altar."** "We have an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat" (13:10).
- **What is the altar?** There have been several views. Many interpreters (such as Bruce, Lane and Cockerill) take it to be Christ's sacrifice on the cross itself; to "eat" is to receive by faith all that this sacrifice brings. Some, especially within the Roman Catholic tradition, take it to be the altar of the Eucharist. Others take it to be the heavenly altar. The Protestant tradition holds that the Lord's Table is not an altar on which a propitiatory sacrifice is offered again (see the discussion of the Mass in chapter 7); yet at the Supper we truly share in "a participation in the blood of Christ" and "a participation in the body of Christ" (1 Cor 10:16), receiving the sacrifice already completed.
- **"No right to eat":** under the law, "no sin offering shall be eaten from which any blood is brought into the tent of meeting to make atonement in the Holy Place; it shall be burned up with fire" (Lev 6:30). The priests of the first covenant could not eat a bite of the greatest sacrifice of the Day of Atonement. Christ is the true sin offering, and all who trust him may receive him; those who still rely on the tent and its sacrifices have, of all people, no share in this one.

**2. "Suffered outside the gate."** "For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the holy places by the high priest as a sacrifice for sin are burned outside the camp. So Jesus also suffered outside the gate in order to sanctify the people through his own blood" (13:11–12).
- **The shadow of the Day of Atonement:** Leviticus says, "the bull for the sin offering and the goat for the sin offering, whose blood was brought in to make atonement in the Holy Place, shall be carried outside the camp. Their skin and their flesh and their dung shall be burned up with fire" (Lev 16:27).
- **Golgotha:** Jesus "went out, bearing his own cross," and the place where he was crucified "was near the city" (John 19:17, 20), outside the walls of Jerusalem.
- **What kind of place:** in the wilderness, outside the camp was where the unclean were sent and where the one under a curse was put to death (Lev 24:14). There Jesus was treated as one cursed: "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us" (Gal 3:13). There he bore in our place the penalty of sin, and so his blood sanctifies the people, once for all (10:10, 14).

**3. "Let us go to him outside the camp."** "Therefore let us go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured. For here we have no lasting city, but we seek the city that is to come" (13:13–14).
- **Leaving what?** Many interpreters (such as Bruce) think the author first of all calls Jewish believers to leave the system centered on the temple and its sacrifices, even if their own people count them apostates. Calvin and others read the words more broadly: leaving the security and honor the world offers, and gladly following the rejected Christ. The two readings do not conflict.
- **"The reproach he endured":** the word "reproach" here is the same as in 11:26, where Moses "considered the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt," and in 10:33, where the readers were "publicly exposed to reproach." They had walked this road before.
- **Outside the camp is also where God is met:** Moses once pitched the tent outside the camp, "and everyone who sought the LORD would go out to the tent of meeting, which was outside the camp" (Exod 33:7). When we go outside the camp, we do not go to an empty place; we go to him, because he is already there.
- **"No lasting city":** Abraham "was looking forward to the city that has foundations" (11:10), and we "have come to Mount Zion and to the city of the living God" (12:22). No earthly city, Jerusalem included, is our final home.

**4. "A sacrifice of praise."** "Through him then let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that acknowledge his name. Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God" (13:15–16).
- **No longer sacrifices for sin:** the atoning sacrifice is complete; "there is no longer any offering for sin" (10:18). Calvin, discussing the Lord's Supper in the Institutes, distinguished sacrifices of propitiation from sacrifices of thanksgiving: the first only Christ could offer, and he has offered it; the second is what people who have received grace offer to God all their lives.
- **"Through him":** even our praise is acceptable to God only through him (1 Pet 2:5). Many take "the fruit of lips" to echo Hosea 14:2 in the Greek Old Testament.
- **Two kinds of sacrifice:** one is praise toward God, the other doing good and sharing with people. True worship is both on the lips and in the hands.

> The point: Jesus suffered outside the gate, bore the curse in our place, and sanctified us through his own blood. So we go outside the camp not to earn anything but to go to him; we have no other sacrifice to offer than thankful praise and acts of love.

## Going Deeper: 13:17–19 Obey Your Leaders

**1. "Obey your leaders and submit to them."** "Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls, as those who will have to give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no advantage to you" (13:17).
- **"Obey":** many take this word to mean being persuaded and following, not blind obedience.
- **Why submit:** because "they are keeping watch over your souls." The word for "keeping watch" originally means going without sleep, like a shepherd guarding the flock at night.
- **Leaders give an account:** leaders themselves must answer to God. God once said, "Behold, I am against the shepherds, and I will require my sheep at their hand" (Ezek 34:10); the apostle charged the elders "to care for the church of God, which he obtained with his own blood" (Acts 20:28), "not domineering over those in your charge" (1 Pet 5:3).

**2. This is never a license for spiritual abuse.**
- **Under God's word:** submission to leaders is submission under God's word. If a leader demands what Scripture forbids, "we must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29). The Lord said that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over others, but "it shall not be so among you" (Matt 20:26).
- **Abuse must not be covered up:** if a leader uses spiritual authority to control, sexually assault or abuse people, that is not the submission this verse speaks of. Reporting to the police is right (Rom 13:4); victims need not stay in danger or reconcile with someone unsafe; even if he confesses, that does not restore his office or give him access again to victims or children. The church must not hide it to keep "family shame" from getting out.

**3. "Pray for us."** "Pray for us, for we are sure that we have a clear conscience, desiring to act honorably in all things. I urge you the more earnestly to do this in order that I may be restored to you the sooner" (13:18–19).
- **Leaders need prayer too:** the author does not stand above the congregation; he asks them to pray for him.
- **"A clear conscience":** many interpreters think someone may have been criticizing him, which is why he says this. What was keeping him from returning, we do not know.

> The point: God gives leaders to the church to keep watch over the flock, and they themselves must give account to the great Shepherd. We honor, pray for and follow faithful leaders, but there is only one true Shepherd, and no one may use his name to harm his sheep.

## Going Deeper: 13:20–25 The God of Peace

**1. "Brought again from the dead."** "Now may the God of peace who brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep, by the blood of the eternal covenant" (13:20).
- **"The God of peace":** things were not necessarily peaceful among the readers; the author has just urged them to "strive for peace with everyone" (12:14). But the source of peace is God.
- **The resurrection named:** Hebrews often speaks of Jesus ascending and sitting at God's right hand; many note that this is the only place in the book that explicitly names his resurrection.
- **"The great shepherd of the sheep":** many take this to echo Isaiah 63:11: "Where is he who brought them up out of the sea with the shepherds of his flock?" The shepherd there is Moses. Moses was the shepherd God brought up out of the Red Sea; Jesus is the great shepherd God brought up from the dead. He is the good shepherd who "lays down his life for the sheep" (John 10:11), the "Shepherd and Overseer of your souls" (1 Pet 2:25). All earthly leaders stand under him.
- **"By the blood of the eternal covenant":** Zechariah says, "because of the blood of my covenant with you, I will set your prisoners free from the waterless pit" (Zech 9:11). The prophets also promised that God would make "an everlasting covenant" (Isa 55:3; Jer 32:40; Ezek 37:26). The new covenant of chapter 8 is this eternal covenant; it was made in the blood of Jesus, and so it will never pass away.

**2. "Working in us that which is pleasing in his sight."** "Equip you with everything good that you may do his will, working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen" (13:21).
- **"Equip":** the same word is used of James and John "mending their nets" in the boat (Matt 4:21). God mends us and equips us so that we can do his will.
- **God works first:** all through the book the author has given many "let us" exhortations; at the end he prays for his readers, asking God himself to work in them. This is what Paul says too: "for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure" (Phil 2:13). Some manuscripts read "you," others "us"; the sense is the same: God works within his people.
- **"Through Jesus Christ":** it is through Jesus Christ that we can do what pleases God, and it is through him that what we do is accepted by God.
- **"To whom be glory":** "whom" may refer to God or to Jesus Christ (see 2 Pet 3:18). Both readings fit Scripture, since glory belongs to the Father and the Son alike.

**3. "My word of exhortation."** "I appeal to you, brothers, bear with my word of exhortation, for I have written to you briefly. You should know that our brother Timothy has been released, with whom I shall see you if he comes soon" (13:22–23).
- **A sermon:** on the "word of exhortation" and the genre of the book, see the introduction to this study. The author says he has written "briefly"; this is a book meant to be listened to with care.
- **Timothy:** nowhere else does the New Testament record Timothy's imprisonment, and we do not know where or when he was held. The author hopes to come to the readers together with him.

**4. "Grace be with all of you."** "Greet all your leaders and all the saints. Those who come from Italy send you greetings. Grace be with all of you" (13:24–25).
- **Leaders a third time:** the author mentions "all your leaders" once more and has the readers greet one another. On "those who come from Italy," see the introduction.
- **Ending in grace:** the book began with God speaking by his Son and ends in grace. Verse 9 said that "it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace"; at the very end the author leaves grace with them all.

> The point: the last word of the book is not another command but a blessing: the God who brought the great Shepherd back from the dead will himself work within his people until they do what is pleasing in his sight. All glory belongs to him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Remember your leaders, those who spoke to you the word of God... Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:7–8). Reaching the last chapter of Hebrews, I remember the first time I read this book. I had just come to faith; I underlined 13:8 and thought it said something obvious. Over the years since, so much has changed. Several pastors have come and gone from our pulpit, some sent elsewhere, some leaving on unhappy terms; some in the congregation went to other churches, some stopped coming, and those of us who stayed have not always known how to look at one another. The biggest change, though, has been in me. My first zeal turned into the habit of having a place in the church and being respected; there was also a stretch when I could not say whether I still believed at all. Looking back, my faith is nothing like a straight line. Yet verse 8 stands exactly between two kinds of leaders: verse 7, those who preached and have now finished their course; verse 17, those keeping watch now. Leaders come and go, but the One they preached has not been replaced. I have never stood because of a particular pastor, or because of myself in some particular season; I stand because of this Jesus Christ, who is the same yesterday and today and forever. I must also confess that over these years I have often chosen to stay inside the "camp." When coworkers joked about faith, I said nothing; when there were difficult people at church, I would rather guard my place and my face than welcome them. I was afraid of being looked down on. But Jesus "suffered outside the gate" (13:12). He was taken to the place where the unclean and the cursed were taken, and there he bore my sin and the shame that should have fallen on me, sanctifying me "through his own blood." So "go to him outside the camp" does not first require me to become brave; it means going to him. He is already there, and because he is there, it is no longer only a place of shame. If you have not yet trusted him, you need not make yourself better first; you can confess your sin, repent, and trust this Jesus who shed his blood for sinners outside the gate. At the end of the book the author gives his readers no further task; he blesses them. I have often thought that whether my faith makes it to the end depends on me. But this blessing says that "the God of peace" will "equip you with everything good," "working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ" (13:20–21). He has already brought the great shepherd of the sheep again from the dead; the eternal covenant was made in blood, and my years of running hot and cold do not cancel it. The church will change, leaders will change, and I will still change; but the One at work in me has always been the same God, through the same Jesus Christ. Having read the whole book, I want only to end with the author's own words: "to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen."
