## 本章脉络

第十三章是全书最后的劝勉和问安。**彼此相爱的生活**（1-6节）：弟兄相爱、接待客旅、记念受苦的人、尊重婚姻、不贪爱钱财，因为主「总不撇下你」。**带领者和不改变的基督**（7-17节）：「耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的」，我们当出到营外就了他去，以颂赞为祭献给神。**代祷、祝福和问安**（18-25节）：愿赐平安的神在各样善事上成全你们。

这一章不是零碎的附录：12:28说要「照神所喜悦的」事奉神，13:16和13:21的「喜悦」原文是同一组词，这一章就讲这样的事奉在生活中的样子。许多解经家也注意到，第7节和第17节都讲到「引导你们的」人，像一个框子，把中间关于基督和祭坛的话围在里面。

## 深读 13:1-6 彼此相爱的生活

**1. 「不知不觉就接待了天使」。** 「你们务要常存弟兄相爱的心。不可忘记用爱心接待客旅；因为曾有接待客旅的，不知不觉就接待了天使。」（13:1-2）
- **亚伯拉罕：** 他看见三个人，「就从帐棚门口跑去迎接他们」（创18:2），原来是耶和华向他显现（创18:1、13；参创19:1）。
- **真正的动机：** 不是为了碰见天使，而是因为主说：「我作客旅，你们留我住」（太25:35）。接待人也要有分辨，特别要顾到孩子的安全。

**2. 「好像与他们同受捆绑」。** 「你们要记念被捆绑的人，好像与他们同受捆绑；也要记念遭苦害的人，想到自己也在肉身之内。」（13:3）读者从前就「体恤了那些被捆锁的人」（10:34）。今天，我们可以为受逼迫的信徒祷告，也可以探访坐监的人，关心他们的家人。

**3. 「婚姻，人人都当尊重」。** 「婚姻，人人都当尊重，床也不可污秽；因为苟合行淫的人，神必要审判。」（13:4）
- **两面的错误：** 有人以为禁欲更属灵，轻看婚姻（提前4:3）；有人放纵情欲，玷污婚床。作者两样都拒绝：婚姻人人都当尊重；婚姻以外的性关系和奸淫，神必审判。
- **更大的恩典：** 哥林多信徒中也有人从前是这样，如今却「已经洗净，成圣，称义了」（林前6:11）。在这方面跌倒的人，可以带着羞愧来到基督面前认罪，他必赦免（约一1:9）。被配偶背叛的人，神也看见你的伤，他的审判是公义的。
- **不等于留在危险中：** 婚姻的尊荣，绝不是要人留在暴力或虐待的危险里；教会也不可为了「家丑不可外扬」替施暴的人遮掩（另见13:17）。

**4. 「我总不撇下你」。** 「你们存心不可贪爱钱财，要以自己所有的为足；因为主曾说：『我总不撇下你，也不丢弃你。』所以我们可以放胆说：主是帮助我的，我必不惧怕；人能把我怎么样呢？」（13:5-6；参诗118:6）我们能知足，是因为主自己与我们同在。这应许是摩西把带领的担子交给约书亚的时候说的（申31:6、8；书1:5）：带领的人换了，应许没有换。人能抢走家业（10:34），却拿不走主的同在。

> 要点：这些不是另外加上的规条，而是蒙了恩的人日常的样子；这一切的根基，是主的应许：他总不撇下我们。

## 深读 13:7-9 昨日、今日、一直到永远

**1. 「要想念他们」。** 「从前引导你们、传神之道给你们的人，你们要想念他们，效法他们的信心，留心看他们为人的结局。」（13:7）「为人的结局」，许多解经家（如布鲁斯）认为是指这些已经离世的带领者怎样走完一生，也许有人为主殉道；也有人认为泛指他们一生行事的结果。读者要效法他们的信心，信靠同一位主，而不是崇拜人。

**2. 「耶稣基督……是一样的」。** 「耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的。」（13:8）
- **前后呼应：** 这一节夹在第7节和第9节之间。带领的人会离世，他们所传的那一位却没有换人，读者就不必追求新奇的教训。
- **回到第一章：** 1:12说「惟有你永不改变」，这话在诗篇102篇原是讲耶和华的，作者却用在儿子身上，显明他是神（参玛3:6）；原文里两处是同一个词，这里却用了耶稣在地上的名字：那位曾经受苦的耶稣，就是永不改变的主。
- **什么没有改变：** 他道成肉身、受苦、复活、升天，所处的境况有过变化；但他是谁、他的信实和他一次成就的救恩，都没有改变（参7:25）。

**3. 「人心靠恩得坚固才是好的」。** 「你们不要被那诸般怪异的教训勾引了去；因为人心靠恩得坚固才是好的，并不是靠饮食。那在饮食上专心的从来没有得着益处。」（13:9）这里的饮食指什么，解经家有几种看法：犹太人的洁净饮食条例；和圣殿献祭或节期相连的筵席，以为吃了能得属灵的益处（如布鲁斯、雷恩）；或者带有禁欲色彩的规条（参西2:16）。作者在9:10已经说，这些都是「属肉体的条例」。今天也有人以为吃斋、守某些饮食禁忌，就能积功德、得平安；坚固人心的却是恩典，不是饮食（参罗14:17）。

> 要点：带领的人会离去，教会的情形也会改变，但我们所信的耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远都是一样的。坚固人心的，是他的恩典。

## 深读 13:10-16 出到营外，就了他去

**1. 「我们有一祭坛」。** 「我们有一祭坛，上面的祭物是那些在帐幕中供职的人不可同吃的。」（13:10）
- **几种看法：** 许多解经家（如布鲁斯、雷恩、柯克里尔）认为，这是指基督在十字架上的献祭本身，「吃」是用信心领受这祭带来的一切。有些人，特别是罗马天主教传统中的一些解经家，认为是指圣餐的祭坛。更正教的传统认为，圣餐桌不是献上挽回祭的祭坛（参第七章关于弥撒的讨论）；但我们在圣餐中确实「同领基督的血」（林前10:16）。
- **「不可同吃」：** 赎罪日的祭肉不可吃（利6:30）；信靠基督的人却能领受这真正的赎罪祭，依靠帐幕的人反倒无分。

**2. 「在城门外受苦」。** 「原来牲畜的血被大祭司带入圣所作赎罪祭；牲畜的身子被烧在营外。所以，耶稣要用自己的血叫百姓成圣，也就在城门外受苦。」（13:11-12）
- **赎罪日的影儿：** 赎罪祭的牛羊要搬到营外焚烧（利16:27）。耶稣「背着自己的十字架出来」，钉十字架的地方「与城相近」（约19:17、20），在耶路撒冷城外。
- **被咒诅的地方：** 营外是安置不洁净之人的地方（利13:46；民5:2-3），也是处死亵渎圣名或干犯安息日、被神定罪之人的地方（利24:14；民15:35-36）。耶稣在那里「为我们受了咒诅」（加3:13；参申21:23），替我们担当了罪的刑罚，所以他的血能叫百姓成圣，而且只一次就成全了（10:10、14）。

**3. 「出到营外，就了他去」。** 「这样，我们也当出到营外，就了他去，忍受他所受的凌辱。我们在这里本没有常存的城，乃是寻求那将来的城。」（13:13-14）
- **离开什么：** 许多解经家（如布鲁斯）认为，作者首先是叫犹太信徒离开以帐幕献祭为中心的前约制度；加尔文等人讲得更宽，指离开世界所给的安全和尊荣。两种理解并不冲突。「凌辱」和11:26摩西「为基督受的凌辱」是同一个词。在华人信徒身上，这可能是祭祖时不肯上香而被长辈责备，或在职场上不肯同流合污而失去升迁。
- **营外也是遇见神的地方：** 有解经家指出，摩西曾把会幕支搭在营外，「凡求问耶和华的，就到营外的会幕那里去」（出33:7）。我们出到营外，是到他那里去。

**4. 「以颂赞为祭」。** 「我们应当靠着耶稣，常常以颂赞为祭献给神，这就是那承认主名之人嘴唇的果子。只是不可忘记行善和捐输的事，因为这样的祭是神所喜悦的。」（13:15-16）赎罪的祭已经成全了，「就不用再为罪献祭了」（10:18）。加尔文区分挽回的祭和感恩的祭：前一种只有基督能献，而且已经献上了；后一种是蒙恩的人献的，也要「靠着耶稣」才蒙悦纳（彼前2:5）。这感恩的祭有两样：嘴唇上的颂赞（参何14:2；诗50:14、23），手中的行善和捐输。

> 要点：耶稣在城门外替我们担当了咒诅，用自己的血叫我们成圣。所以我们出到营外，不是为了赚得什么，而是到他那里去；我们没有别的祭要献，只献上感恩的颂赞和爱人的善行。

## 深读 13:17-19 依从引导你们的

**1. 「好像那将来交帐的人」。** 「你们要依从那些引导你们的，且要顺服；因他们为你们的灵魂时刻警醒，好像那将来交帐的人。你们要使他们交的时候有快乐，不致忧愁；若忧愁就与你们无益了。」（13:17）
- **「依从」：** 原文这个词和说服同一个字根，但在这里的意思就是听从；后面还加上「顺服」。这里的权柄是有界限的：这些人传的是神的道（7节），为灵魂警醒，自己也要交帐，都在大牧人以下（20节）。所以这是对忠心传道者的信任和跟随，不是盲目服从。
- **带领者要交帐：** 神说：「我必与牧人为敌，必向他们的手追讨我的羊」（结34:10）；长老要「牧养神的教会，就是他用自己血所买来的」（徒20:28），「也不是辖制所托付你们的」（彼前5:3）。

**2. 这绝不是纵容属灵虐待。** 顺服带领者，是在神的话以下的顺服；带领者若要人做违背圣经的事，就当「顺从神，不顺从人」（徒5:29）。带领者若利用属灵的权柄控制人、性侵或虐待人，本节并没有要人顺服他；受害的人可以离开，去找一个安全的教会。向警方举报是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人不必和不安全的人和好；即使施害的人认罪，也不等于恢复他的职分，或让他再接近受害者和孩子。教会不可因为「家丑不可外扬」替他隐瞒。这节经文也不可拿来叫人闭口：使带领者忧愁的，是犯罪的人，不是说出真相、保护羊群的人。

**3. 「请你们为我们祷告」。** 「请你们为我们祷告，因我们自觉良心无亏，愿意凡事按正道而行。我更求你们为我祷告，使我快些回到你们那里去。」（13:18-19）作者并不高高在上，他也需要代祷。

> 要点：我们尊重、跟随忠心的带领者，为他们祷告；但他们都要向大牧人交帐，任何人都不可借他的名伤害他的羊。

## 深读 13:20-25 赐平安的神

**1. 「使群羊的大牧人……从死里复活」。** 「但愿赐平安的神，就是那凭永约之血、使群羊的大牧人－我主耶稣从死里复活的神」（13:20）
- **大牧人：** 书中别处也讲到复活（6:2；11:19、35），并一直以耶稣复活升天为前提（1:3；7:16、25），但明说神使耶稣从死里复活的，只有这里。这里也可能是在呼应以赛亚书63:11：神「将百姓和牧养他全群的人从海里领上来」。七十士译本这里作单数的群羊的牧人，用词与本节相同，指的是摩西（希伯来文是复数）。耶稣是神从死里领上来的大牧人（约10:11），地上的带领者都在他以下。
- **「永约之血」：** 先知应许神要立「永约」（赛55:3；耶32:40；参亚9:11；结34:23-25；37:24-26）。第八章所讲的新约，就是这永约；它是用耶稣的血立的，永不过去。

**2. 「在你们心里行他所喜悦的事」。** 「在各样善事上成全你们，叫你们遵行他的旨意；又藉着耶稣基督在你们心里行他所喜悦的事。愿荣耀归给他，直到永永远远。阿们！」（13:21）
- **「成全」：** 这个词的意思是使之齐全、装备；同一个词也用在「补网」（太4:21），书中也用在神为基督「预备了身体」（10:5），以及「诸世界是藉神的话造成的」（11:3）。神亲自使我们齐全，叫我们能遵行他的旨意。
- **神先做工：** 作者全书劝了读者许多事，最后却求神亲自在他们里面做工（参腓2:13），并且是藉着耶稣基督。这里的「在你们心里」，较早、较好的抄本多作在我们心里（和合本依照的是别的抄本），意思没有大分别。

**3. 「劝勉的话」和问安。** 「弟兄们，我略略写信给你们，望你们听我劝勉的话。」（13:22）体裁和「从意大利来的人」见导论。第23节似乎表示提摩太曾被囚，新约别处没有记载这件事。第9节说「人心靠恩得坚固」；全书也以恩典作结：「愿恩惠常与你们众人同在」（13:25）。

> 要点：全书最后不是命令，而是祝福：那叫大牧人从死里复活的神，要亲自在他的百姓里面行他所喜悦的事。荣耀都归给他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「从前引导你们、传神之道给你们的人，你们要想念他们……耶稣基督昨日、今日、一直到永远，是一样的。」（13:7-8）我刚信主时第一次读希伯来书，在13:8下面画了一道线，觉得这句话理所当然。这些年，讲台上换过几位传道人，有人离开了教会；变化最大的其实是我自己。起初的火热，慢慢变成了一种习惯：在教会里有个位置，受人尊重；中间也有过一段日子，我连自己还信不信都说不清。可是第8节正好放在讲两种带领者的经文之间：第7节是已经走完了路的人，第17节是现在正在警醒的人。带领的人来了又去，他们所传的那一位却没有换人。我站得住，从来不是靠某一位牧师，也不是靠某一段时期的自己，而是靠这位一直都一样的耶稣基督。我也要承认，我常常选择留在营里。同事拿信仰开玩笑，我不出声；教会里有坐过牢的、有精神疾病的人，我怕别人把我和他们看成一伙，宁可守住自己的面子。可是耶稣是「在城门外受苦」的（13:12）。他被带到营外，就是不洁净和被定罪的人去的地方，在那里担当了我的罪和本该临到我的羞辱，「用自己的血」叫我成圣。所以，「出到营外，就了他去」，不是先要我变得勇敢，而是到他那里去；他已经在那里了。如果你一直觉得自己是在外面的人，不配进教会、不配到神面前，营外正是耶稣受苦的地方；他就在那里等你。你可以就这样到他那里去，认罪悔改，信靠他。在这卷书的末了，作者没有再交给读者一项任务，而是为他们祝福。这祝福说，是那「赐平安的神」要「在各样善事上成全你们」，又「藉着耶稣基督在你们心里行他所喜悦的事」（13:20-21）。那永约是用血立的，不会因为我这些年的软弱、疑惑和退后而作废。教会会变，我自己也还会变，可是在我里面做工的，一直是同一位神，藉着同一位耶稣基督。引导我的人有的已经离世；可是群羊的大牧人，神已经使他从死里复活，他不会再死，也不会离开他的羊。读完这一整卷书，我只想用作者自己的话来结束：「愿荣耀归给他，直到永永远远。阿们！」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 13 is the book's closing exhortation and greeting. **A life of mutual love** (vv. 1–6): brotherly love, hospitality, remembering those who suffer, honoring marriage and freedom from the love of money, for he has said, "I will never leave you." **Leaders and the unchanging Christ** (vv. 7–17): "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever," so let us go to him outside the camp and offer up a sacrifice of praise to God. **Prayer, blessing and greetings** (vv. 18–25): may the God of peace equip you with everything good.

This chapter is not a loose appendix: 12:28 calls for "acceptable worship," and "pleasing" in 13:16 and 13:21 belongs to the same word family; this chapter shows that worship in daily life. Many interpreters also notice that verses 7 and 17 both speak of "your leaders," forming a frame around the words about Christ and the altar in between.

## Going Deeper: 13:1–6 A Life of Mutual Love

**1. "Entertained angels unawares."** "Let brotherly love continue. Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers, for thereby some have entertained angels unawares" (13:1–2).
- **Abraham:** when he saw three men, "he ran from the tent door to meet them" (Gen 18:2); it was the LORD appearing to him (Gen 18:1, 13; cf. Gen 19:1).
- **The real motive:** not the hope of meeting angels, but the Lord's word: "I was a stranger and you welcomed me" (Matt 25:35). Hospitality needs discernment too, especially for children's safety.

**2. "As though in prison with them."** "Remember those who are in prison, as though in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you also are in the body" (13:3). The readers had once "had compassion on those in prison" (10:34). Today we can pray for persecuted believers, visit those in prison, and care for their families.

**3. "Let marriage be held in honor among all."** "Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the marriage bed be undefiled, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterous" (13:4).
- **Two errors:** some think abstinence more spiritual and look down on marriage (1 Tim 4:3); others indulge their desires and defile the marriage bed. The author rejects both: marriage is to be honored by all, and God will judge sex outside marriage and adultery.
- **Greater grace:** Some of the Corinthians had been such people, yet "you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified" (1 Cor 6:11). Whoever has fallen here can come to Christ with their shame and confess, and he will forgive (1 John 1:9). If you have been betrayed by a spouse, God sees that wound too, and his judgment is just.
- **Not staying in danger:** the honor of marriage never requires remaining in violence or abuse, and the church must not cover it up to keep "family shame" from getting out (see on 13:17).

**4. "I will never leave you."** "Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have, for he has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you.' So we can confidently say, 'The Lord is my helper; I will not fear; what can man do to me?'" (13:5–6; cf. Ps 118:6). We can be content because the Lord himself is with us. This promise was spoken when Moses handed the burden of leadership to Joshua (Deut 31:6, 8; Josh 1:5): the leader changed; the promise did not. People can plunder property (10:34), but not take away the Lord's presence.

> The point: these are not extra rules added on but the everyday shape of people who have received grace; their foundation is the Lord's promise: he will never leave us.

## Going Deeper: 13:7–9 Yesterday and Today and Forever

**1. "Remember your leaders."** "Remember your leaders, those who spoke to you the word of God. Consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith" (13:7). Many interpreters (such as Bruce) take "the outcome of their way of life" to mean the way these now-departed leaders finished their lives, perhaps including some martyrs; others take it more generally as the fruit of their conduct. The readers are to imitate their faith by trusting the same Lord, not to venerate people.

**2. "Jesus Christ is the same."** "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:8).
- **Linking both sides:** this verse stands between verses 7 and 9. Leaders die, but the One they preached has not been replaced; so there is no need to chase novel teachings.
- **Back to chapter 1:** 1:12 says, "But you are the same"; these words were spoken of the LORD in Psalm 102, and the author applies them to the Son, showing that he is God (see Mal 3:6). The original uses the same expression in both places, but here with the name Jesus bore on earth: the Jesus who suffered is the unchanging Lord.
- **What has not changed:** he took flesh, suffered, rose and ascended, so his circumstances changed; but who he is, his faithfulness and the salvation he accomplished once for all have not (see 7:25).

**3. "Strengthened by grace."** "Do not be led away by diverse and strange teachings, for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods, which have not benefited those devoted to them" (13:9). Interpreters offer several views of what these foods are: Jewish food laws; meals connected with temple sacrifice or festivals, eaten in the belief that they brought spiritual benefit (such as Bruce and Lane); or ascetic rules (see Col 2:16). The author has already called such things "regulations for the body" (9:10). Today too some think that fasting or keeping certain food taboos earns merit and brings peace; but what strengthens the heart is grace, not foods (see Rom 14:17).

> The point: leaders will go, and the church's circumstances will change, but the Jesus Christ we trust is the same yesterday and today and forever. What strengthens the heart is his grace.

## Going Deeper: 13:10–16 Go to Him Outside the Camp

**1. "We have an altar."** "We have an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat" (13:10).
- **Several views:** many interpreters (such as Bruce, Lane and Cockerill) take it to be Christ's sacrifice on the cross itself, and to "eat" is to receive by faith all that this sacrifice brings. Some, especially interpreters within the Roman Catholic tradition, take it to be the altar of the Eucharist. The Protestant tradition holds that the Lord's Table is not an altar on which a propitiatory sacrifice is offered (see the discussion of the Mass in chapter 7); yet at the Supper we truly have "a participation in the blood of Christ" (1 Cor 10:16).
- **"No right to eat":** the flesh of the Day of Atonement sacrifice could not be eaten (Lev 6:30), but all who trust Christ may receive this true sin offering, while those who still rely on the tent have no share in it.

**2. "Suffered outside the gate."** "For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the holy places by the high priest as a sacrifice for sin are burned outside the camp. So Jesus also suffered outside the gate in order to sanctify the people through his own blood" (13:11–12).
- **The shadow of the Day of Atonement:** the bull and goat of the sin offering were carried outside the camp and burned (Lev 16:27). Jesus "went out, bearing his own cross," and the place where he was crucified "was near the city" (John 19:17, 20), outside Jerusalem.
- **The place of the curse:** outside the camp was where the unclean were placed (Lev 13:46; Num 5:2–3) and where those condemned by God for blaspheming the Name or breaking the Sabbath were put to death (Lev 24:14; Num 15:35–36). There Jesus became "a curse for us" (Gal 3:13; cf. Deut 21:23), bearing in our place the penalty of sin; so his blood sanctifies the people, once for all (10:10, 14).

**3. "Let us go to him outside the camp."** "Therefore let us go to him outside the camp and bear the reproach he endured. For here we have no lasting city, but we seek the city that is to come" (13:13–14).
- **Leaving what?** Many interpreters (such as Bruce) think the author first of all calls Jewish believers to leave the old-covenant sacrificial order centered on the tabernacle; Calvin and others read it more broadly, as leaving the security and honor the world offers. The two readings do not conflict. "Reproach" is the same word as Moses' "reproach of Christ" in 11:26. For Chinese believers this may mean being rebuked by elders for refusing incense at an ancestral rite, or losing a promotion for refusing to join in wrongdoing at work.
- **Outside the camp is also where God is met:** some commentators point out that Moses once pitched the tent outside the camp, "and everyone who sought the LORD would go out to the tent of meeting, which was outside the camp" (Exod 33:7). Going outside the camp means going to him.

**4. "A sacrifice of praise."** "Through him then let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of lips that acknowledge his name. Do not neglect to do good and to share what you have, for such sacrifices are pleasing to God" (13:15–16). The atoning sacrifice is complete; "there is no longer any offering for sin" (10:18). Calvin distinguished sacrifices of propitiation from sacrifices of thanksgiving: only Christ has offered the first; the second is offered by those who have received grace, and is acceptable only "through him" (1 Pet 2:5). It has two sides: praise on the lips (cf. Hos 14:2; Ps 50:14, 23), and doing good and sharing in the hands.

> The point: outside the gate Jesus bore the curse in our place and sanctified us through his own blood. So we go outside the camp not to earn anything but to go to him; we have no other sacrifice to offer than thankful praise and acts of love.

## Going Deeper: 13:17–19 Obey Your Leaders

**1. "As those who will have to give an account."** "Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls, as those who will have to give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no advantage to you" (13:17).
- **"Obey":** the verb is related to "persuade," but in use it simply means "obey," and the author adds "submit." The authority is bounded by the context: these leaders speak God's word (v. 7), watch over souls, must give account, and stand under the great Shepherd (v. 20). So this is trust in and following of faithful ministers of the word, not blind obedience.
- **Leaders give an account:** God says, "Behold, I am against the shepherds, and I will require my sheep at their hand" (Ezek 34:10); elders are "to care for the church of God, which he obtained with his own blood" (Acts 20:28), "not domineering over those in your charge" (1 Pet 5:3).

**2. This is never a license for spiritual abuse.** Submission to leaders is submission under God's word; if a leader demands what Scripture forbids, "we must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29). If a leader uses spiritual authority to control, sexually assault or abuse people, this verse does not ask anyone to submit to him; those harmed may leave and look for a safe church. Reporting to the police is right (Rom 13:4); victims need not reconcile with someone unsafe; even if the abuser confesses, that does not restore his office or give him access again to victims or children. The church must not hide it to keep "family shame" from getting out. Nor may this verse be used to silence anyone: the one who brings groaning is the one who did wrong, not the one who tells the truth to protect the flock.

**3. "Pray for us."** "Pray for us, for we are sure that we have a clear conscience, desiring to act honorably in all things. I urge you the more earnestly to do this in order that I may be restored to you the sooner" (13:18–19). The author does not stand above the congregation; he too needs their prayers.

> The point: we honor, follow and pray for faithful leaders; but they all give account to the great Shepherd, and no one may use his name to harm his sheep.

## Going Deeper: 13:20–25 The God of Peace

**1. "Brought again from the dead... the great shepherd of the sheep."** "Now may the God of peace who brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great shepherd of the sheep, by the blood of the eternal covenant" (13:20).
- **The great shepherd:** resurrection appears elsewhere (6:2; 11:19, 35), and Jesus' risen life is assumed throughout (1:3; 7:16, 25), but only here is God said to have brought Jesus again from the dead. It may also echo Isaiah 63:11: "Where is he who brought them up out of the sea with the shepherds of his flock?" The Greek Old Testament has the singular, "the shepherd of the sheep," the same wording as here, meaning Moses (the Hebrew has the plural). Jesus is the great shepherd God brought up from the dead (John 10:11). All earthly leaders stand under him.
- **"The blood of the eternal covenant":** the prophets promised that God would make "an everlasting covenant" (Isa 55:3; Jer 32:40; see Zech 9:11; cf. Ezek 34:23–25; 37:24–26). The new covenant of chapter 8 is this eternal covenant; made in the blood of Jesus, it will never pass away.

**2. "Working in us that which is pleasing in his sight."** "Equip you with everything good that you may do his will, working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen" (13:21).
- **"Equip":** the word means to put in order, make complete, equip. It is used of "mending" nets (Matt 4:21), and in Hebrews of God preparing a body for Christ (10:5) and framing the worlds (11:3). God himself makes us complete to do his will.
- **God works first:** all through the book the author has exhorted his readers; at the end he asks God himself to work within them (see Phil 2:13), through Jesus Christ. Where the Chinese Union Version reads "in you," the earlier and better manuscripts mostly read "in us" (the reading behind the ESV); the sense is much the same.

**3. The word of exhortation and greetings.** "I appeal to you, brothers, bear with my word of exhortation, for I have written to you briefly" (13:22). On the genre and "those who come from Italy," see the introduction. Verse 23 seems to imply that Timothy had been imprisoned, which the New Testament nowhere else records. Verse 9 said that "it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace," and the whole book ends, "Grace be with all of you" (13:25).

> The point: the book's last word is not a command but a blessing: the God who brought the great Shepherd back from the dead will himself work in his people what is pleasing in his sight. All glory belongs to him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Remember your leaders, those who spoke to you the word of God... Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever" (13:7–8). When I first read Hebrews as a new believer, I underlined 13:8 and thought it obvious. Over the years, several pastors have come and gone from our pulpit, and some have left the church; but the biggest change has been in me. My first zeal slowly turned into a habit: having a place in the church and being respected; and there was a stretch when I could not say whether I still believed at all. Yet verse 8 stands within the frame formed by two kinds of leaders: verse 7, those who have finished their course; verse 17, those keeping watch now. Leaders come and go, but the One they preached has not been replaced. I have never stood because of a particular pastor, or because of myself in some particular season; I stand because of this Jesus Christ, who is always the same. I must also confess that I have often chosen to stay inside the camp. When coworkers joked about faith, I said nothing; when someone at church had been in prison or struggled with mental illness, I kept my distance, afraid of being counted with them, guarding my face. But Jesus "suffered outside the gate" (13:12). He was taken outside the camp, where the unclean and the condemned were taken, and there he bore my sin and the shame that should have fallen on me, sanctifying me "through his own blood." So "go to him outside the camp" does not first require me to become brave; it means going to him. He is already there. If you have always felt like someone on the outside, unfit for church or for God, outside the camp is exactly where Jesus suffered; he is waiting for you there. You may come to him just as you are, confess your sin, repent, and trust him. At the end of the book the author gives his readers no further task; he blesses them. This blessing says that "the God of peace" will "equip you with everything good," "working in us that which is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ" (13:20–21). The eternal covenant was made in blood, and my years of weakness, doubt and drifting do not cancel it. The church will change, and I will still change; but the One at work in me has always been the same God, through the same Jesus Christ. Some who led me have died; but the great Shepherd of the sheep God has brought again from the dead, and he will not die again or leave his sheep. Having read the whole book, I want only to end with the author's own words: "to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen."
