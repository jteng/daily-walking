## 本章脉络

第一章可以分成四段。**试炼和智慧**（2-8节）：落在百般试炼中，要以为大喜乐，因为信心经过试验就生忍耐；缺少智慧的，应当凭着信心向那厚赐与众人的神祈求。**卑微的和富足的**（9-11节）：卑微的弟兄升高，就该喜乐；富足的降卑，也该如此，因为他必要过去，如同草上的花。**试炼和试探**（12-18节）：忍受试探的人是有福的；人被试探，不可说是被神试探，乃是被自己的私欲牵引；各样美善的恩赐都是从众光之父那里降下来的，他用真道生了我们。**听道和行道**（19-27节）：要快快地听，慢慢地说，慢慢地动怒；要存温柔的心领受所栽种的道；要行道，不要单单听道，像人照镜子走后就忘了；清洁的虔诚，是看顾患难中的孤儿寡妇，保守自己不沾染世俗。

这一章像全书的目录：试炼、智慧、贫富、言语、听道和行道，后面几章都会回来再讲。贯穿这一章的，是两种「生」的对比：私欲「怀了胎，就生出罪来；罪既长成，就生出死来」（15节）；神却「按自己的旨意，用真道生了我们」（18节）。

## 深读 1:2-8 试炼中的喜乐和智慧

**1. 「都要以为大喜乐」。** 「我的弟兄们，你们落在百般试炼中，都要以为大喜乐。」（1:2）
- **「落在」：** 这个词是说忽然遇上、陷入其中，就像那个从耶路撒冷下耶利哥去的人，「落在强盗手中」（路10:30，同一个词）。试炼往往不是我们选的，而是突然临到的。
- **「百般」：** 试炼有许多种：贫穷、疾病、被人欺压（2:6）、亲人离世、工作不顺。雅各的读者多半是分散在异乡的穷苦信徒，他们很清楚什么是试炼。
- **「以为」：** 雅各没有叫人假装不痛，也没有说试炼本身是好事。「以为」是一种判断：我要用信心的眼光来看这件事。这和主耶稣所说的相合：「人若因我辱骂你们，逼迫你们……你们就有福了！应当欢喜快乐。」（太5:11-12）

**2. 「信心经过试验，就生忍耐」。** 「因为知道你们的信心经过试验，就生忍耐。但忍耐也当成功，使你们成全、完备，毫无缺欠。」（1:3-4）
- **「知道」：** 喜乐的根据不是感觉，而是知道神在做什么。保罗也说：「就是在患难中也是欢欢喜喜的；因为知道患难生忍耐。」（罗5:3）
- **「试验」：** 这个词有试金石、炼金的意思，是要验出真假。彼得说，我们的信心被试验，「就比那被火试验仍然能坏的金子更显宝贵」（彼前1:7）。
- **「忍耐」：** 不是咬紧牙关硬撑，而是在压力下仍然站立，不离开神。这个词在雅各书中多次出现（1:3、4、12；5:11）。
- **「成全、完备，毫无缺欠」：** 这是神的目标。原文这个词也有「成熟」的意思，雅各在全书中多次用它（和合本译作「成全」「全备」「完全」，1:4、17、25；3:2）。神要的不只是叫我们度过试炼，而是要藉着试炼，叫我们成为一个完整的人。

**3. 「应当求那厚赐与众人、也不斥责人的神」。** 「你们中间若有缺少智慧的，应当求那厚赐与众人、也不斥责人的神，主就必赐给他。」（1:5）
- **为什么在这里讲智慧？** 第4节说「毫无缺欠」，第5节说「若有缺少」，原文用的是同一个词。在试炼中，我们最缺少的往往不是答案，而是智慧：知道怎样在这件事中敬畏神、怎样走下一步。这是箴言所讲的智慧：「耶和华赐人智慧。」（箴2:6）
- **「厚赐」：** 原文有单纯、毫无保留的意思。神给的时候，心里没有算计。
- **「也不斥责人」：** 神不会一边给，一边数落我们：「你怎么又来了？你早该懂了。」这对常常觉得自己问题太多、不好意思再求的人是很大的安慰。

**4. 「只要凭着信心求，一点不疑惑」。** 「只要凭着信心求，一点不疑惑；因为那疑惑的人，就像海中的波浪，被风吹动翻腾。这样的人不要想从主那里得什么。心怀二意的人，在他一切所行的路上都没有定见。」（1:6-8）
- **「疑惑」不是指心里有疑问：** 雅各不是说，心里有一点疑问的人就得不着。诗篇里有许多祷告，都带着「要到几时呢」的问题（诗13:1）。
- **「心怀二意」：** 原文直译是双心，就是一颗心分成两半。在现存的希腊文献中，雅各可能是最早用这个词的人。这样的人一边求神，一边心里另有依靠，脚踏两条船。他不是不确定神能不能，而是不确定自己要不要神。雅各在4:8还会回到这个词：「心怀二意的人哪，要清洁你们的心！」
- **「没有定见」：** 像波浪一样，风往哪里吹就往哪里去。雅各在3:8用同一个词形容舌头是「不止息的恶物」。

> 要点：试炼不是神离开了我们，而是神在成全我们的信心。神既不爱惜自己的儿子，为我们舍了（罗8:32），就不会在试炼中丢下我们。我们可以向那位「厚赐与众人、也不斥责人」的神祈求智慧，不必怕他嫌我们求得太多。

## 深读 1:9-11 卑微的和富足的

**1. 「卑微的弟兄升高，就该喜乐」。** 「卑微的弟兄升高，就该喜乐。」（1:9）
- **贫穷也是一种试炼：** 雅各刚讲完试炼，就讲到贫富。雅各的许多读者最常遇到的试炼，就是贫穷。
- **「升高」：** 在世人眼中，他没有地位；在神面前，他却是被高举的，是神的儿女，「在信上富足，并承受……国」（2:5）。这里「喜乐」原文是夸口的意思：他可以夸的，不是自己有什么，而是神使他升高。

**2. 「富足的降卑，也该如此」。** 「富足的降卑，也该如此；因为他必要过去，如同草上的花一样。太阳出来，热风刮起，草就枯干，花也凋谢，美容就消没了；那富足的人，在他所行的事上也要这样衰残。」（1:10-11）
- **两种理解：** 这里的「富足的」是谁，解经家有不同的看法。一种看法认为，他也是信徒：富足的弟兄要以自己的「降卑」夸口，就是承认在神面前自己的钱财算不得什么，和穷弟兄一样都是蒙恩的罪人。另一些解经家（如戴维斯）认为，他不是信徒，这里是带着讽刺的话：富人唯一能「夸」的，就是他要像花一样凋谢。雅各在5:1-6对欺压人的富人说话很重，「必要过去」「衰残」也像审判的话，似乎支持第二种看法；但第10节紧接着第9节「卑微的弟兄」，用「也该如此」把两人并列，又似乎支持第一种看法。
- **「如同草上的花」：** 这是以赛亚书的话：「凡有血气的尽都如草；他的美容都像野地的花。草必枯干，花必凋残……惟有我们神的话必永远立定。」（赛40:6-8）在巴勒斯坦，春天的野花很美，可是热风一刮，几天之内就全枯干了。
- **「在他所行的事上」：** 富人可能在忙着做生意的时候，忽然就走了（参4:13-14）。钱财不能叫人站立得住。

> 要点：在神面前，贫穷的人不必自卑，富足的人也不可自恃。我们可以夸的，只有神的恩典；能永远立定的，只有神的话。

## 深读 1:12-18 试炼和试探

**1. 「忍受试探的人是有福的」。** 「忍受试探的人是有福的，因为他经过试验以后，必得生命的冠冕；这是主应许给那些爱他之人的。」（1:12）
- **同一个词：** 和合本这里译作「试探」，第2节译作「试炼」，原文是同一个词。外面的难处是试炼，神要藉着它坚固我们的信心；可是同一件难处，也可能成为试探，引诱我们离开神。
- **「生命的冠冕」：** 冠冕就是生命本身，就是永生。这是神「应许给那些爱他之人的」，所以忍耐的根源不是意志力，而是爱神；我们爱，因为神先爱我们（约一4:19；参启2:10）。

**2. 「人被试探，不可说：『我是被神试探』」。** 「人被试探，不可说：『我是被神试探』；因为神不能被恶试探，他也不试探人。」（1:13）
- **人一犯罪就推卸责任：** 亚当说：「你所赐给我、与我同居的女人，她把那树上的果子给我，我就吃了。」（创3:12）他怪夏娃，最后也怪那位赐他夏娃的神。
- **神试验人，却不试探人：** 圣经说「神要试验亚伯拉罕」（创22:1），那是要成全他的信心。可是神从来不引诱人去犯罪。主祷文说「不叫我们遇见试探」（太6:13），是求神保守我们，不叫试探胜过我们。

**3. 「乃是被自己的私欲牵引诱惑的」。** 「但各人被试探，乃是被自己的私欲牵引诱惑的。私欲既怀了胎，就生出罪来；罪既长成，就生出死来。」（1:14-15）
- **钓鱼的比喻：** 「牵引诱惑」原是渔夫和猎人的用语：用饵把鱼引出藏身的地方，再一把钩住。试探的力量，在于它挂着我们心里本来就想要的东西。
- **怀胎、生产、长成：** 罪不是一下子发生的。我们先在心里接纳了私欲，它就「怀了胎」，然后生出罪的行为，最后长成，带来死亡。越早在心里拒绝它，越容易；而在那最早的一刻，我们也可以立刻转向那位我们求他「不叫我们遇见试探」的父。
- **这不是说魔鬼不存在：** 雅各也说「务要抵挡魔鬼」（4:7）。但他要我们看见：外面的引诱之所以有力，是因为里面有回应它的私欲。我不能说「都是环境逼的」或「我天生就是这个脾气」。

**4. 「各样美善的恩赐……都是从上头来的」。** 「我亲爱的弟兄们，不要看错了。各样美善的恩赐和各样全备的赏赐都是从上头来的，从众光之父那里降下来的；在他并没有改变，也没有转动的影儿。」（1:16-17）
- **「不要看错了」：** 我们最容易看错的，就是神。我们会以为神给我们设了陷阱，或者像有些人说的「命该如此」「老天在整我」；其实圣经的神不是命运，从他那里来的，只有美善的东西。神也许让试炼临到，却是为了成全我们，从来不是为了引诱我们犯罪。
- **「众光之父」：** 神是日月星辰的创造者（创1:14-18）。
- **「没有转动的影儿」：** 太阳会落下，月亮有圆缺，日晷的影子一直在移动；神却永不改变（玛3:6；诗102:27）。他昨天怎样良善，今天也怎样良善。

**5. 「他按自己的旨意，用真道生了我们」。** 「他按自己的旨意，用真道生了我们，叫我们在他所造的万物中好像初熟的果子。」（1:18）
- **两种「生」：** 第15节说，私欲生出罪，罪生出死；第18节说，神用真道生了我们。第15节「生出死来」和第18节「生了我们」，原文用的是同一个动词。这是雅各书里很清楚的福音：我们的新生命不是自己产生的，而是神「按自己的旨意」赐的（参约1:13；彼前1:23）。
- **「真道」：** 就是福音，「所传给你们的福音就是这道」（彼前1:25）。也有少数学者认为这一节是讲神创造人类，但多数解经家认为是讲重生，因为「真道」和第21节「能救你们灵魂的道」相连，新约别处「真理的道」也都是指福音（弗1:13；西1:5）。
- **「初熟的果子」：** 以色列人收割的时候，要先把初熟的庄稼献给神（利23:10），表明整个收成都属于他。信徒是神新创造的第一批果子，将来神要更新万物（罗8:19-23）。

> 要点：试探从我自己的私欲而来，不是从神而来。从神那里来的只有美善的恩赐，其中最大的，就是他按自己的旨意，用福音的真道生了我们。

## 深读 1:19-27 听道和行道

**1. 「快快地听，慢慢地说，慢慢地动怒」。** 「我亲爱的弟兄们，这是你们所知道的。但你们各人要快快地听，慢慢地说，慢慢地动怒，因为人的怒气并不成就神的义。」（1:19-20）
- **先听神的话：** 上下文是讲领受神的道（18、21节），所以「快快地听」首先是指听神的话；当然也包括听别人说话。
- **「慢慢地说」：** 别人还没讲完，我们常常已经想好怎么回答了。雅各在第3章会详细讲舌头。
- **「人的怒气」：** 圣经没有说一切的怒气都是罪（弗4:26）。但我们发怒的时候，往往以为自己是在维护公义，其实多半是在维护自己的面子。这里的「神的义」，不是罗马书所讲神白白赐下、使人称义的义，而是神要我们活出来的公义。人的怒气，活不出这样的义。「回答柔和，使怒消退。」（箴15:1）
- **慢慢地说，不等于不说：** 这节经文不是叫受了欺负或伤害的人闭口不言，也不是要他们压下对罪恶的义愤。在家暴、虐待或其他犯罪面前，说出来、向可信任的人求助、向警察举报，都是对的（罗13:4）。教会和家人也不可用「慢慢地说，慢慢地动怒」来叫受害的人忍气吞声。

**2. 「存温柔的心领受那所栽种的道」。** 「所以，你们要脱去一切的污秽和盈余的邪恶，存温柔的心领受那所栽种的道，就是能救你们灵魂的道。」（1:21）
- **「脱去」：** 像脱去脏衣服一样。「盈余的邪恶」是说心里满溢出来的恶。
- **「所栽种的道」：** 神的道已经种在信徒心里了。这叫人想起神藉耶利米所应许的新约：「我要将我的律法放在他们里面，写在他们心上。」（耶31:33）我们要做的，是像好土一样，存温柔、受教的心去领受，让它长大。
- **「能救你们灵魂的道」：** 救我们的是神的道，不是我们的行为。

**3. 「要行道，不要单单听道」。** 「只是你们要行道，不要单单听道，自己欺哄自己。因为听道而不行道的，就像人对着镜子看自己本来的面目，看见，走后，随即忘了他的相貌如何。」（1:22-24）
- **「自己欺哄自己」：** 只听不行的人，骗的首先是自己。他以为听过了、知道了，就等于做到了。主耶稣说，听了不去行的人，好比把房子盖在沙土上（太7:26）。
- **照镜子的比喻：** 一个人早上照镜子，看见脸上有脏东西，却不去洗，转身就忘了。神的话像镜子一样，让我们看见自己真实的样子；如果看见了却不改变，读经就成了一种自我安慰。
- **这不是叫人少听：** 华人教会很重视讲道和查经，这是好的。雅各不是叫我们少听，而是叫我们听了以后要行。

**4. 「那全备、使人自由之律法」。** 「惟有详细察看那全备、使人自由之律法的，并且时常如此，这人既不是听了就忘，乃是实在行出来，就在他所行的事上必然得福。」（1:25）
- **「详细察看」：** 这个词原本是弯腰往里看的意思（约20:5、11；彼前1:12「天使也愿意详细察看」用同一个词）。照镜子的人也看了（23节），差别不在于看得多仔细，而在于看了以后是走开忘了，还是「时常如此」，并且行出来。
- **「使人自由之律法」：** 律法怎么会使人自由？许多解经家认为，雅各说的是神的律法，但这律法已经由主耶稣成全、解释，如今又因着福音写在信徒心上（耶31:33）。它和2:8「至尊的律法」、2:12「使人自由的律法」是相连的。对一个被神生出来的人，神的命令不是锁链，而是新生命自由呼吸的空间：「我要自由而行，因我素来考究你的训词。」（诗119:45）
- **「在他所行的事上必然得福」：** 人不是听完了才蒙福，而是在行出来的时候就蒙福。

**5. 「清洁没有玷污的虔诚」。** 「若有人自以为虔诚，却不勒住他的舌头，反欺哄自己的心，这人的虔诚是虚的。在神我们的父面前，那清洁没有玷污的虔诚，就是看顾在患难中的孤儿寡妇，并且保守自己不沾染世俗。」（1:26-27）
- **「虔诚」：** 这个词常指宗教生活外在的部分：聚会、禁食、祷告的样子。雅各说，真正的虔诚有三个记号：管住舌头，看顾软弱的人，不被世俗沾染。
- **「孤儿寡妇」：** 在古代，孤儿寡妇是最没有依靠的人。旧约一再说，神「为孤儿寡妇伸冤」（申10:18），「作孤儿的父，作寡妇的伸冤者」（诗68:5）。神怎样看顾他们，他的儿女也要怎样看顾他们。今天，这可能是教会里的单亲妈妈、独居的老人、父母在外打工的孩子。
- **「看顾」：** 主说「我病了，你们看顾我」（太25:36），用的也是这个词，就是亲自关心照顾。撒迦利亚也用这个词称颂神：「因他眷顾他的百姓，为他们施行救赎。」（路1:68）我们原是没有父的人，神先来眷顾了我们；所以我们去看顾孤儿寡妇，是把自己领受的爱传下去。
- **「不沾染世俗」：** 「世俗」是指不认识神、与神为敌的价值观（4:4）。雅各把关怀穷人和个人圣洁放在一起：一个人可以热心做公益，心里却和世人一样贪恋钱财和名声；也可以很注重个人的圣洁，对身边的穷人却视而不见。雅各两样都要。

> 要点：神的话是一面镜子，叫我们看见自己的真相；但这面镜子也是「使人自由之律法」，看见了不必逃开。那用真道生了我们的神，已经把道栽种在我们心里；他要叫这道一点一点长出来，叫我们勒住舌头，看顾软弱的人，保守自己不沾染世俗。

## 默想

**默想：** 「人被试探，不可说：『我是被神试探』……他按自己的旨意，用真道生了我们。」（1:13、18）我很少直接说「是神试探我」，可是心里常有类似的话：要不是公司这样逼我，我也不会说谎；要不是他先惹我，我也不会发那么大的脾气；我就是这样的人，改不了；神既然把我造成这样，就不能怪我。这些话和亚当在园子里说的一样：「你所赐给我、与我同居的女人……」（创3:12）我把罪推给别人，推给环境，最后推给神。雅各却要我诚实地承认：试探之所以能钩住我，是因为我心里本来就想要那块饵。私欲在我里面怀了胎，生出了罪。这不是说别人对我的伤害不算数；他们的罪是他们的，神会公义地审断。我要承认的，只是我自己的那一份。这样承认很难，因为我怕一承认，就只剩下定罪。可是雅各紧接着说：「不要看错了。」从神那里来的，从来没有陷阱，只有「各样美善的恩赐」；最美的恩赐，就是他「按自己的旨意，用真道生了我们」。私欲生出罪和死，神却生出新的生命。私欲在我里面生出的，是罪和死；可是那不是我最深的身份。在基督里，我是神亲自生的儿女，是他的「初熟的果子」。主耶稣是真神，也是真人；他以真实的人性在旷野「受魔鬼的试探」（太4:1），「只是他没有犯罪」（来4:15）。亚当把自己的罪推给别人；主耶稣自己没有罪，却在十字架上把我推不掉的罪担在自己身上。我最怕的那个定罪，已经落在他身上了。所以我不必再找借口来保护自己。我可以在神面前认罪：说出一件我一直推给别人的事，不加「可是」，也不加「要不是」，并且求他帮助我离开它；然后安静地记住：众光之父没有转动的影儿，他今天待我，和他用真道生我的那一天一样好。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 falls into four parts. **Trials and wisdom** (vv. 2–8): count it all joy when you meet trials of various kinds, because the testing of faith produces steadfastness; whoever lacks wisdom should ask in faith of God, who gives generously to all. **The lowly and the rich** (vv. 9–11): let the lowly brother boast in his exaltation, and the rich in his humiliation, because like a flower of the grass he will pass away. **Trial and temptation** (vv. 12–18): blessed is the one who remains steadfast under trial; no one should say he is tempted by God, for each is enticed by his own desire; every good gift comes down from the Father of lights, who brought us forth by the word of truth. **Hearing and doing** (vv. 19–27): be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger; receive with meekness the implanted word; be doers of the word and not hearers only, like a man who looks in a mirror and walks away forgetting; pure religion is to visit orphans and widows in their affliction and to keep oneself unstained from the world.

This chapter works like the letter's table of contents. Trials, wisdom, rich and poor, speech, hearing and doing: the later chapters all come back to them. Running through it is a contrast between two births. Desire "when it has conceived gives birth to sin, and sin when it is fully grown brings forth death" (v. 15); but God "of his own will... brought us forth by the word of truth" (v. 18).

## Going Deeper: 1:2–8 Joy and Wisdom in Trials

**1. "Count it all joy."** "Count it all joy, my brothers, when you meet trials of various kinds" (1:2).
- **"Meet":** the word means to fall into, to be suddenly surrounded by, like the man going down from Jerusalem to Jericho who "fell among robbers" (Luke 10:30, the same word). Trials are seldom chosen; they arrive without warning.
- **"Of various kinds":** trials come in many forms: poverty, illness, oppression (2:6), the death of loved ones, work that goes wrong. James's readers were mostly poor believers scattered far from home; they knew what trials were.
- **"Count":** James does not tell us to pretend there is no pain, nor does he say that trials are good in themselves. "Count" is a judgment: I will look at this with the eyes of faith. This matches the Lord Jesus' words: "Blessed are you when others revile you and persecute you... Rejoice and be glad" (Matt 5:11–12).

**2. "The testing of your faith produces steadfastness."** "For you know that the testing of your faith produces steadfastness. And let steadfastness have its full effect, that you may be perfect and complete, lacking in nothing" (1:3–4).
- **"You know":** joy rests not on feelings but on knowing what God is doing. Paul says the same: "we rejoice in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces endurance" (Rom 5:3).
- **"Testing":** the word has the sense of assaying metal, proving it genuine. Peter says the tested genuineness of our faith is "more precious than gold that perishes though it is tested by fire" (1 Pet 1:7).
- **"Steadfastness":** not grim endurance through clenched teeth, but standing firm under pressure without leaving God. The word recurs in James (1:3, 4, 12; 5:11).
- **"Perfect and complete, lacking in nothing":** this is God's goal. "Perfect" can also mean "mature," and James uses the word often (1:4, 17, 25; 3:2). God's aim is not only to get us through trials but, through them, to make us whole.

**3. "Let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach."** "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him" (1:5).
- **Why wisdom here?** Verse 4 says "lacking in nothing"; verse 5 says "if any of you lacks." The Greek uses the same word. In trials, what we most lack is often not an explanation but wisdom: knowing how to fear God in this situation and what the next step is. This is the wisdom of Proverbs: "For the LORD gives wisdom" (Prov 2:6).
- **"Generously":** the word carries the sense of single-hearted, holding nothing back. When God gives, there is no calculation in his heart.
- **"Without reproach":** God does not give while scolding us: "You again? You should have understood by now." That is great comfort to anyone who feels they have too many problems and is embarrassed to ask again.

**4. "Let him ask in faith, with no doubting."** "But let him ask in faith, with no doubting, for the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea that is driven and tossed by the wind. For that person must not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord; he is a double-minded man, unstable in all his ways" (1:6–8).
- **"Doubting" here does not mean having questions:** James is not saying that anyone with a question in his heart will receive nothing. The Psalms are full of prayers that ask, "How long?" (Ps 13:1).
- **"Double-minded":** literally "two-souled." James may be the first writer in Greek literature to use the word. Such a person asks God while leaning on something else, a foot in each boat. His uncertainty is not whether God can, but whether he himself wants God. James returns to the word in 4:8: "purify your hearts, you double-minded."
- **"Unstable":** like a wave, he goes wherever the wind blows. James uses the same word in 3:8 for the tongue, "a restless evil."

> The point: Trials do not mean God has left us; they mean God is completing our faith. He did not spare his own Son but gave him up for us (Rom 8:32), so he will not abandon us in our trials. We can ask for wisdom from the God who "gives generously to all without reproach," without fearing that we are asking too much.

## Going Deeper: 1:9–11 The Lowly and the Rich

**1. "Let the lowly brother boast in his exaltation."** "Let the lowly brother boast in his exaltation" (1:9).
- **Poverty is also a trial:** James has just spoken of trials and now turns to rich and poor. For many of his readers, the most common trial was poverty.
- **"Exaltation":** in the world's eyes he has no standing; before God he is lifted up, a child of God, "rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom" (2:5). What he may boast in is not what he has, but that God has raised him up.

**2. "And the rich in his humiliation."** "And the rich in his humiliation, because like a flower of the grass he will pass away. For the sun rises with its scorching heat and withers the grass; its flower falls, and its beauty perishes. So also will the rich man fade away in the midst of his pursuits" (1:10–11).
- **Two readings:** interpreters differ on who "the rich" is. One view holds that he too is a believer: the rich brother is to boast in his "humiliation," that is, to admit that before God his wealth counts for nothing, and that he, like the poor brother, is a sinner who has received grace. Another view holds that he is not a believer (for example, Davids), and that the words are ironic: the only thing the rich man can "boast" of is that he will wither like a flower. James's sharp words to oppressive rich people in 5:1–6, and the judgment-like ring of "pass away" and "fade away," seem to support the second view; but the way verse 10 follows straight on from "the lowly brother" seems to support the first.
- **"Like a flower of the grass":** these are Isaiah's words: "All flesh is grass, and all its beauty is like the flower of the field. The grass withers, the flower fades... but the word of our God will stand forever" (Isa 40:6–8). In Palestine the spring wildflowers are beautiful, but when the hot wind blows they wither within days.
- **"In the midst of his pursuits":** the rich man may be busy with his business and suddenly be gone (see 4:13–14). Wealth cannot make anyone stand.

> The point: Before God, the poor need not feel small and the rich must not trust in themselves. The only thing we can boast in is God's grace; the only thing that stands forever is God's word.

## Going Deeper: 1:12–18 Trial and Temptation

**1. "Blessed is the man who remains steadfast under trial."** "Blessed is the man who remains steadfast under trial, for when he has stood the test he will receive the crown of life, which God has promised to those who love him" (1:12).
- **The same word:** the Greek word behind "trials" in verse 2 and "trial" here is also the word behind "tempted" in verse 13 (the Chinese Union Version already uses "temptation" here). The hardship outside us is a trial, through which God strengthens our faith; yet the same hardship can also become a temptation, luring us away from God.
- **"The crown of life":** the crown is life itself, eternal life. God has "promised" it "to those who love him," so the root of steadfastness is not willpower but love for God, and "we love because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19; see Rev 2:10).

**2. "Let no one say when he is tempted, 'I am being tempted by God.'"** "Let no one say when he is tempted, 'I am being tempted by God,' for God cannot be tempted with evil, and he himself tempts no one" (1:13).
- **We shift the blame as soon as we sin:** Adam said, "The woman whom you gave to be with me, she gave me fruit of the tree, and I ate" (Gen 3:12). He blamed Eve, and in the end blamed the God who gave him Eve.
- **God tests, but does not tempt:** Scripture says "God tested Abraham" (Gen 22:1), to bring his faith to maturity. But God never lures anyone into sin. When the Lord's Prayer says "lead us not into temptation" (Matt 6:13), it asks God to keep us so that temptation does not overcome us.

**3. "Lured and enticed by his own desire."** "But each person is tempted when he is lured and enticed by his own desire. Then desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin, and sin when it is fully grown brings forth death" (1:14–15).
- **A fishing image:** "lured and enticed" were words used by fishermen and hunters: drawing a fish out of its hiding place with bait, then hooking it. Temptation is powerful because its bait is something our hearts already want.
- **Conceived, born, fully grown:** sin does not happen all at once. We first welcome desire in the heart, and it "conceives," then gives birth to sinful acts, and finally grows up and brings death. The earlier it is refused in the heart, the easier it is to refuse; and in that first moment we can turn at once to the Father we ask to "lead us not into temptation."
- **This does not deny the devil:** James also says, "Resist the devil" (4:7). But he wants us to see that temptation outside has power because there is desire inside that answers it. I cannot say, "My circumstances forced me," or "I was just born with this temper."

**4. "Every good gift... is from above."** "Do not be deceived, my beloved brothers. Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights with whom there is no variation or shadow due to change" (1:16–17).
- **"Do not be deceived":** the one we most easily misjudge is God. We may imagine God has set a trap for us, or, as some say, "it was fated" or "heaven is out to get me"; but the God of the Bible is not fate, and nothing comes from him but good. God may allow trials, but to mature us, never to entice us to sin.
- **"The Father of lights":** God created the sun, moon and stars (Gen 1:14–18).
- **"No variation or shadow due to change":** the sun sets, the moon waxes and wanes, the shadow on a sundial is always moving; but God never changes (Mal 3:6; Ps 102:27). He was good yesterday, and he is good today.

**5. "Of his own will he brought us forth by the word of truth."** "Of his own will he brought us forth by the word of truth, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creation" (1:18).
- **Two births:** verse 15 says desire gives birth to sin and sin to death; verse 18 says God brought us forth by the word of truth. In Greek, "brings forth" (v. 15) and "brought forth" (v. 18) are the same verb. This is the gospel, plainly stated in James: our new life is not something we produce ourselves; God gives it "of his own will" (see John 1:13; 1 Pet 1:23).
- **"The word of truth":** that is, the gospel: "this word is the good news that was preached to you" (1 Pet 1:25). A few scholars take this verse to speak of God creating humanity, but most interpreters take it as new birth, because "the word of truth" is linked with "the implanted word, which is able to save your souls" in verse 21, and elsewhere in the New Testament "the word of truth" is the gospel (Eph 1:13; Col 1:5).
- **"Firstfruits":** at harvest, Israel was to bring the first of the crop to God (Lev 23:10), showing that the whole harvest belonged to him. Believers are the first fruits of God's new creation; one day God will renew all things (Rom 8:19–23).

> The point: Temptation comes from my own desire, not from God. Nothing comes from God but good gifts, and the greatest of them is that, of his own will, he brought us forth by the word of truth, the gospel.

## Going Deeper: 1:19–27 Hearing and Doing

**1. "Quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger."** "Know this, my beloved brothers: let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger; for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness that God requires" (1:19–20).
- **First, hear God's word:** the context is receiving God's word (vv. 18, 21), so "quick to hear" means first of all hearing God's word, though it of course includes listening to others.
- **"Slow to speak":** we often have our answer ready before the other person has finished. James will say much more about the tongue in chapter 3.
- **"The anger of man":** Scripture does not say that all anger is sin (Eph 4:26). But when we are angry, we usually think we are defending what is right, when mostly we are defending our own face. Here "the righteousness that God requires" is not the righteousness God credits to us in justification, as in Romans. It is the right living God wants, which human anger cannot produce. "A soft answer turns away wrath" (Prov 15:1).
- **Slow to speak is not the same as silent:** this verse does not tell those who have been mistreated or harmed to keep quiet, or to bury their anger at evil. In the face of domestic violence, abuse or other crimes, speaking up, seeking help from someone trustworthy and reporting to the police are right (Rom 13:4). Churches and families must not use "slow to speak, slow to anger" to make victims swallow what was done to them.

**2. "Receive with meekness the implanted word."** "Therefore put away all filthiness and rampant wickedness and receive with meekness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls" (1:21).
- **"Put away":** like taking off dirty clothes. "Rampant wickedness" is evil overflowing from the heart.
- **"The implanted word":** God's word has already been planted in believers' hearts, recalling the new covenant God promised through Jeremiah: "I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their hearts" (Jer 31:33). Our part is to receive it with a meek, teachable heart, like good soil, and let it grow.
- **"Able to save your souls":** what saves us is God's word, not our works.

**3. "Be doers of the word, and not hearers only."** "But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves. For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man who looks intently at his natural face in a mirror. For he looks at himself and goes away and at once forgets what he was like" (1:22–24).
- **"Deceiving yourselves":** the first person a hearer-only deceives is himself. He thinks that having heard and known is the same as having done. The Lord Jesus said that whoever hears his words and does not do them is like a man who built his house on the sand (Matt 7:26).
- **The mirror:** a man looks in the mirror in the morning, sees dirt on his face, does not wash it, turns away and forgets. God's word is like a mirror that shows us what we really are; if we see and do not change, Bible reading becomes a kind of self-comfort.
- **This is not a call to listen less:** Chinese churches value preaching and Bible study highly, and that is good. James does not tell us to hear less, but to do what we hear.

**4. "The perfect law, the law of liberty."** "But the one who looks into the perfect law, the law of liberty, and perseveres, being no hearer who forgets but a doer who acts, he will be blessed in his doing" (1:25).
- **"Looks into":** the word originally meant to stoop and look in (John 20:5, 11; 1 Pet 1:12, "things into which angels long to look"). The man at the mirror also looked intently (v. 23). The difference is not how carefully each one looks, but whether he walks away and forgets, or "perseveres" and does it.
- **"The law of liberty":** how can law set people free? Many interpreters hold that the law James means is God's law as fulfilled and interpreted by the Lord Jesus, now written on believers' hearts through the gospel (Jer 31:33); it is linked with "the royal law" (2:8) and is the standard of judgment in 2:12. For one whom God has brought to birth, his commands are not chains but room for the new life to breathe: "and I shall walk in a wide place, for I have sought your precepts" (Ps 119:45).
- **"Blessed in his doing":** the blessing is not only in the hearing, but in the doing.

**5. "Religion that is pure and undefiled."** "If anyone thinks he is religious and does not bridle his tongue but deceives his heart, this person's religion is worthless. Religion that is pure and undefiled before God and the Father is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained from the world" (1:26–27).
- **"Religion":** the word often refers to the outward side of religious life: worship, fasting, the forms of prayer. James says true religion has three marks: a governed tongue, care for the weak, and a life unstained by the world.
- **"Orphans and widows":** in the ancient world they were the most unprotected people. The Old Testament says again and again that God "executes justice for the fatherless and the widow" (Deut 10:18), and is "Father of the fatherless and protector of widows" (Ps 68:5). As God cares for them, so must his children. Today they may be the single mothers in our church, the elderly who live alone, the children whose parents work far away.
- **"Visit":** it is the word Jesus uses in "I was sick and you visited me" (Matt 25:36): personal care. Zechariah uses the same word of God: "for he has visited and redeemed his people" (Luke 1:68). We were the fatherless whom God came to visit first; when we visit orphans and widows, we pass on the love we were given.
- **"Unstained from the world":** "the world" means the values that do not know God and are hostile to him (4:4). James puts care for the poor and personal holiness side by side. A person can be zealous for good causes while his heart craves money and status as the world does; another can care much about personal holiness while not seeing the poor beside him. James wants both.

> The point: God's word is a mirror that shows us the truth about ourselves; but it is also "the law of liberty," so we need not run from what we see. The God who brought us forth by the word of truth has planted that word in us, and he will make it grow, little by little, into a governed tongue, care for the weak, and a life unstained by the world.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Let no one say when he is tempted, 'I am being tempted by God'... Of his own will he brought us forth by the word of truth" (1:13, 18). I seldom say outright, "God is tempting me," but my heart often says something like it: if my company did not push me so hard, I would not have lied; if he had not provoked me first, I would not have lost my temper like that; this is just who I am, I can't change, and since God made me like this, I cannot be blamed. These are the words Adam spoke in the garden: "The woman whom you gave to be with me..." (Gen 3:12). I push my sin onto others, onto my circumstances, and finally onto God. James wants me to admit honestly that temptation hooks me because my heart already wants the bait. Desire conceived in me and gave birth to sin. This does not mean the wrongs done to me do not count; their sin is theirs, and God will judge it justly. What I am to own is only my part. That is hard to admit, because I fear that once I admit it, only condemnation will be left. But James goes straight on: "Do not be deceived." Nothing that comes from God is a trap. Only "every good gift" comes from him, and the best gift is that "of his own will he brought us forth by the word of truth." Desire gives birth to sin and death; God gives birth to new life. What desire produces in me is sin and death; but that is not who I most deeply am. In Christ I am a child God himself brought forth, one of his "firstfruits." The Lord Jesus, truly God and truly man, was "tempted by the devil" in the wilderness in his true humanity (Matt 4:1), "yet without sin" (Heb 4:15). Adam pushed his own sin onto someone else; Jesus, who had no sin of his own, took on himself on the cross the sin I could not shift onto anyone else. The condemnation I am afraid of has already fallen on him. So I no longer need excuses to protect myself. I can confess before God: name one thing I have kept blaming on others, with no "but" and no "if only," and ask him to help me turn from it. Then I can quietly remember that the Father of lights has no shadow due to change; he is as good to me today as on the day he brought me forth by the word of truth.
