## 本章脉络

第二章分成两大段。**不可按着外貌待人**（1-13节）：信奉荣耀的主耶稣基督，便不可偏心；神拣选了世上的贫穷人，叫他们在信上富足；要全守「爱人如己」这至尊的律法，因为在一条上跌倒，就是犯了众条；要照使人自由的律法说话行事，因为怜悯向审判夸胜。**信心和行为**（14-26节）：说自己有信心却没有行为，这信心不能救人；信心若没有行为就是死的；鬼魔也信，却是战惊；亚伯拉罕献以撒，妓女喇合接待使者，都是信心与行为并行的例子；身体没有灵魂是死的，信心没有行为也是死的。

两段的连接，是第一章最后的话：「清洁没有玷污的虔诚，就是看顾在患难中的孤儿寡妇。」（1:27）偏心待人的人，看不起穷人（2:6）；只有嘴上信心的人，对饥寒的弟兄姊妹说「平平安安地去吧」（2:16）。这两种人有一个共同点：他们自称信主，却看不见身边有需要的人。

## 深读 2:1-7 不可按着外貌待人

**1. 「荣耀的主耶稣基督」。** 「我的弟兄们，你们信奉我们荣耀的主耶稣基督，便不可按着外貌待人。」（2:1）
- **「按着外貌待人」：** 原文是一个词，直译是接受脸面，就是看人的面子、身份来决定怎样待他。旧约说，神「不以貌取人，也不受贿赂」（申10:17），审判的时候「不可偏护穷人，也不可重看有势力的人」（利19:15）。
- **「荣耀的主」：** 雅各在这里称耶稣为荣耀的主。这句话的原文不容易译。有人理解为荣耀之主，就是荣耀所属的那位主（参林前2:8「荣耀的主」；诗24:10「万军之耶和华，他是荣耀的王」）；有人理解为形容词，就是我们这位满有荣耀的主；也有人认为雅各是直接称耶稣为那荣耀，就是旧约中神同在的荣光。无论哪一种，雅各都把旧约中属于耶和华的荣耀归给耶稣。雅各从前不信自己的这位哥哥（约7:5），如今却称他为荣耀的主。雅各的意思也很清楚：真正的荣耀在耶稣身上，不在金戒指和华美的衣服上。信奉荣耀的主的人，怎能再去巴结人的荣耀呢？

**2. 「请坐在这好位上」。** 「若有一个人带着金戒指，穿着华美衣服，进你们的会堂去；又有一个穷人穿着肮脏衣服也进去；你们就重看那穿华美衣服的人，说：『请坐在这好位上』；又对那穷人说：『你站在那里』，或『坐在我脚凳下边。』这岂不是你们偏心待人，用恶意断定人吗？」（2:2-4）
- **「会堂」：** 原文就是「会堂」这个词，也可以泛指聚会。这可能反映早期犹太信徒聚会的样子（雅各在5:14也用「教会」一词）。有些学者（如沃德，1969年）认为，这里描写的是教会处理纠纷的聚会：犹太拉比的著作讨论过法庭上贫富双方的衣着和座位，下文也讲到「断定人」和「公堂」（4、6节），利19:15本身也是审判的处境。另一些学者认为，这是一般的崇拜聚会。无论哪一种，教会都对有钱人另眼相看。
- **今天的样子：** 我们可能不会叫人坐在脚凳下边，可是会不会先招呼开好车的新朋友？会不会让有学历、有职位的人当执事，却很少问那位做清洁工的弟兄的意见？会不会对说话有口音、穿着随便的人客气却冷淡？
- **「用恶意断定人」：** 偏心不是小事，而是把自己放在审判官的位子上，用世人的标准衡量人的价值。

**3. 「神岂不是拣选了世上的贫穷人」。** 「我亲爱的弟兄们，请听，神岂不是拣选了世上的贫穷人，叫他们在信上富足，并承受他所应许给那些爱他之人的国吗？」（2:5）
- **神的眼光：** 主耶稣说：「你们贫穷的人有福了！因为神的国是你们的。」（路6:20；参太5:3）保罗也说：「神却拣选了世上愚拙的，叫有智慧的羞愧。」（林前1:27）
- **不是说贫穷能救人：** 雅各不是说穷人因为穷就得救，而是说神常常拣选世人看不起的人，叫他们「在信上富足」。教会若看不起穷人，就是看不起神所拣选的人。

**4. 「你们反倒羞辱贫穷人」。** 「你们反倒羞辱贫穷人。那富足人岂不是欺压你们、拉你们到公堂去吗？他们不是亵渎你们所敬奉的尊名吗？」（2:6-7）
- **讨好欺压自己的人：** 雅各指出一件很荒谬的事：读者天天受富人欺压，被拉到公堂，可是富人一进教会，他们又去讨好他。人往往希望靠近有权势的人，自己也能沾一点光。
- **「你们所敬奉的尊名」：** 原文直译是那称呼在你们身上的尊名。旧约用这种说法表示归属某人（申28:10；摩9:12，雅各在徒15:17引用过这节）；许多解经家认为，这是指信徒受洗时奉主耶稣的名，从此归在他名下。信徒属于这位荣耀的主，就不必再向人的权势低头。
- **偏心最严重的样子：** 教会如果因为一个人奉献多、地位高、是领袖，就替他掩盖错误，这就是最严重的偏心。若有人在教会中受到性侵、虐待或其他犯罪的伤害，无论施害的人是谁，都要向警察或有关部门举报（罗13:4），保护受害的人；教会绝不可为了面子、为了「家丑不可外扬」而偏护有权势的人。雅各责备的是富人利用法庭欺压穷人，不是说受害的人不可寻求法律的保护。受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必和不安全的人和好；即使受害人愿意饶恕，也不等于施害的人可以恢复职分，或再接触受害人和孩子。

> 要点：信奉荣耀的主的人，不必再巴结人的荣耀。神拣选了世人看不起的人，我们自己也在其中；蒙了这样的拣选，教会就能用神的眼光看人。

## 深读 2:8-13 至尊的律法和怜悯

**1. 「要爱人如己」。** 「经上记着说：『要爱人如己。』你们若全守这至尊的律法才是好的。但你们若按外貌待人，便是犯罪，被律法定为犯法的。」（2:8-9）
- **「至尊的律法」：** 原文直译是王的律法。称为王的律法，可能是因为这是天国之王耶稣所确认的律法：他说爱神和爱人如己「这两条诫命是律法和先知一切道理的总纲」（太22:40）；也可能是因为这条律法是一切诫命中最高的。也有人指出，王的这个词和上文「国」（5节）是同一个词根，意思是天国的律法，就是主耶稣所教导、以爱人如己为中心的律法。保罗也说：「全律法都包在『爱人如己』这一句话之内了。」（加5:14）
- **偏心也是违背律法：** 「要爱人如己」出于利未记19:18，而同一章也说「不可偏护穷人，也不可重看有势力的人」（利19:15）。有人可能以为，对有钱人客气是爱他；雅各说，只爱一部分人就不是爱人如己。

**2. 「只在一条上跌倒，他就是犯了众条」。** 「因为凡遵守全律法的，只在一条上跌倒，他就是犯了众条。原来那说『不可奸淫』的，也说『不可杀人』；你就是不奸淫，却杀人，仍是成了犯律法的。」（2:10-11）
- **律法是一个整体：** 律法不像一串珠子，断了一颗，其余的还在；它更像一块玻璃，打破一处，整块就破了。所有的诫命都出于同一位神，违背其中一条，就是违背那位颁布律法的神。
- **没有人能靠律法站立：** 保罗也引用申命记说：「凡不常照律法书上所记一切之事去行的，就被咒诅。」（加3:10）雅各在这里虽然是讲偏心的问题，他的话却叫我们看见：没有人能凭自己遵守律法而在神面前站立得住（参3:2「我们在许多事上都有过失」）。

**3. 「怜悯原是向审判夸胜」。** 「你们既然要按使人自由的律法受审判，就该照这律法说话行事。因为那不怜悯人的，也要受无怜悯的审判；怜悯原是向审判夸胜。」（2:12-13）
- **「受审判」：** 信徒也要站在神面前交账（罗14:10-12）。这里「使人自由的律法」，就是1:25所说的律法。我们怎样待人，显明我们是否真领受了神的怜悯。
- **「那不怜悯人的，也要受无怜悯的审判」：** 主耶稣讲过那个蒙主人免了一千万银子、却不肯免同伴十两银子的恶仆（太18:23-35）。主人问他：「你不应当怜恤你的同伴，像我怜恤你吗？」（太18:33）不怜悯人的人显明，他从来没有真正明白自己蒙了多大的怜悯。
- **「怜悯原是向审判夸胜」：** 这是全章最有盼望的一句话。按着律法，我们都要被定罪。这里的「怜悯」是谁的怜悯？许多解经家认为是信徒向人所行的怜悯（对照上半节「那不怜悯人的」），常怜悯人的人，到审判时可以坦然无惧。也有人认为是神的怜悯。两种读法其实连在一起：人能怜悯人，是因为先蒙了神的怜悯。人的怜悯不是得救的根据，而是蒙怜悯的凭据。神的怜悯怎样胜过审判？不是神不理会罪，而是他的儿子在十字架上承受了审判，好叫我们得着怜悯。蒙怜悯的人，也会去怜悯人；这样的人到了审判的日子，不必惧怕（太5:7）。怜悯人也不等于掩盖罪，或叫受害的人留在危险中。

> 要点：没有人能靠守律法站立，因为在一条上跌倒，就是犯了众条。可是怜悯向审判夸胜；那位为我们承受审判的主，叫我们这蒙了怜悯的人，也去怜悯人。

## 深读 2:14-19 没有行为的信心

**1. 「这信心能救他吗？」** 「我的弟兄们，若有人说自己有信心，却没有行为，有什么益处呢？这信心能救他吗？」（2:14）
- **「说」自己有信心：** 雅各特意说「若有人说自己有信心」，重点在「说」字。他要检验的，是一个人口里所说的信心是不是真的。
- **「这信心」：** 许多解经家认为，原文的冠词指前面所说的那种信心，就是没有行为的信心。雅各不是说信心不能救人，而是说这一种信心不能救人。

**2. 「平平安安地去吧」。** 「若是弟兄或是姊妹，赤身露体，又缺了日用的饮食；你们中间有人对他们说：『平平安安地去吧！愿你们穿得暖，吃得饱』，却不给他们身体所需用的，这有什么益处呢？这样，信心若没有行为就是死的。」（2:15-17）
- **很属灵的话：** 「平平安安地去吧」是当时常用的祝福语。这个人没有骂人，没有拒绝，甚至说了祝福的话；可是他明明有能力帮助，却让弟兄姊妹饿着肚子回去。今天我们大概会说：「我会为你代祷。」代祷是好的；可是如果我明明能帮一把，代祷就成了我不必伸手的理由。约翰也说：「凡有世上财物的，看见弟兄穷乏，却塞住怜恤的心，爱神的心怎能存在他里面呢？」（约一3:17）
- **「死的」：** 没有行为的信心不是比较弱的信心，而是死的信心。就像一个人有身体的样子，却没有气息。这也不是说，行为不多、常常跌倒的信心就是死的。雅各针对的，是从来不打算动手的那种口头信心；软弱的信心若是投靠基督，救人的是基督，不是信心的大小。

**3. 「你有信心，我有行为」。** 「必有人说：『你有信心，我有行为；你将你没有行为的信心指给我看，我便藉着我的行为，将我的信心指给你看。』」（2:18）
- **难解的一节：** 原文没有引号，反对者的话到哪里结束，解经家有不同的看法。和合本把整节都放在引号里；许多现代译本只把「你有信心，我有行为」当作反对者的话，下面是雅各的回答。许多解经家认为，反对者的意思是：信心和行为是两种不同的恩赐，有人有这个，有人有那个。雅各回答说：在人面前，信心是看不见的，只能藉着行为显明出来。

**4. 「鬼魔也信，却是战惊」。** 「你信神只有一位，你信的不错；鬼魔也信，却是战惊。」（2:19）
- **「神只有一位」：** 这是犹太人每天诵读的信仰告白：「以色列啊，你要听！耶和华－我们神是独一的主。」（申6:4）
- **鬼魔的「信」：** 鬼魔比许多人更清楚耶稣是谁：「我知道你是谁，乃是神的圣者。」（可1:24）可是它们的「信」只是知道，不是投靠；只叫它们战惊，不叫它们爱神。
- **知道不等于信靠：** 一个人可以在教会长大，主日学的答案都会背，可以认同正统的教义，可以背出信经，却从来没有把自己交托给基督。雅各所批评的「信」，正是这种头脑上的认同。

> 要点：口里承认的信心，若从来没有结出爱心的果子，就不是救人的信心，而是死的信心。连鬼魔也知道神是谁；真信心却会投靠神，也会伸手去帮助有需要的人。

## 深读 2:20-26 亚伯拉罕和喇合

**1. 「亚伯拉罕把他儿子以撒献在坛上」。** 「虚浮的人哪，你愿意知道没有行为的信心是死的吗？我们的祖宗亚伯拉罕把他儿子以撒献在坛上，岂不是因行为称义吗？可见，信心是与他的行为并行，而且信心因着行为才得成全。这就应验经上所说：『亚伯拉罕信神，这就算为他的义。』他又得称为神的朋友。」（2:20-23）
- **「死的」：** 有些古卷作「死的」（和合本跟随这些古卷），较早的古卷作无用的（*argē*），和「行为」（*ergōn*）是谐音，好像在说：没有行为的信心，是不作工的信心。两种读法意思相近：没有行为的信心，不能做成信心该做的事。
- **两件事的次序：** 「亚伯拉罕信神，这就算为他的义」记在创世记15:6，那时以撒还没有出生；献以撒记在创世记22章，是多年以后的事。所以雅各不是说，亚伯拉罕因为献以撒才开始被神算为义。他是说，亚伯拉罕早先的信心，后来在献以撒的事上显明出来，经文的话就「应验」了。
- **「信心因着行为才得成全」：** 就像一棵树结了果子，才显明它是活的。神在摩利亚山上说：「现在我知道你是敬畏神的了。」（创22:12）
- **「神的朋友」：** 旧约称亚伯拉罕为神的朋友（代下20:7；赛41:8）。信心不只是一套信念，而是与神亲近的关系。

**2. 「人称义是因着行为，不是单因着信」。** 「这样看来，人称义是因着行为，不是单因着信。」（2:24）这是新约中最难的经文之一，因为保罗说：「人称义是因着信，不在乎遵行律法。」（罗3:28）而且两个人都用亚伯拉罕作例子（罗4:2-3）。本研读在导读中已经简单讲过，这里再看三个关键词：
- **「信」：** 雅各在这里批评的「信」，是鬼魔也有的那种知道（19节）；保罗所说的信，是「信称罪人为义的神」（罗4:5），是投靠。
- **「行为」：** 保罗所排除的，是把人的任何行为当作称义的根据（罗4:4-5；弗2:9）；雅各所说的行为，是信心生出来的爱心行动，就像亚伯拉罕的顺服、喇合的接待。
- **「称义」：** 保罗讲的是罪人怎样在神面前被宣告为义。称义的根据只在基督：他为我们成就了义，又替我们受了刑罚；这义藉着信归算给我们（罗3:24-26；4:5）。至于雅各所说的称义，改革宗和路德宗传统认为，是指这义怎样显明出来（如加尔文）；也有不少福音派解经家认为，是指神在末日审判时的宣告，那时行为是真信心的凭据，不是称义的根据（如穆尔）。天主教传统认为，人最初的称义是白白的恩典（天特会议第六会期，第八章），又引用这一节，教导称义以后，人可以藉着行善使所领受的义增长（第十章）。

无论怎样理解，雅各和保罗都同意：人得救是本乎恩，也因着信，而真信心一定会生出行为来。保罗说我们得救「不是出于行为」，紧接着又说我们是「为要叫我们行善」而造的（弗2:9-10）。改教家常说（如加尔文）：叫人称义的只是信心，但叫人称义的信心不是孤单的。

**3. 「妓女喇合」。** 「妓女喇合接待使者，又放他们从别的路上出去，不也是一样因行为称义吗？身体没有灵魂是死的，信心没有行为也是死的。」（2:25-26）
- **两个很不一样的人：** 亚伯拉罕是以色列人的祖宗，是神的朋友；喇合是外邦人，是妓女。雅各特意把这两个人放在一起：信心不看出身。后来喇合还被列在主耶稣的家谱中（太1:5）。雅各称许的，是她接待、送走探子，并不是称许她说谎。
- **喇合的信心：** 她对探子说：「耶和华－你们的神本是上天下地的神。」（书2:11）她冒着生命危险接待探子，信心就显在行动上。希伯来书也说：「妓女喇合因着信，曾和和平平地接待探子。」（来11:31）
- **「身体没有灵魂是死的」：** 雅各的比喻很特别：他把信心比作身体，把行为比作使身体活着的气息（灵魂）。他不是说行为比信心更重要，而是说：一个从来没有行为的信心，就像一个没有气息的身体，只剩下外形，其实已经死了。

> 要点：神算亚伯拉罕为义，是藉着信（创15:6）；他的信心是真的，又在献以撒的时候显明出来。无论是神的朋友，还是妓女喇合，救人的信心都是活的，会结出顺服和爱心的果子。亚伯拉罕敢把以撒放在坛上，是因为他信「神必自己预备作燔祭的羊羔」（创22:8）；神后来果然没有留下自己的儿子。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我的弟兄们，你们信奉我们荣耀的主耶稣基督，便不可按着外貌待人……怜悯原是向审判夸胜。」（2:1、13）我很少觉得自己偏心。可是回想一下，在教会门口，我先跟谁打招呼？婚宴上安排座位，我把谁放在主桌，把谁放在靠门口的那一桌？小组里谁的意见我会认真听，谁一开口，我心里就先打了折扣？我心里有一张看不见的排名表：学历、收入、口音。我对排名靠前的人热情，对靠后的人客气，却不太想走近。雅各说，这是「用恶意断定人」，在神面前是罪（9节），不只是待人不够周到。为什么我会这样？因为我要面子，想靠近别人的荣耀，好让自己也显得体面一点。可是雅各说，真正的荣耀只有一位：「荣耀的主耶稣基督」。这位荣耀的主，「本来富足，却为你们成了贫穷」（林后8:9）。他降生在马槽里，「没有枕头的地方」（太8:20），最后在十字架上被剥去衣服。他自己选了最后的位子。而我呢？我在神面前，其实正是那个穿着肮脏衣服进来的人。先知撒迦利亚看见大祭司约书亚「穿着污秽的衣服站在使者面前」，耶和华的使者却吩咐说：「你们要脱去他污秽的衣服」；又说：「我使你脱离罪孽，要给你穿上华美的衣服。」（亚3:3-4）神没有叫我站在一边，也没有叫我坐在他脚凳下边；他为我脱去污秽，给我穿上基督的义，又请我上坐。怜悯向审判夸胜，在我身上已经是事实了。所以我不必再靠别人的荣耀来证明自己。下一次在教会门口，我可以先走向那个最不起眼、最没有人招呼的人；不是为了显得自己好，而是因为我自己进门的时候，也是穿着污秽的衣服；那位自己选了末位的主，却迎上来对我说：「朋友，请上座。」（路14:10）

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 falls into two main parts. **No partiality** (vv. 1–13): those who hold the faith in the glorious Lord Jesus Christ must not show favoritism; God chose the poor of the world to be rich in faith; fulfill the royal law, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself," because whoever fails in one point is guilty of all; speak and act as those to be judged under the law of liberty, because mercy triumphs over judgment. **Faith and works** (vv. 14–26): if someone says he has faith but has no works, that faith cannot save him; faith without works is dead; even the demons believe and shudder; Abraham offering Isaac and Rahab receiving the messengers show faith working together with works; as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead.

The two parts are joined by the last words of chapter 1: "Religion that is pure and undefiled... is this: to visit orphans and widows in their affliction" (1:27). Those who show partiality dishonor the poor (2:6); those whose faith is only words say "Go in peace" to a brother or sister without food or clothing (2:16). Both have something in common: they claim to believe, yet they do not see the needy people right beside them.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–7 Show No Partiality

**1. "Our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory."** "My brothers, show no partiality as you hold the faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory" (2:1).
- **"Partiality":** the Greek word literally means "receiving the face," deciding how to treat someone by their face, their standing. The Old Testament says God "is not partial and takes no bribe" (Deut 10:17), and in judging, "You shall not be partial to the poor or defer to the great" (Lev 19:15).
- **"The Lord of glory":** James calls Jesus the Lord of glory. The Greek phrase is not easy to translate. Some read it literally as "the Lord of glory" (compare 1 Cor 2:8; Ps 24:10, "The LORD of hosts, he is the King of glory!"); some as "our glorious Lord"; others think it means that Jesus himself is "the Glory," the glory of God's presence in the Old Testament. On any reading, James gives Jesus the glory the Old Testament ascribes to the LORD; the brother he once did not believe in (John 7:5) he now calls the Lord of glory. And James's point is clear: true glory rests on Jesus, not on gold rings and fine clothing. How can those who hold the faith in the Lord of glory go chasing human glory?

**2. "You sit here in a good place."** "For if a man wearing a gold ring and fine clothing comes into your assembly, and a poor man in shabby clothing also comes in, and if you pay attention to the one who wears the fine clothing and say, 'You sit here in a good place,' while you say to the poor man, 'You stand over there,' or, 'Sit down at my feet,' have you not then made distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil thoughts?" (2:2–4).
- **"Assembly":** the Greek word is *synagōgē*, which can simply mean "gathering," and may reflect what early Jewish believers' meetings looked like (James also uses "church," 5:14). Some scholars (for example, Roy Bowen Ward, 1969) think this describes a church meeting to settle disputes, pointing to rabbinic rules about rich and poor litigants' dress and seating, the "judges" and "court" of verses 4 and 6, and the judicial setting of Lev 19:15. Others take it as an ordinary gathering for worship. Either way, the church is treating the rich man differently.
- **What it looks like today:** we may never tell anyone to sit at our feet. But do we greet the newcomer with the nice car first? Do we make deacons of those with degrees and positions, yet seldom ask the opinion of the brother who works as a cleaner? Are we polite but cool toward people with an accent or casual clothes?
- **"Judges with evil thoughts":** partiality is not a small thing. It puts me in the judge's seat, measuring a person's worth by the world's standards.

**3. "Has not God chosen those who are poor in the world?"** "Listen, my beloved brothers, has not God chosen those who are poor in the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom, which he has promised to those who love him?" (2:5).
- **God's way of seeing:** the Lord Jesus said, "Blessed are you who are poor, for yours is the kingdom of God" (Luke 6:20; see Matt 5:3). Paul says, "But God chose what is foolish in the world to shame the wise" (1 Cor 1:27).
- **Not that poverty saves:** James is not saying the poor are saved because they are poor. He is saying that God often chooses those the world looks down on, and makes them "rich in faith." A church that despises the poor despises those God has chosen.

**4. "But you have dishonored the poor man."** "But you have dishonored the poor man. Are not the rich the ones who oppress you, and the ones who drag you into court? Are they not the ones who blaspheme the honorable name by which you were called?" (2:6–7).
- **Flattering those who oppress you:** James points out something absurd. His readers were oppressed by the rich every day and dragged into court, yet when a rich man walked into church, they rushed to please him. People often hope that by getting close to the powerful, some of their shine will rub off.
- **"The honorable name by which you were called":** literally "the honorable name called over you." The Old Testament uses this idiom for belonging to someone (Deut 28:10; Amos 9:12, which James quotes in Acts 15:17); many take it to refer to baptism in the name of the Lord Jesus, marking believers as his. Believers belong to the Lord of glory, so they need not bow to human power.
- **Partiality at its worst:** if a church covers up a person's wrongdoing because he gives a lot, has high standing or is a leader, that is partiality at its worst. If anyone in the church has suffered sexual assault, abuse or another crime, it must be reported to the police or proper authorities whoever the offender is (Rom 13:4), and the one harmed must be protected. The church must never shield the powerful to save face or because "family shame should stay in the family." James condemns the rich for using the courts to oppress the poor; he is not forbidding victims to seek the law's protection. Victims need not stay in danger or reconcile with someone unsafe, and forgiveness does not restore an offender's office or give him access to victims or children.

> The point: Those who hold the faith in the Lord of glory no longer need to court human glory. God chose those the world looks down on, and we are among them; chosen like that, the church can see people as God sees them.

## Going Deeper: 2:8–13 The Royal Law and Mercy

**1. "You shall love your neighbor as yourself."** "If you really fulfill the royal law according to the Scripture, 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself,' you are doing well. But if you show partiality, you are committing sin and are convicted by the law as transgressors" (2:8–9).
- **"The royal law":** the Greek is literally "the law of the king." It may be called this because Jesus, the King of the kingdom, affirmed it: love of God and love of neighbor are the two commandments on which "depend all the Law and the Prophets" (Matt 22:40). Or it may be because this law ranks highest among the commandments. Others note that "royal" picks up "heirs of the kingdom" (v. 5): this is the law of the kingdom as Jesus taught it, centred on the love command. Paul says, "For the whole law is fulfilled in one word: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself'" (Gal 5:14).
- **Partiality breaks the law too:** "You shall love your neighbor as yourself" comes from Leviticus 19:18, and the same chapter says, "You shall not be partial to the poor or defer to the great" (Lev 19:15). Someone might think that being courteous to the rich is loving him; James says that loving only some people is not loving your neighbor as yourself.

**2. "Fails in one point... accountable for all of it."** "For whoever keeps the whole law but fails in one point has become accountable for all of it. For he who said, 'Do not commit adultery,' also said, 'Do not murder.' If you do not commit adultery but do murder, you have become a transgressor of the law" (2:10–11).
- **The law is a whole:** the law is not a string of beads, where losing one leaves the rest; it is more like a pane of glass: crack it anywhere and the whole pane is broken. All the commandments come from the same God; to break one is to defy the God who gave them all.
- **No one can stand by the law:** Paul also quotes Deuteronomy: "Cursed be everyone who does not abide by all things written in the Book of the Law, and do them" (Gal 3:10). Though James is dealing with partiality here, his words make us see that no one can stand before God by his own law-keeping (see 3:2, "For we all stumble in many ways").

**3. "Mercy triumphs over judgment."** "So speak and so act as those who are to be judged under the law of liberty. For judgment is without mercy to one who has shown no mercy. Mercy triumphs over judgment" (2:12–13).
- **"To be judged":** believers too will stand before God and give account (Rom 14:10–12). "The law of liberty" here is the law of 1:25. How we treat others shows whether we have really received God's mercy.
- **"Judgment is without mercy to one who has shown no mercy":** the Lord Jesus told of the servant who was forgiven ten thousand talents but would not forgive a fellow servant a hundred denarii (Matt 18:23–35). His master asked him, "And should not you have had mercy on your fellow servant, as I had mercy on you?" (Matt 18:33). The one who shows no mercy reveals that he never truly grasped how much mercy he had received.
- **"Mercy triumphs over judgment":** this is the most hopeful sentence in the chapter. By the law we all stand condemned; Many take "mercy" here as the mercy a believer shows (compare "one who has shown no mercy"): the merciful can face judgment without fear. Others take it as God's mercy. The two belong together: we show mercy because we have first received it, and our mercy is the evidence of God's mercy, not the ground of our acquittal. How does God's mercy triumph over judgment? Not by God ignoring sin, but by his Son bearing the judgment on the cross, so that we might receive mercy. Those who have received mercy show mercy, and such people need not fear the day of judgment (Matt 5:7). Showing mercy does not mean covering up sin or leaving the harmed in danger.

> The point: No one can stand by keeping the law, since failing in one point makes us guilty of all. But mercy triumphs over judgment. The Lord who bore the judgment for us makes us, who have received mercy, merciful to others.

## Going Deeper: 2:14–19 Faith Without Works

**1. "Can that faith save him?"** "What good is it, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can that faith save him?" (2:14).
- **"Says" he has faith:** James does not say "if someone has faith," but "if someone says he has faith." He is testing whether the faith a person professes is real.
- **"That faith":** many take the Greek article as pointing back to the faith just described, the faith without works. James is not saying faith cannot save, but that this kind of faith cannot.

**2. "Go in peace."** "If a brother or sister is poorly clothed and lacking in daily food, and one of you says to them, 'Go in peace, be warmed and filled,' without giving them the things needed for the body, what good is that? So also faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead" (2:15–17).
- **Very spiritual words:** "Go in peace" was a common blessing. This person did not curse anyone or refuse anyone; he even spoke a blessing. Yet though able to help, he sent his brother or sister home hungry. Today we might say, "I'll be praying for you." Prayer is good; but when I could plainly help, prayer can become my reason not to reach out my hand. John says the same: "But if anyone has the world's goods and sees his brother in need, yet closes his heart against him, how does God's love abide in him?" (1 John 3:17).
- **"Dead":** faith without works is not "weaker faith." It is dead faith, like a body with the shape of a person but no breath. Nor does this mean that a faith with few works, which often stumbles, is dead. James is aiming at lip-faith that never means to act. A weak faith that clings to Christ saves, because it is Christ who saves, not the size of the faith.

**3. "You have faith and I have works."** "But someone will say, 'You have faith and I have works.' Show me your faith apart from your works, and I will show you my faith by my works" (2:18).
- **A difficult verse:** the Greek has no quotation marks, and interpreters differ on where the objector's words end. The Chinese Union Version puts the whole verse in quotation marks; the ESV and many modern versions take only "You have faith and I have works" as the objector's words, with the rest as James's reply. Many take the objector to mean that faith and works are separate gifts, one person having this and another that. James answers that before other people faith is invisible and can be shown only by works.

**4. "Even the demons believe—and shudder!"** "You believe that God is one; you do well. Even the demons believe—and shudder!" (2:19).
- **"God is one":** this is the confession Jews recited every day: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one" (Deut 6:4).
- **The demons' "faith":** the demons know who Jesus is better than many people do: "I know who you are—the Holy One of God" (Mark 1:24). But their "faith" is only knowledge, not trust. It makes them shudder; it does not make them love God.
- **Knowing is not trusting:** a person can grow up in church and know every Sunday-school answer, agree with orthodox doctrine and recite the creed, yet never entrust himself to Christ. The "faith" James criticizes is exactly this kind of mental agreement.

> The point: A faith that is professed with the lips but never grows into deeds of love is not saving faith but dead faith. Even the demons know who God is; true faith trusts God, and reaches out its hand to those in need.

## Going Deeper: 2:20–26 Abraham and Rahab

**1. "Abraham... offered up his son Isaac on the altar."** "Do you want to be shown, you foolish person, that faith apart from works is useless? Was not Abraham our father justified by works when he offered up his son Isaac on the altar? You see that faith was active along with his works, and faith was completed by his works; and the Scripture was fulfilled that says, 'Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness'—and he was called a friend of God" (2:20–23).
- **"Useless":** some manuscripts read "dead" (followed by the Chinese Union Version); earlier ones read "useless," a Greek play on the word "works" (*argē / ergōn*). The meaning is close either way: faith without works does not do what faith is for.
- **The order of events:** "Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness" is recorded in Genesis 15:6, before Isaac was born; the offering of Isaac comes in Genesis 22, many years later. So James is not saying that Abraham began to be counted righteous because he offered Isaac. He is saying that Abraham's earlier faith was shown in the offering of Isaac, and so the Scripture was "fulfilled."
- **"Faith was completed by his works":** as a tree shows it is alive when it bears fruit. On Mount Moriah God said, "Now I know that you fear God" (Gen 22:12).
- **"A friend of God":** the Old Testament calls Abraham God's friend (2 Chr 20:7; Isa 41:8). Faith is not only a set of beliefs but a relationship of closeness to God.

**2. "A person is justified by works and not by faith alone."** "You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone" (2:24). This is one of the hardest verses in the New Testament, since Paul says, "For we hold that one is justified by faith apart from works of the law" (Rom 3:28), and both use Abraham as their example (Rom 4:2–3). The introduction to this study touched on it briefly; here we look at three key words:
- **"Faith":** the "faith" James criticizes here is the kind of knowing the demons have (v. 19); the faith Paul speaks of is trusting "him who justifies the ungodly" (Rom 4:5), taking refuge in him.
- **"Works":** what Paul excludes is every human work as the ground of justification (Rom 4:4–5; Eph 2:9); the works James speaks of are the deeds of love that faith produces, like Abraham's obedience and Rahab's welcome.
- **"Justified":** Paul is speaking of how a sinner is declared righteous before God, on the sole ground of Christ's righteousness and his bearing our penalty, received through faith (Rom 3:24–26; 4:5). James, as the Reformed and Lutheran traditions read him, is speaking of how that righteousness is shown (for example, Calvin); many evangelical interpreters take it as God's verdict at the final judgment, where works are the evidence of genuine faith, not its ground (for example, Moo). The Catholic tradition holds that initial justification is a free gift (Council of Trent, Session 6, chapter 8), and cites this verse to teach that the justified increase in the righteousness received by doing good works (chapter 10).

However it is understood, James and Paul agree that we are saved by grace through faith, and that true faith always produces works. Paul says we are saved "not a result of works," and at once adds that we were "created in Christ Jesus for good works" (Eph 2:9–10). The Reformers often said (as Calvin put it): we are justified by faith alone, but the faith that justifies is never alone.

**3. "Rahab the prostitute."** "And in the same way was not also Rahab the prostitute justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out by another way? For as the body apart from the spirit is dead, so also faith apart from works is dead" (2:25–26).
- **Two very different people:** Abraham was the father of Israel and the friend of God; Rahab was a Gentile and a prostitute. James deliberately puts them side by side: faith does not depend on background. Rahab later appears in Jesus' genealogy (Matt 1:5). James commends her receiving and sending of the spies, not her lie.
- **Rahab's faith:** she told the spies, "for the LORD your God, he is God in the heavens above and on the earth beneath" (Josh 2:11). She risked her life to shelter them, and her faith showed in action. Hebrews also says, "By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish... because she had given a friendly welcome to the spies" (Heb 11:31).
- **"The body apart from the spirit is dead":** James's picture is surprising: he compares faith to the body, and works to the breath (spirit) that makes the body alive. He is not saying works matter more than faith, but that a faith which never acts is like a body with no breath. It keeps its outward shape, but it is dead.

> The point: God counted Abraham righteous through faith (Gen 15:6); his faith was real, and it was shown when he offered Isaac. Whether the friend of God or Rahab the prostitute, saving faith is alive, and it grows into obedience and love. Abraham could lay Isaac on the altar because he believed "God will provide for himself the lamb" (Gen 22:8); and God later did not spare his own Son.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "My brothers, show no partiality as you hold the faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory... Mercy triumphs over judgment" (2:1, 13). I seldom think of myself as partial. But looking back: at the church door, whom do I greet first? When seating guests at a wedding banquet, whom do I put at the head table, and whom at the table by the door? In small group, whose opinion do I really listen to, and whose do I discount before they have finished speaking? I carry an invisible ranking in my heart: education, income, accent. I am warm to those near the top and polite to those near the bottom, but I do not really want to get close. James calls this being "judges with evil thoughts." In God's sight it is sin (v. 9), not merely a lapse in manners. Why do I do it? Because I care about face, and I want to get close to other people's glory, so that I look a little more respectable too. But James says there is only one true glory: "our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory." This Lord of glory, "though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor" (2 Cor 8:9). He was born in a manger, had "nowhere to lay his head" (Matt 8:20), and at the end was stripped of his clothes on the cross. He chose the lowest place himself. And I? Before God, I am the one who came in wearing shabby clothes. The prophet Zechariah saw the high priest Joshua "standing before the angel, clothed with filthy garments," yet the angel of the LORD commanded, "Remove the filthy garments from him," and said, "Behold, I have taken your iniquity away from you, and I will clothe you with pure vestments" (Zech 3:3–4). God did not tell me to stand over there or sit at his feet. He took away my filth, clothed me in Christ's righteousness, and gave me the place of honor. Mercy triumphing over judgment is already true of me. So I no longer need anyone else's glory to prove myself. Next time I stand at the church door, I can walk first toward the person no one notices and no one greets. Not to look good, but because I too came in wearing filthy clothes, and the One who chose the lowest place for himself came to meet me and said, "Friend, move up higher" (Luke 14:10).
