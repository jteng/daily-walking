## 本章脉络

第三章分成两段。**舌头**（1-12节）：不要多人作师傅，因为作师傅的要受更重的判断；我们在许多事上都有过失，在话语上没有过失的，就是完全人；舌头像马的嚼环、船的舵，虽小却能调动全身；舌头像火，能点着最大的树林；各类的走兽都可以制伏，惟独舌头没有人能制伏；我们用同一个口颂赞神，又咒诅照着神形象被造的人，这是不应当的。**两种智慧**（13-18节）：有智慧的人，要在智慧的温柔上显出善行来；心里怀着苦毒的嫉妒和纷争，那智慧是属地的、属情欲的、属鬼魔的；从上头来的智慧，先是清洁，后是和平，温良柔顺，满有怜悯，多结善果；使人和平的人，用和平栽种义果。

这两段是连在一起的。雅各在1:19说「要快快地听，慢慢地说」，在1:26说虔诚的人要「勒住他的舌头」，这一章把这些话展开来讲。舌头的问题，说到底是心的问题：主耶稣说，「心里所充满的，口里就说出来」（太12:34）。所以雅各讲完舌头，就讲心里的智慧：心里若有嫉妒和纷争，口里就会有咒诅；心里若有从上头来的智慧，口里就会说出和平的话。

## 深读 3:1-2 不要多人作师傅

**1. 「要受更重的判断」。** 「我的弟兄们，不要多人作师傅，因为晓得我们要受更重的判断。」（3:1）
- **为什么先讲师傅？** 在早期教会，教导是很受尊重的职分，有不少人想当。可是教导的人天天用舌头，所以在言语上最容易犯罪，影响也最大。
- **「我们」：** 雅各把自己也放在里面。他自己是教导的人，也知道自己要在神面前交更重的账。这是说教导的人要为所托付的多交账（路12:48；来13:17），不是说他们的得救没有那么稳妥。
- **今天的应用：** 传道人、主日学老师、小组长、在家里教导孩子的父母，都是用话语影响别人的人。这节经文不是叫人不要教导，而是叫人存敬畏的心去教导。教导的人若借讲台或「属灵权柄」压制、操控、羞辱人，就是滥用了神托付的话语。受了这种伤害的人，可以找可信任的人帮助，也可以向教会的长执或所属宗派反映，不必因为对方是「师傅」就默默忍受；教会也不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而叫他们沉默。

**2. 「我们在许多事上都有过失」。** 「原来我们在许多事上都有过失；若有人在话语上没有过失，他就是完全人，也能勒住自己的全身。」（3:2）
- **「过失」：** 原文是绊跌的意思，和2:10「只在一条上跌倒」同一个词。雅各没有说「你们」，而是说「我们」都有。
- **「完全人」：** 这个词和1:4「成全」原文是同一个词，意思是成熟、完整。能管住舌头的人，也能管住全身。可是在话语上从没有过失的，世上除了一位，再没有别人：「他并没有犯罪，口里也没有诡诈。」（彼前2:22）

> 要点：用话语教导别人，是一件要存敬畏之心的事。我们在许多事上都有过失，在话语上尤其如此，没有人能夸口自己的舌头是清洁的。口里没有诡诈的，只有基督；我们教导人，是靠着他的恩典，也是要把人指向他。

## 深读 3:3-12 舌头

**1. 嚼环和舵。** 「我们若把嚼环放在马嘴里，叫它顺服，就能调动它的全身。看哪，船只虽然甚大，又被大风催逼，只用小小的舵，就随着掌舵的意思转动。这样，舌头在百体里也是最小的，却能说大话。」（3:3-5上）
- **小东西，大力量：** 一匹马、一条大船，都由一个很小的东西来控制方向。舌头也是这样：一句话可以改变一个人的一生，一句话也可以毁掉一个家。「生死在舌头的权下。」（箴18:21）
- **「说大话」：** 原文是夸口的意思。舌头虽小，却喜欢把自己说得很大。

**2. 「舌头就是火」。** 「看哪，最小的火能点着最大的树林。舌头就是火，在我们百体中，舌头是个罪恶的世界，能污秽全身，也能把生命的轮子点起来，并且是从地狱里点着的。」（3:5下-6）
- **一点火星：** 一句闲话，一个转发，一句「我只跟你一个人说」，就像一点火星落在干草上。「火缺了柴就必熄灭；无人传舌，争竞便止息。」（箴26:20）今天的微信群和社交媒体，让这火烧得比以前更快、更远。
- **「生命的轮子」：** 原文直译是出生的轮子，很难解。这个说法在当时的希腊文献里也出现过，但雅各只是借用一般的说法，多半是指人从生到死的整个历程。这和佛教的「轮回」毫无关系：人只活一次，没有转世再来。圣经说：「按着定命，人人都有一死，死后且有审判。」（来9:27）舌头的火，可以烧遍一个人的一生。
- **「从地狱里点着的」：** 「地狱」这个词来自希伯来文的「欣嫩子谷」，就是以色列人曾在火中焚烧自己儿女献给偶像的地方（耶7:31；王下23:10）。新约中除了这一处，这个词只出现在主耶稣的口中，指永刑的地方（太5:22）。舌头的恶背后，有属灵的黑暗势力在推动（参3:15「属鬼魔的」）。

**3. 「惟独舌头没有人能制伏」。** 「各类的走兽、飞禽、昆虫、水族，本来都可以制伏，也已经被人制伏了；惟独舌头没有人能制伏，是不止息的恶物，满了害死人的毒气。」（3:7-8）
- **人的能力和人的无能：** 神造人的时候，叫人「管理海里的鱼、空中的鸟、地上的牲畜」（创1:26）。人可以驯服狮子和大象，却驯服不了自己的舌头。
- **「没有人能制伏」：** 这是一句很叫人谦卑的话。雅各不是说管住舌头不重要，而是说单靠人的力量做不到。这句话把我们推到神面前去：「耶和华啊，求你禁止我的口，把守我的嘴！」（诗141:3）
- **「不止息的恶物」：** 「不止息」和1:8的「没有定见」，在多数古抄本中原文是同一个词（有些抄本作管不住的）；16节的「扰乱」也出自同一个词根。舌头就像1:6所说的海浪，一刻不停。

**4. 「颂赞和咒诅从一个口里出来」。** 「我们用舌头颂赞那为主、为父的，又用舌头咒诅那照着神形象被造的人。颂赞和咒诅从一个口里出来！我的弟兄们，这是不应当的！」（3:9-10）
- **聚会中和聚会后：** 早上在诗班唱「哈利路亚」，中午在停车场为了车位和人吵架；在聚会中说「阿们」，回家路上批评讲道的人。这样的事，离我们并不远。
- **「照着神形象被造的人」：** 这是雅各的理由。每一个人都带着神的形象（创1:27；9:6）。咒诅人、羞辱人，就是羞辱那位照自己形象造人的神。一边颂赞神，一边咒诅他的形象，就好像一边称赞一位画家，一边撕他的画。
- **咒诅不只是骂人：** 「你怎么这么笨」「我怎么生了你这样的孩子」「你看看人家」，这些话虽然没有脏字，本质上却是在贬低、伤害一个带着神形象的人，和咒诅是同一个根。如果一个人在家里长期被这样的话贬低、威胁、控制，这就是虐待。受害的人可以向可信任的人或专业机构求助；教会要保护他们，不可只劝他们忍耐，也不可为了「家丑不可外扬」叫他们沉默。若人身安全有危险，要立即报警。

**5. 泉源和果树。** 「泉源从一个眼里能发出甜苦两样的水吗？我的弟兄们，无花果树能生橄榄吗？葡萄树能结无花果吗？咸水里也不能发出甜水来。」（3:11-12）
- **舌头显明心：** 泉水的味道，取决于源头；果子是什么，取决于树是什么。主耶稣说：「善人从他心里所存的善就发出善来；恶人从他心里所存的恶就发出恶来。」（路6:45）
- **问题在源头：** 所以要医治舌头，不能只在嘴上下功夫，要从心里改变。人做不到这件事，神却能。他「按自己的旨意，用真道生了我们」（1:18），这就是重生：神给我们一颗新的心。若你的泉源从来没有被更新过，不要只想改掉几句口头禅，要今天就来到基督面前，承认自己的罪，信靠他。

> 要点：舌头虽小，却像火，没有人能制伏。我们用同一个口颂赞神，又咒诅照着神形象被造的人，显明问题在于我们的心。只有神能改变泉源，叫我们口里发出甜水来。

## 深读 3:13-18 两种智慧

**1. 「在智慧的温柔上显出他的善行来」。** 「你们中间谁是有智慧有见识的呢？他就当在智慧的温柔上显出他的善行来。」（3:13）
- **回到第1节：** 想作师傅的人，都以为自己有智慧。雅各说，智慧不是看你会说什么，而是看你怎样生活。这和第2章所说「信心没有行为也是死的」（2:26）是一样的道理。
- **「温柔」：** 真智慧的记号是温柔，不是锋利，不是口才。1:21也说要「存温柔的心领受那所栽种的道」。

**2. 属地的智慧。** 「你们心里若怀着苦毒的嫉妒和纷争，就不可自夸，也不可说谎话抵挡真道。这样的智慧不是从上头来的，乃是属地的，属情欲的，属鬼魔的。在何处有嫉妒、纷争，就在何处有扰乱和各样的坏事。」（3:14-16）
- **「纷争」：** 原文的意思是自私的野心，为自己争地位、拉帮结派（加5:20译作「结党」）。教会里的许多争吵，表面上是为了真理，其实是为了谁说了算。
- **「不可自夸」：** 嫉妒的人往往把自己的嫉妒说成「我是为了教会好」「我只是坚持原则」。雅各说，这是在「说谎话抵挡真道」。
- **三个描述：** 「属地的」，是只看今生的；「属情欲的」，原文是「属魂的」，就是只凭人天然的想法，没有圣灵（参林前2:14；犹19「属乎血气、没有圣灵」）；「属鬼魔的」，是说这种智慧带着鬼魔的样子，也出于那黑暗的权势（参2:19「鬼魔也信」；4:7「务要抵挡魔鬼」）。它可能看起来很精明、很有效，就像职场和教会里讲的手腕、关系、权术，结果却是「扰乱和各样的坏事」。

**3. 从上头来的智慧。** 「惟独从上头来的智慧，先是清洁，后是和平，温良柔顺，满有怜悯，多结善果，没有偏见，没有假冒。」（3:17）
- **「从上头来的」：** 和1:5、1:17相连：智慧是神白白赐的，「各样美善的恩赐……都是从上头来的」。
- **「先是清洁，后是和平」：** 次序很重要。华人讲「以和为贵」，可是圣经说的和平不是和稀泥、不分是非、息事宁人，而是先有清洁，先对神诚实，然后才有和平。
- **「温良柔顺」：** 「温良」是讲理、宽容，不苛刻；「柔顺」是肯听劝，不固执己见。
- **「没有偏见，没有假冒」：** 「没有偏见」的词根和1:6的「疑惑」、2:4的「偏心待人」相同，所以可以理解为不偏心（参2:1-4），也可以理解为心里专一、不摇摆（参1:6-8）。「没有假冒」就是不戴面具。
- **一幅基督的画像：** 雅各在这里没有提基督的名字，但这一串描写，几乎就是主耶稣的样子。他「心里柔和谦卑」（太11:29），保罗也说到「基督的温柔、和平」（林后10:1）。他是清洁的，又是使人和平的；他满有怜悯，也从无假冒。保罗说，神使基督「成为我们的智慧」（林前1:30）。所以从上头来的智慧不只是一套原则。神把它赐给向他祈求的人（1:5）；这智慧在我们身上活出来，就是基督的样式。

**4. 「用和平所栽种的义果」。** 「并且使人和平的，是用和平所栽种的义果。」（3:18）
- **种和收：** 使人和平的人，好像农夫一样，在和平中撒种，收的是公义的果子。这和1:20正好相对：「人的怒气并不成就神的义」，和平却能结出义果。
- **使人和睦的人：** 主耶稣说：「使人和睦的人有福了！因为他们必称为神的儿子。」（太5:9）主耶稣自己就是那位使人和睦的主，他藉着十字架使我们与神和好。

> 要点：属地的智慧出于嫉妒和自私的野心，结果是扰乱；从上头来的智慧先是清洁，后是和平，满有怜悯。这智慧的样子就是基督，他是神赐给我们的智慧。

## 默想

**默想：** 「颂赞和咒诅从一个口里出来！我的弟兄们，这是不应当的！」（3:10）我说话最不小心的地方，不是在教会，也不是在公司，而是在家里的饭桌上。在外面，我会顾面子，说话有分寸；回到家里，面具一放下，话就变得很尖。这样看来，我在外面的分寸，多半也不是因为敬重人身上神的形象，而是为了自己的面子。对孩子说：「你怎么连这个都不会？」对配偶说：「跟你说了多少遍了。」对年老的父母说：「你别管了，你不懂。」我还常常加一句：「我是为你好。」可是同一个口，早上刚在灵修时说过「主啊，我爱你」。雅各说，这些咒诅的话是「从地狱里点着的」火，而且「没有人能制伏」。我的舌头不听我的话，因为问题不在舌头，而在泉源。然后我想起另一个口。「他并没有犯罪，口里也没有诡诈。他被骂不还口。」（彼前2:22-23）在人最容易还口咒诅的时候，在十字架上被钉、被讥诮的时候，他口里说的是：「父啊！赦免他们；因为他们所做的，他们不晓得。」（路23:34）他「为罪犯代求」（赛53:12），那句话也包括我，也为着我在饭桌上说过的每一句尖刻话。这位口里没有诡诈的主，「被挂在木头上，亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24），也担当了我不洁的口所该受的审判。如果我想起的，不是自己说过的话，而是小时候在饭桌上听过的话，我也可以知道：这位被骂的主，明白被话语刺伤是什么滋味；那些话从来不是神对我的判断。五旬节那天，有「舌头如火焰」落在门徒头上（徒2:3）：另一种火，不是从地狱点着的，而是从天上降下来的。那些曾经争论谁为大的门徒（可9:34；路22:24），如今开始讲说神的大作为（徒2:11）。连彼得也是这样：他曾发咒起誓说「我不认得你们说的这个人」（可14:71），如今他的口也讲说神的大作为。我的舌头，我自己制伏不了；但那位赐下圣灵的主，能改变我的泉源。雅各说，我们的舌头本来是用来「颂赞那为主、为父的」（3:9）；神要更新的，不只是让我少说几句伤人的话，而是叫这同一个口，既颂赞他，也祝福他照着自己形象所造的人。今天晚上回到饭桌上，在开口纠正谁之前，我可以先对家里的那个人说一句感谢或祝福的话，哪怕对方是我的孩子；昨天若说重了，也可以说一声对不起。然后在心里求：「耶和华啊，求你禁止我的口，把守我的嘴！」（诗141:3）即使我明天又说错了，那位在十字架上为钉他的人求赦免的主，已经为我说过的每一句话担当了审判；我这张口最后要做的，和五旬节那天的门徒一样：讲说神的大作为。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 falls into two parts. **The tongue** (vv. 1–12): not many should become teachers, for teachers will be judged with greater strictness; we all stumble in many ways, and whoever does not stumble in what he says is a perfect man; the tongue is like a horse's bit or a ship's rudder, small but able to steer the whole; the tongue is a fire that can set a great forest ablaze; every kind of animal can be tamed, but no human being can tame the tongue; with the same mouth we bless God and curse people made in his likeness, and this ought not to be. **Two kinds of wisdom** (vv. 13–18): let the wise show their works in the meekness of wisdom; if there is bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in the heart, that wisdom is earthly, unspiritual and demonic; the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits; a harvest of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.

The two parts belong together. In 1:19 James said, "be quick to hear, slow to speak," and in 1:26 that the religious person must "bridle his tongue"; this chapter unfolds those words. In the end the problem of the tongue is a problem of the heart. The Lord Jesus said, "out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks" (Matt 12:34). So after the tongue, James turns to the wisdom in the heart. If the heart holds jealousy and selfish ambition, the mouth will curse; if the heart holds the wisdom from above, the mouth will speak peace.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–2 Not Many Should Become Teachers

**1. "Judged with greater strictness."** "Not many of you should become teachers, my brothers, for you know that we who teach will be judged with greater strictness" (3:1).
- **Why begin with teachers?** In the early church, teaching was a highly respected role, and many wanted it. But teachers use their tongues every day, so they are the most likely to sin in speech, and their words carry the furthest.
- **"We":** James includes himself. He is a teacher, and he knows he will give a stricter account before God. This is greater accountability for what has been entrusted (Luke 12:48; Heb 13:17), not a less secure salvation.
- **Today:** pastors, Sunday school teachers, small-group leaders, parents teaching their children at home are all people whose words shape others. This verse does not tell people not to teach; it tells them to teach in the fear of God. A teacher who uses the pulpit or "spiritual authority" to suppress, control or shame people is abusing the word God has entrusted to him. Those who have been harmed this way may seek help from someone trustworthy, or raise it with the church's elders or denomination; they need not suffer in silence because the other person is a "teacher," and the church must not silence them to keep "family shame" from getting out.

**2. "We all stumble in many ways."** "For we all stumble in many ways, and if anyone does not stumble in what he says, he is a perfect man, able also to bridle his whole body" (3:2).
- **"Stumble":** the same word as in 2:10, "fails in one point." James does not say "you" but "we all."
- **"A perfect man":** the same word as "perfect" in 1:4, meaning mature, complete. Whoever can govern his tongue can govern his whole body. Yet there is no one in the world who never stumbles in speech, except one: "He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth" (1 Pet 2:22).

> The point: Teaching others with words is something to do in the fear of God. We all stumble in many ways, especially in speech, and no one can boast that his tongue is clean. Only Christ had no deceit in his mouth; we teach leaning on his grace, and we teach to point people to him.

## Going Deeper: 3:3–12 The Tongue

**1. The bit and the rudder.** "If we put bits into the mouths of horses so that they obey us, we guide their whole bodies as well. Look at the ships also: though they are so large and are driven by strong winds, they are guided by a very small rudder wherever the will of the pilot directs. So also the tongue is a small member, yet it boasts of great things" (3:3–5a).
- **Small thing, great power:** a horse, a great ship, both steered by something very small. So it is with the tongue: one sentence can change a person's life, and one sentence can wreck a family. "Death and life are in the power of the tongue" (Prov 18:21).
- **"Boasts of great things":** small as it is, the tongue likes to make itself big.

**2. "The tongue is a fire."** "How great a forest is set ablaze by such a small fire! And the tongue is a fire, a world of unrighteousness. The tongue is set among our members, staining the whole body, setting on fire the entire course of life, and set on fire by hell" (3:5b–6).
- **A single spark:** a piece of gossip, a forwarded message, an "I'm only telling you" is like a spark falling on dry grass. "For lack of wood the fire goes out, and where there is no whisperer, quarreling ceases" (Prov 26:20). Today's group chats and social media make the fire spread faster and further than ever.
- **"The entire course of life":** literally "the wheel of becoming," a hard phrase. It had some currency in Greek thought, but James uses it in an ordinary sense, probably meaning the whole course of a person's life from birth to death. It has nothing to do with a cycle of rebirth; there is no reincarnation, and we live once: "it is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment" (Heb 9:27). The fire of the tongue can burn through an entire life.
- **"Set on fire by hell":** "hell" is "Gehenna," from the Hebrew name of the Valley of Hinnom, where Israel had once burned its children in the fire to idols (Jer 7:31; 2 Kgs 23:10). Apart from this verse, the New Testament uses the word only on the lips of Jesus, for the place of final punishment (Matt 5:22). Behind the evil of the tongue are dark spiritual powers (see 3:15, "demonic").

**3. "No human being can tame the tongue."** "For every kind of beast and bird, of reptile and sea creature, can be tamed and has been tamed by mankind, but no human being can tame the tongue. It is a restless evil, full of deadly poison" (3:7–8).
- **Human power and human helplessness:** when God made humanity, he gave them dominion "over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the heavens and over the livestock" (Gen 1:26). People can tame lions and elephants, but not their own tongues.
- **"No human being can tame":** this is a humbling sentence. James is not saying that governing the tongue does not matter, but that human strength alone cannot do it. The sentence drives us to God: "Set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth; keep watch over the door of my lips!" (Ps 141:3).
- **"A restless evil":** in most of the oldest manuscripts, "restless" is the same word as "unstable" in 1:8 (some manuscripts read "uncontrollable"), and the "disorder" of verse 16 comes from the same root. Like the wave of the sea in 1:6, the tongue never stops.

**4. "From the same mouth come blessing and cursing."** "With it we bless our Lord and Father, and with it we curse people who are made in the likeness of God. From the same mouth come blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not to be so" (3:9–10).
- **During and after the service:** in the morning we sing "Hallelujah" in the choir; at noon we quarrel with someone over a parking space. During the service we say "Amen"; on the way home we criticize the preacher. This is not far from any of us.
- **"Made in the likeness of God":** this is James's reason. Every person bears God's image (Gen 1:27; 9:6). To curse or humiliate a person is to insult the God who made him in his image. Praising God while cursing his image is like praising a painter while tearing up his painting.
- **Cursing is not only swearing:** "How can you be so stupid?" "How did I end up with a child like you?" "Look at other people's kids." There is no foul language in these words, yet at root they do what cursing does: they demean someone who bears God's image. If someone at home is belittled, threatened or controlled by words like these over a long time, that is abuse. The one harmed may seek help from a trustworthy person or a professional agency, and the church must protect them, not simply urge them to endure, nor silence them to keep "family shame" from getting out. If anyone is in danger, contact the police at once.

**5. The spring and the tree.** "Does a spring pour forth from the same opening both fresh and salt water? Can a fig tree, my brothers, bear olives, or a grapevine produce figs? Neither can a salt pond yield fresh water" (3:11–12).
- **The tongue shows the heart:** the taste of the water depends on its source; the fruit depends on the tree. The Lord Jesus said, "The good person out of the good treasure of his heart produces good, and the evil person out of his evil treasure produces evil" (Luke 6:45).
- **The problem is at the source:** so healing the tongue cannot be done by working on the mouth alone; it needs a changed heart. We cannot do this, but God can: "of his own will he brought us forth by the word of truth" (1:18). This is the new birth: God gives us a new heart. If your spring has never been made new, do not just try to drop a few habits of speech; come to Christ today, confess your sin, and trust him.

> The point: The tongue is small, but it is a fire no one can tame. With the same mouth we bless God and curse people made in his image, which shows that the problem is in our hearts. Only God can change the source, so that fresh water flows from our mouths.

## Going Deeper: 3:13–18 Two Kinds of Wisdom

**1. "Let him show his works in the meekness of wisdom."** "Who is wise and understanding among you? By his good conduct let him show his works in the meekness of wisdom" (3:13).
- **Back to verse 1:** those who want to be teachers all think they are wise. James says wisdom is shown not by what you can say but by how you live. This is the same principle as chapter 2: "faith apart from works is dead" (2:26).
- **"Meekness":** the mark of true wisdom is meekness, not sharpness or eloquence. James 1:21 also says to "receive with meekness the implanted word."

**2. Earthly wisdom.** "But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not boast and be false to the truth. This is not the wisdom that comes down from above, but is earthly, unspiritual, demonic. For where jealousy and selfish ambition exist, there will be disorder and every vile practice" (3:14–16).
- **"Selfish ambition":** the word means pushing for one's own position, forming factions (the same word is translated "rivalries" in Gal 5:20). Many church quarrels look like disputes over truth but are really about who gets to decide.
- **"Do not boast":** jealous people often describe their jealousy as "I'm doing this for the good of the church" or "I'm only standing on principle." James says this is being "false to the truth."
- **Three descriptions:** "earthly" sees only this life; "unspiritual" is literally "soulish," going by natural human thinking without the Spirit (see 1 Cor 2:14; Jude 19, "worldly people, devoid of the Spirit"); "demonic" means this wisdom bears the stamp of the demons and comes from that dark realm (see 2:19, "even the demons believe"; 4:7, "resist the devil"). It may look shrewd and effective, like the maneuvering, connections and power plays found at work and in church, but its fruit is "disorder and every vile practice."

**3. The wisdom from above.** "But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial and sincere" (3:17).
- **"From above":** this links with 1:5 and 1:17. Wisdom is God's free gift: "every good gift... is from above."
- **"First pure, then peaceable":** the order matters. Chinese culture prizes "harmony above all," but biblical peace is not papering over wrong or smoothing things over regardless of right and wrong. Purity comes first, honesty before God, and then peace.
- **"Gentle, open to reason":** "gentle" means reasonable, forbearing, not harsh; "open to reason" means willing to be persuaded, not stubborn.
- **"Impartial and sincere":** "impartial" shares its root with "doubting" in 1:6 and "made distinctions" in 2:4, so "impartial" can mean showing no favoritism (see 2:1–4) or being single-minded and not wavering (see 1:6–8). "Sincere" means wearing no mask.
- **A portrait of Christ:** James does not name Christ here, yet this list is almost a portrait of the Lord Jesus, who is "gentle and lowly in heart" (Matt 11:29); Paul speaks of "the meekness and gentleness of Christ" (2 Cor 10:1). He was pure, and he made peace; he was full of mercy, and he never wore a mask. Paul says God made Christ "our wisdom" (1 Cor 1:30). So the wisdom from above is more than a set of principles: God gives it to those who ask (1:5), and what it forms in us is the likeness of Christ.

**4. "A harvest of righteousness is sown in peace."** "And a harvest of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace" (3:18).
- **Sowing and reaping:** peacemakers are like farmers: they sow in peace and reap a harvest of righteousness. This is the reverse of 1:20: "the anger of man does not produce the righteousness that God requires," but peace bears the fruit of righteousness.
- **Peacemakers:** the Lord Jesus said, "Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God" (Matt 5:9). He himself is the great Peacemaker, who reconciled us to God through the cross.

> The point: Earthly wisdom comes from jealousy and selfish ambition, and its fruit is disorder. The wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, full of mercy. This wisdom looks like Christ, whom God has given to be our wisdom.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "From the same mouth come blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not to be so" (3:10). The place where I am least careful with my words is not church, and not work, but the dinner table at home. Outside, I keep face and choose my words; at home the mask comes off and my words turn sharp. So perhaps even my restraint outside is less reverence for God's image in people than care for my own face. To my child: "How can you not even do this?" To my spouse: "How many times have I told you?" To my aging parents: "Just leave it, you don't understand." And I often add: "I'm saying this for your good." Yet the same mouth said, "Lord, I love you," in my devotions that morning. James says these cursing words are a fire "set on fire by hell," and that "no human being can tame" the tongue. My tongue does not obey me, because the problem is not in the tongue but in the spring. Then I think of another mouth. "He committed no sin, neither was deceit found in his mouth. When he was reviled, he did not revile in return" (1 Pet 2:22–23). At the moment when cursing back would have been most natural, nailed to the cross and mocked, what came from his mouth was "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34). He "makes intercession for the transgressors" (Isa 53:12); that prayer includes me too, and every sharp word I have spoken at the dinner table. He whose mouth knew no deceit "himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" (1 Pet 2:24), and with them the judgment my unclean mouth deserved. And if what comes back to me is not words I have said but words I heard at the table as a child, I can know that this Lord, who was reviled, knows what it is to be wounded by words, and that those words were never God's verdict on me. On the day of Pentecost, "divided tongues as of fire" rested on the disciples (Acts 2:3): another fire, not set by hell but sent down from heaven. Disciples who had argued over which of them was the greatest (Mark 9:34; Luke 22:24) began to tell the mighty works of God (Acts 2:11). So did Peter, who had invoked a curse on himself and sworn, "I do not know this man of whom you speak" (Mark 14:71). I cannot tame my own tongue; but the Lord who gives the Spirit can change my spring. James says our tongues were made to "bless our Lord and Father" (3:9). What God is renewing is not only a mouth that wounds a little less, but one mouth that blesses him and blesses the people made in his likeness. Tonight at the dinner table, before I correct anyone, I can first say one word of thanks or blessing to someone at home, even if it is my child; and if I spoke too harshly yesterday, I can say sorry. Then I can pray in my heart, "Set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth; keep watch over the door of my lips!" (Ps 141:3). Even if I speak wrongly again tomorrow, the Lord who prayed from the cross for those who crucified him has already borne the judgment for every word I have spoken. And what my mouth is finally for is what the disciples' mouths were for at Pentecost: telling the mighty works of God.
