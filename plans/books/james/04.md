## 本章脉络

第四章可以分成三段。**争战的根源和谦卑的路**（1-10节）：争战斗殴是从百体中战斗的私欲来的；我们得不着，是因为不求，或是妄求；与世俗为友就是与神为敌；但神赐更多的恩典，阻挡骄傲的人，赐恩给谦卑的人；所以要顺服神，抵挡魔鬼，亲近神，洁净手，清洁心，在主面前自卑，主就必叫我们升高。**不可彼此批评**（11-12节）：批评弟兄就是批评律法；设立律法和判断人的只有一位，你是谁，竟敢论断别人呢？**主若愿意**（13-17节）：不可说「今天明天我们要往某城里去」，因为明天如何，我们还不知道；我们的生命原是一片云雾；只当说「主若愿意，我们就可以活着，也可以做这事，或做那事」；人若知道行善却不去行，这就是他的罪了。

这一章接着第3章。3:16说：「在何处有嫉妒、纷争，就在何处有扰乱和各样的坏事。」4:1就问：「你们中间的争战斗殴是从哪里来的呢？」雅各一层一层往里挖：外面的争吵，来自里面的私欲；里面的私欲，显明我们的心想要世界多过想要神。可是全章的转折点，是第6节的一句话：「但他赐更多的恩典。」雅各的话很重，可是神的恩典更大。

## 深读 4:1-3 争战从哪里来

**1. 「不是从你们百体中战斗之私欲来的吗？」** 「你们中间的争战斗殴是从哪里来的呢？不是从你们百体中战斗之私欲来的吗？」（4:1）
- **雅各在问根源：** 我们习惯说，吵架是因为对方不讲理，是因为环境压力大。雅各却要我们往里看：争战的根，在我们里面。
- **「私欲」：** 原文是一个常指享乐的词（英文表示享乐主义的词就是从它来的），4:3的「宴乐」也是同一个词。我们想要舒服、想要被尊重、想要照自己的意思；得不到的时候，就和挡在中间的人争。
- **「百体中战斗」：** 这些私欲不只和别人争，也在我们里面争战，叫人不得安宁（参彼前2:11）。

**2. 「你们得不着，是因为你们不求」。** 「你们贪恋，还是得不着；你们杀害嫉妒，又斗殴争战，也不能得。你们得不着，是因为你们不求。你们求也得不着，是因为你们妄求，要浪费在你们的宴乐中。」（4:2-3）
- **「杀害」：** 这个词很重，解经家有不同的理解。许多人认为这是夸张的说法，就像主耶稣说向弟兄动怒就是犯了杀人的罪（太5:21-22）；也有人认为，雅各是在说私欲若不对付，最后会走到哪一步，正像1:15说：「私欲既怀了胎，就生出罪来；罪既长成，就生出死来。」也有人认为，当时有些犹太人因为政治和经济的冲突而真的动刀，雅各是照字面说的；还有少数人（从伊拉斯谟开始）猜测，「杀害」原本是另一个拼法很接近、意思是嫉妒的词，后来抄错了，但没有古抄本支持这个猜测。
- **「因为你们不求」：** 我们想尽办法自己去抢、去争，却没有想到向神求。这是一种骄傲：我自己来。
- **「因为你们妄求」：** 有时候我们也求，可是求的动机只是为了满足自己的享乐。我们把神当作满足欲望的工具，而不是我们所爱的那一位。这和1:5-8正好相对：那里的人缺少智慧，向厚赐的神求；这里的人想要的是宴乐，就算求也是心怀二意。

> 要点：外面的争吵，来自里面的私欲。我们得不着，是因为不求；求也得不着，是因为我们把神当作满足欲望的工具。

## 深读 4:4-10 他赐更多的恩典

**1. 「你们这些淫乱的人哪」。** 「你们这些淫乱的人哪，岂不知与世俗为友就是与神为敌吗？所以凡想要与世俗为友的，就是与神为敌了。」（4:4）
- **旧约的说法：** 雅各用的是先知的语言。「淫乱的人」原文是阴性的「淫妇」一词，正是旧约以神为丈夫、百姓为妻子的说法（参太12:39「邪恶淫乱的世代」）。旧约常把神和以色列的关系比作婚姻，以色列去拜别神，就是对丈夫不忠：「以色列家，你们向我行诡诈，真像妻子行诡诈离开她丈夫一样。」（耶3:20；参何2:2）雅各对信徒这样说，是要他们看见，心向着世界不只是犯了规条，而是伤了爱他们的神的心。
- **「世俗」：** 不是指神所造的世界，而是指不认识神、与神为敌的价值观，就是「肉体的情欲、眼目的情欲，并今生的骄傲」（约一2:16）。
- **不能脚踏两条船：** 主耶稣说：「一个人不能事奉两个主……你们不能又事奉神，又事奉玛门。」（太6:24）想要两边都讨好的人，就是1:8和4:8所说「心怀二意的人」。

**2. 「是恋爱至于嫉妒吗？」** 「你们想经上所说是徒然的吗？神所赐、住在我们里面的灵，是恋爱至于嫉妒吗？」（4:5）
- **难解的一节：** 这一节是雅各书中最难翻译的经文之一。旧约中找不到一模一样的经文，雅各可能是概括旧约的一个主题；也有人认为，「经上所说」是指下一节所引的箴言。解经家主要有两种理解：
- **神的忌邪：** 英文标准译本和许多现代译本理解为，神恋慕他放在我们里面的灵，至于嫉妒。这里的「灵」，有人认为是神赐给人的生命之灵，有人认为是圣灵。神像一位深爱妻子的丈夫，不能容忍她的心分给别人。旧约多次说「耶和华……是忌邪的神」（出20:5；34:14）。
- **人的嫉妒：** 也有一些解经家和译本（如英文钦定本）理解为，神放在我们里面的灵（人的心）常常充满嫉妒的欲望。理由是：这里的「嫉妒」原文是一个在新约中总是指罪的词（参加5:21），从来没有用在神身上。这样理解，就和4:1-2讲的私欲和争斗相连。主张神的忌邪的人则指出，「恋爱」这个动词在别处都是好的意思（腓1:8「切切地想念」；彼前2:2「爱慕」）。
- **和合本的句子：** 和合本以「灵」为主语，并且是一个问句（和1881年英文修订本相近）。可以理解为圣灵恋慕我们至于嫉妒；也可以理解为反问：神所赐的灵，难道会叫人充满嫉妒吗？
- **无论哪一种：** 下一节都说：「但他赐更多的恩典。」我们的心若常常偏向世界，神的恩典就更多；神若是忌邪的，他的忌邪也是出于爱。

**3. 「但他赐更多的恩典」。** 「但他赐更多的恩典，所以经上说：神阻挡骄傲的人，赐恩给谦卑的人。」（4:6）
- **全章的转折：** 雅各刚刚称读者为「淫乱的人」，警告他们与世俗为友就是与神为敌；可是他没有停在定罪上。我们的罪很重，神的恩典更多。神对不忠的百姓，不是转身离开，而是赐更多的恩典。
- **引自箴言：** 「他讥诮那好讥诮的人，赐恩给谦卑的人。」（箴3:34；雅各和彼得都几乎照七十士译本引用，只把「主」换成「神」，彼前5:5）
- **恩典给谁？** 不是给觉得自己不需要的人，而是给谦卑的人。骄傲的人站在神面前说：「我自己来。」谦卑的人说：「我需要你。」主耶稣讲过那个税吏：「凡自高的，必降为卑；自卑的，必升为高。」（路18:14）

**4. 「你们亲近神，神就必亲近你们」。** 「故此，你们要顺服神。务要抵挡魔鬼，魔鬼就必离开你们逃跑了。你们亲近神，神就必亲近你们。有罪的人哪，要洁净你们的手！心怀二意的人哪，要清洁你们的心！」（4:7-8）
- **「故此」：** 下面这一连串命令，都建立在「他赐更多的恩典」上面。不是先做好了才能得恩典，而是因为恩典已经赐下，所以要回到神那里去。
- **顺服神，抵挡魔鬼：** 两件事是一起的。彼得也说：「你们要用坚固的信心抵挡他。」（彼前5:9）魔鬼不是无所不能的，信徒靠着神，可以叫它逃跑。顺服神，不是说要顺服一切自称代表神的人；在家暴、虐待或属灵操控中，离开危险、向警察或可信任的人求助，并不违背顺服神。
- **「你们亲近神，神就必亲近你们」：** 这不是说神要等我们先走第一步。在整本圣经里，总是神先来找人（1:18）。可是这句话告诉我们，回到神那里的路是开着的。就像那个浪子：「相离还远，他父亲看见，就动了慈心，跑去抱着他的颈项。」（路15:20）
- **手和心：** 「洁净你们的手」是行为的悔改，「清洁你们的心」是里面的悔改，这叫人想起诗篇所说「手洁心清」的人（诗24:4）。先知也说：「你们要转向我，我就转向你们。」（亚1:3；玛3:7）

**5. 「将喜笑变作悲哀」。** 「你们要愁苦、悲哀、哭泣，将喜笑变作悲哀，欢乐变作愁闷。务要在主面前自卑，主就必叫你们升高。」（4:9-10）
- **为罪忧伤：** 雅各不是说信徒一辈子都不可以笑，而是说，对自己的罪嘻嘻哈哈、不当一回事的人，要停下来，为罪忧伤。主耶稣说：「哀恸的人有福了！」（太5:4）
- **「主就必叫你们升高」：** 谦卑的路不是通往绝望，而是通往被神高举。神「与心灵痛悔谦卑的人同居；要使谦卑人的灵苏醒」（赛57:15）。

> 要点：我们的心常常偏向世界，可是「他赐更多的恩典」。神阻挡骄傲的人，赐恩给谦卑的人。回到神那里的路是开着的：顺服神，抵挡魔鬼，为罪忧伤，亲近神，他就必亲近我们。

## 深读 4:11-12 不可彼此批评

**「弟兄们，你们不可彼此批评。人若批评弟兄，论断弟兄，就是批评律法，论断律法。你若论断律法，就不是遵行律法，乃是判断人的。设立律法和判断人的，只有一位，就是那能救人也能灭人的。你是谁，竟敢论断别人呢？」**（4:11-12）
- **「批评」：** 原文是说别人的坏话、贬低人，常常是在背后。这和第3章讲的舌头连在一起。
- **为什么批评弟兄就是批评律法？** 律法说「要爱人如己」（2:8）；利未记在同一段里也说「不可在民中往来搬弄是非」（利19:16-18）。我在背后说弟兄的坏话，等于说这条律法不适用于我，我比律法更高。
- **「只有一位」：** 只有神是立法者，也是审判者。主耶稣说：「你们不要论断人。」（太7:1）保罗也说：「时候未到，什么都不要论断，只等主来。」（林前4:5）
- **这不是叫人对罪不闻不问：** 雅各禁止的，是背后中伤、自以为义地定人的罪；不是禁止按真理指出弟兄的错（太18:15-17），也不是禁止如实地说出伤害和罪行。受害的人说出自己遭遇的事、向教会或警察举报，不是「批评弟兄」，而是寻求公义。向警察报案是正当的（罗13:4）。教会也不可以拿这节经文叫受害人闭口，或为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩虐待。

> 要点：只有神是设立律法、审判人的那一位。我在背后论断弟兄，就是把自己放在神的位子上。

## 深读 4:13-17 主若愿意

**1. 「今天明天我们要往某城里去」。** 「嗐！你们有话说：『今天明天我们要往某城里去，在那里住一年，做买卖得利。』」（4:13）
- **做生意的人：** 雅各在这里对一些在各城之间做买卖的人说话。他们是不是教会里的人，解经家看法不一（参5:1-6对财主的话）。雅各没有说做生意或做计划是错的。
- **问题在哪里？** 这段话里，有「我们」、有城、有一年、有利润，却没有神。他们好像自己是时间的主人。

**2. 「你们原来是一片云雾」。** 「其实明天如何，你们还不知道。你们的生命是什么呢？你们原来是一片云雾，出现少时就不见了。」（4:14）
- **箴言的话：** 「不要为明日自夸，因为一日要生何事，你尚且不能知道。」（箴27:1）
- **「云雾」：** 早晨的雾，太阳一出来就散了。先知说恶人「必如早晨的云雾」（何13:3）；诗人也说：「各人最稳妥的时候，真是全然虚幻。」（诗39:5）主耶稣讲过一个财主，计划要盖更大的仓房，神却对他说：「无知的人哪，今夜必要你的灵魂。」（路12:20）

**3. 「主若愿意」。** 「你们只当说：『主若愿意，我们就可以活着，也可以做这事，或做那事。』现今你们竟以张狂夸口；凡这样夸口都是恶的。」（4:15-16）
- **不是一句口头禅：** 保罗也常说「神若许我」「主若许我」（徒18:21；林前4:19）。但这不是一句护身符，也不像有些人说话要讨个吉利。它是一种心态：承认我的生命和计划都在神手中。「我终身的事在你手中。」（诗31:15）
- **「我们就可以活着」：** 雅各先说「活着」，再说「做这事，或做那事」。连明天能不能活着，都在神手里。
- **「张狂夸口」：** 以为自己掌握未来，这不只是不聪明，而是骄傲，是「恶的」。

**4. 「人若知道行善，却不去行」。** 「人若知道行善，却不去行，这就是他的罪了。」（4:17）
- **不作为的罪：** 罪不只是做了不该做的事，也包括没有做该做的事。这句话可能是总结前面的计划：知道应该说「主若愿意」，却仍然照自己的意思过日子；也可能是总结全书：「要行道，不要单单听道」（1:22）。

> 要点：我们的生命是一片云雾，明天如何，我们还不知道。智慧的人不是不做计划，而是把计划放在神手中，说：「主若愿意。」

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们的生命是什么呢？你们原来是一片云雾，出现少时就不见了。你们只当说：『主若愿意，我们就可以活着。』」（4:14-15）我的手机日历排得很满：下个月的出差，暑假的旅行，孩子明年升学，五年后换房，十年后退休。我很少想，这些计划里有没有神；更少想，明天我还在不在。在华人的习惯里，谈到死总觉得不吉利，过年不说，病床前也不敢说。可是雅各偏偏要我说：我的生命是一片云雾。他不是要吓我，而是要我诚实：我不是时间的主人。我心里也常常像那个财主，以为把仓房盖大一点，就可以对自己的灵魂说「安安逸逸地吃喝快乐吧」（路12:19）。我把神放在计划的边上，只在出了问题的时候才去求他。这是骄傲，雅各说这是「恶的」。可是就在这一章，雅各也说：「但他赐更多的恩典。」我的日子像云雾，那位赐恩的主却不是云雾。他也曾在地上过着有限的日子，按着父的时候行事（约7:30）；他说：「没有人夺我的命去，是我自己舍的。」（约10:18）他为我这个把神放在边上的罪人舍了命，担当了我的罪，又把命取回来。如今他说：「不要惧怕！……我曾死过，现在又活了，直活到永永远远；并且拿着死亡和阴间的钥匙。」（启1:17-18）我的明天，和我最怕的那一天，都在这位手里拿着钥匙的主手中。所以「主若愿意」不再是一句叫我不安的话，而是我能说出的最安稳的一句话：主啊，我终身的事在你手中（诗31:15）；我不知道明天，但我知道你。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 falls into three parts. **Where quarrels come from, and the way of humility** (vv. 1–10): quarrels and fights come from the passions at war within us; we do not have because we do not ask, or because we ask wrongly; friendship with the world is enmity with God; but he gives more grace, opposing the proud and giving grace to the humble; so submit to God, resist the devil, draw near to God, cleanse your hands and purify your hearts, humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you. **Do not speak evil against one another** (vv. 11–12): to speak against a brother is to speak against the law; there is only one lawgiver and judge, so who are you to judge your neighbor? **If the Lord wills** (vv. 13–17): do not say, "Today or tomorrow we will go into such and such a town," for you do not know what tomorrow will bring; your life is a mist; instead say, "If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that"; whoever knows the right thing to do and fails to do it, for him it is sin.

This chapter follows on from chapter 3. James 3:16 said, "For where jealousy and selfish ambition exist, there will be disorder and every vile practice." Now 4:1 asks, "What causes quarrels and what causes fights among you?" James digs deeper layer by layer: the quarrels outside come from desires inside; the desires inside show that our hearts want the world more than God. Yet the turning point of the whole chapter is one sentence in verse 6: "But he gives more grace." James's words are severe, but God's grace is greater.

## Going Deeper: 4:1–3 Where Quarrels Come From

**1. "Is it not this, that your passions are at war within you?"** "What causes quarrels and what causes fights among you? Is it not this, that your passions are at war within you?" (4:1).
- **James asks about the root:** we are used to saying we quarrel because the other person is unreasonable, or because we are under pressure. James makes us look inward: the root of the fighting is in us.
- **"Passions":** the Greek word often means pleasures (English "hedonism" comes from it). The same word is translated "passions" in 4:3. We want comfort, respect, our own way; when we do not get them, we fight whoever stands in the way.
- **"At war within you":** these desires do not only fight others; they also war inside us and give us no rest (see 1 Pet 2:11).

**2. "You do not have, because you do not ask."** "You desire and do not have, so you murder. You covet and cannot obtain, so you fight and quarrel. You do not have, because you do not ask. You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, to spend it on your passions" (4:2–3).
- **"Murder":** a strong word, and interpreters understand it differently. Many take it as hyperbole, as when the Lord Jesus said that anger against a brother is murder in the heart (Matt 5:21–22). Others take it as showing where unchecked desire finally leads, the same chain as 1:15: "desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin, and sin when it is fully grown brings forth death." Some think that some Jews of the time did take up the sword in political and economic conflict, and that James means it literally. A few (beginning with Erasmus) have guessed that the word was originally a very similar Greek word meaning "you envy" and was miscopied, but no ancient manuscript supports this.
- **"Because you do not ask":** we try every way to grab and fight for things ourselves, and never think to ask God. That is a kind of pride: I will do it myself.
- **"Because you ask wrongly":** sometimes we do ask, but only to satisfy our own pleasures. We treat God as a tool for our desires rather than the One we love. This is the reverse of 1:5–8: there, people lacking wisdom ask the generous God; here, people want pleasure, and even when they ask, they are double-minded.

> The point: The quarrels outside come from the desires inside. We do not have because we do not ask; we ask and do not receive because we treat God as a tool for our desires.

## Going Deeper: 4:4–10 He Gives More Grace

**1. "You adulterous people!"** "You adulterous people! Do you not know that friendship with the world is enmity with God? Therefore whoever wishes to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God" (4:4).
- **Old Testament language:** James speaks as the prophets did. The Greek word is feminine ("adulteresses"), the Old Testament picture of God as husband and his people as wife (compare "an evil and adulterous generation," Matt 12:39). The Old Testament often pictures God's relationship with Israel as a marriage, and Israel's worship of other gods as unfaithfulness to her husband: "Surely, as a treacherous wife leaves her husband, so have you been treacherous to me, O house of Israel" (Jer 3:20; see Hos 2:2). By speaking to believers this way, James wants them to see that a heart turned toward the world does not merely break a rule; it wounds the God who loves them.
- **"The world":** not the world God made, but the values that do not know God and are hostile to him: "the desires of the flesh and the desires of the eyes and pride in possessions" (1 John 2:16).
- **You cannot keep a foot in each boat:** the Lord Jesus said, "No one can serve two masters... You cannot serve God and money" (Matt 6:24). Whoever tries to please both sides is the "double-minded" person of 1:8 and 4:8.

**2. "He yearns jealously over the spirit."** "Or do you suppose it is to no purpose that the Scripture says, 'He yearns jealously over the spirit that he has made to dwell in us'?" (4:5).
- **A difficult verse:** this is one of the hardest verses in James to translate. No Old Testament verse matches it exactly; James may be summing up an Old Testament theme, or, as others think, "the Scripture says" looks ahead to the quotation in verse 6. Interpreters read it in two main ways:
- **God's jealousy:** the ESV and many modern translations take it to mean that God yearns jealously over the spirit he has made to dwell in us. Some take "the spirit" as the breath of life God gave humanity; others as the Holy Spirit. God is like a husband who loves his wife deeply and cannot bear for her heart to be shared with another. The Old Testament says more than once that the LORD is "a jealous God" (Exod 20:5; 34:14).
- **Human envy:** some interpreters and translations (e.g. the KJV) take it to mean that the spirit God made to dwell in us (the human heart) is full of envious desires. Their reason is that the word for "envy" here (*phthonos*) is always a vice in the New Testament (see Gal 5:21) and is never used of God. Read this way, the verse links with the desires and quarrels of 4:1–2. Those who read it as God's jealousy reply that the verb "yearns" is used positively elsewhere (Phil 1:8; 1 Pet 2:2).
- **The Chinese Union Version:** the CUV makes "the spirit" the subject and phrases the verse as a question, close to the 1881 English Revised Version. It can be read as the Spirit yearning jealously over us, or as a rhetorical question: does the spirit God gave long for envy?
- **Either way:** the next verse says, "But he gives more grace." If our hearts keep leaning toward the world, God's grace is more. If God is jealous, his jealousy comes from love.

**3. "But he gives more grace."** "But he gives more grace. Therefore it says, 'God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble'" (4:6).
- **The turning point of the chapter:** James has just called his readers "adulterous people" and warned them that friendship with the world is enmity with God, but he does not stop at condemnation. Our sin is heavy; God's grace is more. God does not turn his back on an unfaithful people; he gives more grace.
- **From Proverbs:** "Toward the scorners he is scornful, but to the humble he gives favor" (Prov 3:34). James and Peter both quote it almost exactly in the Greek Old Testament wording, with "God" for "the Lord" (1 Pet 5:5).
- **Grace for whom?** Not for those who feel they do not need it, but for the humble. The proud stand before God and say, "I will do it myself." The humble say, "I need you." The Lord Jesus told of the tax collector: "everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, but the one who humbles himself will be exalted" (Luke 18:14).

**4. "Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you."** "Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded" (4:7–8).
- **"Therefore":** this whole series of commands rests on "he gives more grace." It is not that we must first become good to receive grace; it is because grace has already been given that we are to come back to God.
- **Submit to God, resist the devil:** the two go together. Peter says the same: "Resist him, firm in your faith" (1 Pet 5:9). The devil is not all-powerful; believers, relying on God, can make him flee. Submitting to God does not mean submitting to everyone who claims to speak for God. In domestic violence, abuse or spiritual manipulation, leaving danger and seeking help from the police or someone trustworthy is not disobedience to God.
- **"Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you":** this does not mean God waits for us to take the first step. Throughout Scripture it is always God who comes looking first (1:18). But the sentence tells us that the way back to God is open. Like the prodigal: "But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him" (Luke 15:20).
- **Hands and heart:** "cleanse your hands" is repentance in what we do; "purify your hearts" is repentance within, recalling the one with "clean hands and a pure heart" (Ps 24:4). The prophets also said, "Return to me, and I will return to you" (Zech 1:3; Mal 3:7).

**5. "Let your laughter be turned to mourning."** "Be wretched and mourn and weep. Let your laughter be turned to mourning and your joy to gloom. Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you" (4:9–10).
- **Grieving over sin:** James is not saying believers may never laugh. He is saying that those who laugh off their sin and treat it lightly must stop and grieve over it. The Lord Jesus said, "Blessed are those who mourn" (Matt 5:4).
- **"He will exalt you":** the way of humility does not lead to despair but to being lifted up by God. God dwells "with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit, to revive the spirit of the lowly" (Isa 57:15).

> The point: Our hearts keep leaning toward the world, yet "he gives more grace." God opposes the proud and gives grace to the humble. The way back to God is open: submit to God, resist the devil, grieve over sin, draw near to God, and he will draw near to us.

## Going Deeper: 4:11–12 Do Not Speak Evil Against One Another

**"Do not speak evil against one another, brothers. The one who speaks against a brother or judges his brother, speaks evil against the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge. There is only one lawgiver and judge, he who is able to save and to destroy. But who are you to judge your neighbor?"** (4:11–12).
- **"Speak evil against":** the word means to speak against or run someone down, often behind their back. It ties back to the tongue in chapter 3.
- **Why is speaking against a brother speaking against the law?** The law says, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself" (2:8); in the same passage Leviticus says, "You shall not go around as a slanderer among your people" (Lev 19:16–18). When I run down my brother behind his back, I am saying that this law does not apply to me, that I stand above it.
- **"Only one":** God alone is lawgiver and judge. The Lord Jesus said, "Judge not" (Matt 7:1). Paul says, "Therefore do not pronounce judgment before the time, before the Lord comes" (1 Cor 4:5).
- **This is not indifference to sin:** what James forbids is slander and self-righteous condemnation. He does not forbid pointing out a brother's fault in truth (Matt 18:15–17), nor telling the truth about harm and crime. When someone who has been harmed tells what happened, or reports it to the church or the police, that is not "speaking evil against a brother"; it is seeking justice. Reporting crime to the police is right (Rom 13:4). Nor may a church use this verse to silence victims or to hide abuse in order to save face.

> The point: God alone gives the law and judges. When I judge my brother behind his back, I put myself in God's place.

## Going Deeper: 4:13–17 If the Lord Wills

**1. "Today or tomorrow we will go into such and such a town."** "Come now, you who say, 'Today or tomorrow we will go into such and such a town and spend a year there and trade and make a profit'" (4:13).
- **Traders:** James addresses people who trade from town to town. Whether they were church members is debated (compare the address to the rich in 5:1–6). James does not say that business or planning is wrong.
- **So what is the problem?** The sentence has "we," a town, a year and a profit, but no God. They speak as though they were masters of time.

**2. "You are a mist."** "Yet you do not know what tomorrow will bring. What is your life? For you are a mist that appears for a little time and then vanishes" (4:14).
- **From Proverbs:** "Do not boast about tomorrow, for you do not know what a day may bring" (Prov 27:1).
- **"A mist":** morning mist, gone as soon as the sun is up. The prophet says they "shall be like the morning mist" (Hos 13:3); the psalmist says, "Surely all mankind stands as a mere breath!" (Ps 39:5). The Lord Jesus told of a rich man who planned bigger barns, but God said to him, "Fool! This night your soul is required of you" (Luke 12:20).

**3. "If the Lord wills."** "Instead you ought to say, 'If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that.' As it is, you boast in your arrogance. All such boasting is evil" (4:15–16).
- **Not a stock phrase:** Paul too often said "if God wills," "if the Lord wills" (Acts 18:21; 1 Cor 4:19). But it is not a charm, nor like the auspicious words some people use for luck. It is an attitude: acknowledging that my life and plans are in God's hands. "My times are in your hand" (Ps 31:15).
- **"We will live":** James says "live" before "do this or that." Even whether we live tomorrow is in God's hands.
- **"Boast in your arrogance":** to think we control the future is not only unwise; it is pride, and it is "evil."

**4. "Whoever knows the right thing to do and fails to do it."** "So whoever knows the right thing to do and fails to do it, for him it is sin" (4:17).
- **Sins of omission:** sin is not only doing what we should not, but also failing to do what we should. The sentence may sum up the paragraph on plans: knowing we ought to say "If the Lord wills," yet still living our own way. Or it may sum up the whole letter: "be doers of the word, and not hearers only" (1:22).

> The point: Our life is a mist; we do not know what tomorrow will bring. The wise do not stop making plans; they put their plans in God's hands and say, "If the Lord wills."

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "What is your life? For you are a mist that appears for a little time and then vanishes. Instead you ought to say, 'If the Lord wills, we will live'" (4:14–15). The calendar on my phone is full: next month's business trip, the summer holiday, my child's move to a new school next year, a new flat in five years, retirement in ten. I seldom ask whether God is in these plans; I ask even less whether I will be here tomorrow. In Chinese custom, talk of death is thought to bring bad luck; no one mentions it at New Year, and few dare to mention it even at a hospital bedside. Yet James insists that I say it: my life is a mist. He is not trying to frighten me but to make me honest: I am not the master of time. My heart is often like the rich man who thought that if he built bigger barns he could say to his soul, "relax, eat, drink, be merry" (Luke 12:19). I put God at the edge of my plans and only call on him when something goes wrong. That is pride, and James calls it "evil." Yet in this same chapter James also says, "But he gives more grace." My days are a mist, but the Lord who gives grace is not. He too lived limited days on earth and acted according to his Father's time (John 7:30). He said, "No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord" (John 10:18). He laid down his life for me, a sinner who pushed God to the edge, bearing my sin, and he took it up again. Now he says, "Fear not... I died, and behold I am alive forevermore, and I have the keys of Death and Hades" (Rev 1:17–18). My tomorrow, and the day I fear most, are in the hands of the Lord who holds those keys. So "If the Lord wills" is no longer a sentence that unsettles me. It is the most secure sentence I can say: Lord, my times are in your hand (Ps 31:15); I do not know tomorrow, but I know you.
