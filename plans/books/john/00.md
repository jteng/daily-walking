## 为什么要逐卷读

每日读经计划带我们在一年里走遍全本圣经，但步子很快，一天常常四五章。逐卷研读是另一种读法：一次只读一卷书，一天一章，在一章里停下来，看几处值得细看的地方——一个希腊词、一处旧约的回声、各译本之间的差异、作者的一个写作手法。目的不是多知道一些资料，而是学会像作者希望的那样去读他的书。

## 作者与写作背景

这卷书没有在开头署名。书中的见证人自称「耶稣所爱的那门徒」（13:23；21:20），书末说：「为这些事作见证，并且记载这些事的，就是这门徒。」（21:24）早期教会几乎一致认为他就是西庇太的儿子使徒约翰。二世纪的爱任纽曾在少年时听过坡旅甲讲道，而坡旅甲是约翰的门徒；爱任纽记载约翰晚年住在以弗所，并在那里写了这卷福音书。多数学者把成书时间定在主后85至95年之间。现代学界对作者身份有不同的看法，但书中处处可见亲历者的细节：石缸的数目（2:6）、一百五十三条鱼（21:11）、「那时约有午正」（4:6）。

## 钥匙经文：20:31

**1. 作者亲口说明写作目的。** 四福音中只有约翰这样做：「但记这些事要叫你们信耶稣是基督，是神的儿子，并且叫你们信了他，就可以因他的名得生命。」全书所记的神迹、对话、讲论，都是为这个目的挑选出来的（20:30说耶稣另外行了许多神迹，「没有记在这书上」）。所以读每一章都可以问：这一段要我相信耶稣是谁？

**2. 一个字母的差别：「开始相信」还是「继续相信」？** 「叫你们信」的希腊文动词，古抄本有两种写法：*pisteusēte*（不定过去时，有「开始相信」的意味）和 *pisteuēte*（现在时，「持续地相信」）。前者像是写给尚未信主的人的传福音之书，后者像是写给已经相信的人，要他们坚守下去。学者至今没有定论。卡森则从句子结构指出，这句话也可以读成「叫你们信基督就是耶稣」，是在回答「弥赛亚是谁？」这个问题，因此他认为本书首先是一卷向犹太人和归信犹太教的外邦人传福音的书。对读者来说，两种读法都成立：慕道的人在这卷书里遇见基督，信了多年的人在这卷书里被托住，继续信下去。

**3. 「信」是动词，不是名词。** 约翰福音用「信」这个动词大约98次，比其他三卷福音书加起来还多，却一次也没有用名词「信心」（*pistis*）。对约翰来说，信不是一样拥有的东西，而是一个不断发生的动作：来就他、接待他、吃他、住在他里面。他常写「信入」（*pisteuō eis*，「信」加上表示方向的介词），信是朝着一个人走过去。

## 全书的结构

- **序言**（1:1-18）：道成肉身。全书的主题在这里先奏一遍。
- **神迹之书**（1:19–12:50）：耶稣在世人面前行神迹、讲论，引起信与不信两种反应。
- **荣耀之书**（13:1–20:31）：耶稣的「时候」到了。洗脚、楼上讲论、大祭司的祷告、受难、复活。
- **尾声**（21章）：提比哩亚海边，彼得被恢复。

## 七个神迹与七个「我是」

约翰从不把耶稣的神迹叫作「异能」，而叫作「神迹」（*sēmeia*，记号、指标）。记号的意义不在本身，而在它所指向的。他选了七个：变水为酒（2章）、医治大臣的儿子（4章）、医治毕士大池边的瘫子（5章）、五饼二鱼（6章）、在海面上行走（6章）、医治生来瞎眼的（9章）、叫拉撒路复活（11章）。

与神迹并行的，是耶稣七次带着比喻的「我是」：生命的粮（6:35）、世界的光（8:12）、羊的门（10:7）、好牧人（10:11）、复活和生命（11:25）、道路真理生命（14:6）、真葡萄树（15:1）。此外还有几处单独的「我是」（*egō eimi*），最震撼的是8:58：「还没有亚伯拉罕就有了我。」这句话回响着出埃及记3:14神在荆棘火中的自我启示（希腊文七十士译本作 *egō eimi*），听的人听懂了，所以拿石头要打他。

## 读这卷书的三把钥匙

**1. 留意误解。** 约翰最常用的写法，是让人物把耶稣的话听成字面意思：尼哥德慕以为「重生」是再进母腹（3章），撒玛利亚妇人以为「活水」是井水（4章），众人以为「这殿」是圣殿（2章）。每当你读到一个误解，就停下来问：耶稣真正说的是哪一层？

**2. 留意关键词。** 信、生命、光、真理、见证、世界、荣耀、时候、常在（*menō*，也译「住」「存留」）。这些词在书中反复出现，意思一层层加深。

**3. 留意节期。** 逾越节（2、6、11-19章）、住棚节（7-8章）、修殿节（10章）。耶稣常在节期中说话，显明自己就是那节期所预表的实体：住棚节点灯，他说「我是世界的光」；住棚节浇水，他说「人若渴了，可以到我这里来喝」。

## 与另外三卷福音书的不同

约翰福音约有九成的内容是另外三卷没有的：没有登山宝训，没有赶鬼，几乎没有比喻，却有长篇的对话与讲论。二世纪末的亚历山大的革利免称它为「属灵的福音」。这不是说其他福音不属灵，而是说约翰默想同一位耶稣多年之后，写出了更深一层的见证。他不是在纠正其他福音，而是在它们旁边，帮助我们看见同一位主的更深处。

## 默想

## Why Read Book by Book

The daily plan takes us through the whole Bible in a year, but at a brisk pace, often four or five chapters a day. Book study is another way to read: one book at a time, one chapter a day, stopping inside each chapter to look closely at a few places that reward it. That might be a Greek word, an Old Testament echo, a difference between translations, or one of the author's literary habits. The aim is not to collect information but to learn to read the book the way its author meant it to be read.

## Author and Setting

The book carries no name at the top. Its witness calls himself "the disciple whom Jesus loved" (13:23; 21:20), and the closing lines say, "This is the disciple who is bearing witness about these things, and who has written these things" (21:24). The early church agreed almost unanimously that this was John the son of Zebedee. In the second century Irenaeus, who as a boy had heard Polycarp preach (Polycarp was a disciple of John), recorded that John lived at Ephesus in his old age and wrote his Gospel there. Most scholars date it between AD 85 and 95. Modern scholarship holds a range of views on authorship, but the book is full of eyewitness detail: the number of stone jars (2:6), the 153 fish (21:11), "it was about the sixth hour" (4:6).

## The Key Verse: 20:31

**1. The author states his purpose outright.** John is the only Gospel writer who does this: "these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name." Every sign, conversation and discourse was selected for this purpose (20:30 says Jesus did many other signs "not written in this book"). So in every chapter we can ask: what does this passage want me to believe about who Jesus is?

**2. One letter of difference: "come to believe" or "go on believing"?** The ancient manuscripts have two forms of the verb "believe" here: *pisteusēte* (aorist, suggesting "come to believe") and *pisteuēte* (present, "keep on believing"). The first makes John an evangelistic book for those who don't yet believe; the second makes it a book for believers, to help them hold on. Scholars haven't settled it. Carson, arguing from the sentence's grammar, reads it as "that you may believe that the Christ is Jesus," answering the question "Who is the Messiah?", and so sees John first as an evangelistic book for Jews and Gentile converts to Judaism. For the reader, both work: seekers meet Christ in this book, and long-time believers are held up by it and keep believing.

**3. "Believe" is a verb, not a noun.** John uses the verb "believe" about 98 times, more than the other three Gospels combined, yet never once uses the noun "faith" (*pistis*). For John, believing isn't something you possess but something you keep doing: coming to him, receiving him, eating him, abiding in him. He often writes "believe into" (*pisteuō eis*, the verb plus a preposition of direction). Believing means moving toward a person.

## The Shape of the Book

- **Prologue** (1:1-18): The Word became flesh. The book's themes are sounded here first, like an overture.
- **The Book of Signs** (1:19–12:50): Jesus performs signs and teaches in public, drawing out both belief and unbelief.
- **The Book of Glory** (13:1–20:31): Jesus' "hour" has come. The foot-washing, the Upper Room discourse, the high-priestly prayer, the passion and the resurrection.
- **Epilogue** (ch. 21): By the Sea of Tiberias, Peter is restored.

## Seven Signs and Seven "I Am" Sayings

John never calls Jesus' miracles "mighty works" (*dynameis*). He calls them "signs" (*sēmeia*), markers whose meaning lies not in themselves but in what they point to. He selects seven: water into wine (ch. 2), healing the official's son (ch. 4), healing the paralyzed man at Bethesda (ch. 5), feeding the five thousand (ch. 6), walking on the sea (ch. 6), healing the man born blind (ch. 9), and raising Lazarus (ch. 11).

Alongside the signs run seven "I am" sayings with images: the bread of life (6:35), the light of the world (8:12), the door of the sheep (10:7), the good shepherd (10:11), the resurrection and the life (11:25), the way, the truth and the life (14:6), the true vine (15:1). There are also several bare "I am" statements (*egō eimi*), most startlingly 8:58: "before Abraham was, I am." This echoes God's self-revelation at the burning bush in Exodus 3:14 (where the Greek Septuagint has *egō eimi*). His hearers understood him perfectly, which is why they picked up stones.

## Three Keys for Reading John

**1. Watch for misunderstandings.** John's favorite technique is to let a character take Jesus' words at the literal level. Nicodemus thinks being "born again" means re-entering the womb (ch. 3). The Samaritan woman thinks "living water" means well water (ch. 4). The crowd thinks "this temple" means the building (ch. 2). Whenever you meet a misunderstanding, stop and ask what level Jesus was really speaking on.

**2. Watch the key words.** Believe, life, light, truth, witness, world, glory, hour, abide (*menō*, also "remain," "dwell"). These words recur throughout the book, gaining depth each time.

**3. Watch the feasts.** Passover (chs. 2, 6, 11–19), Tabernacles (chs. 7–8), Dedication (ch. 10). Jesus often speaks at a feast, revealing himself as the reality the feast pointed to. At Tabernacles, when great lamps were lit, he says "I am the light of the world." At Tabernacles, when water was poured out, he says "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink."

## How John Differs from the Other Gospels

About ninety percent of John's material appears in none of the other three: no Sermon on the Mount, no exorcisms, almost no parables, but long conversations and discourses. Around AD 200 Clement of Alexandria called it "the spiritual Gospel." He didn't mean the others weren't spiritual. He meant that John, after meditating on the same Jesus for many years, wrote a witness that goes a layer deeper. John isn't correcting the other Gospels. He stands beside them and helps us see further into the same Lord.

## Meditation

Do I want to see clearly, for the first time, who Jesus is? Or am I weary and need to be held up so that I can keep believing?
