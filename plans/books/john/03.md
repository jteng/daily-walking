## 本章脉络

第三章分三段。**尼哥德慕夜访**（1-15节）：一位法利赛人、犹太人的官，夜里来见耶稣，对话从「重生」开始，到「人子被举起来」结束。**神爱世人**（16-21节）：讲论转向更广的宣告，光来到世间，人要么来就光，要么躲在黑暗里。**施洗约翰最后的见证**（22-36节）：约翰的门徒为跟从耶稣的人越来越多而不安，约翰却说：「他必兴旺，我必衰微。」

尼哥德慕是约翰福音里一条安静的线索。他「夜里」来（3:2），后来在公会里替耶稣说了半句公道话（7:50-51），最后在耶稣死后，带着约一百斤的没药和沉香来安葬他（19:39）。这样的分量是给君王用的。约翰特别注明他就是「先前夜里去见耶稣的」，好像在说：那个夜里来的人，似乎一步一步走向了光。他是否完全公开地信了，解经家有不同看法。约翰福音里的「夜」常有象征意味：犹大出去的时候，「那时候是夜间了」（13:30）。

## 深读 3:3「人若不重生，就不能见神的国」

**1. 一个字，两个意思。** 「重生」的「重」，希腊文是 *anōthen*。这个字可以是「再一次」，也可以是「从上头」。和合本译作「重生」，并在小字里注明「或作从上头生」。英文译本也分成两派：传统的「born again」与较新译本的「born from above」。

**2. 尼哥德慕听成了「再一次」。** 他问：「人已经老了，如何能重生呢？岂能再进母腹生出来吗？」（3:4）他把 *anōthen* 理解为时间上的「第二次」。但约翰自己在同一章用这个字的时候，意思明显是「从上头」：「从天上来的是在万有之上」（3:31，「从天上来的」就是 *ho anōthen erchomenos*）。耶稣在十九章对彼拉多说：「若不是从上头赐给你的，你就毫无权柄办我。」（19:11）用的也是这个字。所以耶稣强调的是来源：这个新生命不是人能自己再来一次的，它是从神那里来的。

**3. 两层意思其实都对。** 从上头来的生命，对人来说确实是第二次的出生。这是约翰典型的双关，误解的人只抓住了一半。

**4. 「神的国」在约翰福音里很少见。** 对观福音常说「神的国」，约翰福音却只在这里（3:3、5）用这个词。约翰平常用的对应说法是「永生」。所以本章从「见神的国」开始，到「得永生」（3:15-16）结束，两个说法讲的是同一样东西：神掌权的那种生命，现在就可以进入。

> 要点：重生不是宗教上的自我改进，不是「再来一次」，而是一个从上头来、由神主动赐下的新开始。

## 深读 3:5-8「从水和圣灵生的」

**1. 「水和圣灵」是什么意思？** 解经家有几种看法：有人认为「水」指肉身的出生（羊水），有人认为指洗礼。但在希腊文里，「水和圣灵」共用一个介词（*ex hydatos kai pneumatos*），看起来是一次出生的两个方面，而不是两次出生。

**2. 以西结书的背景。** 许多福音派解经家（如卡森）认为，最好的背景是以西结书36:25-27：「我必用清水洒在你们身上，你们就洁净了……我也要赐给你们一个新心，将新灵放在你们里面。」水象征洁净，灵象征新生命，这正是先知应许的新约。所以耶稣接着说：「你是以色列人的先生，还不明白这事吗？」（3:10）原文这里有冠词，「那位以色列的先生」。一位精通圣经的老师，应该从以西结书就认得出这个应许。

**3. 风与灵是同一个字。** 「风随着意思吹」（3:8）的「风」，和「圣灵」的「灵」，在希腊文里都是 *pneuma*，希伯来文的 *ruach* 也是如此。以西结书37章的枯骨异象里，先知向「气息」（*ruach*）说预言，枯骨就活了。耶稣用这个双关告诉尼哥德慕：你看不见风，却看得见它的果效；圣灵的工作也是这样，人不能控制，却能经历。

## 深读 3:14-15「摩西在旷野怎样举蛇，人子也必照样被举起来」

**1. 民数记21章的故事。** 以色列人在旷野发怨言，神使火蛇进入百姓中间。摩西为百姓祷告，神吩咐他造一条蛇挂在杆子上，摩西就用铜造了一条，「凡被蛇咬的，一望这铜蛇就活了」（民21:9）。治疗的方法不是做什么，而是「望」。耶稣说，信他也是这样：「叫一切信他的都得永生。」

**2. 「举起来」是双关。** 希腊文 *hypsoō* 有「举高」也有「高举、尊荣」的意思。约翰福音三次说人子要被「举起来」（3:14；8:28；12:32-34），在12:33约翰直接解释：「耶稣这话原是指着自己将要怎样死说的。」被钉在十字架上被举起，同时也是被高举得荣耀。以赛亚书52:13（七十士译本）说到受苦的仆人「必被举起，大得荣耀」（按七十士译本直译；和合本作「必被高举上升，且成为至高」），用的正是「举起」和「荣耀」这两个字。在约翰福音里，十字架不只是耶稣的羞辱，也是他的荣耀。

**3. 「必」字。** 「人子也**必**照样被举起来」，希腊文的 *dei* 表示神计划中的必然。本章有三个这样的「必」：「你们**必须**重生」（3:7），「人子也**必**照样被举起来」（3:14），「他**必**兴旺，我**必**衰微」（3:30）。新生、十字架、谦卑，都在神的计划之中。

## 深读 3:16「神爱世人，甚至将他的独生子赐给他们」

**1. 神「怎样」爱：重在方式。** 英文常译作「God so loved」，读者容易以为「so」是在说爱的程度有多大。希腊文的 *houtōs* 更常表示「这样地、以这种方式」。和合本没有把 *houtōs* 单独译出，「甚至」对应的是后面的 *hōste*。所以这节经文首先说的是神**怎样**爱：神是以赐下儿子的方式来爱世人。紧跟着的 *hōste* 加上直述语气的「赐」，强调这不是假设，而是真实发生的结果。当然，以这种方式去爱，本身就显出了爱的深度。这「赐」紧接着3:14的「被举起来」：神赐下儿子，是让他上十字架，替我们承受我们该受的审判，使信他的人「不致灭亡」（3:16），不再在神的震怒之下（3:36）。

**2. 「世人」是谁？** 原文是「世界」（*kosmos*）。这个字在约翰福音出现了将近八十次，常指与神为敌的人类世界：「世界却不认识他」（1:10）；「世人……不爱光，倒爱黑暗」（3:19）。所以神爱「世界」，令人惊讶的不是世界有多大，而是世界有多坏。神爱的是背叛他的人。

**3. 「信」和「永生」。** 「叫一切信他的」，原文是「信入他」（*pisteuōn eis auton*），信是朝向一个人。「永生」（*zōē aiōnios*）字面是「属于那个世代的生命」，就是神国来临时的生命。约翰说这生命现在就可以拥有：「信子的人**有**永生」（3:36），是现在时。

**4. 这话是谁说的？** 希腊文没有引号。耶稣对尼哥德慕的话是到15节为止，还是一直到21节？各译本标点不同：和合本在16节另起引号，英文ESV在15节结束引语，NIV加注说「有解经者认为引语到15节结束」，16-21节可能是约翰的默想。不论是哪一种，这段话都是福音书作者在圣灵默示下给我们的话，权威并没有不同。

> 要点：神的爱不是因为世界可爱，而是不顾世界的不可爱；这爱的证据，是他赐下了独生子。

## 深读 3:30「他必兴旺，我必衰微」

**1. 门徒的不安。** 约翰的门徒来报告：「你所见证的那位，现在施洗，众人都往他那里去了。」（3:26）这听起来像是在抱怨竞争。

**2. 新郎的朋友。** 约翰用婚礼作比喻：「娶新妇的就是新郎；新郎的朋友站着，听见新郎的声音就甚喜乐。」（3:29）在犹太婚俗中，新郎的朋友负责安排婚事、把新娘带到新郎面前。他的工作完成的时候，正是他退到一旁的时候。旧约常以神为以色列的丈夫（何2:16-20；赛62:5），约翰称耶稣为「新郎」，是一个很高的称呼。

**3. 喜乐满足了。** 「故此，我这喜乐满足了。」约翰的喜乐不在于自己被看见，而在于新郎被看见。「满足」（*peplērōtai*）这个动词（*plēroō*），耶稣后来在楼上讲论中也用来说门徒的喜乐（15:11；17:13）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 has three parts. **Nicodemus comes by night** (vv. 1–15): a Pharisee and ruler of the Jews comes to Jesus at night, and the conversation runs from being "born again" to the Son of Man being "lifted up." **God so loved the world** (vv. 16–21): the discourse widens into a broad announcement. The light has come into the world, and people either come to it or hide in the dark. **John the Baptist's final witness** (vv. 22–36): John's disciples are troubled that more and more people are following Jesus, but John says, "He must increase, but I must decrease."

Nicodemus is a quiet thread through John's Gospel. He comes "by night" (3:2). Later he speaks half a sentence in Jesus' defense before the council (7:50–51). Finally, after Jesus' death, he brings about seventy-five pounds of myrrh and aloes to bury him (19:39), a quantity fit for a king. John notes that he was the one "who earlier had come to Jesus by night," as if to say that the man who came in the dark seems to have moved step by step toward the light. Whether he came to full, open faith is debated. Night in John often carries symbolic weight: when Judas goes out, "it was night" (13:30).

## Going Deeper: 3:3 "Unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God"

**1. One word, two meanings.** "Again" translates the Greek *anōthen*, which can mean "again" or "from above." The Chinese Union Version renders it "born again" with a note, "or, born from above." English translations split the same way: the traditional "born again" and the newer "born from above."

**2. Nicodemus hears "again."** He asks, "How can a man be born when he is old? Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb and be born?" (3:4). He takes *anōthen* as a second time. But when John himself uses the word later in this chapter, it clearly means "from above": "He who comes from above is above all" (3:31, "who comes from above" being *ho anōthen erchomenos*). In chapter 19 Jesus tells Pilate, "You would have no authority over me at all unless it had been given you from above" (19:11), with the same word. So Jesus is stressing the source. This new life isn't something a person can do over by himself; it comes from God.

**3. Both meanings are true.** Life from above really is, for us, a second birth. It's a typical Johannine double meaning, and the man who misunderstands has grasped only half of it.

**4. "Kingdom of God" is rare in John.** The other Gospels speak of the kingdom of God often; John uses the phrase only here (3:3, 5). His usual equivalent is "eternal life." So this chapter begins with "seeing the kingdom of God" and ends with "having eternal life" (3:15–16). Both describe the same thing: the life of God's reign, which can be entered now.

> The point: New birth isn't religious self-improvement or a second attempt. It's a new beginning that comes from above and that God himself gives.

## Going Deeper: 3:5–8 "Born of water and the Spirit"

**1. What do "water and Spirit" mean?** Interpreters hold several views. Some think "water" refers to physical birth (the amniotic fluid), others to baptism. But in Greek "water and Spirit" share a single preposition (*ex hydatos kai pneumatos*), which suggests two aspects of one birth rather than two births.

**2. The background in Ezekiel.** Many evangelical interpreters (Carson among them) think the best background is Ezekiel 36:25–27: "I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean... And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you." Water stands for cleansing and spirit for new life; this is the new covenant the prophet promised. That's why Jesus goes on, "Are you the teacher of Israel and yet you do not understand these things?" (3:10). The Greek has the article: "*the* teacher of Israel." A master of the Scriptures should have recognized this promise from Ezekiel.

**3. Wind and Spirit are the same word.** "The wind blows where it wishes" (3:8): "wind" and "Spirit" are both *pneuma* in Greek, as both are *ruach* in Hebrew. In Ezekiel 37's valley of dry bones, the prophet prophesies to the "breath" (*ruach*), and the bones live. Jesus uses the double meaning to tell Nicodemus: you can't see the wind, but you can see what it does. The Spirit's work is like that. No one controls it, but it can be experienced.

## Going Deeper: 3:14–15 "As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up"

**1. The story in Numbers 21.** Israel grumbled in the wilderness, and God sent fiery serpents among the people. Moses prayed, and God told him to make a serpent and set it on a pole, so Moses made one of bronze: "if a serpent bit anyone, he would look at the bronze serpent and live" (Num 21:9). The cure wasn't something to do but somewhere to look. Jesus says believing in him is like that: "that whoever believes in him may have eternal life."

**2. "Lifted up" is a double meaning.** The Greek *hypsoō* means both "raise up" and "exalt." Three times John says the Son of Man must be "lifted up" (3:14; 8:28; 12:32–34), and in 12:33 John explains outright: "He said this to show by what kind of death he was going to die." Being raised on the cross is also being exalted in glory. Isaiah 52:13 in the Septuagint says the suffering servant "shall be lifted up and glorified," using exactly these two words. In John the cross isn't only Jesus' humiliation; it is also his glory.

**3. The word "must."** "So *must* the Son of Man be lifted up." The Greek *dei* signals necessity within God's plan. This chapter has three such "musts": "You *must* be born again" (3:7), "so *must* the Son of Man be lifted up" (3:14), and "He *must* increase, but I *must* decrease" (3:30). New birth, the cross and humility all lie within God's plan.

## Going Deeper: 3:16 "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son"

**1. "So" stresses the manner.** Readers often take "God *so* loved" to describe how much God loved. The Greek *houtōs* more often means "in this way." So the verse first tells us *how* God loved: by giving his Son. The following *hōste* with the indicative "gave" stresses that this is not hypothetical but an actual result. Of course, loving in this way also shows the depth of the love. This "gave" follows directly on 3:14's "lifted up": God gave his Son to the cross, to bear the judgment we deserved, so that believers "should not perish" (3:16) and are no longer under God's wrath (3:36).

**2. Who is "the world"?** The word is *kosmos*, which appears nearly eighty times in John and often means humanity in rebellion against God: "the world did not know him" (1:10); "people loved the darkness rather than the light" (3:19). So the surprise in God loving "the world" isn't how big the world is but how bad it is. God loves people who have turned against him.

**3. "Believe" and "eternal life."** "Whoever believes in him" is literally "believes *into* him" (*pisteuōn eis auton*): believing is moving toward a person. "Eternal life" (*zōē aiōnios*) is literally "the life of the age," the life of the coming kingdom. John says it can be possessed now: "Whoever believes in the Son *has* eternal life" (3:36), in the present tense.

**4. Who is speaking here?** Greek has no quotation marks. Do Jesus' words to Nicodemus end at verse 15 or run through verse 21? Translations punctuate differently: the ESV closes the quotation at verse 15, the Chinese Union Version opens a new quotation at verse 16, and the NIV notes that "some interpreters end the quotation with verse 15," making verses 16–21 the evangelist's reflection. Either way, these are words the Gospel writer gives us under the Spirit's inspiration, with the same authority.

> The point: God's love isn't drawn out by the world's loveliness; it comes in spite of the world's unloveliness. The proof of that love is that he gave his only Son.

## Going Deeper: 3:30 "He must increase, but I must decrease"

**1. The disciples' unease.** John's disciples report, "He who was with you across the Jordan, to whom you bore witness, look, he is baptizing, and all are going to him" (3:26). It sounds like a complaint about competition.

**2. The friend of the bridegroom.** John answers with a wedding picture: "The one who has the bride is the bridegroom. The friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly at the bridegroom's voice" (3:29). In Jewish custom the bridegroom's friend arranged the wedding and brought the bride to the groom. The moment his work was done was the moment he stepped aside. The Old Testament often pictures God as Israel's husband (Hos 2:16–20; Isa 62:5), so calling Jesus "the bridegroom" is a high title.

**3. Joy made complete.** "Therefore this joy of mine is now complete." John's joy lies not in being seen himself but in the bridegroom being seen. The verb behind "complete" (*peplērōtai*, from *plēroō*) is the one Jesus later uses of his disciples' joy in the Upper Room (15:11; 17:13).

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Nicodemus came "by night" and seems to have moved, slowly, step by step toward the light. Is some part of my knowledge of Jesus still "at night," something I'm afraid to own in public? John said, "He must increase, but I must decrease." In my service, family or work, is there a place where I need to step back so that Christ is seen?
