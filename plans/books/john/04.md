## 本章脉络

第四章分两大段。**撒玛利亚妇人**（1-42节）是约翰福音里最长的一段一对一对话：从「给我水喝」开始，经过活水、五个丈夫、在哪座山上敬拜，到耶稣说出「我就是」，最后整座城的人出来见他。**医治大臣的儿子**（43-54节）是第二个神迹，回到加利利的迦拿。

这一章和第三章刚好成对：尼哥德慕是犹太人、男人、宗教领袖，在夜里来；撒玛利亚妇人是外族、女人，也许还处在社会边缘，在正午遇见耶稣。尼哥德慕从「我们知道」开始，最后却不明白；妇人从一句话也不懂开始，最后却跑回城里作见证。耶稣向两个完全不同的人，说了同一个福音。

## 深读 4:4-9「必须经过撒玛利亚」

**1. 「必须」是地理还是神意？** 从犹太到加利利，经过撒玛利亚是最直接的路。犹太史家约瑟夫记载，加利利人上耶路撒冷过节时常常走这条路。所以「必须」可以只是说路线。但约翰用的是 *edei*，正是第三章「必」的那个字（3:7、14、30）。在约翰福音里，这个字常带着神计划的意味。也许两层都有：这条路是最近的，而在这条路上，有一个人是父要耶稣去找的（4:23「父要这样的人拜他」）。

**2. 井旁相遇是旧约的一个熟悉场景。** 亚伯拉罕的仆人在井旁遇见利百加（创24章），雅各在井旁遇见拉结（创29章），摩西在井旁遇见西坡拉（出2章），每一次都以婚姻结束。有学者指出，约翰刚在上一章称耶稣为「新郎」（3:29），这一章就让他坐在「雅各井」旁，遇见一个女人。但这次的相遇完全出人意料：她已经有过五个丈夫。若这个母题在此有回响，新郎要聚集的新妇是他的子民，如今连撒玛利亚人也包括在内。

**3. 正午与疲乏。** 「耶稣因走路困乏，就坐在井旁。那时约有午正。」（4:6）这是1:14「道成了肉身」最具体的画面之一：那创造万物的道，走累了，口渴了。妇人在正午最热的时候来打水，许多解经家认为这暗示她刻意避开其他打水的妇女。

**4. 「没有来往」原文可能更具体。** 「原来犹太人和撒玛利亚人没有来往」（4:9），这个动词（*synchrōntai*）可能有「共用器皿」的意思。若是如此，妇人的惊讶就更具体了：一个犹太人，竟然愿意用撒玛利亚人的水罐喝水。按照当时一些犹太人的看法，这会使他在礼仪上不洁。耶稣跨过的不只是种族的界线，还有洁净礼仪的界线。

## 深读 4:10-15「他也必早给了你活水」

**1. 「活水」又是一个双关。** 「活水」（*hydōr zōn*）在日常用语里指流动的水，泉水、溪水，相对于积在池里的死水。妇人自然听成了这个意思：「井又深，你从哪里得活水呢？」（4:11）这是约翰福音另一个典型的误解：字面层面的听法，和耶稣所指的属灵层面。

**2. 旧约里的活水。** 耶利米书2:13，神说：「我的百姓做了两件恶事，就是离弃我这活水的泉源，为自己凿出池子，是破裂不能存水的池子。」神自己就是活水的泉源。耶稣说他能赐活水，等于宣告他做的是只有神能做的事。

**3. 活水是什么？** 约翰在7:37-39给了答案：耶稣在住棚节说「人若渴了，可以到我这里来喝」，约翰解释：「耶稣这话是指着信他之人要受圣灵说的。」所以活水就是圣灵自己，以及他所带来的新生命，它不是一次性的，而是「在他里头成为泉源，直涌到永生」（4:14）。

**4. 「难道你比他还大吗？」** 妇人问耶稣是否比雅各还大（4:12）。约翰的读者知道答案：是的。这是约翰喜欢用的反讽，人物说出的问题，答案正是福音。

## 深读 4:16-26 五个丈夫与「这和你说话的就是他」

**1. 耶稣没有直接定罪。** 耶稣说「你去叫你丈夫」，妇人说「我没有丈夫」，耶稣说：「你说没有丈夫是不错的。你已经有五个丈夫，你现在有的并不是你的丈夫。」（4:17-18）经文没有说明她的五段婚姻是怎么结束的，可能是丧偶，也可能是被休，当时只有男人能休妻。耶稣的话揭露了真相，却没有羞辱她。但她现在的关系并不合乎神的心意，耶稣没有回避这一点；他揭露罪，是为了把活水赐给她。她的反应是：「我看出你是先知。」

**2. 一个有争议的象征读法。** 有解经家把「五个丈夫」读成象征，指列王纪下17:24-34中被带到撒玛利亚的五族人和他们的神。但经文本身没有暗示要这样读，多数解经家仍把它理解为她真实的生命经历。

**3. 话题转到敬拜，不是逃避。** 妇人问：该在基利心山还是耶路撒冷敬拜？（4:20）这常被说成转移话题。但对一个被人看不起的撒玛利亚女人来说，「我可以在哪里、怎样亲近神」也许正是她心底的问题。

**4. 「我就是」。** 妇人说：「我知道弥赛亚要来。」耶稣回答：「这和你说话的就是他！」（4:26）希腊文是 *egō eimi, ho lalōn soi*，直译「我是，那正在对你说话的」。这是约翰福音第一次出现耶稣口中的 *egō eimi*。在这里它首先的意思是「我就是那弥赛亚」，但熟悉约翰福音的读者，会在后来（6:20；8:58；18:5-6）听到这两个字越来越深的回声。在整卷书里，耶稣最先明说自己是弥赛亚的对象，是一个撒玛利亚女人。

## 深读 4:23-24「用心灵和诚实拜他」

**1. 「时候将到，如今就是了」。** 敬拜的地点之争（基利心山或耶路撒冷）要被超越，不是因为地点不重要，而是因为神的同在现在住在耶稣身上（2:19-21）。

**2. 「救恩是从犹太人出来的」。** 耶稣并没有说两边都对。「你们所拜的，你们不知道；我们所拜的，我们知道，因为救恩是从犹太人出来的。」（4:22）神的救恩是透过以色列的历史、律法和先知临到的，弥赛亚是犹太人。但这救恩不停留在犹太人中间，它现在临到了撒玛利亚。

**3. 「心灵和诚实」是什么？** 和合本译「用心灵和诚实」，中文读者容易理解为「真心诚意」，与外在仪式相对。这不算错，但希腊文的意思更深。原文「心灵和诚实」共用一个介词（*en pneumati kai alētheia*），是一个整体概念，正像3:5的「水和圣灵」。解经家对「灵」是直接指圣灵，还是指圣灵所开启的那种敬拜，看法不一；但从3:5-8和7:39看，这种敬拜离不开圣灵的工作。「诚实」（*alētheia*）就是约翰福音常说的「真理」，而耶稣说「我就是道路、真理、生命」（14:6）。所以真正的敬拜，是在圣灵里、透过那位是真理的耶稣而献上的敬拜。真心是需要的，但真心本身还不够；敬拜需要按着神所启示的真理，借着他所赐的灵。

**4. 「神是个灵」。** 这不是说神只是一种气氛，而是说神不被物质、地点所限制，所以不能只在某一座山上被敬拜。

> 要点：耶稣没有让妇人在两座山之间选一座，而是把她带到自己面前。真正的敬拜，以圣灵和真理为中心，而真理就是基督。

## 深读 4:46-54「那人信耶稣所说的话就回去了」

**1. 「你们」是复数。** 耶稣说：「若不看见神迹奇事，你们总是不信。」（4:48）「你们」是复数，不只是对这位大臣说，也是对加利利众人说，他们欢迎耶稣是因为看过他在耶路撒冷所行的事（4:45）。这呼应2:23-25：因神迹而生的信，还不够深。

**2. 信在神迹之前。** 大臣没有争辩，只是再求一次。耶稣说「回去吧，你的儿子活了」，经文说：「那人信耶稣所说的话就回去了。」（4:50）他在看见以先就信了，信的是耶稣的话。

**3. 信心的两步。** 第二天他知道儿子好转的时辰正是耶稣说话的时辰，于是「他自己和全家就都信了」（4:53）。同一个人，信了两次：先是信耶稣的话足以依靠，后来是全然地信（4:53的「信」没有宾语，是一种完整的信）。约翰福音里的信，就是这样一步步加深的。

**4. 「救世主」。** 撒玛利亚人说：「知道这真是救世主。」（4:42）这个称号在新约中只出现在这里和约翰一书4:14。在罗马世界，这类称号常被用来尊称皇帝。撒玛利亚人认出的救主，不是凯撒，而是一个坐在井旁口渴的犹太人。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 has two main parts. **The Samaritan woman** (vv. 1–42) is the longest one-on-one conversation in John: it starts with "Give me a drink," moves through living water, five husbands and which mountain to worship on, reaches Jesus' "I am he," and ends with a whole town coming out to see him. **The healing of the official's son** (vv. 43–54) is the second sign, back at Cana in Galilee.

This chapter pairs with chapter 3. Nicodemus is a Jew, a man and a religious leader who comes at night; the Samaritan woman is a foreigner, a woman, perhaps on the edge of society, who meets Jesus at noon. Nicodemus begins with "we know" and ends up not understanding; the woman begins understanding nothing and ends up running back to town to bear witness. Jesus gives the same gospel to two completely different people.

## Going Deeper: 4:4–9 "He had to pass through Samaria"

**1. Geography or divine purpose?** Going through Samaria was the most direct route from Judea to Galilee. The Jewish historian Josephus records that Galileans often took this road to the festivals in Jerusalem. So "had to" may simply describe the route. But John uses *edei*, the same word as the "must" of chapter 3 (3:7, 14, 30), and in John the word often carries the sense of God's plan. Perhaps both are true: it was the shortest road, and on that road was a person the Father wanted Jesus to find (4:23, "the Father is seeking such people to worship him").

**2. Meeting at a well is a familiar Old Testament scene.** Abraham's servant meets Rebekah at a well (Gen 24), Jacob meets Rachel at a well (Gen 29), Moses meets Zipporah at a well (Ex 2), and each time it ends in marriage. Some scholars note that John has just called Jesus "the bridegroom" (3:29) and now sits him by "Jacob's well," where he meets a woman. But this meeting is completely unexpected: she has already had five husbands. If the motif echoes here, the bride the bridegroom gathers is his people, now including Samaritans.

**3. Noon and weariness.** "Jesus, wearied as he was from his journey, was sitting beside the well. It was about the sixth hour" (4:6). This is one of the most concrete pictures of 1:14, "the Word became flesh": the Word who made all things is tired and thirsty. The woman comes to draw water at the hottest hour, which many interpreters take as a hint that she was avoiding the other women.

**4. "No dealings" may be more specific.** "For Jews have no dealings with Samaritans" (4:9): the verb (*synchrōntai*) may mean "do not share vessels." If so, the woman's surprise is concrete: a Jew is willing to drink from a Samaritan's water jar. By the view of some Jews at the time, that would make him ritually unclean. Jesus crosses not only an ethnic line but a line of ritual purity.

## Going Deeper: 4:10–15 "He would have given you living water"

**1. "Living water" is another double meaning.** In everyday speech "living water" (*hydōr zōn*) meant flowing water, from a spring or stream, as opposed to stagnant water in a cistern. The woman naturally hears it that way: "the well is deep. Where do you get that living water?" (4:11). This is another typical Johannine misunderstanding: the literal hearing set against the spiritual meaning Jesus intends.

**2. Living water in the Old Testament.** In Jeremiah 2:13 God says, "my people have committed two evils: they have forsaken me, the fountain of living waters, and hewed out cisterns for themselves, broken cisterns that can hold no water." God himself is the fountain of living water. When Jesus says he can give living water, he is claiming to do what only God does.

**3. What is the living water?** John gives the answer in 7:37–39. At the Feast of Tabernacles Jesus says, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink," and John explains: "this he said about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were to receive." So the living water is the Spirit himself and the life he brings. It isn't a one-time drink; it "will become in him a spring of water welling up to eternal life" (4:14).

**4. "Are you greater than our father Jacob?"** The woman asks whether Jesus is greater than Jacob (4:12). John's readers know the answer: yes. It's the kind of irony John loves, where a character's question has the gospel as its answer.

## Going Deeper: 4:16–26 Five Husbands and "I who speak to you am he"

**1. Jesus doesn't simply condemn her.** Jesus says, "Go, call your husband." She says, "I have no husband." Jesus replies, "You are right in saying, 'I have no husband'; for you have had five husbands, and the one you now have is not your husband" (4:17–18). The text doesn't say how her five marriages ended. She may have been widowed or divorced, and at the time only men could divorce. Jesus' words expose the truth without humiliating her. Yet her present relationship is irregular, and Jesus doesn't skip over it. He exposes sin in order to give living water. Her response: "Sir, I perceive that you are a prophet."

**2. A disputed symbolic reading.** Some interpreters read the "five husbands" as a symbol of the five peoples settled in Samaria, with their gods, in 2 Kings 17:24–34. But the text gives no hint that it should be read this way, and most interpreters still take it as her real life story.

**3. The turn to worship isn't evasion.** She asks whether to worship on Mount Gerizim or in Jerusalem (4:20). This is often called changing the subject. But for a despised Samaritan woman, "where and how can I come near to God?" may be the real question of her heart.

**4. "I am he."** The woman says, "I know that Messiah is coming." Jesus answers, "I who speak to you am he" (4:26). The Greek is *egō eimi, ho lalōn soi*, literally "I am, the one speaking to you." This is the first *egō eimi* on Jesus' lips in John. Here it first means "I am the Messiah," but readers who know the Gospel will hear deeper echoes of these words later (6:20; 8:58; 18:5–6). In the whole book, the first person Jesus plainly tells that he is the Messiah is a Samaritan woman.

## Going Deeper: 4:23–24 "Worship in spirit and truth"

**1. "The hour is coming, and is now here."** The dispute over where to worship (Gerizim or Jerusalem) is being surpassed. Not because place doesn't matter, but because God's presence now dwells in Jesus (2:19–21).

**2. "Salvation is from the Jews."** Jesus doesn't say both sides are right: "You worship what you do not know; we worship what we know, for salvation is from the Jews" (4:22). God's salvation came through Israel's history, law and prophets, and the Messiah is a Jew. But that salvation doesn't stay among the Jews; it has now reached Samaria.

**3. What are "spirit and truth"?** The Chinese Union Version renders it "with heart-spirit and sincerity," which readers easily take as "with genuine sincerity," as opposed to outward ritual. That isn't wrong, but the Greek goes deeper. One preposition governs both words (*en pneumati kai alētheia*), making them a single idea, as with "water and Spirit" in 3:5. Interpreters differ on whether "spirit" means the Holy Spirit directly or the kind of worship the Spirit opens up, but in light of 3:5–8 and 7:39 such worship cannot be separated from the Spirit's work. "Truth" (*alētheia*) is John's word for the truth revealed in Jesus, who says, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" (14:6). So true worship is offered in the Spirit and through Jesus, who is the truth. Sincerity is needed, but sincerity alone isn't enough; worship must follow the truth God has revealed, by the Spirit he gives.

**4. "God is spirit."** This doesn't mean God is just an atmosphere. It means God isn't bound by matter or place, so he can't be worshiped only on one mountain.

> The point: Jesus doesn't make the woman choose between two mountains; he brings her to himself. True worship centers on the Spirit and the truth, and the truth is Christ.

## Going Deeper: 4:46–54 "The man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him"

**1. "You" is plural.** Jesus says, "Unless you see signs and wonders you will not believe" (4:48). "You" is plural. He's speaking not only to the official but to the Galileans, who welcomed Jesus because they had seen what he did in Jerusalem (4:45). This echoes 2:23–25: faith born of signs is not yet deep enough.

**2. Faith before the sign.** The official doesn't argue; he simply asks again. Jesus says, "Go; your son will live," and the text says, "The man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him and went on his way" (4:50). He believed before he saw, and what he believed was Jesus' word.

**3. Two steps of faith.** The next day he learns that his son recovered at the very hour Jesus spoke, "And he himself believed, and all his household" (4:53). The same man believes twice: first that Jesus' word could be trusted, then believing outright (4:53 has "believed" with no object, a settled, complete faith). This is how faith deepens in John.

**4. "The Savior of the world."** The Samaritans say, "we know that this is indeed the Savior of the world" (4:42). The title appears in the New Testament only here and in 1 John 4:14. In the Roman world, titles like this were often used to honor the emperor. The Savior the Samaritans recognized wasn't Caesar but a thirsty Jew sitting by a well.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus deliberately crossed lines of ethnicity, gender and ritual to find a woman others perhaps avoided. Is there someone near me I keep avoiding? Am I, like the woman, drinking from other cisterns (achievement, relationships, entertainment) and getting thirstier the more I drink? The official "believed the word that Jesus spoke to him and went on his way." Is there a word of the Lord I need to believe today, before I see?
