## 本章脉络

第五章是一个转折点。**医治瘫子**（1-15节）是第三个神迹，地点在耶路撒冷的毕士大池，时间是安息日。**安息日的冲突**（16-18节）：犹太人逼迫耶稣，不只因为他在安息日医病，更因为他「称神为他的父，将自己和神当作平等」。**耶稣的讲论**（19-47节）分两部分：先说子与父的关系（19-30节），再像在法庭上一样，传召为他作见证的证人（31-47节）。

从这一章开始，约翰福音里「犹太人」想要杀耶稣（5:18）成为一条持续的线索。约翰书中的「犹太人」常常是指反对耶稣的宗教领袖，而不是整个民族。耶稣自己、他的门徒、约翰本人，都是犹太人。

## 深读 5:2-9 毕士大池与三十八年

**1. 考古相符的细节。** 「旁边有五个廊子」（5:2），过去有学者认为「五个廊子」只是象征（如摩西五经）。十九世纪以来，考古学家在耶路撒冷圣安妮教堂旁发现了一个中间有隔墙的双池，四边加上中间，正好可容五个廊子，与约翰的描述相符。这是约翰福音目击细节可靠的又一个例子。

**2. 第4节在哪里？** 有些圣经版本在3-4节有一段话：天使按时下池搅动池水，先下去的病就痊愈。最早、最好的希腊文抄本没有这段话，现代译本多半把它放在脚注里，本应用所用的和合本和ESV版本都略去了第4节。3节下半「等候水动」也不见于同一批早期抄本。这段话很可能是后来的抄写者为了解释第7节「水动的时候」而加上的说明。病人自己的话反映了当时人们的信念，但约翰并没有说这个信念是真的。

**3. 「你要痊愈吗？」** 这个问题看起来多余，却直指人心。病人没有回答「要」，而是解释为什么好不了：「没有人把我放在池子里。」（5:7）他把希望放在池水上，耶稣却完全绕过池水：「起来，拿你的褥子走吧！」医治他的不是池子，而是耶稣的话。

**4. 三十八年。** 有解经家联想到申命记2:14，以色列人从加低斯巴尼亚到过撒烈溪，在旷野漂流了三十八年。这个联想有趣，但约翰没有点明，最稳妥的读法是：这个数字说明他的病有多久、多绝望。

**5. 「不要再犯罪」。** 耶稣后来在殿里对他说：「你已经痊愈了，不要再犯罪，恐怕你遭遇的更加利害。」（5:14）这不表示所有疾病都是某个罪的直接后果，耶稣在9:3明确否认了这种推论。但耶稣提醒他，比身体疾病更可怕的，很可能是指罪带来的永远后果。

## 深读 5:17-18「我父做事直到如今，我也做事」

**1. 神在安息日做不做事？** 创世记2:2说神在第七日「安息了」。但当时犹太教师也承认，神在安息日并没有停止一切：他仍然托住世界，仍然使人出生、使人死亡，仍然施行审判。与耶稣同时代而年长的犹太哲学家斐洛就说神从不停止工作；后来的拉比文献更记载了这样的讨论：神在安息日仍然做两件事，就是赐生命和审判。

**2. 耶稣宣告了同样的特权。** 耶稣说：「我父做事直到如今，我也做事。」意思是：父在安息日不停止的工作，我也不停止。听的人立刻明白：「将自己和神当作平等。」（5:18）

**3. 紧接着的正是那两件事。** 耶稣接下来说的，正好就是犹太教师说神在安息日仍做的两件事：「父怎样叫死人起来，使他们活着，子也照样随自己的意思使人活着」（5:21，赐生命）；「父不审判什么人，乃将审判的事全交与子」（5:22，审判）。耶稣不是偶然打破规矩，他是在宣告自己做的正是神的工作。

## 深读 5:19-30 子与父：平等，又出于父

**1. 「子凭着自己不能做什么」。** 这句话（5:19）乍听像是在说子比父低。但下一句马上说：「父所做的事，子也照样做。」子不能做父没有做的事，但父所做的一切，子都做。这不是能力不足，而是完全的合一：子从不独立于父而行动。

**2. 尊荣的平等。** 「叫人都尊敬子如同尊敬父一样。不尊敬子的，就是不尊敬差子来的父。」（5:23）在一个绝对的一神信仰里，没有人能要求被尊敬「如同」神一样，除非他自己就是神。

**3. 「在自己有生命」。** 「父怎样在自己有生命，就赐给他儿子也照样在自己有生命。」（5:26）「在自己有生命」，就是生命不依赖任何他者，这是只有神才有的属性。子「也照样」在自己有生命。许多教父（如奥古斯丁）从这节经文看出子从父永远受生的关系；加尔文等人则认为它是指道成肉身的中保从父领受赐生命的权柄。两种读法都肯定子在自己有生命、与父同等。

**4. 现在与将来。** 请比较两节：「时候将到，**现在就是了**，死人要听见神儿子的声音，听见的人就要活了」（5:25）；「时候要到，凡在坟墓里的，都要听见他的声音，就出来」（5:28）。第一节有「现在就是了」，第二节没有。前者说的是现在：属灵上死了的人，听见子的声音就出死入生（5:24）。后者说的是将来：身体的复活和最后的审判。约翰福音同时有这两层：永生现在就开始，复活在末日完成。

> 要点：耶稣在这里不是说他像神，而是说他做神所做的事、受神所受的尊荣、拥有神所拥有的生命；同时他在差遣中完全顺从父，而这顺从出于与父同一的旨意，不表示他在本质上低于父。

## 深读 5:31-47 证人一个一个上台

**1. 法庭的场景。** 申命记19:15规定，凡事要凭两三个人的口作见证才能定案。耶稣说：「我若为自己作见证，我的见证就不真。」（5:31）意思是：按你们的标准，单凭我自己的话不够，那么我来传召证人。

**2. 四个（或五个）证人。** 接下来耶稣一个一个列出：施洗约翰（5:33）；父交给他的工作，就是他所行的神迹（5:36）；父自己（5:37）；圣经（5:39）；摩西（5:46，也可以看作圣经见证的代表）。「见证」是约翰福音的关键词之一，整卷书就像一场审判，读者是陪审员。

**3. 「你们查考圣经」是命令还是陈述？** 希腊文 *ereunate* 的形式，可以是命令语气（「你们要查考」），也可以是直述语气（「你们正在查考」）。英文钦定本（KJV）译作命令「你们要查考圣经」；和合本的「你们查考圣经，因你们以为内中有永生」，与ESV、NIV一样，读作直述较自然。从上下文看，直述也较合理：耶稣不是叫他们去读圣经，他们已经读得很勤了，问题是他们读了，却没有看见圣经所指向的那一位。「给我作见证的就是这经。然而，你们不肯到我这里来得生命。」（5:39-40）

**4. 摩西指着谁写？** 「他书上有指着我写的话。」（5:46）最直接的经文是申命记18:15：「耶和华你的神要从你们弟兄中间给你兴起一位先知像我。」但耶稣的意思可能更广：整部摩西五经，从女人的后裔（创3:15）到逾越节的羔羊，都在指向他。

**5. 信的障碍。** 「你们互相受荣耀，却不求从独一之神来的荣耀，怎能信我呢？」（5:44）耶稣指出，不信不只是头脑的问题，也是心的问题：太在乎人的称赞，就很难接受一位被人弃绝的弥赛亚。

## 默想

**默想：** 那病人三十八年把希望放在池水上，耶稣却直接对他说「起来」。我是否也有一个「池子」，一个我以为会让我好起来、其实从来没有让我好起来的东西？我读圣经的时候，是像那些人一样查考了很多，却没有「到他这里来得生命」吗？今天我读经的目标，是知道更多，还是更认识他？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 is a turning point. **The healing of the paralyzed man** (vv. 1–15) is the third sign, at the Pool of Bethesda in Jerusalem, on a Sabbath. **The Sabbath conflict** (vv. 16–18): the Jews persecute Jesus not only for healing on the Sabbath but because he was "calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God." **Jesus' discourse** (vv. 19–47) has two parts: first the relationship of the Son to the Father (vv. 19–30), then, as in a courtroom, a call to the witnesses who testify about him (vv. 31–47).

From this chapter on, "the Jews" seeking to kill Jesus (5:18) becomes a continuing thread. In John, "the Jews" often means the religious leaders who opposed Jesus, not the whole people. Jesus himself, his disciples and John were all Jews.

## Going Deeper: 5:2–9 The Pool of Bethesda and Thirty-Eight Years

**1. A detail that fits the archaeology.** "Which has five roofed colonnades" (5:2): some critics once read the five porticoes as merely symbolic (the five books of Moses). Since the nineteenth century, excavations beside the Church of St. Anne in Jerusalem have revealed a double pool with a central dividing wall; four sides plus the middle would give exactly five colonnades, which fits John's description. It's another example of the reliability of John's eyewitness detail.

**2. Where is verse 4?** Some Bibles have a passage at verses 3–4 saying an angel stirred the water from time to time, and the first one in was healed. The earliest and best Greek manuscripts don't have it, so most modern translations put it in a footnote; both the ESV and the Chinese Union Version used in this app omit verse 4. The end of verse 3 ("waiting for the moving of the water") is missing from the same early manuscripts. It was most likely added by a later copyist to explain the sick man's reference to the water being "stirred up" in verse 7. The man's words reflect what people believed, but John doesn't say the belief was true.

**3. "Do you want to be healed?"** The question seems unnecessary, but it goes to the heart. The man doesn't answer "yes"; he explains why he can't get well: "I have no one to put me into the pool" (5:7). His hope is in the water. Jesus bypasses the water entirely: "Get up, take up your bed, and walk." It wasn't the pool that healed him but Jesus' word.

**4. Thirty-eight years.** Some interpreters recall Deuteronomy 2:14, where Israel wandered thirty-eight years from Kadesh-barnea to the brook Zered. The connection is intriguing, but John doesn't make it. The safest reading is that the number shows how long and how hopeless his illness was.

**5. "Sin no more."** Later Jesus finds him in the temple and says, "See, you are well! Sin no more, that nothing worse may happen to you" (5:14). This doesn't mean every illness is the direct result of a particular sin; Jesus explicitly rejects that inference in 9:3. But he warns him that worse than any bodily illness are, most likely, the eternal consequences of sin.

## Going Deeper: 5:17–18 "My Father is working until now, and I am working"

**1. Does God work on the Sabbath?** Genesis 2:2 says God "rested" on the seventh day. But Jewish teachers of the time also recognized that God didn't stop everything on the Sabbath: he still upheld the world, still caused births and deaths, still judged. Philo, a Jewish philosopher and an older contemporary of Jesus, already said that God never ceases working; later rabbinic writings record the discussion that on the Sabbath God still does two things, giving life and judging.

**2. Jesus claims the same prerogative.** "My Father is working until now, and I am working." In other words, the work the Father doesn't stop on the Sabbath, I don't stop either. His hearers understood at once: "making himself equal with God" (5:18).

**3. What follows is exactly those two works.** What Jesus says next is precisely the two things Jewish teachers said God still does on the Sabbath: "as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will" (5:21, giving life); "The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son" (5:22, judging). Jesus isn't breaking a rule by accident. He is announcing that what he does is God's own work.

## Going Deeper: 5:19–30 The Son and the Father: Equal, and from the Father

**1. "The Son can do nothing of his own accord."** At first this verse (5:19) sounds as if the Son is lower than the Father. But the next sentence says, "For whatever the Father does, that the Son does likewise." The Son can't do what the Father isn't doing, but everything the Father does, the Son does. This isn't a lack of power but complete unity: the Son never acts independently of the Father.

**2. Equal honor.** "That all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him" (5:23). Under strict monotheism, no one can demand to be honored "just as" God is honored, unless he himself is God.

**3. "Life in himself."** "For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself" (5:26). To have life in oneself is to depend on nothing outside oneself for life, which belongs to God alone. The Son "also" has life in himself. Many church fathers (Augustine, for example) saw here the Son's eternal generation from the Father; Calvin and others took it of the incarnate Mediator receiving from the Father the authority to give life. Both readings affirm that the Son has life in himself, equal with the Father.

**4. Present and future.** Compare two verses: "an hour is coming, *and is now here*, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live" (5:25); "an hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out" (5:28). The first has "and is now here"; the second doesn't. The first speaks of the present: the spiritually dead hear the Son's voice and pass from death to life (5:24). The second speaks of the future: bodily resurrection and final judgment. John holds both: eternal life begins now, and resurrection is completed on the last day.

> The point: Jesus isn't saying he is *like* God. He says he does what God does, receives the honor God receives and has the life God has, while in his mission he fully obeys the Father, an obedience that flows from one shared will, not from any lesser divinity.

## Going Deeper: 5:31–47 The Witnesses Take the Stand

**1. A courtroom scene.** Deuteronomy 19:15 requires two or three witnesses to establish a matter. Jesus says, "If I alone bear witness about myself, my testimony is not deemed true" (5:31). In other words: by your standard my own word isn't enough, so I will call witnesses.

**2. Four (or five) witnesses.** Jesus lists them one by one: John the Baptist (5:33); the works the Father gave him, his signs (5:36); the Father himself (5:37); the Scriptures (5:39); and Moses (5:46, who can also be counted as the chief example of Scripture's witness). "Witness" is one of John's key words. The whole book is like a trial, with the reader as juror.

**3. Is "search the Scriptures" a command or a statement?** The Greek form *ereunate* can be imperative ("search!") or indicative ("you search"). The KJV took it as a command ("Search the scriptures"); the ESV and NIV, and most naturally the Chinese Union Version, read it as a statement: "You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life." The context favors the statement. Jesus isn't telling them to read the Bible; they already read it diligently. The problem is that they read without seeing the one the Scriptures point to: "it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life" (5:39–40).

**4. Of whom did Moses write?** "He wrote of me" (5:46). The most direct text is Deuteronomy 18:15: "The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among you." But Jesus may mean more broadly that all of Moses' writings, from the offspring of the woman (Gen 3:15) to the Passover lamb, point to him.

**5. What blocks belief.** "How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the only God?" (5:44). Jesus shows that unbelief isn't only a problem of the head but of the heart. If you care too much about people's praise, it's hard to accept a rejected Messiah.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** For thirty-eight years the sick man put his hope in the pool, and Jesus simply said, "Get up." Do I have a "pool" of my own, something I think will make me well but never has? When I read the Bible, am I like those who searched diligently but would not "come to him that they may have life"? Is my aim in reading today to know more, or to know him better?
