## 本章脉络

第六章是约翰福音最长的一章之一，场景都在加利利。**五饼二鱼**（1-15节）是第四个神迹，众人吃饱以后要强逼耶稣作王。**海面上行走**（16-21节）是第五个神迹，只有门徒看见。**生命之粮的讲论**（22-59节）在迦百农的会堂里，从「你们找我，是因吃饼得饱」开始，到「吃我肉、喝我血」结束。**门徒的分歧**（60-71节）：许多门徒退去，彼得却说：「主啊，你有永生之道，我们还归从谁呢？」

约翰特别注明：「那时犹太人的逾越节近了。」（6:4）逾越节纪念出埃及：神用吗哪喂养旷野中的百姓，摩西带领他们过海。这一章里，耶稣喂饱众人，在海上行走，然后说自己是「从天上降下来的粮」。整章处处回响着出埃及。

## 深读 6:5-15 五个大麦饼与「那要到世间来的先知」

**1. 大麦饼与以利沙。** 只有约翰注明那是「大麦饼」（6:9），这是穷人的食物。列王纪下4:42-44有一个相似的故事：有人给先知以利沙送来二十个大麦饼，以利沙说「把这些给众人吃」，仆人说这怎么够一百人吃，结果众人吃了，还有剩余。耶稣所行的，比以利沙更大：五个饼，五千人。

**2. 收拾零碎。** 「把剩下的零碎收拾起来，免得有糟蹋的。」（6:12）装满了十二个篮子。神的丰盛不是浪费，十二这个数字或许也让人联想到以色列的十二支派。

**3. 「那先知」。** 众人说：「这真是那要到世间来的先知！」（6:14）他们想到的是申命记18:15，神要兴起一位像摩西那样的先知（「一位先知像我」）。摩西给了吗哪，这一位也给了饼，所以他一定就是那位先知。他们的结论没错，但他们想要的是什么？

**4. 耶稣拒绝了王冠。** 「耶稣既知道众人要来强逼他作王，就独自又退到山上去了。」（6:15）他们要一位能让他们吃饱、能推翻罗马的王。耶稣是王，但不是他们想要的那种王。他会在逾越节被称为「犹太人的王」，那时他头上戴的是荆棘的冠冕（19:2-3）。

## 深读 6:16-21「是我，不要怕」

**1. 只有门徒看见的神迹。** 晚上，门徒在海上摇橹，约走了「十里多路」（希腊文是二十五到三十斯他迪，约五六公里），看见耶稣在海面上行走。这个神迹不是给众人看的，而是给刚刚看过五饼二鱼、却还不明白的门徒看的。

**2. 「是我」原文是「我是」。** 耶稣说：「是我，不要怕！」希腊文是 *egō eimi*。在这里它最自然的意思是「是我」，让门徒认出他。但在旧约里，只有神能在海上行走：「他独自铺张苍天，步行在海浪之上」（伯9:8）；「你的道在海中，你的路在大水中」（诗77:19）。一个在海浪上行走、说「我是」的人，读者不能不想到出埃及记3:14。

**3. 「船立时到了」。** 门徒接他上船，「船立时到了他们所要去的地方」（6:21）。诗篇107篇描写神平静风浪：「他就引他们到所愿去的海口。」（诗107:30）约翰没有多加解释，但对熟悉诗篇的读者，这句话说的是同一位神。

## 深读 6:27-35「我就是生命的粮」

**1. 他们找错了东西。** 众人第二天找到耶稣，耶稣说：「你们找我，并不是因见了神迹，乃是因吃饼得饱。」（6:26）他们看见了饼，却没有看见饼所指向的。「不要为那必坏的食物劳力，要为那存到永生的食物劳力。」（6:27）

**2. 神的「工」只有一件。** 他们问：「我们当行什么才算做神的工呢？」（6:28）「工」是复数，他们想要一张清单。耶稣用单数回答：「信神所差来的，这就是做神的工。」（6:29）神要的「工作」，就是信。这不是说信是人赚取救恩的功劳，而是说：人能为神「做」的唯一一件事，就是接受神所做的。

**3. 吗哪与真粮。** 众人提出吗哪：「我们的祖宗在旷野吃过吗哪。」（6:31）耶稣纠正了三件事：赐吗哪的不是摩西，是神；是「赐给」不是「赐过」（现在时）；真正的「从天上来的粮」，不是吗哪，而是一个人。吃吗哪的人还是死了（6:49），吃这粮的人却永远活着。

**4. 第一个「我是」的比喻。** 「我就是生命的粮。到我这里来的，必定不饿；信我的，永远不渴。」（6:35）这是约翰福音七个带比喻的「我是」中的第一个。注意平行句：「到我这里来」和「信我」是同一件事。信就是来到他面前。

## 深读 6:37-44「若不是差我来的父吸引人」

**1. 父的赐给与子的保守。** 「凡父所赐给我的人必到我这里来；到我这里来的，我总不丢弃他。」（6:37）「我总不丢弃」，原文用了很强的双重否定。紧接着：「他所赐给我的，叫我一个也不失落，在末日却叫他复活。」（6:39）一个人的得救，开始于父的赐给，保守在子的手里，完成在末日的复活。

**2. 「吸引」是一个很强的字。** 「若不是差我来的父吸引人，就没有能到我这里来的。」（6:44）「吸引」的希腊文 *helkō*，在别处用来说拖网（21:6、11）、拔刀（18:10）、把人拉到官府面前（徒16:19）。这个动词很强。但关键在上下文：父所赐的人「必到」（6:37），被吸引的人「在末日我要叫他复活」（6:44下；参6:65）。改革宗据此认为这吸引是有效的；阿米念派则引12:32，认为这是可以被拒绝的、使人能够回应的恩典。双方都同意人不能凭自己来到基督面前。

**3. 同时，邀请是真实的。** 同一段经文也说：「我父的意思是叫一切见子而信的人得永生。」（6:40）耶稣后来又说：「我若从地上被举起来，就要吸引万人来归我。」（12:32，同一个「吸引」；这里的「万人」，许多解经家理解为各样的人，包括来求见的希腊人，12:20-21）神的主权和人真实的回应，在约翰福音里同时存在，耶稣没有让我们在两者之间二选一。

> 要点：来到基督面前的人，可以确知：自己能来，是因为父先拉了他；来了以后，子「总不丢弃」。

## 深读 6:51-63「吃我肉、喝我血」

**1. 越说越难。** 耶稣说：「我所要赐的粮就是我的肉，为世人之生命所赐的。」（6:51）这个「为」（*hyper*）是替代的舍命：他为世人舍身。听的人争论：「这个人怎能把他的肉给我们吃呢？」耶稣没有缓和，反而更进一步：「你们若不吃人子的肉，不喝人子的血，就没有生命在你们里面。」（6:53）摩西的律法禁止喝血（利17:10-14），这话对犹太人来说是极大的冒犯。

**2. 动词的变化。** 从54节起，约翰把「吃」换成了另一个字 *trōgō*。有解经家认为这个字（原意「咀嚼」）加强了具体感；也有人指出，在当时它常常只是「吃」的现在时用法（参13:18）。无论如何，耶稣没有把话说软。

**3. 「信」与「吃」的平行。** 请比较40节与54节：「一切见子**而信**的人得永生，并且在末日我要叫他复活」；「**吃我肉、喝我血**的人就有永生，在末日我要叫他复活」。两句的结果完全一样。所以「吃他的肉、喝他的血」，就是以最深的方式信靠他：把他为我们舍弃的身体和流出的血，当作我们生命的唯一来源。

**4. 与圣餐的关系。** 许多读者会想到圣餐。约翰福音没有记载设立圣餐的那一幕，但这段话显然与圣餐有关联。不过，耶稣在这里说话的时候，圣餐还没有设立，听的人不可能理解成圣礼。福音派解经家一般认为：这段讲论说的是靠着他的死而得生命，而圣餐是这个真理的记号和印证，不是另一条得生命的路。

**5. 解读的钥匙。** 耶稣自己给了解读的方法：「叫人活着的乃是灵，肉体是无益的。我对你们所说的话就是灵，就是生命。」（6:63）这话不是要人去咬一口肉体，而是要人借着圣灵、借着他的话，与他联合。

## 深读 6:66-69「主啊，你有永生之道，我们还归从谁呢？」

**1. 大批人离开。** 「从此，他门徒中多有退去的，不再和他同行。」（6:66）那些在五饼二鱼之后要立他作王的人，听了他的话就走了。他们要的是饼，不是赐饼的人。

**2. 耶稣没有追上去。** 他转身问十二个门徒：「你们也要去吗？」（6:67）这个问题的语气（希腊文用 *mē* 开头）预期的是否定的回答：「你们不会也要走吧？」

**3. 彼得的回答。** 「主啊，你有永生之道，我们还归从谁呢？我们已经信了，又知道你是神的圣者。」（6:68-69）彼得并没有说他完全明白了吃肉喝血的意思。他的理由很简单：除了你，没有别的地方可以去。「已经信了，又知道」，两个动词都是完成时，表示一个已经成立、并持续下去的状态。

**4. 就在这时，最黑暗的一句。** 耶稣回答：「我不是拣选了你们十二个门徒吗？但你们中间有一个是魔鬼。」（6:70）约翰在这一章结尾就提到犹大。在最坚定的信心告白旁边，站着一个最终的背叛者。

## 默想

**默想：** 那些人找耶稣，是「因吃饼得饱」。我寻求主，是为了他能给我的「饼」（平安、成功、问题解决），还是为了他自己？当主的话难以接受的时候，我会像那些人一样「退去」，还是像彼得一样说「我们还归从谁呢」？今天有没有一处，是我需要单单信靠他、而不是先要明白一切的？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 is one of John's longest chapters, set entirely in Galilee. **The feeding of the five thousand** (vv. 1–15) is the fourth sign, after which the crowd wants to make Jesus king by force. **Walking on the sea** (vv. 16–21) is the fifth sign, seen only by the disciples. **The bread of life discourse** (vv. 22–59), in the synagogue at Capernaum, runs from "you are seeking me... because you ate your fill of the loaves" to "feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood." **The disciples divided** (vv. 60–71): many turn back, but Peter says, "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life."

John notes, "Now the Passover, the feast of the Jews, was at hand" (6:4). Passover remembered the exodus: God fed his people manna in the wilderness, and Moses led them through the sea. In this chapter Jesus feeds a crowd, walks on the sea and then calls himself "the bread that came down from heaven." The whole chapter is full of exodus echoes.

## Going Deeper: 6:5–15 Five Barley Loaves and "the Prophet who is to come"

**1. Barley loaves and Elisha.** Only John notes that the loaves were "barley" (6:9), the food of the poor. Second Kings 4:42–44 tells a similar story: someone brings the prophet Elisha twenty barley loaves, Elisha says to give them to the people, his servant asks how that could feed a hundred men, and they eat and have some left over. What Jesus does is greater than Elisha: five loaves, five thousand people.

**2. Gathering the fragments.** "Gather up the leftover fragments, that nothing may be lost" (6:12). They fill twelve baskets. God's abundance isn't wasteful, and the number twelve may also recall the twelve tribes of Israel.

**3. "The Prophet."** The people say, "This is indeed the Prophet who is to come into the world!" (6:14). They are thinking of Deuteronomy 18:15, where God promises to raise up "a prophet like" Moses. Moses gave manna, this man gave bread, so he must be that prophet. Their conclusion was right, but what did they want from him?

**4. Jesus refuses the crown.** "Perceiving then that they were about to come and take him by force to make him king, Jesus withdrew again to the mountain by himself" (6:15). They wanted a king who would fill their stomachs and overthrow Rome. Jesus is a king, but not that kind. At Passover he will be called "King of the Jews," and the crown on his head will be made of thorns (19:2–3).

## Going Deeper: 6:16–21 "It is I; do not be afraid"

**1. A sign only the disciples saw.** At night the disciples are rowing on the sea. After about "three or four miles" (twenty-five or thirty stadia), they see Jesus walking on the water. This sign isn't for the crowd. It's for the disciples, who had just seen the loaves and fish and still didn't understand.

**2. "It is I" is literally "I am."** Jesus says, "It is I; do not be afraid." The Greek is *egō eimi*. Here its most natural sense is "It's me," so the disciples recognize him. But in the Old Testament only God walks on the sea: he "trampled the waves of the sea" (Job 9:8); "Your way was through the sea, your path through the great waters" (Ps 77:19). A man walking on the waves and saying "I am" makes the reader think of Exodus 3:14.

**3. "Immediately the boat was at the land."** The disciples take him into the boat, "and immediately the boat was at the land to which they were going" (6:21). Psalm 107 describes God calming the storm: "he brought them to their desired haven" (Ps 107:30). John adds no explanation, but for readers who know the Psalms, this is the same God.

## Going Deeper: 6:27–35 "I am the bread of life"

**1. They were looking for the wrong thing.** The next day the crowd finds Jesus, and he says, "you are seeking me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate your fill of the loaves" (6:26). They saw the bread but not what it pointed to. "Do not labor for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures to eternal life" (6:27).

**2. God's "work" is one thing.** They ask, "What must we do, to be doing the works of God?" (6:28). "Works" is plural; they want a list. Jesus answers in the singular: "This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent" (6:29). The "work" God wants is believing. This doesn't make faith a merit that earns salvation. It means the one thing we can "do" for God is to receive what God has done.

**3. Manna and the true bread.** The crowd brings up the manna: "Our fathers ate the manna in the wilderness" (6:31). Jesus corrects three things. It wasn't Moses who gave the manna, but God. It isn't "gave" but "gives" (present tense). And the true "bread from heaven" isn't manna but a person. Those who ate manna still died (6:49); whoever eats this bread will live forever.

**4. The first "I am" with an image.** "I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst" (6:35). This is the first of John's seven "I am" sayings with an image. Notice the parallel: "comes to me" and "believes in me" are the same thing. To believe is to come to him.

## Going Deeper: 6:37–44 "Unless the Father who sent me draws him"

**1. The Father's gift and the Son's keeping.** "All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out" (6:37). "Never" is an emphatic double negative in Greek. Then: "that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but raise it up on the last day" (6:39). A person's salvation begins with the Father's giving, is kept in the Son's hands and is completed in the resurrection on the last day.

**2. "Draws" is a strong word.** "No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him" (6:44). The Greek *helkō* is used elsewhere for hauling a net (21:6, 11), drawing a sword (18:10) and dragging people before the authorities (Acts 16:19). The verb is strong. But the decisive evidence is the context: everyone the Father gives comes (6:37), and the one who is drawn is raised up on the last day (6:44b; cf. 6:65). Reformed interpreters therefore read the drawing as effectual; Arminian interpreters, pointing to 12:32, read it as an enabling grace that can be resisted. Both agree that no one can come to Christ unaided.

**3. And the invitation is real.** The same passage says, "this is the will of my Father, that everyone who looks on the Son and believes in him should have eternal life" (6:40). Later Jesus says, "And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself" (12:32, the same "draw"; "all people" is widely understood as all kinds of people, including the Greeks who had come to see him, 12:20–21). God's sovereignty and genuine human response stand side by side in John, and Jesus doesn't make us choose one over the other.

> The point: Whoever comes to Christ can be sure that they came because the Father drew them first, and that once they have come, the Son will "never cast them out."

## Going Deeper: 6:51–63 "Feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood"

**1. The words get harder.** Jesus says, "the bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh" (6:51). That "for" (*hyper*) is the language of substitution: he gives his flesh in place of the world. His hearers argue, "How can this man give us his flesh to eat?" Jesus doesn't soften it; he goes further: "unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you" (6:53). The law of Moses forbade drinking blood (Lev 17:10–14), so these words were deeply offensive to Jewish ears.

**2. A change of verb.** From verse 54 John switches "eat" to another word, *trōgō*. Some commentators see added realism in it, since it originally meant "chew." Others note that by John's day it often simply supplied the present tense of "eat" (cf. 13:18). Either way, Jesus doesn't soften his words.

**3. "Believe" and "eat" are parallel.** Compare verses 40 and 54: "everyone who looks on the Son *and believes in him* should have eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day"; "Whoever *feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood* has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day." The results are identical. So to feed on his flesh and drink his blood is to trust him in the deepest way: to take the body he gave and the blood he shed for us as our only source of life.

**4. The connection with the Lord's Supper.** Many readers think of the Lord's Supper. John doesn't record its institution, but this passage clearly connects with it. Still, when Jesus spoke these words the Supper had not yet been instituted, and his hearers couldn't have understood them as a sacrament. Evangelical interpreters generally hold that the discourse is about receiving life through his death, and that the Supper is a sign and seal of this truth, not a separate way to life.

**5. The key to interpretation.** Jesus himself gives the key: "It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh is of no avail. The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life" (6:63). He isn't asking people to bite into flesh. He's calling them to union with himself, through the Spirit and through his word.

## Going Deeper: 6:66–69 "Lord, to whom shall we go?"

**1. Many leave.** "After this many of his disciples turned back and no longer walked with him" (6:66). The people who wanted to crown him after the loaves walk away once they hear his words. They wanted the bread, not the one who gives it.

**2. Jesus doesn't chase them.** He turns to the Twelve: "Do you want to go away as well?" (6:67). The question's form (opening with Greek *mē*) expects a "no": "You're not going to leave too, are you?"

**3. Peter's answer.** "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life, and we have believed, and have come to know, that you are the Holy One of God" (6:68–69). Peter doesn't claim to understand eating flesh and drinking blood. His reason is simple: there's nowhere else to go. "Have believed, and have come to know" are both perfect tense, describing a settled state that continues.

**4. Then the darkest line.** Jesus answers, "Did I not choose you, the Twelve? And yet one of you is a devil" (6:70). John ends this chapter by naming Judas. Beside the firmest confession of faith stands the man who will finally betray him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The crowd sought Jesus "because you ate your fill of the loaves." Do I seek the Lord for the "bread" he can give me (peace, success, problems solved), or for himself? When his words are hard to accept, do I "turn back" like the crowd, or say with Peter, "to whom shall we go?" Is there one place today where I need simply to trust him, without first understanding everything?
