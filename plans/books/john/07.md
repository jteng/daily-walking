## 本章脉络

第七章与第八章除了开头在加利利的一段（7:1-9），都发生在住棚节期间的耶路撒冷，气氛越来越紧张。**弟兄的不信**（1-9节）：耶稣的弟兄催他上耶路撒冷去显扬自己。**暗中上去**（10-13节）：众人私下议论他。**节期当中的教导**（14-24节）：「这个人没有学过，怎么明白书呢？」**他是基督吗？**（25-36节）：众人从他从哪里来、要往哪里去，争论他的身份。**节期的末日**（37-39节）：耶稣站着高声呼喊。**众人分裂**（40-52节）：有人说他是那先知，有人说是基督，差役空手回去，尼哥德慕替他说话。

住棚节（又称收藏节）是犹太人最欢乐的节期，在秋收之后，人们住在棚子里七天，纪念祖先在旷野住帐棚、神一路供应他们。据后来的犹太文献《密释纳》记载，节期中有两个著名的仪式：每天早晨，祭司从西罗亚池打水，用金壶带到圣殿浇在祭坛上，为来年的雨水祈求；晚上，圣殿的妇女院点起巨大的灯台，照亮全城。第七章的「活水」和第八章的「世界的光」，就是在这两个仪式的背景下说的。

## 深读 7:3-9「连他的弟兄也不信他」

**1. 弟兄的建议。** 「你离开这里上犹太去吧，叫你的门徒也看见你所行的事……你如果行这些事，就当将自己显明给世人看。」（7:3-4）听起来像是在帮他宣传，约翰却直接说明：「因为连他的弟兄说这话，是因为不信他。」（7:5）他们看见了神迹，却把耶稣当作一个需要打响名号的人。

**2. 「我的时候还没有到」。** 耶稣说：「我的时候还没有到；你们的时候常是方便的。」（7:6）这里的「时候」是 *kairos*（合适的时机），与2:4的「时候」（*hōra*）不同字，但意思相关：耶稣的一切行动，都按父的时间表，不按群众的期待。

**3. 后来的故事。** 不信的弟兄后来怎样了？复活的主向雅各显现（林前15:7），升天之后，耶稣的弟兄们和门徒一同恒切祷告（徒1:14），雅各成为耶路撒冷教会的领袖，并写了雅各书。这一章的「不信」，不是故事的结局。

## 深读 7:14-24「人若立志遵着他的旨意行」

**1. 「没有学过」。** 犹太人希奇：「这个人没有学过，怎么明白书呢？」（7:15）意思是他没有在任何拉比门下受过正式训练。耶稣回答：「我的教训不是我自己的，乃是那差我来者的。」（7:16）他的权威不是来自某个学派，而是来自父。

**2. 认识真理的条件。** 「人若立志遵着他的旨意行，就必晓得这教训或是出于神，或是我凭着自己说的。」（7:17）「立志」原文是「愿意」（*thelē*）。耶稣说，认识真理的障碍不只在头脑，也在意志。一个人若愿意顺服神，就能分辨耶稣的话是否出于神。顺服不是认识之后的事，而是认识的一部分。

**3. 一个从小推到大的论证。** 耶稣指出他们自己也在安息日做工：如果孩子出生第八天正好是安息日，他们照样行割礼（7:22-23）。割礼只关乎身体的一部分，而耶稣在安息日叫「一个人全然好了」。这是犹太教师常用的「由轻推重」论证：如果为了遵守律法可以在安息日处理身体的一部分，为什么不可以在安息日医治整个人？「不可按外貌断定是非，总要按公平断定是非。」（7:24）

## 深读 7:37-39「人若渴了，可以到我这里来喝」

**1. 时间与地点。** 「节期的末日，就是最大之日」（7:37），可能是第七天，也可能是第八天的严肃会。若是第七天，那天的打水仪式最隆重，祭司会绕祭坛七次。在这样的时刻，众人刚刚看完金壶里的水浇在祭坛上，耶稣站起来高声说：「人若渴了，可以到我这里来喝。」住棚节所祈求的水，现在站在他们面前。

**2. 「腹中」与「心中」。** 「从他腹中要流出活水的江河来」（7:38），和合本按原文 *koilia* 译作「腹中」，英文ESV意译为「心中」。在希伯来人的语言里，腹是人最深处的所在。

**3. 「他」是谁？一个标点的问题。** 希腊文没有标点，37-38节可以有两种断句。传统读法（和合本、ESV）：「信我的人……从**他**（信徒）腹中要流出活水的江河。」另一种读法：「人若渴了，可以到我这里来；信我的人可以来喝。如经上所说：从**他**（基督）腹中要流出活水的江河。」两种读法都有早期教父支持（前者多见于俄利根等希腊教父，后者多见于希坡律陀、居普良等西方教父）。第二种读法也与以西结书47章（水从圣殿流出）和撒迦利亚书14:8（活水从耶路撒冷出来，而撒迦利亚14章正是说住棚节）相合；约翰在2:19-21已经告诉我们，耶稣以自己的身体为殿；而在十字架上，从他的肋旁流出血和水来（19:34）。两种读法都符合整卷书的教导：活水的源头是基督，而信他的人领受了圣灵，也成为活水流出去的管道。

**4. 「那时还没有赐下圣灵来」。** 约翰解释：「耶稣这话是指着信他之人要受圣灵说的。那时还没有赐下圣灵来，因为耶稣尚未得着荣耀。」（7:39）原文直译是「那时还没有圣灵」，和合本和ESV都补上了「赐下」。这不是说圣灵那时不存在，旧约里圣灵早就在工作。意思是：圣灵以新约的方式、住在所有信徒里面的那种浇灌，要等到耶稣被「举起来」得荣耀之后才发生（20:22；徒2章）。在约翰福音里，「得荣耀」就是指十字架、复活和升天。

> 要点：住棚节每年为雨水祷告，祈求神供应。耶稣宣告：你们所等候的供应，就是我；我所赐的，是圣灵。

## 深读 7:40-52 众人的争论与几处反讽

**1. 「基督岂是从加利利出来的吗？」** 有人反对：「经上岂不是说『基督是大卫的后裔，从大卫本乡伯利恒出来的』吗？」（7:42）约翰没有更正他们。他的读者多半已经从马太福音和路加福音知道，耶稣正是生在伯利恒。说话的人自以为驳倒了他，其实说出了真相。

**2. 「从来没有像他这样说话的！」** 祭司长派去捉拿耶稣的差役空手回去，只说：「从来没有像他这样说话的！」（7:46）连奉命抓他的人都被他的话折服了。

**3. 尼哥德慕的半句话。** 尼哥德慕说：「不先听本人的口供，不知道他所做的事，难道我们的律法还定他的罪吗？」（7:51）他没有公开承认信耶稣，但他站出来要求程序公正。那个夜里来的人，在光中走了一小步。

**4. 「加利利没有出过先知」。** 法利赛人反驳他：「你且去查考，就可知道加利利没有出过先知。」（7:52）这句话其实不对：先知约拿就是加利利迦特希弗人（王下14:25）。也有一份很早的抄本（P66）在这里写作「**那**先知不从加利利出来」，指的是申命记18章那位像摩西的先知。不论哪一种，说这话的人自称最懂圣经，却在一句话里暴露了自己的盲点。

## 默想

**默想：** 耶稣的弟兄天天和他在一起，却不信他，后来雅各却成了教会的柱石（加2:9），弟兄们也加入了祷告的教会（徒1:14）。我是否为某位家人的不信灰心？耶稣说「人若立志遵着他的旨意行，就必晓得」，我是否在等完全明白了才肯顺服？今天我在什么事上「渴」了？我是到他那里去喝，还是到别处去找？

## The Shape of the Chapter

After a brief opening scene in Galilee (7:1–9), chapters 7 and 8 take place in Jerusalem during the Feast of Tabernacles, and the tension keeps rising. **His brothers' unbelief** (vv. 1–9): Jesus' brothers urge him to go to Jerusalem and make a name for himself. **Going up in secret** (vv. 10–13): the crowds whisper about him. **Teaching at the middle of the feast** (vv. 14–24): "How is it that this man has learning, when he has never studied?" **Is he the Christ?** (vv. 25–36): the crowds argue over his identity from where he comes from and where he is going. **The last day of the feast** (vv. 37–39): Jesus stands and cries out. **The crowd divided** (vv. 40–52): some say he is the Prophet, others the Christ; the officers come back empty-handed, and Nicodemus speaks up for him.

Tabernacles (also called Booths or Ingathering) was the most joyful Jewish feast. After the autumn harvest, people lived in booths for seven days, remembering how their ancestors lived in tents in the wilderness while God provided for them. Later Jewish sources (the Mishnah) describe two famous rituals of the feast. Each morning a priest drew water from the Pool of Siloam and carried it in a golden pitcher to pour on the altar, praying for the coming year's rain. In the evening huge lamps were lit in the temple's Court of the Women, lighting up the city. The "living water" of chapter 7 and the "light of the world" of chapter 8 are spoken against the background of these two rituals.

## Going Deeper: 7:3–9 "Not even his brothers believed in him"

**1. The brothers' advice.** "Leave here and go to Judea, that your disciples also may see the works you are doing... If you do these things, show yourself to the world" (7:3–4). It sounds like they're helping promote him, but John says plainly, "For not even his brothers believed in him" (7:5). They had seen his signs, yet they treated Jesus as someone who needed to build a reputation.

**2. "My time has not yet come."** Jesus says, "My time has not yet come, but your time is always here" (7:6). "Time" here is *kairos* (the right moment), a different word from the "hour" (*hōra*) of 2:4, but related in meaning: all Jesus' actions follow the Father's timetable, not the crowd's expectations.

**3. How the story continues.** What happened to the unbelieving brothers? The risen Lord appeared to James (1 Cor 15:7). After the ascension, Jesus' brothers were devoting themselves to prayer with the disciples (Acts 1:14). James became a leader of the Jerusalem church and wrote the letter of James. The "unbelief" of this chapter isn't the end of the story.

## Going Deeper: 7:14–24 "If anyone's will is to do God's will"

**1. "Never studied."** The Jews marvel, "How is it that this man has learning, when he has never studied?" (7:15). They mean he had no formal training under a rabbi. Jesus answers, "My teaching is not mine, but his who sent me" (7:16). His authority comes not from a school but from the Father.

**2. The condition for knowing the truth.** "If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know whether the teaching is from God or whether I am speaking on my own authority" (7:17). Jesus says the barrier to knowing the truth isn't only in the head but in the will. Someone willing to obey God will be able to tell whether Jesus' words come from God. Obedience isn't what comes after knowing; it is part of knowing.

**3. An argument from lesser to greater.** Jesus points out that they work on the Sabbath too: if a baby's eighth day falls on a Sabbath, they still circumcise him (7:22–23). Circumcision concerns one part of the body, while Jesus made "a man's whole body well" on the Sabbath. This is the "light to heavy" argument Jewish teachers often used: if you may deal with one part of the body on the Sabbath to keep the law, why not heal the whole person? "Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment" (7:24).

## Going Deeper: 7:37–39 "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink"

**1. The time and place.** "On the last day of the feast, the great day" (7:37) may be the seventh day or the solemn assembly of the eighth. If it was the seventh, the water ceremony that day was at its grandest, with the priests circling the altar seven times. At such a moment, with the crowd having just watched water from the golden pitcher poured on the altar, Jesus stands and cries out, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink." The water the feast prayed for now stands in front of them.

**2. "Belly" and "heart."** "Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water" (7:38). The Greek is *koilia*, literally "belly," which the Chinese Union Version keeps; the ESV renders it "heart." In Hebrew idiom the belly is a person's innermost self.

**3. Whose heart? A question of punctuation.** Greek has no punctuation, and verses 37–38 can be divided two ways. The traditional reading (Chinese Union Version, ESV): "Whoever believes in me... out of *his* [the believer's] heart will flow rivers of living water." The other reading: "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me, and let the one who believes in me drink. As the Scripture has said, out of *his* [Christ's] heart will flow rivers of living water." Both readings have early patristic support (the first largely among Greek fathers such as Origen, the second among Western writers such as Hippolytus and Cyprian). The second reading also fits Ezekiel 47 (water flowing from the temple) and Zechariah 14:8 (living waters flowing out from Jerusalem, in a chapter that speaks of the Feast of Booths). John has already told us that Jesus spoke of his body as the temple (2:19–21), and on the cross blood and water flow from his side (19:34). Both readings fit the whole book's teaching: the source of living water is Christ, and those who believe in him receive the Spirit and become channels through which that water flows out.

**4. "As yet the Spirit had not been given."** John explains: "Now this he said about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were to receive, for as yet the Spirit had not been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified" (7:39). The Greek literally says "for not yet was the Spirit"; the ESV and the Chinese Union Version supply "given." This doesn't mean the Spirit didn't yet exist; the Spirit was at work throughout the Old Testament. It means the new-covenant outpouring of the Spirit, dwelling in every believer, had to wait until Jesus was "lifted up" and glorified (20:22; Acts 2). In John, "glorified" refers to the cross, resurrection and ascension.

> The point: Every year at Tabernacles Israel prayed for rain and God's provision. Jesus announces that the provision they are waiting for is himself, and what he gives is the Spirit.

## Going Deeper: 7:40–52 The Crowd's Argument and Several Ironies

**1. "Is the Christ to come from Galilee?"** Some object, "Has not the Scripture said that the Christ comes from the offspring of David, and comes from Bethlehem, the village where David was?" (7:42). John doesn't correct them. His readers most likely already knew from Matthew and Luke that Jesus was born in Bethlehem. The speakers think they've refuted him, but they've actually stated the truth.

**2. "No one ever spoke like this man!"** The officers sent by the chief priests to arrest Jesus come back empty-handed and can only say, "No one ever spoke like this man!" (7:46). Even the men ordered to seize him were won over by his words.

**3. Nicodemus's half sentence.** Nicodemus says, "Does our law judge a man without first giving him a hearing and learning what he does?" (7:51). He doesn't openly confess faith in Jesus, but he stands up for due process. The man who came by night takes one small step in the light.

**4. "No prophet arises from Galilee."** The Pharisees retort, "Search and see that no prophet arises from Galilee" (7:52). That isn't actually true: the prophet Jonah came from Gath-hepher in Galilee (2 Kgs 14:25). One very early manuscript (P66) reads "*the* Prophet does not arise from Galilee," referring to the prophet like Moses of Deuteronomy 18. Either way, the men who claim to know the Scriptures best expose their blind spot in a single sentence.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus' brothers lived with him every day and didn't believe in him, yet James later became a pillar of the church (Gal 2:9), and the brothers joined the praying church (Acts 1:14). Am I discouraged by a family member's unbelief? Jesus said, "If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know." Am I waiting to understand everything before I'll obey? Where am I "thirsty" today? Do I go to him to drink, or look somewhere else?
