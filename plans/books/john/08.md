## 本章脉络

第八章仍在住棚节期间的圣殿里。**行淫时被拿的妇人**（7:53–8:11，和合本作8:1-11）是一段在抄本流传上很特别的经文。**我是世界的光**（12-20节）：在点灯的妇女院附近，耶稣宣告自己是光。**我所去的地方，你们不能到**（21-30节）：耶稣说他们若不信，必要死在罪中。**真理必叫你们得以自由**（31-47节）：对话转为激烈的争论，关于谁才是亚伯拉罕真正的子孙。**还没有亚伯拉罕就有了我**（48-59节）：全章在最高的宣告中达到顶点，众人拿起石头要打他。

这一章的争论非常尖锐，耶稣说出了「你们是出于你们的父魔鬼」这样的话（8:44）。读这一章要记得：这是犹太人内部的辩论，说话的耶稣是犹太人，写书的约翰也是犹太人。历史上有人曾经用这类经文来为反犹太主义辩护，这是对经文严重的误用。耶稣严厉的话，是对着拒绝真理的人心说的，不是对着一个民族。

## 深读 8:1-11 行淫时被拿的妇人

**1. 一段特别的经文。** 这段故事一般标为7:53–8:11（和合本把7:53并入8:1）。它不见于最早、最好的希腊文抄本（包括两份三世纪的蒲草纸抄本，以及四世纪的西乃抄本和梵蒂冈抄本），早期希腊教父注释约翰福音时也没有提到它。在后来的抄本里，它出现在不同的位置：有的在这里，有的在约翰福音7:36之后或21:25之后，也有的在路加福音21:38之后。所以多数学者，包括许多福音派学者（如卡森），认为它原本不是约翰福音的一部分。

**2. 为什么还保留在圣经里？** 许多学者同时认为，这很可能是一件真实发生过的事，在教会中口传保存，后来才被抄进福音书里。它的内容与耶稣在其他福音书中的形象完全一致。和合本把它保留在正文中，英文ESV用双括号标出并加注说明。读的时候，我们可以从中学习耶稣的样式，但不宜单凭这一段来建立教义。

**3. 一个陷阱。** 文士和法利赛人说：「摩西在律法上吩咐我们把这样的妇人用石头打死。你说该把她怎么样呢？」（8:5）他们并不真关心这个妇人，「他们说这话，乃试探耶稣」（8:6）。若耶稣说不要打，他就违背摩西；若说要打，他就违背罗马法律（罗马不准犹太人自行执行死刑，18:31），也违背他一向对罪人的怜悯。而且，律法要求通奸的男女两人都要治死（利20:10；申22:22），那个男人在哪里？

**4. 地上画字。** 耶稣弯腰用指头在地上画字，经文没有说他写了什么，许多猜测都只是猜测。他直起腰说：「你们中间谁是没有罪的，谁就可以先拿石头打她。」（8:7）他没有否定律法，而是把审判者也带到律法面前。众人「从老到少，一个一个地都出去了」。

**5. 恩典与圣洁一起。** 只剩下耶稣和那妇人。唯一真正无罪、有资格丢石头的那一位说：「我也不定你的罪。去吧，从此不要再犯罪了！」（8:11）他能这样说，并不是废掉律法，而是因为他来不是要定罪，乃是要拯救（3:17），并且他自己要在十字架上承担罪的刑罚（罗8:1-3）。两句话缺一不可：没有第一句，就没有福音；没有第二句，恩典就变成了纵容。

## 深读 8:12「我是世界的光」

**1. 住棚节的灯。** 住棚节期间，圣殿妇女院立着巨大的金灯台，晚上点亮，据说光照亮了全耶路撒冷。约翰特别注明耶稣说这些话的地点是「殿里的库房」（8:20），库房就在妇女院。在那些灯台旁边，耶稣说：「我是世界的光。」

**2. 旷野里的火柱。** 住棚节纪念的是旷野的旅程，那时神在火柱中引导百姓，夜间照亮他们的路（出13:21-22）。耶稣说「跟从我的，就不在黑暗里走」，正是火柱的画面：光不只是让人看见，更是在前面领路。

**3. 「世界的」光。** 不只是以色列的光，而是世界的。以赛亚书49:6说神的仆人要「作外邦人的光」。这也呼应序言：「那光是真光，照亮一切生在世上的人。」（1:9）

**4. 下一章的实例。** 耶稣在第九章再次说「我在世上的时候，是世上的光」（9:5），随即医好一个生来瞎眼的人。第八章说的，第九章就行出来了。

## 深读 8:24、28 被和合本补上的「基督」

**1. 原文没有「基督」。** 和合本8:24译作「你们若不信我是基督，必要死在罪中」，8:28译作「必知道我是基督」。但希腊文在这两处都只有 *egō eimi*，就是「我是」。和合本为了通顺加上了「基督」（有些版本用不同字体标示）。英文ESV译作「I am he」。

**2. 这个差别很重要。** 单独的「我是」，回响着以赛亚书43:10：「便可以知道，且信服我，又明白我就是耶和华。」和合本在这里补了「耶和华」，七十士译本只作 *egō eimi*（我是），正和和合本在约翰福音8:24、28补上「基督」一样。那是神自己的宣告。耶稣说：你们若不信「我是」，必要死在罪中。要信的不只是他是一位弥赛亚，更是他与父同享那位「我是」之神的身份，却又与父有别（8:28下「我没有一件事是凭着自己做的」）。

**3. 「你们举起人子以后」。** 8:28直译是：「你们举起人子以后，必知道『我是』。」把他举起来的，正是他们自己，也就是把他钉在十字架上。十字架不只是他们对耶稣的判决，也会成为耶稣身份最清楚的显明。

## 深读 8:31-36「真理必叫你们得以自由」

**1. 说给「信他的犹太人」。** 这段话的对象是「信他的犹太人」（8:31），但他们的反应很快变成拿石头要打他（8:59）。这又是2:23-25的情况：一种很浅的「信」。耶稣给的标准是：「你们若常常遵守我的道，就真是我的门徒。」「常常遵守」原文是「住在」（*menō*），真门徒是住在他话语里的人。

**2. 「我们从来没有作过谁的奴仆」。** 他们回答：「我们是亚伯拉罕的后裔，从来没有作过谁的奴仆。」（8:33）这句话在历史上很难成立：以色列人在埃及作过奴隶，被掳到巴比伦，此刻正在罗马统治之下。但耶稣指的不是政治上的奴役：「所有犯罪的就是罪的奴仆。」（8:34）

**3. 儿子与奴仆。** 「奴仆不能永远住在家里；儿子是永远住在家里。」（8:35）奴仆在家中没有永久的地位，儿子却有。「所以天父的儿子若叫你们自由，你们就真自由了。」（8:36）使人自由的「真理」，不是一套抽象的知识，而是一个人：那位儿子（14:6）。

> 要点：这节常被引用为求知或言论自由的格言，但在原文的上下文里，它说的是：只有住在基督话语里、被那位儿子释放的人，才能从罪的奴役中得自由。

## 深读 8:56-59「还没有亚伯拉罕就有了我」

**1. 亚伯拉罕看见了他的日子。** 「你们的祖宗亚伯拉罕欢欢喜喜地仰望我的日子，既看见了就快乐。」（8:56）亚伯拉罕怎样「看见」？解经家指向神对他的应许（创12:3，万族要因他得福），以及以撒的出生、摩利亚山上的羊羔（创22:8、14「在耶和华的山上必有预备」）。亚伯拉罕凭信心远远望见了应许的成就（来11:13）。

**2. 两个动词的对比。** 「还没有亚伯拉罕就有了我」，希腊文是 *prin Abraam genesthai egō eimi*。说到亚伯拉罕，用的是 *genesthai*（出生、成为、开始存在）；说到自己，用的是 *eimi*（是、存在），而且是现在时。这正是序言里的对比：万物是「成了」（*egeneto*，1:3），道是「本有」（*ēn*，1:1）。耶稣不是说「我比亚伯拉罕早出生」（那应该说「我在亚伯拉罕以前就已经有了」），而是说：亚伯拉罕有开始，我没有。

**3. 出埃及记3:14。** 神在荆棘火中对摩西说「我是自有永有的」，七十士译本作 *egō eimi ho ōn*。这句话也回响以赛亚书43:10-13的「我是」（见上文8:24）。耶稣绝对地用「我是」，听的人完全明白了。

**4. 石头。** 「于是他们拿石头要打他。」（8:59）利未记24:16规定，亵渎耶和华名的必要被石头打死。他们的反应证明他们听懂了：耶稣宣称的，是神的名与神的永恒。如果他的宣称不是真的，他就是亵渎者；如果是真的，他们拿起石头所要打的，就是神。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 8 is still in the temple during the Feast of Tabernacles. **The woman caught in adultery** (7:53–8:11) is a passage with an unusual manuscript history. **I am the light of the world** (vv. 12–20): near the Court of the Women, where the lamps were lit, Jesus announces that he is the light. **Where I am going, you cannot come** (vv. 21–30): Jesus warns that unless they believe, they will die in their sins. **The truth will set you free** (vv. 31–47): the conversation becomes a fierce dispute over who are Abraham's true children. **Before Abraham was, I am** (vv. 48–59): the chapter reaches its climax in the highest claim, and the crowd picks up stones.

The dispute in this chapter is very sharp. Jesus even says, "You are of your father the devil" (8:44). We need to remember that this is an argument within Judaism: Jesus, who is speaking, was a Jew, and John, who wrote it down, was a Jew. In history some have used texts like this to justify antisemitism, which is a grave misuse of Scripture. Jesus' severe words are aimed at hearts that refuse the truth, not at a people.

## Going Deeper: 7:53–8:11 The Woman Caught in Adultery

**1. An unusual passage.** This story isn't found in the earliest and best Greek manuscripts (including two third-century papyri and the fourth-century Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Vaticanus), and early Greek church fathers commenting on John don't mention it. In later manuscripts it appears in different places: here, after John 7:36, after John 21:25, or after Luke 21:38. So most scholars, including many evangelicals (Carson among them), believe it wasn't originally part of John's Gospel.

**2. Why is it still in our Bibles?** Many scholars also think it is very likely a true event, preserved in the church's oral tradition and later copied into the Gospels. Its content fits completely with the Jesus of the other Gospels. The Chinese Union Version keeps it in the main text; the ESV marks it with double brackets and a note. We can learn from Jesus' example here, but it isn't wise to build doctrine on this passage alone.

**3. A trap.** The scribes and Pharisees say, "Now in the Law Moses commanded us to stone such women. So what do you say?" (8:5). They don't really care about the woman; "This they said to test him" (8:6). If Jesus says not to stone her, he contradicts Moses. If he says to stone her, he defies Roman law (Rome didn't allow the Jews to carry out executions themselves, 18:31) and his own consistent mercy toward sinners. And the law required both the man and the woman in adultery to be put to death (Lev 20:10; Deut 22:22). Where was the man?

**4. Writing on the ground.** Jesus bends down and writes on the ground with his finger. The text doesn't say what he wrote, and the many guesses are only guesses. He stands up and says, "Let him who is without sin among you be the first to throw a stone at her" (8:7). He doesn't deny the law; he brings the judges themselves before it. They go away "one by one, beginning with the older ones."

**5. Grace and holiness together.** Only Jesus and the woman remain. The one person truly without sin, the only one qualified to throw a stone, says, "Neither do I condemn you; go, and from now on sin no more" (8:11). He can say this without setting the law aside because he came not to condemn but to save (3:17), and he would himself bear the condemnation of sin on the cross (Rom 8:1–3). Neither sentence can be dropped. Without the first there is no gospel; without the second, grace turns into permissiveness.

## Going Deeper: 8:12 "I am the light of the world"

**1. The lamps of Tabernacles.** During the feast, great golden lampstands stood in the temple's Court of the Women. They were lit at night, and their light was said to shine over all Jerusalem. John notes that Jesus spoke these words "in the treasury" (8:20), which was in the Court of the Women. Beside those lamps Jesus says, "I am the light of the world."

**2. The pillar of fire in the wilderness.** Tabernacles remembered the wilderness journey, when God led his people in a pillar of fire that lit their way at night (Ex 13:21–22). "Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness" is the picture of that pillar: the light doesn't only let you see; it goes ahead and leads.

**3. The light "of the world."** Not only Israel's light, but the world's. Isaiah 49:6 says God's servant will be "a light for the nations." This also echoes the Prologue: "The true light, which enlightens everyone, was coming into the world" (1:9).

**4. The example in the next chapter.** In chapter 9 Jesus says again, "As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world" (9:5), and then heals a man born blind. What chapter 8 says, chapter 9 enacts.

## Going Deeper: 8:24, 28 The "Christ" the Chinese Union Version Adds

**1. "Christ" isn't in the Greek.** The Chinese Union Version renders 8:24 "unless you believe that I am the Christ, you will die in your sins," and 8:28 "you will know that I am the Christ." But in both places the Greek has only *egō eimi*, "I am." The Chinese translators added "Christ" for smoothness (some editions mark it in a different typeface). The ESV has "I am he."

**2. The difference matters.** The bare "I am" echoes the Septuagint of Isaiah 43:10: "that you may know and believe me and understand that I am he" (*egō eimi*), God's own declaration. Jesus says that unless you believe "I am," you will die in your sins. What must be believed is not only that he is a messiah, but that he shares the identity of the God who says "I am," while remaining distinct from the Father (8:28b, "I do nothing on my own authority").

**3. "When you have lifted up the Son of Man."** Verse 28 says, "When you have lifted up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am he." The ones who will lift him up are they themselves, by crucifying him. The cross will be not only their verdict on Jesus but the clearest revelation of who he is.

## Going Deeper: 8:31–36 "The truth will set you free"

**1. Spoken to "the Jews who had believed in him."** These words are addressed to "the Jews who had believed in him" (8:31), yet their response soon becomes picking up stones (8:59). It's the situation of 2:23–25 again: a shallow kind of "belief." Jesus gives the standard: "If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples." "Abide" is *menō*: true disciples live in his word.

**2. "We have never been enslaved to anyone."** They answer, "We are offspring of Abraham and have never been enslaved to anyone" (8:33). Historically that's hard to maintain: Israel was enslaved in Egypt, exiled to Babylon and was under Roman rule at that very moment. But Jesus isn't speaking of political slavery: "everyone who commits sin is a slave to sin" (8:34).

**3. Son and slave.** "The slave does not remain in the house forever; the son remains forever" (8:35). A slave has no permanent place in the household; a son does. "So if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed" (8:36). The "truth" that sets free isn't abstract knowledge but a person: the Son (14:6).

> The point: This verse is often quoted as a motto for learning or free speech, but in context it means that only those who abide in Christ's word and are set free by the Son are freed from slavery to sin.

## Going Deeper: 8:56–59 "Before Abraham was, I am"

**1. Abraham saw his day.** "Your father Abraham rejoiced that he would see my day. He saw it and was glad" (8:56). How did Abraham "see"? Interpreters point to God's promise to him (Gen 12:3, all the families of the earth blessed in him), the birth of Isaac, and the ram on Mount Moriah (Gen 22:8, 14, "On the mount of the LORD it shall be provided"). By faith Abraham saw the fulfillment of the promise from afar (Heb 11:13).

**2. A contrast of two verbs.** "Before Abraham was, I am" is *prin Abraam genesthai egō eimi*. For Abraham the verb is *genesthai* (to be born, to come into being); for himself it is *eimi* (to be), in the present tense. This is exactly the Prologue's contrast: all things "came to be" (*egeneto*, 1:3), while the Word simply "was" (*ēn*, 1:1). Jesus isn't saying "I was born earlier than Abraham" (that would be "before Abraham was, I was"). He's saying Abraham had a beginning, and he doesn't.

**3. Exodus 3:14.** At the burning bush God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM," which the Septuagint renders *egō eimi ho ōn*. It also echoes the "I am he" of Isaiah 43:10–13 (see 8:24 above). Jesus uses "I am" absolutely, and his hearers understood perfectly.

**4. Stones.** "So they picked up stones to throw at him" (8:59). Leviticus 24:16 required that whoever blasphemes the name of the LORD be stoned. Their reaction shows they understood: Jesus was claiming God's name and God's eternity. If the claim isn't true, he is a blasphemer. If it is true, the one they picked up stones to strike is God.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus said to the woman, "Neither do I condemn you; go, and from now on sin no more." Do I hold on to one of these sentences and forget the other? Where am I still "a slave to sin" while thinking I'm free? Jesus says "I am." The God who was before Abraham and spoke to Moses from the burning bush is present in my situation now. How do I need to respond to this "I am"?
