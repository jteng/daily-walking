## 本章脉络

第九章是一个完整的戏剧，场景一幕接一幕。**医治**（1-7节）：门徒问「是谁犯了罪」，耶稣用泥抹在瞎子眼上，叫他去西罗亚池洗。**邻舍的疑问**（8-12节）。**法利赛人第一次审问**（13-17节）。**父母被传**（18-23节）：他们怕被赶出会堂，不敢多说。**第二次审问**（24-34节）：被医好的人越说越勇敢，最后被赶出去。**耶稣找到他**（35-38节）：「主啊，我信！」**属灵的瞎眼**（39-41节）。

这一章有两条相反的线。被医好的人越来越看得清楚：他称耶稣为「一个人，名叫耶稣」（9:11）、「先知」（9:17）、「从神来的」（9:33），最后说「主啊，我信」并拜他（9:38）。法利赛人却越来越瞎：从「这个人不是从神来的」（9:16）到「我们知道这人是个罪人」（9:24），最后把真理赶出了会堂。这是第八章「我是世界的光」的实例：光来了，有人看见，有人反而瞎了。

## 深读 9:1-5「是谁犯了罪？」

**1. 门徒的问题。** 「拉比，这人生来是瞎眼的，是谁犯了罪？是这人呢？是他父母呢？」（9:2）当时普遍认为疾病是罪的直接惩罚。至于一个人怎么可能在出生以前犯罪，当时的犹太教师有讨论，有人认为胎儿在母腹中就可能犯罪。

**2. 耶稣拒绝这个推论。** 「也不是这人犯了罪，也不是他父母犯了罪。」（9:3）耶稣不是说他们完全无罪，而是说这个瞎眼不是某个具体罪行的报应。这一句话释放了无数受苦的人：苦难不一定是神对某个罪的惩罚。

**3. 「是要在他身上显出神的作为来」。** 这句话有两种读法。常见的读法是：他生来瞎眼，目的是要显出神的作为。另一种读法把它接到下一句：「……是要显出神的作为来，**所以**趁着白日，我们必须做那差我来者的工。」第二种读法避免了「神让他瞎了几十年就为了这一刻」的印象。不论哪一种，耶稣把问题从「为什么」转向了「现在神要做什么」。

**4. 「我们必须」。** 「趁着白日，**我们**必须做那差我来者的工。」（9:4）耶稣说「我们」，把门徒也包括在内。面对苦难，门徒不是坐着讨论谁该负责的旁观者，而是要一同做工的人。

## 深读 9:6-7 泥与西罗亚池

**1. 泥土与创造。** 耶稣「吐唾沫在地上，用唾沫和泥抹在瞎子的眼睛上」（9:6）。为什么用泥？许多解经家联想到创世记2:7，神「用地上的尘土造人」。这个人生来缺了眼睛的功能，耶稣用泥土，好像在完成创造。这个联想没有在经文中明说，但很合乎约翰福音从「太初」开始的整体主题。

**2. 「西罗亚」就是「奉差遣」。** 约翰特意翻译了池子的名字：「西罗亚翻出来就是『奉差遣』。」（9:7）在约翰福音里，耶稣反复称父为「差我来的」，他自己就是那「奉差遣」的。瞎子在「奉差遣」的池子里洗，就看见了。约翰似乎在暗示：真正使人看见的，是那位被父差来的。

**3. 住棚节的池子。** 西罗亚池正是住棚节祭司每天打水的地方（参第七章）。耶稣刚在节期宣告「人若渴了，可以到我这里来喝」、「我是世界的光」，现在，西罗亚的水和世界的光，在一个瞎子身上相遇了。

## 深读 9:13-34 审问与被赶出会堂

**1. 又是安息日。** 「耶稣和泥开瞎子眼睛的日子是安息日。」（9:14）当时的规条把揉面团之类的动作列为安息日禁止的工作，和泥抹眼可能被视为同类。法利赛人的逻辑是：他不守安息日，所以不是从神来的。被医好的人的逻辑是：他能开瞎子的眼睛，所以是从神来的。

**2. 「赶出会堂」。** 「犹太人已经商议定了，若有认耶稣是基督的，要把他赶出会堂。」（9:22）「赶出会堂」（*aposynagōgos*）这个词只出现在约翰福音（9:22；12:42；16:2）。有学者认为这反映了约翰写作时代（一世纪末）犹太会堂对基督徒的排斥；也有学者认为，在耶稣的时代，地方会堂对跟随耶稣的人采取这类措施，是完全可能的。对当时的人来说，被赶出会堂不只是宗教上的处分，也意味着在社区中被孤立。父母宁可不说真话，也不愿付这个代价。

**3. 「有一件事我知道」。** 第二次审问时，被医好的人说：「他是个罪人不是，我不知道；有一件事我知道，从前我是眼瞎的，如今能看见了。」（9:25）他没有神学训练，回答不了所有的问题，但他有一个任何人都无法否认的见证。

**4. 越说越勇敢。** 他甚至反问：「莫非你们也要作他的门徒吗？」（9:27）又说：「这人若不是从神来的，什么也不能做。」（9:33）法利赛人回答：「你全然生在罪孽中，还要教训我们吗？」（9:34）讽刺的是，他们用的正是门徒一开始的假设：生来瞎眼，必是罪的结果。

## 深读 9:35-38「主啊，我信！」

**1. 耶稣找他。** 「耶稣听说他们把他赶出去，后来遇见他。」（9:35）原文是「找到他」。被会堂赶出去的人，被好牧人找到了。这为第十章的好牧人讲论铺了路：牧人认识自己的羊，并且出去找它们。

**2. 「神的儿子」还是「人子」？** 和合本译作「你信神的儿子吗？」但最早的抄本（包括两份三世纪的蒲草纸抄本和四世纪的两大抄本）作「你信**人子**吗？」英文ESV作「Son of Man」。「人子」是耶稣最常用来称呼自己的方式，回响着但以理书7:13那位驾云而来、得着权柄的「人子」，也连到9:39的「审判」。

**3. 同一个字，两个意思。** 他问：「主啊，谁是人子，叫我信他呢？」（9:36）这里的「主啊」（*kyrie*），在希腊文里也可以只是「先生」的礼貌称呼，英文ESV在这里就译作「sir」。耶稣说：「你已经看见他，现在和你说话的就是他。」他回答：「主啊（*kyrie*），我信！」就拜耶稣（9:38）。同一个字，从「先生」变成了「主」。

**4. 他「已经看见」了。** 「你已经**看见**他」，这句话对一个生来瞎眼的人有双重的意义：他的肉眼第一次看见的人之一，就是耶稣；而现在，他的心眼也看见了。「拜」（*proskyneō*）在约翰福音里用于敬拜神（4:20-24），他向耶稣下拜，耶稣没有拒绝。

## 深读 9:39-41「能看见的，反瞎了眼」

**1. 审判的反转。** 「我为审判到这世上来，叫不能看见的，可以看见；能看见的，反瞎了眼。」（9:39）这与3:17「神差他的儿子降世，不是要定世人的罪」并不矛盾。耶稣来的目的是拯救，但光一来，就把人分开了：承认自己瞎了的人得以看见，自以为看见的人就暴露出真正的瞎眼。

**2. 「我们也瞎了眼吗？」** 法利赛人问：「难道我们也瞎了眼吗？」耶稣回答：「你们若瞎了眼，就没有罪了；但如今你们说『我们能看见』，所以你们的罪还在。」（9:40-41）最危险的瞎眼，是不知道自己瞎了。

> 要点：一个没有神学训练的乞丐，因着一件确实的经历和诚实的心，一步步认出了耶稣；一群饱学的宗教领袖，因着骄傲，一步步把自己关在黑暗里。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 9 is a complete drama, played out scene by scene. **The healing** (vv. 1–7): the disciples ask "who sinned?", and Jesus anoints the blind man's eyes with mud and sends him to wash in the Pool of Siloam. **The neighbors' questions** (vv. 8–12). **The Pharisees' first interrogation** (vv. 13–17). **The parents summoned** (vv. 18–23): afraid of being put out of the synagogue, they won't say more. **The second interrogation** (vv. 24–34): the healed man grows bolder and bolder until they throw him out. **Jesus finds him** (vv. 35–38): "Lord, I believe." **Spiritual blindness** (vv. 39–41).

Two lines run in opposite directions through this chapter. The healed man sees more and more clearly: he calls Jesus "the man called Jesus" (9:11), "a prophet" (9:17), "from God" (9:33), and finally says, "Lord, I believe," and worships him (9:38). The Pharisees grow blinder and blinder: from "This man is not from God" (9:16) to "We know that this man is a sinner" (9:24), until they throw the truth out of the synagogue. This is chapter 8's "I am the light of the world" in action: when the light comes, some see and some go blind.

## Going Deeper: 9:1–5 "Who sinned?"

**1. The disciples' question.** "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" (9:2). It was widely believed that illness was the direct punishment for sin. As for how someone could sin before birth, Jewish teachers of the time debated it, and some thought a child could sin in the womb.

**2. Jesus rejects the inference.** "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents" (9:3). Jesus isn't saying they were sinless, but that this blindness wasn't payback for a particular sin. This one sentence has freed countless sufferers: suffering is not necessarily God's punishment for a particular sin.

**3. "That the works of God might be displayed in him."** This can be read two ways. The usual reading: he was born blind so that God's works could be displayed. The other reading joins it to the next sentence: "...but that the works of God might be displayed in him, *we* must work the works of him who sent me while it is day." The second reading avoids the impression that God left him blind for decades just for this moment. Either way, Jesus turns the question from "why?" to "what is God going to do now?"

**4. "We must."** "*We* must work the works of him who sent me while it is day" (9:4). Jesus says "we," including the disciples. Faced with suffering, disciples aren't spectators debating who's to blame; they are people called to work alongside him.

## Going Deeper: 9:6–7 Mud and the Pool of Siloam

**1. Mud and creation.** Jesus "spat on the ground and made mud with the saliva. Then he anointed the man's eyes with the mud" (9:6). Why mud? Many interpreters recall Genesis 2:7, where God formed man "of dust from the ground." This man was born without working eyes, and Jesus uses earth as if finishing the work of creation. The text doesn't say so explicitly, but it fits John's whole theme beginning with "in the beginning."

**2. "Siloam" means "Sent."** John deliberately translates the pool's name: "the pool of Siloam (which means Sent)" (9:7). In John, Jesus repeatedly calls the Father "him who sent me"; he himself is the one who is "sent." The blind man washes in the pool called "Sent" and sees. John seems to hint that the one who truly makes people see is the one the Father sent.

**3. The pool of Tabernacles.** Siloam is exactly where the priests drew water each day at the Feast of Tabernacles (see chapter 7). Jesus has just proclaimed at the feast, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink," and "I am the light of the world." Now the water of Siloam and the light of the world meet in a blind man.

## Going Deeper: 9:13–34 The Interrogations and Being Put Out of the Synagogue

**1. Another Sabbath.** "Now it was a Sabbath day when Jesus made the mud and opened his eyes" (9:14). The rules of the time listed actions like kneading dough as forbidden Sabbath work, and making mud to anoint eyes might be counted the same way. The Pharisees' logic: he doesn't keep the Sabbath, so he isn't from God. The healed man's logic: he can open the eyes of the blind, so he is from God.

**2. "Put out of the synagogue."** "The Jews had already agreed that if anyone should confess Jesus to be Christ, he was to be put out of the synagogue" (9:22). The word *aposynagōgos* appears only in John (9:22; 12:42; 16:2). Some scholars think it reflects synagogues expelling Christians in John's own day, at the end of the first century. Others think it was quite possible for local synagogues to take such measures against Jesus' followers in his lifetime. For people then, being put out of the synagogue wasn't only a religious penalty; it meant being cut off from the community. The parents would rather not tell the truth than pay that price.

**3. "One thing I do know."** At the second interrogation the healed man says, "Whether he is a sinner I do not know. One thing I do know, that though I was blind, now I see" (9:25). He had no theological training and couldn't answer every question, but he had a testimony no one could deny.

**4. Growing bolder.** He even asks, "Do you also want to become his disciples?" (9:27), and says, "If this man were not from God, he could do nothing" (9:33). The Pharisees reply, "You were born in utter sin, and would you teach us?" (9:34). The irony is that they use the very assumption the disciples started with: born blind must mean born in sin.

## Going Deeper: 9:35–38 "Lord, I believe"

**1. Jesus looks for him.** "Jesus heard that they had cast him out, and having found him..." (9:35). The man thrown out of the synagogue is found by the good shepherd. This prepares for the good shepherd discourse of chapter 10: the shepherd knows his sheep and goes out to find them.

**2. "Son of God" or "Son of Man"?** The Chinese Union Version reads "Do you believe in the Son of God?" But the earliest manuscripts (including two third-century papyri and the two great fourth-century codices) read "Do you believe in the *Son of Man*?", which the ESV follows. "Son of Man" is Jesus' most common way of referring to himself. It echoes the "son of man" of Daniel 7:13, who comes with the clouds and receives authority, and it connects with the "judgment" of 9:39.

**3. One word, two meanings.** The man asks, "And who is he, sir, that I may believe in him?" (9:36). "Sir" is *kyrie*, which can be simply polite or can mean "Lord"; the Chinese Union Version renders it "Lord" here. Jesus says, "You have seen him, and it is he who is speaking to you." He answers, "Lord (*kyrie*), I believe," and worships him (9:38). The same word moves from "sir" to "Lord."

**4. He "has seen" him.** "You have *seen* him": for a man born blind, these words mean two things. One of the first people his eyes ever saw was Jesus, and now the eyes of his heart see too. "Worship" (*proskyneō*) is used in John for worshiping God (4:20–24). The man bows down to Jesus, and Jesus doesn't refuse it.

## Going Deeper: 9:39–41 "Those who see may become blind"

**1. Judgment turned upside down.** "For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind" (9:39). This doesn't contradict 3:17, "God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world." Jesus came to save, but when the light comes it divides people: those who admit they're blind are given sight, and those who think they see are exposed in their real blindness.

**2. "Are we also blind?"** The Pharisees ask, "Are we also blind?" Jesus answers, "If you were blind, you would have no guilt; but now that you say, 'We see,' your guilt remains" (9:40–41). The most dangerous blindness is not knowing you're blind.

> The point: A beggar with no theological training, through one undeniable experience and an honest heart, recognizes Jesus step by step. A group of learned religious leaders, through pride, shut themselves step by step in the dark.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** When suffering comes, is my first question "whose fault is it?" or "what does God want to do here?" My testimony may not answer every theological question, but can I say, "One thing I do know"? What is that one thing? Is there some "we see" pride in me that keeps me blind to my own blindness?
