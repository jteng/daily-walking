## 本章脉络

第十章分两个时段。**好牧人的讲论**（1-21节）紧接着第九章：法利赛人刚把那个被医好的人赶出会堂，耶稣就说起羊圈、门、盗贼、雇工和好牧人。**修殿节的对峙**（22-42节）发生在两三个月以后的冬天，犹太人围着他问：「你若是基督，就明明地告诉我们。」耶稣说「我与父原为一」，他们又拿起石头。

要读懂这一章，必须先读以西结书34章。神在那里责备以色列的牧人（领袖）只顾喂养自己，「瘦弱的，你们没有养壮……被逐的，你们没有领回」（结34:4）。然后神说：「我必亲自寻找我的羊。」（结34:11）又说：「我必立一牧人照管他们……就是我的仆人大卫。」（结34:23）神亲自牧养，又有一位大卫的子孙作牧人，这两个应许都在耶稣身上应验。第九章的法利赛人正像那些坏牧人，把一只羊赶了出去；而耶稣，像神应许的那样，亲自去把它找回来。

## 深读 10:1-6 羊认得他的声音

**1. 羊圈与看门的。** 古时的羊圈常是石墙围起的院子，晚上几群羊关在一起，由看门的守着。早上，各个牧人来到门口呼叫，自己的羊就从混在一起的羊群中走出来，跟着他走。

**2. 「按着名叫自己的羊」。** 牧人「按着名叫自己的羊，把羊领出来……羊也跟着他，因为认得他的声音」（10:3-4）。这是很个人的关系。刚刚被赶出会堂的那个人，正是听见了耶稣的声音，就跟从了他（9:35-38）。

**3. 「放出」。** 牧人「放出」自己的羊（*ekballō*，10:4），这个字在9:34-35被译作「赶出去」：法利赛人把他「赶出」会堂。约翰的读者也许会注意到这个对比：会堂把他赶出去，牧人却把自己的羊放出来，带往草场。

**4. 他们不明白。** 「耶稣将这比喻告诉他们，但他们不明白所说的是什么意思。」（10:6）这里的「比喻」是 *paroimia*（象征性的说法），不是对观福音的 *parabolē*。约翰福音没有对观福音那种故事型的比喻，但有这类延伸的象征。

## 深读 10:7-10「我就是羊的门」

**1. 两个画面。** 耶稣先说自己是「门」（10:7、9），后来说自己是「牧人」（10:11、14）。有一个常被提到的说法：在野外的临时羊圈没有门，牧人自己躺在入口，用身体当门。这个说法可以帮助想象，但古代资料并不多，不宜当作定论。两个画面说的是同一件事：通往神的生命，只有经过他。

**2. 「出入得草吃」。** 「凡从我进来的，必然得救，并且出入得草吃。」（10:9）「出入」是旧约描写平安生活的说法（申28:6；诗121:8「你出你入，耶和华要保护你」）。得救不是被关起来，而是在牧人的看顾下自由地生活。

**3. 「得的更丰盛」。** 「盗贼来，无非要偷窃，杀害，毁坏；我来了，是要叫羊得生命，并且得的更丰盛。」（10:10）「丰盛」（*perisson*）是满溢、超过所需。这里的「生命」就是约翰福音一再说的永生。这节常被用来支持「信耶稣就会物质富足」的教导，但上下文说的是羊在牧人带领下所得的生命，而这位牧人马上要说的是「为羊舍命」。

## 深读 10:11-18「好牧人为羊舍命」

**1. 「好」是哪一种好？** 「好牧人」的「好」是 *kalos*，不是一般表示「善良」的 *agathos*。*kalos* 有「美好、高贵、真正、理想」的意思。耶稣是那位真正的、理想的、美好的牧人，所有其他的牧人都只是影子。

**2. 「为羊」。** 「好牧人为羊舍命。」（10:11）「为」是 *hyper*，本义是「为了」，在这里的上下文中（参11:50）带着代替的意思。牧人死，是为了羊不死。这是约翰福音对十字架代赎意义的清楚宣告之一。卡亚法在下一章也会不知不觉地说出同样的话：「一个人替百姓死」（11:50）。

**3. 雇工与牧人。** 雇工「看见狼来，就撇下羊逃走」（10:12），因为羊不是他自己的。差别不在能力，而在关系：牧人与羊是彼此相属的。

**4. 甘心舍命。** 「没有人夺我的命去，是我自己舍的。我有权柄舍了，也有权柄取回来。」（10:18）耶稣的死不是一场意外，也不是被逼迫的失败。在受难的记载里，约翰特别强调这一点：耶稣主动走出去迎向来捉拿他的人（18:4），在十字架上「知道各样的事已经成了」，才「将灵魂交付神了」（19:28-30）。

**5. 「合成一群」。** 「我另外有羊，不是这圈里的；我必须领他们来……并且要合成一群，归一个牧人了。」（10:16）「另外的羊」指外邦人。值得注意的是，这里说的是一「群」（*poimnē*），不是一个「圈」（*aulē*）。拉丁文武加大译本把它译成「一个圈」，英文钦定本跟着译作「one fold」，好像合一要靠同一个围墙；和合本按原文译作「合成一群」。使羊合一的，不是围墙，而是那一位牧人。

> 要点：好牧人与雇工的分别，在十字架上显明：他不是在羊面前逃走，而是在羊前面替它们死，又为它们复活。

## 深读 10:22-30 修殿节与「我与父原为一」

**1. 修殿节是什么？** 「在耶路撒冷有修殿节，是冬天的时候。」（10:22）主前167年，叙利亚王安提阿古四世亵渎了圣殿，在祭坛上献猪。三年后，犹大·马加比收复耶路撒冷，洁净圣殿，重新献殿（马加比一书4章），这就是修殿节，今天称为光明节（Hanukkah）。这个节期纪念神的殿被重新「分别为圣」。

**2. 「你叫我们犹疑不定到几时呢？」** 犹太人围着他说：「你若是基督，就明明地告诉我们。」（10:24）耶稣回答，他的话和他的工作早已说明了：「我已经告诉你们，你们不信。」问题不在信息不清楚，而在「你们不是我的羊」（10:26）。

**3. 双重的手。** 「我又赐给他们永生；他们永不灭亡，谁也不能从我手里把他们夺去。我父把羊赐给我……谁也不能从我父手里把他们夺去。」（10:28-29）羊在子的手里，也在父的手里。信徒的安全感，不建立在自己抓得多紧，而建立在谁抓住了他们。

**4. 「原为一」的那个「一」。** 「我与父原为一。」（10:30）希腊文的「一」是中性的 *hen*，不是阳性的 *heis*。如果是阳性，意思会接近「一个位格」，好像父与子是同一个人，这正是后来「形态论」的错误。中性的 *hen* 表示「一体、合一」：在旨意、工作、能力上完全合一。上下文说的是父与子同样保守羊，而犹太人的反应（再拿石头，「你是个人，反将自己当作神」，10:33）显示他们听到的不只是目标一致，而是神性的宣告。约翰要读者明白：这不只是目标一致，而是子与父同有神性（参1:1；5:18；10:38「父在我里面，我也在父里面」）。教会后来用「三位一体」的语言总结这类经文（不单单建立在这一个字上）：位格有别，本质为一。

## 深读 10:34-36「我曾说你们是神」

**1. 诗篇82篇。** 耶稣引用诗篇82:6：「我曾说：你们是神，都是至高者的儿子。」诗篇82篇里，神在众「神」中施行审判，这些「神」是谁，解经家有几种看法：以色列的审判官（受神托付执掌审判，却审判不公）、天上议会中的灵界存在、或在西奈山领受律法的以色列。耶稣所说「承受神道的人」，较合第一或第三种。

**2. 由轻推重的论证。** 耶稣的意思是：如果圣经可以称那些「承受神道的人」为「神」，而「经上的话是不能废的」（10:35），那么，「父所分别为圣、又差到世间来的」那一位，称自己为神的儿子，怎能算是亵渎？这不是说耶稣只是在那些审判官的意义上是「神」，而是说：按你们自己的圣经，你们的指控不能成立；接着他指向他所行的事作为更大的证据（10:37-38）。

**3. 「分别为圣」与修殿节。** 「父所分别为圣」的「分别为圣」（*hagiazō*），正是修殿节所庆祝的观念：殿被重新分别为圣（参马加比一书4:48）。在修殿节，耶稣说自己是父所分别为圣的那一位。马加比洁净了一座石头的殿，而那真正被分别为圣、神住在其中的殿，此刻就站在所罗门的廊下（参2:21）。

**4. 「经上的话是不能废的」。** 这句插话很简短，却显出耶稣对圣经的看法：连诗篇中一个词的用法，他都当作有约束力的神的话。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 10 has two time frames. **The good shepherd discourse** (vv. 1–21) follows directly on chapter 9: the Pharisees have just thrown the healed man out of the synagogue, and Jesus begins talking about a sheepfold, a door, thieves, hired hands and the good shepherd. **The confrontation at the Feast of Dedication** (vv. 22–42) happens two or three months later, in winter. The Jews surround him and say, "If you are the Christ, tell us plainly." Jesus says, "I and the Father are one," and again they pick up stones.

To understand this chapter, read Ezekiel 34 first. There God rebukes Israel's shepherds (its leaders) for feeding only themselves: "The weak you have not strengthened... the strayed you have not brought back" (Ezek 34:4). Then God says, "I, I myself will search for my sheep" (Ezek 34:11), and, "I will set up over them one shepherd, my servant David" (Ezek 34:23). God himself will shepherd, and a Davidic shepherd will: Jesus fulfills both promises. The Pharisees of chapter 9 are like those bad shepherds, driving a sheep out; and Jesus, as God promised, goes himself to bring it back.

## Going Deeper: 10:1–6 The Sheep Know His Voice

**1. The sheepfold and the gatekeeper.** An ancient sheepfold was often a courtyard enclosed by stone walls. At night several flocks were kept together, watched by a gatekeeper. In the morning each shepherd came to the gate and called, and his own sheep came out from the mixed flock and followed him.

**2. "He calls his own sheep by name."** The shepherd "calls his own sheep by name and leads them out... and the sheep follow him, for they know his voice" (10:3–4). It's a deeply personal relationship. The man just thrown out of the synagogue heard Jesus' voice and followed him (9:35–38).

**3. "Brings out."** The shepherd "brings out" his sheep (*ekballō*, 10:4), the same word translated "cast out" in 9:34–35, where the Pharisees "cast" the man out of the synagogue. John's readers may notice the contrast: the synagogue threw him out, but the shepherd leads his own out to pasture.

**4. They didn't understand.** "This figure of speech Jesus used with them, but they did not understand what he was saying to them" (10:6). "Figure of speech" here is *paroimia* (a symbolic saying), not the *parabolē* of the other Gospels. John has no story parables of the Synoptic kind, but he does have extended images like this one.

## Going Deeper: 10:7–10 "I am the door of the sheep"

**1. Two pictures.** Jesus first calls himself "the door" (10:7, 9), then "the shepherd" (10:11, 14). It's often said that temporary sheepfolds in the open country had no gate, so the shepherd lay across the entrance as the door. That helps the imagination, but ancient evidence for it is thin, so it shouldn't be treated as settled. Both pictures say the same thing: the way into God's life is through him alone.

**2. "Go in and out and find pasture."** "If anyone enters by me, he will be saved and will go in and out and find pasture" (10:9). "Going in and out" is an Old Testament way of describing a secure life (Deut 28:6; Ps 121:8, "The LORD will keep your going out and your coming in"). Salvation isn't being shut in; it's living freely under the shepherd's care.

**3. "Have it abundantly."** "The thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy. I came that they may have life and have it abundantly" (10:10). "Abundantly" (*perisson*) means overflowing, more than enough. The "life" here is the eternal life John keeps speaking of. This verse is often used to support teaching that faith in Jesus brings material wealth, but the context is the life sheep receive under the shepherd's leading, and this shepherd is about to speak of laying down his life for the sheep.

## Going Deeper: 10:11–18 "The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep"

**1. What kind of "good"?** "Good" here is *kalos*, not the ordinary word for moral goodness, *agathos*. *Kalos* carries the sense of beautiful, noble, genuine, ideal. Jesus is the true, ideal, beautiful shepherd; every other shepherd is a shadow.

**2. "For the sheep."** "The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep" (10:11). "For" is *hyper*, basically "on behalf of"; in this context (cf. 11:50) it carries the sense of substitution. The shepherd dies so that the sheep don't. This is one of John's clearest statements of the substitutionary meaning of the cross. In the next chapter Caiaphas will unwittingly say the same: "one man should die for the people" (11:50).

**3. Hired hand and shepherd.** The hired hand "sees the wolf coming and leaves the sheep and flees" (10:12), because the sheep aren't his. The difference isn't ability but relationship: shepherd and sheep belong to each other.

**4. Laying down his life freely.** "No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord. I have authority to lay it down, and I have authority to take it up again" (10:18). Jesus' death is neither an accident nor a forced defeat. In the passion narrative John stresses this: Jesus steps forward to meet those who come to arrest him (18:4), and on the cross, "knowing that all was now finished," he "gave up his spirit" (19:28–30).

**5. "One flock."** "And I have other sheep that are not of this fold. I must bring them also... So there will be one flock, one shepherd" (10:16). The "other sheep" are Gentiles. Notice it says one "flock" (*poimnē*), not one "fold" (*aulē*). Jerome's Latin Vulgate rendered it "one fold," and the King James followed with "one fold," as if unity depended on a single enclosure; the Chinese Union Version, like modern translations, follows the Greek with "one flock." What makes the sheep one isn't a wall but the one shepherd.

> The point: The difference between the good shepherd and the hired hand is revealed at the cross. He doesn't run from the wolf; he goes ahead of his sheep and dies in their place, and rises again for them.

## Going Deeper: 10:22–30 The Feast of Dedication and "I and the Father are one"

**1. What was the Feast of Dedication?** "At that time the Feast of Dedication took place at Jerusalem. It was winter" (10:22). In 167 BC the Syrian king Antiochus IV desecrated the temple, offering a pig on the altar. Three years later Judas Maccabeus retook Jerusalem, cleansed the temple and rededicated it (1 Maccabees 4). That is the Feast of Dedication, known today as Hanukkah. It remembers the temple being "consecrated" again.

**2. "How long will you keep us in suspense?"** The Jews surround him: "If you are the Christ, tell us plainly" (10:24). Jesus answers that his words and works have already said so: "I told you, and you do not believe." The problem isn't unclear information but that "you are not part of my flock" (10:26).

**3. Two hands.** "I give them eternal life, and they will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of my hand. My Father, who has given them to me... no one is able to snatch them out of the Father's hand" (10:28–29). The sheep are in the Son's hand and in the Father's. A believer's security doesn't rest on how tightly they hold on, but on who holds them.

**4. The "one" of "are one."** "I and the Father are one" (10:30). The Greek "one" is the neuter *hen*, not the masculine *heis*. The masculine would come close to "one person," as if Father and Son were the same individual, which is exactly the later error of modalism. The neuter *hen* means "one thing, a unity": complete oneness in will, work and power. The context is the Father and the Son keeping the sheep alike, and the Jews' reaction (picking up stones again; "you, being a man, make yourself God," 10:33) shows they heard not just shared purpose but a claim to deity. John means more than shared purpose: the Son shares the Father's deity (cf. 1:1; 5:18; 10:38, "the Father is in me and I am in the Father"). The church later summarized texts like these (not resting on this one word alone) in the language of the Trinity: distinct persons, one essence.

## Going Deeper: 10:34–36 "I said, you are gods"

**1. Psalm 82.** Jesus quotes Psalm 82:6: "I said, 'You are gods, sons of the Most High, all of you.'" In Psalm 82 God judges among the "gods." Interpreters hold several views of who they are: Israel's judges (entrusted with judgment, they judged unjustly), members of the heavenly council, or Israel receiving the law at Sinai. Jesus' phrase "to whom the word of God came" fits the first or third best.

**2. An argument from lesser to greater.** Jesus' point: if Scripture can call those "to whom the word of God came" "gods," and "Scripture cannot be broken" (10:35), then how can "him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world" be blaspheming when he calls himself the Son of God? He isn't saying he is "god" only in the judges' sense. He's saying that by your own Scripture your charge doesn't stand, and then he points to his works as greater evidence (10:37–38).

**3. "Consecrated" and the Feast of Dedication.** "Consecrated" (*hagiazō*) is the very idea the Feast of Dedication celebrated: the temple consecrated again (cf. 1 Macc 4:48). At Dedication, Jesus says he is the one the Father consecrated. The Maccabees cleansed a temple of stone, but the truly consecrated temple, where God dwells, is standing in Solomon's colonnade (see 2:21).

**4. "Scripture cannot be broken."** This brief aside shows Jesus' view of Scripture: even the use of one word in a psalm he treats as the binding word of God.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The good shepherd calls his sheep "by name." Do I believe that in the Lord's eyes I'm not a number in a crowd but someone he calls by name? Whose "voice" have I been listening to lately? "No one will snatch them out of my hand." When I feel I can't hold on to my faith, can I rest my security in the hands that hold me?
