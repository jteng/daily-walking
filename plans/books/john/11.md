## 本章脉络

第十一章是第七个、也是最大的神迹，也是神迹之书的高峰。**消息与耽延**（1-16节）：拉撒路病了，耶稣却在原地多住了两天。**耶稣与马大**（17-27节）：「复活在我，生命也在我。」**耶稣与马利亚**（28-37节）：「耶稣哭了。」**坟墓前**（38-44节）：「拉撒路出来！」**公会的决议**（45-57节）：大祭司该亚法说「一个人替百姓死」，从那日起，他们就商议要杀耶稣。

这一章有一个深刻的反讽：耶稣给了拉撒路生命，这件事却直接导致了他自己的死。叫一个人从坟墓里出来的代价，是他自己要进到坟墓里去。

## 深读 11:3-6 爱与耽延

**1. 姊妹的信息。** 「主啊，你所爱的人病了。」（11:3）她们没有说「请你快来」，只是陈述事实，相信耶稣的爱会采取行动。

**2. 「所以」。** 5-6节在原文里是这样接的（直译）：「耶稣素来爱马大和她妹子并拉撒路。**所以**，听见拉撒路病了，就在所居之地仍住了两天。」英文ESV保留了这个「所以」（So）；和合本没有译出这个连接词。原文的逻辑令人吃惊：耶稣耽延，不是因为不够爱，而恰恰是因为爱。他要给她们的，比她们所求的更大。

**3. 「为神的荣耀」。** 「这病不至于死，乃是为神的荣耀，叫神的儿子因此得荣耀。」（11:4）这和9:3的话很像。但这一次，「得荣耀」有双重的意思：一方面是神迹显出他的荣耀，另一方面，这个神迹引发了公会杀他的决定，而在约翰福音里，十字架正是他「得荣耀」的时刻（12:23）。

**4. 多马的勇气。** 门徒提醒耶稣，犹太人刚刚要拿石头打他。多马说：「我们也去和他同死吧。」（11:16）多马常被记得是「多疑的多马」（20:25），但在这里，他是第一个愿意和耶稣一同赴死的门徒。

## 深读 11:21-27「复活在我，生命也在我」

**1. 马大的信心与失望。** 「主啊，你若早在这里，我兄弟必不死。就是现在，我也知道，你无论向神求什么，神也必赐给你。」（11:21-22）失望与信心，在同一口气里说出来。马利亚后来说了一模一样的第一句话（11:32）。四天来，两姊妹大概不止一次对彼此这样说。

**2. 正确，却还不够的神学。** 耶稣说：「你兄弟必然复活。」马大回答：「我知道在末日复活的时候，他必复活。」（11:23-24）马大的神学完全正确，这是法利赛人和许多犹太人所信的末日复活。但她把复活放在遥远的将来，当作一个教义。

**3. 「我就是复活」。** 耶稣回答：「复活在我，生命也在我。」（11:25）和合本译得很美，但原文是更直接的「我是」：*egō eimi hē anastasis kai hē zōē*，「我就是复活，我就是生命」。这是七个「我是」中的第五个。耶稣把复活从一个将来的事件，变成了一个现在站在马大面前的人。

**4. 两句应许，两种死。** 「信我的人虽然死了，也必复活；凡活着信我的人必永远不死。」（11:25-26）这两句看起来矛盾，其实说的是两层：第一句说肉身的死：信他的人身体会死，但必复活。第二句说永远的死：信他的人永远不会与神隔绝，死亡对他们不再是终点。耶稣接着问：「你信这话吗？」

**5. 马大的告白。** 「主啊，是的，我信你是基督，是神的儿子，就是那要临到世界的。」（11:27）这几乎就是全书写作目的（20:31）的原话：「叫你们信耶稣是基督，是神的儿子。」在约翰福音里，最完整的信心告白之一，出自一位在悲伤中的女子。

## 深读 11:33-38「耶稣哭了」

**1. 不只是悲伤。** 「耶稣看见她哭……就心里悲叹，又甚忧愁。」（11:33）「悲叹」的希腊文 *embrimaomai*，在其他地方带有强烈的愤慨、甚至怒气的意思（可1:43；14:5）。英文ESV译作「deeply moved」，并在脚注说「或作：心里愤慨」。耶稣的情绪不只是难过，还有一种强烈的义怒。

**2. 向什么发怒？** 解经家看法不一：有人认为是对死亡本身，罪带进世界的那个仇敌；有人认为是对周围的不信；也有人认为两者都有。许多福音派解经家（如华菲德〔B. B. Warfield〕在《我们主的情感生活》中）倾向第一种：耶稣站在坟墓前，面对的是死亡这个闯入神美好创造的入侵者。38节又说「耶稣又心里悲叹」，他带着这种愤慨走到坟墓前。

**3. 两种哭。** 马利亚和犹太人的「哭」（11:33）是 *klaiō*，常指出声的、哀号的哭。耶稣「哭了」（11:35）用的是另一个字 *dakryō*，指流泪。这节在英文圣经里是最短的一节（*dakryō* 在新约里也只出现在这里），却显出道成肉身最温柔的一面：那即将叫死人复活的主，在复活之前，先为死亡流泪。他知道几分钟之后拉撒路就要出来，他仍然哭了。

> 要点：耶稣面对死亡，既哭泣，也愤慨。他不把死亡美化成「自然的一部分」，而是把它当作要被他打败的仇敌；同时他与哀哭的人一同哀哭。

## 深读 11:39-44「拉撒路出来！」

**1. 「他现在必是臭了」。** 「你们把石头挪开。」马大说：「主啊，他现在必是臭了，因为他死了已经四天了。」（11:39）约翰特别强调「四天」（11:17、39）。后来的犹太文献记载一种说法，认为人死后三天内灵魂还在身体附近徘徊，第四天才完全离开。不论当时是否普遍这样想，「四天」的意思很清楚：拉撒路确确实实死了，没有任何人能说这只是昏迷。

**2. 祷告是为了旁人。** 耶稣举目望天说：「父啊，我感谢你，因为你已经听我。我也知道你常听我，但我说这话是为周围站着的众人，叫他们信是你差了我来。」（11:41-42）他还没开口求，就已经感谢了。

**3. 一声呼叫。** 「拉撒路出来！」（11:43）这呼应5:25、28：「死人要听见神儿子的声音，听见的人就要活了……凡在坟墓里的，都要听见他的声音，就出来。」拉撒路是那个将来大复活的预演。古人说笑：幸好耶稣叫了名字，不然所有坟墓里的人都要出来了。

**4. 裹着的布与留下的布。** 「那死人就出来了，手脚裹着布，脸上包着手巾。」（11:44）拉撒路出来时，还被裹尸布包着，需要别人「解开」；他回到的是一个还会再死的生命。请对照二十章：耶稣复活后，门徒在坟墓里看见「细麻布还放在那里，又看见耶稣的裹头巾没有和细麻布放在一处，是另在一处卷着」（20:6-7）。拉撒路是被叫回到这个会死的生命；耶稣却是进入一个再也不死的生命，把死亡的裹布留在了身后。

## 深读 11:47-53「一个人替百姓死」

**1. 公会的恐惧。** 「若这样由着他，人人都要信他，罗马人也要来夺我们的地土和我们的百姓。」（11:48）「地土」原文是「地方」，很可能指圣殿。他们怕失去圣殿和民族。讽刺的是，他们杀了耶稣，约四十年后（主后70年），罗马人还是来了，毁了圣殿。

**2. 该亚法的话。** 「你们不知道什么。独不想一个人替百姓死，免得通国灭亡，就是你们的益处。」（11:49-50）该亚法说这话，是政治上的算计：牺牲一个人，保全大局。

**3. 不自觉的预言。** 约翰解释：「他这话不是出于自己，是因他本年作大祭司，所以预言耶稣将要替这一国死。」（11:51）该亚法的意思是「用他换我们的平安」，神却借着他的口说出了更深的真理：「替」（*hyper*，10:11「为羊舍命」的同一个字）百姓死，就是代替的死。「本年作大祭司」不是说大祭司一年一任（该亚法任职约十八年），而是「在那关键的一年」。

**4. 不只为一国。** 「也不但替这一国死，并要将神四散的子民都聚集归一。」（11:52）这呼应10:16「另外的羊……合成一群」。耶稣的死，要把所有属神的人，从各族各民中聚集起来。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 11 is the seventh and greatest sign and the climax of the Book of Signs. **The message and the delay** (vv. 1–16): Lazarus is ill, but Jesus stays where he is two more days. **Jesus and Martha** (vv. 17–27): "I am the resurrection and the life." **Jesus and Mary** (vv. 28–37): "Jesus wept." **At the tomb** (vv. 38–44): "Lazarus, come out." **The council's decision** (vv. 45–57): the high priest Caiaphas says that "one man should die for the people," and from that day they plan to kill Jesus.

The chapter holds a profound irony: Jesus gives Lazarus life, and that very act leads directly to his own death. The cost of calling one man out of the tomb is that he himself must go into it.

## Going Deeper: 11:3–6 Love and Delay

**1. The sisters' message.** "Lord, he whom you love is ill" (11:3). They don't say "please come quickly." They simply state the fact, trusting that Jesus' love will act.

**2. "So."** In Greek verses 5–6 run: "Now Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus. *So*, when he heard that Lazarus was ill, he stayed two days longer in the place where he was." The ESV keeps the "so"; the Chinese Union Version leaves the connective untranslated. The logic is startling: Jesus delays not because he loves too little, but precisely because he loves. He means to give them more than they asked for.

**3. "For the glory of God."** "This illness does not lead to death. It is for the glory of God, so that the Son of God may be glorified through it" (11:4). This sounds like 9:3. But this time "glorified" has a double meaning. The sign reveals his glory, and it also triggers the council's decision to kill him, and in John the cross is the moment of his glorification (12:23).

**4. Thomas's courage.** The disciples remind Jesus that the Jews were just trying to stone him. Thomas says, "Let us also go, that we may die with him" (11:16). Thomas is usually remembered as "doubting Thomas" (20:25), but here he is the first disciple willing to die with Jesus.

## Going Deeper: 11:21–27 "I am the resurrection and the life"

**1. Martha's faith and disappointment.** "Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. But even now I know that whatever you ask from God, God will give you" (11:21–22). Disappointment and faith in the same breath. Mary later says exactly the same first sentence (11:32). Over those four days the sisters had probably said it to each other more than once.

**2. Correct, but not yet enough.** Jesus says, "Your brother will rise again." Martha answers, "I know that he will rise again in the resurrection on the last day" (11:23–24). Martha's theology is entirely correct; it's the resurrection at the last day that the Pharisees and many Jews believed. But she puts resurrection in the distant future, as a doctrine.

**3. "I am the resurrection."** Jesus answers, "I am the resurrection and the life" (11:25). The Chinese Union Version renders it beautifully as "resurrection is in me, life is in me," but the Greek is the more direct "I am": *egō eimi hē anastasis kai hē zōē*. It's the fifth of the seven "I am" sayings. Jesus turns resurrection from a future event into a person standing in front of Martha.

**4. Two promises, two deaths.** "Whoever believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live, and everyone who lives and believes in me shall never die" (11:25–26). They sound contradictory but speak of two levels. The first is bodily death: believers' bodies will die, but they will rise. The second is eternal death: those who believe will never be cut off from God; death is no longer the end for them. Then Jesus asks, "Do you believe this?"

**5. Martha's confession.** "Yes, Lord; I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God, who is coming into the world" (11:27). These are almost the exact words of the book's stated purpose (20:31): "that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God." One of the fullest confessions of faith in John comes from a grieving woman.

## Going Deeper: 11:33–38 "Jesus wept"

**1. More than sadness.** "When Jesus saw her weeping... he was deeply moved in his spirit and greatly troubled" (11:33). "Deeply moved" translates *embrimaomai*, which elsewhere carries strong indignation, even anger (Mark 1:43; 14:5). The ESV has "deeply moved," with a footnote: "Or was indignant." Jesus' emotion isn't only grief but a strong, righteous anger.

**2. Anger at what?** Interpreters differ. Some say at death itself, the enemy sin brought into the world; some say at the unbelief around him; some say both. Many evangelical interpreters (B. B. Warfield, for example, in "The Emotional Life of Our Lord") lean toward the first: standing before the tomb, Jesus faces death as an intruder into God's good creation. Verse 38 says again that Jesus was "deeply moved," and he goes to the tomb carrying that indignation.

**3. Two kinds of weeping.** The "weeping" of Mary and the Jews (11:33) is *klaiō*, often loud wailing. "Jesus wept" (11:35) uses another word, *dakryō*, to shed tears. It's the shortest verse in the English Bible, yet it shows the tenderest side of the incarnation: the Lord who is about to raise the dead first weeps over death. He knew that in a few minutes Lazarus would walk out, and still he wept.

> The point: Facing death, Jesus both weeps and is indignant. He doesn't dress death up as "part of nature"; he treats it as an enemy he will defeat, while weeping with those who weep.

## Going Deeper: 11:39–44 "Lazarus, come out"

**1. "By this time there will be an odor."** "Take away the stone." Martha says, "Lord, by this time there will be an odor, for he has been dead four days" (11:39). John stresses "four days" (11:17, 39). Later Jewish writings record a belief that the soul lingered near the body for three days and left for good on the fourth. Whether or not that belief was common then, "four days" makes the point plain: Lazarus was truly dead, and no one could say it was only a coma.

**2. A prayer for those standing by.** Jesus lifts his eyes and says, "Father, I thank you that you have heard me. I knew that you always hear me, but I said this on account of the people standing around, that they may believe that you sent me" (11:41–42). He gives thanks before he has even asked.

**3. One cry.** "Lazarus, come out" (11:43). This echoes 5:25, 28: "the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live... all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out." Lazarus is a preview of the great resurrection to come. An old saying goes that it's a good thing Jesus called Lazarus by name, or every tomb would have emptied.

**4. Grave clothes worn and grave clothes left.** "The man who had died came out, his hands and feet bound with linen strips, and his face wrapped with a cloth" (11:44). Lazarus comes out still wrapped and has to be "unbound"; the life he returns to will end in death again. Compare chapter 20: after Jesus rose, the disciples saw "the linen cloths lying there, and the face cloth, which had been on Jesus' head, not lying with the linen cloths but folded up in a place by itself" (20:6–7). Lazarus was called back into a life that would end in death; Jesus entered a life that never dies, leaving the wrappings of death behind.

## Going Deeper: 11:47–53 "One man should die for the people"

**1. The council's fear.** "If we let him go on like this, everyone will believe in him, and the Romans will come and take away both our place and our nation" (11:48). "Place" most likely means the temple. They fear losing the temple and the nation. The irony is that they killed Jesus, and about forty years later (AD 70) the Romans came anyway and destroyed the temple.

**2. Caiaphas's words.** "You know nothing at all. Nor do you understand that it is better for you that one man should die for the people, not that the whole nation should perish" (11:49–50). Caiaphas means it as political calculation: sacrifice one man to save the whole.

**3. An unwitting prophecy.** John explains: "He did not say this of his own accord, but being high priest that year he prophesied that Jesus would die for the nation" (11:51). Caiaphas meant "trade him for our safety," but God spoke a deeper truth through his mouth. To die "for" (*hyper*, the same word as "lays down his life *for* the sheep" in 10:11) the people is a substitutionary death. "High priest that year" doesn't mean high priests served one-year terms (Caiaphas served about eighteen years). It means "in that decisive year."

**4. Not for one nation only.** "And not for the nation only, but also to gather into one the children of God who are scattered abroad" (11:52). This echoes 10:16's "other sheep... one flock." Jesus' death will gather all who belong to God from every people.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus delayed because he loved. Am I in a season of waiting, wondering why the Lord hasn't come yet? Can I believe that his love is in the waiting too? Martha's theology was entirely correct, but she put resurrection in the distant future. Jesus asks me today: "I am the resurrection and the life. Do you believe this?" When I face loss and death, what does that faith change?
