## 本章脉络

第十二章是神迹之书的结尾，也是通往荣耀之书的大门。**伯大尼的膏抹**（1-11节）：马利亚把极贵的香膏抹在耶稣脚上。**进入耶路撒冷**（12-19节）：众人拿着棕树枝喊「和散那」。**希腊人求见**（20-36节）：耶稣说「人子得荣耀的时候到了」，又说一粒麦子要落在地里死了。**不信与以赛亚的预言**（37-43节）。**最后的公开呼召**（44-50节）。

从2:4「我的时候还没有到」开始，「时候」这条线在整卷书里一再出现。到了12:23，耶稣终于说：「人子得荣耀的时候到了。」引出这句话的，是几个希腊人来求见他。外邦人开始来到他面前，这是他要被「举起来、吸引万人」的信号。

## 深读 12:1-8 一斤极贵的香膏

**1. 价值。** 马利亚拿着「一斤极贵的真哪哒香膏」（12:3），哪哒香膏从印度一带进口。犹大说可以卖「三百个银币」（*dēnarion*），和合本译作「三十两银子」。一个银币是一个工人一天的工资，三百个银币差不多是一年的收入。

**2. 抹脚，用头发擦。** 一般膏抹是抹在头上（马太、马可记载她也浇在耶稣头上），约翰却只记她抹耶稣的脚，又用头发去擦。在当时的文化里，一个女人在众人面前把头发放下来，是很不寻常、甚至有失体统的举动。马利亚不顾别人的眼光，把最贵重的东西、最卑微的姿态，都献给了耶稣。「屋里就满了膏的香气」，这是约翰式的细节：她的奉献，整个屋子都闻得到。

**3. 犹大的「关心穷人」。** 「这香膏为什么不卖……周济穷人呢？」（12:5）约翰揭露他的动机：「他说这话，并不是挂念穷人，乃因他是个贼，又带着钱囊，常取其中所存的。」（12:6）最冠冕堂皇的批评，有时出自最自私的心。

**4. 「为我安葬之日存留的」。** 耶稣说：「由她吧！她是为我安葬之日存留的。」（12:7）马利亚也许并不完全明白，但她的举动预先指向了耶稣的死与安葬。六天以后，他就要被安葬，而那时尼哥德慕要带来约一百斤的没药和沉香（19:39）。

## 深读 12:12-16 棕树枝、和散那与驴驹

**1. 棕树枝的意义。** 「就拿着棕树枝出去迎接他。」（12:13）棕树枝在当时是民族胜利的象征：西门·马加比收复耶路撒冷的堡垒时（主前141年），人们就是拿着棕树枝欢呼（马加比一书13章51节），后来犹太人反抗罗马时铸的钱币上也有棕树。众人迎接的，是一位他们期待能带来政治解放的王。

**2. 「和散那」。** 「和散那！奉主名来的以色列王是应当称颂的！」这出自诗篇118:25-26。「和散那」原是希伯来文的祷告「求你拯救」，后来成为欢呼的用语。众人喊着「求你拯救」，心里想的是脱离罗马；耶稣要成就的却是更深的拯救：救人脱离罪和神的审判，而他要借着替我们死来成就。「以色列王」这几个字不在诗篇里，是众人加上的。

**3. 驴驹的回答。** 耶稣「得了一个驴驹，就骑上」（12:14），应验撒迦利亚书9:9。在众人的政治期待中，耶稣用一个无声的举动回答：他是王，却是骑着驴驹、谦和的王，不是骑着战马的征服者。撒迦利亚书接着说，神「必除灭以法莲的战车」，而这位王「必向列国讲和平」（亚9:10）。

**4. 「不要惧怕」。** 约翰的引文是「锡安的民哪，不要惧怕！」而撒迦利亚书原文是「应当大大喜乐」。「不要惧怕」可能来自以赛亚书40:9或西番雅书3:16，而西番雅书3:15正说到「以色列的王耶和华在你中间」。约翰把几处经文合在一起，强调这位王来，带来的是安慰。

**5. 门徒后来才明白。** 「这些事门徒起先不明白，等到耶稣得了荣耀以后才想起这话是指着他写的。」（12:16）这和2:22一样：有些事要在十字架和复活之后，才能读懂。

## 深读 12:20-28「人子得荣耀的时候到了」

**1. 希腊人来了。** 「先生，我们愿意见耶稣。」（12:21）这些希腊人很可能是敬畏神的外邦人，上来过节礼拜。法利赛人刚刚说「世人都随从他去了」（12:19），紧接着，「世人」的代表就来了。

**2. 一粒麦子。** 耶稣没有直接回答见不见他们，而是说：「一粒麦子不落在地里死了，仍旧是一粒；若是死了，就结出许多子粒来。」（12:24）希腊人要「见」耶稣，但他们真正能与耶稣相遇，要等到他死了以后。十字架将打开一条路，让各国的人都能来到他面前。

**3. 同样的道路。** 「爱惜自己生命的，就失丧生命；在这世上恨恶自己生命的，就要保守生命到永生。」（12:25）「恨恶」是闪族语言中强烈的对比说法，意思是「两相比较，把它放在次位」，不是心理上的自我厌恶。麦子的原则不只适用于耶稣，也适用于跟随他的人。

**4. 约翰福音的客西马尼。** 「我现在心里忧愁，我说什么才好呢？父啊，救我脱离这时候；但我原是为这时候来的。父啊，愿你荣耀你的名！」（12:27-28）约翰福音没有记载客西马尼园的祷告，但这几句话有同样的挣扎和同样的顺服。耶稣是真实的人，他面对十字架时真实地忧愁；他也是顺服的子，他选择了父的荣耀。

**5. 天上的声音。** 「我已经荣耀了我的名，还要再荣耀。」（12:28）这是约翰福音里唯一一次从天上来的声音。「已经荣耀」是在耶稣的事奉和神迹中，「还要再荣耀」是在即将来临的十字架和复活中。

> 要点：一粒麦子的比喻，是整卷约翰福音对十字架的总结：死亡不是失败，而是结出许多子粒的唯一道路。

## 深读 12:31-33「我若从地上被举起来」

**1. 两个「现在」。** 「现在这世界受审判，这世界的王要被赶出去。」（12:31）表面上是世界审判了耶稣，其实是世界在十字架前受了审判；而罪人当受的刑罚，落在了那位替我们被举起来的人身上。「这世界的王」指撒但（14:30；16:11）。在十字架上，看起来得胜的黑暗权势，其实被赶了出去。

**2. 第三次「举起来」。** 「我若从地上被举起来，就要吸引万人来归我。」（12:32）约翰立刻解释：「耶稣这话原是指着自己将要怎样死说的。」（12:33）这是三次「举起来」（3:14；8:28）的最后一次，也最清楚：被钉在十字架上高高举起，同时就是被高举得荣耀。

**3. 「万人」。** （有些抄本作「万物」。）在希腊人来求见的上下文中，「万人」最自然的意思是「各样的人」，不只犹太人，也包括外邦人。这呼应10:16和11:52：要把神四散的子民聚集归一。

## 深读 12:37-43「以赛亚因为看见他的荣耀」

**1. 神迹之书的总结。** 「他虽然在他们面前行了许多神迹，他们还是不信他。」（12:37）约翰在神迹之书的结尾，用两处以赛亚书来解释这个令人心碎的事实：以赛亚书53:1「我们所传的有谁信呢？」以及以赛亚书6:10（约翰引作「主叫他们瞎了眼，硬了心」，12:40）。约翰的引法明白指出，使人心刚硬的是神。

**2. 「他们所以不能信」。** 这句话（12:39）很沉重。约翰同时说了两件事：他们「不信」（12:37，是他们自己的拒绝），他们「不能信」（12:39，神的审判使他们的心刚硬）。圣经常常把这两者并列：人拒绝光，神就任凭他们停在黑暗里。约翰并不调和这两面：神在审判中主权地使人心刚硬（参6:44），而人仍要为拒绝光完全负责。没有一个真心要来的人会被拒之门外（6:37）。

**3. 以赛亚看见了谁的荣耀？** 「以赛亚因为看见他的荣耀，就指着他说这话。」（12:41）以赛亚书6章，以赛亚看见的是「主坐在高高的宝座上」，撒拉弗喊着「圣哉！圣哉！圣哉！万军之耶和华」。约翰说，以赛亚所看见的，是耶稣的荣耀。这是约翰福音对耶稣神性最直接的宣告之一。许多学者还指出，以赛亚书6:1形容主「高高」在宝座上，与52:13说受苦的仆人「被高举上升」，用的是相近的字。约翰似乎是把两处连在一起读：宝座上的主，和被举起来的仆人，是同一位。

**4. 暗中的信徒。** 「虽然如此，官长中却有好些信他的，只因法利赛人的缘故，就不承认……这是因他们爱人的荣耀过于爱神的荣耀。」（12:42-43）这呼应5:44。约翰对暗中的信徒没有简单地定罪，但他清楚指出问题所在：怕失去人的称赞，也就是我们常说的「面子」。

## 默想

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 12 closes the Book of Signs and opens the door to the Book of Glory. **The anointing at Bethany** (vv. 1–11): Mary pours costly ointment on Jesus' feet. **The entry into Jerusalem** (vv. 12–19): the crowd waves palm branches and shouts "Hosanna." **The Greeks seek Jesus** (vv. 20–36): Jesus says, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified," and speaks of a grain of wheat falling into the earth and dying. **Unbelief and Isaiah's prophecy** (vv. 37–43). **The final public appeal** (vv. 44–50).

From "My hour has not yet come" in 2:4, the thread of the "hour" has run through the whole book. At 12:23 Jesus finally says, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified." What prompts it is a few Greeks asking to see him. Gentiles are beginning to come to him, the signal that he is about to be "lifted up" and "draw all people."

## Going Deeper: 12:1–8 A Pound of Costly Ointment

**1. The value.** Mary takes "a pound of expensive ointment made from pure nard" (12:3), imported from around India. Judas says it could have been sold for "three hundred denarii" (the Chinese Union Version renders this "thirty taels of silver"). A denarius was a laborer's daily wage, so three hundred was about a year's income.

**2. Anointing his feet and wiping them with her hair.** Anointing was usually done on the head (Matthew and Mark say she poured it on Jesus' head), but John records only that she anointed his feet and wiped them with her hair. In that culture, a woman letting down her hair in public was highly unusual, even improper. Mary ignored what others thought and gave Jesus her most precious possession in the humblest posture. "The house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume": a very Johannine detail. The whole house could smell her devotion.

**3. Judas's "concern for the poor."** "Why was this ointment not sold for three hundred denarii and given to the poor?" (12:5). John exposes his motive: "He said this, not because he cared about the poor, but because he was a thief, and having charge of the moneybag he used to help himself to what was put into it" (12:6). The most high-minded criticism sometimes comes from the most selfish heart.

**4. "For the day of my burial."** Jesus says, "Leave her alone, so that she may keep it for the day of my burial" (12:7). Mary may not have fully understood, but her act became a foreshadowing of Jesus' death. Six days later he would be buried, and Nicodemus would bring about seventy-five pounds of myrrh and aloes (19:39).

## Going Deeper: 12:12–16 Palm Branches, Hosanna and a Donkey's Colt

**1. What palm branches meant.** "So they took branches of palm trees and went out to meet him" (12:13). Palm branches were then a symbol of national victory. When Simon Maccabeus took the Jerusalem citadel (141 BC), people celebrated with palm branches (1 Maccabees 13:51), and coins minted later during the Jewish revolts against Rome bore palm trees. The crowd was welcoming a king they hoped would bring political liberation.

**2. "Hosanna."** "Hosanna! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, even the King of Israel!" comes from Psalm 118:25–26. "Hosanna" was originally a Hebrew prayer, "Save, please!", which became a shout of acclaim. The crowd cried "save us," thinking of rescue from Rome; Jesus was about to accomplish a far deeper salvation: rescue from sin and God's judgment, won by dying in our place. "Even the King of Israel" isn't in the psalm; it's the crowd's addition.

**3. The donkey's answer.** Jesus "found a young donkey and sat on it" (12:14), fulfilling Zechariah 9:9. Into the crowd's political hopes Jesus answers with a silent act: he is a king, but a humble one on a donkey's colt, not a conqueror on a warhorse. Zechariah goes on: God says, "I will cut off the chariot from Ephraim," and this king "shall speak peace to the nations" (Zech 9:10).

**4. "Fear not."** John's quotation reads "Fear not, daughter of Zion," while Zechariah has "Rejoice greatly." "Fear not" may come from Isaiah 40:9 or Zephaniah 3:16, and Zephaniah 3:15 has just said, "The King of Israel, the LORD, is in your midst." John combines several texts to stress that this king comes bringing comfort.

**5. The disciples understood later.** "His disciples did not understand these things at first, but when Jesus was glorified, then they remembered that these things had been written about him" (12:16). As in 2:22, some things can only be understood after the cross and resurrection.

## Going Deeper: 12:20–28 "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified"

**1. The Greeks arrive.** "Sir, we wish to see Jesus" (12:21). These Greeks were probably God-fearing Gentiles who had come up to worship at the feast. The Pharisees had just said, "Look, the world has gone after him" (12:19), and immediately representatives of "the world" appear.

**2. A grain of wheat.** Jesus doesn't directly say whether he will see them. He says, "unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit" (12:24). The Greeks want to "see" Jesus, but their real meeting with him must wait until after he dies. The cross will open the way for people of every nation to come to him.

**3. The same road.** "Whoever loves his life loses it, and whoever hates his life in this world will keep it for eternal life" (12:25). "Hate" is a strong Semitic idiom of contrast, meaning "put second by comparison," not psychological self-loathing. The principle of the grain of wheat applies not only to Jesus but to those who follow him.

**4. John's Gethsemane.** "Now is my soul troubled. And what shall I say? 'Father, save me from this hour'? But for this purpose I have come to this hour. Father, glorify your name" (12:27–28). John doesn't record the prayer in Gethsemane, but these words carry the same struggle and the same surrender. Jesus is truly human; facing the cross, he is truly troubled. He is also the obedient Son, and he chooses the Father's glory.

**5. A voice from heaven.** "I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again" (12:28). This is the only voice from heaven in John. "Have glorified" refers to Jesus' ministry and signs; "will glorify again" to the coming cross and resurrection.

> The point: The parable of the grain of wheat sums up John's whole view of the cross: death isn't defeat but the only way to bear much fruit.

## Going Deeper: 12:31–33 "When I am lifted up from the earth"

**1. Two "nows."** "Now is the judgment of this world; now will the ruler of this world be cast out" (12:31). The cross looks like the world judging Jesus; in fact the world is judged at the cross, and the penalty sinners deserved falls on the one lifted up in our place. "The ruler of this world" is Satan (14:30; 16:11). At the cross, the powers of darkness that seem to win are actually cast out.

**2. The third "lifted up."** "And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself" (12:32). John explains at once: "He said this to show by what kind of death he was going to die" (12:33). This is the last and clearest of the three "lifted up" sayings (3:14; 8:28): raised high on the cross, he is at the same moment exalted in glory.

**3. "All people."** (Some manuscripts read "all things.") With the Greeks just arrived, "all people" most naturally means people of every kind, Gentiles as well as Jews. It echoes 10:16 and 11:52: gathering into one the scattered children of God.

## Going Deeper: 12:37–43 "Isaiah saw his glory"

**1. The Book of Signs summed up.** "Though he had done so many signs before them, they still did not believe in him" (12:37). At the end of the Book of Signs John explains this heartbreaking fact with two passages from Isaiah: Isaiah 53:1, "Lord, who has believed what he heard from us?" (as John quotes it, 12:38), and Isaiah 6:10, which John renders, "He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart" (12:40). John's form makes explicit that God is the one acting in the hardening.

**2. "Therefore they could not believe."** This sentence (12:39) is heavy. John says two things at once: they "did not believe" (12:37, their own refusal), and they "could not believe" (12:39, God's judgment hardening their hearts). Scripture often sets these side by side: when people reject the light, God gives them over to the darkness. John does not resolve the two: God sovereignly hardens in judgment (cf. 6:44), and people remain fully responsible for rejecting the light. No one who truly wants to come is turned away (6:37).

**3. Whose glory did Isaiah see?** "Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory and spoke of him" (12:41). In Isaiah 6 the prophet saw "the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up," while the seraphim cried, "Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts." John says that what Isaiah saw was Jesus' glory. It's one of John's most direct statements of Jesus' deity. Many scholars also note that Isaiah 6:1 describes the Lord as "high and lifted up," and Isaiah 52:13 says the suffering servant "shall be high and lifted up," with closely matching words. John seems to read the two together: the Lord on the throne and the servant lifted up are the same person.

**4. Secret believers.** "Nevertheless, many even of the authorities believed in him, but for fear of the Pharisees they did not confess it... for they loved the glory that comes from man more than the glory that comes from God" (12:42–43). This echoes 5:44. John doesn't simply condemn secret believers, but he names the problem plainly: fear of losing people's approval.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus himself is the grain of wheat that fell into the earth and died, so that I could become part of the harvest. Mary's perfume was the natural response of someone greatly loved. As I think of what he gave for me, is there something I've been clutching that begins to loosen? When I fear what people think, is it because I haven't yet tasted enough of the glory that comes from God?
