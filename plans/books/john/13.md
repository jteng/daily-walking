## 本章脉络

第十三章开始了「荣耀之书」。镜头从公开的群众转到楼上的房间，只剩下耶稣和门徒。**洗脚**（1-20节）：耶稣脱了衣服，拿手巾束腰，洗门徒的脚。**预言被卖**（21-30节）：耶稣把蘸过的饼递给犹大，犹大出去了，「那时候是夜间了」。**新命令**（31-35节）：「我怎样爱你们，你们也要怎样相爱。」**预言彼得不认主**（36-38节）。

从第十三章到第十七章，是约翰福音独有的「楼上讲论」，占了全书约四分之一。前十二章记载了大约三年的事奉，接下来的五章，只记载了一个晚上。约翰要我们放慢脚步，听耶稣在受难前对属他的人说的最后的话。

## 深读 13:1「就爱他们到底」

**1. 一节总纲。** 这一节是整个荣耀之书的标题：「逾越节以前，耶稣知道自己离世归父的时候到了。他既然爱世间属自己的人，就爱他们到底。」逾越节、时候、离世归父、爱，后面八章都在展开这几个词。

**2. 「到底」的两层意思。** 「到底」的希腊文是 *eis telos*，可以是「到最后」（时间上，一直爱到生命的尽头），也可以是「到极致」（程度上，爱到最完全的地步）。约翰很可能两层都要。这个 *telos*（终点、完成）在十字架上会再次出现：耶稣最后说「成了」（*tetelestai*，19:30），是同一个字根。他爱他们到底，而在十字架上，这爱「成了」。

**3. 「属自己的人」。** 1:11说「他到自己的地方来，自己的人倒不接待他」，那里的「自己的人」指拒绝他的以色列。在这里，「属自己的人」是接待他的门徒。神迹之书以被拒绝开始，荣耀之书以爱属自己的人开始。

## 深读 13:3-11 脱了衣服，拿手巾束腰

**1. 他知道自己是谁。** 「耶稣知道父已将万有交在他手里，且知道自己是从神出来的，又要归到神那里去，就离席站起来……」（13:3-4）约翰特意先写耶稣的身份，再写他的举动。洗脚不是因为他忘了自己是谁，而恰恰是因为他知道。真正的谦卑不是自我贬低，而是有安全感的人甘心服事。

**2. 奴仆的工作。** 在当时，洗客人的脚是奴仆的工作，有些犹太文献甚至认为这工作太卑微，不该要求犹太奴仆去做。门徒中没有一个人主动去做，路加福音记载他们那晚还在争论谁为大（路22:24）。

**3. 一个演出来的比喻。** 「脱了衣服」的「脱」（*tithēsin*），正是10:11「好牧人为羊舍命」的「舍」；洗完以后「穿上衣服」的「穿上」（*elaben*，13:12），正是10:17-18「舍去，好再取回来」的「取」。约翰的读者会注意到：耶稣放下外衣、俯身洗脚、又取回外衣，就是一个演出来的十字架：放下生命、为人洁净、再取回生命。

**4. 「你就与我无分了」。** 彼得说：「你永不可洗我的脚！」耶稣说：「我若不洗你，你就与我无分了。」（13:8）这句话显出洗脚不只是谦卑的榜样，更指向耶稣为人洁净罪的工作。不接受他的洁净，就与他无分。

**5. 洗澡与洗脚。** 彼得又转向另一个极端：「不但我的脚，连手和头也要洗。」耶稣说：「凡洗过澡的人，只要把脚一洗，全身就干净了。」（13:9-10）许多解经家这样理解：信主时，人一次全然被洁净（洗澡）；在每天的行走中，仍需要不断地被洁净（洗脚），如约翰一书1:9所说的认罪与赦免。「你们是干净的，然而不都是干净的」，指的是犹大。

## 深读 13:12-17「你们也当彼此洗脚」

**1. 榜样。** 「我是你们的主，你们的夫子，尚且洗你们的脚，你们也当彼此洗脚。我给你们作了榜样。」（13:14-15）「榜样」（*hypodeigma*）是一个可以照着做的模式。

**2. 要照字面实行吗？** 有些教会把洗脚当作一个礼仪来遵守，这在教会历史上也有先例。多数教会则认为，耶稣要的是洗脚所代表的那种服事：甘心做最卑微的事来服事弟兄姊妹。两种做法都可以，但若只守礼仪而没有那份心，就失去了重点。

**3. 「若是去行就有福了」。** 「你们既知道这事，若是去行就有福了。」（13:17）知道和去做之间有一段距离，福气在去做的那一边。

## 深读 13:21-30 一点饼与「那时候是夜间了」

**1. 耶稣所爱的门徒。** 「有一个门徒，是耶稣所爱的，侧身挨近耶稣的怀里。」（13:23）这是「耶稣所爱的那门徒」第一次出现。当时吃饭是侧躺在矮桌旁，用左肘支撑。「怀里」（*kolpos*）这个字在1:18也出现过：子「在父怀里」。正如子在父怀里，所以能将父表明出来；这位门徒在耶稣怀里，所以能为耶稣作见证（21:24）。

**2. 蘸过的饼。** 「我蘸一点饼给谁，就是谁。」（13:26）在当时的筵席上，主人亲手把一块蘸过的饼递给某位客人，常是一种尊荣的表示。耶稣在最后一刻，仍然向犹大伸出了友谊的手。诗篇41:9的预言在这里应验：「同我吃饭的人用脚踢我。」（13:18）

**3. 撒但入了他的心。** 「他吃了以后，撒但就入了他的心。」（13:27）约翰同时说了两件事：魔鬼把卖耶稣的意思放在犹大心里（13:2），犹大自己是个贼（12:6）。人的罪与撒但的工作交织在一起，而犹大始终要为自己的选择负责。

**4. 「那时候是夜间了」。** 「犹大受了那点饼，立刻就出去。那时候是夜间了。」（13:30）这是约翰福音里最短、最沉重的一句话之一。它是一个时间记录，也是一个属灵的描写：犹大离开了世界的光（8:12），走进了黑夜。

## 深读 13:34-35「我怎样爱你们，你们也要怎样相爱」

**1. 新在哪里？** 「爱人如己」在利未记19:18就有了，为什么说是「新命令」？新的是标准：「我怎样爱你们，你们也要怎样相爱。」旧的标准是「如己」，新的标准是「如基督」。而基督刚刚用洗脚、马上要用十字架，显明了这爱的样式。

**2. 时机。** 耶稣说这话，是在犹大出去之后、彼得不认主的预言之前。新命令夹在背叛和失败中间，这不是巧合：门徒要彼此相爱的群体，正是一群会失败、需要被饶恕的人。

**3. 世人认出门徒的记号。** 「你们若有彼此相爱的心，众人因此就认出你们是我的门徒了。」（13:35）耶稣没有说「你们若有正确的教义」或「你们若有大能的神迹」，而是「彼此相爱」。教会内部的爱，本身就是向世界的见证。

> 要点：新命令的「新」，不在于要我们爱，而在于爱的尺度：像那位脱下外衣、跪下来洗我们脚、又为我们舍命的主那样去爱。

## 深读 13:36-38「我愿意为你舍命！」

彼得说：「主啊，我为什么现在不能跟你去？我愿意为你舍命！」（13:37）彼得用的，正是好牧人的话：「舍命」（*tēn psychēn ... thēsō*），和10:11一模一样。他真心想做好牧人为羊所做的事，但他还不明白：必须先是好牧人为他舍命，他才能为好牧人舍命。耶稣回答：「鸡叫以先，你要三次不认我。」（13:38）这个故事没有在这里结束：在21:15-19，复活的主会三次问彼得「你爱我吗」，并告诉他，将来他真的会为主舍命。

## 默想

**默想：** 耶稣「知道自己是谁」，所以能放下外衣去洗脚。我的服事，是出于安全感，还是出于想被人看见？有没有一个人，是我需要「洗他的脚」，而我一直不愿意的？彼得想为主舍命，却先要经历失败和被饶恕。我是否也需要先接受主为我所做的，才有力量为他去做？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 13 opens the Book of Glory. The camera moves from the public crowds to an upper room, with only Jesus and his disciples. **The foot washing** (vv. 1–20): Jesus lays aside his garments, ties a towel around his waist and washes the disciples' feet. **The betrayal foretold** (vv. 21–30): Jesus hands Judas a piece of dipped bread, Judas goes out, "and it was night." **The new commandment** (vv. 31–35): "just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another." **Peter's denial foretold** (vv. 36–38).

Chapters 13–17 are the Upper Room Discourse, found only in John and making up about a quarter of the book. The first twelve chapters covered about three years of ministry; the next five cover a single evening. John wants us to slow down and listen to Jesus' last words to his own before his suffering.

## Going Deeper: 13:1 "He loved them to the end"

**1. A heading for everything that follows.** This verse is the title of the whole Book of Glory: "Now before the Feast of the Passover, when Jesus knew that his hour had come to depart out of this world to the Father, having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end." Passover, hour, departing to the Father, love: the next eight chapters unfold these words.

**2. Two senses of "to the end."** "To the end" is *eis telos*, which can mean "to the last" (in time, loving them until his life's end) or "to the uttermost" (in degree, loving them completely). John likely means both. This *telos* (end, completion) reappears at the cross: Jesus' last word, "It is finished" (*tetelestai*, 19:30), shares the root. He loved them to the end, and at the cross that love was "finished."

**3. "His own."** In 1:11, "He came to his own, and his own people did not receive him," "his own" meant the Israel that rejected him. Here "his own" are the disciples who received him. The Book of Signs began with rejection; the Book of Glory begins with love for his own.

## Going Deeper: 13:3–11 He Laid Aside His Garments and Tied a Towel Around His Waist

**1. He knew who he was.** "Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he had come from God and was going back to God, rose from supper..." (13:3–4). John deliberately describes Jesus' identity before his action. He washes feet not because he forgot who he was, but precisely because he knew. True humility isn't putting yourself down; it's someone secure choosing to serve.

**2. A slave's job.** Washing guests' feet was a slave's task; some Jewish writings even considered it too menial to require of a Jewish slave. None of the disciples volunteered. Luke records that they were still arguing that night about which of them was the greatest (Luke 22:24).

**3. An acted parable.** "Laid aside" his garments (*tithēsin*) is the very verb of 10:11, "lays down his life for the sheep." Afterward he "put on" his garments (*elaben*, 13:12), the verb of 10:17–18, "lay it down that I may take it up again." John's readers would notice: Jesus laying aside his robe, stooping to wash, then taking it up again is an acted-out cross: laying down his life, cleansing others, taking his life up again.

**4. "You have no share with me."** Peter says, "You shall never wash my feet." Jesus answers, "If I do not wash you, you have no share with me" (13:8). This shows the foot washing is more than an example of humility; it points to Jesus' work of cleansing from sin. Refuse his cleansing, and you have no part in him.

**5. Bathing and foot washing.** Peter swings to the other extreme: "Lord, not my feet only but also my hands and my head!" Jesus says, "The one who has bathed does not need to wash, except for his feet, but is completely clean" (13:9–10). Many interpreters take it this way: when we first believe, we are cleansed once and completely (the bath); in our daily walk we still need ongoing cleansing (the feet), the confession and forgiveness of 1 John 1:9. "You are clean, but not every one of you" refers to Judas.

## Going Deeper: 13:12–17 "You also ought to wash one another's feet"

**1. An example.** "If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example" (13:14–15). "Example" (*hypodeigma*) is a pattern to follow.

**2. Should it be done literally?** Some churches keep foot washing as a rite, with precedent in church history. Most churches understand Jesus to want the kind of service it represents: willingly doing the lowliest tasks for brothers and sisters. Either practice can be right, but keeping the rite without the heart misses the point.

**3. "Blessed are you if you do them."** "If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them" (13:17). There's a distance between knowing and doing, and the blessing lies on the doing side.

## Going Deeper: 13:21–30 A Morsel of Bread and "It Was Night"

**1. The disciple whom Jesus loved.** "One of his disciples, whom Jesus loved, was reclining at table close to Jesus" (13:23), literally "in Jesus' bosom." This is the first appearance of "the disciple whom Jesus loved." Diners then reclined at low tables, leaning on the left elbow. "Bosom" (*kolpos*) also appears in 1:18: the Son is "at the Father's side," literally "in the bosom of the Father." As the Son, close to the Father, makes the Father known, so this disciple, close to Jesus, bears witness to Jesus (21:24).

**2. The dipped morsel.** "It is he to whom I will give this morsel of bread when I have dipped it" (13:26). At a banquet then, a host handing a guest a dipped morsel himself was often a mark of honor. To the last moment, Jesus held out the hand of friendship to Judas. Psalm 41:9 is fulfilled: "He who ate my bread has lifted his heel against me" (13:18).

**3. Satan entered him.** "Then after he had taken the morsel, Satan entered into him" (13:27). John says two things together: the devil put the betrayal into Judas's heart (13:2), and Judas himself was a thief (12:6). Human sin and Satan's work are intertwined, and Judas remains responsible for his choice.

**4. "And it was night."** "So, after receiving the morsel of bread, he immediately went out. And it was night" (13:30). It's one of the shortest and heaviest sentences in John. It records the time, and it describes a spiritual reality: Judas left the light of the world (8:12) and walked into the night.

## Going Deeper: 13:34–35 "Just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another"

**1. What's new?** "Love your neighbor as yourself" was already in Leviticus 19:18, so why call this a "new commandment"? What's new is the standard: "just as I have loved you." The old standard was "as yourself"; the new standard is "as Christ." And Christ has just shown what that love looks like with the foot washing, and is about to show it with the cross.

**2. The timing.** Jesus says this after Judas goes out and before he foretells Peter's denial. The new commandment is set between betrayal and failure, and that's no accident: the community called to love one another is a community of people who fail and need forgiveness.

**3. The mark the world will recognize.** "By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another" (13:35). Jesus didn't say "if you have correct doctrine" or "if you do mighty miracles," but "if you have love for one another." Love within the church is itself a witness to the world.

> The point: The "new" in the new commandment isn't that we must love but the measure of love: to love like the Lord who laid aside his robe, knelt to wash our feet and laid down his life for us.

## Going Deeper: 13:36–38 "I will lay down my life for you"

Peter says, "Lord, why can I not follow you now? I will lay down my life for you" (13:37). Peter uses the good shepherd's very words: "lay down my life" (*tēn psychēn ... thēsō*), exactly as in 10:11. He sincerely wants to do what the good shepherd does for the sheep, but he doesn't yet understand that the good shepherd must first lay down his life for him before he can lay down his life for the shepherd. Jesus answers, "the rooster will not crow till you have denied me three times" (13:38). The story doesn't end here: in 21:15–19 the risen Lord will ask Peter three times, "Do you love me?" and tell him that one day he really will lay down his life for him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus "knew who he was," so he could lay aside his robe and wash feet. Does my service come from security, or from wanting to be seen? Is there someone whose "feet I need to wash," and I've been unwilling? Peter wanted to die for the Lord but first had to go through failure and forgiveness. Do I also need first to receive what the Lord has done for me before I have strength to do anything for him?
