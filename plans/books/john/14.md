## 本章脉络

第十四章是楼上讲论的第一部分。门徒刚刚听到三个令人不安的消息：耶稣要离开（13:33），其中一个人要出卖他（13:21），彼得要三次不认他（13:38）。耶稣的第一句话就是：「你们心里不要忧愁。」**父家里的住处**（1-4节）。**多马的问题**（5-7节）：「我就是道路、真理、生命。」**腓力的问题**（8-14节）：「人看见了我，就是看见了父。」**另一位保惠师**（15-26节）：圣灵要来，父与子要与爱主的人同住。**平安**（27-31节）：「我留下平安给你们。」

这一章有一个特色：门徒不断打断耶稣发问。多马（14:5）、腓力（14:8）、犹大（不是加略人，14:22）。他们的问题都很诚实，也都显出他们还不明白。耶稣没有责备他们发问，而是借着每一个问题，把他们带得更深。

## 深读 14:1-3「在我父的家里有许多住处」

**1. 「信」是命令还是陈述？** 「你们信神，也当信我。」（14:1）希腊文的两个「信」，形式都可以是直述（你们信），也可以是命令（你们要信）。所以可以译作「你们信神，也信我」，也可以是「要信神，也要信我」。和合本把第一个当直述、第二个当命令。不论哪一种，耶稣都把对他的信，放在和对神的信同等的位置上。

**2. 「住处」不是豪宅。** 英文钦定本译作「many mansions」，这来自拉丁文 *mansiones*，原意是「停留的地方」，不是今天说的「豪宅」。希腊文 *monai* 来自约翰最爱用的动词 *menō*（住、常在）。耶稣的重点不是房子有多大，而是有地方让我们**住下来**、与他同在。

**3. 同一个字在23节。** *monē* 这个字在新约里只出现两次，另一次就在14:23：「我们要到他那里去，与他同**住**。」第2节说的是将来我们去到父家里住；第23节说的是现在父与子来到我们这里住。约翰福音的盼望总是这样：将来的应许，今天就开始了。

**4. 「我必再来」。** 「我若去为你们预备了地方，就必再来接你们到我那里去，我在哪里，叫你们也在那里。」（14:3）这个「再来」最直接是指他在末日的再来；有些解经家认为也包括复活后的显现和圣灵的降临。天堂最美的地方不是住处本身，而是「我在哪里，叫你们也在那里」。

## 深读 14:5-7「我就是道路、真理、生命」

**1. 多马的诚实。** 「主啊，我们不知道你往哪里去，怎么知道那条路呢？」（14:5）多马要一张地图，耶稣给他一个人。

**2. 三个词的关系。** 「我就是道路、真理、生命；若不藉着我，没有人能到父那里去。」（14:6）这是七个「我是」中的第六个。三个词不是并列的三样东西，许多解经家认为「道路」是主词，「真理」和「生命」说明他为什么是道路：他是道路，因为他就是真理（启示父），他就是生命（赐人永生）。上下文的问题是「那条路」，而后半句说的也是「到父那里去」。他之所以是道路，是因为他自己要经过十字架、担当我们的罪，到父那里去（14:2-4、28），为我们开了路。

**3. 排他性的宣告。** 「若不藉着我，没有人能到父那里去。」这是新约中最排他的宣告之一，在一个强调多元的时代特别刺耳。但这不是傲慢，而是事实陈述：如果耶稣真是从父那里来、要回到父那里去的那一位（13:3），那么他就是唯一认识路、也唯一是路的。使徒行传4:12说出同样的信息：「除他以外，别无拯救。」

**4. 认识与看见。** 「你们若认识我，也就认识我的父。从今以后，你们认识他，并且已经看见他。」（14:7）这回到序言：「从来没有人看见神，只有在父怀里的独生子将他表明出来。」（1:18）

## 深读 14:8-14「人看见了我，就是看见了父」

**1. 腓力的请求。** 「求主将父显给我们看，我们就知足了。」（14:8）这呼应摩西的请求（出33:18「求你显出你的荣耀给我看」）。人心最深处，想要看见神。

**2. 「这样长久」。** 耶稣的回答带着叹息：「腓力，我与你们同在这样长久，你还不认识我吗？人看见了我，就是看见了父。」（14:9）三年来，腓力天天看着的那张脸，就是神的脸。

**3. 不是形态论。** 这句话不是说耶稣就是父，好像同一位神换了不同的面具出现。耶稣马上说：「我在父里面，父在我里面。」（14:10）两位彼此内住，不是同一位。教会后来用「互相内住」来描述这种关系：父与子有别，却彼此完全住在对方里面，所以看见子，就是看见父。

**4. 「比这更大的事」。** 「我所做的事，信我的人也要做，并且要做比这更大的事，因为我往父那里去。」（14:12）门徒怎么可能做比耶稣更大的事？关键在最后一句：「因为我往父那里去。」耶稣复活升天、差下圣灵以后，福音从一个小地方传到全世界，五旬节一天就有三千人信主。「更大」是指范围和救恩历史的阶段，不一定是更惊人的神迹。

**5. 「奉我的名」。** 「你们奉我的名无论求什么，我必成就，叫父因儿子得荣耀。」（14:13）「奉我的名」不是一句结尾的咒语，而是按着耶稣的身份和心意来求。所以这应许有一个目的：「叫父因儿子得荣耀。」

## 深读 14:15-26 另一位保惠师

**1. 「另外一位」。** 「我要求父，父就另外赐给你们一位保惠师。」（14:16）「另外」是 *allos*，常有「同一类的另一位」的意思（虽然这个区分不是绝对的）。圣灵是和耶稣同样的那种帮助者。耶稣在门徒身边做的事，圣灵要继续做，而且要「在你们里面」（14:17）。

**2. 「保惠师」是什么？** 希腊文 *paraklētos* 的原意是「被召到身旁的人」，在法庭上是辩护人、代言人。英文译本有「Comforter」「Counselor」「Advocate」「Helper」等不同译法，各抓住一面。中文译经传统（和合本沿用）译作「保惠师」，常被理解为「保护、施恩惠、教导的老师」，把几种意思合在一起。

**3. 耶稣是第一位保惠师。** 约翰一书2:1说：「若有人犯罪，在父那里我们有一位中保，就是那义者耶稣基督。」这里的「中保」就是 *paraklētos*。耶稣在世时是门徒身边的帮助者（14:25），升天后在父面前为我们辩护；同一个称号也用在他身上。圣灵在我们里面为耶稣作见证，所以圣灵是「另外一位」保惠师。

**4. 「我不撇下你们为孤儿」。** 「我不撇下你们为孤儿，我必到你们这里来。」（14:18）门徒怕被丢下。这首先可能指复活后的显现（14:19「你们却看见我」），也延续到圣灵的内住。「我们要到他那里去，与他同住」（14:23），父、子、圣灵，都在这一章里应许要与信徒同在。

**5. 圣灵的教导。** 「他要将一切的事指教你们，并且要叫你们想起我对你们所说的一切话。」（14:26）这首先是对使徒的应许：圣灵会帮助他们想起并明白耶稣的话，这正是新约圣经得以写成的基础。约翰福音本身，就是这个应许的成就之一（参2:22；12:16）。

## 深读 14:27-31「我留下平安给你们」

**1. 临别的赠礼。** 「我留下平安给你们；我将我的平安赐给你们。」（14:27）犹太人见面和道别都说「平安」（*shalom*）。耶稣在离别前说的，不是一句客套话，而是一份遗产。

**2. 不像世人所赐的。** 「我所赐的，不像世人所赐的。」世人的平安取决于环境：没有冲突、没有危险。耶稣的平安在他即将被出卖、被钉十字架的那个晚上说出，它不取决于环境，而取决于他自己。

**3. 「父是比我大的」。** 「你们若爱我，因我到父那里去，就必喜乐，因为父是比我大的。」（14:28）这节经文在教会历史上曾被用来否认子的神性。但在约翰福音的整体中（1:1；5:18；10:30；20:28），这不可能是说子在本质上低于父。正统的解释主要有两种：奥古斯丁等人认为，这是就道成肉身、取了奴仆形像的子而说的；另有教父认为，这是指父是子的源头，就位格的次序而言。两种解释都不否认子与父同等是神。

**4. 「起来，我们走吧！」** 「起来，我们走吧！」（14:31）然而接下来还有三章讲论。有学者认为讲论是在前往客西马尼园的路上继续的（第十五章的葡萄树比喻也许就在经过葡萄园时说的），也有学者有其他看法。不论如何，这句话提醒我们：耶稣是主动地走向十字架的。

## 默想

**默想：** 耶稣在自己最艰难的夜晚，还在对门徒说「你们心里不要忧愁」。今天什么事让我心里忧愁？我能不能把它带到这位说「我就是道路、真理、生命」的主面前？像多马和腓力一样，我心里有什么诚实的问题，可以直接问他？耶稣说「我不撇下你们为孤儿」，在我感到孤单的时候，我是否记得圣灵就住在我里面？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 14 is the first part of the Upper Room Discourse. The disciples have just heard three troubling things: Jesus is leaving (13:33), one of them will betray him (13:21), and Peter will deny him three times (13:38). Jesus' first words are, "Let not your hearts be troubled." **Rooms in the Father's house** (vv. 1–4). **Thomas's question** (vv. 5–7): "I am the way, and the truth, and the life." **Philip's question** (vv. 8–14): "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father." **Another Helper** (vv. 15–26): the Spirit will come, and the Father and the Son will make their home with those who love Jesus. **Peace** (vv. 27–31): "Peace I leave with you."

One feature of this chapter is that the disciples keep interrupting Jesus with questions: Thomas (14:5), Philip (14:8), Judas (not Iscariot, 14:22). Their questions are honest, and they show the disciples still don't understand. Jesus doesn't rebuke them for asking; he uses each question to take them deeper.

## Going Deeper: 14:1–3 "In my Father's house are many rooms"

**1. Is "believe" a command or a statement?** "Believe in God; believe also in me" (14:1). Both Greek verbs can be read as statements ("you believe") or commands ("believe!"). So it can mean "You believe in God; believe also in me," or "Believe in God; believe also in me." The Chinese Union Version takes the first as a statement and the second as a command. Either way, Jesus places faith in himself on the same level as faith in God.

**2. "Rooms," not mansions.** The King James has "many mansions," from the Latin *mansiones*, which meant "places to stay," not grand houses. The Greek *monai* comes from John's favorite verb, *menō* (to remain, abide). Jesus' point isn't the size of the house but that there is a place for us to *settle* and be with him.

**3. The same word in verse 23.** *Monē* appears only twice in the New Testament, the other time in 14:23: "we will come to him and make our *home* with him." Verse 2 speaks of our going to dwell in the Father's house in the future; verse 23 speaks of the Father and the Son coming to dwell with us now. John's hope is always like this: the future promise begins today.

**4. "I will come again."** "And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and will take you to myself, that where I am you may be also" (14:3). This "coming again" most directly refers to his return at the end; some interpreters think it also includes the resurrection appearances and the coming of the Spirit. The best thing about heaven isn't the room itself but "where I am you may be also."

## Going Deeper: 14:5–7 "I am the way, and the truth, and the life"

**1. Thomas's honesty.** "Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?" (14:5). Thomas wants a map; Jesus gives him a person.

**2. How the three words relate.** "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me" (14:6). This is the sixth of the seven "I am" sayings. The three words aren't three separate things. Many interpreters see "the way" as the main point, with "the truth" and "the life" explaining why he is the way: he is the way because he is the truth (revealing the Father) and the life (giving eternal life). The question in context is about "the way," and the second half of the verse is about "coming to the Father." He is the way because he himself goes to the Father through the cross, bearing our sin (14:2–4, 28), opening the way for us.

**3. An exclusive claim.** "No one comes to the Father except through me." This is one of the most exclusive claims in the New Testament, and it grates in an age that prizes pluralism. But it isn't arrogance; it's a statement of fact. If Jesus truly came from the Father and is returning to the Father (13:3), then he alone knows the way and he alone is the way. Acts 4:12 says the same: "there is salvation in no one else."

**4. Knowing and seeing.** "If you had known me, you would have known my Father also. From now on you do know him and have seen him" (14:7). This returns to the Prologue: "No one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (1:18).

## Going Deeper: 14:8–14 "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father"

**1. Philip's request.** "Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us" (14:8). This echoes Moses' request (Ex 33:18, "Please show me your glory"). The deepest longing of the human heart is to see God.

**2. "So long."** Jesus' answer carries a sigh: "Have I been with you so long, and you still do not know me, Philip? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9). For three years the face Philip looked at every day was the face of God.

**3. Not modalism.** This doesn't mean Jesus *is* the Father, as if one God appeared wearing different masks. Jesus immediately says, "I am in the Father and the Father is in me" (14:10). Two persons dwelling in each other, not one and the same. The church later described this as mutual indwelling: Father and Son are distinct yet fully dwell in each other, so to see the Son is to see the Father.

**4. "Greater works than these."** "Whoever believes in me will also do the works that I do; and greater works than these will he do, because I am going to the Father" (14:12). How could disciples do greater works than Jesus? The key is the last clause: "because I am going to the Father." After Jesus rose, ascended and sent the Spirit, the gospel spread from one small land to the whole world; on the day of Pentecost alone, three thousand believed. "Greater" refers to scope and to the stage of salvation history, not necessarily to more spectacular miracles.

**5. "In my name."** "Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son" (14:13). "In my name" isn't a closing formula but asking in keeping with who Jesus is and what he wants. So the promise has a purpose: "that the Father may be glorified in the Son."

## Going Deeper: 14:15–26 Another Helper

**1. "Another."** "And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Helper" (14:16). "Another" is *allos*, which often means "another of the same kind" (though the distinction isn't absolute). The Spirit is the same kind of helper as Jesus. What Jesus did beside the disciples, the Spirit will continue to do, and he will be "in you" (14:17).

**2. What is a "Helper"?** The Greek *paraklētos* originally means "one called alongside," in a courtroom an advocate or counsel for the defense. English versions have "Comforter," "Counselor," "Advocate," "Helper," each catching one side of it. The Chinese translation tradition, followed by the Chinese Union Version, uses *baohuishi*, often explained as "a teacher who protects and shows kindness," combining several of these senses.

**3. Jesus is the first Helper.** First John 2:1 says, "if anyone does sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous." "Advocate" there is *paraklētos*. Jesus was the disciples' helper while he was with them (14:25), and now pleads our case before the Father; the same title is used of him. The Spirit bears witness to Jesus within us, so the Spirit is "another" Helper.

**4. "I will not leave you as orphans."** "I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you" (14:18). The disciples were afraid of being left behind. This most likely refers first to his resurrection appearances (14:19, "you will see me"), and continues in the Spirit's indwelling. "We will come to him and make our home with him" (14:23): Father, Son and Spirit all promise in this chapter to be with believers.

**5. The Spirit's teaching.** "He will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you" (14:26). This is first a promise to the apostles: the Spirit would help them remember and understand Jesus' words, which is the foundation on which the New Testament was written. John's Gospel itself is one fulfillment of this promise (cf. 2:22; 12:16).

## Going Deeper: 14:27–31 "Peace I leave with you"

**1. A parting gift.** "Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you" (14:27). Jews greeted and parted with "peace" (*shalom*). What Jesus says before leaving isn't a pleasantry but a bequest.

**2. "Not as the world gives."** "Not as the world gives do I give to you." The world's peace depends on circumstances: no conflict, no danger. Jesus speaks of his peace on the night he will be betrayed and the eve of his crucifixion. It doesn't depend on circumstances; it depends on him.

**3. "The Father is greater than I."** "If you loved me, you would have rejoiced, because I am going to the Father, for the Father is greater than I" (14:28). In church history this verse has been used to deny the Son's deity. But within John as a whole (1:1; 5:18; 10:30; 20:28), it can't mean the Son is lesser in essence. Orthodox interpretation has taken two main lines. Augustine and others read it of the incarnate Son, who took the form of a servant. Other fathers read it of the Father as the source of the Son, in the order of the persons. Neither reading denies that the Son is equally God with the Father.

**4. "Rise, let us go from here."** "Rise, let us go from here" (14:31). Yet three more chapters of discourse follow. Some scholars think the discourse continued on the way to Gethsemane (perhaps the vine of chapter 15 was spoken passing a vineyard); others take different views. Either way, the words remind us that Jesus walked toward the cross of his own accord.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** On the hardest night of his life, Jesus was still telling his disciples, "Let not your hearts be troubled." What is troubling my heart today? Can I bring it to the Lord who says, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life"? Like Thomas and Philip, what honest question do I have that I could ask him directly? Jesus said, "I will not leave you as orphans." When I feel alone, do I remember that the Spirit lives in me?
