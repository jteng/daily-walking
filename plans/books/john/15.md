## 本章脉络

第十五章是楼上讲论的第二部分。**葡萄树与枝子**（1-11节）：「我是真葡萄树……你们要常在我里面。」**朋友与彼此相爱**（12-17节）：「人为朋友舍命，人的爱心没有比这个大的。」**世界的恨恶**（18-25节）：「世人若恨你们，你们知道，恨你们以先已经恨我了。」**圣灵与门徒的见证**（26-27节）。

前十一节里，「常在」（*menō*，也译「住」）这个字出现了十次以上。如果要用一个词总结这一章，就是「常在」：常在他里面、常在他的话里、常在他的爱里。这个字在约翰福音一开始就出现了：施洗约翰看见圣灵降下来「住在」耶稣身上（1:32），最初的门徒问「拉比，在哪里住？」又「与他同住」（1:38-39）。第十五章把这份同住，从地理上的，变成了生命上的。

## 深读 15:1-3「我是真葡萄树」

**1. 以色列是葡萄树。** 在旧约里，葡萄树常是以色列的象征：「你从埃及挪出一棵葡萄树，赶出外邦人，把这树栽上。」（诗80:8）但这棵葡萄树一再令神失望：「他指望的是公平，谁知倒有暴虐。」（赛5:7）「我栽你是上等的葡萄树……你怎么向我变为外邦葡萄树的坏枝子呢？」（耶2:21）

**2. 「真」葡萄树。** 所以当耶稣说「我是**真**葡萄树」，他是在说：以色列应该是、却没有成为的那棵树，就是我。他是那位真以色列，结出神一直期待的果子。这是七个「我是」中的最后一个。神的子民不再是以民族来界定，而是以与他的联结来界定：枝子属于葡萄树。

**3. 剪去与修理。** 「凡属我不结果子的枝子，他就剪去；凡结果子的，他就修理干净，使枝子结果子更多。」（15:2）有一个流行的说法，认为「剪去」（*airei*）应该译作「举起来」，像园丁把垂在地上的枝子扶起来。这个字确实可以有「举起」的意思，但在这里的上下文（15:6「丢在外面……烧了」）中，多数解经家和译本都理解为「除去」。

**4. 一个文字游戏。** 「修理干净」（*kathairei*）和下一节的「干净」（*katharoi*）是同一个字根。「现在你们因我讲给你们的道，已经干净了。」（15:3）门徒已经被修剪过，用的工具是耶稣的话。神修剪我们，很多时候是透过他的话：它指出我们需要放下的，也洁净我们。

## 深读 15:4-8「离了我，你们就不能做什么」

**1. 常在是彼此的。** 「你们要常在我里面，我也常在你们里面。」（15:4）这不是单方面的努力，而是一种关系：枝子留在树上，树的生命就流进枝子里。

**2. 「不能做什么」。** 「我是葡萄树，你们是枝子。常在我里面的，我也常在他里面，这人就多结果子；因为离了我，你们就不能做什么。」（15:5）不是「能做的比较少」，而是「不能做什么」。人可以很忙、很有成就，却在神眼中没有结出一个属灵的果子。

**3. 果子是什么？** 上下文给了线索：遵守他的命令（15:10）、喜乐（15:11）、彼此相爱（15:12）、为他作见证（15:27），以及因此有人信主（15:16，「分派你们去结果子」）。果子是基督的生命在我们身上自然的流露，加拉太书5:22称之为「圣灵所结的果子」。

**4. 丢在外面的枝子。** 「人若不常在我里面，就像枝子丢在外面枯干，人拾起来，扔在火里烧了。」（15:6）这是一个严肃的警告。这是否表示真信徒会失去救恩？福音派内部有不同的理解。改革宗传统认为，这些枝子是表面上与基督有关联、却从未真正与他联合的人，像犹大（他刚刚离开这个房间，13:30）；约翰一书2:19也说「他们从我们中间出去，却不是属我们的」。另一些传统则认为这是真实的危险。各方都同意：这节经文的目的不是让人猜测别人的结局，而是呼召每一个人常在他里面。

**5. 祷告与果子。** 「你们若常在我里面，我的话也常在你们里面，凡你们所愿意的，祈求，就给你们成就。」（15:7）条件是「我的话常在你们里面」：一个被他的话塑造的人，所求的会越来越是他所要的。

## 深读 15:12-17「乃称你们为朋友」

**1. 最大的爱。** 「人为朋友舍命，人的爱心没有比这个大的。」（15:13）耶稣说这话的时候，离他舍命只有几个小时。这不是一句抽象的道德格言，而是他即将要做的事。罗马书5:8更进一步：「惟有基督在我们还作罪人的时候为我们死。」他为之舍命的「朋友」，原本是他的仇敌。

**2. 从仆人到朋友。** 「以后我不再称你们为仆人，因仆人不知道主人所做的事。我乃称你们为朋友。」（15:15）在旧约里，被称为神的「朋友」的是亚伯拉罕（赛41:8；雅2:23）和摩西（出33:11，「好像人与朋友说话一般」）。现在这个称号临到门徒。朋友与仆人的区别，不在于是否顺服（15:14「你们若遵行我所吩咐的，就是我的朋友了」），而在于是否被告知主人的心意。

**3. 「是我拣选了你们」。** 「不是你们拣选了我，是我拣选了你们，并且分派你们去结果子，叫你们的果子常存。」（15:16）当时的拉比通常是由门徒选择老师；耶稣却主动拣选门徒。这份拣选不是为了特权，而是为了差遣：「分派你们去结果子」。

> 要点：枝子不需要用力「挤出」果子，它只需要留在树上。常在基督里，不是一种技巧，而是一份关系：留在他的话里、他的爱里，果子自然会结出来。

## 深读 15:18-27 世界的恨恶与两种见证

**1. 被恨是正常的。** 「世人若恨你们，你们知道，恨你们以先已经恨我了。」（15:18）门徒不必惊讶。「你们若属世界，世界必爱属自己的；只因你们不属世界……所以世界就恨你们。」（15:19）「世界」在这里又是那个与神为敌的人类社会（参3:16的讨论）。

**2. 「无故地恨我」。** 「这要应验他们律法上所写的话，说：『他们无故地恨我。』」（15:25）这句话出自诗篇（35:19；69:4）。耶稣称诗篇为「律法」，这在当时是指整本旧约的常见用法。诗篇69篇在约翰福音里已经出现过（2:17），它是受苦义人的诗篇。

**3. 圣灵的见证与门徒的见证。** 「我要从父那里差保惠师来，就是从父出来真理的圣灵；他来了，就要为我作见证。你们也要作见证。」（15:26-27）在一个恨恶的世界里，门徒不是孤军作战：圣灵与他们一同作见证。「从父出来」这句话后来成为尼西亚信经的用语，东西方教会关于圣灵是否「也从子出来」的争论，就与这节经文有关。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 15 is the second part of the Upper Room Discourse. **The vine and the branches** (vv. 1–11): "I am the true vine... Abide in me." **Friends who love one another** (vv. 12–17): "Greater love has no one than this, that someone lays down his life for his friends." **The world's hatred** (vv. 18–25): "If the world hates you, know that it has hated me before it hated you." **The witness of the Spirit and the disciples** (vv. 26–27).

In the first eleven verses the word "abide" (*menō*, also "remain," "dwell") appears ten times or more. If one word sums up this chapter, it's "abide": abide in him, in his word, in his love. The word appears at the very start of John: John the Baptist sees the Spirit descend and "remain" on Jesus (1:32), and the first disciples ask, "Rabbi, where are you staying?" and "stayed with him" (1:38–39). Chapter 15 turns that staying from a matter of location into a matter of life.

## Going Deeper: 15:1–3 "I am the true vine"

**1. Israel as the vine.** In the Old Testament the vine is often a symbol of Israel: "You brought a vine out of Egypt; you drove out the nations and planted it" (Ps 80:8). But this vine repeatedly disappointed God: "he looked for justice, but behold, bloodshed" (Isa 5:7); "I planted you a choice vine, wholly of pure seed. How then have you turned degenerate and become a wild vine?" (Jer 2:21).

**2. The "true" vine.** So when Jesus says, "I am the *true* vine," he's saying: the tree Israel was meant to be but never became, that is me. He is the true Israel, bearing the fruit God always looked for. This is the last of the seven "I am" sayings. God's people are no longer defined by ethnicity but by union with him: the branches belong to the vine.

**3. Taking away and pruning.** "Every branch of mine that does not bear fruit he takes away, and every branch that does bear fruit he prunes, that it may bear more fruit" (15:2). A popular idea holds that "takes away" (*airei*) should be translated "lifts up," like a gardener raising branches off the ground. The word can mean "lift," but in this context (15:6, "thrown away... burned") most interpreters and translations take it as "remove."

**4. A play on words.** "Prunes" (*kathairei*) and "clean" (*katharoi*) in the next verse share a root. "Already you are clean because of the word that I have spoken to you" (15:3). The disciples have already been pruned, and the tool was Jesus' word. God often prunes us through his word: it shows us what we need to let go of, and it cleanses us.

## Going Deeper: 15:4–8 "Apart from me you can do nothing"

**1. Abiding is mutual.** "Abide in me, and I in you" (15:4). This isn't one-sided effort but a relationship: the branch stays on the vine, and the vine's life flows into the branch.

**2. "Nothing."** "I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing" (15:5). Not "less," but "nothing." A person can be very busy and very successful and yet, in God's eyes, bear no spiritual fruit at all.

**3. What is the fruit?** The context gives clues: keeping his commandments (15:10), joy (15:11), loving one another (15:12), bearing witness to him (15:27), and people coming to faith as a result (15:16, "appointed you that you should go and bear fruit"). Fruit is the natural overflow of Christ's life in us, what Galatians 5:22 calls "the fruit of the Spirit."

**4. Branches thrown away.** "If anyone does not abide in me he is thrown away like a branch and withers; and the branches are gathered, thrown into the fire, and burned" (15:6). This is a solemn warning. Does it mean true believers can lose their salvation? Evangelicals understand it differently. The Reformed tradition holds that these branches are people outwardly connected to Christ who were never truly united to him, like Judas (who had just left this room, 13:30); 1 John 2:19 says, "They went out from us, but they were not of us." Other traditions see it as a real danger. All agree that the verse isn't meant to make us speculate about other people's end but to call each of us to abide in him.

**5. Prayer and fruit.** "If you abide in me, and my words abide in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you" (15:7). The condition is "my words abide in you": a person shaped by his words will increasingly ask for what he wants.

## Going Deeper: 15:12–17 "I have called you friends"

**1. The greatest love.** "Greater love has no one than this, that someone lays down his life for his friends" (15:13). When Jesus said this, he was only hours from laying down his life. It isn't an abstract moral maxim; it's what he was about to do. Romans 5:8 goes further: "God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us." The "friends" he died for were once his enemies.

**2. From servants to friends.** "No longer do I call you servants, for the servant does not know what his master is doing; but I have called you friends" (15:15). In the Old Testament those called God's "friend" were Abraham (Isa 41:8; Jas 2:23) and Moses (Ex 33:11, "as a man speaks to his friend"). Now the title is given to the disciples. The difference between friend and servant isn't whether they obey (15:14, "You are my friends if you do what I command you") but whether they are told the master's mind.

**3. "I chose you."** "You did not choose me, but I chose you and appointed you that you should go and bear fruit and that your fruit should abide" (15:16). In those days disciples usually chose their rabbi; Jesus chose his disciples. That choice was not for privilege but for mission: "appointed you that you should go and bear fruit."

> The point: A branch doesn't strain to squeeze out fruit; it only needs to stay on the vine. Abiding in Christ isn't a technique but a relationship: staying in his word and his love, and the fruit comes.

## Going Deeper: 15:18–27 The World's Hatred and Two Witnesses

**1. Being hated is normal.** "If the world hates you, know that it has hated me before it hated you" (15:18). The disciples needn't be surprised. "If you were of the world, the world would love you as its own; but because you are not of the world... therefore the world hates you" (15:19). "The world" here is again human society set against God (see the discussion of 3:16).

**2. "Hated me without a cause."** "But the word that is written in their Law must be fulfilled: 'They hated me without a cause'" (15:25). The words come from the Psalms (35:19; 69:4). Jesus calls the Psalms "Law," a common usage then for the whole Old Testament. Psalm 69 has already appeared in John (2:17); it's a psalm of the suffering righteous one.

**3. The Spirit's witness and the disciples' witness.** "But when the Helper comes, whom I will send to you from the Father, the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father, he will bear witness about me. And you also will bear witness" (15:26–27). In a hostile world the disciples don't fight alone: the Spirit bears witness alongside them. "Who proceeds from the Father" later became the language of the Nicene Creed, and the dispute between Eastern and Western churches over whether the Spirit proceeds "and from the Son" turns on this verse.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Apart from me you can do nothing." How much of my recent busyness has flowed from abiding in him, and how much is only my own effort? Is God using his word right now to "prune" some part of my life? Jesus calls me his "friend." How does that change the way I think about prayer and obedience?
