## 本章脉络

第十六章是楼上讲论的第三部分，也是讲论的结尾。**将来的逼迫**（1-4节）：「凡杀你们的就以为是事奉神。」**圣灵的工作**（5-15节）：「我去是与你们有益的。」圣灵要为罪、为义、为审判使世界知罪，又要引导门徒明白一切的真理。**忧愁变为喜乐**（16-24节）：「等不多时，你们就不得见我；再等不多时，你们还要见我。」**我已经胜了世界**（25-33节）。

门徒在这一章里最大的困惑是「不多的时候」（16:16-18）：他们反复问这句话是什么意思。耶稣说的是他的死与复活：三天之内，他们会从最深的忧愁走到最大的喜乐。但这个模式也是整个基督徒生命的模式：在等待主再来的「不多的时候」里，我们经历忧愁，却确知它要变为喜乐。

## 深读 16:7「我去是与你们有益的」

**1. 难以置信的一句话。** 「然而，我将真情告诉你们，我去是与你们有益的；我若不去，保惠师就不到你们这里来；我若去，就差他来。」（16:7）门徒最不想听到的就是耶稣要离开，耶稣却说这对他们有益。

**2. 为什么？** 耶稣在世的时候，一次只能在一个地方，和少数人在一起。圣灵来了以后，就住在每一个信徒里面（14:17），在世界的每一个角落。而且，如7:39所说，新约时代圣灵的浇灌，要等到耶稣得荣耀之后。十字架、复活、升天，是圣灵降临的前提。

**3. 今天的我们。** 这也是对今天读者的安慰。我们常想：如果我能像门徒那样亲眼看见耶稣就好了。耶稣说：有圣灵住在你里面，比那更好，因为借着圣灵，基督自己与我们同在（14:18、23），而20:29也说「没有看见就信的有福了」。

## 深读 16:8-11 为罪、为义、为审判

**1. 和合本的「自己责备自己」。** 和合本译作「他既来了，就要叫世人为罪、为义、为审判，自己责备自己」。希腊文的结构是「他要使世界知罪」（*elenxei*），主词是圣灵。这个动词 *elenchō* 的意思是「揭露、证明有罪、使人知罪」，像法庭上检察官揭露罪证。和合本的「自己责备自己」点出了结果（人的良心被定罪），而原文强调的是圣灵主动的工作。

**2. 三件事的解释。** 耶稣自己解释了三件事（16:9-11），但这几节很简洁，解经家有不同的理解。一个常见的读法是：
- **为罪**，「是因他们不信我」：圣灵要揭露世界最根本的罪：不信耶稣。
- **为义**，「是因我往父那里去」：世界判定耶稣是罪人，把他钉在十字架上；但父接纳他回到自己那里，证明他才是义的。圣灵要揭露世界对「义」的错误判断。（也有解经家认为，三项都是在揭露世界自己的罪、自己虚假的义和错误的审判。）
- **为审判**，「是因这世界的王受了审判」：十字架看起来是世界审判了耶稣，实际上是撒但被审判了（12:31）。

**3. 圣灵在传福音中的角色。** 这段经文告诉我们，让人认罪悔改，不是传道人的口才能做到的，而是圣灵的工作。门徒的任务是作见证（15:27），圣灵的任务是使人知罪。

## 深读 16:12-15「他要引导你们明白一切的真理」

**1. 「你们现在担当不了」。** 「我还有好些事要告诉你们，但你们现在担当不了。」（16:12）有些真理，要等到十字架和复活之后才能明白。

**2. 引导进入真理。** 「只等真理的圣灵来了，他要引导你们明白一切的真理；因为他不是凭自己说的，乃是把他所听见的都说出来，并要把将来的事告诉你们。」（16:13）这首先是对使徒的应许，和14:26一样。圣灵引导使徒明白并记录关于基督的真理，这正是新约圣经得以写成的基础。这节经文不是说每一个信徒都会直接领受新的启示，而是说圣灵借着使徒所传的话，引导教会进入真理。

**3. 圣灵荣耀基督。** 「他要荣耀我，因为他要将受于我的告诉你们。」（16:14）圣灵的工作不是把注意力引向自己，而是引向基督。要辨别某种所谓「属灵」的经验或教导，最好的试金石就是：它是否荣耀基督？

**4. 三位一体的交流。** 「凡父所有的，都是我的，所以我说，他要将受于我的告诉你们。」（16:15）父所有的属于子，子所有的由圣灵传达给门徒。在这短短一节里，可以看到父、子、圣灵之间完美的交流。

## 深读 16:20-24 生产的妇人

**1. 一个熟悉的比喻。** 「妇人生产的时候就忧愁，因为她的时候到了；既生了孩子，就不再记念那苦楚，因为欢喜世上生了一个人。」（16:21）旧约常用生产的痛苦来描写神子民在新时代来临前的苦难（赛26:17-18；弥4:9-10）；而16:22「我要再见你们，你们的心就喜乐了」，也与以赛亚书66:14相呼应。

**2. 「她的时候」。** 「因为她的**时候**到了」，用的是 *hōra*，正是整卷书里耶稣的「时候」的那个字。门徒的忧愁和耶稣的十字架，是同一个「时候」：痛苦是真实的，但它是生产的痛苦，不是毫无意义的痛苦。

**3. 不是取代，而是转变。** 「你们的忧愁要变为喜乐。」（16:20）不是忧愁之后来了另一样东西，而是忧愁本身被转变：生产的痛苦本身变成了新生命的喜乐。十字架本身，变成了救恩。

**4. 没有人能夺去的喜乐。** 「我要再见你们，你们的心就喜乐了；这喜乐也没有人能夺去。」（16:22）建立在复活上的喜乐，没有任何环境能夺走。

**5. 两种「问」。** 「到那日，你们什么也就不问我了……你们若向父求什么，他必因我的名赐给你们。」（16:23）这节有两个不同的动词：「问」（*erōtaō*）和「求」（*aiteō*）。*erōtaō* 本身也可指请求（如16:26；17:9），但按16:19的上下文，这里多半指发问。和合本很好地分辨了出来。门徒现在充满了问题（16:17-19）；复活以后，他们的问题会得到解答，而他们可以直接奉耶稣的名向父祈求。

## 深读 16:32-33「我已经胜了世界」

**1. 被撇下，却不孤单。** 「看哪，时候将到，且是已经到了，你们要分散，各归自己的地方去，留下我独自一人；其实我不是独自一人，因为有父与我同在。」（16:32）耶稣知道门徒会在几个小时内离开他。他没有责备，只是说：父与我同在。

**2. 在他里面有平安，在世上有苦难。** 「我将这些事告诉你们，是要叫你们在我里面有平安。在世上，你们有苦难。」（16:33）两个地点、两种处境，同时成立：在世上，门徒有苦难；在基督里，他们有平安。基督徒的平安不是没有苦难，而是在苦难中，有基督作我们更深的安息之所。

**3. 「我已经胜了世界」。** 「但你们可以放心，我已经胜了世界。」「胜了」（*nenikēka*）是完成时，表示一个已经完成、并持续有效的胜利。耶稣说这话的时候，还没有被捉拿，还没有上十字架；但他说得好像已经完成了。在他眼中，十字架不是可能的失败，而是确定的胜利。约翰一书5:4接着说：「使我们胜了世界的，就是我们的信心。」

> 要点：讲论以「我已经胜了世界」作结。门徒即将四散，耶稣即将被钉，但他宣告的不是「我会尽力」，而是「我已经胜了」。

## 默想

**默想：** 耶稣说「我去是与你们有益的」，我是否真的相信，借着住在我里面的圣灵，基督自己就与我同在？我现在的忧愁，在神手中不是白白的痛苦；而最终使它变为喜乐的，是「我要再见你们」：是见到他自己。「在世上，你们有苦难；但你们可以放心，我已经胜了世界。」今天我需要把哪一样苦难，放在这个已经完成的胜利之下？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 16 is the third part of the Upper Room Discourse and its conclusion. **Persecution to come** (vv. 1–4): "whoever kills you will think he is offering service to God." **The Spirit's work** (vv. 5–15): "it is to your advantage that I go away." The Spirit will convict the world concerning sin, righteousness and judgment, and will guide the disciples into all the truth. **Sorrow turned to joy** (vv. 16–24): "A little while, and you will see me no longer; and again a little while, and you will see me." **I have overcome the world** (vv. 25–33).

The disciples' biggest puzzle in this chapter is "a little while" (16:16–18); they keep asking what it means. Jesus is speaking of his death and resurrection: within three days they will move from the deepest sorrow to the greatest joy. But the pattern also shapes the whole Christian life. In the "little while" of waiting for the Lord's return, we know sorrow, yet we know for certain that it will turn to joy.

## Going Deeper: 16:7 "It is to your advantage that I go away"

**1. A hard saying to believe.** "Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you" (16:7). The last thing the disciples wanted to hear was that Jesus was leaving, and he says it's for their good.

**2. Why?** On earth Jesus could be in only one place at a time, with a few people. When the Spirit comes, he dwells in every believer (14:17), in every corner of the world. And as 7:39 says, the new-covenant outpouring of the Spirit had to wait until Jesus was glorified. The cross, resurrection and ascension are the precondition of the Spirit's coming.

**3. For us today.** This comforts today's readers too. We often think, if only I could have seen Jesus with my own eyes like the disciples. Jesus says that having the Spirit live in you is better than that, because through the Spirit Christ himself is with us (14:18, 23), and 20:29 blesses "those who have not seen and yet have believed."

## Going Deeper: 16:8–11 Concerning Sin, Righteousness and Judgment

**1. The Chinese Union Version's "reproach itself."** The Chinese Union Version renders this "he will make the world, concerning sin, righteousness and judgment, reproach itself." The Greek structure is "he will convict the world" (*elenxei*), with the Spirit as subject. The verb *elenchō* means to expose, to prove guilty, to convict, like a prosecutor laying out the evidence. The Chinese rendering highlights the result (the conscience condemned), while the Greek stresses the Spirit's active work.

**2. The three items explained.** Jesus explains the three items himself (16:9–11), but very briefly, and interpreters differ. One common reading:
- **Concerning sin**, "because they do not believe in me": the Spirit exposes that the world's most basic sin is not believing in Jesus.
- **Concerning righteousness**, "because I go to the Father": the world judged Jesus a sinner and crucified him, but the Father received him back to himself, proving that he is the righteous one. The Spirit exposes the world's false verdict about righteousness. (Other interpreters read all three items as exposing the world's own sin, its false righteousness and its false judgment.)
- **Concerning judgment**, "because the ruler of this world is judged": the cross looks like the world judging Jesus, but in fact Satan is judged (12:31).

**3. The Spirit's role in evangelism.** This passage tells us that bringing people to conviction and repentance isn't something a preacher's eloquence can do; it's the Spirit's work. The disciples' task is to bear witness (15:27); the Spirit's task is to convict.

## Going Deeper: 16:12–15 "He will guide you into all the truth"

**1. "You cannot bear them now."** "I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now" (16:12). Some truths could only be understood after the cross and resurrection.

**2. Guided into the truth.** "When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth, for he will not speak on his own authority, but whatever he hears he will speak, and he will declare to you the things that are to come" (16:13). As in 14:26, this is first a promise to the apostles. The Spirit guided them to understand and record the truth about Christ, which is the foundation on which the New Testament was written. The verse doesn't say every believer will receive new revelation directly; it says the Spirit guides the church into the truth through the apostles' word.

**3. The Spirit glorifies Christ.** "He will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and declare it to you" (16:14). The Spirit's work doesn't draw attention to himself but to Christ. A good test of any supposedly "spiritual" experience or teaching is whether it glorifies Christ.

**4. Communion within the Trinity.** "All that the Father has is mine; therefore I said that he will take what is mine and declare it to you" (16:15). What the Father has belongs to the Son, and what the Son has the Spirit makes known to the disciples. In this one short verse we glimpse perfect communion among Father, Son and Spirit.

## Going Deeper: 16:20–24 A Woman in Labor

**1. A familiar image.** "When a woman is giving birth, she has sorrow because her hour has come, but when she has delivered the baby, she no longer remembers the anguish, for joy that a human being has been born into the world" (16:21). The Old Testament often uses the pain of childbirth for the sufferings of God's people before the new age comes (Isa 26:17–18; Mic 4:9–10), and 16:22, "I will see you again, and your hearts will rejoice," closely echoes Isaiah 66:14.

**2. "Her hour."** "Because her *hour* has come" uses *hōra*, the very word for Jesus' "hour" throughout the book. The disciples' sorrow and Jesus' cross belong to the same "hour": the pain is real, but it's the pain of birth, not meaningless pain.

**3. Not replaced but transformed.** "Your sorrow will turn into joy" (16:20). It isn't that something else comes after the sorrow; the sorrow itself is transformed, as labor pain becomes the joy of new life. The cross itself becomes salvation.

**4. Joy no one can take away.** "I will see you again, and your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you" (16:22). Joy founded on the resurrection can't be taken away by any circumstance.

**5. Two kinds of "asking."** "In that day you will ask nothing of me. Truly, truly, I say to you, whatever you ask of the Father in my name, he will give it to you" (16:23). The verse uses two different verbs, *erōtaō* and *aiteō*. *Erōtaō* can mean either "question" or "request" (cf. 16:26; 17:9), but in light of 16:19 it most likely means "ask questions" here, while *aiteō* means "make requests." The Chinese Union Version distinguishes them well. The disciples are full of questions now (16:17–19). After the resurrection their questions will be answered, and they can make requests directly to the Father in Jesus' name.

## Going Deeper: 16:32–33 "I have overcome the world"

**1. Left alone.** "Behold, the hour is coming, indeed it has come, when you will be scattered, each to his own home, and will leave me alone. Yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me" (16:32). Jesus knows the disciples will leave him within hours. He doesn't rebuke them; he simply says, the Father is with me.

**2. Peace in him, tribulation in the world.** "I have said these things to you, that in me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation" (16:33). Two places, two conditions, both true at once: in the world the disciples will have tribulation; in Christ they have peace. Christian peace isn't the absence of trouble but having Christ as a deeper refuge within it.

**3. "I have overcome the world."** "But take heart; I have overcome the world." "Have overcome" (*nenikēka*) is perfect tense: a victory completed and still in force. When Jesus says this, he hasn't yet been arrested or crucified, but he speaks as though it's already done. In his eyes the cross isn't a possible defeat but a certain victory. First John 5:4 continues: "this is the victory that has overcome the world, our faith."

> The point: The discourse ends with "I have overcome the world." The disciples are about to scatter and Jesus is about to be crucified, but what he declares isn't "I'll do my best" but "I have overcome."

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus said, "it is to your advantage that I go away." Do I truly believe that through the Spirit who lives in me, Christ himself is with me? My present sorrow is not wasted pain in God's hands; what finally turns it to joy is "I will see you again": seeing him. "In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world." What trouble do I need to place under that finished victory today?
