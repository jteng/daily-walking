## 本章脉络

第十七章是新约中记载耶稣最长的一篇祷告。楼上讲论结束了，耶稣「举目望天」，门徒在旁边听着他与父说话。十六世纪的路德宗神学家称它为「大祭司的祷告」，因为耶稣在这里像大祭司一样，在献上自己之前为百姓代求。**为自己祷告**（1-5节）：「愿你荣耀你的儿子。」**为门徒祷告**（6-19节）：「求你保守他们……用真理使他们成圣。」**为将来的信徒祷告**（20-26节）：「使他们都合而为一。」

这篇祷告从耶稣自己，向外一圈圈扩大：先是他自己，然后是身边的门徒，最后是「那些因他们的话信我的人」（17:20），也就是今天读这一章的每一个基督徒。我们读的，是耶稣为我们所献的祷告。

## 深读 17:1-5「认识你独一的真神……这就是永生」

**1. 「时候到了」。** 「父啊，时候到了，愿你荣耀你的儿子，使儿子也荣耀你。」（17:1）从2:4「我的时候还没有到」，到12:23「人子得荣耀的时候到了」，到这里，「时候」这条线走到了终点。耶稣求父荣耀他，意思是借着十字架、复活和升天，显明他的荣耀；而这荣耀的目的，是「使儿子也荣耀你」。

**2. 永生的定义。** 「认识你独一的真神，并且认识你所差来的耶稣基督，这就是永生。」（17:3）这是约翰福音中唯一一处直接为「永生」下定义的经文。永生不只是时间上无限延长的生命，而是一种关系：认识神。在圣经里，「认识」不只是头脑上的知道，而是亲密的、有关系的认识，耶利米书31:34应许新约中「他们从最小的到至大的都必认识我」。

**3. 一神信仰中的子。** 这一节同时说「独一的真神」和「你所差来的耶稣基督」。耶稣在宣告一神信仰的同一句话里，把认识他自己和认识独一的真神放在一起，作为永生的内容。没有一个受造物能这样说。

**4. 「已成全了」。** 「我在地上已经荣耀你，你所托付我的事，我已成全了。」（17:4）十字架还没有发生，耶稣却说「已成全了」。这与16:33「我已经胜了世界」一样，是确信的语气；它也预告了十字架上的最后一句话：「成了。」（19:30）

**5. 创世以前的荣耀。** 「父啊，现在求你使我同你享荣耀，就是未有世界以先，我同你所有的荣耀。」（17:5）这是约翰福音对耶稣先存最清楚的宣告之一，呼应序言：「太初有道，道与神同在。」耶稣不是在求得到一份新的荣耀，而是求回到他本来就有的荣耀里，只是这一次，他是带着人性、带着被钉的伤痕回去的。

## 深读 17:6-19 保守与成圣

**1. 「他们本是你的」。** 「你从世上赐给我的人，我已将你的名显明与他们。他们本是你的，你将他们赐给我。」（17:6）门徒先是属于父的，父把他们赐给子。这回响6:37-39：「凡父所赐给我的人必到我这里来……叫我一个也不失落。」

**2. 「保守」。** 「圣父啊，求你因你所赐给我的名保守他们。」（17:11）耶稣在世时亲自保守了他们（17:12），现在他要离开，就把他们交在父的手里。这是10:28-29「谁也不能从我父手里把他们夺去」的祷告版本。

**3. 「灭亡之子」。** 「其中除了那灭亡之子，没有一个灭亡的，好叫经上的话得应验。」（17:12）这指犹大。「灭亡之子」是一种闪族的表达法，意思是「注定灭亡的人」。同一个希腊词组在帖撒罗尼迦后书2:3用来描写末世的「大罪人」，和合本在那里译作「沉沦之子」。犹大的失落不是耶稣保守失败，而是应验了圣经（13:18引诗41:9）；同时，犹大仍要为自己的选择负责。

**4. 在世界里，不属世界。** 「我不求你叫他们离开世界，只求你保守他们脱离那恶者。」（17:15）基督徒不是要逃离世界，而是在世界里被保守。「他们不属世界，正如我不属世界一样」（17:14、16），「你怎样差我到世上，我也照样差他们到世上」（17:18）。不属世界，却被差进世界：这是门徒身份的两面。

**5. 「用真理使他们成圣」。** 「求你用真理使他们成圣；你的道就是真理。」（17:17）「成圣」（*hagiazō*）的意思是「分别出来归给神」。神用来使人成圣的工具，是他的道。不是靠离开世界，而是靠真理。

**6. 「自己分别为圣」。** 「我为他们的缘故，自己分别为圣，叫他们也因真理成圣。」（17:19）这里的「分别为圣」是同一个字 *hagiazō*。在旧约里，这个字用于祭司被分别出来（出28:41），也用于献给神的祭物（申15:19「都要分别为圣，归耶和华」）。耶稣说这话时，正走向十字架：他把自己分别出来作祭物，好叫门徒可以被分别出来归给神。这也回响10:36：「父所分别为圣、又差到世间来的。」

## 深读 17:20-23「使他们都合而为一」

**1. 为我们祷告。** 「我不但为这些人祈求，也为那些因他们的话信我的人祈求。」（17:20）今天每一个因为使徒所传、所写的话而信主的人，都在这个祷告之中。

**2. 合一的样式。** 「使他们都合而为一。正如你父在我里面，我在你里面，使他们也在我们里面。」（17:21）门徒合一的样式，是父与子的合一；门徒合一的基础，是「在我们里面」，也就是与神的联合。这不首先是组织上的统一，而是生命上的合一。

**3. 合一的目的。** 「叫世人可以信你差了我来。」（17:21）又说：「叫世人知道你差了我来，也知道你爱他们如同爱我一样。」（17:23）基督徒的合一本身就是福音的见证（参13:35）。分裂与纷争，会让世界看不见基督。

**4. 在真理中的合一。** 这个合一不能脱离前面的祷告：「求你用真理使他们成圣。」（17:17）耶稣所求的合一，是在他的道、他的真理中的合一，不是以放弃真理为代价的合一。但同样，真理也不能成为不合一的借口。

> 要点：耶稣在被出卖的那个晚上，为今天的我们祷告：愿我们被父保守，被真理分别为圣，在他里面合而为一，叫世人可以信。

## 深读 17:24-26「叫他们看见你所赐给我的荣耀」

**1. 耶稣的心愿。** 「父啊，我在哪里，愿你所赐给我的人也同我在那里，叫他们看见你所赐给我的荣耀。」（17:24）「愿」（*thelō*）是一个很强的字，是子对父表达的心愿。耶稣最深的心愿之一，是我们与他同在，看见他的荣耀。这呼应14:3：「我在哪里，叫你们也在那里。」

**2. 创立世界以前的爱。** 「因为创立世界以前，你已经爱我了。」（17:24）在有任何受造物之前，父与子之间就有永恒的爱。神不是因为孤单才创造人；他在永恒中就是爱（约壹4:8）。而这爱，耶稣要让它「在他们里面」（17:26）。

**3. 结语。** 「我已将你的名指示他们，还要指示他们，使你所爱我的爱在他们里面，我也在他们里面。」（17:26）祷告结束在爱和内住：父爱子的那份爱，要在门徒里面；而子自己，也要在他们里面。祷告完了，耶稣就出去，过了汲沦溪，走进园子（18:1）。

## 默想

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 17 is the longest recorded prayer of Jesus in the New Testament. The Upper Room Discourse is over; Jesus "lifted up his eyes to heaven," and the disciples listen as he speaks with the Father. Sixteenth-century Lutheran theologians called it "the high-priestly prayer," because here, like a high priest, Jesus intercedes for his people before offering himself. **Jesus prays for himself** (vv. 1–5): "glorify your Son." **For his disciples** (vv. 6–19): "keep them... sanctify them in the truth." **For future believers** (vv. 20–26): "that they may all be one."

The prayer widens in circles: first Jesus himself, then the disciples around him, and finally "those who will believe in me through their word" (17:20), which means every Christian reading this chapter today. What we're reading is Jesus' prayer for us.

## Going Deeper: 17:1–5 "That they know you, the only true God... this is eternal life"

**1. "The hour has come."** "Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify you" (17:1). From "My hour has not yet come" (2:4) to "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified" (12:23) to here, the thread of the "hour" reaches its end. Jesus asks the Father to glorify him, meaning to reveal his glory through the cross, resurrection and ascension, and the purpose of that glory is "that the Son may glorify you."

**2. Eternal life defined.** "And this is eternal life, that they know you the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent" (17:3). This is the only verse in John that directly defines eternal life. Eternal life isn't merely life extended forever; it's a relationship: knowing God. In Scripture, "knowing" isn't only head knowledge but intimate, relational knowledge. Jeremiah 31:34 promises that in the new covenant "they shall all know me, from the least of them to the greatest."

**3. The Son within monotheism.** This verse speaks of "the only true God" and "Jesus Christ whom you have sent" together. In the same sentence that confesses one God, Jesus places knowing himself alongside knowing the only true God as the content of eternal life. No creature could say that.

**4. "Having accomplished."** "I glorified you on earth, having accomplished the work that you gave me to do" (17:4). The cross hasn't happened yet, but Jesus speaks of the work as accomplished. Like "I have overcome the world" (16:33), it's the language of certainty, and it anticipates his last word from the cross: "It is finished" (19:30).

**5. Glory before the world existed.** "And now, Father, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed" (17:5). This is one of John's clearest statements of Jesus' pre-existence, echoing the Prologue: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God." Jesus isn't asking for a new glory but to return to the glory he always had, only this time returning with his humanity and the scars of the cross.

## Going Deeper: 17:6–19 Kept and Sanctified

**1. "Yours they were."** "I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world. Yours they were, and you gave them to me" (17:6). The disciples first belonged to the Father, who gave them to the Son. This echoes 6:37–39: "All that the Father gives me will come to me... that I should lose nothing."

**2. "Keep them."** "Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me" (17:11). On earth Jesus kept them himself (17:12); now that he is leaving, he entrusts them to the Father's hands. This is the prayer form of 10:28–29, "no one is able to snatch them out of the Father's hand."

**3. "The son of destruction."** "Not one of them has been lost except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled" (17:12). This refers to Judas. "Son of destruction" is a Semitic expression meaning "one destined for destruction." The same Greek phrase is used in 2 Thessalonians 2:3 of the end-time "man of lawlessness," where the ESV renders it "the son of destruction" too. Judas's loss isn't a failure of Jesus' keeping but the fulfillment of Scripture (13:18, quoting Ps 41:9); at the same time, Judas remains responsible for his choice.

**4. In the world, not of it.** "I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from the evil one" (17:15). Christians aren't meant to escape the world but to be kept within it. "They are not of the world, just as I am not of the world" (17:14, 16), and "As you sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world" (17:18). Not of the world, yet sent into it: these are the two sides of a disciple's identity.

**5. "Sanctify them in the truth."** "Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth" (17:17). "Sanctify" (*hagiazō*) means to set apart for God. The tool God uses to sanctify people is his word. Not by leaving the world, but by the truth.

**6. "I consecrate myself."** "And for their sake I consecrate myself, that they also may be sanctified in truth" (17:19). "Consecrate" is the same word, *hagiazō*. In the Old Testament it was used for priests being set apart (Ex 28:41) and for offerings dedicated to God (Deut 15:19, "you shall dedicate to the LORD your God"). Jesus says this as he walks toward the cross: he sets himself apart as the sacrifice so that the disciples can be set apart for God. It also echoes 10:36: "him whom the Father consecrated and sent into the world."

## Going Deeper: 17:20–23 "That they may all be one"

**1. A prayer for us.** "I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word" (17:20). Everyone who has come to faith through what the apostles preached and wrote is included in this prayer.

**2. The pattern of unity.** "That they may all be one, just as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be in us" (17:21). The pattern of the disciples' unity is the unity of Father and Son; its basis is "in us," union with God. This is first of all not organizational uniformity but a unity of life.

**3. The purpose of unity.** "So that the world may believe that you have sent me" (17:21), and, "so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you loved me" (17:23). Christian unity is itself a witness to the gospel (cf. 13:35). Division and strife keep the world from seeing Christ.

**4. Unity in the truth.** This unity can't be separated from the earlier petition: "Sanctify them in the truth" (17:17). The unity Jesus prays for is unity in his word and truth, not unity bought by abandoning the truth. Equally, truth must not become an excuse for disunity.

> The point: On the night he was betrayed, Jesus prayed for us today: that we would be kept by the Father, set apart by the truth, and made one in him, so that the world may believe.

## Going Deeper: 17:24–26 "To see my glory"

**1. Jesus' desire.** "Father, I desire that they also, whom you have given me, may be with me where I am, to see my glory that you have given me" (17:24). "Desire" (*thelō*) is a strong word, the Son expressing his will to the Father. One of Jesus' deepest desires is that we be with him and see his glory. This echoes 14:3: "where I am you may be also."

**2. Love before the foundation of the world.** "Because you loved me before the foundation of the world" (17:24). Before anything was created, there was eternal love between the Father and the Son. God didn't create humanity out of loneliness; he has always been love (1 John 4:8). And this love Jesus means to be "in them" (17:26).

**3. The close.** "I made known to them your name, and I will continue to make it known, that the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them" (17:26). The prayer ends with love and indwelling: the Father's love for the Son will be in the disciples, and the Son himself will be in them. When the prayer ends, Jesus goes out across the Kidron Valley into a garden (18:1).

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus says eternal life is "that they know you." Does my knowledge of God stop at knowing about him, or am I truly coming to know him? In the world but not of it: in what ways am I too much like the world, and in what ways am I too far from the place I've been sent? Jesus prayed for our unity. What one concrete thing can I do for the unity of Christ's body?
