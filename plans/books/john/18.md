## 本章脉络

第十八章是受难记载的开始，场景快速转换。**在园子里被捉拿**（1-11节）：耶稣说「我就是」，来捉拿他的人倒退跌倒。**在亚那面前与彼得第一次不认主**（12-18节）。**亚那的审问**（19-24节）。**彼得第二、三次不认主**（25-27节）。**在彼拉多面前**（28-40节）：「我的国不属这世界」，「真理是什么呢？」，众人选了巴拉巴。

约翰的受难记载有一个鲜明的特色：耶稣始终是掌权的那一位。他主动走出来迎向捉拿他的人，他保护门徒，他在审问中质问审判他的人，他告诉彼拉多权柄从哪里来（19:11）。表面上是耶稣受审，实际上，在约翰的笔下，被审判的是世界。

## 深读 18:4-9「我就是」

**1. 他主动出来。** 「耶稣知道将要临到自己的一切事，就出来对他们说：『你们找谁？』」（18:4）在约翰福音里，耶稣不是被找到的，而是自己走出来的。这呼应10:18：「没有人夺我的命去，是我自己舍的。」

**2. 「我就是」。** 他们说「找拿撒勒人耶稣」，耶稣说：「我就是！」（18:5）希腊文是 *egō eimi*。表面上这只是「我就是你们要找的人」，但约翰接着说：「耶稣一说『我就是』，他们就退后倒在地上。」（18:6）约翰在短短四节之内三次重复这句话（18:5、6、8）。读过8:58的读者不会错过它的分量：那里的「就有了我」，原文也是 *egō eimi*（参第八章）。在被捉拿的那一刻，耶稣仍然是那位「我是」。在旧约里，人在神显现时俯伏在地（结1:28；但10:9）；而诗篇27:2说，作恶的人前来攻击时「就绊跌仆倒」。

**3. 好牧人保护羊。** 「你们若找我，就让这些人去吧。」（18:8）约翰说这是应验耶稣的话：「你所赐给我的人，我没有失落一个。」（18:9；参6:39；17:12）好牧人在狼来的时候不逃走，而是站在羊和狼中间（10:11-12）。

## 深读 18:10-11「我父所给我的那杯」

**1. 彼得的刀。** 彼得拔刀砍下大祭司仆人马勒古的右耳。只有约翰记下了彼得和马勒古的名字。彼得在13:37说「我愿意为你舍命」，他是真心的，只是用错了方法。

**2. 「那杯」。** 「收刀入鞘吧，我父所给我的那杯，我岂可不喝呢？」（18:11）约翰福音没有记载客西马尼园里「把这杯撤去」的祷告，但这一句话里有同样的「杯」。在旧约里，「杯」常象征神的忿怒和审判（诗75:8；赛51:17；耶25:15）。耶稣要喝的，是神对罪的审判；而他是替我们喝的。

## 深读 18:15-27 炭火旁的「我不是」

**1. 交错的两个场景。** 约翰把彼得不认主和耶稣受审交错着写：彼得在院子里（18:15-18），耶稣在亚那面前（18:19-24），彼得又在院子里（18:25-27）。读者的视线在两人之间来回。

**2. 「我就是」与「我不是」。** 使女问：「你不也是这人的门徒吗？」彼得说：「我不是。」（18:17）希腊文是 *ouk eimi*。在园子里，耶稣面对兵丁说「我是」（*egō eimi*）；在院子里，彼得面对一个使女说「我不是」（*ouk eimi*）。约翰很可能有意形成这个对比：耶稣坦然承认，彼得却否认。

**3. 炭火。** 「仆人和差役因为天冷，就生了炭火……彼得也同他们站着烤火。」（18:18）「炭火」（*anthrakia*）这个字在新约里只出现两次，另一次在21:9：复活的主在提比哩亚海边生了炭火，在那里三次问彼得「你爱我吗」。彼得在一堆炭火旁三次不认主，又在另一堆炭火旁三次被恢复。

**4. 耶稣在审问中。** 亚那问耶稣的门徒和教训，耶稣说：「我从来是明明地对世人说话……你为什么问我呢？可以问那听见的人。」（18:20-21）后来的犹太法典（《密释纳》）要求先传证人，不可要被告自证其罪；这些规定在耶稣时代是否已经通行，学者看法不一，但耶稣的话至少表明：按律法的精神，定罪要凭证人（申19:15）。被差役打了，耶稣说：「我若说的不是，你可以指证那不是；我若说的是，你为什么打我呢？」（18:23）他没有报复，也没有沉默不言，而是平静地指出不公。

## 深读 18:28-32 不进衙门，恐怕染了污秽

**1. 深刻的讽刺。** 「他们自己却不进衙门，恐怕染了污秽，不能吃逾越节的筵席。」（18:28）他们小心翼翼，不肯进外邦人的房子，免得在礼仪上不洁；同时却在谋害一个无辜的人。这是马太福音23:24所说的「蠓虫你们就滤出来，骆驼你们倒吞下去」。

**2. 「逾越节的筵席」与年代问题。** 这一节与第一章讨论过的19:14相关：如果「吃逾越节的筵席」是指逾越节当晚的晚餐，那么耶稣就是在宰杀逾越节羊羔的那天被钉，比对观福音（记载最后晚餐就是逾越节晚餐）早一天。另一些解经家（如卡森）认为，这里的「逾越节」可以泛指整个节期，包括接下来七天的除酵节筵席，这样就与对观福音一致。两种读法都有福音派学者支持。

**3. 「我们没有杀人的权柄」。** 「彼拉多说：『你们自己带他去，按着你们的律法审问他吧。』犹太人说：『我们没有杀人的权柄。』」（18:31）在罗马统治下，犹太公会一般没有执行死刑的权力。约翰说，这是要应验「耶稣所说自己将要怎样死的话」（18:32）：犹太人的死刑是用石头打死，罗马的死刑是钉十字架，也就是被「举起来」（12:32-33）。

## 深读 18:33-38「我的国不属这世界」

**1. 「你是犹太人的王吗？」** 这是彼拉多最关心的问题：这个人是不是一个政治威胁？

**2. 「不属这世界」。** 「我的国不属这世界；我的国若属这世界，我的臣仆必要争战。」（18:36）「属」（*ek*）的意思是「出于、来自」，不是「不在这世界里」。耶稣的国不是从这世界的权力来源来的，所以不用这世界的方法（刀剑）来推进。这不表示他的国与这世界无关，而是说它的来源和性质不同。

**3. 「我为此而生」。** 「你说我是王。我为此而生，也为此来到世间，特为给真理作见证。凡属真理的人就听我的话。」（18:37）「为此而生，也为此来到世间」，两个说法，一个说出生，一个暗示他从别处而来（3:31「从天上来的」）。「听我的话」呼应10:3、27：「羊也听他的声音。」

**4. 「真理是什么呢？」** 彼拉多说了这句话，「又出来到犹太人那里」（18:38），没有等回答。他问「真理是什么」，而那位说「我就是真理」（14:6）的，就站在他面前。

## 深读 18:39-40「不要这人，要巴拉巴！」

「他们又喊着说：『不要这人，要巴拉巴！』这巴拉巴是个强盗。」（18:40）「强盗」（*lēstēs*）在当时也用于武装的叛乱分子；马可福音15:7说他和作乱的人一同捆绑，「他们作乱的时候，曾杀过人」。讽刺的是，耶稣被指控为政治叛乱者，而众人选择释放的，正是一个真正的叛乱者。约翰自己的重点是这个交换：真正的叛乱者得释放，无罪的那一位被定罪，正如该亚法不自觉说出的「一个人替百姓死是有益的」（18:14；参11:50）。有人也注意到「巴拉巴」在亚兰文可能是「父的儿子」的意思，但约翰没有点出这一点。

> 要点：在约翰的受难记载里，耶稣不是命运的受害者。他主动出来，用「我是」使兵丁仆倒，保护门徒，喝下父给他的杯；被审判的那一位，其实是真正的王。

## 默想

**默想：** 耶稣说「我就是」，彼得说「我不是」。但耶稣在园子里说「就让这些人去吧」，他喝下那杯，好叫不认他的彼得得释放；巴拉巴得释放，无罪的那一位被定罪。我就是那个被放走的人。今天，当我因害怕而想隐藏自己属于他的时候（在家族聚会、祭祖的场合、同事的饭局上），这个交换能给我什么勇气？彼拉多问「真理是什么」却不等回答。今天我是否愿意停下来，听那位就是真理的主说话？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 18 opens the passion narrative, and the scenes shift quickly. **The arrest in the garden** (vv. 1–11): Jesus says "I am he," and those who came to seize him draw back and fall. **Before Annas, and Peter's first denial** (vv. 12–18). **Annas questions Jesus** (vv. 19–24). **Peter's second and third denials** (vv. 25–27). **Before Pilate** (vv. 28–40): "My kingdom is not of this world," "What is truth?", and the crowd chooses Barabbas.

John's passion narrative has a striking feature: Jesus is always the one in control. He steps out to meet those who come to arrest him, he protects his disciples, he questions those who question him, and he tells Pilate where authority comes from (19:11). On the surface Jesus is on trial; in John's telling, it is the world that is being judged.

## Going Deeper: 18:4–9 "I am he"

**1. He comes forward.** "Then Jesus, knowing all that would happen to him, came forward and said to them, 'Whom do you seek?'" (18:4). In John, Jesus isn't found; he steps out himself. This echoes 10:18: "No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord."

**2. "I am he."** They say, "Jesus of Nazareth," and Jesus says, "I am he" (18:5). The Greek is *egō eimi*. On the surface it simply means "I'm the one you're looking for," but John adds, "When Jesus said to them, 'I am he,' they drew back and fell to the ground" (18:6). John repeats the phrase three times in four verses (18:5, 6, 8), and readers who remember 8:58 won't miss its weight: at the very moment of his arrest, Jesus is still the "I am." In the Old Testament people fall on their faces when God appears (Ezek 1:28; Dan 10:9), and Psalm 27:2 says that when evildoers assail, "it is they who stumble and fall."

**3. The good shepherd protects his sheep.** "If you seek me, let these men go" (18:8). John says this fulfilled Jesus' words: "Of those whom you gave me I have lost not one" (18:9; cf. 6:39; 17:12). When the wolf comes, the good shepherd doesn't run; he stands between the sheep and the wolf (10:11–12).

## Going Deeper: 18:10–11 "The cup that the Father has given me"

**1. Peter's sword.** Peter draws his sword and cuts off the right ear of the high priest's servant, Malchus. Only John names Peter and Malchus. Peter said in 13:37, "I will lay down my life for you." He meant it; he just went about it the wrong way.

**2. "The cup."** "Put your sword into its sheath; shall I not drink the cup that the Father has given me?" (18:11). John doesn't record the Gethsemane prayer, "remove this cup," but the same "cup" is in this sentence. In the Old Testament the "cup" often symbolizes God's wrath and judgment (Ps 75:8; Isa 51:17; Jer 25:15). What Jesus will drink is God's judgment on sin, and he drinks it for us.

## Going Deeper: 18:15–27 "I Am Not" by the Charcoal Fire

**1. Two interwoven scenes.** John weaves Peter's denials together with Jesus' trial: Peter in the courtyard (18:15–18), Jesus before Annas (18:19–24), Peter in the courtyard again (18:25–27). The reader's eye moves back and forth between the two.

**2. "I am" and "I am not."** The servant girl asks, "You also are not one of this man's disciples, are you?" Peter says, "I am not" (18:17), in Greek *ouk eimi*. In the garden Jesus faced soldiers and said "I am" (*egō eimi*); in the courtyard Peter faced a servant girl and said "I am not" (*ouk eimi*). John very likely intends the contrast: Jesus openly confesses, while Peter denies.

**3. The charcoal fire.** "Now the servants and officers had made a charcoal fire, because it was cold, and they were standing and warming themselves. Peter also was with them" (18:18). "Charcoal fire" (*anthrakia*) appears only twice in the New Testament, the other time in 21:9, where the risen Lord makes a charcoal fire by the Sea of Tiberias and there asks Peter three times, "Do you love me?" Beside one charcoal fire Peter denies the Lord three times; beside another he is restored three times.

**4. Jesus under questioning.** Annas asks Jesus about his disciples and his teaching. Jesus says, "I have spoken openly to the world... Why do you ask me? Ask those who have heard me" (18:20–21). Later Jewish law (the Mishnah) required witnesses rather than self-incrimination; whether those rules held in Jesus' day is debated, but Jesus' point stands on the law's own demand for witnesses (Deut 19:15). Struck by an officer, Jesus says, "If what I said is wrong, bear witness about the wrong; but if what I said is right, why do you strike me?" (18:23). He doesn't retaliate, and he doesn't stay silent; he calmly names the injustice.

## Going Deeper: 18:28–32 They Would Not Enter, So as Not to Be Defiled

**1. A deep irony.** "They themselves did not enter the governor's headquarters, so that they would not be defiled, but could eat the Passover" (18:28). They carefully avoided ritual defilement from entering a Gentile's house while plotting to kill an innocent man. It's what Matthew 23:24 calls "straining out a gnat and swallowing a camel."

**2. "Eat the Passover" and the chronology question.** This verse connects with 19:14, discussed in chapter 1. If "eat the Passover" means the Passover meal that evening, Jesus was crucified on the day the Passover lambs were slaughtered, a day earlier than the other Gospels (which present the Last Supper as the Passover meal). Other interpreters (Carson, for example) take "Passover" here more broadly for the whole festival, including the meals of the following seven days of Unleavened Bread, which agrees with the other Gospels. Both readings have evangelical support.

**3. "It is not lawful for us to put anyone to death."** "Pilate said to them, 'Take him yourselves and judge him by your own law.' The Jews said to him, 'It is not lawful for us to put anyone to death'" (18:31). Under Roman rule the council generally had no power to carry out executions. John says this fulfilled Jesus' words "showing by what kind of death he was going to die" (18:32): the Jewish death penalty was stoning, but the Roman one was crucifixion, being "lifted up" (12:32–33).

## Going Deeper: 18:33–38 "My kingdom is not of this world"

**1. "Are you the King of the Jews?"** This is Pilate's main concern: is this man a political threat?

**2. "Not of this world."** "My kingdom is not of this world. If my kingdom were of this world, my servants would have been fighting" (18:36). "Of" (*ek*) means "from, out of," not "not located in this world." Jesus' kingdom doesn't come from this world's sources of power, so it doesn't advance by this world's methods (the sword). It doesn't mean his kingdom has nothing to do with this world, but that its source and nature are different.

**3. "For this purpose I was born."** "You say that I am a king. For this purpose I was born and for this purpose I have come into the world, to bear witness to the truth. Everyone who is of the truth listens to my voice" (18:37). "Born" and "come into the world": one speaks of his birth, the other hints that he came from elsewhere (3:31, "he who comes from heaven"). "Listens to my voice" echoes 10:3, 27: "The sheep hear his voice."

**4. "What is truth?"** Pilate says this and "went back outside to the Jews" (18:38) without waiting for an answer. He asks "What is truth?" while the one who said "I am the truth" (14:6) stands in front of him.

## Going Deeper: 18:39–40 "Not this man, but Barabbas!"

"They cried out again, 'Not this man, but Barabbas!' Now Barabbas was a robber" (18:40). "Robber" (*lēstēs*) was also used then for armed insurrectionists; Mark 15:7 says he had "committed murder in the insurrection." The irony is that Jesus is accused of political rebellion, and the man the crowd chooses to release is a real rebel. John's own point is the exchange: the real rebel goes free and the innocent one is condemned, just as Caiaphas unwittingly said, "it would be expedient that one man should die for the people" (18:14; cf. 11:50). Some also note that "Barabbas" may mean "son of the father" in Aramaic, though John doesn't draw attention to it.

> The point: In John's passion narrative Jesus is no victim of fate. He steps forward, his "I am" sends soldiers to the ground, he protects his disciples and drinks the cup the Father gives him; the one on trial is the true King.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Jesus said "I am he"; Peter said "I am not." Yet in the garden Jesus said "let these men go," and he drank the cup so that Peter, who denied him, could go free. Barabbas walked out, and the innocent one was condemned. I am the one who was let go. When fear tempts me today to hide that I belong to him, what courage does that exchange give me? Pilate asked "What is truth?" and didn't wait for an answer. Am I willing today to stop and listen to the Lord who is the truth?
