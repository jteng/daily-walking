## 本章脉络

第十九章是全书的中心，也是从2:4「我的时候还没有到」一路指向的那个「时候」（12:23；13:1；17:1）。**鞭打与戏弄**（1-7节）：「你们看这个人！」**彼拉多与权柄**（8-16节）：「除了凯撒，我们没有王。」**钉十字架**（17-27节）：三种文字的名号，拈阄分衣，耶稣把母亲交托给所爱的门徒。**耶稣的死**（28-30节）：「我渴了」，「成了！」**肋旁的血和水**（31-37节）。**安葬**（38-42节）：两位暗中的门徒走到光中。

约翰在这一章里多次引用经上的话（19:24、28、36-37）。他要读者看见：十字架不是计划出了差错，而是旧约一路指向的那件事。还有一个反讽贯穿全章：罗马兵丁戏弄耶稣是王，彼拉多写下「犹太人的王」，众人喊「除掉他」，但在约翰的读者眼中，这一切都在宣告同一个真理：他真的是王，而十字架就是他的宝座。

## 深读 19:1-16「你们看这个人！」与「除了凯撒，我们没有王」

**1. 被戏弄的王。** 兵丁用荆棘编作冠冕，给他穿上紫袍，说「恭喜，犹太人的王啊！」（19:2-3）他们以为是在开玩笑，其实说出了真相。

**2. 「你们看这个人！」** 彼拉多把耶稣带出来说：「你们看这个人！」（19:5）他可能是想说：看，这个被打得不成人样的人，有什么威胁？希望激起众人的怜悯。但约翰的读者听到的是更深的意思：这就是那个人，道成肉身的那一位（1:14），完全的人，在这里承受人类的罪。

**3. 权柄从哪里来。** 彼拉多说：「你岂不知我有权柄释放你，也有权柄把你钉十字架吗？」耶稣回答：「若不是从上头赐给你的，你就毫无权柄办我。」（19:10-11）「从上头」（*anōthen*）正是3:3「重生」的那个字。站着受审的那一位，告诉审判他的人，他的权柄从哪里来。

**4. 「除了凯撒，我们没有王」。** 彼拉多说：「看哪，这是你们的王！」祭司长回答：「除了凯撒，我们没有王。」（19:14-15）这是全书最沉重的一句话之一。在旧约里，以色列真正的王是神自己：基甸说「惟有耶和华管理你们」（士8:23），百姓要求立王时，神说「他们不是厌弃你，乃是厌弃我，不要我作他们的王」（撒上8:7）。在逾越节，纪念神从法老手中拯救他们的日子，祭司长宣告他们唯一的王是凯撒。

**5. 「约有午正」。** 「那日是预备逾越节的日子，约有午正。」（19:14）马可福音15:25说钉十字架是在「巳初」（上午九点左右）。解经家提出几种解释：古人计时多是大概的估算，「午正」和「巳初」都只是把上午约略划分；也有人认为约翰用的是不同的计时法。关于「预备逾越节的日子」的年代问题，请参第一章和第十八章的讨论。

## 深读 19:17-24 三种文字的名号与没有缝儿的里衣

**1. 「耶稣背着自己的十字架出来」。** 约翰没有提到古利奈人西门（对观福音记载他被强迫替耶稣背十字架），而是强调耶稣「背着自己的十字架」（19:17）。这不是矛盾，耶稣很可能先自己背，途中才由西门接过去；但约翰的强调与全书一致：耶稣是主动走向十字架的。有人也联想到以撒背着燔祭的柴上山（创22:6）。

**2. 三种文字。** 名号「是用希伯来、罗马、希腊三样文字写的」（19:20）：宗教的语言、政治的语言、文化的语言。彼拉多无意中向全世界宣告了耶稣是王。祭司长要他改成「他自己说：我是犹太人的王」，彼拉多说：「我所写的，我已经写上了。」（19:22）

**3. 没有缝儿的里衣。** 兵丁分他的衣服，「又拿他的里衣，这件里衣原来没有缝儿，是上下一片织成的」（19:23）。「上下」原文是「从上头」（*ek tōn anōthen*），又是*anōthen*这个字。有解经家指出，犹太史家约瑟夫记载大祭司的长袍也是一片织成、没有缝的，所以约翰也许暗示耶稣是那位真正的大祭司（参第十七章）。这个联想没有明说，但很合乎约翰的写法。

**4. 应验诗篇22篇。** 「这要应验经上的话说：他们分了我的外衣，为我的里衣拈阄。」（19:24；诗22:18）诗篇22篇以「我的神，我的神！为什么离弃我？」开始，是一首受苦义人的诗，后半却转为「列国的万族都要在你面前敬拜」（22:27），最后一节说「这事是他所行的」（22:31）。约翰不需要引用整首诗，读者会想起它。

## 深读 19:25-27「看，你的母亲！」

**1. 十字架下的人。** 「站在耶稣十字架旁边的，有他母亲与他母亲的姊妹，并革罗罢的妻子马利亚，和抹大拉的马利亚。」（19:25）门徒大多逃散了，留下来的多是妇女，和耶稣所爱的那门徒。

**2. 最后的照顾。** 「耶稣见母亲和他所爱的那门徒站在旁边，就对他母亲说：『母亲，看，你的儿子！』又对那门徒说：『看，你的母亲！』从此，那门徒就接她到自己家里去了。」（19:26-27）在承受世界的罪的同时，耶稣仍然照顾自己的母亲。这里的「母亲」，原文和迦拿婚筵一样是「妇人」（*gynai*，参第二章）。迦拿时「时候还没有到」，现在时候到了。

**3. 新的家。** 许多解经家也看见这一幕的象征意义：在十字架下，一个新的家诞生了，属于耶稣的人彼此成为家人。但首先，这是一个儿子对母亲真实的爱。

## 深读 19:28-30「我渴了」与「成了」

**1. 「知道各样的事已经成了」。** 「这事以后，耶稣知道各样的事已经成了，为要使经上的话应验，就说：『我渴了。』」（19:28）道成了肉身（1:14），而肉身会渴。那位在井旁对撒玛利亚妇人说「人若喝我所赐的水就永远不渴」（4:14）的主，现在说「我渴了」。诗篇69:21说：「我渴了，他们拿醋给我喝。」

**2. 牛膝草。** 「他们就拿海绒蘸满了醋，绑在牛膝草上，送到他口。」（19:29）牛膝草这种细小的植物，不太适合撑起海绵，所以约翰特别提到它，很可能有用意：在出埃及的那一夜，以色列人正是用牛膝草蘸羊羔的血，涂在门框上（出12:22）。又是一个逾越节的记号。

**3. 「成了」。** 「耶稣尝了那醋，就说：『成了！』」（19:30）希腊文只有一个字：*tetelestai*，完成时，「已经完成，其果效持续」。两节之前，约翰刚用同一个字说耶稣「知道各样的事已经成了」（19:28）。这也与13:1「爱他们到底」（*eis telos*）和17:4「你所托付我的事，我已成全了」是同一个字根。父所托付的工作，就是借着他的死成就救恩，完成了。有一个流行的说法，认为古代的税单收据上写着这个字，意思是「付清了」。这个说法有些根据，但学者对它的分量有不同看法，不宜太强调。不论如何，这一声不是失败者的叹息，而是得胜者的宣告：他在十字架上替我们死，担当了我们该受的刑罚和神的忿怒（参18:11「那杯」；赛53:5-6），正如逾越节羊羔的血使审判越过；这就是施洗约翰所说「除去世人罪孽」（1:29）的方法。罪债已经付清，救恩已经成就，不需要再添加什么。

**4. 「将灵魂交付神了」。** 「便低下头，将灵魂交付神了。」（19:30）原文是「交付了灵」（*paredōken to pneuma*）。他不是被夺去生命，而是主动把生命交出去（10:18）。有解经家也注意到，「交付」这个字在受难记载里一再出现：犹大「交付」他（18:2），祭司长「交付」他（18:30），彼拉多「交付」他（19:16），最后，他自己交付了灵。

> 要点：「成了」是一个字，却是福音的总结：救恩不是要我们去完成，而是耶稣已经完成了。我们所能做的，就是相信那已经完成的工作。

## 深读 19:31-37 血和水，与看见的人的见证

**1. 骨头没有被打断。** 犹太人求彼拉多打断被钉之人的腿，好叫他们快死。兵丁来到耶稣那里，「见他已经死了，就不打断他的腿」（19:33）。约翰说这应验了「他的骨头一根也不可折断」（19:36），这是逾越节羊羔的规定（出12:46；民9:12），也呼应诗篇34:20。第一章施洗约翰所说的「神的羔羊」，在这里完全显明了。

**2. 血和水。** 「惟有一个兵拿枪扎他的肋旁，随即有血和水流出来。」（19:34）这首先证明耶稣真的死了。约翰特别强调这件事，可能是为了回应早期教会中一些人（后来被称为「幻影派」）的说法：他们认为耶稣只是看起来有身体，没有真正受苦而死。约翰一书5:6说：「这藉着水和血而来的，就是耶稣基督。」许多解经家也在血和水中看见象征：血是赎罪，水是圣灵所赐的生命（7:38-39）。

**3. 看见的人的见证。** 「看见这事的那人就作见证－他的见证也是真的，并且他知道自己所说的是真的－叫你们也可以信。」（19:35）这是全书中最直接的目击者宣告之一。约翰在这里停下来，直接对读者说话：我亲眼看见了，我说的是真的，好叫你们信。

**4. 仰望被扎的。** 「经上又有一句说：『他们要仰望自己所扎的人。』」（19:37；亚12:10）撒迦利亚书12:10说，神要把「那施恩叫人恳求的灵」浇灌下来，「他们必仰望我，就是他们所扎的」：在撒迦利亚书里，被扎的那一位，是耶和华自己。

## 深读 19:38-42 两位暗中的门徒

**1. 亚利马太人约瑟。** 「有亚利马太人约瑟，是耶稣的门徒，只因怕犹太人，就暗暗地作门徒。」（19:38）他去求彼拉多领耶稣的身体，这是公开表明立场的行动。

**2. 尼哥德慕。** 「又有尼哥德慕，就是先前夜里去见耶稣的，带着没药和沉香约有一百斤前来。」（19:39）第三章的那个夜访者，在耶稣死后，带着足以安葬君王的香料来了。在耶稣活着的时候，他们没有公开站出来；在耶稣死的时候，在门徒都逃走的时候，他们站了出来。十字架吸引人（12:32），连最胆怯的人也被吸引了。

**3. 园子里的新坟墓。** 「在耶稣钉十字架的地方有一个园子，园子里有一座新坟墓。」（19:41）约翰提到「园子」，在18:1耶稣在一个园子里被捉拿，在这里葬在一个园子里，到20:15，马利亚以为复活的耶稣是「看园的」。有解经家联想到伊甸园：人在一个园子里堕落，新的创造也在一个园子里开始。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 19 is the center of the whole book and the "hour" anticipated since 2:4 ("my hour has not yet come") and announced in 12:23; 13:1; 17:1. **Flogging and mockery** (vv. 1–7): "Behold the man!" **Pilate and authority** (vv. 8–16): "We have no king but Caesar." **The crucifixion** (vv. 17–27): the title in three languages, the soldiers casting lots for his clothing, and Jesus entrusting his mother to the disciple he loved. **The death of Jesus** (vv. 28–30): "I thirst," "It is finished." **Blood and water from his side** (vv. 31–37). **The burial** (vv. 38–42): two secret disciples step into the light.

Several times in this chapter John appeals to Scripture (19:24, 28, 36–37). He wants readers to see that the cross wasn't a plan gone wrong but the very thing the Old Testament pointed to. An irony also runs through the chapter: soldiers mock Jesus as king, Pilate writes "King of the Jews," the crowd shouts "Away with him," yet to John's readers all of it proclaims the same truth. He really is king, and the cross is his throne.

## Going Deeper: 19:1–16 "Behold the man!" and "We have no king but Caesar"

**1. The mocked king.** The soldiers twist a crown of thorns, put a purple robe on him and say, "Hail, King of the Jews!" (19:2–3). They think it's a joke; they're telling the truth.

**2. "Behold the man!"** Pilate brings Jesus out and says, "Behold the man!" (19:5). He may have meant: look at this beaten wreck of a man; what threat is he? hoping to stir the crowd's pity. But John's readers hear more: this is *the* man, the Word made flesh (1:14), fully human, bearing humanity's sin.

**3. Where authority comes from.** Pilate says, "Do you not know that I have authority to release you and authority to crucify you?" Jesus answers, "You would have no authority over me at all unless it had been given you from above" (19:10–11). "From above" (*anōthen*) is the very word of 3:3, "born again / from above." The man on trial tells his judge where his authority comes from.

**4. "We have no king but Caesar."** Pilate says, "Behold your King!" The chief priests answer, "We have no king but Caesar" (19:14–15). It's one of the heaviest sentences in the book. In the Old Testament Israel's true king was God himself. Gideon said, "the LORD will rule over you" (Judg 8:23), and when the people demanded a king, God said, "they have not rejected you, but they have rejected me from being king over them" (1 Sam 8:7). At Passover, the feast remembering God's rescue from Pharaoh, the chief priests declare that their only king is Caesar.

**5. "About the sixth hour."** "Now it was the day of Preparation of the Passover. It was about the sixth hour" (19:14). Mark 15:25 says the crucifixion was at "the third hour" (about 9 a.m.). Interpreters suggest several explanations: ancient timekeeping was approximate, and both the "third" and the "sixth" hour roughly divide the morning; others think John used a different reckoning. On the chronology of "the day of Preparation of the Passover," see the discussion in chapters 1 and 18.

## Going Deeper: 19:17–24 The Title in Three Languages and the Seamless Tunic

**1. "Bearing his own cross."** John doesn't mention Simon of Cyrene (whom the other Gospels say was forced to carry the cross); he stresses that Jesus went out "bearing his own cross" (19:17). There's no contradiction: Jesus likely carried it first, and Simon took it over on the way. But John's emphasis fits the whole book: Jesus goes to the cross of his own accord. Some also recall Isaac carrying the wood for the burnt offering up the mountain (Gen 22:6).

**2. Three languages.** The title "was written in Aramaic, in Latin, and in Greek" (19:20): the languages of religion, politics and culture. Pilate unwittingly announced Jesus' kingship to the whole world. When the chief priests asked him to change it to "This man said, I am King of the Jews," Pilate answered, "What I have written I have written" (19:22).

**3. The seamless tunic.** The soldiers divide his garments, "But the tunic was seamless, woven in one piece from top to bottom" (19:23). "From top" is literally "from above" (*ek tōn anōthen*), *anōthen* once again. Some interpreters note that the Jewish historian Josephus describes the high priest's robe as woven in one piece without a seam, so John may be hinting that Jesus is the true high priest (see chapter 17). John doesn't say so, but it fits his way of writing.

**4. Psalm 22 fulfilled.** "This was to fulfill the Scripture which says, 'They divided my garments among them, and for my clothing they cast lots'" (19:24; Ps 22:18). Psalm 22 begins, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" a psalm of the suffering righteous one, yet it turns to "all the families of the nations shall worship before you" (22:27), and its last verse says, "he has done it" (22:31). John doesn't need to quote the whole psalm; readers will remember it.

## Going Deeper: 19:25–27 "Behold, your mother!"

**1. Those at the cross.** "Standing by the cross of Jesus were his mother and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene" (19:25). Most of the disciples had fled; those who remained were mostly women, along with the disciple Jesus loved.

**2. His last care.** "When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved standing nearby, he said to his mother, 'Woman, behold, your son!' Then he said to the disciple, 'Behold, your mother!' And from that hour the disciple took her to his own home" (19:26–27). While bearing the world's sin, Jesus still cares for his mother. He addresses her, as at Cana, as "woman" (*gynai*; see chapter 2). At Cana his hour had not yet come; now it has.

**3. A new family.** Many interpreters also see the symbolism of this scene: at the cross a new family is born, and those who belong to Jesus become family to one another. But first of all, it is a son's real love for his mother.

## Going Deeper: 19:28–30 "I thirst" and "It is finished"

**1. "Knowing that all was now finished."** "After this, Jesus, knowing that all was now finished, said (to fulfill the Scripture), 'I thirst'" (19:28). The Word became flesh (1:14), and flesh thirsts. The Lord who told the Samaritan woman at the well, "whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never be thirsty forever" (4:14), now says, "I thirst." Psalm 69:21 says, "for my thirst they gave me sour wine to drink."

**2. Hyssop.** "So they put a sponge full of the sour wine on a hyssop branch and held it to his mouth" (19:29). Hyssop is a small plant, not well suited to holding up a sponge, so John's mention of it is probably deliberate: on the night of the exodus, the Israelites used hyssop to dip the lamb's blood and paint it on their doorposts (Ex 12:22). Another Passover sign.

**3. "It is finished."** "When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, 'It is finished'" (19:30). In Greek it is one word, *tetelestai*, in the perfect tense: "finished, with lasting effect." Two verses earlier John used the same word: Jesus knew "that all was now finished" (19:28). It also shares a root with "he loved them to the end" (*eis telos*, 13:1) and "having accomplished the work that you gave me to do" (17:4). The work the Father gave him, to accomplish salvation by his death, is complete. A popular claim holds that ancient tax receipts bore this word, meaning "paid in full." There's some basis for it, but scholars weigh it differently, so it shouldn't be pressed. Either way, this is no sigh of defeat but a victor's announcement. On the cross he died in our place, bearing the penalty and the wrath of God that we deserved (cf. "the cup," 18:11; Isa 53:5–6), just as the Passover blood turned judgment away. This is how the Lamb "takes away the sin of the world" (1:29). The debt of sin is paid, salvation is accomplished, and nothing more needs to be added.

**4. "Gave up his spirit."** "And he bowed his head and gave up his spirit" (19:30). Literally he "handed over the spirit" (*paredōken to pneuma*). His life wasn't taken; he gave it up (10:18). Some interpreters also notice that "hand over" recurs through the passion narrative: Judas handed him over (18:2), the chief priests handed him over (18:30), Pilate handed him over (19:16), and finally he handed over his own spirit.

> The point: "It is finished" is one word, and it sums up the gospel. Salvation isn't something we must complete; Jesus has completed it. What we can do is believe in the finished work.

## Going Deeper: 19:31–37 Blood and Water, and the Eyewitness's Testimony

**1. No bone broken.** The Jews asked Pilate to have the legs of the crucified broken to hasten their deaths. "But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs" (19:33). John says this fulfilled "Not one of his bones will be broken" (19:36), the rule for the Passover lamb (Ex 12:46; Num 9:12), echoed also in Psalm 34:20. The "Lamb of God" John the Baptist announced in chapter 1 is fully revealed here.

**2. Blood and water.** "But one of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once there came out blood and water" (19:34). First, this proves Jesus really died. John may stress it to answer some in the early church (later called Docetists) who taught that Jesus only seemed to have a body and didn't truly suffer and die. First John 5:6 says Jesus "came by water and blood." Many interpreters also see symbolism here: the blood for atonement, the water for the life the Spirit gives (7:38–39).

**3. The eyewitness's testimony.** "He who saw it has borne witness—his testimony is true, and he knows that he is telling the truth—that you also may believe" (19:35). This is one of the most direct eyewitness claims in the whole book. John stops and speaks straight to the reader: I saw it myself, what I say is true, so that you may believe.

**4. Looking on the pierced one.** "And again another Scripture says, 'They will look on him whom they have pierced'" (19:37; Zech 12:10). Zechariah 12:10 says God will pour out "a spirit of grace and pleas for mercy," and "they look on me, on him whom they have pierced": in Zechariah the one pierced is the LORD himself.

## Going Deeper: 19:38–42 Two Secret Disciples

**1. Joseph of Arimathea.** "Joseph of Arimathea, who was a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear of the Jews" (19:38). He goes to Pilate to ask for Jesus' body, a public act that declares where he stands.

**2. Nicodemus.** "Nicodemus also, who earlier had come to Jesus by night, came bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about seventy-five pounds in weight" (19:39). The night visitor of chapter 3, after Jesus' death, comes with spices enough to bury a king. While Jesus lived, they didn't stand up in public; at his death, when the disciples had fled, they did. The cross draws people (12:32), even the most timid.

**3. A new tomb in a garden.** "Now in the place where he was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb" (19:41). John mentions gardens: in 18:1 Jesus was arrested in a garden, here he is buried in one, and in 20:15 Mary will take the risen Jesus for "the gardener." Some interpreters think of Eden: humanity fell in a garden, and the new creation begins in one.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The chief priests said, "We have no king but Caesar." Is there some "Caesar" in my life competing with Jesus for the throne? Jesus said, "It is finished." Am I still trying to add something to my salvation? Joseph and Nicodemus stepped forward at the most dangerous moment. Where is the cross calling me today to stop being a secret disciple?
