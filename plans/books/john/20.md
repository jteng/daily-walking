## 本章脉络

第二十章是复活的一天，从清早到晚上，再到八天以后。**空坟墓**（1-10节）：马利亚、彼得和那门徒先后来到坟墓。**耶稣向抹大拉的马利亚显现**（11-18节）：「马利亚。」「拉波尼！」**向门徒显现**（19-23节）：「愿你们平安！」「你们受圣灵！」**多马**（24-29节）：「我的主！我的神！」**写这书的目的**（30-31节）。

这一章有一个清楚的模式：每一个人都从不信或不明白，走到信。马利亚以为有人把主的身体挪走了，后来认出了他；那门徒「看见就信了」；关着门的门徒从惧怕到喜乐；多马从「我总不信」到全书最高的信心告白。约翰也在结尾转向读者：你们没有看见，却可以信。

## 深读 20:1-10 空坟墓与留下的布

**1. 天还黑的时候。** 「七日的第一日清早，天还黑的时候，抹大拉的马利亚来到坟墓那里。」（20:1）约翰特别写「天还黑」。在这卷书里，黑暗常有象征意义（1:5；13:30）。新的一天，新的创造，就在黑暗还没有完全退去的时候开始了。

**2. 女人作第一个见证。** 在当时的犹太社会，女人的见证在法庭上常不被采信。如果复活的故事是后人编造的，大概不会让妇女成为第一批见证人。四福音都记载，最先发现空坟墓的是妇女，这是复活记载真实性的一个旁证。

**3. 细麻布与裹头巾。** 彼得进到坟墓里，「就看见细麻布还放在那里，又看见耶稣的裹头巾没有和细麻布放在一处，是另在一处卷着」（20:6-7）。如果有人偷走了身体，不会花时间把裹尸布解下来、还把头巾卷好放在一旁。这和拉撒路形成对比（11:44）：拉撒路出来时还被裹着，需要别人解开；耶稣把死亡的裹布留在了身后。

**4. 「看见就信了」。** 「先到坟墓的那门徒也进去，看见就信了。因为他们还不明白圣经的意思，就是耶稣必要从死里复活。」（20:8-9）那门徒信了什么？最自然的理解是：他相信耶稣复活了，虽然他还不能从圣经去解释这件事。信有时走在明白的前面。

## 深读 20:11-18「马利亚」

**1. 以为是看园的。** 马利亚在坟墓外哭，转过身来，看见耶稣站在那里，却不知道是耶稣，「以为是看园的」（20:15）。许多读者在这里看见一个反讽：她把他当作园丁，而他正是新创造这座园子的主（参19:41；创2:8、15）。

**2. 一个名字。** 「耶稣说：『马利亚。』马利亚就转过来，用希伯来话对他说：『拉波尼！』」（20:16）她认出他，不是因为看清了他的脸，而是因为他叫了她的名字。这正是10:3所说的：「羊也听他的声音。他按着名叫自己的羊，把羊领出来。」「希伯来话」在这里指当时犹太人说的亚兰话，英文ESV作「Aramaic」。

**3. 「不要摸我」。** 「耶稣说：『不要摸我，因我还没有升上去见我的父。』」（20:17）和合本译作「不要摸我」，但希腊文是现在时的命令（*mē mou haptou*），意思更接近「不要一直抓住我」、「不要拉着我不放」，英文ESV译作「Do not cling to me」。马利亚想要抓住她刚刚失而复得的主，好像要回到从前的关系。耶稣说：一种新的关系要开始了，不是靠肉身的抓住，而是借着他升天以后赐下的圣灵。

**4. 「我的父，也是你们的父」。** 「你往我弟兄那里去，告诉他们说，我要升上去见我的父，也是你们的父，见我的神，也是你们的神。」（20:17）在约翰福音中，这是耶稣第一次称门徒为「我弟兄」。借着十字架和复活，他的父成了他们的父。但他没有说「我们的父」，而是「我的父，也是你们的父」：他本是神的独生子，他们则是借着他成为神的儿女（1:12）。

**5. 第一个传复活信息的人。** 「抹大拉的马利亚就去告诉门徒说：『我已经看见了主。』」（20:18）她成了第一个宣告复活的人。

## 深读 20:19-23「父怎样差遣了我，我也照样差遣你们」

**1. 关着的门。** 「那日晚上，门徒所在的地方，因怕犹太人，门都关了。耶稣来，站在当中。」（20:19）复活的身体是真实的身体（他给他们看手和肋旁），却不再受原有的限制。

**2. 「愿你们平安」。** 耶稣两次说「愿你们平安」（20:19、21）。这是平常的问候（*shalom*），但在这个时刻，它带着全新的分量：14:27所应许的平安，借着十字架成就了。他给他们看手和肋旁，平安是建立在那些伤痕上的：他在十字架上担当了我们的罪，所以能把平安赐给我们。

**3. 差遣。** 「父怎样差遣了我，我也照样差遣你们。」（20:21）约翰福音一再称父为「差我来的」。现在，被差的那一位，差遣他的门徒。门徒的使命，是延续耶稣的使命（参17:18）。

**4. 吹一口气。** 「说了这话，就向他们吹一口气，说：『你们受圣灵！』」（20:22）「吹气」（*emphysaō*）在新约里只出现在这里。七十士译本在创世记2:7用这个字描写神「将生气吹在他鼻孔里」，以西结书37:9也用它（「气息啊……吹在这些被杀的人身上，使他们活了」）。约翰似乎在说：复活的主正在开始一个新的创造。

**5. 这与五旬节有什么关系？** 解经家有几种看法：有人认为这就是约翰版的五旬节；有人认为这是圣灵初步的赐予，五旬节是更完全的浇灌；也有人（如卡森）认为这是一个预表性的举动，预告即将在五旬节发生的事。这个问题也要和7:39、16:7以及使徒行传1:4-5一并考虑（圣灵要在耶稣得荣耀、离去之后才赐下，门徒要在耶路撒冷等候），因此多数福音派学者倾向后两种看法。

**6. 赦罪的权柄。** 「你们赦免谁的罪，谁的罪就赦免了；你们留下谁的罪，谁的罪就留下了。」（20:23）这里的「你们」是复数，是对聚集的门徒说的。这不是说门徒自己有赦罪的能力。在福音派的理解中，这是指教会借着宣讲福音所带有的权柄：凡因所传的福音而信的，可以确知罪已赦免；凡拒绝的，罪就仍在（参3:18、36）。

## 深读 20:24-29「我的主！我的神！」

**1. 多马的条件。** 「我非看见他手上的钉痕，用指头探入那钉痕，又用手探入他的肋旁，我总不信。」（20:25）多马要的证据非常具体，而且是关于伤痕的。他要确认，复活的这一位，就是那位被钉的。

**2. 耶稣知道他说的话。** 八天以后，耶稣再来，直接对多马说：「伸过你的指头来，摸我的手；伸出你的手来，探入我的肋旁。不要疑惑，总要信！」（20:27）耶稣用多马自己的话回应他，温和地责备，却先满足了他的需要。「不要疑惑，总要信」，直译是「不要成为不信的，要成为信的」。

**3. 全书的高峰。** 「多马说：『我的主！我的神！』」（20:28）经文没有说多马真的伸手去摸。看见就够了。他的告白是全书的高峰：序言以「道就是神」（1:1）开始，这里以一个门徒称耶稣为「我的神」结束，前后呼应。希腊文是 *ho kyrios mou kai ho theos mou*，原文说「多马对他说」，所以这不是一句惊叹，而是向耶稣说的告白。有学者指出，当时的罗马皇帝多米田曾以「我们的主和神」自称（他的官方书信用这个称号），约翰的读者若在多米田的时代读到这句话，会明白这个告白的分量：真正配得这称呼的，不是凯撒，而是复活的耶稣。

**4. 没有看见就信的有福了。** 「耶稣对他说：『你因看见了我才信；那没有看见就信的有福了。』」（20:29）这句话是对多马说的，更是对后来所有的读者说的。我们没有像多马那样看见，但我们并不因此吃亏：没有看见而信的人，耶稣称为有福。

> 要点：约翰福音从「道就是神」开始，在多马「我的主！我的神！」达到高峰。整卷书要读者走的路，就是多马走的路：从疑惑到敬拜。

## 深读 20:30-31 写这书的目的

「耶稣在门徒面前另外行了许多神迹，没有记在这书上。但记这些事要叫你们信耶稣是基督，是神的儿子，并且叫你们信了他，就可以因他的名得生命。」（20:30-31）多马的告白之后，约翰立刻转向读者，说明全书的目的。导读里讨论过这节的细节：「信」可能是「开始相信」，也可能是「继续相信」；卡森认为这句话应当读成「叫你们信基督、神的儿子，就是耶稣」。不论哪一种，约翰在多马之后这样说，是在邀请我们：多马因为看见而信，你们可以因为这本书所记的见证而信。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 20 covers the day of resurrection, from early morning to evening, and then eight days later. **The empty tomb** (vv. 1–10): Mary, Peter and the other disciple come to the tomb one after another. **Jesus appears to Mary Magdalene** (vv. 11–18): "Mary." "Rabboni!" **He appears to the disciples** (vv. 19–23): "Peace be with you." "Receive the Holy Spirit." **Thomas** (vv. 24–29): "My Lord and my God!" **The purpose of the book** (vv. 30–31).

The chapter follows a clear pattern: each person moves from unbelief or incomprehension to faith. Mary thinks someone has taken the Lord's body, then recognizes him; the other disciple "saw and believed"; the disciples behind locked doors move from fear to joy; Thomas moves from "I will never believe" to the book's highest confession of faith. And at the end John turns to the reader: you have not seen, but you can believe.

## Going Deeper: 20:1–10 The Empty Tomb and the Cloths Left Behind

**1. While it was still dark.** "Now on the first day of the week Mary Magdalene came to the tomb early, while it was still dark" (20:1). John notes that it was still dark. In this book darkness often carries symbolic weight (1:5; 13:30). The new day, the new creation, begins while the darkness has not yet fully lifted.

**2. A woman as the first witness.** In Jewish society at the time, a woman's testimony was often not accepted in court. If the resurrection story had been invented later, it would hardly have made women the first witnesses. All four Gospels record that women were the first to find the tomb empty, which is one piece of evidence for the truthfulness of the resurrection accounts.

**3. The linen cloths and the face cloth.** Peter went into the tomb and "saw the linen cloths lying there, and the face cloth, which had been on Jesus' head, not lying with the linen cloths but folded up in a place by itself" (20:6–7). Someone stealing a body wouldn't stop to unwrap the grave clothes and fold the face cloth to one side. Contrast Lazarus (11:44): he came out still wrapped and needed to be unbound; Jesus left the wrappings of death behind.

**4. "He saw and believed."** "Then the other disciple, who had reached the tomb first, also went in, and he saw and believed; for as yet they did not understand the Scripture, that he must rise from the dead" (20:8–9). What did he believe? The most natural reading is that he believed Jesus had risen, even though he couldn't yet explain it from Scripture. Sometimes faith runs ahead of understanding.

## Going Deeper: 20:11–18 "Mary"

**1. Supposing him to be the gardener.** Mary is weeping outside the tomb. She turns and sees Jesus standing there but doesn't know it's Jesus, "supposing him to be the gardener" (20:15). Many readers see an irony here: she takes him for the gardener, and he is indeed the one at work in the garden of the new creation (cf. 19:41; Gen 2:8, 15).

**2. One name.** "Jesus said to her, 'Mary.' She turned and said to him in Aramaic, 'Rabboni!'" (20:16). She recognizes him not because she sees his face clearly but because he calls her by name. This is exactly 10:3: "The sheep hear his voice, and he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out."

**3. "Do not cling to me."** "Jesus said to her, 'Do not cling to me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father'" (20:17). The Chinese Union Version, like the King James ("Touch me not"), reads "Do not touch me," but the Greek is a present imperative (*mē mou haptou*), closer to "stop holding on to me." Mary wants to hold on to the Lord she has just found again, as if to return to the old relationship. Jesus says a new relationship is about to begin, not by holding on physically but through the Spirit he will give after his ascension.

**4. "My Father and your Father."** "Go to my brothers and say to them, 'I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God'" (20:17). In John's Gospel this is the first time Jesus calls the disciples "my brothers." Through the cross and resurrection his Father has become their Father. Yet he doesn't say "our Father" but "my Father and your Father": he is God's Son by nature; they become God's children through him (1:12).

**5. The first to announce the resurrection.** "Mary Magdalene went and announced to the disciples, 'I have seen the Lord'" (20:18). She became the first to proclaim the resurrection.

## Going Deeper: 20:19–23 "As the Father has sent me, even so I am sending you"

**1. Locked doors.** "On the evening of that day, the first day of the week, the doors being locked where the disciples were for fear of the Jews, Jesus came and stood among them" (20:19). His risen body is a real body (he shows them his hands and side), yet no longer bound by its former limits.

**2. "Peace be with you."** Twice Jesus says, "Peace be with you" (20:19, 21). It was an ordinary greeting (*shalom*), but at this moment it carries new weight: the peace promised in 14:27 has been won through the cross. He shows them his hands and side; the peace rests on those wounds: he bore our sins there, so he can give us peace.

**3. Sent.** "As the Father has sent me, even so I am sending you" (20:21). John's Gospel repeatedly calls the Father "him who sent me." Now the one who was sent sends his disciples. Their mission continues his (cf. 17:18).

**4. He breathed on them.** "And when he had said this, he breathed on them and said to them, 'Receive the Holy Spirit'" (20:22). "Breathed" (*emphysaō*) occurs only here in the New Testament. The Septuagint uses it in Genesis 2:7, where God "breathed into his nostrils the breath of life," and in Ezekiel 37:9 ("Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe on these slain, that they may live"). John seems to be saying that the risen Lord is carrying out a new creation.

**5. How does this relate to Pentecost?** Interpreters hold several views. Some think this is John's version of Pentecost; some think it is an initial gift of the Spirit, with Pentecost the fuller outpouring; others (Carson among them) see an acted prophecy anticipating what would happen at Pentecost. The question must be weighed with 7:39, 16:7 and Acts 1:4–5 (the Spirit is given after Jesus is glorified and goes away; the disciples are told to wait in Jerusalem), which is why most evangelicals favor the second or third view.

**6. The authority to forgive.** "If you forgive the sins of anyone, they are forgiven; if you withhold forgiveness from anyone, it is withheld" (20:23). "You" is plural, spoken to the gathered disciples. This doesn't mean the disciples have power of their own to forgive sins. In the evangelical understanding, it refers to the authority the church carries in proclaiming the gospel: whoever believes the gospel proclaimed can be sure their sins are forgiven; whoever rejects it remains in their sin (cf. 3:18, 36).

## Going Deeper: 20:24–29 "My Lord and my God!"

**1. Thomas's conditions.** "Unless I see in his hands the mark of the nails, and place my finger into the mark of the nails, and place my hand into his side, I will never believe" (20:25). Thomas wants very specific evidence, and it's about the wounds. He wants to be sure the risen one is the one who was crucified.

**2. Jesus knows what he said.** Eight days later Jesus comes again and speaks directly to Thomas: "Put your finger here, and see my hands; and put out your hand, and place it in my side. Do not disbelieve, but believe" (20:27). Jesus answers Thomas with his own words. His rebuke is gentle, and he first meets Thomas's need. "Do not disbelieve, but believe" is literally "do not become unbelieving, but believing."

**3. The summit of the book.** "Thomas answered him, 'My Lord and my God!'" (20:28). The text doesn't say Thomas actually reached out to touch. Seeing was enough. His confession is the high point of the whole book: the Prologue opened with "the Word was God" (1:1), and here a disciple calls Jesus "my God," the two forming bookends. The Greek is *ho kyrios mou kai ho theos mou*, and the text says Thomas "answered him," so this isn't an exclamation of surprise but a confession addressed to Jesus. Some scholars note that the emperor Domitian styled himself "our Lord and God" (his official letters used the title). If John's readers met these words in Domitian's day, they would feel their weight: the one who truly deserves that title isn't Caesar but the risen Jesus.

**4. Blessed are those who have not seen.** "Jesus said to him, 'Have you believed because you have seen me? Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed'" (20:29). The words are spoken to Thomas, but even more to every later reader. We haven't seen as Thomas did, but we aren't at a disadvantage: Jesus calls blessed those who believe without seeing.

> The point: John's Gospel begins with "the Word was God" and reaches its summit in Thomas's "My Lord and my God!" The road the whole book invites readers to walk is the road Thomas walked: from doubt to worship.

## Going Deeper: 20:30–31 The Purpose of the Book

"Now Jesus did many other signs in the presence of the disciples, which are not written in this book; but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:30–31). Right after Thomas's confession, John turns to the reader and states the purpose of the whole book. The introduction discussed the details: "believe" may mean "come to believe" or "go on believing," and Carson reads the sentence as "that you may believe that the Christ, the Son of God, is Jesus." Either way, by saying this right after Thomas, John is inviting us: Thomas believed because he saw; you can believe because of the testimony written in this book.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** The risen Lord called Mary by name and came with his wounds to meet Thomas's doubt. What doubt can I bring to him as honestly as Thomas did? Jesus told Mary to stop clinging to him. Am I holding on to some past way of experiencing the Lord, when he wants to lead me into a new relationship? Thomas said, "My Lord and my God!" How can I confess him that way today, not only with my mouth but with my life?
