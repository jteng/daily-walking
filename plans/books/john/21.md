## 本章脉络

第二十章以写书的目的作结，看起来全书已经完结了，第二十一章却像一段尾声。**提比哩亚海边的捕鱼**（1-14节）：整夜一无所获，耶稣叫他们把网撒在船的右边，网满了一百五十三条大鱼。**恢复彼得**（15-19节）：耶稣三次问「你爱我吗」。**那门徒的结局**（20-23节）：「与你何干？你跟从我吧！」**结语**（24-25节）：这门徒的见证是真的。

学者对这一章是原本就有，还是后来补上的，有不同看法。但现存所有的希腊文抄本都有第二十一章，它和全书的语言、主题都紧密相连：炭火、羊、「跟从我」、那门徒的见证。第二十章说复活的主对世界的意义（差遣、赦罪、多马的告白）；第二十一章说复活的主怎样对待一个失败的门徒。

## 深读 21:1-14 一百五十三条鱼

**1. 回到加利利。** 「西门彼得对他们说：『我打鱼去。』他们说：『我们也和你同去。』」（21:3）有人认为彼得这是放弃了，回到旧行业；也有人认为他们只是在等候主的时候，做他们熟悉的事。经文没有评断。但「那一夜并没有打着什么」，直到主开口。

**2. 似曾相识的一幕。** 耶稣在岸上说：「你们把网撒在船的右边，就必得着。」（21:6）路加福音5章记载，耶稣最初呼召彼得时，也有一次整夜徒劳、照着他的话撒网就网满了鱼的经历。复活的主用同样的方式，提醒彼得他最初是怎样被呼召的。

**3. 「是主！」** 那门徒先认出来：「是主！」彼得「就束上一件外衣，跳在海里」（21:7）。两个门徒，两种性格：一个先看见，一个先行动。

**4. 一百五十三条。** 「那网满了大鱼，共一百五十三条；鱼虽这样多，网却没有破。」（21:11）这个数字引起了很多解释。耶柔米说当时的希腊动物学家认为世上有一百五十三种鱼，象征福音要临到万族，但现存的古代资料并不支持这个说法；奥古斯丁注意到153是从1加到17的和，作了一些数字上的推想。最简单的解释是：这是一个目击者的细节，当时有人真的数了。约翰特别注明「网却没有破」，与路加福音5:6网险些裂开形成对比，许多解经家在这里看见一个象征：无论有多少人被带进来，主的网都容得下。

**5. 主预备的早饭。** 「他们上了岸，就看见那里有炭火，上面有鱼，又有饼。」（21:9）他们还没有把鱼拿上岸，耶稣已经预备好了。他又说：「把刚才打的鱼拿几条来。」（21:10）主不需要他们的鱼，却邀请他们把劳力的成果带来。

## 深读 21:15-17「你爱我吗？」

**1. 又是炭火。** 「炭火」（*anthrakia*）这个字在新约里只出现两次：18:18和21:9。彼得在大祭司院子里的炭火旁三次不认主；现在，在另一堆炭火旁，耶稣三次问他「你爱我吗」。主没有回避彼得的失败，而是在相似的场景中，温柔地医治了他的伤痛。

**2. 「约翰的儿子西门」。** 耶稣三次都称他「约翰的儿子西门」，而不是「彼得」（磐石）。这是1:42耶稣第一次见他时所用的称呼：「你是约翰的儿子西门，你要称为矶法。」耶稣好像带他回到起点，重新开始。

**3. 「比这些更深吗？」** 第一次的问题是：「你爱我比这些更深吗？」（21:15）「这些」可以指其他门徒（你爱我比他们爱我更深吗？），也可以指船、网、鱼这些东西。许多解经家倾向第一种：彼得曾经说过「我愿意为你舍命」（13:37），马可福音还记载他说「众人虽然跌倒，我总不能」（可14:29）。耶稣在问：你还认为自己比别人更爱我吗？彼得的回答没有再和别人比较，只说：「主啊，是的，你知道我爱你。」

**4. 两个「爱」字。** 前两次耶稣问「你爱我吗」用的是 *agapaō*，彼得三次回答用的都是 *phileō*；第三次耶稣也改用 *phileō*。有人认为这里有深意：*agapaō* 是更高的、舍己的爱，彼得不敢说他有，只敢说 *phileō*（朋友的爱），而耶稣第三次就「降到」彼得的层次来问他。但许多解经家（如卡森）认为这是约翰惯用的同义词交替：约翰在3:35用 *agapaō*、在5:20用 *phileō*，同样说「父爱子」；这段经文里的「喂养／牧养」、「小羊／羊」、「知道」（两个不同的希腊字）也都在交替。较稳妥的读法是：重点不在两个字的差别，而在问题被问了三次。

**5. 「就忧愁」。** 「彼得因为耶稣第三次对他说『你爱我吗』，就忧愁。」（21:17）第三次的问题碰到了他最痛的地方：三次不认主。他的回答也从「你知道我爱你」变成了「主啊，你是无所不知的；你知道我爱你」。他不再倚靠自己的表白，而是投靠主的全知：你知道我所有的失败，你也知道我真的爱你。

**6. 「你喂养我的羊」。** 每一次彼得回答，耶稣就给他一个托付：「你喂养我的小羊」、「你牧养我的羊」、「你喂养我的羊」。不认主的人，被托付去牧养主的羊。好牧人（10:11）把他的羊交给一个被恢复的罪人去照顾。彼得后来在彼得前书5:1-4劝勉众长老「牧养在你们中间神的群羊」，并称耶稣为「牧长」。注意耶稣说的是「**我的**羊」：羊始终属于主。

> 要点：耶稣恢复彼得，不是叫他忘记失败，而是在失败的地方重新问他「你爱我吗」，然后把羊交给他。服事的资格，不是从不失败，而是被主饶恕、并且爱他。

## 深读 21:18-19 伸出手来

**1. 预言彼得的死。** 「你年少的时候，自己束上带子，随意往来；但年老的时候，你要伸出手来，别人要把你束上，带你到不愿意去的地方。」（21:18）约翰解释：「耶稣说这话是指着彼得要怎样死，荣耀神。」（21:19）在古代文献中，「伸出手来」有时用来指钉十字架。革利免一书（约主后96年）已提到彼得殉道；后来特土良明说他在尼禄时期于罗马被钉十字架，优西比乌引俄利根说他是倒钉的。

**2. 实现了他的话。** 彼得曾说「我愿意为你舍命」（13:37），那时他做不到。现在耶稣告诉他：你将来真的会为我舍命，而且你的死要「荣耀神」，这正是约翰福音对耶稣自己的死的描写（12:23；13:31）；「要怎样死」这个说法，也正是12:33、18:32说耶稣自己的死所用的话。

**3. 「你跟从我吧！」** 这是耶稣对彼得最早的呼召（可1:17），也是他最后的命令；它也成就了13:36的应许：「后来却要跟我去」。从起点到终点，门徒的道路就是「跟从我」这三个字。

## 深读 21:20-25「与你何干？你跟从我吧！」

**1. 比较的试探。** 彼得转过来，看见那门徒跟着，就问：「主啊，这人将来如何？」（21:21）刚刚被恢复，他就开始和别人比较。

**2. 耶稣的回答。** 「我若要他等到我来的时候，与你何干？你跟从我吧！」（21:22）希腊文在最后一句加了一个强调的「你」（*sy*）：「**你**，跟从我！」每个门徒有各自的道路，彼得走向殉道，那门徒也许活到很老。主对每个人的安排不同，我们的责任不是比较，而是跟从。

**3. 更正一个谣言。** 「于是这话传在弟兄中间，说那门徒不死。其实，耶稣不是说他不死。」（21:23）这节经文显示，约翰写作的时候，早期教会中流传着那门徒会活到主再来的说法。约翰更正它：耶稣只是说「若我要他等……」。许多学者认为，这暗示那门徒年纪很大，或者已经去世，所以需要澄清。

**4. 见证是真的。** 「为这些事作见证，并且记载这些事的就是这门徒；我们也知道他的见证是真的。」（21:24）「我们」可能是那门徒所在的教会群体，为他的见证作保证。全书以见证开始（1:7，施洗约翰「为光作见证」），也以见证结束。

**5. 世界也容不下。** 「耶稣所行的事还有许多，若是一一地都写出来，我想，所写的书就是世界也容不下了。」（21:25）约翰福音以宇宙的尺度开始：「太初有道」；也以宇宙的尺度结束：这位道成肉身的主所做的，全世界的书都写不完。我们读完的这二十一章，只是他的一小部分，却足以叫我们信，并因信得生命。

## 默想

**默想：** 彼得在一堆炭火旁失败，又在另一堆炭火旁被恢复。我有没有一个「炭火」的地方，一个我失败、一直不敢再去面对的地方？主今天可能正在那里，问我「你爱我吗」。我是否常常像彼得一样，问「这人将来如何？」耶稣对我说：「与你何干？你跟从我吧！」读完约翰福音，我要怎样回应这位「太初有道」、又为我舍命复活的主？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 20 ended with the book's statement of purpose, as if the Gospel were finished, but chapter 21 follows like an epilogue. **Fishing by the Sea of Tiberias** (vv. 1–14): after a night of catching nothing, Jesus tells them to cast the net on the right side of the boat, and it fills with 153 large fish. **Peter restored** (vv. 15–19): Jesus asks three times, "Do you love me?" **What about the other disciple?** (vv. 20–23): "What is that to you? You follow me!" **The conclusion** (vv. 24–25): this disciple's testimony is true.

Scholars differ on whether this chapter was part of the original book or added later. But every surviving Greek manuscript includes chapter 21, and it is closely tied to the language and themes of the whole Gospel: the charcoal fire, the sheep, "follow me," the beloved disciple's witness. Chapter 20 shows what the risen Lord means for the world (sending, forgiveness, Thomas's confession); chapter 21 shows how the risen Lord treats a disciple who failed.

## Going Deeper: 21:1–14 One Hundred Fifty-Three Fish

**1. Back in Galilee.** "Simon Peter said to them, 'I am going fishing.' They said to him, 'We will go with you'" (21:3). Some think Peter was giving up and going back to his old trade; others think they were simply doing what they knew while waiting for the Lord. The text doesn't judge. But "that night they caught nothing," until the Lord spoke.

**2. A familiar scene.** From the shore Jesus says, "Cast the net on the right side of the boat, and you will find some" (21:6). Luke 5 records that when Jesus first called Peter, there was also a fruitless night followed by a net filled with fish at his word. The risen Lord does the same thing again, reminding Peter how he was first called.

**3. "It is the Lord!"** The beloved disciple recognizes him first: "It is the Lord!" Peter "put on his outer garment... and threw himself into the sea" (21:7). Two disciples, two temperaments: one sees first, one acts first.

**4. One hundred fifty-three.** "Full of large fish, 153 of them. And although there were so many, the net was not torn" (21:11). The number has drawn many interpretations. Jerome claimed Greek zoologists counted 153 kinds of fish, symbolizing the gospel reaching every people, but the surviving ancient sources don't support this. Augustine noted that 153 is the sum of the numbers 1 to 17 and built speculations on it. The simplest explanation is that it's an eyewitness detail: someone actually counted. John notes that "the net was not torn," in contrast to Luke 5:6, where the nets were breaking, and many interpreters see a symbol here: however many are brought in, the Lord's net holds.

**5. Breakfast the Lord prepared.** "When they got out on land, they saw a charcoal fire in place, with fish laid out on it, and bread" (21:9). Before they brought any of their catch ashore, Jesus had breakfast ready. Then he says, "Bring some of the fish that you have just caught" (21:10). The Lord doesn't need their fish, but he invites them to bring the fruit of their labor.

## Going Deeper: 21:15–17 "Do you love me?"

**1. Another charcoal fire.** "Charcoal fire" (*anthrakia*) appears only twice in the New Testament: 18:18 and 21:9. Beside the charcoal fire in the high priest's courtyard Peter denied the Lord three times; now, beside another charcoal fire, Jesus asks him three times, "Do you love me?" The Lord doesn't avoid Peter's failure; in a similar setting he gently heals it.

**2. "Simon, son of John."** All three times Jesus calls him "Simon, son of John," not "Peter" (the rock). It's what Jesus called him at their first meeting: "So you are Simon the son of John? You shall be called Cephas" (1:42). It's as if Jesus takes him back to the beginning to start again.

**3. "More than these?"** The first question is, "do you love me more than these?" (21:15). "These" could mean the other disciples (do you love me more than they do?) or the boat, nets and fish. Many interpreters favor the first: Peter had said, "I will lay down my life for you" (13:37), and Mark records him saying, "Even though they all fall away, I will not" (Mark 14:29). Jesus is asking: do you still think you love me more than the others? Peter's answer makes no comparison now: "Yes, Lord; you know that I love you."

**4. Two words for "love."** The first two times Jesus asks "do you love me," he uses *agapaō*; Peter answers all three times with *phileō*; the third time Jesus also switches to *phileō*. Some see deep meaning here: *agapaō* is the higher, self-giving love that Peter dares not claim, so he says only *phileō* (the love of friends), and the third time Jesus comes down to Peter's level. But many interpreters (Carson among them) see John's habit of alternating synonyms: John uses *agapaō* in 3:35 and *phileō* in 5:20 to say the same thing, "the Father loves the Son," and this passage also alternates "feed/tend," "lambs/sheep" and two Greek words for "know." The safer reading is that the point lies not in the difference between the two words but in the question being asked three times.

**5. "Peter was grieved."** "Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, 'Do you love me?'" (21:17). The third question touched his deepest wound: three denials. His answer changes from "you know that I love you" to "Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you." He no longer relies on his own declaration; he entrusts himself to the Lord's knowledge: you know all my failures, and you also know I truly love you.

**6. "Feed my sheep."** Each time Peter answers, Jesus gives him a charge: "Feed my lambs," "Tend my sheep," "Feed my sheep." The man who denied the Lord is entrusted with shepherding the Lord's sheep. The good shepherd (10:11) hands his flock to a restored sinner. Peter later urged the elders to "shepherd the flock of God that is among you" and called Jesus "the chief Shepherd" (1 Pet 5:1–4). Note that Jesus says "*my* sheep": the sheep always belong to the Lord.

> The point: Jesus doesn't restore Peter by making him forget his failure but by asking, in the very place of failure, "Do you love me?" and then entrusting him with the sheep. The qualification for service isn't never failing; it is being forgiven by the Lord and loving him.

## Going Deeper: 21:18–19 "You will stretch out your hands"

**1. Peter's death foretold.** "When you were young, you used to dress yourself and walk wherever you wanted, but when you are old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you and carry you where you do not want to go" (21:18). John explains: "This he said to show by what kind of death he was to glorify God" (21:19). In ancient writings, "stretching out the hands" was sometimes used of crucifixion. 1 Clement (c. AD 96) already refers to Peter's martyrdom; Tertullian later states he was crucified at Rome under Nero, and Eusebius, citing Origen, says he was crucified head-down.

**2. His words fulfilled.** Peter once said, "I will lay down my life for you" (13:37), and at the time he couldn't. Now Jesus tells him that one day he truly will, and that his death will "glorify God," the very way John describes Jesus' own death (12:23; 13:31), and "by what kind of death" repeats the wording used of Jesus' own death in 12:33 and 18:32.

**3. "Follow me."** This was Jesus' first call to Peter (Mark 1:17), and it is his last command; it also fulfills the promise of 13:36: "you will follow afterward." From beginning to end, the disciple's road is these two words.

## Going Deeper: 21:20–25 "What is that to you? You follow me!"

**1. The temptation to compare.** Peter turns, sees the beloved disciple following and asks, "Lord, what about this man?" (21:21). Just restored, he starts comparing himself with someone else.

**2. Jesus' answer.** "If it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? You follow me!" (21:22). The Greek adds an emphatic "you" (*sy*): "*You*, follow me!" Each disciple has a road of their own. Peter's leads to martyrdom; the other disciple's perhaps to old age. The Lord's plan for each is different. Our task isn't to compare but to follow.

**3. Correcting a rumor.** "So the saying spread abroad among the brothers that this disciple was not to die; yet Jesus did not say to him that he was not to die" (21:23). This verse shows that when John wrote, a rumor circulated in the early church that the beloved disciple would live until the Lord returned. John corrects it: Jesus only said "if it is my will that he remain..." Many scholars think this suggests the disciple was very old, or had already died, so the rumor needed clearing up.

**4. The testimony is true.** "This is the disciple who is bearing witness about these things, and who has written these things, and we know that his testimony is true" (21:24). "We" may be the church community around the beloved disciple, vouching for his testimony. The book begins with witness (1:7, John the Baptist "came as a witness, to bear witness about the light") and ends with witness.

**5. The world could not contain them.** "Now there are also many other things that Jesus did. Were every one of them to be written, I suppose that the world itself could not contain the books that would be written" (21:25). John's Gospel began on a cosmic scale, "In the beginning was the Word," and ends on one: all the books in the world could not record everything this incarnate Lord has done. The twenty-one chapters we've read are only a small part of it, yet enough for us to believe and, believing, to have life.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Peter failed beside one charcoal fire and was restored beside another. Do I have a "charcoal fire," a place where I failed and have never dared to face again? The Lord may be there today, asking, "Do you love me?" Am I often like Peter, asking "Lord, what about this man?" Jesus says to me, "What is that to you? You follow me!" Having read John's Gospel, how will I respond to the Lord who was in the beginning and who died and rose for me?
