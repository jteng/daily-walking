## 本章脉络

第一章可以分成四段。**感谢和祷告**（1-11节）：保罗每逢想念腓立比人就感谢神，因为他们从头一天就同心兴旺福音，他深信神必成全这善工；他求神使他们的爱心在知识和见识上多而又多。**捆锁叫福音兴旺**（12-18节）：保罗被囚，御营全军都知道他是为基督的缘故；有人出于嫉妒传基督，有人出于好意，无论怎样，基督被传开了，他就欢喜。**活着与死去**（19-26节）：保罗不知道自己会被释放还是被处死，但无论生死，他只要基督在他身上「照常显大」；「我活着就是基督，我死了就有益处」。**与福音相称**（27-30节）：腓立比人要同有一个心志，站立得稳，不怕敌人，因为为基督受苦也是神所赐的恩典。

全章的重心不在保罗自己的景况，而在福音和基督：福音兴旺了吗？基督被传开、被显大了吗？

## 深读 1:3-8 从头一天直到如今

**1. 带着喜乐的感谢。** 「我每逢想念你们，就感谢我的神；每逢为你们众人祈求的时候，常是欢欢喜喜地祈求」（1:3-4）。保罗在监里，想起腓立比人，第一个反应是感谢神。他不是先感谢腓立比人，而是感谢「我的神」，因为他们身上的好，都是神所做的。

**2. 同心兴旺福音。** 「因为从头一天直到如今，你们是同心合意地兴旺福音」（1:5）。「同心合意」原文是 *koinōnia*，意思是相交、有分（和合本在别处常译作「交通」），这是腓立比书的一个关键词（参2:1「交通」；3:10「一同受苦」；4:14-15）。腓立比人和保罗一同有分于福音：他们为他祷告，差人帮助他，供给他的需要（4:15-16），也和他一同为福音受苦（1:29-30）。福音事工不只是传道人的事，每一个信徒都可以有分。

**3. 神必成全。** 「我深信那在你们心里动了善工的，必成全这工，直到耶稣基督的日子」（1:6）。「你们」在这里是复数，保罗首先是对整个教会说的；但神在教会中的工作，也包括他在每一个信徒身上的工作。神开始的救恩，他必定完成（参罗8:29-30；约10:28-29；关于圣徒恒忍的不同理解，见导读）。我们的把握不在于自己抓得多紧，而在于神不放手。

**4. 基督耶稣的心肠。** 「我体会基督耶稣的心肠，切切地想念你们众人」（1:8）。「心肠」原文指人的内脏，古人以为情感从那里发出，所以这里指深切的爱，就像中文说的牵肠挂肚。保罗爱他们，不是凭自己天然的感情，而是带着基督的爱来爱。

> 要点：神开始了福音的工作，也必完成。我们与福音「有分」，是神的恩典；我们的把握，在于神不放手。

## 深读 1:9-11 爱心多而又多

**1. 有知识的爱。** 「我所祷告的，就是要你们的爱心在知识和各样见识上多而又多」（1:9）。保罗没有求神给腓立比人更多的知识来代替爱，也没有求神只给他们更多的热情而不要知识。他求的是一种有知识、有分辨力的爱。爱若没有知识，就容易被人利用，或者好心做错事；知识若没有爱，就叫人自高自大（林前8:1）。

**2. 能分别是非。** 「使你们能分别是非，作诚实无过的人，直到基督的日子」（1:10）。「分别是非」原文的意思是：经过试验，认可那更美的事。信徒不只是要避开明显的恶，也要在许多「都可以」的事中，分辨什么是最美、最讨神喜悦的。

**3. 靠着耶稣基督结果子。** 「并靠着耶稣基督结满了仁义的果子，叫荣耀称赞归与神」（1:11）。仁义的果子是「靠着耶稣基督」结出来的，不是靠我们自己。果子的目的，也不是叫我们得称赞，而是「叫荣耀称赞归与神」。

> 要点：保罗为腓立比人求的，不是顺利的日子，而是越来越深、越来越有分辨力的爱，并且这一切都要靠着基督，归荣耀给神。

## 深读 1:12-18 捆锁叫福音兴旺

**1. 所遭遇的事更是叫福音兴旺。** 「弟兄们，我愿意你们知道，我所遭遇的事更是叫福音兴旺」（1:12）。腓立比人大概担心保罗被囚，福音的工作就停下来了。保罗却告诉他们，正好相反。「兴旺」这个词原文的意思是进展、向前推进；保罗在1:25用同一个词说腓立比人在信心上的「长进」。捆锁没有拦住福音，反倒叫福音往前走。

**2. 御营全军都知道了。** 「以致我受的捆锁在御营全军和其余的人中，已经显明是为基督的缘故」（1:13）。「御营」可能指罗马皇帝的禁卫军（如果保罗在罗马），也可能指总督的府第（如果是在行省）。保罗大概日夜被兵丁看守（参徒28:16、20），一班一班的兵丁轮流看守他，也就一个一个地听见了福音。保罗的锁链，成了他的讲台。

**3. 弟兄们越发放胆。** 「并且那在主里的弟兄多半因我受的捆锁就笃信不疑，越发放胆传神的道，无所惧怕」（1:14）。人们看见保罗在锁链中仍然忠心，神仍然与他同在，自己就不那么怕了。

**4. 动机不纯的传道人。** 「有的传基督是出于嫉妒纷争，也有的是出于好意……这有何妨呢？或是假意，或是真心，无论怎样，基督究竟被传开了。为此，我就欢喜」（1:15-18）。
- **他们传的是真福音：** 这些人和3:2的假师傅不同。他们传的是基督，只是动机不好，想趁保罗被囚的时候抬高自己，「加增我捆锁的苦楚」（1:17）。对传「别的福音」的人，保罗从不让步（加1:8-9）；对传真福音、只是动机不纯的人，他却把评判交给神（参林前4:4-5）。
- **保罗不以自己为中心：** 保罗能够欢喜，是因为他看重的不是自己的名声，而是基督被传开。
- **不是说动机无所谓：** 保罗并没有称赞嫉妒纷争（参2:3），他只是不让别人的错，夺去他因基督而有的喜乐。

> 要点：神能使用锁链，也能使用不完全的人，叫福音兴旺。一个以基督为中心的人，就算自己受委屈，只要基督被传开，他仍然可以欢喜。

## 深读 1:19-26 活着就是基督，死了就有益处

**1. 「终必叫我得救」。** 「因为我知道，这事藉着你们的祈祷和耶稣基督之灵的帮助，终必叫我得救」（1:19）。「得救」这个词在这里可以有两种理解：
- **从监里得释放：** 保罗在1:25-26说他会再到腓立比人那里去，所以有些解经家认为这里指他会被释放。
- **在神面前得着最终的辩白与救恩：** 1:20紧接着说「无论是生是死」，可见保罗并不确定自己会活下来。许多解经家注意到，这句话几乎是逐字引用七十士译本（旧约希腊文译本）的约伯记13:16（和合本「这要成为我的拯救」），约伯在苦难中盼望的是神最后的辩白。按照这个理解，保罗的意思是：无论审判的结果怎样，神最终必为他伸冤，他在神面前不会蒙羞。
不论哪一种，保罗的把握都建立在信徒的祷告和「耶稣基督之灵」的帮助上，不是建立在罗马法庭上。

**2. 叫基督照常显大。** 「只要凡事放胆，无论是生是死，总叫基督在我身上照常显大」（1:20）。「显大」就像用放大镜，叫人看清楚基督有多么伟大。保罗无论生死都甘心，他在乎的是人从他身上看见基督有多么美好。

**3. 「我活着就是基督」。** 「因我活着就是基督，我死了就有益处」（1:21）。保罗活着的意义、内容和目的，都是基督：认识他，事奉他，爱他，叫他被显大。正因为活着是基督，死了才是「有益处」，因为死了就能更完全地得着基督。如果活着是为了钱财、名声或家人，死就只能是损失。

**4. 离世与基督同在。** 「我正在两难之间，情愿离世与基督同在，因为这是好得无比的」（1:23）。
- **信徒死后立刻与基督同在：** 保罗在别处用「睡了」形容信徒的死（帖前4:13；林前15:20），但这里清楚表明，那不是没有知觉地等候：离世就是「与基督同在」（参林后5:8；路23:43）。这是在身体复活以前的居间状态。
- **但这还不是最终的盼望：** 最终的盼望是主再来的时候，我们的身体复活、改变，「和他自己荣耀的身体相似」（3:21；参林前15:42-44）。
- **不是轻看今生：** 保罗说「好得无比」，并不是厌世。他也看见，留在世上「为你们更是要紧的」（1:24）。

**5. 为别人的益处留下来。** 「我既然这样深信，就知道仍要住在世间，且与你们众人同住，使你们在所信的道上又长进又喜乐」（1:25）。保罗愿意留下，不是因为他留恋今生，而是为了别人在信心上的益处。这里已经可以看见第二章的主题：「各人不要单顾自己的事，也要顾别人的事」（2:4）。

> 要点：一个人能说「死了就有益处」，是因为他能说「活着就是基督」。基督在哪里，哪里就是最好的；所以无论生死，信徒都在他手中。

## 深读 1:27-30 行事为人与福音相称

**1. 像天上国民那样生活。** 「只要你们行事为人与基督的福音相称」（1:27）。这里的「行事为人」原文和以弗所书常用的「行事为人」（行走）不同，是从城邦、公民这类词来的，意思是像公民那样过日子。腓立比城里许多人以自己是罗马公民为荣；保罗却提醒信徒，他们更是「天上的国民」（3:20），要活得配得上这个国籍。

**2. 同有一个心志，齐心努力。** 「知道你们同有一个心志，站立得稳，为所信的福音齐心努力」（1:27）。「齐心努力」原文的画面是运动员或士兵并肩奋斗（参4:3「一同劳苦」）。教会面对外面的压力，最需要的是里面的同心。

**3. 不怕敌人的惊吓。** 「凡事不怕敌人的惊吓，这是证明他们沉沦，你们得救都是出于神」（1:28）。信徒在逼迫中不惊慌，本身就是一个记号：敌人以为自己赢了，其实他们正走向沉沦；信徒看起来像输了，其实他们的得救「是出于神」。

**4. 受苦也是恩赐。** 「因为你们蒙恩，不但得以信服基督，并要为他受苦」（1:29）。「蒙恩」原文的词根就是「恩典」，意思是：这是神白白赐给你们的。信心是神所赐的，为基督受苦也是神所赐的。这不是说神喜欢看我们受苦，也不是说一切苦难都是为基督受的；而是说，为基督的名受苦，表明我们真是属于他的人，和基督、和众使徒走在同一条受苦的路上（1:30；参徒5:41；彼前4:13）。

> 要点：我们是天上的国民，要活出与福音相称的生活。信心和为基督受苦都是神所赐的恩典，所以在逼迫中，我们可以站稳，不必惊慌。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因我活着就是基督，我死了就有益处」（1:21）。如果要我诚实地填空：「我活着就是____」，我会填什么？也许是工作和事业，是一个安稳的家，是别人的认可，是退休以后的好日子。这些本身都不是坏事，可是当这些占了基督的位置，后面那句话就说不出口了：如果我活着是为了别人的认可，死了就什么都没有了；如果活着是为了事业，死了就是一切归零。所以我怕死，也怕失去；一旦这些东西受到威胁，我就紧紧抓住，对身边的人发脾气。保罗能说「死了就有益处」，不是因为他特别勇敢，而是因为基督已经为他死了又复活了（罗5:8；林前15:20）。基督已经替他受了罪的刑罚（林前15:3），所以死亡对他不再是刑罚，而是回家，「离世与基督同在」。今天，我能不能在神面前承认，有哪一样东西在我心里占了基督的位置，已经成了我心里的偶像？我不必一下子把这些都丢掉，却可以把它放回原位，交在主的手中，然后对他说：「主啊，我还不能完全说出『我活着就是基督』，可是你已经为我舍了自己。求你成为我活着的中心。」我还不能完全说出这句话，可是那为我舍命的基督，正是那「好得无比」的（1:23）；他值得我用一生去学会这句话。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 falls into four parts. **Thanksgiving and prayer** (vv. 1–11): whenever Paul remembers the Philippians he thanks God, because they have been partners in the gospel from the first day, and he is sure God will complete the good work; he prays that their love may abound more and more in knowledge and discernment. **Imprisonment advances the gospel** (vv. 12–18): Paul is a prisoner, and the whole imperial guard knows it is for Christ; some preach Christ from envy, others from good will, but in every way Christ is proclaimed, and Paul rejoices. **Living and dying** (vv. 19–26): Paul does not know whether he will be released or executed, but whether by life or by death he wants Christ to be "honored in my body"; "to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain." **Worthy of the gospel** (vv. 27–30): the Philippians are to stand firm in one spirit and not be frightened by opponents, because suffering for Christ is also a gift of God's grace.

The focus of the chapter is not Paul's circumstances but the gospel and Christ: is the gospel advancing? Is Christ being proclaimed and honored?

## Going Deeper: 1:3–8 From the First Day Until Now

**1. Thanks with joy.** "I thank my God in all my remembrance of you, always in every prayer of mine for you all making my prayer with joy" (1:3–4). In prison, Paul's first response when he thinks of the Philippians is to thank God. He thanks "my God" before he thanks the Philippians, because all the good in them is God's work.

**2. Partnership in the gospel.** "Because of your partnership in the gospel from the first day until now" (1:5). The Greek word is *koinōnia*, fellowship or sharing, a key word in Philippians (see 2:1, "participation"; 3:10, "share his sufferings"; 4:14–15). The Philippians shared in the gospel with Paul: they prayed for him, sent people to help him, supplied his needs (4:15–16), and suffered with him for the gospel (1:29–30). Gospel work is not only for preachers; every believer can share in it.

**3. God will complete it.** "And I am sure of this, that he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ" (1:6). "You" here is plural; Paul speaks first to the whole church, but God's work in the church includes his work in each believer. The salvation God begins, he will surely finish (see Rom 8:29–30; John 10:28–29; on differing views of perseverance, see the introduction). Our confidence does not lie in how tightly we hold on, but in God's not letting go.

**4. The affection of Christ Jesus.** "For God is my witness, how I yearn for you all with the affection of Christ Jesus" (1:8). "Affection" translates a word for the inward parts, which the ancients took as the seat of the emotions; it means deep love. Paul loves them not with mere natural feeling but with the love of Christ.

> The point: God began the work of the gospel, and he will complete it. Our share in the gospel is God's grace; our confidence lies in his not letting go.

## Going Deeper: 1:9–11 Love That Abounds More and More

**1. Love with knowledge.** "And it is my prayer that your love may abound more and more, with knowledge and all discernment" (1:9). Paul does not ask God to give the Philippians more knowledge in place of love, nor more fervor without knowledge. He asks for a love that knows and discerns. Love without knowledge is easily exploited or does harm with good intentions; knowledge without love "puffs up" (1 Cor 8:1).

**2. Approving what is excellent.** "So that you may approve what is excellent, and so be pure and blameless for the day of Christ" (1:10). The phrase means to test and approve what is better. Believers are not only to avoid obvious evil, but among many things that are "permitted," to discern what is best and most pleasing to God.

**3. Fruit through Jesus Christ.** "Filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God" (1:11). The fruit of righteousness "comes through Jesus Christ," not through our own strength. And its purpose is not our praise but "the glory and praise of God."

> The point: What Paul asks for the Philippians is not an easier life but a love that grows ever deeper and more discerning, all of it through Christ and to the glory of God.

## Going Deeper: 1:12–18 Imprisonment That Advances the Gospel

**1. What has happened has advanced the gospel.** "I want you to know, brothers, that what has happened to me has really served to advance the gospel" (1:12). The Philippians probably feared that with Paul in prison, the gospel work had stopped. Paul tells them the opposite. The word means "progress"; Paul uses it again in 1:25 for the Philippians' "progress" in the faith. The chains did not stop the gospel; they moved it forward.

**2. The whole imperial guard knows.** "So that it has become known throughout the whole imperial guard and to all the rest that my imprisonment is for Christ" (1:13). The "imperial guard" (*praetorium*) may mean the emperor's Praetorian Guard (if Paul was in Rome) or a governor's headquarters (if he was in a province). Paul was probably guarded by soldiers day and night (see Acts 28:16, 20); as shift after shift guarded him, soldier after soldier heard the gospel. Paul's chain became his pulpit.

**3. The brothers are bolder.** "And most of the brothers, having become confident in the Lord by my imprisonment, are much more bold to speak the word without fear" (1:14). When people saw Paul faithful in chains and God still with him, they became less afraid themselves.

**4. Preachers with mixed motives.** "Some indeed preach Christ from envy and rivalry, but others from good will… What then? Only that in every way, whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed, and in that I rejoice" (1:15–18).
- **They preached the true gospel:** these people were different from the false teachers of 3:2. They preached Christ, but from bad motives, trying to raise themselves up while Paul was in prison, "thinking to afflict me in my imprisonment" (1:17). Paul never gave way to those who preached "a different gospel" (Gal 1:8–9), but those who preached the true gospel with impure motives he left to God's judgment (see 1 Cor 4:4–5).
- **Paul did not make himself the center:** Paul could rejoice because what mattered to him was not his own reputation but that Christ was proclaimed.
- **Motives still matter:** Paul did not praise envy and rivalry (see 2:3); he simply refused to let other people's wrong take away his joy in Christ.

> The point: God can use chains, and he can use imperfect people, to advance the gospel. A person whose center is Christ can rejoice even when wronged, so long as Christ is proclaimed.

## Going Deeper: 1:19–26 To Live Is Christ, to Die Is Gain

**1. "This will turn out for my deliverance."** "For I know that through your prayers and the help of the Spirit of Jesus Christ this will turn out for my deliverance" (1:19). The word translated "deliverance" (*sōtēria*, usually "salvation") can be understood in two ways:
- **Release from prison:** in 1:25–26 Paul says he will come to the Philippians again, so some interpreters take this to mean his release.
- **Final vindication and salvation before God:** 1:20 goes straight on to say "whether by life or by death," so Paul was not sure he would live. Many interpreters note that the words "this will turn out for my deliverance" are almost exactly Job 13:16 in the Septuagint (the Greek Old Testament), where Job in his suffering hopes for God's final vindication. On this reading Paul means: whatever the court decides, God will finally vindicate him, and he will not be put to shame before God.
Either way, Paul's confidence rests on the prayers of believers and "the help of the Spirit of Jesus Christ," not on a Roman court.

**2. That Christ may be honored.** "As it is my eager expectation and hope that I will not be at all ashamed, but that with full courage now as always Christ will be honored in my body, whether by life or by death" (1:20). The word translated "honored" means "made great," like a magnifying glass that helps people see how great Christ is. Paul is content either way; what he cares about is that people see from his life how glorious Christ is.

**3. "To me to live is Christ."** "For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain" (1:21). For Paul the meaning, content and goal of life is Christ: knowing him, serving him, loving him, making him great. Only because to live is Christ can to die be "gain," for in death he gains Christ more fully. If life is for money, reputation or family, death can only be loss.

**4. To depart and be with Christ.** "My desire is to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better" (1:23).
- **Believers are with Christ at once when they die:** Paul elsewhere calls believers' death "sleep" (1 Thess 4:13; 1 Cor 15:20), but here he makes clear that this is not an unconscious waiting: to depart is to "be with Christ" (see 2 Cor 5:8; Luke 23:43). This is the "intermediate state" before the resurrection of the body.
- **This is not yet the final hope:** the final hope is that when the Lord comes, our bodies will be raised and transformed "to be like his glorious body" (3:21; see 1 Cor 15:42–44).
- **Not a contempt for this life:** "far better" is not world-weariness. Paul also sees that "to remain in the flesh is more necessary on your account" (1:24).

**5. Staying for others' good.** "Convinced of this, I know that I will remain and continue with you all, for your progress and joy in the faith" (1:25). Paul is willing to stay, not because he clings to this life but for others' good in the faith. Here we already see the theme of chapter 2: "Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others" (2:4).

> The point: A person can say "to die is gain" because he can say "to live is Christ." Wherever Christ is, that is best; so in life and in death the believer is in his hands.

## Going Deeper: 1:27–30 A Life Worthy of the Gospel

**1. Living as citizens of heaven.** "Only let your manner of life be worthy of the gospel of Christ" (1:27). The verb here is not the "walk" Ephesians uses so often; it is a word related to "citizen" and "city," meaning to live as a citizen. Many in Philippi were proud of their Roman citizenship, but Paul reminds believers that "our citizenship is in heaven" (3:20), and they are to live in a way that fits that citizenship.

**2. One spirit, side by side.** "That you are standing firm in one spirit, with one mind striving side by side for the faith of the gospel" (1:27). "Striving side by side" is a word picturing athletes or soldiers contending together (see 4:3, "labored side by side"). When the church faces pressure from outside, what it needs most is unity within.

**3. Not frightened by opponents.** "And not frightened in anything by your opponents. This is a clear sign to them of their destruction, but of your salvation, and that from God" (1:28). Believers who are not thrown into panic by persecution are themselves a sign: the opponents think they have won but are heading for destruction; believers look as if they are losing, but their salvation is "from God."

**4. Suffering is also a gift.** "For it has been granted to you that for the sake of Christ you should not only believe in him but also suffer for his sake" (1:29). "Granted" comes from the root of the word "grace": this is God's free gift to you. Faith is God's gift, and so is suffering for Christ. This does not mean God enjoys watching us suffer, nor that all suffering is suffering for Christ. It means that suffering for Christ's name shows we truly belong to him and share the experience of Christ himself and of the apostles (1:30; see Acts 5:41; 1 Pet 4:13).

> The point: We are citizens of heaven and are to live lives worthy of the gospel. Faith and suffering for Christ are both gifts of God's grace, so under pressure we can stand firm without panic.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain" (1:21). If I honestly filled in the blank, "For me to live is ____," what would I write? Perhaps my work and career, a secure home, other people's approval, a good retirement. None of these is bad in itself, but when one of them takes Christ's place, I cannot say the second half of the verse. If to live is other people's approval, at death there is nothing left; if to live is my career, at death everything goes to zero. So I fear death and I fear loss; when these things are threatened I clutch them tighter and snap at the people around me. Paul could say "to die is gain," not because he was especially brave, but because Christ had already died for him and risen again (Rom 5:8; 1 Cor 15:20). Christ had borne the punishment for his sins in his place (1 Cor 15:3), so death was no longer punishment for him but going home, "to depart and be with Christ." Today, can I admit before God which thing in my heart has taken Christ's place and become an idol? I do not need to throw it away all at once, but I can put it back where it belongs, in the Lord's hands, and say to him: "Lord, I cannot yet fully say 'to live is Christ,' but you have already given yourself for me. Be the center of my life." I cannot yet fully say these words, but the Christ who gave himself for me is the one who is "far better" (1:23), and he is worth a lifetime of learning to say them.
