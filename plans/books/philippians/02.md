## 本章脉络

第二章有四段。**同心与谦卑**（1-4节）：既然在基督里有劝勉、安慰、圣灵的交通，信徒就要意念相同，不可结党，各人看别人比自己强。**基督的虚己与升高**（5-11节）：基督本有神的形象，却虚己，取了奴仆的形象，顺服以至于死；所以神将他升为至高，万膝都要向他跪拜。**做成得救的工夫**（12-18节）：信徒要恐惧战兢做成得救的工夫，因为是神在他们心里运行；不发怨言，在弯曲悖谬的世代中好像明光照耀；保罗甘心被浇奠在他们信心的祭物上。**两位同工**（19-30节）：提摩太真实挂念他们的事；以巴弗提为基督的工作几乎至死。

5-11节是全章的心脏。前面的劝勉（谦卑、顾别人的事）和后面的两个榜样（提摩太、以巴弗提），都是从基督的心流出来的。

## 深读 2:1-4 各人看别人比自己强

**1. 劝勉的根基。** 「所以，在基督里若有什么劝勉，爱心有什么安慰，圣灵有什么交通，心中有什么慈悲怜悯」（2:1）。这里的「若」不是怀疑，而是「既然」的意思：在基督里确实有劝勉，确实有安慰，确实有圣灵的交通。保罗先提醒他们已经领受了什么，然后才说「你们就要……」（2:2）。

**2. 合一的心。** 「你们就要意念相同，爱心相同，有一样的心思，有一样的意念，使我的喜乐可以满足」（2:2）。保罗一句话里用了四个说法来讲同心。他不是要每个人意见都一样，而是要大家有同一个方向、同一种爱，就是以基督为中心。

**3. 两个病根。** 「凡事不可结党，不可贪图虚浮的荣耀」（2:3）。
- **「结党」：** 原文指为了自己的利益拉帮结派、争取地位（1:17用的也是这个词）。
- **「虚浮的荣耀」：** 原文就是空的荣耀，指追求别人眼中的面子和地位，其实里面是空的。
这两样东西最能拆毁教会：一样叫人分党，一样叫人比较。

**4. 谦卑与关心别人。** 「只要存心谦卑，各人看别人比自己强。各人不要单顾自己的事，也要顾别人的事」（2:3-4）。
- **「看别人比自己强」：** 不是说要假装别人在每一方面都比我好，也不是叫我否定神给我的恩赐，而是在心里把别人的需要和益处放在自己前面。
- **「也要顾」：** 保罗没有说不可以顾自己的事，而是说「不要单顾」。照顾自己并没有错，错在只顾自己。
- **这不是叫人任人欺压：** 「看别人比自己强」不是叫受虐待、被控制的人继续忍受，也不是叫教会为了「和睦」而掩盖罪恶。基督谦卑，却从来没有和罪妥协。遇到虐待或犯罪的事，向警方或法庭举报是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必和仍不安全的人和好；教会也不可用「家丑不可外扬」为理由遮掩。

> 要点：信徒能够谦卑、顾别人的事，是因为在基督里已经领受了劝勉、安慰和爱。我们不是靠着空的荣耀活着，而是靠着基督所给的满足。

## 深读 2:5-8 他反倒虚己

**1. 以基督的心为心。** 「你们当以基督耶稣的心为心」（2:5）。这句话原文可以有两种理解：一种是：你们要有基督耶稣那样的心，把基督当作榜样；另一种是：你们彼此之间要存这样的心，就是你们在基督耶稣里本该有的心，强调我们已经与基督联合。两种理解并不冲突：基督是我们的榜样，但他首先是我们的救主；我们效法他，是因为我们已经在他里面（参2:1）。

**2. 一首关于基督的诗。** 2:6-11的句子很有节奏，所以许多学者认为这是一首早期教会的诗歌，保罗把它引用在这里；也有学者（如戈登·费）认为这是保罗自己写的，是一段很有诗意的散文。无论是哪一种，这段经文都是新约中关于基督最重要的经文之一。

**3. 「本有神的形象」。** 「他本有神的形象，不以自己与神同等为强夺的」（2:6）。
- **「本有」：** 原文是现在分词，意思是他一直是、本来就是。基督在降生以先就已经存在，他不是后来才变成神的。
- **「神的形象」：** 这里的「形象」不只是外表，而是一个人真实的本质显露出来的样子。基督「本有神的形象」，就是说他真是神，与父「同等」（参约1:1；5:18；西2:9）。
- **「强夺的」：** 这个词的意思学者有不同理解。有人理解为要抓住不放的东西：他没有紧紧抓住与神同等的地位不放。也有许多学者（如罗伊·胡佛、N. T. 赖特）认为意思是拿来为自己谋利益的东西：他本来就与神同等，却没有利用这个地位为自己打算。两种理解都说明：基督没有把他的神性当作为自己争取好处的工具。

**4. 「反倒虚己」。** 「反倒虚己，取了奴仆的形象，成为人的样式」（2:7）。
- **虚己不是放弃神性：** 「虚己」原文的意思是倒空自己。十九世纪有些神学家（如德国的戈特弗里德·托马修斯、英国的查尔斯·戈尔）认为基督降生时放下了全知、全能等神的属性，这叫「虚己论」。但这段经文没有说他倒空了什么，而是说他怎样倒空自己：就是「取了奴仆的形象，成为人的样式」。他没有减少什么，而是加上了人性；他仍然是神，同时成为真正的人（参约1:14；西2:9）。
- **「奴仆的形象」：** 和上一节的「神的形象」对照。那位本有神的形象的，取了奴仆的形象。他不只是成为人，而且是成为最低微的人。

**5. 「以至于死，且死在十字架上」。** 「既有人的样子，就自己卑微，存心顺服，以至于死，且死在十字架上」（2:8）。
- **一步一步往下走：** 从神的形象到奴仆的形象，从人的样式到死，从死到十字架上的死。
- **十字架是最羞辱的死：** 在罗马世界，钉十字架是给奴隶和叛乱者的刑罚，罗马公民一般不会被钉十字架。腓立比人以罗马公民为荣，保罗却告诉他们，他们的主死在奴隶的刑具上。
- **他是为我们死的：** 这段经文的重点是基督的谦卑，但我们不可忘记他为什么要死。他「担当多人的罪」（赛53:12），神「使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义」（林后5:21）。他的顺服不只是给我们的榜样，更是替我们成就的救恩。
- **子的顺服不是说他比父小：** 子顺服父，是他在救赎中甘心担当的职分；他「与神同等」，本质上并不比父小。父、子、圣灵是独一的神，同尊同荣。

> 要点：基督本有神的形象，却甘心一路往下走，直到十字架。他不是失去了什么，而是为了我们舍弃了一切。我们的谦卑，是从他的谦卑流出来的。

## 深读 2:9-11 神将他升为至高

**1. 「所以」。** 「所以，神将他升为至高，又赐给他那超乎万名之上的名」（2:9）。前面是基督自己往下走，这里是神把他升上来。
- **不是说基督靠着顺服才成为神：** 他「本有神的形象」，从来就是神。这里说的是降卑的那一位，以神人的身份被高举，坐在父的右边（参徒2:33、36）。
- **「超乎万名之上的名」：** 从2:11来看，这个名就是「主」。七十士译本（旧约希腊文译本）用「主」这个词来翻译神的名「耶和华」。

**2. 万膝要跪拜。** 「叫一切在天上的、地上的，和地底下的，因耶稣的名无不屈膝，无不口称『耶稣基督为主』，使荣耀归与父神」（2:10-11）。这是引用以赛亚书45:23，神说：「万膝必向我跪拜；万口必凭我起誓。」在以赛亚书里，这是独一真神耶和华说的话；保罗把它用在耶稣身上。这表明耶稣与父同享独一神的尊荣，而且这样做「使荣耀归与父神」，不是与父争荣耀。

**3. 有一天，每一个人都要承认。** 「一切在天上的、地上的，和地底下的」：天使、活着的人、死了的人，连邪灵也包括在内，都要承认耶稣是主。这不是说所有人最后都会得救（参1:28；3:19），而是说所有人最后都要承认他的主权：有人是欢喜地敬拜，有人是被迫地降服。今天还是恩典的日子，现在就承认他为主的人，将来就会欢喜地跪拜。

> 要点：神高举那位为我们降卑到十字架上的耶稣，使他成为万主之主。今天在他面前屈膝，是喜乐；将来每一个膝头都要屈下。

## 深读 2:12-18 神在你们心里运行

**1. 做成，不是赚取。** 「就当恐惧战兢做成你们得救的工夫」（2:12）。保罗不是说要靠行为得救（参3:9；弗2:8-9），而是说要把已经领受的救恩活出来，一直做到完成。
- **对象是谁：** 「你们」是复数。有学者（如杰拉尔德·霍桑）强调这是对整个教会说的，是教会群体的健康和合一；更多的解经家认为这也包括每个人在成圣上的长进。两者都在经文里。
- **「恐惧战兢」：** 不是怕神随时把我们丢掉，而是在圣洁的神面前存敬畏的心，认真对待罪和顺服。

**2. 是神在你们心里运行。** 「因为你们立志行事都是神在你们心里运行，为要成就他的美意」（2:13）。这是12节的根基。我们之所以能「做成」，是因为神在我们里面「运行」：不只是帮助我们去做，连「立志」，就是想要做的心，也是神给的。这不叫我们被动，反而叫我们有盼望：我们努力，是因为神先在我们里面工作。

**3. 不发怨言。** 「凡所行的，都不要发怨言，起争论」（2:14）。「发怨言」这个词让人想起以色列人在旷野向摩西、亚伦发怨言（出16:7；民14:2；参林前10:10）。他们蒙了拯救，却抱怨神的带领。
- **怨言和哀诉不一样：** 诗篇里有许多向神倾心吐意的哀诉（诗62:8），那是带着信心来到神面前。怨言却是在背后埋怨，不信神的美意。
- **不是要人对不义沉默：** 不发怨言，不是叫人看见罪恶、受了伤害也一声不吭。正当地说出问题、寻求帮助，和背后发怨言是两回事。

**4. 好像明光照耀。** 「使你们无可指摘，诚实无伪，在这弯曲悖谬的世代作神无瑕疵的儿女。你们显在这世代中，好像明光照耀，将生命的道表明出来」（2:15-16）。「弯曲悖谬的世代」出自申命记32:5，那里说的是不忠的以色列；如今，神的儿女是在这样的世代中发光。我们发光，不是靠自己的光，而是「将生命的道表明出来」。

**5. 被浇奠也是喜乐。** 「我以你们的信心为供献的祭物，我若被浇奠在其上，也是喜乐」（2:17）。旧约献祭时，有时要把酒浇在祭物上作为「奠祭」（民28:7）。保罗把腓立比人的信心比作祭物，把自己可能的殉道比作浇在上面的酒。他把自己的生命看作一点点的酒，倒在别人的信心上，而且以此为喜乐（参提后4:6）。

> 要点：我们能努力活出救恩，是因为神先在我们心里运行。信徒不靠自己发光，而是在弯曲的世代中，把生命的道表明出来。

## 深读 2:19-30 提摩太和以巴弗提

**1. 提摩太：真实挂念别人。** 「因为我没有别人与我同心，实在挂念你们的事。别人都求自己的事，并不求耶稣基督的事」（2:20-21）。提摩太就是2:4的活例子：他不单顾自己的事，也顾别人的事。保罗说他「待我像儿子待父亲一样」（2:22），他是在长期一同事奉中被证明的人。

**2. 以巴弗提：几乎至死。** 以巴弗提是腓立比教会差来、把馈送带给保罗的人（2:25；4:18）。他「实在是病了，几乎要死；然而神怜恤他」（2:27）。
- **保罗没有隐藏忧愁：** 保罗说，神怜恤以巴弗提，「也怜恤我，免得我忧上加忧」（2:27）。使徒也会担心，也会忧愁；信心不是没有眼泪。
- **神并不总是医治：** 保罗没有说他「宣告」以巴弗提得医治。神怜恤他，是神的恩典；保罗在别处也留下了生病的同工（提后4:20）。
- **「不顾性命」：** 「因他为做基督的工夫，几乎至死，不顾性命」（2:30）。「不顾性命」原文是一个赌博用语，意思是拿生命去冒险。他把自己的生命押在基督的工作上。

**3. 尊重这样的人。** 「你们要在主里欢欢乐乐地接待他，而且要尊重这样的人」（2:29）。以巴弗提不是使徒，也没有什么出名的讲道，他只是一个忠心送东西、照顾人的弟兄。保罗却说，教会要尊重这样的人。

> 要点：提摩太和以巴弗提是活出基督之心的普通人：一个真心挂念别人，一个为基督的工作把性命置之度外。神看重的，正是这样的人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他本有神的形象，不以自己与神同等为强夺的；反倒虚己，取了奴仆的形象」（2:6-7）。我最常在什么时候觉得「吃亏」？也许是在单位里，功劳被别人拿走了；也许是在家里，我做得最多，却没有人看见；也许是在教会里，我服事了多年，新来的人一下子就被选上了。我心里会说：「凭什么是我让？」于是我开始计算，开始埋怨，开始和人比较。基督却是那位最有资格说「凭什么」的。他本来与神同等，却甘心取了奴仆的形象，一路走到十字架上。他在十字架上吃的亏，是替我吃的：我的罪，他担了；我该受的羞辱，他受了。在他面前，我才看见自己不是吃亏的人，而是白白得了一切的人。（这不是叫我在虐待和不公面前一声不吭；那样的事要说出来，要寻求帮助。）今天，我能不能把那件让我觉得「吃亏」的事带到主面前，先向他承认我心里的计算和埋怨，然后求他让我看见，我在他里面已经拥有的有多丰富？神高举了那位甘心降卑的基督，他也看见我在暗中所做的一切。我不必自己去争那份荣耀，因为基督已经把他的一切给了我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 has four parts. **Unity and humility** (vv. 1–4): since there is encouragement in Christ, comfort from love and participation in the Spirit, believers are to be of the same mind, do nothing from rivalry, and count others more significant than themselves. **Christ's self-emptying and exaltation** (vv. 5–11): though in the form of God, Christ made himself nothing, took the form of a servant, and was obedient to the point of death; therefore God highly exalted him, and every knee will bow to him. **Working out salvation** (vv. 12–18): believers are to work out their salvation with fear and trembling, because God works in them; without grumbling they shine as lights in a crooked and twisted generation; Paul is glad to be poured out on the offering of their faith. **Two fellow workers** (vv. 19–30): Timothy is genuinely concerned for them; Epaphroditus nearly died for the work of Christ.

Verses 5–11 are the heart of the chapter. The exhortations before them (humility, looking to others' interests) and the two examples after them (Timothy, Epaphroditus) flow from the mind of Christ.

## Going Deeper: 2:1–4 Count Others More Significant

**1. The ground of the appeal.** "So if there is any encouragement in Christ, any comfort from love, any participation in the Spirit, any affection and sympathy" (2:1). The "if" does not express doubt; it means "since": there really is encouragement in Christ, comfort, participation in the Spirit. Paul first reminds them of what they have received, and only then says "complete my joy by…" (2:2).

**2. A united mind.** "Complete my joy by being of the same mind, having the same love, being in full accord and of one mind" (2:2). In one sentence Paul uses four expressions for unity. He does not want everyone to hold the same opinions, but to share one direction and one love, with Christ at the center.

**3. Two roots of sickness.** "Do nothing from rivalry or conceit" (2:3).
- **"Rivalry":** forming factions and seeking position for one's own interest (the same word as in 1:17).
- **"Conceit":** literally "empty glory," chasing face and status in others' eyes while being empty within.
These two things do more than anything to tear a church apart: one divides people into parties, the other sets them comparing.

**4. Humility and care for others.** "But in humility count others more significant than yourselves. Let each of you look not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others" (2:3–4).
- **"Count others more significant":** this does not mean pretending others are better than me in every way, nor denying the gifts God has given me. It means that in my heart I put others' needs and good ahead of my own.
- **"Not only":** Paul does not forbid looking after one's own interests; he says "not only." Caring for oneself is not wrong; caring only for oneself is.
- **This is not a call to accept abuse:** "count others more significant" does not tell someone who is abused or controlled to keep enduring it, nor tell a church to cover up sin for the sake of "peace." Christ was humble, yet he never made peace with sin. Reporting abuse or crime to the police or courts is right (Rom 13:4). Victims need not stay in danger or reconcile with someone still unsafe. And the church must not hide abuse to save face.

> The point: Believers can be humble and look to others' interests because in Christ they have already received encouragement, comfort and love. We do not live on empty glory but on the fullness Christ gives.

## Going Deeper: 2:5–8 He Made Himself Nothing

**1. Have this mind.** "Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus" (2:5). The Greek can be read in two ways: "have the same mind that Christ Jesus had," taking Christ as our example; or "have this mind among yourselves, the mind that is yours in Christ Jesus," stressing our union with him. The two do not conflict. Christ is our example, but first he is our Savior; we imitate him because we are already in him (see 2:1).

**2. A poem about Christ.** The lines of 2:6–11 have a marked rhythm, so many scholars think this is an early Christian hymn that Paul quotes here. Others (such as Gordon Fee) think Paul wrote it himself as exalted, poetic prose. Either way, it is one of the most important passages about Christ in the New Testament.

**3. "In the form of God."** "Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped" (2:6).
- **"Though he was":** the Greek is a present participle: he was always, by nature, in the form of God. Christ existed before he was born; he did not become God later.
- **"The form of God":** "form" here is not mere outward appearance but the way a thing's true nature shows itself. Christ being "in the form of God" means that he is truly God, with "equality with God" (see John 1:1; 5:18; Col 2:9).
- **"A thing to be grasped":** scholars understand this word in different ways. Some take it as "something to hold on to": he did not cling to his equality with God. Many others (such as Roy Hoover and N. T. Wright) take it as "something to use for his own advantage": he was already equal with God but did not use that status to serve himself. Either way, Christ did not treat his deity as a tool for getting things for himself.

**4. "He made himself nothing."** "But made himself nothing, taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men" (2:7).
- **Self-emptying is not giving up deity:** the verb means "emptied himself." Some nineteenth-century theologians (such as Gottfried Thomasius in Germany and Charles Gore in England) held that at the incarnation Christ laid aside divine attributes such as omniscience and omnipotence; this is called "kenotic theology." But the passage does not say what he emptied himself of. It says how he emptied himself: by "taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men." He did not subtract anything; he added a human nature. He remained God and at the same time became truly man (see John 1:14; Col 2:9).
- **"The form of a servant":** set against "the form of God" in the previous verse. The one who was in the form of God took the form of a slave. He became not only a man, but the lowest of men.

**5. "To the point of death, even death on a cross."** "And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross" (2:7–8).
- **Step by step downward:** from the form of God to the form of a servant, from human likeness to death, from death to death on a cross.
- **The cross was the most shameful death:** in the Roman world crucifixion was for slaves and rebels; Roman citizens were not normally crucified. The Philippians were proud to be Roman citizens, yet Paul tells them their Lord died on a slave's instrument of execution.
- **He died for us:** the passage stresses Christ's humility, but we must not forget why he died. "He bore the sin of many" (Isa 53:12); "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (2 Cor 5:21). His obedience is not only an example for us; it is the salvation he accomplished in our place.
- **The Son's obedience does not make him less than the Father:** the Son's obedience to the Father is the role he willingly took in redemption; he has "equality with God" and is not lesser in nature. Father, Son and Holy Spirit are the one God, equal in power and glory.

> The point: Christ was in the form of God, yet he willingly went all the way down to the cross. He did not lose anything; he gave up everything for us. Our humility flows from his.

## Going Deeper: 2:9–11 God Highly Exalted Him

**1. "Therefore."** "Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every name" (2:9). Before, Christ himself went down; here, God lifts him up.
- **Christ did not become God by obeying:** he was "in the form of God" and always God. What is said here is that the one who humbled himself was exalted as the God-man and seated at the Father's right hand (see Acts 2:33, 36).
- **"The name that is above every name":** from 2:11, the name is "Lord." The Septuagint (the Greek Old Testament) uses this word, *kyrios*, to translate God's name, the LORD (YHWH).

**2. Every knee will bow.** "So that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father" (2:10–11). This quotes Isaiah 45:23, where God says, "To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear allegiance." In Isaiah these are the words of the one true God, the LORD; Paul applies them to Jesus. This shows that Jesus shares the honor of the one God, and he does so "to the glory of God the Father," not in rivalry with him.

**3. One day everyone will confess.** "In heaven and on earth and under the earth": angels, the living, the dead, even evil spirits will confess that Jesus is Lord. This does not mean that everyone will finally be saved (see 1:28; 3:19), but that everyone will finally acknowledge his lordship: some in joyful worship, some in forced submission. Today is still the day of grace; those who confess him as Lord now will bow with joy then.

> The point: God has exalted Jesus, who humbled himself to the cross for us, and made him Lord of all. To bow before him today is joy; one day every knee will bow.

## Going Deeper: 2:12–18 God Works in You

**1. Work out, not earn.** "Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" (2:12). Paul does not mean we are saved by works (see 3:9; Eph 2:8–9) but that we are to live out the salvation we have received, all the way to its completion.
- **Who is addressed:** "you" is plural. Some scholars (such as Gerald Hawthorne) stress that Paul speaks to the whole church about its health and unity; more interpreters hold that it also includes each person's growth in holiness. Both are in the text.
- **"With fear and trembling":** not fear that God might cast us off at any moment, but reverence before a holy God, taking sin and obedience seriously.

**2. God works in you.** "For it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure" (2:13). This is the ground of verse 12. We can "work out" because God "works in" us; he not only helps us act, he gives even the "will," the desire to act. This does not make us passive; it gives us hope. We work because God is already at work in us.

**3. Without grumbling.** "Do all things without grumbling or questioning" (2:14). "Grumbling" recalls Israel grumbling against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness (Exod 16:7; Num 14:2; see 1 Cor 10:10). They had been rescued, yet they complained about God's leading.
- **Grumbling is not lament:** the Psalms are full of laments that pour out the heart to God (Ps 62:8); that is coming to God in faith. Grumbling complains behind his back and does not trust his good purpose.
- **Not a call to silence about wrong:** to stop grumbling is not to say nothing when we see sin or are harmed. Naming a problem rightly and seeking help is a different thing from complaining behind someone's back.

**4. Shining as lights.** "That you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without blemish in the midst of a crooked and twisted generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world, holding fast to the word of life" (2:15–16). "Crooked and twisted generation" comes from Deuteronomy 32:5, which speaks of unfaithful Israel; now God's children shine in such a generation. We shine not by our own light but by "holding fast to the word of life."

**5. Poured out with joy.** "Even if I am to be poured out as a drink offering upon the sacrificial offering of your faith, I am glad and rejoice with you all" (2:17). In Old Testament worship, wine was sometimes poured out on a sacrifice as a "drink offering" (Num 28:7). Paul likens the Philippians' faith to the sacrifice and his own possible martyrdom to the wine poured over it. He sees his life as a little wine poured out on other people's faith, and he rejoices in it (see 2 Tim 4:6).

> The point: We can work hard at living out our salvation because God is already at work in our hearts. Believers do not shine with their own light; in a crooked generation they hold out the word of life.

## Going Deeper: 2:19–30 Timothy and Epaphroditus

**1. Timothy: genuinely concerned for others.** "For I have no one like him, who will be genuinely concerned for your welfare. They all seek their own interests, not those of Jesus Christ" (2:20–21). Timothy is a living example of 2:4: he looks not only to his own interests but to the interests of others. Paul says "as a son with a father he has served with me in the gospel" (2:22); he was proven over long years of serving together.

**2. Epaphroditus: near to death.** Epaphroditus was the one the Philippian church sent to bring its gift to Paul (2:25; 4:18). "Indeed he was ill, near to death. But God had mercy on him" (2:27).
- **Paul did not hide his sorrow:** God had mercy on Epaphroditus, "and not only on him but on me also, lest I should have sorrow upon sorrow" (2:27). An apostle can worry and grieve; faith is not the absence of tears.
- **God does not always heal:** Paul does not say he "declared" Epaphroditus healed. God's mercy to him was grace; elsewhere Paul left a co-worker behind sick (2 Tim 4:20).
- **"Risking his life":** "for he nearly died for the work of Christ, risking his life" (2:30). "Risking" is a gambler's word: to stake one's life. He staked his life on the work of Christ.

**3. Honor such men.** "So receive him in the Lord with all joy, and honor such men" (2:29). Epaphroditus was not an apostle and was known for no great sermons; he was a brother who faithfully delivered a gift and cared for someone. Yet Paul says the church must honor people like him.

> The point: Timothy and Epaphroditus were ordinary people who lived out the mind of Christ: one genuinely cared for others, the other staked his life on the work of Christ. These are the people God values.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but made himself nothing, taking the form of a servant" (2:6–7). When do I most often feel I have been shortchanged? Perhaps at work someone else took credit for what I did; perhaps at home I do the most and no one notices; perhaps at church I have served for years and a newcomer was chosen over me at once. Inside I say, "Why should I be the one to give way?" So I start keeping score, complaining and comparing. Yet Christ is the one who most had the right to say "Why me?" He was equal with God, yet he willingly took the form of a servant and went all the way to the cross. The loss he took on the cross, he took in my place: he bore my sin, and he bore the shame I deserved. Before him I see that I am not the one who has been shortchanged, but the one who has been given everything freely. (This is not a call to stay silent in the face of abuse or injustice; such things must be spoken of, and help must be sought.) Today, can I bring to the Lord the thing that makes me feel shortchanged, first confess the scorekeeping and complaining in my heart, and then ask him to show me how rich I already am in him? God exalted Christ, who willingly humbled himself, and he sees everything I do in secret. I do not need to fight for that glory myself, because Christ has already given me all that is his.
