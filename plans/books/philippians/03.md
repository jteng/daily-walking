## 本章脉络

第三章有四段。**防备靠肉体的人**（1-6节）：保罗警告腓立比人防备那些要外邦信徒受割礼的人；真受割礼的，是以神的灵敬拜、在基督耶稣里夸口、不靠肉体的人；论到靠肉体，保罗比谁都有资格。**有损的与至宝**（7-11节）：保罗把从前以为有益的都当作有损的，为要得着基督，并在他里面有「信基督的义」。**向着标竿直跑**（12-16节）：保罗还没有完全，但他忘记背后，努力面前，向着标竿直跑。**天上的国民**（3:17–4:1）：有许多人是基督十字架的仇敌，专以地上的事为念；我们却是天上的国民，等候救主从天上降临。

全章的对比是：靠自己的义，还是得着基督的义；以地上的事为念，还是等候天上来的救主。

## 深读 3:1-6 不靠着肉体

**1. 「要靠主喜乐」。** 「弟兄们，我还有话说，你们要靠主喜乐」（3:1）。在严厉的警告之前，保罗先说「要靠主喜乐」。这不是随口一句客套话。下面要讲的，正是喜乐的敌人：靠自己的义来找安全感。

**2. 三个严厉的称呼。** 「应当防备犬类，防备作恶的，防备妄自行割的」（3:2）。
- **他们是谁：** 从3:3-5来看，这些人看重割礼和律法，所以大多数解经家认为他们是主张外邦信徒要受割礼的犹太裔基督徒（和加拉太书里的人相似）；也有人认为是不信基督的犹太人。保罗当时可能还没有看见他们到了腓立比，而是预先提醒。
- **「犬类」：** 犹太人有时称外邦人为狗，意思是不洁净的。保罗把这个称呼反过来，用在那些看重外在洁净的人身上。
- **「妄自行割的」：** 原文里「割礼」（*peritomē*）和「妄自行割」（*katatomē*）发音相近。保罗的意思是：如果割礼不指向基督，就只是在身体上划一刀而已。
- **严厉是为了保护：** 保罗对假福音毫不让步，因为假福音叫人离开基督的恩典，也夺去福音带来的喜乐（参加5:2-4）。但这不是叫我们用难听的话骂一切和我们意见不同的人；保罗是在保护福音的根基。

**3. 真受割礼的。** 「因为真受割礼的，乃是我们这以神的灵敬拜、在基督耶稣里夸口、不靠着肉体的」（3:3）。旧约本来就说，神要的是心里的割礼（申10:16；30:6；耶4:4）。真正属神的人有三个记号：
- **以神的灵敬拜：** 不是靠外在的仪式，而是靠圣灵来到神面前；
- **在基督耶稣里夸口：** 不是夸自己有什么，而是夸基督为我做了什么；
- **不靠着肉体：** 「肉体」在这里指人靠自己可以拿出来的一切资格、身份和成就。

**4. 保罗的履历。** 「我第八天受割礼；我是以色列族、便雅悯支派的人，是希伯来人所生的希伯来人。就律法说，我是法利赛人；就热心说，我是逼迫教会的；就律法上的义说，我是无可指摘的」（3:5-6）。
- **生来就有的：** 他按律法第八天受割礼，是纯正的以色列人，属于出过扫罗王的便雅悯支派。「希伯来人所生的希伯来人」，许多人认为是指他的父母都是讲希伯来话的犹太人。
- **自己挣来的：** 在律法上是最严谨的法利赛人，热心到逼迫教会，按人所能看见的律法标准，他是「无可指摘的」。
- **「无可指摘」是什么意思：** 保罗不是说他从来没有犯罪（参罗7:7-11），而是说按照当时人眼中守律法的标准，没有人能挑他的错。

> 要点：人很容易把出身、学历、教会资历或敬虔的表现当作在神面前的依靠。真正属神的人可以放下这些依靠，因为基督为我们所做的已经足够，所以他们只在基督里夸口。

## 深读 3:7-11 为要得着基督

**1. 从「有益」到「有损」。** 「只是我先前以为与我有益的，我现在因基督都当作有损的」（3:7）。「有益」和「有损」是做生意记账的用语。保罗好像把账本重新算了一遍：从前记在盈利一栏的，认识基督以后，都改记在亏损一栏。原因只有一个：「因基督」。

**2. 万事都当作有损的。** 「不但如此，我也将万事当作有损的，因我以认识我主基督耶稣为至宝。我为他已经丢弃万事，看作粪土，为要得着基督」（3:8）。
- **「至宝」：** 原文指超越一切的价值。认识基督的价值超过一切，其他东西和他相比，都算不得什么。
- **「认识我主基督耶稣」：** 这不只是知道一些关于基督的事，而是亲身认识他，与他有生命的相交。注意保罗说「我主」：这是个人的认识。
- **「粪土」：** 这个词很重，可以指垃圾或粪便。保罗不是说出身和学问本身是污秽的，而是说：人若拿这些当作在神面前的依靠，跟基督一比，就和垃圾一样。

**3. 两种义。** 「并且得以在他里面，不是有自己因律法而得的义，乃是有信基督的义，就是因信神而来的义」（3:9）。这是全章最重要的一句话。
- **自己的义：** 是人靠守律法挣来的义，是从自己出来的。
- **从神而来的义：** 不是人做出来的，而是神赐下的，是神的礼物（参罗3:21-22；林后5:21）。
- **在基督里：** 这义是我们「在他里面」得着的。我们与基督联合，他的义就算为我们的义（这叫归算）。神这样做是公义的，因为那无罪的基督在十字架上代替我们担当了罪和罪的刑罚（林后5:21）；信心本身没有功劳，只是接受基督。「得以在他里面」也指向末日：到那一天神宣判的时候，我们是站在基督里面的。
- **两种理解：** 保罗新观学者（如汤姆·赖特）认为，自己的义主要是指以律法和犹太身份为标记的立约地位；从神而来的义，是神所赐的立约子民身份。赖特不认为基督的义归算给信徒。宗教改革的传统（马丁·路德、加尔文；今天如约翰·派博、汤姆·施赖纳、莫伊塞斯·席尔瓦）则指出，保罗在3:5-6列出的也包括个人的成就（「就热心说……就律法上的义说，我是无可指摘的」），所以自己的义是靠守律法挣来的。从神而来的义，是神因基督的缘故白白赐给罪人的；罪人因此在神面前被称为义。
- **「信基督的义」：** 许多学者（如莫伊塞斯·席尔瓦和大多数改革宗解经家）理解为信基督，因为保罗下半句说，这义是「因信神而来的」，这里的信显然是指我们的信心；也有学者（如理查德·海斯）理解为基督的信实，认为下半句是指我们对这信实的回应。和合本和大多数译本都译作「信基督」。无论哪一种，义的根基都是基督，信心是接受这义的途径。

**4. 认识他复活的大能，也一同受苦。** 「使我认识基督，晓得他复活的大能，并且晓得和他一同受苦，效法他的死，或者我也得以从死里复活」（3:10-11）。
- **复活的大能在先：** 保罗先说复活的大能，再说一同受苦。我们能和基督一同受苦，是因为那叫他从死里复活的大能，已经在我们里面运行。
- **效法他的死：** 就是像2:8的基督那样，走一条谦卑、舍己、顺服的路。
- **「或者我也得以」：** 这不是说保罗怀疑自己能不能复活（参3:20-21；1:6）。许多解经家认为，这是在表达谦卑和渴慕：不论走什么路，哪怕是死，他只求最后能得着复活。

> 要点：保罗把一切都重新算了账：与基督相比，一切都是有损的。我们在神面前的义，不是自己挣来的，而是在基督里从神白白领受的。

## 深读 3:12-16 忘记背后，努力面前

**1. 还没有完全。** 「这不是说我已经得着了，已经完全了；我乃是竭力追求，或者可以得着基督耶稣所以得着我的」（3:12）。连使徒保罗都说自己还没有完全。这句话提醒那些以为自己已经「到了」的人，也安慰那些因为自己还不完全而灰心的人。

**2. 基督先得着了我。** 「或者可以得着基督耶稣所以得着我的」（3:12）。原文这句话可以理解为：基督耶稣为此得着了我；也可以理解为：因为基督耶稣得着了我。无论哪一种，这都是保罗追求的根基。他之所以追求，不是为了让基督接纳他，而是因为基督已经「得着」了他：在大马士革的路上，基督抓住了这个逼迫教会的人（徒9:3-6）。我们追求基督，是因为他先追上了我们。

**3. 忘记背后。** 「我只有一件事，就是忘记背后，努力面前的，向着标竿直跑」（3:13-14）。
- **忘记什么：** 从上下文看，「背后」首先是指3:4-6那些他从前引以为傲的资格；也包括他逼迫教会的过去（参提前1:13-15）。
- **「忘记」不是失忆：** 保罗自己在3:6就提到过去。「忘记」的意思是不再让过去决定现在：不再靠过去的成就，也不再被过去的罪捆绑。
- **像赛跑的人：** 「努力面前」「直跑」都是赛跑的用语（参林前9:24）。一个赛跑的人如果一直回头看，就跑不好。

**4. 从上面召我来得的奖赏。** 「要得神在基督耶稣里从上面召我来得的奖赏」（3:14）。奖赏是什么？从3:8-11和3:20-21来看，就是完全得着基督，在复活中与他同在。这不是我们赚来的工钱，而是神召我们来得的，是恩典的奖赏。

**5. 照着已到的地步行。** 「所以我们中间，凡是完全人总要存这样的心……然而，我们到了什么地步，就当照着什么地步行」（3:15-16）。这里的「完全人」和3:12的「完全」是同一个字根，在这里可以理解为成熟的人（有人认为保罗也在温和地讽刺那些自以为「完全」的人）。成熟的标志，正是知道自己还没有完全（3:12）。保罗不要求每个人在每件事上马上和他看法一样，他相信「神也必以此指示你们」。

> 要点：基督已经得着了我，所以我可以放下过去的骄傲和罪疚，向着他直跑。信徒追求，不是为了蒙悦纳，而是因为已蒙悦纳。

## 深读 3:17–4:1 我们却是天上的国民

**1. 效法保罗。** 「弟兄们，你们要一同效法我，也当留意看那些照我们榜样行的人」（3:17）。保罗不是说自己完全（3:12），而是说：像我一样，把一切当作有损的，向着基督直跑（参林前11:1）。

**2. 十字架的仇敌。** 「因为有许多人行事是基督十字架的仇敌。我屡次告诉你们，现在又流泪地告诉你们：他们的结局就是沉沦；他们的神就是自己的肚腹。他们以自己的羞辱为荣耀，专以地上的事为念」（3:18-19）。
- **他们是谁：** 解经家看法不同。有人（如莱特福特、戈登·费）认为是自称信主、却把恩典当作放纵的机会、只顾口腹的人；有人认为仍是3:2那些主张割礼的教师，「肚腹」指饮食的条例，「羞辱」指割礼的部位；也有人认为是泛指不信的人。第一种理解比较符合「行事」和「专以地上的事为念」，但难有定论。
- **「流泪地」：** 保罗说到这些人，不是冷冰冰地定罪，而是流着泪。这也是对我们的警告：一个人可以天天在教会里，心里却以肚腹为神。保罗流泪，是因为他们的结局是沉沦；今天回转的人，仍然可以蒙赦免。

**3. 天上的国民。** 「我们却是天上的国民」（3:20）。
- **在腓立比的背景下：** 腓立比是罗马的殖民地，城里许多人是退伍军人的后代，他们虽然离罗马很远，却有罗马公民的身份，按罗马的法律生活。保罗说，信徒更是如此：我们住在地上，但我们的「国籍」在天上。
- **不是逃避今生：** 天上的国民不是不理地上的事，而是在地上按天国的样式生活（1:27），像一个住在外地的公民一样，代表自己的国。

**4. 等候救主。** 「并且等候救主，就是主耶稣基督从天上降临。他要按着那能叫万有归服自己的大能，将我们这卑贱的身体改变形状，和他自己荣耀的身体相似」（3:20-21）。
- **「救主」：** 在旧约，救主是耶和华自己的称号：「除我以外没有救主」（赛43:11）。保罗把这称号归给耶稣。在罗马殖民地，皇帝有时也被尊为救主，腓立比人也会听出其中的对比：我们等候的救主是主耶稣基督。
- **「卑贱的身体」：** 就是会生病、会衰老、会死的身体。这身体不是要被丢弃，而是要被「改变形状」（参林前15:42-44、51-53）。
- **基督的大能保证这事：** 那「能叫万有归服自己的大能」，是只有神才有的权能，就是2:10所说万膝都要向他跪拜的主权（参赛45:23）。

**5. 靠主站立得稳。** 「我所亲爱、所想念的弟兄们……你们应当靠主站立得稳」（4:1）。这一节是第三章的结论：既然我们是天上的国民，等候救主，就要靠主站稳。

> 要点：我们的国籍在天上，我们等候的救主必改变我们卑贱的身体。所以我们不必以地上的事为念，而可以靠主站立得稳。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我们却是天上的国民，并且等候救主，就是主耶稣基督从天上降临」（3:20）。我常常觉得自己属于哪里？也许我在海外生活了很多年，回国时被人说「你已经不是这里的人了」，在国外又总觉得自己是外人；也许我刚换了城市或工作，名片上的头衔没有了，突然不知道怎样介绍自己；也许我身体越来越差，觉得自己在这个世界上越来越没有位置。人很容易靠一张证件、一个户口、一份工作、一个圈子来回答「我是谁，我属于哪里」，可是这些东西都可能被拿走。腓立比城里许多人以罗马公民的身份为荣，保罗却告诉他们，有一个更确实的国籍。这个国籍不是我考来的，也不是我申请来的，而是基督用自己的血为我换来的：「不是有自己……的义，乃是有信基督的义」（3:9）。保罗失去了出身和名声给他的一切，却说他得着的是「至宝」（3:8）：不只是一个新身份，而是基督自己。我也许在地上漂泊不定，但主耶稣说，属他的人「名记录在天上」（路10:20）。今天，我能不能把那种「哪里都不属于」的感觉带到主面前，向他说出来？然后，我能不能对他说：「主啊，我的名字记在天上，是你用血为我写上的。」这个会生病、会衰老的身体，也不是我最后的样子；我们等候的救主，要将它「改变形状，和他自己荣耀的身体相似」（3:21）。他要接我们回家。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 has four parts. **Beware of those who trust in the flesh** (vv. 1–6): Paul warns the Philippians against those who want Gentile believers circumcised; the true circumcision are those who worship by the Spirit of God, glory in Christ Jesus and put no confidence in the flesh; and if it came to confidence in the flesh, Paul had more reason than anyone. **Loss and surpassing worth** (vv. 7–11): Paul counts as loss everything he once thought gain, in order to gain Christ and be found in him with "that which comes through faith in Christ." **Pressing on toward the goal** (vv. 12–16): Paul is not yet perfect, but forgetting what lies behind and straining forward, he presses on toward the goal. **Citizens of heaven** (3:17–4:1): many walk as enemies of the cross of Christ, with minds set on earthly things; but our citizenship is in heaven, and we await a Savior from there.

The contrast running through the chapter is this: a righteousness of my own, or the righteousness found in Christ; minds set on earthly things, or waiting for the Savior from heaven.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–6 No Confidence in the Flesh

**1. "Rejoice in the Lord."** "Finally, my brothers, rejoice in the Lord" (3:1). Before a stern warning, Paul says "rejoice in the Lord." This is not a passing pleasantry. What follows is about an enemy of joy: seeking security in a righteousness of one's own.

**2. Three harsh names.** "Look out for the dogs, look out for the evildoers, look out for those who mutilate the flesh" (3:2).
- **Who they were:** from 3:3–5 they valued circumcision and the law, so most interpreters take them to be Jewish Christians who insisted that Gentile believers be circumcised (like those in Galatians); some think they were Jews who did not believe in Christ. Paul may not yet have seen them in Philippi and may be warning ahead of time.
- **"Dogs":** Jews sometimes called Gentiles dogs, meaning unclean. Paul turns the name around and applies it to those who prized outward cleanness.
- **"Those who mutilate the flesh":** in Greek "circumcision" (*peritomē*) and "mutilation" (*katatomē*) sound alike. Paul's point is that if circumcision does not point to Christ, it is only a cut in the body.
- **Harshness to protect:** Paul gives no ground to a false gospel because it draws people away from Christ's grace and robs them of the gospel's joy (see Gal 5:2–4). But this does not license us to use ugly names for everyone who disagrees with us; Paul is protecting the foundation of the gospel.

**3. The real circumcision.** "For we are the real circumcision, who worship by the Spirit of God and glory in Christ Jesus and put no confidence in the flesh" (3:3). The Old Testament already said that God wanted circumcision of the heart (Deut 10:16; 30:6; Jer 4:4). Those who truly belong to God bear three marks:
- **They worship by the Spirit of God:** coming to God not by outward ritual but by the Spirit;
- **They glory in Christ Jesus:** boasting not in what they have but in what Christ has done for them;
- **They put no confidence in the flesh:** "flesh" here means every qualification, status and achievement a person can produce from himself.

**4. Paul's résumé.** "Circumcised on the eighth day, of the people of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of Hebrews; as to the law, a Pharisee; as to zeal, a persecutor of the church; as to righteousness, under the law blameless" (3:5–6).
- **What he was born with:** circumcised on the eighth day as the law required, of pure Israelite descent, of the tribe of Benjamin, which gave Israel King Saul, and "a Hebrew of Hebrews," which many take to mean born to Hebrew-speaking Jewish parents.
- **What he achieved:** a Pharisee, the strictest party in keeping the law; so zealous that he persecuted the church; and by the visible standard of the law, "blameless."
- **What "blameless" means:** Paul does not mean he never sinned (see Rom 7:7–11), but that by the standard of law-keeping people could see, no one could fault him.

> The point: It is easy to rely before God on our background, education, years in the church or a record of devotion. Those who truly belong to God can lay these props down, because what Christ has done for them is enough; so they glory in Christ alone.

## Going Deeper: 3:7–11 That I May Gain Christ

**1. From "gain" to "loss."** "But whatever gain I had, I counted as loss for the sake of Christ" (3:7). "Gain" and "loss" are bookkeeping words. Paul has redone his accounts: what he once entered under "assets," after knowing Christ, he moved to "losses." There is one reason: "for the sake of Christ."

**2. Everything as loss.** "Indeed, I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things and count them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ" (3:8).
- **"Surpassing worth":** the value of knowing Christ surpasses everything; compared with him, nothing else counts.
- **"Knowing Christ Jesus my Lord":** not only knowing facts about Christ but knowing him personally, in relationship. Note that Paul says "my Lord": it is personal knowledge.
- **"Rubbish":** a strong word that can mean refuse or dung. Paul does not say that background and learning are filthy in themselves, but that if we rely on them before God, compared with Christ they are like garbage.

**3. Two kinds of righteousness.** "And be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own that comes from the law, but that which comes through faith in Christ, the righteousness from God that depends on faith" (3:9). This is the most important sentence of the chapter.
- **A righteousness of my own:** earned by keeping the law, coming from oneself.
- **The righteousness from God:** not produced by us but given by God as a gift (see Rom 3:21–22; 2 Cor 5:21).
- **In Christ:** we receive this righteousness by being "found in him." United to Christ, his righteousness is counted as ours (this is called imputation). God can do this justly because Christ, who knew no sin, bore our sin and its penalty in our place on the cross (2 Cor 5:21); faith adds nothing of its own but receives him. "Be found in him" also looks ahead to the last day: when God gives his verdict, we will be found in Christ.
- **Two readings:** scholars of the "New Perspective" (such as N. T. Wright) read "a righteousness of my own" mainly as the covenant status marked by Torah and Jewish identity, and "the righteousness from God" as God's gift of covenant membership; Wright does not hold that Christ's own righteousness is imputed. The Reformation tradition (Martin Luther and Calvin; today John Piper, Thomas Schreiner, Moisés Silva) notes that Paul's list in 3:5–6 includes personal achievement ("as to zeal… as to righteousness, under the law blameless"), so "my own righteousness" is a righteousness earned by law-keeping, and the righteousness from God is the status of "righteous" that God gives a sinner on account of Christ.
- **"Faith in Christ":** many (for example, Moisés Silva and most Reformed interpreters) read it as "faith in Christ," pointing out that Paul goes straight on to "the righteousness from God that depends on faith," which is our faith. Others (for example, Richard Hays) read "the faithfulness of Christ" and take the second phrase as our response to it. The CUV, the ESV and most translations read "faith in Christ." Either way, the ground of righteousness is Christ, and faith is the means by which we receive it.

**4. Knowing his resurrection power, sharing his sufferings.** "That I may know him and the power of his resurrection, and may share his sufferings, becoming like him in his death, that by any means possible I may attain the resurrection from the dead" (3:10–11).
- **Resurrection power comes first:** Paul speaks of resurrection power before sharing sufferings. We can share Christ's sufferings because the power that raised him from the dead is already at work in us.
- **Becoming like him in his death:** walking, like Christ in 2:8, a path of humility, self-giving and obedience.
- **"By any means possible":** this does not mean Paul doubted he would be raised (see 3:20–21; 1:6). Many interpreters take it as an expression of humility and longing: whatever the path, even death, he wants only to reach the resurrection at the end.

> The point: Paul redid all his accounts: compared with Christ, everything is loss. Our righteousness before God is not earned by us but received freely from God in Christ.

## Going Deeper: 3:12–16 Forgetting What Lies Behind

**1. Not yet perfect.** "Not that I have already obtained this or am already perfect, but I press on to make it my own, because Christ Jesus has made me his own" (3:12). Even the apostle Paul says he is not yet perfect. This is a warning to those who think they have "arrived," and a comfort to those discouraged because they are still imperfect.

**2. Christ made me his own first.** "Because Christ Jesus has made me his own" (3:12). The Greek can mean "because" or "that for which" Christ Jesus made me his own. Either way, this is the ground of Paul's pursuit. He does not press on so that Christ will accept him, but because Christ has already laid hold of him: on the Damascus road Christ seized this persecutor of the church (Acts 9:3–6). We pursue Christ because he first caught up with us.

**3. Forgetting what lies behind.** "But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal" (3:13–14).
- **What is forgotten:** in context, "what lies behind" means first the qualifications he once took pride in (3:4–6); it also includes his past as a persecutor of the church (see 1 Tim 1:13–15).
- **"Forgetting" is not amnesia:** Paul himself mentions his past in 3:6. To "forget" means no longer letting the past rule the present: no longer resting on past achievements, and no longer being bound by past sins.
- **Like a runner:** "straining forward" and "press on" are racing words (see 1 Cor 9:24). A runner who keeps looking back cannot run well.

**4. The prize of the upward call.** "For the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus" (3:14). What is the prize? From 3:8–11 and 3:20–21, it is to gain Christ fully and be with him in the resurrection. It is not wages we earn but what God has called us to receive, a prize of grace.

**5. Hold true to what we have attained.** "Let those of us who are mature think this way… Only let us hold true to what we have attained" (3:15–16). Paul uses a word from the same root as "perfect" in 3:12, here meaning "mature" (some hear a gentle irony toward those who claimed to be "perfect"). The mark of maturity is precisely knowing that one is not yet perfect (3:12). Paul does not require everyone to agree with him at once on everything; he trusts that "God will reveal that also to you."

> The point: Christ has already made me his own, so I can lay down both past pride and past guilt and press on toward him. Believers do not press on in order to be accepted, but because they already are.

## Going Deeper: 3:17–4:1 Our Citizenship Is in Heaven

**1. Imitate Paul.** "Brothers, join in imitating me, and keep your eyes on those who walk according to the example you have in us" (3:17). Paul does not claim to be perfect (3:12). He means: like me, count everything as loss and press on toward Christ (see 1 Cor 11:1).

**2. Enemies of the cross.** "For many, of whom I have often told you and now tell you even with tears, walk as enemies of the cross of Christ. Their end is destruction, their god is their belly, and they glory in their shame, with minds set on earthly things" (3:18–19).
- **Who they were:** interpreters differ. Some (for example, J. B. Lightfoot and Gordon Fee) see professing believers who treated grace as license and lived for their appetites. Others see the same teachers who urged circumcision as in 3:2, with "belly" pointing to food laws and "shame" to circumcision. Still others see unbelievers in general. The first reading fits "walk" and "minds set on earthly things" well, but certainty is not possible.
- **"With tears":** Paul does not speak of them with cold condemnation, but in tears. This is also a warning to us: a person can be in church every week while his belly is his god. Paul weeps because their end is destruction; whoever turns back today can still be forgiven.

**3. Citizens of heaven.** "But our citizenship is in heaven" (3:20).
- **In Philippi's setting:** Philippi was a Roman colony; many of its residents, descendants of settled veterans, held Roman citizenship and lived under Roman law, though they lived far from Rome. Paul says believers are even more so: we live on earth, but our citizenship is in heaven.
- **Not an escape from this life:** citizens of heaven do not ignore earthly things; they live on earth in the manner of the heavenly kingdom (1:27), like citizens living abroad who represent their homeland.

**4. Awaiting a Savior.** "And from it we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ, who will transform our lowly body to be like his glorious body, by the power that enables him even to subject all things to himself" (3:20–21).
- **"Savior":** in the Old Testament, "Savior" is the LORD's own title: "besides me there is no savior" (Isa 43:11). Paul gives it to Jesus. Readers in a Roman colony, where emperors were sometimes honored as "savior," would also have heard a contrast: the Savior we await is the Lord Jesus Christ.
- **"Our lowly body":** the body that grows sick, ages and dies. It will not be thrown away but transformed (see 1 Cor 15:42–44, 51–53).
- **Christ's power guarantees it:** "the power that enables him even to subject all things to himself" belongs to God alone; it is the lordship of 2:10, before which every knee will bow to him (see Isa 45:23).

**5. Stand firm in the Lord.** "Therefore, my brothers, whom I love and long for… stand firm thus in the Lord, my beloved" (4:1). This verse concludes chapter 3: since we are citizens of heaven awaiting our Savior, we are to stand firm in the Lord.

> The point: Our citizenship is in heaven, and the Savior we await will transform our lowly bodies. So we need not set our minds on earthly things; we can stand firm in the Lord.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But our citizenship is in heaven, and from it we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ" (3:20). Where do I usually feel I belong? Perhaps I have lived abroad for many years; when I go home I am told, "You're not really from here anymore," and abroad I still feel like an outsider. Perhaps I have just changed city or job, the title on my business card is gone, and I suddenly do not know how to introduce myself. Perhaps my health is failing and I feel I have less and less of a place in this world. It is easy to answer "who am I, and where do I belong?" with a document, a household registration, a job or a circle of friends, but any of these can be taken away. Many in Philippi were proud of their Roman citizenship, but Paul told them of a surer one. This citizenship is not something I earned by passing a test or won by applying; Christ bought it for me with his own blood: "not having a righteousness of my own… but that which comes through faith in Christ" (3:9). Paul lost everything his birth and reputation had given him, yet he called what he gained "surpassing worth" (3:8): not only a new status, but Christ himself. I may be unsettled on earth, but the Lord Jesus says that the names of those who belong to him "are written in heaven" (Luke 10:20). Today, can I bring to the Lord that feeling of "belonging nowhere" and tell him about it? Then can I say to him, "Lord, my name is written in heaven, and you wrote it there with your blood"? This body that falls ill and grows old is not my final form either; the Savior we await "will transform our lowly body to be like his glorious body" (3:21). He will bring us home.
