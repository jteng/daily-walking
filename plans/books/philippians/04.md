## 本章脉络

第四章是全信的结尾，有四段。**在主里同心**（1-3节）：保罗劝两位在福音上与他一同劳苦的姊妹友阿蝶和循都基要在主里同心，也请一位同工帮助她们。**喜乐、平安和思念**（4-9节）：要靠主常常喜乐；当叫众人知道你们谦让的心；应当一无挂虑，凡事藉着祷告、祈求和感谢告诉神，神的平安必保守你们；凡是真实的、可敬的……都要思念，并且去行。**知足的秘诀**（10-20节）：保罗为腓立比人的馈送感谢神，他已经学会了无论在什么景况都可以知足，因为「我靠着那加给我力量的，凡事都能做」；神必照他荣耀的丰富，使他们一切所需用的都充足。**问安和祝福**（21-23节）：连凯撒家里的人也问他们安。

全章贯穿着两个词：「主」和「平安」。在主里同心，靠主喜乐，主已经近了；神的平安保守我们，赐平安的神与我们同在。

## 深读 4:2-3 要在主里同心

**1. 点名劝勉。** 「我劝友阿蝶和循都基，要在主里同心」（4:2）。保罗在一封要公开宣读的信里点了两个人的名字，可见她们的不和已经影响到教会。但他对两个人用了同一个「劝」字，没有偏袒哪一方，也没有说谁对谁错。

**2. 她们是同工。** 「因为她们在福音上曾与我一同劳苦」（4:3）。「一同劳苦」和1:27「齐心努力」是同一个词，有并肩作战的意思。这两位姊妹不是爱闹事的人，而是曾和保罗一起为福音拼搏的同工。腓立比的教会是从河边一群妇女开始的（徒16:13-15），妇女在这个教会里一直有重要的服事。忠心的同工也可能闹不和；不和不能抹去她们过去的劳苦，但也不能放着不管。

**3. 「在主里」同心。** 和合本译作「同心」的这个词，就是2:2、2:5的「心」（思念、存心）。保罗不是只要她们表面上握手言和，而是要她们一同「以基督耶稣的心为心」（2:5）。合一的根基不是谁让步了，而是她们都在主里。

**4. 「真实同负一轭的」。** 「我也求你这真实同负一轭的，帮助这两个女人」（4:3）。这个人是谁，解经家有不同看法：有人认为是路加，有人认为是以巴弗提或西拉，也有人认为「同负一轭的」原文是一个人名（叙西古）。无论是谁，保罗都请第三个人来帮助她们和好。弟兄姊妹之间的冲突，有时需要一个可信、成熟的人在中间帮助。

**5. 名字都在生命册上。** 「他们的名字都在生命册上」（4:3；参启3:5）。保罗在劝她们和好之前，先提醒所有人：你们的名字都已经记在神的册子上了。她们在神面前的身份，比她们之间的分歧更深、更确实。

**6. 和好不等于掩盖伤害。** 这两位姊妹的不和，看起来是同工之间的分歧。但如果冲突牵涉到虐待、欺压或犯罪，教会就不可只是叫双方「在主里同心」。向警方或法庭举报是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必和仍不安全的人和好；饶恕不等于恢复犯错者的职分，也不等于让他再接近受害者或孩子；教会更不可为了「家丑不可外扬」而遮掩。

> 要点：教会里忠心的同工也会不和。保罗不偏袒，也不放任，而是提醒她们：你们都在主里，名字都在生命册上，所以要以基督的心为心。

## 深读 4:4-7 靠主喜乐，一无挂虑

**1. 靠主常常喜乐。** 「你们要靠主常常喜乐。我再说，你们要喜乐」（4:4）。保罗说「常常」，又说「我再说」，可见他知道这不容易。这里的关键是「靠主」：不是靠环境喜乐，而是在主里喜乐。环境会变，主不变。喜乐是一个命令，因为它不是一种情绪，而是把眼光转向主的结果；但这不是说信徒不可以忧伤（参2:27；罗12:15）。

**2. 谦让的心。** 「当叫众人知道你们谦让的心。主已经近了」（4:5）。「谦让」这个词很难翻译，可以指宽容、温和、通情达理、不坚持自己的权利。信徒为什么能不坚持自己的权利？因为「主已经近了」。
- **「主已经近了」的两种理解：** 一种是时间上的近，主快要再来了（参3:20）；一种是空间上的近，主就在我们身边（参诗145:18）。两种意思都合乎圣经，保罗可能是有意让这句话两面都可以读。
- **为什么不必争：** 如果主快要来，一切都要在他面前显明，我就不必现在拼命为自己争；如果主就在身边，我就不必怕吃亏。

**3. 应当一无挂虑。** 「应当一无挂虑，只要凡事藉着祷告、祈求，和感谢，将你们所要的告诉神」（4:6）。
- **「一无挂虑」：** 这和耶稣说的「不要为生命忧虑」（太6:25）是同一个词。这不是叫我们不负责任、不去计划，而是不让忧虑掌管我们的心。
- **「凡事」：** 挂虑的反面不是什么都不想，而是把「凡事」都带到神面前。没有一件事太小，也没有一件事太大。
- **「和感谢」：** 在把需要告诉神的同时感谢他，就是提醒自己：这位神过去是信实的，他现在也在听。
- **忧虑症不是信心小：** 有些人的焦虑是一种需要医治的病。这节经文不是说他们灵性不好；寻求医生和专业辅导的帮助，和祷告并不冲突。

**4. 神的平安必保守。** 「神所赐、出人意外的平安必在基督耶稣里保守你们的心怀意念」（4:7）。
- **「出人意外」：** 这平安超过人的理解。也就是说，问题也许还没有解决，人看不出你为什么能平安，但神的平安已经在那里。
- **「保守」：** 这是一个军事用语，意思是驻军看守。腓立比是一个有罗马驻军的城市。神的平安就像一支驻军，守住我们的心和思想，不让忧虑攻进来。
- **「在基督耶稣里」：** 这平安不是一种心理技巧，而是因为我们在基督里，已经与神和好（罗5:1）。

> 要点：信徒可以不被忧虑掌管，不是因为问题都解决了，而是因为主已经近了。我们把凡事带着感谢告诉神，神的平安就在基督里守住我们的心。

## 深读 4:8-9 这些事你们都要思念

**1. 八样美好的事。** 「凡是真实的、可敬的、公义的、清洁的、可爱的、有美名的，若有什么德行，若有什么称赞，这些事你们都要思念」（4:8）。
- **用了当时人熟悉的词：** 这些词有好几个是当时希腊哲学家讲道德时常用的。保罗的意思不是说，外邦人的道德就等于福音，而是说：凡是真正美好的东西，都是从神来的，信徒都可以欣赏、可以思想。
- **「思念」：** 这不只是想一想，而是仔细考虑、让它塑造自己。我们心里常常想的，会慢慢塑造我们是什么样的人。
- **最完全的真实、可爱，是基督自己：** 当我们思念这些美好的事，最终会把我们带到那位一切美善的源头。

**2. 去行出来。** 「你们在我身上所学习的，所领受的，所听见的，所看见的，这些事你们都要去行，赐平安的神就必与你们同在」（4:9）。思念要带出行动。4:7说神所赐的平安，4:9说「赐平安的神」：我们不只是得到平安，更是得到那位赐平安的神亲自同在。

> 要点：我们心里思念什么，就会成为什么样的人。思念神所造一切真实、可爱的事，就会被带到基督面前；赐平安的神也必与我们同在。

## 深读 4:10-20 我已经学会了知足

**1. 不是因为缺乏。** 「我并不是因缺乏说这话；我无论在什么景况都可以知足，这是我已经学会了」（4:11）。保罗感谢腓立比人的馈送，却很小心，不让他们以为他在暗示再要。
- **「知足」：** 这个词是当时斯多亚派哲学家常用的，意思是不受外在环境左右，靠自己就够了。保罗用了这个词，却改变了它的意思：他的知足不是靠自己，而是靠基督（4:13）。
- **「学会了」：** 知足不是天生的，而是学来的，而且是在「处卑贱」和「处丰富」两种景况里都学（4:12）。丰富的时候也需要学知足，因为富足同样会叫人忘记神（箴30:8-9）。
- **「秘诀」：** 这个词原文是当时宗教里「入门」的意思，指得知了奥秘。保罗说，他已经得知了知足的秘诀，下一节就告诉我们这秘诀是什么。

**2. 凡事都能做。** 「我靠着那加给我力量的，凡事都能做」（4:13）。
- **上下文：** 这句话常被人用在考试、比赛、做生意上，好像是说信主的人想做什么都能成功。但从上下文看，「凡事」是指4:12的各种景况：饱足或饥饿，有余或缺乏。保罗的意思是：无论在什么景况中，我都能靠基督知足、忠心地活着。
- **「那加给我力量的」：** 秘诀不在于保罗自己有多坚强，而在于基督不断地加给他力量（参林后12:9）。

**3. 同受患难原是美事。** 「然而，你们和我同受患难原是美事」（4:14）。保罗虽然知足，却没有因此轻看别人的爱心。他说「你们和我同受患难」，用的是1:5「同心合意」的那个字根：腓立比人送钱，是在和保罗一同有分于他的患难。

**4. 极美的香气。** 「我已经充足，因我从以巴弗提受了你们的馈送，当作极美的香气，为神所收纳、所喜悦的祭物」（4:18）。
- **馈送是献给神的：** 腓立比人以为是在帮助保罗，保罗却说，这是献给神的「祭物」（参2:17；来13:16）。
- **「归在你们的帐上」：** 「我并不求什么馈送，所求的就是你们的果子渐渐增多，归在你们的帐上」（4:17）。保罗又用了记账的词（参3:7-8）。他高兴的不是自己得了什么，而是腓立比人在神面前的果子增多了。
- **腓立比人自己并不富裕：** 保罗在别处说，马其顿众教会「在极穷之间还格外显出他们乐捐的厚恩」（林后8:2）。

**5. 神必使你们充足。** 「我的神必照他荣耀的丰富，在基督耶稣里，使你们一切所需用的都充足」（4:19）。
- **「我的神」：** 保罗用的是很个人的称呼：供应过我的那位神，也会供应你们。
- **「所需用的」：** 是「需用的」，不是「想要的」。这不是成功神学的保证，保罗自己也经历过饥饿和缺乏（4:12）。
- **「照他荣耀的丰富」：** 不是「从」他的丰富中拿一点出来，而是「照着」他的丰富，按他丰富的程度来供应。
- **「在基督耶稣里」：** 神最大的供应，就是基督自己。

**6. 愿荣耀归给父神。** 「愿荣耀归给我们的父神，直到永永远远。阿们！」（4:20）。一段关于钱的话，结尾却是颂赞。一切的供应，最终都是为了神的荣耀。

> 要点：知足的秘诀不在于环境，也不在于自己有多坚强，而在于基督不断加给我们力量。神必照他荣耀的丰富，在基督里供应我们一切所需用的。

## 深读 4:21-23 凯撒家里的人

**1. 凯撒家里的人。** 「众圣徒都问你们安。在凯撒家里的人特特地问你们安」（4:22）。「凯撒家里的人」不一定是皇帝的亲属，而是指为皇帝服务的奴隶和自由人，有的在皇宫里，有的在各地的官署里。在罗马权力的中心，福音已经生了根（参1:13）。腓立比人知道这件事，就会得到很大的鼓励。

**2. 以恩典结束。** 「愿主耶稣基督的恩常在你们心里！」（4:23）。这封信从「恩惠、平安」开始（1:2），也以「恩」结束。信徒的喜乐、合一、知足，都是从基督的恩典开始，也靠基督的恩典完成。

> 要点：福音能进到凯撒的家里，也能进到我们的家里。这封信从恩典开始，也以恩典结束。

## 默想

**默想：** 「应当一无挂虑，只要凡事藉着祷告、祈求，和感谢，将你们所要的告诉神。神所赐、出人意外的平安必在基督耶稣里保守你们的心怀意念」（4:6-7）。最近让我睡不着的是什么？也许是一张体检报告，也许是公司在裁员，也许是孩子不肯和我说话，也许是父母一个人住在老家，我却离得很远。我常常在心里一遍又一遍地想这些事，好像想得越多，就越能控制。可是我越想越乱，越乱越怕。保罗写这句话的时候，他自己可能也面对着死刑的判决（1:20）。他没有说「不要想了，没事的」，而是说「凡事……告诉神」：把那些在心里打转的事，一件一件说出来，交给那位听祷告的神。（如果我的焦虑已经重到影响吃饭、睡觉和工作，寻求医生或辅导的帮助，也是神给我的恩典。）今天，我能不能拿一张纸，把挂虑的事写下来，每一件都变成一句向神的祈求？然后在每一句旁边，写下一件神过去在这件事上或别的事上向我显出的信实，为它感谢神。也许问题明天还在，但我不是一个人面对它。基督已经为我死了、复活了，他在父面前为我代求；在他里面，神的平安就像驻军一样守着我的心。主已经近了。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 4 closes the letter and has four parts. **Agreeing in the Lord** (vv. 1–3): Paul urges Euodia and Syntyche, two women who had labored with him in the gospel, to agree in the Lord, and asks a co-worker to help them. **Joy, peace and right thinking** (vv. 4–9): rejoice in the Lord always; let your reasonableness be known to everyone; do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving make your requests known to God, and the peace of God will guard you; whatever is true, honorable… think about these things and practice them. **The secret of contentment** (vv. 10–20): Paul thanks God for the Philippians' gift; he has learned to be content in whatever situation, for "I can do all things through him who strengthens me"; God will supply every need of theirs according to his riches in glory. **Greetings and blessing** (vv. 21–23): even those of Caesar's household send greetings.

Two words run through the chapter: "Lord" and "peace." Agree in the Lord, rejoice in the Lord, the Lord is at hand; the peace of God will guard us, and the God of peace will be with us.

## Going Deeper: 4:2–3 Agree in the Lord

**1. Named in public.** "I entreat Euodia and I entreat Syntyche to agree in the Lord" (4:2). Paul names two people in a letter to be read aloud to the church, which shows their disagreement was already affecting the church. But he uses the same verb, "I entreat," for each of them; he takes no side and does not say who is right.

**2. They were co-workers.** "Who have labored side by side with me in the gospel" (4:3). "Labored side by side" is the same word as "striving side by side" in 1:27, a picture of fighting shoulder to shoulder. These two women were not troublemakers but co-workers who had contended alongside Paul for the gospel. The Philippian church began with a group of women by the river (Acts 16:13–15), and women had important ministries in this church from the start. Faithful co-workers can fall out; their quarrel does not erase their past labor, but it cannot be left alone either.

**3. Agree "in the Lord."** The verb translated "agree" is the same "mind" word as in 2:2 and 2:5. Paul does not want them merely to shake hands outwardly, but to share together "this mind… which is yours in Christ Jesus" (2:5). The ground of their unity is not that one of them gives way, but that both are in the Lord.

**4. "True companion."** "Yes, I ask you also, true companion, help these women" (4:3). Interpreters differ on who this is: some suggest Luke, others Epaphroditus or Silas, and some think the Greek word for "companion" (*syzygos*) is a proper name, Syzygus. Whoever it was, Paul asks a third person to help them be reconciled. Conflict between brothers and sisters sometimes needs a trusted, mature person to help in the middle.

**5. Names in the book of life.** "Whose names are in the book of life" (4:3; see Rev 3:5). Before urging reconciliation, Paul reminds everyone: your names are already written in God's book. Their standing before God is deeper and surer than their disagreement.

**6. Reconciliation is not covering up harm.** The quarrel between these two women seems to be a disagreement between co-workers. But if a conflict involves abuse, oppression or crime, the church must not simply tell both sides to "agree in the Lord." Reporting abuse or crime to the police or courts is right (Rom 13:4). Victims need not stay in danger or reconcile with someone still unsafe. Forgiveness does not restore an offender's office or give him access to victims or children. And the church must never hide abuse to save face.

> The point: Faithful co-workers in a church can fall out. Paul neither takes sides nor lets it go; he reminds them that both are in the Lord with their names in the book of life, so they are to share the mind of Christ.

## Going Deeper: 4:4–7 Rejoice in the Lord, Do Not Be Anxious

**1. Rejoice in the Lord always.** "Rejoice in the Lord always; again I will say, Rejoice" (4:4). Paul says "always" and then "again I will say," which shows he knows this is not easy. The key is "in the Lord": not joy in circumstances but joy in the Lord. Circumstances change; the Lord does not. Joy is commanded because it is not a mood but what happens when we turn our eyes to the Lord; yet this does not mean believers may not grieve (see 2:27; Rom 12:15).

**2. Reasonableness.** "Let your reasonableness be known to everyone. The Lord is at hand" (4:5). The word translated "reasonableness" is hard to render; it can mean forbearance, gentleness, not insisting on one's rights. Why can believers let go of insisting on their rights? Because "the Lord is at hand."
- **Two readings of "the Lord is at hand":** near in time, soon to return (see 3:20); or near in space, beside us (see Ps 145:18). Both are biblical, and Paul may intend both.
- **Why we need not fight:** if the Lord is coming soon and everything will be laid bare before him, I need not fight for myself so hard now; if the Lord is beside me, I need not fear being shortchanged.

**3. Do not be anxious about anything.** "Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God" (4:6).
- **"Do not be anxious":** the same word Jesus used in "do not be anxious about your life" (Matt 6:25). This does not mean being irresponsible or refusing to plan, but not letting worry rule our hearts.
- **"In everything":** the opposite of anxiety is not thinking about nothing, but bringing "everything" to God. Nothing is too small, and nothing too large.
- **"With thanksgiving":** to thank God while telling him our needs is to remind ourselves that this God has been faithful before and is listening now.
- **An anxiety disorder is not small faith:** some people's anxiety is an illness that needs treatment. This verse does not say their spiritual life is poor; seeking a doctor's and a counselor's help does not conflict with prayer.

**4. The peace of God will guard you.** "And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus" (4:7).
- **"Surpasses all understanding":** this peace goes beyond human understanding. The problem may not be solved yet, and others may not see why you can be at peace, but God's peace is already there.
- **"Guard":** a military word, meaning to keep watch as a garrison. Philippi was a city with a Roman garrison. God's peace is like a garrison stationed around our hearts and minds, keeping anxiety from breaking in.
- **"In Christ Jesus":** this peace is not a mental technique; it comes because in Christ we are already at peace with God (Rom 5:1).

> The point: Believers need not be ruled by anxiety, not because every problem has been solved, but because the Lord is at hand. When we bring everything to God with thanksgiving, his peace guards our hearts in Christ.

## Going Deeper: 4:8–9 Think About These Things

**1. Eight good things.** "Finally, brothers, whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is commendable, if there is any excellence, if there is anything worthy of praise, think about these things" (4:8).
- **Words people of his day knew:** several of these words were common in Greek philosophers' teaching on virtue. Paul does not mean that pagan morality equals the gospel, but that everything truly good comes from God, and believers may value it and think about it.
- **"Think about":** not just a passing thought, but careful consideration that lets it shape us. What we dwell on slowly shapes who we become.
- **What is most fully true and lovely is Christ himself:** as we think about these good things, they lead us in the end to the source of all goodness.

**2. Practice them.** "What you have learned and received and heard and seen in me—practice these things, and the God of peace will be with you" (4:9). Thinking must lead to doing. Verse 7 speaks of "the peace of God"; verse 9 of "the God of peace." We receive not only peace but the presence of the God who gives it.

> The point: What we dwell on in our hearts, we become. Thinking about all that God has made true and lovely leads us to Christ, and the God of peace will be with us.

## Going Deeper: 4:10–20 I Have Learned to Be Content

**1. Not from need.** "Not that I am speaking of being in need, for I have learned in whatever situation I am to be content" (4:11). Paul thanks the Philippians for their gift but takes care that they do not think he is hinting for more.
- **"Content":** the word was a favorite of the Stoic philosophers, meaning self-sufficient, untouched by outward circumstances. Paul uses the word but changes its meaning: his contentment comes not from himself but from Christ (4:13).
- **"I have learned":** contentment is not inborn but learned, and learned both in being "brought low" and in "abounding" (4:12). Abundance also needs contentment to be learned, for riches can equally make us forget God (Prov 30:8–9).
- **"The secret":** the Greek word was used in the religions of the day for being initiated into a mystery. Paul says he has learned the secret of contentment, and the next verse tells us what it is.

**2. All things through him.** "I can do all things through him who strengthens me" (4:13).
- **The context:** this verse is often applied to exams, sports or business, as if believers can succeed at whatever they attempt. But in context, "all things" means the situations of 4:12: plenty and hunger, abundance and need. Paul means that in any situation he can live content and faithful through Christ.
- **"Him who strengthens me":** the secret is not how strong Paul is but that Christ keeps giving him strength (see 2 Cor 12:9).

**3. Kind to share my trouble.** "Yet it was kind of you to share my trouble" (4:14). Though Paul is content, he does not make light of others' love. "Share" uses the same root as "partnership" in 1:5: by sending money, the Philippians shared in Paul's affliction.

**4. A fragrant offering.** "I am well supplied, having received from Epaphroditus the gifts you sent, a fragrant offering, a sacrifice acceptable and pleasing to God" (4:18).
- **The gift was given to God:** the Philippians thought they were helping Paul, but Paul says it was a "sacrifice" to God (see 2:17; Heb 13:16).
- **"To your credit":** "Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit that increases to your credit" (4:17). Paul uses bookkeeping words again (see 3:7–8). He is glad not for what he has received but because the Philippians' fruit before God has increased.
- **The Philippians were not rich:** elsewhere Paul says of the Macedonian churches that "in a severe test of affliction, their abundance of joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity on their part" (2 Cor 8:2).

**5. God will supply.** "And my God will supply every need of yours according to his riches in glory in Christ Jesus" (4:19).
- **"My God":** Paul speaks personally: the God who has supplied me will supply you.
- **"Every need":** needs, not wants. This is not a guarantee of the prosperity gospel; Paul himself knew hunger and need (4:12).
- **"According to his riches in glory":** not a little taken "from" his riches, but "according to" them, in a measure that matches his wealth.
- **"In Christ Jesus":** God's greatest provision is Christ himself.

**6. Glory to the Father.** "To our God and Father be glory forever and ever. Amen" (4:20). A passage about money ends in praise. Every provision is in the end for the glory of God.

> The point: The secret of contentment is not in circumstances or in our own strength, but in Christ who keeps strengthening us. God will supply every need of ours in Christ, according to his riches in glory.

## Going Deeper: 4:21–23 Caesar's Household

**1. Those of Caesar's household.** "All the saints greet you, especially those of Caesar's household" (4:22). "Caesar's household" does not necessarily mean the emperor's relatives; it refers to the slaves and freedmen who served the emperor, some in the palace and some in government offices across the empire. At the center of Roman power the gospel had taken root (see 1:13). Knowing this would greatly encourage the Philippians.

**2. Ending with grace.** "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit" (4:23). The letter begins with "grace… and peace" (1:2) and ends with "grace." Believers' joy, unity and contentment all begin with the grace of Christ and are completed by it.

> The point: The gospel can enter Caesar's household, and it can enter ours. This letter begins with grace and ends with grace.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus" (4:6–7). What has been keeping me awake lately? Perhaps a medical report, perhaps layoffs at work, perhaps a child who will not talk to me, perhaps parents living alone in my hometown while I am far away. I go over these things in my mind again and again, as if thinking about them more would let me control them. But the more I think, the more confused I get, and the more confused, the more afraid. When Paul wrote these words, he himself may have been facing a death sentence (1:20). He did not say, "Stop thinking about it, it'll be fine," but "in everything… let your requests be made known to God": say aloud, one by one, the things circling in your mind, and hand them to the God who hears prayer. (If my anxiety has become heavy enough to affect my eating, sleeping and work, seeking a doctor's or counselor's help is also God's grace to me.) Today, can I take a sheet of paper, write down my worries, and turn each one into a request to God? Then beside each request, can I write one way God has shown me his faithfulness, in this matter or another, and thank him for it? The problem may still be there tomorrow, but I will not face it alone. Christ has died and risen for me and intercedes for me before the Father; in him, God's peace stands guard over my heart like a garrison. The Lord is at hand.
