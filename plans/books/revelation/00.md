## 这卷书在讲什么

启示录的第一句话，就说明了这是一卷什么样的书：「耶稣基督的启示，就是神赐给他，叫他将必要快成的事指示他的众仆人。」（1:1）「启示」这个词（*apokalypsis*）的意思是揭开、显露，好像把幔子拉开，叫人看见原来隐藏的事。许多学者认为，「耶稣基督的启示」同时有两层意思：这启示是从耶稣基督来的，也是关于耶稣基督的。所以这卷书一开始摆在我们面前的，不是一张末日的时间表，而是耶稣基督自己：「那诚实作见证的、从死里首先复活、为世上君王元首的耶稣基督」（1:5）。

收信的是亚细亚的七个教会（1:4、11）。有的教会正在受苦：士每拿的信徒贫穷，被人毁谤，有人将要被下在监里（2:9-10）；在别迦摩，主「忠心的见证人安提帕」已经被杀（2:13）。有的教会却过得太舒服，像老底嘉自己说的：「我是富足，已经发了财，一样都不缺」（3:17）。对受苦的人，这卷书说：坐在宝座上的仍然是神，被杀的羔羊已经得胜。对安逸、妥协的人，这卷书说：不要被这世界的荣华迷住，因为它必要倾倒。

书中有许多叫人惊惧的画面：揭开的印，吹响的号，倒出的碗，龙和兽，还有大城巴比伦。可是全书的中心不在这些画面上，而在宝座和羔羊身上（4-5章）。约翰听见长老说的是「犹大支派中的狮子」，看见的却是「羔羊站立，像是被杀过的」（5:5-6）。神得胜的方法，就是十字架。到了书的末了，新天新地降临，有大声音说：「看哪，神的帐幕在人间」（21:3）；神的仆人「也要见他的面」（22:4）。

在新约里，只有这卷书一开头就宣告读它、听它的人有福：「念这书上预言的和那些听见又遵守其中所记载的，都是有福的。」（1:3）全书一共七次说到「有福」（1:3；14:13；16:15；19:9；20:6；22:7、14）。这卷书不是写来吓唬人的，而是要叫人蒙福。

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 「我－约翰」。** 作者四次称自己为约翰（1:1、4、9；22:8）。他是神的「仆人」（1:1），又是读者的「弟兄」，和他们「在耶稣的患难、国度、忍耐里一同有分」（1:9）。他没有说明自己是哪一位约翰，可见七个教会都认识他。二世纪中叶的游斯丁说，领受这些启示的约翰是基督的一位使徒（《与特来弗对话》第八十一章）。二世纪末的爱任纽多次引用这卷书，说是主的门徒约翰写的。

**2. 另一种看法。** 三世纪中叶，亚历山大的主教狄奥尼修斯比较了约翰福音、约翰一书和启示录的文笔。他认为启示录的希腊文不如福音书和书信通顺；福音书和约翰一书的作者不写自己的名字，启示录的作者却一再写出来。所以他认为，启示录是一位圣洁、蒙了感动、名叫约翰的人写的，却不一定是使徒约翰。他还提到，以弗所有两座坟墓都被称为约翰的坟墓（优西比乌《教会史》第七卷第二十五章）。现代也有学者认为，作者是长老约翰或另一位名叫约翰的先知。不过许多学者指出，文笔上的差别可能来自体裁的不同：启示录记录的是异象，满了旧约的用语，和福音书、书信的写法本来就不一样。这卷书和约翰福音也有相近的地方，例如在新约中，只有这两卷书称耶稣为「道」（19:13；约1:1）。本研读和约翰一书导读一样，采用传统的看法：作者是使徒约翰。无论采用哪一种看法，这卷书都是「神的道和耶稣基督的见证」（1:2）。

**3. 拔摩海岛。** 约翰说，他「为神的道，并为给耶稣作的见证，曾在那名叫拔摩的海岛上」（1:9）。拔摩是爱琴海上的一个小岛，在以弗所的西南方。多数人认为，约翰是因为传道被放逐到那里。优西比乌引用早期的记载说，约翰在多米田在位时被放逐到拔摩，后来回到以弗所（《教会史》第三卷第十八章、第二十章）。约翰是在「主日」被圣灵感动，看见这些异象的（1:10）。

**4. 写作的时间。** 学者主要有两种看法：
- **主后95年前后，多米田在位的末期：** 这是多数学者的看法（例如毕尔、葛兰·奥斯本）。爱任纽说，约翰看见这异象，离他那一代不远，差不多在多米田在位的末了（《驳异端》第五卷第三十章第三节）。这些学者还指出，老底嘉在主后60年的大地震以后，没有靠罗马的帮助，自己重建了城（塔西佗《编年史》第十四卷第二十七章），这很合老底嘉自夸「一样都不缺」（3:17）；而用「巴比伦」称呼罗马，在主后70年圣殿被毁以后的犹太作品中最常见。
- **主后68年前后，尼禄死后不久：** 一些学者（例如约翰·罗宾逊、肯尼斯·詹特里）这样主张。他们认为，第11章丈量圣殿，好像表示圣殿还在；第17章的「七位王」可以从罗马最早的几位皇帝数起；兽的数目六百六十六，也可以按希伯来字母算成尼禄该撒这个名字（13:18）。
本研读采用较晚的年代。不过前五章的解释并不取决于这个问题；到了后面的章节，读者会看到年代怎样影响某些经文的解释。

**5. 教会面对的压力。** 从前常有人说，多米田在整个罗马帝国大规模地逼迫基督徒；现代许多学者认为，这方面的证据并不充分。七封信让我们看见的压力，多半是地方性的：
- **敬拜皇帝：** 亚细亚各城热心为罗马和皇帝建庙，许多学者认为以弗所在多米田时也建了一座皇帝的庙。不肯参与的人，容易被看作不忠于本城和皇帝。
- **生意和宴席：** 行会的聚餐常和偶像连在一起，于是有人教导信徒「吃祭偶像之物」也无妨（2:14、20）。
- **会堂的敌对：** 士每拿和非拉铁非的信徒，受到一些犹太人的毁谤（2:9；3:9）。
- **安逸和妥协：** 主对撒狄说「按名你是活的，其实是死的」（3:1），老底嘉「不冷也不热」（3:15）。
无论是受逼迫还是过得安逸，启示录都要信徒看清：世上的国看起来强大，真正作王的却是我主和主基督（11:15）。

**6. 七个真实的教会。** 这七个城都在罗马的亚细亚省，就是今天土耳其的西部。从以弗所往北到士每拿、别迦摩，再往东南到推雅推喇、撒狄、非拉铁非、老底嘉，正好是一条环形的路线，送信的人可以按这个次序走一圈。圣经中的「七」常有完全的意思，所以许多学者认为，这七个教会也代表各地、各时代的教会。每封信的末了都说：「圣灵向众教会所说的话，凡有耳的，就应当听！」（2:7）有些人（例如《司可福串珠圣经》）把七个教会看作教会历史上先后的七个时期，老底嘉就是末世的教会；另一些人认为，这是七个历史上真实的教会，每一封信都向各时代所有的教会说话。第2-3章会再讨论这个问题。

## 钥匙经文：1:3；1:17-18；21:3-5；22:20

**1. 「听见又遵守」。** 「念这书上预言的和那些听见又遵守其中所记载的，都是有福的，因为日期近了。」（1:3）「念」的是一个人，「听见」的却是许多人。这让我们看见当时的情景：教会聚集的时候，有一个人把整卷书念出来，众人一同听。约翰盼望的是众人「遵守」，不只是解开其中的谜。启示录是要人听进去、活出来的话：敬拜神，不拜兽；在患难中忍耐；至死忠心（2:10；14:12）。

**2. 「我曾死过，现在又活了」。** 「我一看见，就仆倒在他脚前，像死了一样。他用右手按着我，说：『不要惧怕！我是首先的，我是末后的，又是那存活的；我曾死过，现在又活了，直活到永永远远；并且拿着死亡和阴间的钥匙。』」（1:17-18）后面的灾难、争战和审判，都要在这句话之下来读。拿着死亡和阴间钥匙的，正是为我们死过、又复活了的那一位。

**3. 「我将一切都更新了」。** 「我听见有大声音从宝座出来说：『看哪，神的帐幕在人间。他要与人同住，他们要作他的子民。神要亲自与他们同在，作他们的神。神要擦去他们一切的眼泪；不再有死亡，也不再有悲哀、哭号、疼痛，因为以前的事都过去了。』坐宝座的说：『看哪，我将一切都更新了！』」（21:3-5）这是整本圣经故事的结局。从伊甸园到会幕，从圣殿到道成肉身，神一直应许要住在他百姓中间（利26:11-12；结37:27；约1:14）。到那日，这应许要完全成就。神不是把这个世界丢弃，而是「将一切都更新了」。

**4. 「主耶稣啊，我愿你来」。** 「证明这事的说：『是了，我必快来！』阿们！主耶稣啊，我愿你来！」（22:20）圣经最后的一个应许，是主说「我必快来」；最后的一个祷告，是教会回答「我愿你来」。保罗在哥林多前书末了用亚兰文写的「主必要来」（林前16:22），可能也是初期教会同样的祷告。启示录不是要我们害怕主的再来，而是教我们盼望他来。

## 全书结构

启示录大致可以这样分：
- **序言**（1:1-8）：耶稣基督的启示；念的、听的、遵守的有福；问安和颂赞；「看哪，他驾云降临」。
- **人子的异象**（1:9-20）：约翰在拔摩；人子在七个金灯台中间。
- **给七个教会的信**（2-3章）：以弗所、士每拿、别迦摩、推雅推喇、撒狄、非拉铁非、老底嘉。
- **宝座和羔羊**（4-5章）：天上的宝座；用七印封严的书卷；被杀的羔羊配展开书卷。
- **七印**（6:1-8:1）：中间插入受印的十四万四千人，和从各国来、没有人能数过来的许多人（第7章）。
- **七号**（8:2-11:19）：中间插入小书卷和两个见证人（10:1-11:14）。
- **妇人、龙和两只兽**（12-14章）：天上的争战；兽的印记；羔羊和跟随他的人；地上的收割。
- **七碗**（15-16章）：神的大怒倒在地上。
- **巴比伦倾倒**（17:1-19:10）：大淫妇和她所骑的兽；为巴比伦的哀歌；天上的哈利路亚；羔羊的婚筵。
- **骑白马的和最后的审判**（19:11-20:15）：基督得胜；一千年；撒但被扔在火湖里；白色的大宝座。
- **新天新地和新耶路撒冷**（21:1-22:5）：神与人同住；生命水的河和生命树。
- **结语**（22:6-21）：「看哪，我必快来！」；不可加添，不可删去；「主耶稣啊，我愿你来！」

读结构时，有两个问题会影响我们怎样读整卷书：
- **1:19的分法：** 「所以你要把所看见的，和现在的事，并将来必成的事，都写出来。」（1:19）时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）常按这一节把全书分成三段：第1章是「所看见的」，第2-3章是「现在的事」，第4-22章是「将来必成的事」。毕尔等学者却认为，这三句话描写的是整卷书，因为书中每一部分都同时讲到过去、现在和将来。
- **印、号、碗是先后发生，还是重复叙述？** 未来派常认为，七印、七号、七碗是先后发生的事（有人认为第七印里包含了七号，第七号里又包含了七碗）。另一些学者认为，这三组「七」是从不同的角度、一次比一次强烈地描写同一段时期，就是从基督第一次来到他再来之间的时期。这种重述的读法很早就有，三世纪的维克多利努已经这样读；二十世纪的威廉·亨德里克森在《得胜有余》中也这样主张。值得留意的是，第六印、第七号和第七碗，好像都已经到了末了（6:12-17；11:15-18；16:17-21）。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 一卷书，三种文体。** 启示录同时是三种文体：
- **启示文学：** 「耶稣基督的启示」（1:1）。像但以理书和撒迦利亚书的一些部分一样，用异象、象征和天使的解说，揭开天上的实况和历史的结局。
- **预言：** 「这书上预言」（1:3）。像旧约的先知一样，呼召神的百姓悔改、忠心。
- **书信：** 「约翰写信给亚细亚的七个教会」（1:4）。有问安，也有祝福（22:21），是写给真实的人、真实的处境的。
所以读启示录，先要问：这段话对当年亚细亚的信徒是什么意思？然后再问：对今天的我们又是什么意思？

**2. 用旧约来读。** 启示录几乎没有正式引用旧约，却处处是旧约的回声；有学者统计，全书提到旧约的地方有几百处。人子驾云而来，出自但以理书（但7:13）；四活物让人想起以西结书第1章和以赛亚书第6章；金灯台和橄榄树出自撒迦利亚书第4章；号和碗的灾难，常叫人想起出埃及时的十灾；新天新地出自以赛亚书第65章。读不懂一个画面时，最好的办法常常是先回到旧约，看神从前在那里怎样用这个画面。

**3. 象征和数字。** 启示录用画面说话。约翰自己解释了一些画面：「七星就是七个教会的使者，七灯台就是七个教会」（1:20）；「大龙就是那古蛇，名叫魔鬼，又叫撒但」（12:9）。许多学者认为，书中的数字多有象征意义：「七」表示完全，「十二」常和神的百姓相连，「一千」表示极大的数目。包衡还指出一个常见的写法：约翰先听见一样，转过来看见的却是另一样，而看见的解释了听见的。他听见「犹大支派中的狮子」，看见的是被杀的羔羊（5:5-6）；他听见受印的有「十四万四千」，看见的是「许多的人，没有人能数过来」（7:4、9）。

**4. 四种读法。** 历代教会读启示录，大致有四种读法：
- **过去派：** 认为书中多数的预言（特别是第4-19章）已经在一世纪应验，指的是耶路撒冷被毁或罗马的逼迫（例如肯尼斯·詹特里）。这种读法认真看待最初的读者和「必要快成」的话。部分过去派仍相信基督将来要身体再来；完全过去派却认为，连主的再来和死人复活都已经发生了，这和历代教会的信经（例如《尼西亚信经》说，主要从荣耀里降临，审判活人死人）不合。
- **历史派：** 认为这卷书按次序预言了从使徒时代到主再来的教会历史。马丁·路德和许多宗教改革者这样读，常把兽或大淫妇和当时的罗马教廷连在一起。这种读法认真看待这卷书对整个教会时代说话。
- **未来派：** 认为第4-22章大部分仍在将来，特别是主再来以前的大灾难。时代论者（例如司可福、华尔伏）和许多历史前千禧年派（例如乔治·赖德）都是这样读。这种读法认真看待这卷书指向一个真实的结局。
- **理想派（又称属灵派）：** 认为这卷书不是指某些特定的事件，而是描写神和撒但、教会和世界之间历世历代的争战（例如十九世纪的威廉·米利根）。这种读法认真看待这卷书对每一代信徒说话。
本研读和许多福音派学者一样（例如毕尔、包衡、葛兰·奥斯本），采取综合的读法：这卷书写给一世纪七个真实的教会；它所揭示的争战和原则，向每个时代的教会说话；它也指向将来真实的结局，就是基督身体的再来、死人复活、最后的审判和新天新地。

**5. 千禧年。** 第20章说到「一千年」（20:4-6），历代信徒有四种主要的看法：
- **无千禧年派：** 认为一千年象征从基督第一次来到他再来之间的整个时期；撒但被捆绑，是不能再拦阻福音传到万国。奥古斯丁在《上帝之城》第二十卷这样解释；现代的代表有安东尼·胡克玛。
- **历史前千禧年派：** 认为基督先再来，然后在地上作王一千年（或一段很长的时期）；教会要经过大灾难。早期教会的游斯丁和爱任纽都持这种看法；现代的代表有乔治·赖德。
- **时代论前千禧年派：** 也认为基督在千禧年以前再来，但把以色列和教会分开，多数认为教会在大灾难以前被提（例如司可福、华尔伏、查尔斯·莱里）。不少华人教会深受这种看法的影响。
- **后千禧年派：** 认为福音要广传，世界要经过一段长久的公义与和平，然后基督才再来（例如约拿单·爱德华兹）。
这四种看法在福音派教会中都有。他们都相信：基督要亲自、身体地再来，死人要复活，神要审判世界，并且要有新天新地。第20章会再详细讨论。

**6. 「必要快成」和「日期近了」。** 这卷书一开头和末了都说到「快」和「近」（1:1、3；22:6、10、20）。过去派认为，这是指书写成以后不久的一世纪事件。许多学者则认为，自从基督复活升天，末后的日子就已经开始了，神计划中下一件大事就是主的再来，所以它一直是「近」的；而主看时间和我们不同，他的「耽延」其实是宽容，等候人悔改（彼后3:8-9）。也有人认为「快」可以指事情来到时的突然。无论如何，主耶稣说过，那日子那时辰没有人知道（太24:36；徒1:7）。这些话不是要我们推算日期，而是叫教会时时预备好。

**7. 小心误用这卷书。** 启示录常被人误用，华人教会也不例外：
- **定日期：** 历史上有许多人宣布过主再来或被提的日期，例如1992年韩国的达米宣教会、2011年美国的哈罗德·康平；华人教会中也常有类似的传言。日期一次次过去，有人因此辞去工作、变卖产业，事后灰心，甚至离开教会。
- **猜测六百六十六：** 约翰说「在这里有智慧」（13:18），历代却有许多人把这数目套在某个人物、某样科技上；疫情那几年，也有人说健康码或疫苗就是兽的印记。有些古抄本把这数目写成六百一十六，爱任纽已经知道这件事（《驳异端》第五卷第三十章第一节），他也劝人不要贸然说出敌基督的名字（同上，第三节）。在启示录里，兽的印记（13:16-17）和神仆人额上的印、羔羊和父的名（7:3；14:1）相对。经文关心的，首先是我属于谁、敬拜谁。
- **东方闪电（自称全能神教会）：** 这个异端声称基督已经再来，成为一位中国女子；他们的材料常说，她就是那展开书卷的羔羊，又带来了圣经以外的新话语。可是启示录说，配展开书卷的，是那「像是被杀过的」羔羊（5:6），就是为我们钉十字架又复活的耶稣；书末也警告，不可在这书的预言上加添什么（22:18-19）。如果家人或朋友陷进这类团体，可以和牧者商量，不要独自去他们的聚会；如果有人身控制、威胁或骗取钱财的事，应当报警。
- **用恐惧传道：** 有些讲道只用灾难和兽来吓人，叫人害怕，却不叫人看见羔羊。这卷书本来是要叫人「有福」的。
这些提醒不是叫我们怀疑每一位讲启示录的传道人。许多忠心的牧者谨慎、敬虔地讲解这卷书，我们应当敬重他们（帖前5:12-13）。

**8. 在敬拜中读。** 启示录满了天上的诗歌：「圣哉！圣哉！圣哉！主神是昔在、今在、以后永在的全能者」（4:8）；被杀的羔羊配得「权柄、丰富、智慧、能力、尊贵、荣耀、颂赞」（5:12）；「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国」（11:15）；还有摩西的歌和羔羊的歌（15:3-4），以及天上的哈利路亚（19:1-8）。韩德尔《弥赛亚》的《哈利路亚》大合唱，就用了这卷书的话。这卷书要我们从天上的宝座来看地上的事，然后敬拜，然后忍耐。最初的听众是在聚会中一次听完全书的；我们也可以找一个时间，像他们一样，一次把全书念出来、听下去。

## 默想

**默想：** 「耶稣基督的启示……念这书上预言的和那些听见又遵守其中所记载的，都是有福的。」（1:1、3）外婆那本圣经，启示录那几页几乎还是新的。九十年代初，她在乡下的家庭聚会里听一位巡回传道人讲末世，墙上挂着一张手画的大图：七印、七号、七碗，一只七头十角的兽，旁边还写着一个年份。那一年过去了，什么事也没有发生，可是那一晚的害怕却留了下来。从那以后，她每年读经读到启示录就跳过去，也劝孙辈别读，说「读了心里不平安」。她的孙子却正好相反。疫情那几年，他转发过「健康码就是兽的印记」的文章；现在他有一本笔记，把新闻里的战争、地震、物价一条一条对到号和碗上。外婆不敢打开这卷书；孙子打开了，却只在里面找密码。

其实两个人错过的是同一件事。这卷书的第一句话就说，它是「耶稣基督的启示」（1:1）。幔子拉开，先显出来的不是一张图表，而是一个人。约翰刚问完安，就忍不住称颂他：「他爱我们，用自己的血使我们脱离罪恶，又使我们成为国民，作他父神的祭司。」（1:5-6）许多解经家留意到，「爱」在原文是现在时态，「使我们脱离」却是不定过去时：他用自己的血一次把我们释放了，也一直爱着我们。约翰叫我们「遵守」以前，先把这根基放在我们面前。站在宝座前的那许多人，不是解开了什么密码，而是「用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了」（7:14）。这卷书所应许的福，从来不是给最会推算的人，也不是给最不怕的人，而是给被羔羊的血洗净、又来听他说话的人。

如果我就是那个孙子，曾经花很多年找记号，把吓人的预言转给家人，甚至向朋友说过一个日期，后来却落空了，我可以看看约翰自己。书快结束的时候，他听见、看见了这一切，竟然在天使脚前俯伏要拜他，而且不止一次（19:10；22:8-9）。领受这些异象的使徒，也会被传信的那一位吸引住，忘了那位主。天使没有因此把书从他手里拿走，只说：「千万不可！……你要敬拜神。」又说：「预言中的灵意乃是为耶稣作见证。」（19:10）接着天使还说：「不可封了这书上的预言」（22:10）。这卷书仍然交在约翰手里。主对我也是这样：他不是说「你不配读这卷书」，而是把我的眼目从记号转回到他身上。如果我是那位外婆，多年来一想到这卷书就害怕，主也没有因为我跳过了它就不再爱我；他所赐的这卷书，本来就是要叫我蒙福的。

所以，「遵守」这书，不是先把每一个记号算清楚，而是听见了，就敬拜神，不向这世界的兽屈膝；在难处中忍耐；不被巴比伦的繁华买去；并且盼望他来。也许外婆和孙子可以坐在一起，一个念、一个听，像最初的教会那样，从第1章念起，念到第4-5章的宝座和羔羊，再念到第21-22章的新天新地。

到了书的末了，主耶稣用自己的名字说话：「我－耶稣差遣我的使者为众教会将这些事向你们证明。我是大卫的根，又是他的后裔。我是明亮的晨星。」（22:16）晨星在夜里最黑的时候升起，看见它的人就知道，黑夜不会一直下去。那卷叫外婆害怕的书，最后落在的不是一只兽，而是一个名字；孙子一直在找的，从来不是一个数字，而是一个人。在一切的号声和碗以先，在墙上任何一张图表以先，他已经在那里：爱我们、用自己的血使我们脱离罪恶的耶稣。在最黑的夜里先升起来的，是他。

## What This Book Is About

The first sentence of Revelation tells us what kind of book it is: "The revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave him to show to his servants the things that must soon take place" (1:1). The word "revelation" (*apokalypsis*) means an uncovering or unveiling, like a curtain drawn back so that what was hidden can be seen. Many scholars think "the revelation of Jesus Christ" carries two senses at once: it comes from Jesus Christ, and it is about Jesus Christ. So what the book first sets before us is not a timetable of the end, but Jesus Christ himself: "Jesus Christ the faithful witness, the firstborn of the dead, and the ruler of kings on earth" (1:5).

It is addressed to seven churches in Asia (1:4, 11). Some of them were suffering. The believers in Smyrna were poor and slandered, and some were about to be thrown into prison (2:9–10). In Pergamum, "Antipas my faithful witness" had already been killed (2:13). Other churches were far too comfortable, like Laodicea, which said of itself, "I am rich, I have prospered, and I need nothing" (3:17). To those who suffer, the book says: God is still on the throne, and the slain Lamb has already conquered. To the comfortable and compromising, it says: do not be dazzled by the splendor of this world, for it will fall.

The book is full of fearful pictures: seals opened, trumpets blown, bowls poured out, a dragon and beasts, and the great city Babylon. Yet the center of the book is not these pictures. It is the throne and the Lamb (chs. 4–5). John hears the elder speak of "the Lion of the tribe of Judah," but what he sees is "a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain" (5:5–6). God's way of victory is the cross. At the end of the book a new heaven and a new earth come, and a loud voice says, "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man" (21:3); his servants "will see his face" (22:4).

In the New Testament, only this book begins by declaring a blessing on those who read and hear it: "Blessed is the one who reads aloud the words of this prophecy, and blessed are those who hear, and who keep what is written in it" (1:3). Seven times in all the book says "blessed" (1:3; 14:13; 16:15; 19:9; 20:6; 22:7, 14). It was not written to frighten people. It was written so that they would be blessed.

## Author and Setting

**1. "I, John."** The writer calls himself John four times (1:1, 4, 9; 22:8). He is God's "servant" (1:1), and the readers' "brother and partner in the tribulation and the kingdom and the patient endurance that are in Jesus" (1:9). He does not explain which John he is, so the seven churches must have known him. In the mid-second century Justin Martyr says that the John who received these revelations was one of Christ's apostles (*Dialogue with Trypho* 81). At the end of the second century Irenaeus quotes the book many times as the work of John, the Lord's disciple.

**2. Another view.** In the mid-third century Dionysius, bishop of Alexandria, compared the style of John's Gospel, 1 John and Revelation. He judged that the Greek of Revelation was less polished than that of the Gospel and the letter, and that while the writer of the Gospel and 1 John never gives his own name, the writer of Revelation gives it again and again. So he concluded that Revelation was written by a holy and inspired man named John, but not necessarily by the apostle. He also mentions that in Ephesus two tombs were each said to be John's (Eusebius, *Church History* 7.25). Some modern scholars likewise think the author was "John the elder" or another prophet named John. But many scholars point out that the difference in style may come from the difference in genre: Revelation records visions and is saturated with Old Testament language, and so was always going to read differently from a Gospel or a letter. Revelation and John's Gospel also have things in common; for example, they are the only New Testament books that call Jesus "the Word" (19:13; John 1:1). As in the introduction to 1 John, this study takes the traditional view that the author is the apostle John. On any view, the book is "the word of God and the testimony of Jesus Christ" (1:2).

**3. The island of Patmos.** John says he "was on the island called Patmos on account of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus" (1:9). Patmos is a small island in the Aegean Sea, southwest of Ephesus. Most think John had been exiled there for preaching. Eusebius, drawing on earlier accounts, says that John was banished to Patmos under Domitian and later returned to Ephesus (*Church History* 3.18, 3.20). It was "on the Lord's day," "in the Spirit," that John saw these visions (1:10).

**4. Date.** Scholars hold two main views:
- **Around AD 95, late in Domitian's reign:** This is the view of most scholars (for example G. K. Beale and Grant Osborne). Irenaeus says that John saw the vision not long before his own generation, toward the end of Domitian's reign (*Against Heresies* 5.30.3). These scholars also note that after the great earthquake of AD 60 Laodicea rebuilt itself without Roman help (Tacitus, *Annals* 14.27), which fits Laodicea's boast that it needed nothing (3:17); and that calling Rome "Babylon" is most common in Jewish writings after the temple was destroyed in AD 70.
- **Around AD 68, soon after Nero's death:** Some scholars argue for this (for example John A. T. Robinson and Kenneth Gentry). They suggest that the measuring of the temple in chapter 11 implies the temple was still standing; that the "seven kings" of chapter 17 can be counted from the first Roman emperors; and that the number of the beast, 666, can be reckoned from the Hebrew letters of "Nero Caesar" (13:18).
This study follows the later date. But nothing in chapters 1–5 depends on the question; in later chapters readers will see how the date affects the reading of some passages.

**5. The pressures the churches faced.** It used to be said that Domitian carried out a great empire-wide persecution of Christians; many modern scholars think the evidence for this is not strong. The pressures the seven letters show us were mostly local:
- **Emperor worship:** The cities of Asia competed to build temples to Rome and the emperors, and many scholars think Ephesus gained one under Domitian. Those who would not take part could easily be seen as disloyal to their city and to Caesar.
- **Trade and banquets:** Guild meals were often bound up with idols, so some taught believers that they could "eat food sacrificed to idols" without harm (2:14, 20).
- **Hostility from the synagogue:** Believers in Smyrna and Philadelphia were slandered by some Jews (2:9; 3:9).
- **Comfort and compromise:** Sardis had "the reputation of being alive" but was dead (3:1); Laodicea was "neither cold nor hot" (3:15).
Whether under persecution or in comfort, Revelation wants believers to see clearly: the kingdoms of the world look strong, but the one who reigns is our Lord and his Christ (11:15).

**6. Seven real churches.** All seven cities lay in the Roman province of Asia, in what is now western Turkey. North from Ephesus to Smyrna and Pergamum, then southeast to Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia and Laodicea, they form a circuit that a messenger could travel in that order. In Scripture "seven" often carries the sense of completeness, so many scholars think these seven churches also stand for the church in every place and age. Each letter ends, "He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches" (2:7). Some (for example the *Scofield Reference Bible*) read the seven churches as seven successive periods of church history, with Laodicea as the church of the last days; others read them as seven real historical churches, each letter speaking to all the churches of every age. Chapters 2–3 return to this question.

## Key Verses: 1:3; 1:17–18; 21:3–5; 22:20

**1. "Those who hear, and who keep."** "Blessed is the one who reads aloud the words of this prophecy, and blessed are those who hear, and who keep what is written in it, for the time is near." (1:3) The one who reads aloud is singular; those who hear are plural. This shows us the original scene: when the church gathered, one person read the whole book aloud and everyone listened together. What John hopes for is that they will "keep" it, not merely crack its riddles. Revelation is a word to be taken in and lived out: worship God and not the beast; endure in tribulation; be faithful unto death (2:10; 14:12).

**2. "I died, and behold I am alive."** "When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead. But he laid his right hand on me, saying, 'Fear not, I am the first and the last, and the living one. I died, and behold I am alive forevermore, and I have the keys of Death and Hades.'" (1:17–18) The disasters, battles and judgments that follow are all to be read under this sentence. The one who holds the keys of Death and Hades is the one who died for us and rose again.

**3. "I am making all things new."** "And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, 'Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God. He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning nor crying nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away.' And he who was seated on the throne said, 'Behold, I am making all things new.'" (21:3–5) This is the ending of the whole story of the Bible. From Eden to the tabernacle, from the temple to the incarnation, God kept promising to live among his people (Lev 26:11–12; Ezek 37:27; John 1:14). On that day the promise will be fully kept. God does not throw this world away; he is "making all things new."

**4. "Come, Lord Jesus!"** "He who testifies to these things says, 'Surely I am coming soon.' Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!" (22:20) The Bible's last promise is the Lord saying, "I am coming soon"; its last prayer is the church answering, "Come." The Aramaic "Our Lord, come!" at the end of 1 Corinthians (1 Cor 16:22) may well be the same prayer of the early church. Revelation does not teach us to dread the Lord's return. It teaches us to long for it.

## Structure of the Book

Revelation can be divided roughly like this:
- **Prologue** (1:1–8): the revelation of Jesus Christ; blessed are those who read, hear and keep; greeting and doxology; "Behold, he is coming with the clouds."
- **The vision of the Son of Man** (1:9–20): John on Patmos; one like a son of man among seven golden lampstands.
- **Letters to seven churches** (chs. 2–3): Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, Laodicea.
- **The throne and the Lamb** (chs. 4–5): the throne in heaven; the scroll sealed with seven seals; the slain Lamb is worthy to open it.
- **Seven seals** (6:1–8:1): with an interlude of the 144,000 who are sealed and the great multitude from every nation that no one could number (ch. 7).
- **Seven trumpets** (8:2–11:19): with an interlude of the little scroll and the two witnesses (10:1–11:14).
- **The woman, the dragon and the two beasts** (chs. 12–14): war in heaven; the mark of the beast; the Lamb and those who follow him; the harvest of the earth.
- **Seven bowls** (chs. 15–16): the wrath of God poured out on the earth.
- **The fall of Babylon** (17:1–19:10): the great prostitute and the beast she rides; laments over Babylon; hallelujahs in heaven; the marriage supper of the Lamb.
- **The rider on the white horse and the last judgment** (19:11–20:15): Christ's victory; the thousand years; Satan thrown into the lake of fire; the great white throne.
- **The new heaven and new earth and the new Jerusalem** (21:1–22:5): God dwelling with his people; the river of the water of life and the tree of life.
- **Epilogue** (22:6–21): "Behold, I am coming soon"; nothing to be added or taken away; "Come, Lord Jesus!"

Two questions about the structure shape how we read the whole book:
- **How to divide by 1:19:** "Write therefore the things that you have seen, those that are and those that are to take place after this." (1:19) Dispensational scholars (for example John Walvoord) often divide the book into three parts by this verse: chapter 1 is "the things that you have seen," chapters 2–3 "those that are," and chapters 4–22 "those that are to take place after this." Beale and others think the three phrases describe the whole book, since every part of it speaks of past, present and future at once.
- **Are the seals, trumpets and bowls in sequence, or retellings?** Futurists usually read the seven seals, trumpets and bowls as events that follow one another (some think the seventh seal contains the seven trumpets, and the seventh trumpet the seven bowls). Other scholars think the three sets of seven describe the same period from different angles and with growing intensity: the time between Christ's first coming and his return. This "recapitulation" reading is old; Victorinus of Pettau read the book this way in the third century, and in the twentieth William Hendriksen argued for it in *More Than Conquerors*. It is worth noticing that the sixth seal, the seventh trumpet and the seventh bowl each seem to bring us to the end (6:12–17; 11:15–18; 16:17–21).

## How to Read This Book

**1. One book, three genres.** Revelation is three kinds of writing at once:
- **Apocalypse:** "The revelation of Jesus Christ" (1:1). Like parts of Daniel and Zechariah, it uses visions, symbols and angelic interpreters to unveil what is true in heaven and how history will end.
- **Prophecy:** "The words of this prophecy" (1:3). Like the Old Testament prophets, it calls God's people to repent and be faithful.
- **Letter:** "John to the seven churches that are in Asia" (1:4). It has a greeting and a closing blessing (22:21), and was written to real people in real situations.
So in reading Revelation, first ask what a passage meant to those believers in Asia, and then ask what it means for us today.

**2. Read it with the Old Testament.** Revelation hardly ever formally quotes the Old Testament, yet it echoes it everywhere; by some scholars' counts it alludes to the Old Testament several hundred times. The son of man coming with the clouds comes from Daniel (Dan 7:13). The four living creatures recall Ezekiel 1 and Isaiah 6. The lampstands and olive trees come from Zechariah 4. The plagues of the trumpets and bowls recall the plagues of the exodus. The new heavens and new earth come from Isaiah 65. When a picture is hard to understand, the best first step is often to go back to the Old Testament and see how God used that picture there.

**3. Symbols and numbers.** Revelation speaks in pictures. John explains some of them himself: "the seven stars are the angels of the seven churches, and the seven lampstands are the seven churches" (1:20); "the great dragon... that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan" (12:9). Many scholars think most of the book's numbers are symbolic: seven for completeness, twelve often tied to the people of God, a thousand for a vast number. Bauckham also points to a pattern: John hears one thing, then turns and sees another, and what he sees interprets what he heard. He hears of "the Lion of the tribe of Judah" and sees the slain Lamb (5:5–6); he hears the number of the sealed, "144,000," and sees "a great multitude that no one could number" (7:4, 9).

**4. Four approaches.** Through the centuries the church has read Revelation in four main ways:
- **Preterist:** Most of the prophecies (especially chs. 4–19) were fulfilled in the first century, in the fall of Jerusalem or Rome's persecution (for example Kenneth Gentry). This approach takes seriously the first readers and the words "must soon take place." Partial preterists still look for Christ's bodily return; full preterists hold that even the Lord's return and the resurrection have already happened, which conflicts with the church's historic creeds (the Nicene Creed confesses that he will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead).
- **Historicist:** The book foretells, in order, the history of the church from the apostles to the Lord's return. Martin Luther and many Reformers read it this way, often linking the beast or the prostitute with the papacy of their day. This approach takes seriously that the book speaks to the whole church age.
- **Futurist:** Most of chapters 4–22 are still future, especially a great tribulation before the Lord's return. Dispensationalists (for example Scofield and Walvoord) and many historic premillennialists (for example George Eldon Ladd) read it this way. This approach takes seriously that the book points to a real end.
- **Idealist (or spiritual):** The book does not point to particular events but portrays the conflict, in every age, between God and Satan, the church and the world (for example William Milligan in the nineteenth century). This approach takes seriously that the book speaks to every generation of believers.
Like many evangelical scholars (for example Beale, Richard Bauckham and Grant Osborne), this study reads eclectically: the book was written to seven real churches in the first century; the conflict and principles it unveils speak to the church in every age; and it points to a real end still to come, Christ's bodily return, the resurrection of the dead, the last judgment and the new heaven and new earth.

**5. The millennium.** Chapter 20 speaks of "a thousand years" (20:4–6). Believers have held four main views:
- **Amillennial:** The thousand years symbolize the whole period between Christ's first and second comings; Satan is bound in that he can no longer stop the gospel from going to the nations. Augustine explains it this way in *The City of God*, book 20; a modern representative is Anthony Hoekema.
- **Historic premillennial:** Christ returns first and then reigns on earth for a thousand years (or a long period); the church passes through the great tribulation. Justin Martyr and Irenaeus in the early church held this view; a modern representative is George Eldon Ladd.
- **Dispensational premillennial:** Christ also returns before the millennium, but Israel and the church are kept distinct, and most hold that the church is raptured before the tribulation (for example Scofield, Walvoord and Charles Ryrie). Many Chinese churches have been deeply shaped by this view.
- **Postmillennial:** The gospel will spread until the world enjoys a long age of righteousness and peace, and then Christ will return (for example Jonathan Edwards).
All four views are found in evangelical churches. All agree that Christ will return personally and bodily, the dead will be raised, God will judge the world, and there will be a new heaven and a new earth. Chapter 20 discusses this further.

**6. "Must soon take place" and "the time is near."** The book's opening and closing both speak of "soon" and "near" (1:1, 3; 22:6, 10, 20). Preterists take this to mean first-century events shortly after the book was written. Many scholars think instead that since Christ's resurrection and ascension the last days have already begun, and the next great event in God's plan is the Lord's return, so it is always "near"; and the Lord does not count time as we do, for his apparent "slowness" is patience, waiting for people to repent (2 Pet 3:8–9). Some also take "soon" to include the suddenness of its coming. In any case, the Lord Jesus said that no one knows the day or the hour (Matt 24:36; Acts 1:7). These words are not given so that we can calculate dates, but so that the church will always be ready.

**7. Beware of misusing the book.** Revelation is often misused, and Chinese churches are no exception:
- **Setting dates:** Many have announced the date of the Lord's return or the rapture, for example the Dami Mission in Korea in 1992 and Harold Camping in America in 2011; similar rumors have often run through Chinese churches. As the dates passed, some who had quit their jobs or sold their property were left disillusioned, and some left the church.
- **Guessing at 666:** John says, "This calls for wisdom" (13:18), yet through the centuries many have pinned the number on some person or some technology; during the pandemic years some said the health code or the vaccine was the mark of the beast. Some ancient manuscripts read the number as 616, which Irenaeus already knew (*Against Heresies* 5.30.1), and he also advised against rashly naming the antichrist (5.30.3). In Revelation the mark of the beast (13:16–17) stands opposite the seal on the foreheads of God's servants, the name of the Lamb and his Father (7:3; 14:1). What the text cares about first is whom I belong to and whom I worship.
- **Eastern Lightning (which calls itself the Church of Almighty God):** This sect claims that Christ has already returned as a Chinese woman; its materials often say that she is the Lamb who opens the scroll, and that she has brought new words beyond the Bible. But Revelation says the one worthy to open the scroll is the Lamb "as though it had been slain" (5:6), Jesus, crucified for us and risen; and at its end the book warns against adding anything to its prophecy (22:18–19). If a family member or friend is drawn into such a group, talk with a pastor and do not go to their meetings alone; where there is physical control, threats or the taking of money by deceit, report it to the police.
- **Preaching by fear:** Some preaching uses only disasters and beasts to frighten people, and never shows them the Lamb. The book was meant to make people "blessed."
These warnings are not a reason to suspect every preacher who teaches Revelation. Many faithful pastors expound the book carefully and reverently, and we should honor them (1 Thess 5:12–13).

**8. Read it in worship.** Revelation is full of the songs of heaven: "Holy, holy, holy, is the Lord God Almighty, who was and is and is to come!" (4:8); the slain Lamb is worthy "to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honor and glory and blessing" (5:12); "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ" (11:15); the song of Moses and the song of the Lamb (15:3–4); and the hallelujahs of heaven (19:1–8). The "Hallelujah" chorus of Handel's *Messiah* takes its words from this book. Revelation teaches us to look at earth from the throne in heaven, and then to worship, and then to endure. Its first hearers heard the whole book at one sitting in the gathered church; we too can set aside a time to read it all aloud and listen, as they did.

## Meditation

**Meditation:** "The revelation of Jesus Christ... Blessed is the one who reads aloud the words of this prophecy, and blessed are those who hear, and who keep what is written in it." (1:1, 3) In Grandma's Bible, the pages of Revelation are still almost new. In the early 1990s, at a house-church meeting in her village, she heard a traveling preacher speak on the end times. On the wall hung a large hand-drawn chart: seven seals, seven trumpets, seven bowls, a beast with seven heads and ten horns, and a year written beside it. That year came and went and nothing happened, but the fear of that evening stayed. Ever since, when her yearly reading reaches Revelation she skips it, and she tells her grandchildren not to read it either: "It will only unsettle you." Her grandson is the opposite. During the pandemic years he forwarded articles saying the health code was the mark of the beast; now he keeps a notebook matching wars, earthquakes and prices in the news, one by one, to the trumpets and bowls. Grandma does not dare open the book; her grandson has opened it, but he is only looking for a code.

In fact both have missed the same thing. The book's very first sentence says it is "the revelation of Jesus Christ" (1:1). When the curtain is drawn back, what appears first is not a chart but a person. John has barely finished his greeting before he breaks into praise: "To him who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood and made us a kingdom, priests to his God and Father" (1:5–6). Many interpreters note that "loves" is in the present tense, while "freed" is an aorist: by his blood he set us free once for all, and he goes on loving us. Before John asks us to "keep" anything, he lays this foundation. The great multitude before the throne did not crack a code; they "washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14). The blessing this book promises was never for the best calculator, or for the one with the least fear. It is for those washed by the Lamb's blood who come to hear him speak.

If I am that grandson, if I spent years hunting for signs, forwarded frightening predictions to my family, or even told friends a date that then came to nothing, I can look at John himself. Near the end of the book, having heard and seen all these things, he fell down to worship at the angel's feet, and not only once (19:10; 22:8–9). The apostle who received these visions could also be captivated by the messenger and lose sight of the Lord. The angel did not take the book away from him. He said only, "You must not do that!... Worship God," and, "the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy" (19:10). Then he went on: "Do not seal up the words of the prophecy of this book" (22:10). The book stayed in John's hands. The Lord deals with me the same way: he does not say, "You are unfit to read this book." He turns my eyes from the signs back to himself. And if I am that grandmother, afraid of this book for decades, the Lord has not stopped loving me because I skipped it; the book he gave was meant from the start to bless me.

So to "keep" this book is not first to work out every sign. It is to hear, and then to worship God and not bow to the beasts of this world; to endure in hard places; not to be bought by Babylon's luxury; and to long for him to come. Perhaps the grandmother and the grandson could sit together, one reading aloud and one listening, as the first churches did, beginning at chapter 1, reading on to the throne and the Lamb of chapters 4–5, and on to the new heaven and new earth of chapters 21–22.

At the end of the book the Lord Jesus speaks in his own name: "I, Jesus, have sent my angel to testify to you about these things for the churches. I am the root and the descendant of David, the bright morning star." (22:16) The morning star rises in the darkest part of the night, and whoever sees it knows the night will not go on forever. The book that frightened Grandma does not end on a beast but on a name; what her grandson was searching for was never a number but a person. Before every trumpet and every bowl, before any chart on any wall, he is already there: Jesus, who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood. In the darkest night, the first to rise is he.
