## 本章脉络

第1章分三段。第1-3节是全书的题记：这是「耶稣基督的启示」，念的、听的、遵守的，都是有福的。第4-8节是问安和颂赞：约翰颂赞那位爱我们、用血使我们脱离罪恶的主，并宣告「他驾云降临」。第9-20节是约翰在拔摩岛上看见的异象：七个金灯台中间有一位好像人子的；约翰仆倒在他脚前，像死了一样，主却对他说「不要惧怕」，又吩咐他写信给七个教会。

这一章也为七封信打下根基：每封信的开头，主都用这个异象里的一个特征介绍自己（如2:1、8、18）。读七封信以前，要先看清楚写信的是谁。作者、年代和读启示录的几种进路，请参看导读。

## 深读 1:1-3 耶稣基督的启示

**1. 「耶稣基督的启示」。** 「启示」原文（*apokalypsis*）的意思是揭开、显明，全书的书名就从这里来。这启示是从耶稣基督来的（「神赐给他」），也是关于耶稣基督的；许多释经者认为两层意思都在其中。启示录首先不是一份要我们破解的时间表，而是揭开一位人物，就是耶稣基督。

**2. 「指示」。** 神赐给基督，基督差遣使者，使者告诉约翰，约翰再写给众仆人（1:1；参22:16）。「指示」这个词（*sēmainō*）有用记号表明的意思（参约12:33）。许多人（如毕尔）认为，约翰一开头就提示读者，这卷书多用象征说话；而他自己是作见证的，把「自己所看见的都证明出来」（1:2）。

**3. 「必要快成的事」。** 这句话让人想起但以理对王说的话：神「已将日后必有的事指示尼布甲尼撒王」（但2:28）。但以理说「日后」，约翰却说「快」，第3节又说「日期近了」。许多释经者（如毕尔）认为，这是因为但以理所盼望的末后的日子，已经随着基督的受死和复活开始了。其他读法怎样理解这个「快」字，请参看导读。

**4. 「念……听见又遵守……都是有福的」。** 「念这书上预言的和那些听见又遵守其中所记载的，都是有福的，因为日期近了。」（1:3）「念」是单数，「听见」是复数：当时多数信徒没有自己的书卷，这卷书是要在聚会中由一个人大声念、众人一同听的。这是全书七个「有福」的第一个（其余在14:13；16:15；19:9；20:6；22:7、14）。预言不是为了满足好奇心，而是为了叫人遵守（22:7）。

## 深读 1:4-8 恩惠、平安归与你们

**1. 恩惠和平安的来源。** 启示录也是一封书信（参22:21）。恩惠和平安从「那昔在、今在、以后永在的神」而来，这让人想起神在荆棘中说的「我是自有永有的」（出3:14）。「以后永在」直译是那将要来的：神不只是永远存在，他还要来。所以到了神「执掌大权作王」的时候，长老就只称他为「昔在、今在」的（11:17）。恩惠平安也从「七灵」和耶稣基督而来。

**2. 「七灵」是谁？** 有两种主要的读法：
- **圣灵：** 许多释经者（如毕尔、包衡）认为，七灵是指圣灵，七表示完全。恩惠和平安只能从神而来，七灵又排在父和子中间；撒迦利亚书第4章的七盏灯和「七眼」都和耶和华的灵连在一起（亚4:2、6、10）；羔羊的七眼也「就是神的七灵」（5:6）。
- **七位天使：** 另一些释经者（如大卫·奥尼）认为，这是指侍立在神面前的七位天使（8:2；参《多比传》12.15）。
本书采取第一种读法。

**3. 耶稣基督的三个称号。** 「并那诚实作见证的、从死里首先复活、为世上君王元首的耶稣基督」（1:5）。许多人指出，这三个称号都和诗篇89篇有关：神论到大卫的后裔说，「我也要立他为长子，为世上最高的君王」（诗89:27）；同一篇诗也说到「天上确实的见证」（诗89:37，指月亮为约作见证）。当时亚细亚的众城争相为罗马皇帝建庙，这些小小的教会要知道：真正掌权的不是该撒，而是那忠心到死（提前6:13）、又从死里复活的耶稣（西1:18）。

**4. 「他爱我们，用自己的血使我们脱离罪恶」。** 约翰一提到耶稣的名字，就忍不住颂赞他（1:5-6）：
- **「他爱我们」：** 原文是现在式，许多人认为这表示他一直在爱我们。（英文钦定本所依据的后期抄本作不定过去时，就是爱了我们。）
- **「用自己的血使我们脱离罪恶」：** 「脱离」原文是释放、松开；英文钦定本所依据的抄本多一个字母，意思就变成洗去，和合本和英文标准版都照较早的抄本。无论哪一种，都是靠「他的血」：他为我们舍命，替我们担当了罪的刑罚，才把我们从罪的捆绑里释放出来。
- **「又使我们成为国民，作他父神的祭司」：** 「国民」原文是国度一词。神在西乃山对以色列人说：「你们要归我作祭司的国度」（出19:6）；现在，这应许落在一群在罗马帝国里没有地位的信徒身上（参5:10）。

**5. 「看哪，他驾云降临！」** 「看哪，他驾云降临！众目要看见他，连刺他的人也要看见他；地上的万族都要因他哀哭。」（1:7）这一节合用了但以理书7:13和撒迦利亚书12:10（参约19:37）；耶稣自己也把这两处连在一起（太24:30）。
- **怎样理解这次「降临」：** 部分过去派（如肯尼斯·詹特里）认为，这一节主要是指主后70年基督藉罗马军队审判耶路撒冷。本书和多数释经者一样，认为它指向基督将来亲身、可见的再来（参徒1:11），许多过去派也承认有这样的再来。所以，凡说基督已经在隐秘处、化身为某个人再来的，例如东方闪电（自称全能神教会），都不可信（太24:26；参看导读）。
- **「哀哭」：** 这是悔改的哀哭，还是在审判面前的惊恐？包衡等人认为，这里像撒迦利亚书一样，盼望列国悔改；另一些释经者认为，这是不信的人在审判来临时的哀哭。后面的异象两样都写到了。

**6. 「我是阿拉法，我是俄梅戛」。** 阿拉法和俄梅戛是希腊字母的头一个和末一个（1:8）：神是一切的开始，也是一切的终结。「全能者」（*pantokratōr*）在七十士译本里常用来翻译「万军之耶和华」。全书末了，耶稣也用同样的话说自己（22:13）。

> 要点：在一切审判和争战的异象以先，约翰先告诉我们：基督爱我们，已经用血释放了我们，使我们成为神的国民和祭司。启示录里的一切，都是写给这群蒙爱的人。

## 深读 1:9-11 在拔摩岛上

**1. 「你们的弟兄」。** 「我－约翰就是你们的弟兄，和你们在耶稣的患难、国度、忍耐里一同有分。」（1:9）约翰没有用权威的头衔介绍自己，而是称自己是和读者一同受苦的弟兄。许多人指出，原文这三个词共用一个冠词，连在一起：信徒现在就已经在神的国里，但这国是在患难中、靠着忍耐活出来的。这三样都「在耶稣的」里面，因为耶稣自己先走过这条路：先受苦，后得荣耀。

**2. 「那名叫拔摩的海岛」。** 拔摩是爱琴海上的一个小岛，在以弗所西南方。约翰在那里，是「为神的道，并为给耶稣作的见证」；后面用几乎相同的话描写为主被杀的人（6:9；20:4），所以他很可能是因信受苦。早期教会的传统（如优西比乌《教会史》3.18）说，他是被放逐到这岛上的。

**3. 「当主日」。** 「当主日，我被圣灵感动」（1:10）。「主日」是什么日子？
- **七日的第一日：** 多数释经者认为是指星期日。原文用的是一个形容词（*kyriakē*），意思是属主的；保罗说「主的晚餐」，用的也是这个词（林前11:20），不是先知常说的「耶和华的日子」那种说法。《十二使徒遗训》第十四章说信徒在主日聚集擘饼；伊格那丢《致马内夏人书》9.1也说，信徒不再守安息日，而是照着主日生活。
- **主的日子（末日）：** 一些时代论的释经者认为，这是说约翰在灵里被带到将来「主的日子」，看见末日的事。
本书采取第一种读法。约翰被隔离在海岛上，不能和众教会一同聚会，主却在这一天向他显现。「被圣灵感动」直译是在灵里，包衡等人认为这是全书分段的记号（1:10；4:2；17:3；21:10）。

**4. 「大声音如吹号」。** 这让人想起神在西乃山降临时「角声甚大」（出19:16）。这声音吩咐约翰写信给七个教会（1:11）。这七座城的次序，正好是送信的人从以弗所出发绕一圈的路线。亚细亚不只有这七个教会（例如歌罗西），选这七个，很可能是因为七常表示完全；每封信的结尾也都说：「圣灵向众教会所说的话，凡有耳的，就应当听！」（2:7）

**5. 七个教会和教会历史。** 这七封信怎样对后来的教会说话？有两种主要读法：
- **预表教会历史的时期：** 时代论的读法以《司可福串注圣经》（1909年）为代表。它承认这是约翰当时真实的七个教会，信息也是对各时代的教会和每个信徒说的；但它又认为，七封信按次序预表教会历史的七个时期，从使徒时代的以弗所，到末后冷淡的老底嘉。更早也有一些历史派的解经家这样读。华人教会最熟悉的，是倪柝声《教会的正统》的讲法，他把非拉铁非看作十九世纪弟兄会所代表的恢复。
- **同时并存的光景：** 另一些释经者（如毕尔、包衡、格兰特·奥斯本）认为，七封信所描写的光景，在每个时代的教会里都同时存在；经文本身没有指出先后的时期，每封信又都是「向众教会」说的。
本书采取第二种读法。两种读法都同意：今天的教会要从这七封信里听见主的声音。

## 深读 1:12-16 灯台中间的那一位

**1. 「七个金灯台」。** 约翰「转过身来，要看是谁发声与我说话；既转过来，就看见七个金灯台」（1:12），第20节说，七灯台就是七个教会。祭司要在耶和华面前「常收拾精金灯台上的灯」（利24:4）；撒迦利亚看见的是「一个纯金的灯台」（亚4:2），约翰看见的却是七个。神百姓的灯台，现在是众教会了。教会要发光，但光不是教会自己的。

**2. 「灯台中间有一位好像人子」。** 主不在远处，而是在灯台「中间」（1:13）。教会有的贫穷，有的受逼迫，有的冷淡，主都在他们中间行走（2:1）。
- **「好像人子」：** 这是出自但以理书7:13，也是耶稣最常用来称呼自己的名号（可14:62）。
- **「他的头与发皆白，如白羊毛，如雪」（1:14）：** 这是但以理描写「亘古常在者」的话（但7:9）。在但以理书里，人子被领到亘古常在者面前，和他有分别（但7:13）；约翰却把亘古常在者的特征用在人子身上。人子与父有分别，却同享神的荣耀。

**3. 长衣和金带。** 「身穿长衣，直垂到脚，胸间束着金带」（1:13）。直垂到脚的长衣这个词，七十士译本用来指大祭司的外袍（出28:4），也用来指以西结看见的那位「身穿细麻衣」的人（结9:2）；收拾灯台也是祭司的职分。这身打扮也像但以理书第10章那位「腰束乌法精金带」的（但10:5）。两种意思可以同时成立：人子像大祭司一样照管他的教会，也像君王一样掌权。

**4. 眼目、脚和声音。** 「眼目如同火焰；脚好像在炉中锻炼光明的铜；声音如同众水的声音。」（1:14-15）
- **「眼目如同火焰」：** 没有什么能在他眼前隐藏（参但10:6）。在推雅推喇的信里，主用这个称号介绍自己，接着说「我是那察看人肺腑心肠的」（2:18、23）。这双眼睛看透一切，也审判一切（19:12）。
- **「脚好像……光明的铜」：** 显出他的坚定和能力（参但10:6；结1:7）。
- **「声音如同众水的声音」：** 以西结说，以色列神的荣光来的时候，「他的声音如同多水的声音」（结43:2；参结1:24）。这又是把耶和华的特征用在人子身上。

**5. 七星、利剑和面貌。** 「他右手拿着七星，从他口中出来一把两刃的利剑；面貌如同烈日放光。」（1:16）七星是七个教会的使者（1:20）：教会在他手中。神使他仆人的「口如快刀」（赛49:2），弥赛亚要「以口中的杖击打世界」（赛11:4）；这剑是他审判的话，他用这话攻击悖逆的人（2:12、16；19:15）。他的面貌让人想起底波拉的歌「如日头出现，光辉烈烈」（士5:31），也让人想起登山变像时，「脸面明亮如日头」（太17:2）。

这些描写不是要我们画出一幅图来。读这些象征，先问旧约在哪里用过这个形象，比先问它对应今天的哪一件新闻更可靠。

> 要点：教会是灯台，光却是从灯台中间那一位来的。他有亘古常在者的荣耀，与父同为神；他是大祭司，是审判者，是用话语掌权的君王；他就在我们中间行走。

## 深读 1:17-20 不要惧怕

**1. 「仆倒在他脚前，像死了一样」。** 「我一看见，就仆倒在他脚前，像死了一样。」（1:17上）约翰曾经在晚饭时「侧身挨近耶稣的怀里」（约13:23），现在看见得荣耀的主，也站不住了（参结1:28；赛6:5）。最接近的是但以理书第10章：但以理看见那位身穿细麻衣的，「一听见就面伏在地沉睡了」（但10:9）。

这里的仆倒是惊惧，经文没有说是拜。不过值得留意：约翰后来两次在天使脚前俯伏要拜，天使都说：「千万不可！……你要敬拜神。」（19:10；22:8-9）四活物和长老却「俯伏在羔羊面前」（5:8），一切被造之物都把颂赞归给「坐宝座的和羔羊」（5:13-14）。基督配得只有神才配得的敬拜。

**2. 「他用右手按着我，说：『不要惧怕！』」** 那拿着七星的右手，现在按在约翰身上（1:17）。但以理也有同样的经历：「忽然，有一手按在我身上」（但10:10），那位天上的使者对他说：「但以理啊，不要惧怕！」（但10:12）在这里，扶起约翰的是主自己。他接着说的，是他自己是谁。

**3. 「我是首先的，我是末后的，又是那存活的」。** 主给约翰不要惧怕的理由，全在他自己身上（1:17-18）：
- **「首先的」「末后的」：** 这是耶和华在以赛亚书里对自己的称呼：「我是首先的，我是末后的；除我以外再没有真神。」（赛44:6；参41:4；48:12）耶稣把耶和华独有的称号用在自己身上。
- **「那存活的」：** 他不是从别处得到生命，他自己就是生命的源头（参约11:25）。
- **「我曾死过，现在又活了」：** 原文直译是我成了死的：这位永活的主真的死了。「现在又活了」前面有「看哪」一词，好像要约翰看清楚：死过的那一位，现在「直活到永永远远」。

**4. 「拿着死亡和阴间的钥匙」。** 「阴间」原文是 *hadēs*，指死人所在的地方，不等于后来的火湖（20:14）。在启示录里，死亡和阴间常像两个辖制人的权势一起出现（6:8；20:13-14）。
- **钥匙表示权柄：** 神要把「大卫家的钥匙」放在他仆人的肩头上，「他开，无人能关；他关，无人能开」（赛22:22）；主在给非拉铁非的信里，也说自己是「拿着大卫的钥匙」的（3:7）。
- **死亡不再有最后的话：** 耶稣说，「阴间的权柄不能胜过」他的教会（太16:18）；他「藉着死败坏那掌死权的」（来2:14）。
- **写给要受苦的教会：** 对士每拿教会说「你务要至死忠心」的，正是「那首先的、末后的、死过又活的」（2:8、10）。历代为主坐监、殉道的信徒，包括中国教会许多前辈，能忠心到底，是因为知道死亡的钥匙在主那里。

**5. 「所看见的，和现在的事，并将来必成的事」。** 主再次吩咐约翰写下来（1:19）。时代论的释经者（如《司可福串注圣经》、约翰·华尔伏）认为，这是全书的三段大纲：第1章、第2-3章、第4-22章。另一些释经者（如毕尔）认为，这是但以理书2:28-29的回音，整个异象同时包含现在和将来的事（参看导读）。

**6. 「七星和七个金灯台的奥秘」。** 「那七星就是七个教会的使者，七灯台就是七个教会。」（1:20）「使者」原文就是天使一词，可以有三种理解：
- **天上的天使：** 这个词在启示录别处都指天使，所以许多人（如毕尔）认为，每个教会都有代表它的天使。
- **教会的使者或领袖：** 历来许多释经者（如马太·亨利）认为，这是指各教会的牧者，或代表教会来取信的人。
- **教会的属灵光景：** 也有释经者认为，这是把教会本身拟人化来说的。
无论哪一种，信都是对整个教会说的。最要紧的是：七星在他的右手中，教会的安危不在罗马，也不在教会自己，而在这位主手中。

> 要点：在圣洁的基督面前，人站立不住；但这位圣洁的主，也是为我们死过、现在又活着、拿着死亡和阴间钥匙的那一位。所以他能对仆倒的人说：「不要惧怕！」

## 默想

**默想：** 「我一看见，就仆倒在他脚前，像死了一样。」（1:17）我心里的耶稣，多年来就像主日学画像里那样：抱着小羊，面带微笑，好像不大会动怒。可是这一章的这一位，「眼目如同火焰」（1:14），他说自己是「那察看人肺腑心肠的」（2:23）。我想起的，是心里那些人看不见的事：对教会里一位同工多年的轻看，他在会上一开口，我心里就已经把他的话打了折扣；朋友的事工兴旺、常被请去讲道，我嘴上恭喜，心里却酸了好几天；在祷告会上，我的祷告说得很流畅，心里想的却是别人怎样看我。这些事没有人查得出来，在这双眼睛面前却没有一样藏得住。

约翰曾经靠在耶稣的怀里，连他看见这一位，也「像死了一样」。我若读这一章从来没有怕过，也许是我把主看得太小了；我若一想起他的眼睛就只想躲开，也许是我还没有听清楚他接下来说的话。

他说的第一句话是「不要惧怕」。他没有说我心里那些事不算什么，也没有叫约翰先站起来证明自己。他说的是他自己：「我是首先的，我是末后的，又是那存活的；我曾死过，现在又活了」（1:17-18）。凡投靠他的人，那双看透我们的眼睛，是一位为我们死过的主的眼睛；他所看见的每一样，他都已经在十字架上担当了。约翰在第5节就说了：「他爱我们，用自己的血使我们脱离罪恶」。所以我不必在他面前装作没事。我可以照实说出这些事的名字，承认这是得罪他的，求他赦免；若我的轻看已经伤了那位同工，也去向他认错。

也许我这样认过许多次，又一再落回同样的轻看和嫉妒里，觉得没有脸再到他面前。可是约翰写的「他爱我们」，原文是现在式：不是从前爱过，等我失败了就收回，而是现在还爱着。他看见我一再跌倒，也看见他自己为我流的血。

我心底最怕的，是这些事会有最后的话：有一天站在他面前，它们会把我关在门外。可是主在这里说的最后一句话，正是对着这个怕说的：他「拿着死亡和阴间的钥匙」。看透我的那双眼睛，和拿着钥匙的，是同一位为我死过、现在又活了的主。门开不开，不在我心里那些暗处，而在他。有一天，他要开门，叫在他里面的人出来，从此「不再有死亡」（21:4）。钥匙在他那里。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 has three parts. **The title** (vv. 1–3): this is "the revelation of Jesus Christ," and blessed are those who read, hear and keep it. **The greeting and doxology** (vv. 4–8): John praises the one who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood, and announces, "Behold, he is coming with the clouds." **The vision on Patmos** (vv. 9–20): John sees one like a son of man among seven golden lampstands; he falls at his feet as though dead, but the Lord says, "Fear not," and commands him to write to the seven churches.

This chapter also lays the foundation for the seven letters: each opens with the Lord introducing himself by a feature from this vision (e.g. 2:1, 8, 18). Before we read the letters, we need to see clearly who is writing them. For the author, the date and the approaches to reading Revelation, see the introduction.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–3 The Revelation of Jesus Christ

**1. "The revelation of Jesus Christ."** The Greek word *apokalypsis* means an unveiling or disclosure, and the book takes its name from it. The revelation comes from Jesus Christ ("which God gave him"), and it is about Jesus Christ; many interpreters think both senses are present. Revelation is not first of all a timetable for us to crack; it unveils a person, Jesus Christ.

**2. "Made it known."** God gives the revelation to Christ, Christ sends his angel, the angel tells John, and John writes to God's servants (1:1; cf. 22:16). The verb (*sēmainō*) can carry the sense of making known by signs (see John 12:33). Many (e.g. Beale) think John is signaling from the start that this book speaks largely through symbols; and he himself is a witness who "bore witness... even to all that he saw" (1:2).

**3. "The things that must soon take place."** The phrase recalls Daniel's words to the king: God "has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will be in the latter days" (Dan 2:28). Daniel says "in the latter days," but John says "soon," and verse 3 adds, "the time is near." Many interpreters (e.g. Beale) take this to mean that the last days Daniel looked for have already begun with Christ's death and resurrection. For how other approaches understand "soon," see the introduction.

**4. "Blessed is the one who reads aloud... and blessed are those who hear, and who keep."** "Blessed is the one who reads aloud the words of this prophecy, and blessed are those who hear, and who keep what is written in it, for the time is near" (1:3). "The one who reads" is singular; "those who hear" is plural: most believers then owned no scrolls, and this book was meant to be read aloud by one person in the gathering while everyone listened. This is the first of the book's seven beatitudes (the others are in 14:13; 16:15; 19:9; 20:6; 22:7, 14). Prophecy is given not to satisfy curiosity but to be kept (22:7).

## Going Deeper: 1:4–8 Grace to You and Peace

**1. Where grace and peace come from.** Revelation is also a letter (cf. 22:21). Grace and peace come from "him who is and who was and who is to come," which recalls God's words at the bush, "I AM WHO I AM" (Exod 3:14). John does not say "who will be" but "who is to come": God does not merely exist forever; he is coming. So when God has "begun to reign," the elders call him only the one "who is and who was" (11:17). Grace and peace also come from the "seven spirits" and from Jesus Christ.

**2. Who are the "seven spirits"?** There are two main readings:
- **The Holy Spirit:** many interpreters (e.g. Beale, Bauckham) take the seven spirits as the Holy Spirit, with seven signifying fullness. Grace and peace can come only from God, and the seven spirits stand between the Father and the Son; in Zechariah 4 the seven lamps and the "seven eyes" are tied to the Spirit of the LORD (Zech 4:2, 6, 10); and the Lamb's seven eyes "are the seven spirits of God" (5:6).
- **Seven angels:** other interpreters (e.g. David Aune) take them as the seven angels who stand before God (8:2; cf. Tobit 12.15).
This study takes the first reading.

**3. Three titles of Jesus Christ.** "...and from Jesus Christ the faithful witness, the firstborn of the dead, and the ruler of kings on earth" (1:5). Many point out that all three titles echo Psalm 89: God says of David's heir, "I will make him the firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth" (Ps 89:27), and the same psalm speaks of "a faithful witness in the skies" (Ps 89:37, the moon as witness to the covenant). The cities of Asia competed to build temples to the Roman emperor; these small churches needed to know that the one who truly rules is not Caesar but Jesus, faithful to death (1 Tim 6:13) and risen from the dead (Col 1:18).

**4. "To him who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood."** As soon as John names Jesus, he breaks into praise (1:5–6):
- **"Who loves us":** the verb is present tense, which many take to mean that he goes on loving us. (The later manuscripts behind the King James Version have an aorist, "loved us.")
- **"Has freed us from our sins by his blood":** "freed" means released or loosed; the manuscripts behind the King James Version have a word one letter longer, meaning "washed," and the CUV and the ESV both follow the earlier manuscripts. Either way, it is "by his blood": by giving his life for us, bearing the penalty of sin in our place, he set us free from sin's bondage.
- **"And made us a kingdom, priests to his God and Father":** at Sinai God said to Israel, "You shall be to me a kingdom of priests" (Exod 19:6); now that promise rests on a group of believers who had no standing in the Roman Empire (cf. 5:10).

**5. "Behold, he is coming with the clouds."** "Behold, he is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see him, even those who pierced him, and all tribes of the earth will wail on account of him" (1:7). The verse combines Daniel 7:13 and Zechariah 12:10 (cf. John 19:37); Jesus himself joined the two (Matt 24:30).
- **What "coming" is meant:** some partial preterists (e.g. Kenneth Gentry) read the verse mainly of Christ's judgment-coming on Jerusalem through the Roman armies in AD 70. This study, with most interpreters, takes it of his future, personal, visible return (cf. Acts 1:11), which many preterists also affirm. So any claim that Christ has already returned in some hidden place, in the person of some individual, such as the claim of Eastern Lightning (which calls itself the Church of Almighty God), is not to be believed (Matt 24:26; see the introduction).
- **"Wail":** is this the mourning of repentance or the terror of judgment? Bauckham and others think that here, as in Zechariah, John holds out hope that the nations will repent; other interpreters take it as the wailing of unbelievers when judgment arrives. The later visions describe both.

**6. "I am the Alpha and the Omega."** Alpha and omega are the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet (1:8): God is the beginning of all things and their end. "The Almighty" (*pantokratōr*) often renders "the LORD of hosts" in the Septuagint. At the end of the book Jesus says the same of himself (22:13).

> The point: before any vision of judgment or battle, John first tells us that Christ loves us, has already freed us by his blood, and has made us God's kingdom and priests. Everything in Revelation is written to these beloved people.

## Going Deeper: 1:9–11 On the Island Called Patmos

**1. "Your brother."** "I, John, your brother and partner in the tribulation and the kingdom and the patient endurance that are in Jesus" (1:9). John does not introduce himself with a title of authority; he is a brother who suffers alongside his readers. Many note that in Greek the three nouns share one article and belong together: believers are already in God's kingdom, but it is lived out in tribulation and by endurance. All three are "in Jesus," who walked this road first: suffering first, then glory.

**2. "The island called Patmos."** Patmos is a small island in the Aegean, southwest of Ephesus. John was there "on account of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus"; later the book uses almost the same words for those slain for the Lord (6:9; 20:4), so he most likely suffered for his faith. Early church tradition (e.g. Eusebius, *Church History* 3.18) says he was banished to the island.

**3. "On the Lord's day."** "I was in the Spirit on the Lord's day" (1:10). What day is this?
- **The first day of the week:** most interpreters take it as Sunday. The Greek uses an adjective (*kyriakē*), belonging to the Lord, the same word Paul uses for "the Lord's supper" (1 Cor 11:20), not the phrase the prophets use for "the day of the LORD." The *Didache* (14.1) speaks of gathering on the Lord's day to break bread; Ignatius (*Magnesians* 9.1) says believers no longer keep the Sabbath but live according to the Lord's day.
- **The day of the Lord (the end):** some dispensational interpreters take it to mean John was carried in the Spirit into the future "day of the Lord" and saw the events of the end.
This study takes the first reading. John was cut off on an island and could not gather with the churches, yet on that day the Lord appeared to him. Bauckham and others take "in the Spirit" as a marker of the book's structure (1:10; 4:2; 17:3; 21:10).

**4. "A loud voice like a trumpet."** This recalls God's descent on Sinai with "a very loud trumpet blast" (Exod 19:16). The voice commands John to write to the seven churches (1:11). The cities' order follows a courier's circuit from Ephesus. Asia had more than seven churches (Colossae, for instance); these seven were probably chosen because seven often signifies completeness, and each letter ends, "He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches" (2:7).

**5. The seven churches and church history.** How do these seven letters speak to later churches? There are two main readings:
- **Foreshadowing periods of church history:** the dispensational reading is best known from the *Scofield Reference Bible* (1909). It accepts that these were seven real churches of John's day and that the messages speak to churches of every age and to each believer; but it adds that the seven letters, in order, foreshadow seven periods of church history, from apostolic Ephesus to lukewarm Laodicea at the end. Some earlier historicist interpreters read them in a similar way. The form best known in Chinese churches is Watchman Nee's in *The Orthodoxy of the Church*, which sees Philadelphia as the recovery represented by the nineteenth-century Brethren.
- **Conditions present together:** other interpreters (e.g. Beale, Bauckham, Grant Osborne) hold that the conditions the letters describe exist side by side in the church of every age; the text itself marks no succession of periods, and each letter speaks "to the churches."
This study takes the second reading. Both agree that the church today must hear the Lord's voice in these seven letters.

## Going Deeper: 1:12–16 The One Among the Lampstands

**1. "Seven golden lampstands."** "Then I turned to see the voice that was speaking to me, and on turning I saw seven golden lampstands" (1:12); verse 20 says the seven lampstands are the seven churches. The priest was to "arrange the lamps on the lampstand of pure gold before the LORD regularly" (Lev 24:4); Zechariah saw "a lampstand all of gold" (Zech 4:2), but John sees seven. The lampstand of God's people is now the churches. The church is to give light, but the light is not its own.

**2. "In the midst of the lampstands one like a son of man."** The Lord is not far off; he is "in the midst" of the lampstands (1:13). Poor, persecuted or cold, the churches have the Lord walking among them (2:1).
- **"One like a son of man":** this comes from Daniel 7:13, and "the Son of Man" was the title Jesus most often used for himself (Mark 14:62).
- **"The hairs of his head were white like wool, as white as snow" (1:14):** these are Daniel's words for "the Ancient of Days" (Dan 7:9). Daniel distinguishes the son of man from the Ancient of Days, to whom he is presented (Dan 7:13); John gives the Son the Ancient of Days' own features. The Son is distinct from the Father yet shares his divine glory.

**3. Robe and sash.** "Clothed with a long robe and with a golden sash around his chest" (1:13). The word for the long robe is used in the Septuagint for the high priest's robe (Exod 28:4) and for the "man clothed in linen" Ezekiel saw (Ezek 9:2), and tending the lampstand was a priest's duty. The clothing also resembles the figure in Daniel 10 "with a belt of fine gold from Uphaz around his waist" (Dan 10:5). Both meanings can stand together: the Son of Man cares for his churches like a high priest and rules like a king.

**4. Eyes, feet and voice.** "His eyes were like a flame of fire, his feet were like burnished bronze, refined in a furnace, and his voice was like the roar of many waters" (1:14–15).
- **"His eyes were like a flame of fire":** nothing is hidden from his sight (cf. Dan 10:6). In the letter to Thyatira the Lord introduces himself with this title and goes on, "I am he who searches mind and heart" (2:18, 23). These eyes see through everything and judge everything (19:12).
- **"His feet were like burnished bronze":** showing his steadfastness and strength (cf. Dan 10:6; Ezek 1:7).
- **"His voice was like the roar of many waters":** Ezekiel says that when the glory of the God of Israel came, "the sound of his coming was like the sound of many waters" (Ezek 43:2; cf. Ezek 1:24). Again, a feature of the LORD is given to the Son of Man.

**5. Stars, sword and face.** "In his right hand he held seven stars, from his mouth came a sharp two-edged sword, and his face was like the sun shining in full strength" (1:16). The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches (1:20): the churches are in his hand. God made his servant's "mouth like a sharp sword" (Isa 49:2), and the Messiah "shall strike the earth with the rod of his mouth" (Isa 11:4); the sword is his word of judgment, by which he wars against the rebellious (2:12, 16; 19:15). His face recalls Deborah's song, "like the sun as he rises in his might" (Judg 5:31), and the transfiguration, when Jesus' "face shone like the sun" (Matt 17:2).

These descriptions are not meant to be drawn as a picture. In reading them, asking first where the Old Testament used an image is a safer guide than asking which news story it matches today.

> The point: the church is the lampstand, but the light comes from the one in the midst of the lampstands. He shares the glory of the Ancient of Days and is God with the Father; he is the high priest, the judge and the king who rules by his word; and he walks among us.

## Going Deeper: 1:17–20 "Fear Not"

**1. "I fell at his feet as though dead."** "When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead" (1:17a). John once reclined "close to Jesus" at supper (John 13:23), yet now, seeing the glorified Lord, he cannot stand (cf. Ezek 1:28; Isa 6:5). The closest parallel is Daniel 10: seeing the man clothed in linen, Daniel "fell on my face in deep sleep with my face to the ground" (Dan 10:9).

John's falling here is terror, and the text does not call it worship. But notice: when he later twice falls to worship at an angel's feet, he is told, "You must not do that!... Worship God" (19:10; 22:8–9), while the living creatures and elders "fell down before the Lamb" (5:8) and every creature gives praise "to him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb" (5:13–14). Christ receives the worship that belongs to God alone.

**2. "He laid his right hand on me, saying, 'Fear not.'"** The right hand that holds the seven stars is now laid on John (1:17). Daniel had the same experience: "a hand touched me" (Dan 10:10), and the heavenly figure said, "Fear not, Daniel" (Dan 10:12). Here it is the Lord himself who raises John. What he says next is who he is.

**3. "I am the first and the last, and the living one."** Every reason the Lord gives John not to fear lies in himself (1:17–18):
- **"The first and the last":** this is how the LORD names himself in Isaiah: "I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god" (Isa 44:6; cf. Isa 41:4; 48:12). Jesus takes to himself the title the LORD reserves for himself alone.
- **"The living one":** he does not receive life from elsewhere; he is the source of life (cf. John 11:25).
- **"I died, and behold I am alive":** the Greek reads literally "I became dead": the ever-living Lord truly died. "Behold" stands before "I am alive," as if to make John look closely: the one who died is "alive forevermore."

**4. "I have the keys of Death and Hades."** "Hades" (*hadēs*) means the place of the dead; it is not the same as the later lake of fire (20:14). In Revelation, Death and Hades often appear together like two powers that hold people captive (6:8; 20:13–14).
- **Keys mean authority:** God will place "the key of the house of David" on his servant's shoulder: "He shall open, and none shall shut; and he shall shut, and none shall open" (Isa 22:22); in the letter to Philadelphia the Lord calls himself the one "who has the key of David" (3:7).
- **Death no longer has the last word:** Jesus said "the gates of hell shall not prevail against" his church (Matt 16:18); "through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death" (Heb 2:14).
- **Written to churches about to suffer:** the one who tells Smyrna, "Be faithful unto death," is "the first and the last, who died and came to life" (2:8, 10). Believers imprisoned and martyred for the Lord through the ages, many forebears of the Chinese church among them, stayed faithful because they knew the keys of death are with him.

**5. "Those that are and those that are to take place after this."** The Lord again tells John to write (1:19). Dispensational interpreters (e.g. the *Scofield Reference Bible*, John Walvoord) take this as a three-part outline of the book: chapter 1, chapters 2–3, chapters 4–22. Other interpreters (e.g. Beale) hear an echo of Daniel 2:28–29 and take the whole vision to mix present and future throughout (see the introduction).

**6. "The mystery of the seven stars... and the seven golden lampstands."** "The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches, and the seven lampstands are the seven churches" (1:20). "Angels" can be understood in three ways:
- **Heavenly angels:** everywhere else in Revelation the word means angels, so many (e.g. Beale) take it that each church has an angel who represents it.
- **The churches' messengers or leaders:** many interpreters through the centuries (e.g. Matthew Henry) take it as each church's pastor, or the delegates who came for the letters.
- **The church's spiritual state:** other interpreters take the "angel" as a personification of the church itself.
Whichever is taken, the letters speak to the whole church. Most important, the seven stars are in his right hand: the church's safety rests not in Rome, nor in the church itself, but in the hand of this Lord.

> The point: no one can stand before the holy Christ; but this holy Lord is also the one who died for us, is alive now and holds the keys of Death and Hades. That is why he can say to those who have fallen, "Fear not."

## Meditation

**Meditation:** "When I saw him, I fell at his feet as though dead" (1:17). For years the Jesus in my mind looked like the Sunday-school pictures: holding a lamb, smiling, hardly ever angry. But the one in this chapter has "eyes like a flame of fire" (1:14), and calls himself "he who searches mind and heart" (2:23). What comes to mind are the things in my heart that no one sees: years of quiet contempt for a co-worker at church, so that whenever he speaks in a meeting I have already discounted what he says; a friend whose ministry flourishes and who is often invited to preach, whom I congratulate aloud while I sour inside for days; my prayers at the prayer meeting, fluent and well-phrased, while my mind is on what others think of me. No one can audit these things, but before these eyes none of them stays hidden.

John once leaned on Jesus' chest, yet when he saw this one, even he fell "as though dead." If I have read this chapter and never been afraid, perhaps I have made the Lord too small; if the thought of his eyes makes me only want to hide, perhaps I have not yet heard clearly what he says next.

His first words are "Fear not." He does not say the things in my heart do not matter, nor does he tell John to get up and prove himself first. He speaks of himself: "I am the first and the last, and the living one. I died, and behold I am alive" (1:17–18). For all who trust him, the eyes that see through us are the eyes of a Lord who died for us; everything he sees, he has already borne on the cross. John said it in verse 5: "To him who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood." So I need not pretend before him. I can call these things by their names, admit that they are sin against him and ask his forgiveness; and if my contempt has already wounded that co-worker, I can go and own it to him.

Perhaps I have confessed like this many times and keep slipping back into the same contempt and envy, and feel I have no face left to come before him. But John writes "who loves us," in the present tense: not a love he once had and withdraws when I fail, but a love he has now. He sees me fall again and again, and he also sees the blood he shed for me.

My deepest fear is that these things will have the last word: that one day, standing before him, they will shut me out. But the last thing the Lord says here speaks to exactly that fear: he has "the keys of Death and Hades." The eyes that see through me and the one who holds the keys are the same Lord, who died for me and is alive. Whether the door opens does not rest with the dark places in my heart; it rests with him. One day he will open it and bring out those who are in him, and "death shall be no more" (21:4). The keys are with him.
