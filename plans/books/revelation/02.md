## 本章脉络

第二、三章是复活的基督口授给亚细亚七个教会的七封信。七封信大体按同一个格式写成，第二章是其中的前四封：

**写信的格式：**
- **收信人：**「你要写信给……教会的使者」（「使者」是谁，见第一章，1:20）。
- **基督的自称：**取用第一章异象中的一个特征，往往正合那个教会的处境。
- **「我知道」：**主先看见，然后才说话；接着是称赞或责备（本章中士每拿只受称赞；另见非拉铁非，3:7-13），以及悔改、持守的呼召。
- **「凡有耳的，就应当听」：**每封信写给一个教会，却是「众教会」都要听的。
- **给得胜者的应许：**大多在全书末了的新天新地里再次出现（如生命树，22:2）。

**本章的四封信：**
- **以弗所**（1-7节）：正统、勤劳，却离弃了起初的爱心。
- **士每拿**（8-11节）：贫穷、受毁谤，主对他们只有安慰。
- **别迦摩**（12-17节）：坚守主名，却容让了巴兰的教训。
- **推雅推喇**（18-29节）：爱心越来越多，却容让耶洗别。

**这七个教会是什么？** 时代论的读法（以1909年的《司可福串注圣经》为代表）承认这是七个真实的教会，信息也是给众教会和每个信徒的；但他们又认为，这七个教会预示教会历史的几个时期。至于把每个教会一一对应到某个时代（如非拉铁非是复兴和宣教的时代），是后来更流行的讲法。本研读和许多福音派学者一样，把它们读作同时并存、又向每个时代说话的七个真实教会（详见第一章）。

## 深读 2:1-7 以弗所：离弃了起初的爱心

**1. 以弗所这座城。** 以弗所是罗马亚细亚省最重要的港口和交通枢纽之一，城中有亚底米女神的大庙（参徒19:27）。保罗在这里住了将近三年（徒20:31），后来提摩太在这里牧会（提前1:3），按古代传统，使徒约翰晚年也住在这里（爱任纽《驳异端》3.1.1）。

**2. 主的称赞是真实的。** 基督「在七个金灯台中间行走」，亲自察看众教会。「我知道你的行为、劳碌、忍耐，也知道你不能容忍恶人。你也曾试验那自称为使徒却不是使徒的，看出他们是假的来。」（2:2）保罗曾警告以弗所的长老：「我去之后必有凶暴的豺狼进入你们中间，不爱惜羊群。」（徒20:29）几十年后，这个教会确实守住了真道；约在主后110年，伊格那丢也称赞他们不让异端在他们中间撒种（伊格那丢《致以弗所人书》9.1）。主没有因为要责备他们，就抹杀他们的忠心。

**3. 「你把起初的爱心离弃了」。** 「然而有一件事我要责备你，就是你把起初的爱心离弃了。」（2:4）这是什么爱？释经者有几种看法：
- **对基督的爱：**像以色列「幼年的恩爱，婚姻的爱情」（耶2:2）。他们还在为主的名劳苦，却不再像起初那样爱主。
- **彼此相爱：**保罗当年称赞他们「亲爱众圣徒」（弗1:15），如今他们善于分辨真假，却可能变得多疑、严厉。
- **两样都有：**有些释经者（如格兰特·奥斯本）认为不必二选一，因为在约翰的书信里，爱神和爱弟兄是分不开的（约一4:20-21）。也有人（如毕尔）把它和见证的热心衰退连在一起，因为主警告要挪去的是灯台。

以弗所的问题不是懒惰，也不是异端，而是在勤劳和正统之中，爱渐渐冷了。主耶稣预言过：「许多人的爱心才渐渐冷淡了。」（太24:12）

**4. 回想、悔改、行起初所行的事。** 「应当回想你是从哪里坠落的，并要悔改，行起初所行的事。」（2:5）回想，不是怀念过去的情绪，而是记起基督起初怎样爱我们；悔改，是承认这是罪，不只是状态不好；起初的爱也有它的行为：读他的话、向他倾心、接待人、关心软弱的人。

**5. 「把你的灯台从原处挪去」。** 一个教会若失去了爱，即使教义正确、事工兴旺，也可能失去作见证的地位。可是警告是在还有机会的时候发出的：「你若不悔改」，可见主仍在等候。

**6. 尼哥拉一党。** 「你恨恶尼哥拉一党人的行为，这也是我所恨恶的。」（2:6）这一党是谁，资料很少。爱任纽认为他们跟从使徒行传6:5所记的执事尼哥拉（《驳异端》1.26.3），亚历山大的革利免却为尼哥拉本人辩护（《杂记》3.4），现代学者多半认为难以确定。有人推测，尼哥拉的字义（胜过百姓）和犹太传统对巴兰名字的解释（吞灭百姓）相近。从别迦摩的信看来（2:14-15），他们的教训大概和巴兰相似：与偶像文化妥协。主恨恶的是他们的「行为」，没有叫教会去恨恶人。

**7. 生命树的果子。** 「得胜的，我必将神乐园中生命树的果子赐给他吃。」（2:7）人犯罪以后，神「安设基路伯和四面转动发火焰的剑，要把守生命树的道路」（创3:24）。如今基督应许，那条被封住的路要重新打开（22:2）。「得胜」不是靠自己的力量，而是「因羔羊的血和自己所见证的道」（12:11）。

> 要点：主看见我们的劳苦，也看见冷淡了的爱；他责备，是要我们回到他起初爱我们的爱里。

## 深读 2:8-11 士每拿：至死忠心

**1. 「那首先的、末后的、死过又活的」。** 士每拿是富裕的港口城市，以忠于罗马闻名；主后26年，亚细亚各城争取为皇帝提庇留建庙，士每拿胜出（塔西佗《编年史》4.55-56）。在这样的城里拒绝向皇帝献香，就显得格外不忠。基督向这个教会自称「死过又活的」（1:18），这对将要受苦、甚至将要死的信徒是最大的安慰：他已经走过死亡，又活了。

**2. 「你的贫穷（你却是富足的）」。** 士每拿的信徒在富裕的城里贫穷，可能是因为拒绝行会的偶像宴席而失去生意。主说他们「却是富足的」，正和自以为富足的老底嘉相反（3:17）。

**3. 「撒但一会的」：一个必须小心读的词。** 「也知道那自称是犹太人所说的毁谤话，其实他们不是犹太人，乃是撒但一会的人。」（2:9）这大概是指士每拿当地会堂的一些人，他们向官府毁谤基督徒。许多学者认为，在罗马帝国，犹太人可以不拜皇帝，基督徒一旦被指为不属于犹太人，就失去了这层保护。读这节经文必须小心：
- 写这话的约翰自己是犹太人，耶稣和使徒也都是犹太人。「撒但」的意思是敌对者，在法庭上就是控告者（参12:10）；下一节的「魔鬼」原意是毁谤者，正对应这里的「毁谤话」。这里说的是一群人在某一件事上站在控告者一边，不是在说犹太民族。
- 「其实他们不是犹太人」的意思，接近保罗所说的：「惟有里面作的，才是真犹太人。」（罗2:29）
- 教会历史上，有人拿这类经文来仇视、迫害犹太人，这是可耻的罪。今天的读者不可以借这节经文轻视任何一个民族。

**4. 「你务要至死忠心」。** 「你将要受的苦你不用怕。魔鬼要把你们中间几个人下在监里，叫你们被试炼，你们必受患难十日。你务要至死忠心，我就赐给你那生命的冠冕。」（2:10）
- 主没有应许他们不受苦，而是先告诉他们：苦难是有限的。「十日」很可能象征一段短暂而有定期的时间（参但1:12）。
- 背后是魔鬼的作为，但掌权的仍是主：连「几个人」「十日」都在他的数算之中。
- 「生命的冠冕」是比赛得胜者或有功之人所戴的花冠，不是君王的王冠。古代作者曾把士每拿山上环绕的建筑比作城的冠冕，有学者（如威廉·拉姆齐）认为这里可能暗指此事。属世的冠冕会枯萎，主所赐的是「生命」。

**5. 坡旅甲。** 士每拿的主教坡旅甲，据传是使徒约翰的门徒：爱任纽在给弗罗林的信中回忆，他年少时听坡旅甲讲述自己与约翰的交往（优西比乌《教会史》5.20.6）。约在主后155年，地方官对坡旅甲说，只要咒骂基督，就放了他。按《坡旅甲殉道记》的记载，他回答说：「我事奉他八十六年，他从来没有亏待我；我怎能亵渎那救我的王呢？」（9.3）记载又说火没有烧着他，行刑的人就用刀刺死了他（16.1）。后来，士每拿教会真的有人「至死忠心」。

**6. 「必不受第二次死的害」。** 「得胜的，必不受第二次死的害。」（2:11）信徒可能要受第一次的死，但第二次的死（20:14）「在他们身上没有权柄」（20:6）。

> 要点：主没有应许忠心的人不受苦，却应许苦难有期限；那死过又活的主，必赐生命的冠冕。

## 深读 2:12-17 别迦摩：撒但座位之处

**1. 「那有两刃利剑的」。** 别迦摩是亚达罗王朝的旧都，山上满是神庙；主前29年，它成为亚细亚第一个获准为奥古斯都和罗马女神建庙的城市（迪奥·卡西乌斯《罗马史》51.20.6-7；参塔西佗《编年史》4.37）。罗马总督握有判人死刑的「剑权」；有学者认为，基督自称「有两刃利剑的」（1:16），是提醒信徒：真正掌握生死和审判的，不是罗马的剑，而是他口中的话。

**2. 「撒但座位之处」。** 「我知道你的居所，就是有撒但座位之处。」（2:13）这可能指宙斯的大祭坛、以蛇为标记的医神崇拜（参12:9），或整城的偶像崇拜；许多学者（如毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）认为最合上下文的是皇帝崇拜和罗马政权的中心，因为安提帕正是在这里被杀。无论是哪一种，主没有叫他们离开这城，只说「我知道」他们住在那里有多难。

**3. 「我忠心的见证人安提帕」。** 主称他为「我忠心的见证人」，几乎就是基督自己的称号：「那诚实作见证的……耶稣基督」（1:5）。这位殉道者名字被记下，是因为他像他的主。

**4. 巴兰的教训。** 「在你那里有人服从了巴兰的教训；这巴兰曾教导巴勒将绊脚石放在以色列人面前，叫他们吃祭偶像之物，行奸淫的事。」（2:14）巴兰咒诅不了以色列，就献计叫摩押女子引诱他们（民31:16）。别迦摩的危险是里面的妥协：行会宴席和节庆常在神庙里举行，有人可能教导说「偶像在世上算不得什么」（参林前8:4），参加无妨。他们没有否认基督，却想两边都要。今天也许是公司开业时拜财神，或生意场上推不掉的应酬；后者需要智慧分辨，可是问题仍是同一个：我们是不是想两边都要。

**5. 「用我口中的剑攻击他们」。** 「所以，你当悔改；若不悔改，我就快临到你那里，用我口中的剑攻击他们。」（2:16）主叫全教会悔改，因为容让这教训是全教会的事；但他「攻击」的是教导和跟从的人。他的剑就是他的话。

**6. 隐藏的吗哪和白石。** 「得胜的，我必将那隐藏的吗哪赐给他，并赐他一块白石，石上写着新名；除了那领受的以外，没有人能认识。」（2:17）
- **隐藏的吗哪：**摩西曾吩咐把一罐吗哪「存在耶和华面前」（出16:33）。凡拒绝偶像宴席的人，主必亲自用天上的粮喂养他们；这粮就是基督自己（约6:35）。
- **白石：**意思不能确定（无罪的判决、赴宴的凭证等）；无论哪种，主都接纳那些拒绝偶像宴席的人。
- **新名：**神应许他的子民「必得新名的称呼，是耶和华亲口所起的」（赛62:2）。别人也许只看见一个不合群的怪人，主却给我们一个属他的名字。

> 要点：最大的危险不一定是逼迫，而是不知不觉的妥协；主用天上的吗哪和新名喂养、认领属他的人。

## 深读 2:18-29 推雅推喇：容让耶洗别

**1. 「眼目如火焰、脚像光明铜的神之子」。** 推雅推喇是七城中名气最小、地位最不显要的一个，信却是最长的一封。这城以行会著称，卖紫色布的吕底亚就是推雅推喇人（徒16:14）。基督在这里自称「神之子」，这是七封信中唯一一次（参诗2:7）：神之子用火焰般的眼目察看他的教会。

**2. 一个在爱心上进步的教会。** 「我知道你的行为、爱心、信心、勤劳、忍耐，又知道你末后所行的善事，比起初所行的更多。」（2:19）推雅推喇正好和以弗所相反：以弗所能分辨真假，却失去了爱；推雅推喇在爱心上越来越好，却不能分辨真假。真理和爱心，缺一不可。

**3. 「那自称是先知的妇人耶洗别」。** 「然而，有一件事我要责备你，就是你容让那自称是先知的妇人耶洗别教导我的仆人，引诱他们行奸淫，吃祭偶像之物。」（2:20）
- 这大概是教会中一个真实而有影响力的妇人。「耶洗别」很可能是象征的名字，让人想起把巴力崇拜带进以色列的王后（王上16:31）。
- 不参加行会宴席可能就没有生意；她的教导很可能给了信徒一个妥协的理由。
- 「行奸淫」可能是字面上的性犯罪，也可能是旧约常用的比喻，指拜偶像。许多释经者认为两层意思都有。
- 「耶洗别」这个名字不可以拿来给敢于发言的姊妹，或向教会反映问题的人贴标签；这里所责备的，是引诱神仆人拜偶像、行淫乱的假教导。

**4. 借属灵权柄引诱人。** 经文直接责备的是假教导；拿它来看今天有人借属灵权柄性侵他人，是一种类比，但原则相通。若有人以先知、属灵导师或领袖的身份，利用属灵权柄引诱人与他发生性关系，这不是两个人之间的软弱，而是滥用权柄的虐待。教会不可以「容让」：
- 这类事若涉及性侵、性剥削或任何犯罪，向警方和司法机关举报是对的（罗13:4）；
- 受害的人不必留在危险里，也不必勉强和可能再伤害自己的人和好；
- 即使施害的人认罪、蒙神赦免，也不等于可以恢复他的职分，更不等于可以再接近受害人或孩子；
- 教会不可以为了面子、为了「家丑不可外扬」把事情压下去。

同时也要记得：这封信不是叫我们怀疑每一位传道人。大多数牧者忠心劳苦，应当受到尊重（帖前5:12-13）；彼得劝长老「按着神旨意照管」群羊，「也不是辖制所托付你们的」（彼前5:2-3）。对忠心的领袖尊重，对滥权的人不容让，都是爱教会。

**5. 主的忍耐与审判。** 「我曾给她悔改的机会，她却不肯悔改她的淫行。」（2:21）主先给了时间。接着他说要叫她「病卧在床」，「叫众教会知道，我是那察看人肺腑心肠的」（2:22-23；参耶17:10）。「病卧在床」原文只说「床」；这个词也可以指宴席上斜靠的榻。许多释经者认为这里带着讽刺：淫乱的床或偶像宴席的榻，要变成病床。主看的不是恩赐和名声，而是人心。

**6. 「撒但深奥之理」。** 「至于你们推雅推喇其余的人，就是一切不从那教训、不晓得他们素常所说撒但深奥之理的人……」（2:24）有人认为那些教师自称明白神的深奥之理，主讽刺地说那其实是撒但的；也有人认为他们真的说，要认识撒但的深处才能胜过它。无论哪种读法，主对忠心的人只说：「我不将别的担子放在你们身上。但你们已经有的，总要持守，直等到我来。」（2:24-25）他没有加给他们新的秘诀。

**7. 制伏列国的权柄与晨星。** 「那得胜又遵守我命令到底的，我要赐给他权柄制伏列国……我又要把晨星赐给他。」（2:26-28）
- 这是诗篇第二篇的弥赛亚应许（诗2:8-9）：在行会和官府面前没有权势的信徒，将来要与基督一同掌权。
- 「晨星」最可能是指基督自己：「我是明亮的晨星。」（22:16）最大的赏赐不是权柄，而是得着他。

> 要点：爱心离开了真理，会容让伤害羊群的人；真理离开了爱心，会变得冷酷。那察看人心的神之子，应许把他自己赐给我们。

## 默想

**默想：** 「然而有一件事我要责备你，就是你把起初的爱心离弃了。」（2:4）我在教会服事了十几年：主日学、司琴、周六打扫礼堂、带小组，大家说我是教会的老黄牛。可是这几年我越来越清楚：我读经，多半是为了预备下个主日的课；我祷告，常常是轮到我才开口。服事完回到家，心里剩下的是累，还有对那些不来帮忙的人的埋怨。我对教义很清楚，谁讲错了一句我听得出来，可是对身边的人，我越来越没有耐心。

以弗所教会就是这样：不懒，不糊涂，可是爱冷了。我最怕的，是主对我说的也是这句话。

也有人读到「起初的爱心」，心里是另一种难过：我从来没有那种火热的时候。也许我是在基督徒家庭长大的，也许是慢慢想明白才信的，从来没有在聚会中流泪，也说不出哪一天心里像着了火。这节经文是不是在说，我连可以回去的地方都没有？

先看是谁在说这话。他是「在七个金灯台中间行走的」（2:1）。我心冷的这些年，他不在远处，他一直在教会中间行走，看见我每一个累得说不出话的周六，也看见我心里的埋怨。他说「我知道」，不是在记我的账，因为他先说的是：「我知道你的行为、劳碌、忍耐。」他没有抹掉我的劳苦。

再看「起初的爱心」是从哪里来的。约翰在这卷书一开头就说：「他爱我们，用自己的血使我们脱离罪恶。」（1:5）在多数学者所取的抄本里，这个「爱」是现在式：他现在仍爱我们。写下这话的约翰，在他的福音书里也记下主怎样爱门徒：「他既然爱世间属自己的人，就爱他们到底。」（约13:1）所以，「起初的爱」最先不是我的感觉，而是他的爱；我的爱从来只是回应。对那个从来没有感觉过火热的人，这是好消息：你不需要回到一种你从来没有过的情绪里，你可以回到他一直都在的爱里。对那个曾经火热、如今冷淡的人，这也是好消息：「回想你是从哪里坠落的」，不是叫你拼命挤出当年的眼泪，而是叫你记起，起初是他先爱你。

所以我的悔改，可以从这里开始：承认我的冷淡和埋怨是罪，不只是太累。有时冷淡是因为真的透支了；这时请长执分担，或者暂停一项服事，并不等于不爱主。可是我最需要的，不是再多做一样，而是坐下来，读那句「他爱我们」，让他的爱重新成为我服事的起点。

主对以弗所的警告是严厉的，可是这警告是在还有时间的时候说的。人第一次失去乐园的时候，神安设了「四面转动发火焰的剑，要把守生命树的道路」（创3:24）。我凭自己的劳碌，再怎样努力，也回不到那棵树那里。可是那位眼目如火焰的主，自己走上了十字架，担当了那剑所代表的审判，把路重新打开。今天他仍在灯台中间行走，也走进冷了的心里；有一天，我一切的劳碌都要止息，他要亲自把神乐园中生命树的果子赐给我吃。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Revelation 2 and 3 contain seven letters dictated by the risen Christ to seven churches in the Roman province of Asia. They follow a common pattern, and chapter 2 holds the first four:

**The pattern of the letters:**
- **Address:** "To the angel of the church in … write" (on the "angel," see chapter 1, 1:20).
- **Christ's title:** a feature from the vision of chapter 1, often fitted to the church's situation.
- **"I know":** the Lord sees first, then speaks; praise or rebuke follows (in this chapter Smyrna receives only praise; see also Philadelphia, 3:7–13), with a call to repent and hold fast.
- **"He who has an ear, let him hear":** each letter is addressed to one church but meant for "the churches."
- **A promise to the one who conquers:** most reappear in the new creation at the end of the book (the tree of life, 22:2).

**The four letters in this chapter:**
- **Ephesus** (vv. 1–7): orthodox and hard-working, yet it has abandoned its first love.
- **Smyrna** (vv. 8–11): poor and slandered; the Lord has only comfort for it.
- **Pergamum** (vv. 12–17): holding fast the name, yet tolerating the teaching of Balaam.
- **Thyatira** (vv. 18–29): growing in love, yet tolerating "Jezebel."

**What are the seven churches?** The dispensational reading, best known from the *Scofield Reference Bible* (1909), accepts that these were real churches with a message for all churches and every believer, but adds that the seven also foreshadow periods of church history; matching each church to an era (Philadelphia as the age of revival and missions, for example) is a later, popular form of that view. This study, with many evangelical scholars, reads them as seven real churches that existed side by side and speak to every age (see chapter 1).

## Going Deeper: 2:1–7 Ephesus: The Love You Had at First

**1. The city.** Ephesus was one of the great ports and road hubs of the province of Asia, home to the temple of Artemis (cf. Acts 19:27). Paul stayed there nearly three years (Acts 20:31), Timothy later served there (1 Tim 1:3), and early tradition says the apostle John spent his last years there (Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 3.1.1).

**2. The praise is real.** Christ "walks among the seven golden lampstands," looking closely at each church. "I know your works, your toil and your patient endurance, and how you cannot bear with those who are evil, but have tested those who call themselves apostles and are not, and found them to be false" (2:2). Paul had warned the Ephesian elders, "after my departure fierce wolves will come in among you, not sparing the flock" (Acts 20:29). Decades later this church had guarded the truth; around AD 110 Ignatius also praised them for not letting false teachers sow their seed among them (Ignatius, *To the Ephesians* 9.1). The Lord does not erase their faithfulness because he has a rebuke to give.

**3. "You have abandoned the love you had at first."** "But I have this against you, that you have abandoned the love you had at first" (2:4). What love is meant? Interpreters suggest:
- **Love for Christ:** like Israel's "devotion of your youth, your love as a bride" (Jer 2:2). They still toiled for his name, but no longer loved him as they once did.
- **Love for one another:** Paul once thanked God for their "love toward all the saints" (Eph 1:15). Now, skilled at testing what is true, they may have become suspicious and severe.
- **Both:** some commentators (e.g. Grant Osborne) think we need not choose, since in John's letters love for God and love for the brothers cannot be separated (1 John 4:20–21). Others (e.g. Beale) connect the lost love with a fading zeal for witness, since the lampstand is what the Lord threatens to remove.

Ephesus's problem is neither laziness nor heresy. In the middle of hard work and sound doctrine, love has quietly grown cold. Jesus foretold it: "the love of many will grow cold" (Matt 24:12).

**4. Remember, repent, do the first works.** "Remember therefore from where you have fallen; repent, and do the works you did at first" (2:5). To remember is not nostalgia for past feelings but recalling how Christ first loved us; to repent is to call it sin, not just a bad season; and first love has its deeds: listening to his word, pouring out the heart to him, welcoming people, caring for the weak.

**5. "Remove your lampstand from its place."** A church that loses love may keep sound doctrine and busy programs and still lose its place as a witness. But the warning comes while there is still time: "unless you repent." He is still waiting.

**6. The Nicolaitans.** "Yet this you have: you hate the works of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate" (2:6). We know very little about this group. Irenaeus connected them with Nicolaus of Acts 6:5 (*Against Heresies* 1.26.3), but Clement of Alexandria defended Nicolaus himself (*Stromateis* 3.4), and most modern scholars think the link cannot be confirmed. Some guess that the name ("conqueror of the people") echoes a Jewish explanation of "Balaam" ("devourer of the people"). The letter to Pergamum (2:14–15) suggests their teaching resembled Balaam's: accommodation to the surrounding idol culture. The Lord hates their *works*; he does not tell the church to hate the people.

**7. The tree of life.** "To the one who conquers I will grant to eat of the tree of life, which is in the paradise of God" (2:7). After the fall, God "placed the cherubim and a flaming sword that turned every way to guard the way to the tree of life" (Gen 3:24). Now Christ promises that the closed way will be opened again (22:2). And believers conquer not by their own strength but "by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" (12:11).

> The point: The Lord sees our labor and the love gone cold. He rebukes to bring us back to the love with which he first loved us.

## Going Deeper: 2:8–11 Smyrna: Faithful unto Death

**1. "The first and the last, who died and came to life."** Smyrna was a wealthy port famous for its loyalty to Rome; in AD 26, when the cities of Asia competed to build a temple to the emperor Tiberius, Smyrna won (Tacitus, *Annals* 4.55–56). In such a city, refusing to offer incense to the emperor looked like disloyalty. To this church Christ calls himself the one "who died and came to life" (1:18), the deepest comfort to believers who would suffer and perhaps die: he has passed through death and lives.

**2. "Your poverty (but you are rich)."** The believers in Smyrna were poor in a rich city, perhaps from losing trade when they refused the guilds' idol feasts. The Lord says they are "rich," the reverse of Laodicea, which thought itself rich (3:17).

**3. "A synagogue of Satan": words to read with care.** "and the slander of those who say that they are Jews and are not, but are a synagogue of Satan" (2:9). This probably refers to some members of a local synagogue who slandered Christians before the authorities. Many scholars think that Jews were excused from worshiping the emperor, so once Christians were declared not to be Jews, they lost that shelter. This verse must be read carefully:
- John, who wrote it, was himself a Jew, as were Jesus and the apostles. "Satan" means "adversary," in court the accuser (cf. 12:10); and "devil" in the next verse means "slanderer," matching the "slander" here. The verse speaks of particular people who, in this matter, sided with the accuser. It is not a statement about the Jewish people.
- "Who say that they are Jews and are not" is close to Paul's "a Jew is one inwardly" (Rom 2:29).
- In church history some have used such verses to despise and persecute Jews. That was a shameful sin, and no reader today may use this verse to look down on any people.

**4. "Be faithful unto death."** "Do not fear what you are about to suffer. Behold, the devil is about to throw some of you into prison, that you may be tested, and for ten days you will have tribulation. Be faithful unto death, and I will give you the crown of life" (2:10).
- The Lord does not promise that they will escape suffering, but he tells them in advance that it is limited. "Ten days" is likely a symbol for a short, fixed period (cf. Dan 1:12).
- The devil is behind it, but the Lord still rules: even "some of you" and "ten days" are counted by him.
- "The crown of life" is the wreath given to a victor in the games or to someone honored for service, not a king's diadem. Ancient writers spoke of the ring of buildings on Smyrna's hill as its "crown," and some scholars (e.g. William Ramsay) hear an allusion. Earthly wreaths wither; the Lord gives *life*.

**5. Polycarp.** Polycarp, bishop of Smyrna, is said to have been a disciple of the apostle John: Irenaeus, writing to Florinus, recalled hearing Polycarp as a boy tell of his dealings with John (Eusebius, *Church History* 5.20.6). Around AD 155 Polycarp was told he would be released if he cursed Christ. According to the *Martyrdom of Polycarp*, he answered, "Eighty-six years I have served him, and he has done me no wrong. How can I blaspheme my King who saved me?" (9.3). The account says the fire did not consume him, and he was stabbed to death (16.1). Later, some in the church at Smyrna were truly "faithful unto death."

**6. "Will not be hurt by the second death."** "The one who conquers will not be hurt by the second death" (2:11). Believers might suffer the first death, but over them the second death (20:14) "has no power" (20:6).

> The point: The Lord does not promise that the faithful will not suffer, but that suffering has a limit; the one who died and came to life gives the crown of life.

## Going Deeper: 2:12–17 Pergamum: Where Satan's Throne Is

**1. "Him who has the sharp two-edged sword."** Pergamum, the old Attalid capital, stood on a hill crowded with temples, and in 29 BC it became the first city in Asia allowed to build a temple to Augustus and Roma (Dio Cassius, *Roman History* 51.20.6–7; cf. Tacitus, *Annals* 4.37). The Roman governor held the "right of the sword," the power of capital punishment. Some scholars think Christ's title (1:16) reminds believers that the one who truly holds life, death and judgment is not Rome's sword but the word of his mouth.

**2. "Where Satan's throne is."** "I know where you dwell, where Satan's throne is" (2:13). The phrase may point to the great altar of Zeus, to the serpent-emblem cult of Asclepius (cf. 12:9), or to the city's idolatry as a whole; many scholars (e.g. Beale, Grant Osborne) think the imperial cult and the seat of Roman power fit best, since Antipas was killed there. Whichever it is, the Lord does not tell them to leave the city; he says "I know" how hard it is to live there.

**3. "Antipas my faithful witness."** The Lord calls him "my faithful witness," nearly Christ's own title: "Jesus Christ the faithful witness" (1:5). The martyr's name is remembered because he was like his Lord.

**4. The teaching of Balaam.** "You have some there who hold the teaching of Balaam, who taught Balak to put a stumbling block before the sons of Israel, so that they might eat food sacrificed to idols and practice sexual immorality" (2:14). When Balaam could not curse Israel, he advised that the women of Moab entice them (Num 31:16). Pergamum's danger was compromise from within: guild banquets and festivals were often held in temples, and some may have taught that "an idol has no real existence" (cf. 1 Cor 8:4), so taking part was harmless. They did not deny Christ, but wanted both. Today it may be an offering to the god of wealth when a company opens, or business entertaining that seems impossible to refuse; the latter calls for wisdom, but the question is the same: do we want both?

**5. "War against them with the sword of my mouth."** "Therefore repent. If not, I will come to you soon and war against them with the sword of my mouth" (2:16). The whole church is told to repent, because tolerating this teaching is the whole church's fault; but the Lord's war is against those who teach and follow it. His sword is his word.

**6. Hidden manna and a white stone.** "To the one who conquers I will give some of the hidden manna, and I will give him a white stone, with a new name written on the stone that no one knows except the one who receives it" (2:17).
- **Hidden manna:** Moses had a jar of manna placed "before the LORD to be kept throughout your generations" (Exod 16:33). All who refuse the idol feasts the Lord himself will surely feed with the bread of heaven; that bread is Christ himself (John 6:35).
- **A white stone:** the meaning is uncertain (a verdict of acquittal, a token admitting a guest to a feast, and others); on any reading, those who refused the idol banquet are welcomed by the Lord.
- **A new name:** God promised his people that they would "be called by a new name that the mouth of the LORD will give" (Isa 62:2). Others may see only an odd person who would not join in; the Lord gives a name that marks us as his.

> The point: The greatest danger is not always persecution but quiet compromise. The Lord feeds and claims his own with hidden manna and a new name.

## Going Deeper: 2:18–29 Thyatira: Tolerating Jezebel

**1. "The Son of God, who has eyes like a flame of fire, and whose feet are like burnished bronze."** Thyatira was the least known and least important of the seven cities, but its letter is the longest. The city was known for its trade guilds, and Lydia, "a seller of purple goods," came from there (Acts 16:14). Only here in the seven letters does Christ call himself "the Son of God" (cf. Ps 2:7): the Son of God searches his church with eyes like flame.

**2. A church growing in love.** "I know your works, your love and faith and service and patient endurance, and that your latter works exceed the first" (2:19). Thyatira is the mirror image of Ephesus: Ephesus could tell true from false but had lost its love; Thyatira was growing in love but could not tell true from false. Truth and love cannot do without each other.

**3. "That woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess."** "But I have this against you, that you tolerate that woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess and is teaching and seducing my servants to practice sexual immorality and to eat food sacrificed to idols" (2:20).
- She was probably a real, influential woman in the church. "Jezebel" is probably a symbolic name recalling the queen who brought Baal worship into Israel (1 Kgs 16:31).
- Staying away from guild banquets could mean losing one's trade; her teaching probably gave believers a reason to compromise.
- "Sexual immorality" may be literal sexual sin, or the Old Testament image for idolatry. Many interpreters think both are in view.
- The name must not become a label for women who speak up or for those who raise concerns; the sin named here is false teaching that leads God's servants into idolatry and immorality.

**4. Using spiritual authority to seduce.** The passage directly rebukes false teaching; applying it to sexual abuse under spiritual authority today is an analogy, but the principle carries over. If someone uses the standing of a "prophet," spiritual mentor or leader to draw people into sexual relationships, that is not a weakness between two people; it is abuse of authority. The church may not "tolerate" it:
- where it involves sexual assault, sexual exploitation or any crime, reporting it to the police and courts is right (Rom 13:4);
- those harmed need not stay in danger or be pressed to reconcile with someone who may harm them again;
- even if the offender confesses and is forgiven by God, that does not restore his office or give him access again to victims or children;
- the church must never hide it to save face or out of the instinct not to air family shame.

At the same time, this letter does not call us to suspect every minister. Most pastors labor faithfully and should be honored (1 Thess 5:12–13); Peter urges elders to exercise oversight "as God would have you," "not domineering over those in your charge" (1 Pet 5:2–3). Honoring faithful leaders and refusing to tolerate abusers are both ways of loving the church.

**5. The Lord's patience and judgment.** "I gave her time to repent, but she refuses to repent of her sexual immorality" (2:21). The Lord gave time first. Then he says he will throw her "onto a sickbed," and "all the churches will know that I am he who searches mind and heart" (2:22–23; cf. Jer 17:10). The Greek says simply "bed," *klinē*, a word that can also mean the couch a guest reclined on at a banquet. Many commentators see irony here: the couch of immorality, or the dining couch of the guild feasts, becomes a bed of sickness. The Lord looks not at gifts and reputation but at the heart.

**6. "The deep things of Satan."** "But to the rest of you in Thyatira, who do not hold this teaching, who have not learned what some call the deep things of Satan…" (2:24). Some think the teachers claimed to know the "deep things" of God, and the Lord says with irony that they are really Satan's; others think they actually said one must know the depths of Satan to overcome him. Either way, the Lord says to the faithful only this: "I do not lay on you any other burden. Only hold fast what you have until I come" (2:24–25). He gives them no new secret.

**7. Authority over the nations and the morning star.** "The one who conquers and who keeps my works until the end, to him I will give authority over the nations … And I will give him the morning star" (2:26–28).
- This quotes the messianic promise of Psalm 2 (Ps 2:8–9). Believers who had no power before guilds and magistrates will one day reign with Christ.
- "The morning star" most likely means Christ himself: "I am … the bright morning star" (22:16). The greatest reward is not authority but him.

> The point: Love cut off from truth tolerates those who harm the flock; truth cut off from love grows hard. The Son of God who searches the heart promises to give us himself.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "But I have this against you, that you have abandoned the love you had at first" (2:4). I have served at church for more than ten years: Sunday school, the piano, cleaning the hall on Saturdays, leading a small group. People call me the church's faithful old ox. But in recent years I have seen it more and more clearly: I read the Bible mostly to prepare next Sunday's lesson; I pray mostly when it is my turn. When I get home after serving, what is left is tiredness, and resentment toward those who never help. I know my doctrine well and can hear when someone gets a sentence wrong, but I have less and less patience with the people beside me.

The church in Ephesus was like this: not lazy or muddled, yet love had grown cold. What I fear most is that the Lord would say the same to me.

Others read "the love you had at first" with a different sorrow: I never had a time of fire. Perhaps I grew up in a Christian family; perhaps I came to faith slowly by thinking it through. I have never wept in a meeting, and I cannot name a day when my heart burned. Is this verse telling me I have nowhere even to go back to?

First, look at who is speaking. He is the one "who walks among the seven golden lampstands" (2:1). In my cold years he was not far off. He was walking among the churches all along, and he saw every Saturday when I was too tired to speak, and the resentment in my heart. When he says "I know," he is not keeping a record against me, for the first thing he says is, "I know your works, your toil and your patient endurance." He does not erase my labor.

Then, look at where "first love" comes from. At the very start of this book John writes of Christ, "To him who loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood" (1:5). In the Greek text most scholars follow, "loves" is present tense: he loves us still. The same John recorded in his Gospel how the Lord loved his disciples: "having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end" (John 13:1). So "first love" was never first my feeling; it was his love, and mine has only ever been an answer to it. For the one who never felt the fire, this is good news: you do not need to return to an emotion you never had; you can return to a love that has always been there. For the one who once burned and is now cold, it is good news too: "remember … from where you have fallen" does not mean squeezing out the tears of years ago. It means remembering that, from the beginning, he loved you first.

So my repentance can begin here: admitting that my coldness and resentment are sin, not just tiredness. Sometimes the coldness comes from being truly worn out, and then asking the elders to share the load, or stepping back from one ministry for a season, does not mean I have stopped loving the Lord. But what I need most is not one more task. It is to sit down and read "him who loves us," and let his love become again the place my serving starts from.

The Lord's warning to Ephesus is severe, but it is spoken while there is still time. When humanity first lost paradise, God placed "a flaming sword that turned every way to guard the way to the tree of life" (Gen 3:24). By my own labor, however hard I try, I cannot get back to that tree. But the Lord whose eyes are like a flame of fire went to the cross himself, bore the judgment that sword stood for, and opened the way again. Today he still walks among the lampstands and comes into hearts gone cold; and one day, when all my toil is over, he himself will give me the fruit of the tree of life in the paradise of God.
