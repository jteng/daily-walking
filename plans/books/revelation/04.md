## 本章脉络

第4章和第5章是同一个异象。第2、3章，主耶稣向地上的七个教会说话；到了第4章，约翰被召「上到这里来」，看见天上的宝座。第4章的中心是那坐在宝座上的神，他因为创造了万物而受敬拜；第5章的中心是羔羊，他因为曾被杀、用血买了人而受敬拜。先有创造主，后有救赎主；两章合起来，是全书往后一切事的根基。

第4章可以分成四段：
- **4:1-2上，天上有门开了：** 约翰听见那吹号的声音（1:10），被圣灵感动，进入天上的异象。
- **4:2下-6上，宝座和宝座周围：** 坐在宝座上的那一位，围着宝座的虹，二十四位长老，闪电雷轰，七盏火灯，玻璃海。
- **4:6下-8，四活物：** 他们昼夜不住地说「圣哉！圣哉！圣哉！」
- **4:9-11，长老的敬拜：** 他们把冠冕放在宝座前，承认神「配得荣耀、尊贵、权柄」，因为他创造了万物。

全章最常出现的词就是「宝座」，单在这一章就出现了十几次。约翰所描写的一切，都是按着和宝座的关系来排列的：在宝座上、围着宝座、从宝座中发出、在宝座前。往后的印、号、碗，都要从这个宝座出来（6:1；8:2；16:1、17）。所以，读启示录的人不论采取哪一种读法（过去派、历史派、未来派或灵意派），在这一点上大多是一致的：第4、5章所描写的，是天上此时此刻的实情；各种读法的分歧，主要是从第6章才开始。

## 深读 4:1-2上 天上有门开了

**1. 「此后」和「以后必成的事」。** 「此后，我观看，见天上有门开了。我初次听见好像吹号的声音，对我说：『你上到这里来，我要将以后必成的事指示你。』」（4:1）主耶稣在第1章已经吩咐约翰，要把「所看见的，和现在的事，并将来必成的事，都写出来」（1:19）。「以后必成的事」这句话，许多释经者（如毕尔）认为是回应但以理书：但以理对尼布甲尼撒说，「只有一位在天上的神能显明奥秘的事。他已将日后必有的事指示尼布甲尼撒王」（但2:28；参2:45）。但以理所说的那位在天上的神，现在要把同样的事指示约翰。
- **「此后」：** 第一个「此后」，是说约翰看完七封信以后又看见了新的异象，是讲异象的先后；第二个「以后必成的事」，是讲这些事要在什么时候发生。前者是约翰看见的次序，不一定等于事情发生的次序。
- **「必」：** 这个「必」和1:1的「必要快成的事」是同一个意思。这些事要成就，不是因为命运，而是因为神已经定了。

**2. 「你上到这里来」：两种读法。** 这句话在中文教会里常有争论。
- **时代论的读法：** 许多时代论的释经者（如《司可福串注圣经》和约翰·华尔伍德）认为，约翰被提到天上，是教会被提的一个预表；他们指出，「教会」这个词在第4-19章不再出现，所以教会在这段时期已经不在地上。
- **其他的读法：** 许多其他释经者（无论是历史性前千禧年派的乔治·赖德，无千禧年派的安东尼·胡克玛，还是毕尔）认为，这是约翰个人在异象中蒙召，像以西结在迦巴鲁河边「天就开了」（结1:1），像以赛亚在圣殿里看见主（赛6:1），也像旧约的先知「站在耶和华的会中」（耶23:18、22）。他们指出，第4-19章虽然不用「教会」这个词，却多次讲到地上的「圣徒」（如13:7），而全书本来就是写给「众教会」的（22:16）。
两种读法的信徒都相信主要亲自再来。不过，这一节本身讲的，是约翰看见了什么，而不是教会什么时候被提；这个问题要从全书和整本圣经来看，不宜单凭这一节下结论。

**3. 「我立刻被圣灵感动」。** 「我立刻被圣灵感动，见有一个宝座安置在天上」（4:2）。「被圣灵感动」在启示录出现了四次（1:10；4:2；17:3；21:10），每一次都开启一个新的大异象，许多学者（如包衡）认为这是全书结构的一个记号。约翰不是靠自己的想象力或修炼爬上天去，是圣灵带他进去。这也提醒我们：人能看见天上的实情，只是因为神愿意启示。

**4. 约翰最先看见的是什么？** 约翰被召上去，要看的是「以后必成的事」，就是后面那些印、号、碗、龙和兽。可是神没有先给他看灾难，而是先给他看宝座。七个教会中，有的正在受逼迫（2:10），有的有人为主殉道（2:13），有的被小看、力量不大（3:8）。对他们来说，世界的中心好像是罗马，是该撒的宝座，是地方上的权势。神让约翰先看见：真正的宝座在天上，而且有一位坐在上面。

> 要点：约翰在看见地上的一切风暴以前，先看见了天上的宝座。启示录不是先叫人知道将来会发生什么，而是先叫人知道是谁在掌权。

## 深读 4:2下-6上 宝座和坐在宝座上的

**1. 「有一个宝座安置在天上，又有一位坐在宝座上」。** 旧约的先知多次看见神的宝座：米该雅「看见耶和华坐在宝座上，天上的万军侍立在他左右」（王上22:19）；以赛亚「见主坐在高高的宝座上」（赛6:1）；以西结看见活物头上的穹苍之上「有宝座的形象」（结1:26）；但以理「见有宝座设立，上头坐着亘古常在者」（但7:9）。诗篇也说：「耶和华在天上立定宝座；他的权柄统管万有。」（诗103:19）约翰所看见的，就是这同一个宝座。「安置」这个词，是说宝座是定在那里的，不会被推翻，也不会空着。

**2. 「好像碧玉和红宝石」。** 「看那坐着的，好像碧玉和红宝石」（4:3）。约翰没有描写神的形状。以西结至少还说「有仿佛人的形状」（结1:26），约翰却只讲宝石的光彩。这是敬畏的克制：神是不可用形象表达的（参出20:4），人只能讲他荣耀的光辉。
- **碧玉：** 后来圣城降下的时候，「城的光辉如同极贵的宝石，好像碧玉，明如水晶」（21:11），城里「有神的荣耀」。
- **大祭司的胸牌：** 胸牌上的十二块宝石，按和合本，第一块是「红宝石」，最后一块是「碧玉」（出28:17、20）。有些释经者认为，约翰可能有意用这一头一尾的两块宝石，暗示坐宝座的那一位，就是与他的百姓立约的神；不过这只是一种可能的联想。

**3. 「又有虹围着宝座」。** 「又有虹围着宝座，好像绿宝石。」（4:3）这里的背景，首先是以西结书：「下雨的日子，云中虹的形状怎样，周围光辉的形状也是怎样。这就是耶和华荣耀的形象。」（结1:28）而云中的虹，又要把读者带回到洪水以后：「我把虹放在云彩中，这就可作我与地立约的记号了。」（创9:13）
- **审判和怜悯：** 洪水是神对罪的审判；虹是神在审判之后起誓说，「水就再不氾滥、毁坏一切有血肉的物了」（创9:15）。宝座有闪电、雷轰发出（4:5），往后的审判也要从这里出来；可是这宝座四围先有一道虹。
- **「围着」：** 云中的虹是半圆的，宝座四围的虹却是「围着」的。原文这个词（*iris*）也可以指光环。无论怎样理解，约翰所看见的是：宝座的四面八方，都被立约的记号围着。
- **以赛亚书的回响：** 神后来藉以赛亚对他的百姓说：「这事在我好像挪亚的洪水。我怎样起誓不再使挪亚的洪水漫过遍地，我也照样起誓不再向你发怒，也不斥责你。」（赛54:9）神把挪亚的约，当作他向百姓守约施慈爱的保证。

**4. 二十四位长老。** 「宝座的周围又有二十四个座位；其上坐着二十四位长老，身穿白衣，头上戴着金冠冕。」（4:4）他们是谁，主要有两种看法：
- **代表神全体子民：** 许多释经者（如毕尔）认为，二十四是以色列十二支派加上羔羊的十二使徒；后来圣城的门上写着十二支派的名字，根基上写着十二使徒的名字（21:12、14）。他们身穿的白衣、头戴的冠冕、所坐的座位，都是主应许给得胜者的（3:5、18；2:10；3:21）。大卫把祭司分为二十四班（代上24章），也可能是背景之一。
- **天上的灵界受造者：** 另一些释经者（如大卫·奥尼、罗伯特·芒斯）认为，他们是天上议会中的天使类受造者（参王上22:19）。他们指出，长老拿着「众圣徒的祈祷」（5:8），又向约翰解释圣徒是谁（7:13-14），好像和圣徒是有分别的。
两种读法都同意：宝座周围不是空荡荡的，有一群敬拜者，他们的冠冕是从神领受的，也要还给神（4:10）。

**5. 闪电、雷轰和七盏火灯。** 「有闪电、声音、雷轰从宝座中发出；又有七盏火灯在宝座前点着；这七灯就是神的七灵。」（4:5）
- **西奈山：** 神在西奈山降临的时候，「在山上有雷轰、闪电，和密云，并且角声甚大，营中的百姓尽都发颤」（出19:16）。这个宝座是圣洁的神的宝座。往后每到一组审判的末了，这几样都要再出现，而且越来越强（8:5；11:19；16:18）。
- **「神的七灵」：** 这和1:4「他宝座前的七灵」是一样的。许多释经者认为这是指圣灵，七表示完全，背景是撒迦利亚书的七盏灯（亚4:2、10）和以赛亚书描写耶和华的灵的七重说法（赛11:2）；也有释经者认为是指在神面前侍立的七位天使（参8:2）。第5章说「七眼，就是神的七灵，奉差遣往普天下去的」（5:6），这比较支持第一种读法。

**6. 「好像一个玻璃海，如同水晶」。** 「宝座前好像一个玻璃海，如同水晶。」（4:6）以西结看见活物头上的穹苍「看着像可畏的水晶」（结1:22）；摩西和长老看见以色列的神，「他脚下仿佛有平铺的蓝宝石，如同天色明净」（出24:10）。所罗门的圣殿里也有一个「铜海」（王上7:23）。另外，在启示录和但以理书里，海常常是混乱和邪恶的所在，兽是从海里上来的（但7:3；启13:1），新天新地里「海也不再有了」（21:1）。有些释经者因此认为，在宝座前，连海也平静得像玻璃一样；后来得胜的人就「站在玻璃海上」（15:2）。几种背景不一定互相排斥，共同的意思是：宝座前一切都清澈、平静、在神的掌管之下。

> 要点：宝座发出闪电雷轰，因为坐在上面的是圣洁的神；可是宝座四围有一道虹，因为这位神是守约的神。

## 深读 4:6下-8 四活物：圣哉！圣哉！圣哉！

**1. 以西结书和以赛亚书的结合。** 「宝座中和宝座周围有四个活物，前后遍体都满了眼睛。」（4:6）约翰所看见的四活物，同时带着以西结书和以赛亚书的特征：
- **以西结书的活物：** 以西结看见「四个活物的形象」（结1:5），各有人、狮子、牛、鹰的脸（结1:10），后来他知道「他们是基路伯」（结10:20）；轮辋周围也「满有眼睛」（结1:18；10:12）。
- **以赛亚书的撒拉弗：** 撒拉弗「各有六个翅膀」，彼此呼喊「圣哉！圣哉！圣哉！」（赛6:2-3）。
约翰把两个异象合在一起，表示他所看见的，就是先知们所看见的同一位神。不同的是，以西结的每个活物都有四张脸，约翰的四活物却是各像一样。

**2. 狮子、牛犊、人、飞鹰。** 「第一个活物像狮子，第二个像牛犊，第三个脸面像人，第四个像飞鹰。」（4:7）许多释经者（如包衡、毕尔）认为，这四样分别代表野兽、家畜、人类和飞鸟中最尊贵的，所以四活物代表全部受造的活物，在宝座前献上全受造界的敬拜。教会早期，爱任纽把四活物和四卷福音书连在一起（《驳异端》3.11.8），后来成了教会艺术中常见的象征；这是教会的传统联想，不是约翰在这里的重点。

**3. 「遍体内外都满了眼睛」。** 眼睛象征警醒和洞察。他们一直看着神，也看着神所做的一切；他们的敬拜是睁着眼睛的敬拜，是因为看见了才赞美。

**4. 「圣哉！圣哉！圣哉！」** 「他们昼夜不住地说：圣哉！圣哉！圣哉！主神是昔在、今在、以后永在的全能者。」（4:8）
- **三次「圣哉」：** 希伯来文用重复来表示最高级。常有人指出，旧约很少像以赛亚书第6章这样，把神的一项属性连说三次。神的圣洁，是他和一切受造之物截然不同的那份尊荣，也是他对罪的完全拒绝。以赛亚看见了，就说「祸哉！我灭亡了！」（赛6:5）
- **「全能者」：** 这个词（*pantokratōr*）在七十士译本里常用来翻译「万军之耶和华」，意思是掌管万有的那一位。在启示录里，它几乎都用在神身上（1:8；11:17；15:3）。罗马皇帝自称有权，但真正的全能者只有一位。
- **「昔在、今在、以后永在」：** 第1章原文的次序是今在、昔在、以后永在（1:4、8），和合本按中文的习惯都译作「昔在、今在、以后永在」；这里原文是昔在在先。到了第11章和第16章，天上的敬拜者只说「昔在、今在」（11:17；16:5），因为那时他「执掌大权作王了」，「以后永在」已经来到。许多释经者认为，这个称呼的背景是神在荆棘中向摩西启示的名字（出3:14）。

**5. 「昼夜不住地说」。** 这是天上永不停止的敬拜。启示录后来讲到另一种「昼夜」：那些拜兽和兽像的人，「昼夜不得安宁」（14:11）。人总要敬拜一样东西；敬拜神的，昼夜不住地赞美，敬拜兽的，昼夜不得安宁。

> 要点：天上的敬拜从神的圣洁开始。人对神的认识若只有慈爱而没有圣洁，就会把恩典看得太便宜；神的圣洁越被看见，恩典就越显得奇妙。

## 深读 4:9-11 你是配得的

**1. 把冠冕放在宝座前。** 「每逢四活物将荣耀、尊贵、感谢归给那坐在宝座上、活到永永远远者的时候，那二十四位长老就俯伏在坐宝座的面前敬拜那活到永永远远的，又把他们的冠冕放在宝座前」（4:9-10）。冠冕是他们所领受的尊荣，现在却放在宝座前。这是承认：一切尊荣都是从神来的，也要归给神。有学者（如大卫·奥尼）指出，罗马帝国的宫廷礼仪里，臣属的君王也会把冠冕放在皇帝面前，表示臣服；若是这样，约翰的读者会明白，真正配得这种敬拜的，不是该撒，而是坐在天上宝座上的神。

**2. 「我们的主，我们的神」。** 「我们的主，我们的神，你是配得荣耀、尊贵、权柄的」（4:11）。罗马史家苏埃托尼乌斯记载，图密善喜欢别人称他为我们的主和神（《罗马十二帝王传·图密善传》第十三章）。若启示录写于图密善年间，这句敬拜就格外有分量；即使写于更早的时候，皇帝崇拜在亚细亚各城也已经很盛行。对七个教会的信徒来说，这句话不是空洞的套语，而是一种立场：我们的主只有一位，不是该撒。不过，这一点不必夸大；当时信徒受的压力，多半是地方上的，来自城中的皇帝崇拜、行会和会堂的冲突，未必是全帝国统一的逼迫。

**3. 「因为你创造了万物」。** 「因为你创造了万物，并且万物是因你的旨意被创造而有的。」（4:11）
- **敬拜的理由：** 长老敬拜神的理由，是神创造了万物。神配得敬拜，不是因为他满足了我们的需要，而是因为他是神；我们和万物的存在，都是从他来的。
- **「因你的旨意」：** 万物的存在，不是偶然，也不是神不得已，而是出于神的旨意。神不需要世界，他创造世界是出于他的自由和美善。
- **「被创造而有的」：** 原文的次序是先说存在、后说被造，有释经者认为这是强调：万物从被造到如今一直存在，都靠着神的旨意。创造之后，神并没有离开，他一直托住万有。

**4. 从第4章到第5章。** 第4章的敬拜是向创造主献上的：「你创造了万物」（4:11）；第5章的敬拜是向救赎主献上的：「你曾被杀，用自己的血……买了人来」（5:9）。这个次序很重要：那位要救赎世界、更新万物的（21:5），就是起初创造万物的那一位。他不会放弃他所造的世界。一个人若只知道第4章，可能会觉得神高高在上、令人战兢；第5章要告诉我们，这宝座中间站着一只羔羊。

> 要点：神配得敬拜，首先因为他是创造主；万物和我自己，都是因他的旨意而存在的。这位神不会放弃他所造的。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我立刻被圣灵感动，见有一个宝座安置在天上，又有一位坐在宝座上……又有虹围着宝座，好像绿宝石。」（4:2-3）一位姊妹每天晚饭后都要看新闻。公司在裁员，她这个年纪最先被考虑；房贷还有二十年；父亲的检查报告要下星期才出来；国际上又有战争，物价一直在涨。教会里有人传来一篇讲末世的文章，说种种迹象都已经应验了，她看完心里更慌。她不是不信神，但她心里有一个声音：这个世界失控了，我的生活也失控了。

约翰在拔摩海岛上，看见的世界比我们更失控。教会在受压，有人已经殉道，帝国的权势好像无边无际。可是神让他先看见的，不是地上的乱局，而是「有一个宝座安置在天上」，而且「有一位坐在宝座上」。宝座没有空着，也没有摇动。约翰接下来要看见的印、号、碗，都要从这个宝座出来；没有一样风暴是在宝座以外刮起来的。

我也许已经在焦虑中做了许多不好的事：对家人发脾气，整夜在网上找各种消息，想把一切都抓在手里，又因为抓不住而不再祷告。焦虑本身不是信心大的表现，但神也没有因此把我赶出他的面前。在第5章，约翰要看见那宝座中间站着一只羔羊，像是被杀过的；他是为我这个心里慌乱、又靠自己想抓住一切的人死的。我不需要先让自己平静下来，才可以来到这个宝座前；正是在我不平静的时候，我被请到这里来看。

我仍然要面对那份检查报告，仍然要找工作或保住工作，可以向弟兄姊妹求助，也可以请人为我祷告。但我所面对的这一切，都在这个宝座之下。神曾指着云中的虹说：「我看见，就要记念我与地上各样有血肉的活物所立的永约。」（创9:16）记念这约的，是神自己，不是我。他后来又说：「大山可以挪开，小山可以迁移；但我的慈爱必不离开你」（赛54:10）。这慈爱的约，在羔羊的血里已经立定了。今晚的新闻也许不会改变，但我可以抬头看：那位坐在宝座上的，四围有一道虹，好像绿宝石；那是他立约的怜悯，把宝座整个围住了，也把我围在里面。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapters 4 and 5 are a single vision. In chapters 2 and 3 the Lord Jesus speaks to seven churches on earth; in chapter 4 John is summoned to "come up here" and sees the throne in heaven. At the center of chapter 4 is God seated on the throne, worshiped because he created all things; at the center of chapter 5 is the Lamb, worshiped because he was slain and by his blood ransomed a people. First the Creator, then the Redeemer; together the two chapters are the foundation for everything that follows in the book.

Chapter 4 falls into four parts:
- **4:1–2a, a door open in heaven:** John hears the voice like a trumpet (1:10), is "in the Spirit," and enters the heavenly vision.
- **4:2b–6a, the throne and what surrounds it:** the One seated on the throne, the rainbow around it, the twenty-four elders, lightning and thunder, the seven torches of fire, the sea of glass.
- **4:6b–8, the four living creatures:** day and night they never cease to say, "Holy, holy, holy."
- **4:9–11, the elders' worship:** they cast their crowns before the throne and confess that God is "worthy... to receive glory and honor and power," because he created all things.

The word that appears most in the chapter is "throne," more than a dozen times in this chapter alone. Everything John describes is arranged by its relation to the throne: on the throne, around the throne, from the throne, before the throne. The seals, trumpets and bowls that follow all come from this throne (6:1; 8:2; 16:1, 17). So whichever approach a reader takes to Revelation (preterist, historicist, futurist or idealist), most agree on this point: chapters 4 and 5 describe the present reality of heaven, and the approaches mainly part ways from chapter 6 onward.

## Going Deeper: 4:1–2a A Door Open in Heaven

**1. "After this" and "what must take place after this."** "After this I looked, and behold, a door standing open in heaven! And the first voice, which I had heard speaking to me like a trumpet, said, 'Come up here, and I will show you what must take place after this'" (4:1). In chapter 1 the Lord had already told John to "write therefore the things that you have seen, those that are and those that are to take place after this" (1:19). Many interpreters (e.g. Beale) hear in "what must take place" an echo of Daniel, who told Nebuchadnezzar, "there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will be in the latter days" (Dan 2:28; cf. 2:45). The God in heaven of whom Daniel spoke now shows the same kind of things to John.
- **"After this":** the first "after this" refers to the order of John's visions: after the seven letters he saw something new. The second, "what must take place after this," concerns when the events happen. The order in which John sees things is not necessarily the order in which they occur.
- **"Must":** this "must" is the same as in 1:1, "the things that must soon take place." These things will happen, not because of fate, but because God has determined them.

**2. "Come up here": two readings.** This phrase is often debated in Chinese churches.
- **The dispensational reading:** many dispensational interpreters (e.g. the Scofield Reference Bible and John Walvoord) see John's being taken up into heaven as a picture of the rapture of the church. They point out that the word "church" does not appear in chapters 4–19, and conclude that the church is no longer on earth during this period.
- **Other readings:** many other interpreters (whether historic premillennial like George Eldon Ladd, amillennial like Anthony Hoekema, or Beale) take it as John's personal summons in a vision, like Ezekiel by the Chebar canal when "the heavens were opened" (Ezek 1:1), like Isaiah seeing the Lord in the temple (Isa 6:1), and like the prophets who "stood in the council of the LORD" (Jer 23:18, 22). They point out that although chapters 4–19 do not use the word "church," they often speak of "saints" on earth (e.g. 13:7), and the whole book was written "for the churches" (22:16).
Believers who hold either reading confess that the Lord will return in person. Still, this verse itself is about what John saw, not about when the church is caught up; that question has to be weighed from the whole book and the whole Bible, not settled from this verse alone.

**3. "At once I was in the Spirit."** "At once I was in the Spirit, and behold, a throne stood in heaven" (4:2). "In the Spirit" occurs four times in Revelation (1:10; 4:2; 17:3; 21:10), each time opening a major new vision, and many scholars (e.g. Bauckham) see it as a marker of the book's structure. John does not climb into heaven by his own imagination or spiritual technique; the Spirit brings him in. Human beings see the realities of heaven only because God is pleased to reveal them.

**4. What does John see first?** John is summoned to see "what must take place after this," the seals, trumpets and bowls, the dragon and the beasts. Yet God does not show him the disasters first; he shows him the throne. Among the seven churches, some were about to suffer (2:10), one had a martyr (2:13), and one had "but little power" (3:8). To them the center of the world seemed to be Rome, Caesar's throne, the local powers. God lets John see first that the true throne is in heaven, and that someone is seated on it.

> The point: Before John sees any of the storms on earth, he sees the throne in heaven. Revelation does not first tell us what will happen; it first tells us who reigns.

## Going Deeper: 4:2b–6a The Throne and the One Seated on It

**1. "A throne stood in heaven, with one seated on the throne."** The Old Testament prophets saw God's throne more than once. Micaiah said, "I saw the LORD sitting on his throne, and all the host of heaven standing beside him on his right hand and on his left" (1 Kgs 22:19). Isaiah "saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up" (Isa 6:1). Ezekiel saw, above the expanse over the living creatures, "the likeness of a throne" (Ezek 1:26). Daniel saw that "thrones were placed, and the Ancient of days took his seat" (Dan 7:9). And the psalm says, "The LORD has established his throne in the heavens, and his kingdom rules over all" (Ps 103:19). John sees this same throne. It "stood": it is set in place, it will not be overturned, and it is not empty.

**2. "The appearance of jasper and carnelian."** "And he who sat there had the appearance of jasper and carnelian" (4:3). John does not describe God's form. Ezekiel at least says "a likeness with a human appearance" (Ezek 1:26); John speaks only of the brilliance of precious stones. This is reverent restraint: God cannot be captured in an image (cf. Exod 20:4), and we can speak only of the radiance of his glory.
- **Jasper:** later, when the holy city comes down, it has "the glory of God, its radiance like a most rare jewel, like a jasper, clear as crystal" (21:11).
- **The high priest's breastpiece:** of its twelve stones, the first is a sardius (the same stone as "carnelian" here) and the last is a jasper (Exod 28:17, 20). Some interpreters suggest John may be using the first and last stones to hint that the One on the throne is the God who has bound himself in covenant to his people; but this is only a possible association.

**3. "Around the throne was a rainbow."** "And around the throne was a rainbow that had the appearance of an emerald" (4:3). The background is first Ezekiel: "Like the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud on the day of rain, so was the appearance of the brightness all around. Such was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD" (Ezek 1:28). And the bow in the cloud takes the reader back to the flood: "I have set my bow in the cloud, and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and the earth" (Gen 9:13).
- **Judgment and mercy:** the flood was God's judgment on sin; the bow was God's oath after judgment that "the waters shall never again become a flood to destroy all flesh" (Gen 9:15). Lightning and thunder come from this throne (4:5), and the coming judgments will come from here too; yet first a bow surrounds the throne.
- **"Around":** a bow in the cloud is an arc, but this bow goes "around" the throne. The Greek word (*iris*) can also mean a halo. However it is understood, John sees the throne encircled on every side by the sign of the covenant.
- **An echo in Isaiah:** God later told his people through Isaiah, "This is like the days of Noah to me: as I swore that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth, so I have sworn that I will not be angry with you, and will not rebuke you" (Isa 54:9). God made the covenant with Noah a pledge of his covenant love to his people.

**4. The twenty-four elders.** "Around the throne were twenty-four thrones, and seated on the thrones were twenty-four elders, clothed in white garments, with golden crowns on their heads" (4:4). There are two main views of who they are:
- **Representatives of the whole people of God:** many interpreters (e.g. Beale) take twenty-four as the twelve tribes of Israel plus the twelve apostles of the Lamb; the city's gates later bear the names of the twelve tribes and its foundations the names of the twelve apostles (21:12, 14). Their white garments, crowns and thrones are all things the Lord promised to those who conquer (3:5, 18; 2:10; 3:21). David's division of the priests into twenty-four courses (1 Chr 24) may also lie in the background.
- **Heavenly beings:** other interpreters (e.g. David Aune, Robert Mounce) take them as angelic members of the heavenly council (cf. 1 Kgs 22:19). They note that the elders hold "the prayers of the saints" (5:8) and explain to John who the saints are (7:13–14), as if they were distinct from the saints.
Both readings agree that the throne is not surrounded by emptiness: there is a company of worshipers whose crowns were received from God and are given back to him (4:10).

**5. Lightning, thunder and seven torches.** "From the throne came flashes of lightning, and rumblings and peals of thunder, and before the throne were burning seven torches of fire, which are the seven spirits of God" (4:5).
- **Sinai:** when God came down on Sinai, "there were thunders and lightnings and a thick cloud on the mountain and a very loud trumpet blast, so that all the people in the camp trembled" (Exod 19:16). This is the throne of the holy God. At the close of each later series of judgments, these signs return, growing stronger each time (8:5; 11:19; 16:18).
- **"The seven spirits of God":** these are the same as "the seven spirits who are before his throne" (1:4). Many interpreters take them as the Holy Spirit, seven signifying fullness, with the seven lamps of Zechariah (Zech 4:2, 10) and the sevenfold description of the Spirit of the LORD in Isaiah 11:2 in the background; others take them as the seven angels who stand before God (cf. 8:2). Chapter 5 speaks of "seven eyes, which are the seven spirits of God sent out into all the earth" (5:6), which favors the first reading.

**6. "As it were a sea of glass, like crystal."** "And before the throne there was as it were a sea of glass, like crystal" (4:6). Ezekiel saw over the living creatures an expanse "shining like awe-inspiring crystal" (Ezek 1:22); Moses and the elders saw the God of Israel, and "there was under his feet as it were a pavement of sapphire stone, like the very heaven for clearness" (Exod 24:10). Solomon's temple also had a "sea" of cast metal (1 Kgs 7:23). In Revelation and Daniel, moreover, the sea is often the place of chaos and evil: the beasts rise out of it (Dan 7:3; Rev 13:1), and in the new creation "the sea was no more" (21:1). So some interpreters see here a sea made calm as glass before the throne; later those who conquer stand "beside the sea of glass" (15:2). These backgrounds need not exclude one another; together they say that before the throne all is clear, still and under God's rule.

> The point: Lightning and thunder come from the throne because the One seated on it is the holy God; yet a rainbow surrounds the throne because this God keeps covenant.

## Going Deeper: 4:6b–8 The Four Living Creatures: Holy, Holy, Holy

**1. Ezekiel and Isaiah together.** "And around the throne, on each side of the throne, are four living creatures, full of eyes in front and behind" (4:6). The four living creatures bear features from both Ezekiel and Isaiah:
- **Ezekiel's living creatures:** Ezekiel saw "the likeness of four living creatures" (Ezek 1:5), with the faces of a man, a lion, an ox and an eagle (Ezek 1:10), and later he knew "that they were cherubim" (Ezek 10:20); their rims were "full of eyes all around" (Ezek 1:18; 10:12).
- **Isaiah's seraphim:** "each had six wings," and they called to one another, "Holy, holy, holy" (Isa 6:2–3).
By joining the two visions, John shows that he is seeing the same God the prophets saw. One difference: each of Ezekiel's creatures has four faces, while each of John's creatures resembles one.

**2. Lion, ox, man, eagle.** "The first living creature like a lion, the second living creature like an ox, the third living creature with the face of a man, and the fourth living creature like an eagle in flight" (4:7). Many interpreters (e.g. Bauckham, Beale) take the four as the noblest of wild animals, domestic animals, humankind and birds, so that the four living creatures represent all living creation, offering creation's worship before the throne. In the early church Irenaeus linked the four creatures to the four Gospels (*Against Heresies* 3.11.8), and this later became a common symbol in Christian art; it is a traditional association in the church, not John's point here.

**3. "Full of eyes all around and within."** Eyes signify watchfulness and insight. They are always looking at God and at all he does; their worship is worship with eyes open, praise that comes from having seen.

**4. "Holy, holy, holy."** "And day and night they never cease to say, 'Holy, holy, holy, is the Lord God Almighty, who was and is and is to come!'" (4:8).
- **Three times "holy":** Hebrew uses repetition to express the superlative. It is often noted that the Old Testament seldom repeats one attribute of God three times as Isaiah 6 does. God's holiness is his majesty, utterly distinct from every created thing, and his complete rejection of sin. When Isaiah saw it, he said, "Woe is me! For I am lost" (Isa 6:5).
- **"Almighty":** this word (*pantokratōr*) is often used in the Septuagint to translate "the LORD of hosts," meaning the One who rules over all. In Revelation it is used almost always of God (1:8; 11:17; 15:3). Roman emperors claimed power, but there is only one Almighty.
- **"Who was and is and is to come":** in chapter 1 the order is "who is and who was and who is to come" (1:4, 8); here "who was" comes first. In chapters 11 and 16 the heavenly worshipers say only "who is and who was" (11:17; 16:5), because by then he has "taken [his] great power and begun to reign," and the "is to come" has arrived. Many interpreters hear behind this title the name God revealed to Moses at the burning bush (Exod 3:14).

**5. "Day and night they never cease."** This is the unending worship of heaven. Revelation later speaks of another "day and night": those who worship the beast and its image "have no rest, day or night" (14:11). Everyone worships something; those who worship God praise him day and night without ceasing, and those who worship the beast have no rest day or night.

> The point: Heaven's worship begins with God's holiness. A knowledge of God that has love without holiness makes grace cheap; the more God's holiness is seen, the more wonderful his grace appears.

## Going Deeper: 4:9–11 "Worthy Are You"

**1. Crowns cast before the throne.** "And whenever the living creatures give glory and honor and thanks to him who is seated on the throne, who lives forever and ever, the twenty-four elders fall down before him who is seated on the throne and worship him who lives forever and ever. They cast their crowns before the throne" (4:9–10). The crowns are honors they have received, and now they lay them before the throne. They confess that all honor comes from God and returns to God. Some scholars (e.g. David Aune) note that in Roman imperial court ceremony client kings also laid their crowns before the emperor as a sign of submission; if that is in view, John's readers would understand that the one worthy of such homage is not Caesar but God on the heavenly throne.

**2. "Our Lord and God."** "Worthy are you, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power" (4:11). The Roman historian Suetonius records that Domitian liked to be addressed as "our Lord and God" (*Domitian* 13.2). If Revelation was written under Domitian, this confession carries special weight; even if it was written earlier, the imperial cult was already flourishing in the cities of Asia. For believers in the seven churches this line was no empty formula but a stand: we have one Lord, and it is not Caesar. Still, this should not be overstated: the pressure believers faced was mostly local, from the imperial cult in their cities, the trade guilds and conflict with the synagogue, not necessarily a single empire-wide persecution.

**3. "For you created all things."** "For you created all things, and by your will they existed and were created" (4:11).
- **The reason for worship:** the elders worship God because he created all things. God is worthy of worship not because he meets our needs but because he is God; our existence and the existence of all things come from him.
- **"By your will":** the existence of all things is neither accident nor necessity forced on God; it comes from his will. God does not need the world; he created it out of his freedom and goodness.
- **"They existed and were created":** the unusual order has led some interpreters to suggest an emphasis that all things, from their creation until now, continue to exist by God's will. God did not withdraw after creating; he upholds all things.

**4. From chapter 4 to chapter 5.** The worship of chapter 4 is offered to the Creator: "you created all things" (4:11); the worship of chapter 5 is offered to the Redeemer: "you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God" (5:9). The order matters: the One who will redeem the world and make all things new (21:5) is the One who created all things in the beginning. He will not abandon the world he made. Someone who knew only chapter 4 might feel that God is far above and terrifying; chapter 5 tells us that a Lamb stands at the center of this throne.

> The point: God is worthy of worship first because he is the Creator; all things, and I myself, exist by his will. This God will not abandon what he has made.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "At once I was in the Spirit, and behold, a throne stood in heaven, with one seated on the throne... and around the throne was a rainbow that had the appearance of an emerald" (4:2–3). A sister watches the news every evening after dinner. Her company is laying people off, and at her age she will be among the first considered; the mortgage has twenty years left; her father's test results will not come back until next week; there is another war abroad, and prices keep rising. Someone at church forwards an article on the end times claiming that all the signs have now been fulfilled, and after reading it she is more frightened than before. It is not that she does not believe in God, but a voice inside her says: the world is out of control, and so is my life.

On the island of Patmos John saw a world more out of control than ours. The churches were under pressure, some had already died for Christ, and the empire's power seemed to have no limit. Yet what God showed him first was not the chaos on earth but "a throne stood in heaven," "with one seated on the throne." The throne is not empty, and it is not shaking. The seals, trumpets and bowls John will see next all come from this throne; no storm arises outside it.

I may already have done many wrong things in my anxiety: snapped at my family, searched the internet for news late into the night, tried to hold everything in my own grip, and then, unable to, stopped praying. Anxiety is not a sign of great faith, but God has not driven me from his presence because of it. In chapter 5 John will see a Lamb standing at the center of that throne, as though it had been slain; he died for me, someone whose heart is in turmoil and who tries to keep hold of everything by myself. I do not need to calm myself first before I may come before this throne; it is precisely in my unrest that I am invited up here to look.

I will still have to face those test results, still have to find work or keep it; I can ask brothers and sisters for help and ask others to pray for me. But all that I face lies beneath this throne. God once said of the bow in the cloud, "I will see it and remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is on the earth" (Gen 9:16). The one who remembers the covenant is God himself, not me. Later he said, "For the mountains may depart and the hills be removed, but my steadfast love shall not depart from you" (Isa 54:10). That covenant of steadfast love has been sealed in the blood of the Lamb. Tonight's news may not change, but I can lift my eyes: around the One seated on the throne is a rainbow like an emerald, his covenant mercy encircling the whole throne, and encircling me within it.
