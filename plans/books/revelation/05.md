## 本章脉络

第5章接续第4章的同一个异象。宝座、四活物、二十四位长老都还在，但焦点从坐宝座的那一位，转到一卷书和一只羔羊身上。全章可以分成四段：
- **5:1-4，封严的书卷：** 坐宝座的右手中有书卷，用七印封严了；天上地下没有一个配展开的，约翰就大哭。
- **5:5-7，狮子和羔羊：** 长老说「不要哭」，犹大支派中的狮子已得胜；约翰转眼一看，却是一只像是被杀过的羔羊站立着，他前来拿了书卷。
- **5:8-10，新歌：** 四活物和长老俯伏在羔羊面前，唱新歌说：「你配……因为你曾被杀」。
- **5:11-14，越来越大的敬拜：** 千千万万的天使，然后是天上、地上、地底下、沧海里的一切被造之物，把颂赞、尊贵、荣耀、权势归给坐宝座的和羔羊。

这一章敬拜的圈子一圈比一圈大。第4章的敬拜献给创造主，第5章的敬拜献给羔羊，到了全章的末了，同样的敬拜同时归给「坐宝座的和羔羊」（5:13）。

## 深读 5:1-4 谁配展开那书卷

**1. 「里外都写着字，用七印封严了」。** 「我看见坐宝座的右手中有书卷，里外都写着字，用七印封严了。」（5:1）这卷书的背景，主要来自以西结书和但以理书。
- **以西结的书卷：** 以西结看见「有一只手向我伸出来，手中有一书卷」，「内外都写着字，其上所写的有哀号、叹息、悲痛的话」（结2:9-10）。
- **但以理封闭的书：** 神吩咐但以理：「你要隐藏这话，封闭这书，直到末时。」（但12:4）
- **「七印」：** 七是完全的数目，这卷书封得严严实实；「里外都写着字」，表示内容是完整的。

**2. 这书卷是什么？** 释经者有几种主要的看法：
- **神对历史的计划，包括审判和救赎：** 许多释经者（如毕尔）认为，这卷书是神要在世上建立他国度的旨意，包括对罪恶的审判和对他百姓的救赎；羔羊揭开七印的时候，这些事就一一展开。包衡认为，第10章那「展开」的小书卷（10:2）就是这卷书。他还有一个独特的看法：七印只是预备；书卷真正的内容，是神要藉着教会忠心的见证使列国悔改归向他，要到第10-11章才显明出来。
- **契约或产业的契据：** 有些通俗的未来派作者根据耶利米买地时「封缄的那一张」契约（耶32:10-11），以及罗马时代用七个见证人的印封住的遗嘱，认为这是神把地和产业归给合法继承人的契据，只有那位有资格的救赎者才能打开。
- **生命册：** 也有人把它和「羔羊生命册」（13:8；20:12）连在一起，不过持这种看法的人较少。
这几种看法都承认：这卷书关乎神对世界的旨意最终怎样成就，而这旨意在神的右手中。

**3. 「有谁配」。** 「我又看见一位大力的天使大声宣传说：『有谁配展开那书卷，揭开那七印呢？』」（5:2）天使问的是「有谁配」。原文这个词（*axios*）是够资格的意思，和4:11、5:9、5:12的「配得」是同一个词。要展开这卷书，需要的不只是能力，更是资格。「在天上、地上、地底下，没有能展开、能观看那书卷的。」（5:3）连天上的天使，连那位大力的天使，也不配。

**4. 「我就大哭」。** 「因为没有配展开、配观看那书卷的，我就大哭。」（5:4）约翰不是因为好奇心得不到满足而哭。他被召上来，要看「以后必成的事」（4:1）；如果这卷书没有人能打开，神对世界的审判和救赎好像就停在那里，地上受冤屈的人就没有人为他们伸冤，教会的苦难也看不到尽头。约翰哭，是因为他渴望神的旨意成就。

> 要点：神对世界的旨意握在他的右手中，但天上地下没有一个受造的配展开它。历史的答案，不在人自己身上。

## 深读 5:5-7 狮子和羔羊

**1. 「犹大支派中的狮子」。** 「长老中有一位对我说：『不要哭！看哪，犹大支派中的狮子，大卫的根，他已得胜，能以展开那书卷，揭开那七印。』」（5:5）这个称号来自雅各临终前对犹大的祝福：「犹大是个小狮子……圭必不离犹大，杖必不离他两脚之间，直等细罗来到，万民都必归顺。」（创49:9-10）犹太人很早就把这段话读作弥赛亚的应许，例如约在第一世纪末写成的《以斯拉四书》，就把异象中的狮子解释为出于大卫后裔的弥赛亚（12.31-32）。

**2. 「大卫的根」。** 以赛亚说：「到那日，耶西的根立作万民的大旗；外邦人必寻求他」（赛11:10；参罗15:12）。在以赛亚书里，「耶西的根」是从耶西根上长出来的那位大卫的王（参赛11:1）。主耶稣在启示录的末了说：「我是大卫的根，又是他的后裔」（22:16），许多释经者（如大卫·奥尼、罗伯特·芒斯）认为这也是强调他是大卫家的弥赛亚。也有人进一步看见：大卫的子孙同时是大卫的主（可12:35-37），大卫的王朝是从他得着生命的。

**3. 「他已得胜」。** 「已得胜」原文是不定过去式，许多释经者认为是指一件已经完成的事。他是在什么时候得胜的？启示录的答案很清楚：「就如我得了胜，在我父的宝座上与他同坐一般」（3:21）；弟兄胜过那控告者，「是因羔羊的血」（12:11）。他的得胜是在十字架和复活中完成的。

**4. 听见的是狮子，看见的是羔羊。** 「我又看见宝座与四活物，并长老之中有羔羊站立，像是被杀过的」（5:6）。约翰听见的是「狮子」，转过来看见的却是「羔羊」。许多学者（如包衡）指出，这是全书最关键的一个转折：犹太人盼望一位像狮子的弥赛亚；这位弥赛亚却是借着被杀，像羔羊一样得胜的。
- **羔羊的背景：** 逾越节的羔羊，神说：「我一见这血，就越过你们去」（出12:13）；以赛亚书那位受苦的仆人，「像羊羔被牵到宰杀之地」，「为我们的过犯受害，为我们的罪孽压伤」（赛53:7、5）；施洗约翰说：「看哪，神的羔羊，除去世人罪孽的！」（约1:29）
- **羔羊并没有取代狮子：** 「羔羊」这个称呼在启示录里用了将近三十次，几乎都是指基督。这只羔羊也有忿怒（6:16），也要争战并得胜（17:14）。狮子的意象并没有被废掉，而是被重新解释：他的能力，正是在舍命中显明的。

**5. 「站立，像是被杀过的」。** 「像是被杀过的」原文是完成式分词，许多释经者认为，这表示他被杀这件事的果效和记号一直存留。他「站立」着，因为他已经复活了；他「像是被杀过的」，因为他身上仍带着被杀的记号。他就是第1章那位说「我曾死过，现在又活了」的主（1:18）。

**6. 「七角七眼」。** 「有七角七眼，就是神的七灵，奉差遣往普天下去的。」（5:6）
- **七角：** 角在旧约中象征能力（申33:17；参但7:7）。七角表示完全的能力。这只羔羊不是软弱的。
- **七眼：** 撒迦利亚说：「这七眼乃是耶和华的眼睛，遍察全地」（亚4:10）。在这里，耶和华的眼睛成了羔羊的眼睛，而且就是「神的七灵」。若七灵是指圣灵（参1:4的讨论），那么圣灵既是神的灵，也是羔羊所差遣的（参约15:26）。这里可以看见父、子、圣灵的分别和合一：坐宝座的、羔羊、奉差遣往普天下去的灵。

**7. 「从坐宝座的右手里拿了书卷」。** 「这羔羊前来，从坐宝座的右手里拿了书卷。」（5:7）但以理看见「有一位像人子的，驾着天云而来，被领到亘古常在者面前，得了权柄、荣耀、国度」（但7:13-14）。约翰所看见的，许多释经者认为就是这件事的成就：被杀的羔羊从神手中接过了历史的书卷。世界的结局，掌握在为世人被钉的那一位手里。

**8. 只有羔羊配展开。** 东方闪电（自称全能神教会）宣称他们的末世女基督已经展开了书卷和七印（参看导读）。启示录却说，配展开书卷的，是那位「曾被杀，用自己的血」买了人的羔羊（5:9），不是后来另外出现的什么人（参约一4:2-3）。家人若被这类团体控制，可以和牧者一起寻求帮助；若有限制人身自由、骗取钱财或伤害人的事，应当报警。

> 要点：神的能力最清楚地显明在被杀的羔羊身上。他是狮子，但他是借着舍命得胜的。

## 深读 5:8-10 新歌

**1. 「盛满了香的金炉；这香就是众圣徒的祈祷」。** 「他既拿了书卷，四活物和二十四位长老就俯伏在羔羊面前，各拿着琴和盛满了香的金炉；这香就是众圣徒的祈祷。」（5:8）诗人说：「愿我的祷告如香陈列在你面前！」（诗141:2）地上受苦的圣徒的祷告，都被盛在金炉中，摆在宝座前。到了第8章，「那香的烟和众圣徒的祈祷从天使的手中一同升到神面前」，随后天使把坛上的火倒在地上（8:4-5）。圣徒的祷告，是神在历史中行事的一部分。

**2. 「他们唱新歌」。** 旧约多次呼召人「唱新歌」（诗33:3；96:1；赛42:10），往往是因为神行了新的拯救。这里的新歌，是为羔羊所成就的救赎而唱的。

**3. 「因为你曾被杀，用自己的血……买了人来」。** 「你配拿书卷，配揭开七印；因为你曾被杀，用自己的血从各族、各方、各民、各国中买了人来，叫他们归于神」（5:9）。
- **配的理由：** 羔羊配拿书卷的理由，不是他的能力，而是他曾被杀。十字架就是他的资格。
- **「买」：** 这个词（*agorazō*）原是市场上买东西的意思。人被罪辖制，羔羊用自己的血作代价，把人买回来归于神。主耶稣自己说，他来是要「舍命作多人的赎价」（可10:45）。启示录开头也说，他「用自己的血使我们脱离罪恶」（1:5）。他代替我们死，承担了我们罪的刑罚，我们才得以归于神。
- **「从各族、各方、各民、各国」：** 包衡指出，这四个词的组合在启示录里出现了七次（如7:9；13:7），背景是但以理书「各方、各国、各族的人都事奉他」（但7:14）。羔羊所买的人，不只属于一个民族。福音不是洋教，也不是哪一个民族的宗教。
- **抄本的差异：** 英文钦定本这两节都作「我们」（买了我们，叫我们作王）。第9节确有许多抄本（包括西乃抄本）有「我们」，但亚历山大抄本没有宾语；现代经文校勘多采用这个较短的读法，因为第10节作「他们」的抄本证据很强，作「我们」的只见于较后期的抄本。和合本的「人」和英文标准版里相应的词，都是译者补上的。有人根据「我们」的读法，主张二十四位长老是蒙救赎的人；不过单凭这个异文，不能断定长老的身份。

**4. 「成为国民，作祭司归于神」。** 「又叫他们成为国民，作祭司归于神，在地上执掌王权。」（5:10）神在西奈山对以色列说：「你们要归我作祭司的国度，为圣洁的国民。」（出19:6）这个呼召，现在在羔羊所买赎的人身上成就了（参1:6；彼前2:9）。
- **祭司：** 每一个蒙救赎的人，都可以直接来到神面前，也为世人代求，把神介绍给人。
- **「在地上执掌王权」：** 有些抄本作现在式，有些作将来式；现代经文校勘和英文标准版采用将来式。许多释经者认为两样意思都有道理：信徒现在已经被立为国民（1:6），将来要在新天新地里「作王，直到永永远远」（22:5）。「在地上」也提醒我们，神的救赎不是要把人带离受造的世界，而是要更新这个世界。

> 要点：羔羊配得一切，因为他曾被杀。他的血不但赦免了人的罪，也把一群人买回来，使他们作神的祭司和国民。

## 深读 5:11-14 归给坐宝座的和羔羊

**1. 千千万万的天使。** 「我又看见且听见，宝座与活物并长老的周围有许多天使的声音；他们的数目有千千万万」（5:11）。但以理也看见，在亘古常在者面前，「事奉他的有千千，在他面前侍立的有万万」（但7:10）。在这里，他们一同向羔羊高声颂赞。

**2. 七样的颂赞。** 「大声说：曾被杀的羔羊是配得权柄、丰富、智慧、能力、尊贵、荣耀、颂赞的。」（5:12）七样，又是完全的数目。天使称他为「曾被杀的羔羊」：天上称颂他时，没有避开他的死，反而用他的死来称呼他。在世人眼中，钉十字架是羞辱和软弱；在天上，这却是他配得一切的理由（参林前1:23-24）。

**3. 一切被造之物。** 「我又听见在天上、地上、地底下、沧海里，和天地间一切所有被造之物，都说：但愿颂赞、尊贵、荣耀、权势都归给坐宝座的和羔羊，直到永永远远！」（5:13）先前天上、地上、地底下没有一个能展开、配展开书卷的（5:3-4），现在天上、地上、地底下一切被造的都来敬拜。保罗也说，「叫一切在天上的、地上的，和地底下的，因耶稣的名无不屈膝」（腓2:10），这句话的背景是以赛亚书，神在那里说：「万膝必向我跪拜」（赛45:23）。

**4. 羔羊和坐宝座的同受敬拜。** 这是本章在基督论上最重要的一点。
- **启示录严格的一神信仰：** 约翰两次俯伏要拜天使，天使都说：「千万不可！……你要敬拜神。」（19:10；22:9）神也说：「我必不将我的荣耀归给假神」（赛42:8）；这里的「假神」，希伯来原文直译是「别的」：神的荣耀，不归给他以外的任何一位。在这卷书里，敬拜只能归给神。
- **羔羊却领受同样的敬拜：** 四活物和长老「俯伏在羔羊面前」（5:8），正如他们在第4章俯伏在坐宝座的面前（4:10）；同一首颂赞，同时归给「坐宝座的和羔羊」（5:13）。许多学者（如包衡）认为，这表示约翰把耶稣列在独一真神的身份之内，而不是把他当作第二位神，或一位次等的、受造的神。这和主耶稣所说的一致：「叫人都尊敬子如同尊敬父一样。」（约5:23）
- **分别而合一：** 羔羊和坐宝座的是有分别的，羔羊是从坐宝座的手中接过书卷的（5:7）。可是到了全书的末了，城里只有「神和羔羊的宝座」，而且「他的仆人都要事奉他」（22:3），这里用的是单数的「他」。启示录没有用后来《尼西亚信经》的用语，却已经提供了教会后来所承认之事的根据：子与父同受敬拜、同享荣耀，子与父同质；神只有一位。
- **对今天的意义：** 有些团体（如耶和华见证人）认为耶稣是受造的，不应当受敬拜。启示录第5章却让我们看见，天上全体都在敬拜他。我们向耶稣祷告、向他歌唱，不是把荣耀从父那里拿走，而是照着父的心意尊敬子。

**5. 「阿们」和俯伏。** 「四活物就说：『阿们！』众长老也俯伏敬拜。」（5:14）敬拜最后的回应，不是更多的话，而是说：是的，正是这样；然后俯伏在他面前。

> 要点：天上对羔羊的敬拜，和对坐宝座者的敬拜完全一样。被杀的羔羊不是神之外的另一位神，而是与父同受敬拜的子。

## 默想

**默想：** 「长老中有一位对我说：『不要哭！看哪，犹大支派中的狮子，大卫的根，他已得胜……』我又看见……有羔羊站立，像是被杀过的」（5:5-6）。一间小教会这几年一直在压力之下：聚会的地方换了三次，几个年轻的家庭搬走了，有的肢体在单位里因为信仰被冷落，传道人一个人撑着，已经累得说话都没有力气。弟兄姊妹每次祷告会都求同一件事：求主大能地出手，叫那些为难教会的人受到报应，叫门一下子都打开，叫所有人都看见神是活的。他们心里求的，是一只狮子。可是一年又一年过去，情况没有翻转。有一次祷告会结束，一位长老在空了一半的客厅里，像约翰一样哭了：好像没有人能打开这卷书，好像神的手停住了。

天上那位长老对约翰说：「不要哭！看哪，犹大支派中的狮子」。约翰转过身去，看见的却是一只羔羊，像是被杀过的。神并没有骗约翰：这只羔羊确实是狮子，他有七角，整个历史的书卷都在他手中，到了日子，他要审判，为地上一切受冤屈的人伸冤（6:16-17）。可是他「已得胜」（5:5），是在十字架上得胜的；天上称他配得一切，理由是「你曾被杀」（5:9）。他的国度不是靠打倒仇敌开始的，而是靠他为仇敌流血开始的；那些胜过控告者的圣徒，也是「因羔羊的血和自己所见证的道」得胜的（12:11）。

这并不是说，我们不该求神出手。启示录里圣徒的祷告，都盛在金炉里，摆在宝座前（5:8），没有一句白白落在地上。这也不是说，教会受压就是失败。可是在这几年里，我们也许不只是哭：有人埋怨过传道人不够刚强，有人暗暗计算哪天可以离开，有人心里盼望的，与其说是神的国，不如说是对方的倒台。羔羊所流的血，正是为这样的人流的；我们可以在他面前承认这些，不必再为自己辩护。他的资格，就是他曾被杀，为了买我们归于神，叫我们成为国民，作祭司（5:9-10）。

我们仍然可以每周聚在一起，为城市、为那些为难教会的人、也为自己祷告；也可以一起扶持那位累坏了的传道人。在那间空了一半的客厅里，我们还可以和天上千千万万的声音一同唱：「曾被杀的羔羊是配得权柄、丰富、智慧、能力、尊贵、荣耀、颂赞的」（5:12）。这首歌，不必等到局面翻转了才开始唱。我们不知道这卷书接下来要怎样展开。但我们知道是谁拿着它：不是一位远离苦难的神，而是那位为我们被杀的羔羊。他没有被打倒；他身上仍然带着被杀的记号，却站立着，就站在宝座中间。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 5 continues the vision of chapter 4. The throne, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders are all still there, but the focus moves from the One seated on the throne to a scroll and a Lamb. The chapter falls into four parts:
- **5:1–4, the sealed scroll:** in the right hand of the One on the throne is a scroll sealed with seven seals; no one in heaven or on earth is worthy to open it, and John weeps loudly.
- **5:5–7, the Lion and the Lamb:** an elder says, "Weep no more"; the Lion of the tribe of Judah has conquered. John looks, and sees instead a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain, who comes and takes the scroll.
- **5:8–10, the new song:** the four living creatures and the elders fall down before the Lamb and sing a new song: "Worthy are you... for you were slain."
- **5:11–14, widening worship:** myriads of angels, and then every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth and in the sea, give blessing, honor, glory and might to the One who sits on the throne and to the Lamb.

The circle of worship in this chapter grows wider and wider. The worship of chapter 4 is offered to the Creator, the worship of chapter 5 to the Lamb, and at the close of the chapter the same worship is given together "to him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb" (5:13).

## Going Deeper: 5:1–4 Who Is Worthy to Open the Scroll?

**1. "Written within and on the back, sealed with seven seals."** "Then I saw in the right hand of him who was seated on the throne a scroll written within and on the back, sealed with seven seals" (5:1). The background of this scroll comes mainly from Ezekiel and Daniel.
- **Ezekiel's scroll:** Ezekiel saw "a hand was stretched out to me, and behold, a scroll of a book was in it"; "it had writing on the front and on the back, and there were written on it words of lamentation and mourning and woe" (Ezek 2:9–10).
- **Daniel's sealed book:** God told Daniel, "shut up the words and seal the book, until the time of the end" (Dan 12:4).
- **"Seven seals":** seven is the number of completeness; the scroll is sealed tight, and "written within and on the back" suggests that its contents are complete.

**2. What is the scroll?** Interpreters hold several main views:
- **God's plan for history, including judgment and redemption:** many interpreters (e.g. Beale) take the scroll as God's purpose to establish his kingdom in the world, including judgment on evil and the redemption of his people; as the Lamb breaks the seals, these things begin to unfold. Bauckham identifies the "little scroll open in his hand" (10:2) with this same scroll, and argues distinctively that the seals are preliminary and that its real content, God's purpose to bring the nations to repentance through the church's faithful witness, is disclosed from chapters 10–11 on.
- **A covenant or a title deed:** some popular futurist writers point to the "sealed deed of purchase" when Jeremiah bought land (Jer 32:10–11), and to Roman wills sealed with the seals of seven witnesses, and take the scroll as the deed by which God gives the earth and its inheritance to its rightful heir, which only a qualified redeemer can open.
- **The book of life:** some connect it with "the book of life of the Lamb" (13:8; 20:12), though fewer hold this view.
All these views agree that the scroll concerns how God's purpose for the world will finally be fulfilled, and that this purpose is in God's right hand.

**3. "Who is worthy?"** "And I saw a strong angel proclaiming with a loud voice, 'Who is worthy to open the scroll and break its seals?'" (5:2). The angel asks "who is worthy?" The word (*axios*) means deserving, qualified; it is the same word as "worthy" in 4:11, 5:9 and 5:12. To open this scroll requires not only power but worthiness. "And no one in heaven or on earth or under the earth was able to open the scroll or to look into it" (5:3). Not even the angels of heaven, not even the strong angel, is worthy.

**4. "I began to weep loudly."** "And I began to weep loudly because no one was found worthy to open the scroll or to look into it" (5:4). John does not weep because his curiosity has been frustrated. He was summoned to see "what must take place after this" (4:1); if no one can open the scroll, God's judgment and redemption of the world seem to stall, the wrongs on earth will not be set right, and the churches' suffering has no end in sight. John's tears are the tears of a man who longs for God's purpose to be accomplished.

> The point: God's purpose for the world is held in his right hand, but no creature in heaven or on earth is worthy to open it. The answer to history does not lie in human beings themselves.

## Going Deeper: 5:5–7 The Lion and the Lamb

**1. "The Lion of the tribe of Judah."** "And one of the elders said to me, 'Weep no more; behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered, so that he can open the scroll and its seven seals'" (5:5). The title comes from Jacob's blessing of Judah before his death: "Judah is a lion's cub... The scepter shall not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, until tribute comes to him; and to him shall be the obedience of the peoples" (Gen 49:9–10). Jews read these words early on as a messianic promise; *4 Ezra*, for example, written around the end of the first century, interprets the lion of its vision as the Messiah from the posterity of David (12.31-32).

**2. "The Root of David."** Isaiah says, "In that day the root of Jesse, who shall stand as a signal for the peoples—of him shall the nations inquire" (Isa 11:10; cf. Rom 15:12). In Isaiah "the root of Jesse" is the Davidic king who springs from Jesse's root (cf. Isa 11:1). At the end of Revelation the Lord Jesus says, "I am the root and the descendant of David" (22:16), and many interpreters (e.g. David Aune, Robert Mounce) take this the same way, stressing that he is the Davidic Messiah. Some also see that David's son is David's Lord (Mark 12:35–37), the one from whom David's line draws its life.

**3. "Has conquered."** "Has conquered" is an aorist, which many interpreters take to point to an accomplished event. When did he conquer? Revelation answers clearly: "as I also conquered and sat down with my Father on his throne" (3:21); the brothers conquered the accuser "by the blood of the Lamb" (12:11). His victory was won at the cross and the resurrection.

**4. He hears "Lion" and sees "Lamb."** "And between the throne and the four living creatures and among the elders I saw a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain" (5:6). John hears "Lion"; he turns and sees a Lamb. Many scholars (e.g. Bauckham) see this as the decisive turn of the whole book: the lion-like Messiah Israel hoped for conquers as the slain Lamb.
- **The background of the Lamb:** the Passover lamb, of which God said, "when I see the blood, I will pass over you" (Exod 12:13); Isaiah's suffering servant, "like a lamb that is led to the slaughter," who "was wounded for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities" (Isa 53:7, 5); and John the Baptist's witness, "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29).
- **The Lamb does not replace the Lion:** "Lamb" is used nearly thirty times in Revelation, almost always of Christ. This Lamb also has wrath (6:16) and will make war and conquer (17:14). The lion image is not abolished but reinterpreted: his power is displayed in the giving of his life.

**5. "Standing, as though it had been slain."** "As though it had been slain" is a perfect participle in Greek, which many interpreters take to mean that the effect and the marks of his slaying remain. He is "standing" because he has risen; he looks "as though it had been slain" because he still bears the marks of his death. He is the Lord of chapter 1 who said, "I died, and behold I am alive forevermore" (1:18).

**6. "Seven horns and seven eyes."** "With seven horns and with seven eyes, which are the seven spirits of God sent out into all the earth" (5:6).
- **Seven horns:** in the Old Testament horns signify power (Deut 33:17; cf. Dan 7:7). Seven horns mean complete power. This Lamb is not weak.
- **Seven eyes:** Zechariah says, "These seven are the eyes of the LORD, which range through the whole earth" (Zech 4:10). Here the eyes of the LORD have become the Lamb's eyes, and they are "the seven spirits of God." If the seven spirits are the Holy Spirit (see the discussion of 1:4), then the Spirit is both the Spirit of God and the one sent by the Lamb (cf. John 15:26). We see here the distinction and the unity of Father, Son and Spirit: the One seated on the throne, the Lamb, and the Spirit sent out into all the earth.

**7. "He took the scroll from the right hand of him who was seated on the throne."** "And he went and took the scroll from the right hand of him who was seated on the throne" (5:7). Daniel saw "one like a son of man" who "came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him. And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom" (Dan 7:13–14). Many interpreters see John witnessing the fulfillment of this: the slain Lamb receives the scroll of history from God's hand. The outcome of the world lies in the hands of the one who was crucified for the world.

**8. Only the Lamb is worthy to open it.** Eastern Lightning (which calls itself the Church of Almighty God) claims that its female "Christ" of the last days has opened the scroll and its seals (see the Introduction). Revelation says that the one worthy to open the scroll is the Lamb who "was slain, and by [his] blood... ransomed people for God" (5:9), not some other figure who appears later (cf. 1 John 4:2–3). If a family member is controlled by such a group, seek help together with a pastor; where personal freedom is restricted, money is extorted or people are harmed, report it to the police.

> The point: God's power is displayed most clearly in the slain Lamb. He is the Lion, but he conquered by giving his life.

## Going Deeper: 5:8–10 The New Song

**1. "Golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints."** "And when he had taken the scroll, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each holding a harp, and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints" (5:8). The psalmist prayed, "Let my prayer be counted as incense before you" (Ps 141:2). The prayers of the suffering saints on earth are gathered in golden bowls before the throne. In chapter 8 "the smoke of the incense, with the prayers of the saints, rose before God from the hand of the angel," and then the angel throws fire from the altar on the earth (8:4–5). The saints' prayers are part of how God acts in history.

**2. "They sang a new song."** The Old Testament repeatedly calls for a "new song" (Ps 33:3; 96:1; Isa 42:10), usually for a new act of salvation. This new song is sung for the redemption the Lamb has accomplished.

**3. "For you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people."** "Worthy are you to take the scroll and to open its seals, for you were slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation" (5:9).
- **The reason he is worthy:** the Lamb is worthy to take the scroll not because of his power but because he was slain. The cross is his qualification.
- **"Ransomed":** this word (*agorazō*) originally meant buying in the marketplace. People were held in bondage by sin, and the Lamb paid the price with his own blood to buy them back for God. The Lord Jesus himself said he came "to give his life as a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45). Revelation's opening likewise says he "has freed us from our sins by his blood" (1:5). He died in our place and bore the penalty of our sins, so that we might belong to God.
- **"From every tribe and language and people and nation":** this fourfold combination appears seven times in Revelation, as Bauckham notes (e.g. 7:9; 13:7), with Daniel in the background: "that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him" (Dan 7:14). Those the Lamb ransomed do not belong to one nation alone; the gospel is no foreign religion, nor any one people's.
- **A textual variant:** the KJV reads "hast redeemed us," and in the next verse "hast made us... and we shall reign." In 5:9 many manuscripts, including Sinaiticus, do have "us"; Alexandrinus has no object, and most modern critical texts follow that shorter reading because "them" in 5:10 is strongly attested. The "us/we" of 5:10 comes from late manuscripts. The word "people" in the ESV, and its counterpart in the CUV, is supplied by the translators. Some use the "us" reading to argue that the twenty-four elders are redeemed human beings, but the variant by itself cannot settle who the elders are.

**4. "A kingdom and priests to our God."** "And you have made them a kingdom and priests to our God, and they shall reign on the earth" (5:10). At Sinai God said to Israel, "you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" (Exod 19:6). That calling is now fulfilled in those the Lamb has ransomed (cf. 1:6; 1 Pet 2:9).
- **Priests:** every redeemed person may come directly before God, interceding for the world and making God known to it.
- **"They shall reign on the earth":** some manuscripts have a present tense, others a future; modern critical texts and the ESV follow the future. Many interpreters see truth in both ideas: believers have already been made a kingdom (1:6), and will "reign forever and ever" in the new creation (22:5). "On the earth" also reminds us that God's redemption does not take people out of the created world but renews it.

> The point: The Lamb is worthy of everything because he was slain. His blood not only forgives sins; it buys back a people and makes them priests and a kingdom to God.

## Going Deeper: 5:11–14 To Him Who Sits on the Throne and to the Lamb

**1. Myriads of angels.** "Then I looked, and I heard around the throne and the living creatures and the elders the voice of many angels, numbering myriads of myriads and thousands of thousands" (5:11). Daniel too saw before the Ancient of Days that "a thousand thousands served him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him" (Dan 7:10). Here they raise their voices together to the Lamb.

**2. Sevenfold praise.** "Saying with a loud voice, 'Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honor and glory and blessing!'" (5:12). Seven again is the number of completeness. The angels call him "the Lamb who was slain": heaven's praise does not avoid his death; it names him by it. In the world's eyes crucifixion was shame and weakness; in heaven it is the reason he is worthy of all (cf. 1 Cor 1:23–24).

**3. Every creature.** "And I heard every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea, and all that is in them, saying, 'To him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be blessing and honor and glory and might forever and ever!'" (5:13). Earlier no one in heaven or on earth or under the earth was able or worthy to open the scroll (5:3–4); now everything created in heaven, on earth and under the earth worships. Paul too says "that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth" (Phil 2:10), echoing Isaiah, where God says, "To me every knee shall bow" (Isa 45:23).

**4. The Lamb worshiped with the One on the throne.** This is the chapter's most important Christological point.
- **Revelation's strict monotheism:** twice John falls down to worship an angel, and twice he is told, "You must not do that!... Worship God" (19:10; 22:9). God himself says, "my glory I give to no other" (Isa 42:8): not to any other at all. In this book, worship belongs to God alone.
- **Yet the Lamb receives the same worship:** the living creatures and elders "fell down before the Lamb" (5:8), just as they fell down before the One on the throne in chapter 4 (4:10); the same doxology is given together "to him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb" (5:13). Many scholars (e.g. Bauckham) conclude that John includes Jesus within the identity of the one true God, not as a second god or a lesser, created god. This agrees with what the Lord Jesus said: "that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father" (John 5:23).
- **Distinct yet one:** the Lamb is distinct from the One on the throne; he takes the scroll from his hand (5:7). Yet at the end of the book the city has "the throne of God and of the Lamb," and "his servants will worship him" (22:3), with the singular "him." Revelation does not use the later language of the Nicene Creed, yet it supplies the ground for what the church came to confess: the Son shares the Father's worship and glory and is of one substance with him; and God is one.
- **What it means today:** some groups (e.g. Jehovah's Witnesses) hold that Jesus is a created being who should not be worshiped. Revelation 5 shows all of heaven worshiping him. When we pray to Jesus and sing to him, we do not take glory away from the Father; we honor the Son as the Father wills.

**5. "Amen" and falling down.** "And the four living creatures said, 'Amen!' and the elders fell down and worshiped" (5:14). The last response of worship is not more words, but to say, "Yes, so it is," and fall down before him.

> The point: Heaven's worship of the Lamb is the same as its worship of the One on the throne. The slain Lamb is not another god beside God, but the Son worshiped together with the Father.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And one of the elders said to me, 'Weep no more; behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered'... And... I saw a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain" (5:5–6). For several years a small church has lived under pressure. It has moved its meeting place three times; several young families have moved away; some members are shut out at work because of their faith; the preacher carries everything alone and is so worn out that he can barely speak with any strength. At every prayer meeting the brothers and sisters ask for the same thing: Lord, act in power, let those who trouble the church be repaid, throw every door open at once, let everyone see that God is alive. What they are asking for is a lion. But year after year passes, and nothing is overturned. One evening, after the prayer meeting, an elder sits in the half-empty living room and weeps as John did: it seems no one can open this scroll, as if God's hand has stopped.

The elder in heaven said to John, "Weep no more; behold, the Lion." John turned and saw a Lamb, as though it had been slain. God did not deceive John: this Lamb truly is the Lion; he has seven horns, the whole scroll of history is in his hand, and when the day comes he will judge and every wrong on earth will be set right (6:16–17). But he "has conquered" (5:5) at the cross; heaven calls him worthy of everything because "you were slain" (5:9). His kingdom did not begin by striking his enemies down but by shedding his blood for his enemies; and the saints who conquer the accuser conquer "by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" (12:11).

This does not mean we should not ask God to act. The prayers of the saints in Revelation are gathered in golden bowls before the throne (5:8), and not one word falls to the ground in vain. Nor does it mean a church under pressure has failed. Yet in these years we may have done more than weep: some have complained that the preacher is not strong enough, some have quietly worked out when they could leave, and what some longed for was less the kingdom of God than the downfall of the other side. The blood this Lamb shed was shed for people like that; we can confess these things before him and stop defending ourselves. his worthiness is that he was slain to ransom us for God and make us a kingdom and priests (5:9–10).

We can still gather every week and pray for our city, for those who trouble the church, and for ourselves; we can hold up the exhausted preacher together. In that half-empty living room we can also join the myriads of heaven and sing, "Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honor and glory and blessing!" (5:12). That song does not have to wait until things turn around. We do not know how this scroll will unfold next. But we know who holds it: not a God far from suffering, but the Lamb who was slain for us. He has not been struck down; he still bears the marks of his slaying, and he stands, at the very center of the throne.
