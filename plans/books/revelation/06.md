## 本章脉络

第5章末了，被杀的羔羊从坐宝座的右手中拿了书卷；第6章，他一个一个地揭开七印。本章记的是前六印，第七印要等到8:1才揭开。全章可以分成三段：
- **6:1-8，四匹马：** 前四印揭开的时候，四活物各说一声「你来！」，就有白马、红马、黑马、灰色马先后出来，带来争战、流血、饥荒和死亡。
- **6:9-11，祭坛底下的灵魂：** 第五印揭开，约翰看见为神的道被杀之人的灵魂，向神呼求伸冤；他们得着白衣，又被吩咐「还要安息片时」。
- **6:12-17，忿怒的大日：** 第六印揭开，地大震动，天象崩坏；地上从君王到奴仆的人都藏起来，喊着说：「谁能站得住呢？」

这一章最要紧的一件事，是谁在揭开这些印。不是撒但，不是罗马，也不是偶然的命运，而是「羔羊」（6:1）。每一匹马出来以前，都有宝座前的活物发出命令；骑马的能做什么，经文一再说是「赐给他」的（6:2、4、8）。地上的灾难看起来乱成一片，其实没有一样在羔羊的手以外。

**七印和后面的七号、七碗是什么关系？** 导读已经简单介绍过这个问题，这里只就七印说明一次，后面的七号和七碗会再回到这里。
- **重述的读法：** 三世纪的维克多利努、二十世纪的威廉·亨德里克森，以及毕尔等学者认为，七印描写的是从基督升天到他再来的整个时期；第六印已经到了末日（6:12-17）。后面的七号、七碗再从不同的角度、一次比一次强烈地重述这同一段时期。他们指出，第六印、第七号、第七碗都好像到了终局（11:15-18；16:17-21）。
- **先后发生、层层套叠的读法：** 时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，七印是将来七年大灾难的前一段，发生在教会被提以后；第七印揭开的时候，里面包含了七号，第七号又包含了七碗，所以三组「七」是一组接一组、一层套一层的。
- **折中的读法：** 乔治·赖德、格兰特·奥斯本等学者大致认为，三组「七」既有先后的推进，也有重叠；七印可以说是主耶稣在橄榄山上所说「灾难的起头」（太24:8），七号、七碗则更直接地指向末了。
无论采用哪一种读法，都不会改变本章的重心：书卷在羔羊手中，历史的每一页都是他揭开的。

## 深读 6:1-2 白马和骑马的

**1. 「你来！」** 「我看见羔羊揭开七印中第一印的时候，就听见四活物中的一个活物，声音如雷，说：『你来！』」（6:1）四匹马的画面来自撒迦利亚书：先知在夜间看见红马、黄马和白马（亚1:8），又看见四辆车套着红马、黑马、白马、有斑点的马，天使说：「这是天的四风，是从普天下的主面前出来的。」（亚6:5）无论在撒迦利亚书还是在这里，这些马都是神从他面前差遣出来的。

**2. 「胜了又要胜」。** 「我就观看，见有一匹白马；骑在马上的，拿着弓，并有冠冕赐给他。他便出来，胜了又要胜。」（6:2）这位骑白马的是谁，释经者有几种主要的看法：
- **基督，或是福音的得胜：** 爱任纽似乎已经把「胜了又要胜」用在基督身上（《驳异端》第四卷第二十一章第三节），威廉·亨德里克森也认为这是得胜的基督；维克多利努和历史前千禧年派的乔治·赖德，则认为这是福音的传扬。他们的理由是：在启示录里，白色常常和圣洁、得胜相连（3:4-5）；第19章那位骑白马的明明是基督（19:11）；「得胜」这个词也用在羔羊身上（5:5）；主耶稣也说过，「福音必须先传给万民」（可13:10）。
- **征服的势力，甚至是假冒基督的敌基督：** 约翰·华尔伏认为，这是将来那位要以和平为名来统治世界的人物；毕尔认为，这是撒但的势力假冒基督的样子出来争战。他们的理由是：四个骑马的是一组，后面三个都带来灾害，第一个也应当如此；这里的骑马者拿的是弓，第19章的基督口中出来的却是利剑（19:15）。那一位头上戴着「许多冠冕」（19:12），这里却只「有冠冕赐给他」。此外，主耶稣在橄榄山上讲末世，也是先说「将来有好些人冒我的名来」，然后才说打仗、饥荒、地震（太24:5-7）。
- **一世纪的军事侵略：** 也有学者认为，最初的读者会想起罗马东边以骑射闻名的帕提亚人。
这几种看法也有相通的地方。许多学者虽然认为第一位骑马者不是基督，也承认约翰是刻意把他写得像基督，因为邪恶的势力常常冒充真的那一位。

**3. 「赐给他」。** 无论骑白马的是谁，经文都说冠冕是「赐给他」的。启示录常用这样被动的语气，表示真正掌权的是神：第二位骑马的「有权柄给了」他（6:4），连后面的兽也是「任凭它」与圣徒争战（13:7）。邪恶有真实的力量，却没有最后的权柄。

> 要点：第一印揭开时，地上看见的是一个出来争战得胜的骑马者；天上看见的，却是羔羊揭开了印。无论他是谁，他的冠冕都是领受来的。

## 深读 6:3-8 红马、黑马、灰色马

**1. 红马：夺去太平。** 「就另有一匹马出来，是红的，有权柄给了那骑马的，可以从地上夺去太平，使人彼此相杀；又有一把大刀赐给他。」（6:4）不少释经者注意到，「杀」这个词，和5:6羔羊「像是被杀过的」那个「杀」是同一个词；第五印的殉道者，也是这样「被杀」的（6:9）。红马所带来的流血，有时也临到神的百姓身上。

**2. 黑马：饥荒和物价。** 「我听见在四活物中似乎有声音说：『一钱银子买一升麦子，一钱银子买三升大麦；油和酒不可糟蹋。』」（6:6）「一钱银子」（*denarion*）是当时工人一天的工钱（太20:2）；这里所说的「一升」，大约是一个人一天的口粮。做一整天的工，只够自己一天的麦子，或三份便宜的大麦，家人就要挨饿。这是饥荒的物价。「油和酒不可糟蹋」有几种解释：橄榄树和葡萄树根深，不像麦子那样容易受旱灾影响；也有人认为这是讽刺，穷人买不起面包，富人的油和酒却照样有。无论哪一种，这都是一场有限度的饥荒，神为它定了界限。

**3. 灰色马：死亡和阴府。** 「我就观看，见有一匹灰色马；骑在马上的，名字叫作死，阴府也随着他；有权柄赐给他们，可以用刀剑、饥荒、瘟疫、野兽，杀害地上四分之一的人。」（6:8）「灰色」在原文是青灰、发绿的颜色，就是尸体的颜色。「刀剑、饥荒、瘟疫、野兽」正是神从前藉以西结所说的「四样大灾」（结14:21）。这里说「四分之一」，到了七号的时候是「三分之一」（8:7-12），到了七碗就没有再说分数了；审判一次比一次重，但在这里仍然是有限的。

**4. 和主耶稣的末世讲论。** 许多释经者注意到，前五印的次序和主耶稣在橄榄山上的讲论很接近：假基督、争战、饥荒、瘟疫、逼迫，然后是天象的震动（太24:4-29；路21:10-11）。主耶稣说：「这些事是必须有的，只是末期还没有到」，「这都是灾难的起头」（太24:6、8）。这几匹马所代表的事在每一个世代都可能出现；不要把新闻当作时间表，听见这些事，「总不要惊慌」（太24:6；关于定日期，见导读），也不要以为与自己无关。

> 要点：争战、饥荒和死亡是真实的，也是可怕的；但它们都是被差遣出来的，它们的权柄是领受来的，界限也是神定的。羔羊没有失去对历史的掌管。

## 深读 6:9-11 祭坛底下的灵魂

**1. 「在祭坛底下」。** 「揭开第五印的时候，我看见在祭坛底下，有为神的道、并为作见证被杀之人的灵魂」（6:9）。在旧约的献祭里，祭牲的血要「倒在……燔祭坛的脚那里」（利4:7）。约翰看见殉道者的灵魂在祭坛底下，这是说，他们的死在神眼中是献给他的祭。保罗也这样看自己的死：「我现在被浇奠，我离世的时候到了。」（提后4:6；参腓2:17）约翰自己也是为同样的缘故被放逐（1:9），别迦摩的安提帕已经这样死了（2:13）。

**2. 「要等到几时呢？」** 「大声喊着说：『圣洁真实的主啊，你不审判住在地上的人，给我们伸流血的冤，要等到几时呢？』」（6:10）他们称神为「主」，这个词（*despotēs*）指有绝对主权的主人。他们的呼求，不是为自己报私仇，而是求神显明他的圣洁和真实：如果神是圣洁的，流无辜人血的罪就不能一直不受审判。诗篇里也常有这样的呼求（诗79:10）。后来在巴比伦倾倒的时候，天上就宣告：神「向淫妇讨流仆人血的罪，给他们伸冤」（19:2）。

**3. 白衣和「安息片时」。** 「于是有白衣赐给他们各人；又有话对他们说，还要安息片时，等着一同作仆人的和他们的弟兄也像他们被杀，满足了数目。」（6:11）
- **白衣：** 在地上，他们被定罪、被处死；在天上，神先给他们穿上白衣，就是宣告他们是得胜的、清白的（3:4-5）。
- **安息：** 他们不是在焦虑中等候，而是在神面前安息。许多释经者也从这里看见，信徒死后、复活以前，灵魂在主那里，有知觉，也有安息。
- **「满足了数目」：** 这不是说神要凑够一个死亡的指标，而是说，连教会的苦难也在神的计划之中，有它的界限和日子。
- **只指殉道者吗？** 有些释经者认为是；另一些（例如毕尔）认为，他们代表所有为主作见证而受苦的信徒。无论如何，神没有忘记任何一个为他的名受苦的人。

> 要点：在地上，殉道者的血好像白流了；在天上，它被看作献在坛下的祭。神不一定立刻伸冤，但他听见了，他给他们白衣，也定了伸冤的日子。

## 深读 6:12-17 谁能站得住呢

**1. 天象的崩坏。** 「揭开第六印的时候，我又看见地大震动，日头变黑像毛布，满月变红像血，天上的星辰坠落于地，如同无花果树被大风摇动，落下未熟的果子一样。天就挪移，好像书卷被卷起来；山岭海岛都被挪移离开本位。」（6:12-14）这些画面几乎都来自旧约的先知（珥2:31；赛13:10；34:4）。释经者有不同的理解：
- **字面的宇宙灾变：** 许多未来派的学者认为，这是主再来以前真实的天象变化。
- **先知的画面：** 另一些学者指出，以赛亚书用同样的话描写巴比伦和以东的倾倒（赛13:10；34:4），表示人所倚靠的整个秩序都要崩塌。
两种看法都同意，地上的人在这里认出「忿怒的大日」已经临到（6:17）。重述的读法认为这就是最后的审判；先后发生的读法（例如约翰·华尔伏）则认为这是神忿怒的开始，七号、七碗还要接着倾倒。主耶稣也用同样的话说到他的再来（太24:29-30）。

**2. 从君王到奴仆。** 「地上的君王、臣宰、将军、富户、壮士，和一切为奴的、自主的，都藏在山洞和岩石穴里」（6:15）。约翰列了七类人，从最有权势的到最卑微的；到了那一天，他们一同躲藏，地位、财富、武力都保护不了他们。以赛亚早就说过，耶和华使地大震动的时候，「人就进入石洞，进入土穴，躲避耶和华的惊吓和他威严的荣光」（赛2:19）。

**3. 「把我们藏起来」。** 「向山和岩石说：『倒在我们身上吧！把我们藏起来，躲避坐宝座者的面目和羔羊的忿怒；因为他们忿怒的大日到了，谁能站得住呢？』」（6:16-17）这句话出自何西阿书：「他们必对大山说：遮盖我们！对小山说：倒在我们身上！」（何10:8）主耶稣走向十字架的路上，也对耶路撒冷的妇女引用过这句话（路23:30）。
- **他们怕的是「面目」：** 他们宁可被山压死，也不愿看见坐宝座者的面。这是从伊甸园就开始的事：亚当和他的妻子犯罪以后，「就藏在园里的树木中，躲避耶和华神的面」（创3:8）。到了启示录的末了，神的仆人「也要见他的面」（22:4）；同一个面，有人渴望看见，有人宁死也要躲开。
- **「羔羊的忿怒」：** 这是一个叫人吃惊的说法。羔羊本来是温柔、被杀的；可是正因为他是为罪人被杀的那一位，拒绝他的人最后要面对的，正是他。他的忿怒不是失控的脾气，而是圣洁的审判（14:10）。

**4. 「谁能站得住呢？」** 旧约里常常这样问：「你怒气一发，谁能在你面前站得住呢？」（诗76:7）「他发忿恨，谁能立得住呢？」（鸿1:6）「他显现的时候，谁能立得住呢？」（玛3:2）这个问题的答案，就在下一章。第七印没有马上揭开，约翰先看见神的仆人受印（7:3），又看见「许多的人，没有人能数过来」，「站在宝座和羔羊面前」（7:9）。第6章的人藏在山洞里，第7章的人站在宝座前。他们站得住，不是因为他们比别人强，而是因为他们「曾用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了」（7:14）。保罗也说，我们「靠着他的血称义，就更要藉着他免去神的忿怒」（罗5:9；参帖前1:10）。

> 要点：在那日，没有一座山能把人藏起来，也没有人能凭自己站在神面前。能站立的，只有那些投靠羔羊、藏身在他里面的人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「向山和岩石说：『倒在我们身上吧！把我们藏起来，躲避坐宝座者的面目和羔羊的忿怒；因为他们忿怒的大日到了，谁能站得住呢？』」（6:16-17）老冯每天早上七点出门，衬衫熨得平平整整，跟妻子说去公司。其实他那家小装修公司半年前就关了。他先是向亲戚借钱给工人结工钱，后来又在网上借贷，再后来想「翻本」，就开始赌。现在欠下的数目，他自己都不敢算。他每天把车开到河边，在车里一坐就是一天，到了傍晚再回家吃饭。妻子问起，他就说「还行，就是忙」。主日他照常坐在教会后排，散会第一个走，怕有人问他近况。他不是不知道该说，只是每多藏一天，就越不敢说。

人犯了罪，第一件事常常就是躲。亚当和他的妻子吃了那果子，「就藏在园里的树木中，躲避耶和华神的面」（创3:8）。第六印揭开的时候，从君王到奴仆，人人都在找山洞。树木藏不住亚当，山也藏不住君王。我们躲的不只是家人的眼光，更是神的面。我们各人也有自己的河边：一笔没告诉配偶的开销，一件瞒了父母多年的事，一个一再重犯、从来不敢说出口的罪。我在躲的是什么？我怕的是丢脸，还是神的面？可是在园中，神来寻找亚当的第一句话，不是宣判，而是一个问题：「你在哪里？」（创3:9）他还在找那个躲起来的人。

「谁能站得住呢？」答案是：凭自己，没有人能站得住，老冯不能，我们也不能。可是这一章叫人惧怕的那一位，正是第5章那「像是被杀过的」羔羊（5:6）。人要躲避的，是坐宝座者和羔羊的忿怒（6:16-17）；这位羔羊却已经在十字架上，替信靠他的人担当了神向罪所发的忿怒：「神使那无罪的，替我们成为罪，好叫我们在他里面成为神的义。」（林后5:21）山和岩石藏不住任何人；能藏住罪人的，只有羔羊自己。投靠他，不是再一次逃跑，而是走出来，到光中认罪悔改，对他说：「我在这里。」

老冯要走出来，会很痛。他要向妻子说实话，最好是请一位可信的牧者或弟兄陪着；要停止赌博，寻求专业的戒赌辅导或互助小组；要和家人一起诚实地面对债务。如果有人上门威胁、暴力催债，应当报警。如果老冯已经说过一次「再也不赌了」，又跌倒了，羔羊的血并没有因此失效，他仍然可以今天再回来。如果你像他一样，坐在河边的时候曾经想过「不如一了百了」，「家里没有我反而好」，请今天就告诉一个人：你的配偶、牧者、一位可信的朋友，或者打当地的心理危机热线。债可以慢慢还，你的命不能拿来抵债；羔羊为你流了血，不是要你再拿自己的命去还。如果你是那位妻子，知道了真相以后，你不必替他隐瞒，也不必在压力下签字替他承担债务；如果有危险，先保护自己和孩子。他犯的错不是你的错，你也可以把你的愤怒和害怕带到主面前。

那一天，许多人要向山喊：「倒在我们身上吧！」主耶稣走向十字架的时候，在出城的路上就引用过这句话（路23:30）。他没有去找山洞，而是一直走到各各他，让本该落在我们身上的忿怒落在他自己身上。那位曾被杀的羔羊，从死里复活，现在站在宝座中间（5:6）。所以老冯不必再躲在河边的车里，不必躲在教会的后排，也不必躲在「还行，就是忙」这句话后面；我也不必再躲在我自己的那一句话后面。主啊，我从躲藏的地方出来；「你是我藏身之处」（诗32:7），求你作我惟一的藏身之处。

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 5 the slain Lamb took the scroll from the right hand of the One on the throne; in chapter 6 he breaks its seals one by one. This chapter opens the first six seals; the seventh waits until 8:1. The chapter falls into three parts:
- **6:1–8, four horses:** as each of the first four seals is opened, one of the living creatures says, "Come!", and a white, a bright red, a black and a pale horse come out in turn, bringing conquest, bloodshed, famine and death.
- **6:9–11, the souls under the altar:** at the fifth seal John sees the souls of those slain for the word of God crying out to God for justice; they are given white robes and told to "rest a little longer."
- **6:12–17, the great day of wrath:** at the sixth seal there is a great earthquake and the heavens come apart; everyone on earth, from kings to slaves, hides and cries out, "Who can stand?"

The most important thing in this chapter is who is opening the seals. Not Satan, not Rome, not blind fate, but "the Lamb" (6:1). Before each horse comes out, one of the living creatures before the throne gives the command; what each rider can do, the text says again and again, "was given" to him (6:2, 4, 8). Disaster on earth looks like chaos, yet none of it lies outside the Lamb's hand.

**How do the seals relate to the trumpets and bowls that follow?** The introduction raised this question briefly. It is set out once here for the seals, and the trumpet and bowl chapters will point back.
- **A recapitulation reading:** Victorinus of Pettau in the third century, William Hendriksen in the twentieth, and scholars such as Beale take the seven seals to describe the whole period from Christ's ascension to his return, with the sixth seal already reaching the last day (6:12–17). The trumpets and bowls then retell the same period from other angles and with growing intensity. They note that the sixth seal, the seventh trumpet and the seventh bowl each seem to bring us to the end (11:15–18; 16:17–21).
- **A sequential, telescoping reading:** dispensational scholars (e.g. John Walvoord) take the seals as the first part of a future seven-year tribulation that follows the rapture of the church. When the seventh seal is opened it contains the seven trumpets, and the seventh trumpet contains the seven bowls, so the three series follow one another, each nested in the last.
- **Between the two:** scholars such as George Eldon Ladd and Grant Osborne broadly see both forward movement and overlap among the three series; the seals can be read as what Jesus on the Mount of Olives called "the beginning of the birth pains" (Matt 24:8), while the trumpets and bowls point more directly to the end.
Whichever reading we take, the center of the chapter does not change: the scroll is in the Lamb's hand, and he is the one who opens every page of history.

## Going Deeper: 6:1–2 The White Horse and Its Rider

**1. "Come!"** "Now I watched when the Lamb opened one of the seven seals, and I heard one of the four living creatures say with a voice like thunder, 'Come!'" (6:1). The picture of four horses comes from Zechariah: the prophet saw in the night "a man riding on a red horse... and behind him were red, sorrel, and white horses" (Zech 1:8), and later four chariots drawn by red, black, white and dappled horses, which the angel explained: "These are going out to the four winds of heaven, after presenting themselves before the LORD of all the earth" (Zech 6:5). There and here alike, the horses are sent out from God's presence.

**2. "Conquering, and to conquer."** "And I looked, and behold, a white horse! And its rider had a bow, and a crown was given to him, and he came out conquering, and to conquer" (6:2). Interpreters hold several main views of this rider:
- **Christ, or the victory of the gospel:** Irenaeus seems already to apply "conquering, and to conquer" to Christ (*Against Heresies* 4.21.3), and William Hendriksen takes the rider as the conquering Christ; Victorinus and the historic premillennialist George Eldon Ladd take it as the preaching of the gospel. They argue that white in Revelation is tied to holiness and victory (3:4–5); the rider on the white horse in chapter 19 is plainly Christ (19:11); the verb "conquer" is used of the Lamb (5:5); and Jesus said, "the gospel must first be proclaimed to all nations" (Mark 13:10).
- **A conquering power, even a counterfeit Christ:** John Walvoord takes the rider as the future figure who will rule the world in the name of peace; Beale takes him as satanic forces going out to war in an imitation of Christ. They argue that the four riders form a set, and since the other three bring harm, so does the first; this rider carries a bow, while Christ in chapter 19 has a sharp sword coming from his mouth (19:15) and "many diadems" on his head (19:12), whereas here "a crown was given to him"; and Jesus' teaching on the Mount of Olives begins with "many will come in my name" before it speaks of wars, famines and earthquakes (Matt 24:5–7).
- **Military invasion in the first century:** some scholars think the first readers would have recalled the Parthians, the famous mounted archers on Rome's eastern frontier.
These views can partly be held together: many who deny that the first rider is Christ grant that he is deliberately drawn to look like Christ, because evil so often imitates the real thing.

**3. "Was given."** Whoever the rider is, the text says the crown "was given" to him. Revelation often uses the passive this way to show that God is the one who truly rules: the second rider "was permitted" (6:4), and even the beast later "was allowed to make war on the saints" (13:7). Evil has real power, but not the final authority.

> The point: When the first seal is opened, earth sees a rider going out to conquer; heaven sees the Lamb breaking the seal. Whoever he is, his crown was given to him.

## Going Deeper: 6:3–8 The Red, Black and Pale Horses

**1. The red horse: peace taken away.** "And out came another horse, bright red. Its rider was permitted to take peace from the earth, so that men should slay one another, and he was given a great sword" (6:4). A number of interpreters note that the word "slay" is the same as in "the Lamb... slain" (5:6), and that the martyrs of the fifth seal were "slain" in the same way (6:9). The bloodshed the red horse brings sometimes falls on God's people too.

**2. The black horse: famine and prices.** "And I heard what seemed to be a voice in the midst of the four living creatures, saying, 'A quart of wheat for a denarius, and three quarts of barley for a denarius, and do not harm the oil and wine!'" (6:6). A denarius was a laborer's wage for a day (Matt 20:2); the measure translated "a quart" was about one person's daily ration. A full day's work buys wheat for one person for one day, or three portions of cheaper barley, and the family goes hungry. These are famine prices. "Do not harm the oil and wine" is explained in several ways: olive trees and vines have deep roots and suffer from drought less quickly than grain; or, some suggest, it is an irony, with bread beyond the poor while the oil and wine of the better-off remain. Either way, this is a limited famine, with bounds set by God.

**3. The pale horse: Death and Hades.** "And I looked, and behold, a pale horse! And its rider's name was Death, and Hades followed him. And they were given authority over a fourth of the earth, to kill with sword and with famine and with pestilence and by wild beasts of the earth" (6:8). The word "pale" means a greenish, ashen color, the color of a corpse. "Sword, famine, wild beasts, and pestilence" are the "four disastrous acts of judgment" God named through Ezekiel (Ezek 14:21). Here the harm reaches "a fourth"; under the trumpets it is "a third" (8:7–12), and under the bowls no fraction is given. Judgment grows heavier each time, but here it is still limited.

**4. Jesus' teaching on the end.** Many interpreters note how closely the first five seals follow Jesus' teaching on the Mount of Olives: false christs, wars, famines, pestilences, persecution, and then the shaking of the heavens (Matt 24:4–29; Luke 21:10–11). Jesus said, "this must take place, but the end is not yet... All these are but the beginning of the birth pains" (Matt 24:6, 8). What these horses stand for can appear in any generation; do not turn the news into a timetable, for "see that you are not alarmed" (Matt 24:6; on date-setting, see the introduction), and do not think it has nothing to do with us.

> The point: War, famine and death are real and dreadful; but each is sent out, given its authority and given its limits. The Lamb has not lost his hold on history.

## Going Deeper: 6:9–11 The Souls Under the Altar

**1. "Under the altar."** "When he opened the fifth seal, I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slain for the word of God and for the witness they had borne" (6:9). In the Old Testament sacrifices, the blood of the offering was poured out "at the base of the altar of burnt offering" (Lev 4:7). John sees the souls of the martyrs under the altar: their deaths are seen as an offering to God. Paul saw his own death this way: "For I am already being poured out as a drink offering, and the time of my departure has come" (2 Tim 4:6; cf. Phil 2:17). John himself was exiled for the same reason (1:9), and Antipas in Pergamum had already died this way (2:13).

**2. "How long?"** "They cried out with a loud voice, 'O Sovereign Lord, holy and true, how long before you will judge and avenge our blood on those who dwell on the earth?'" (6:10). The word for "Sovereign Lord" (*despotēs*) means a master with absolute authority. Their cry is not private revenge but a plea that God show himself holy and true: if God is holy, the shedding of innocent blood cannot go unjudged forever. The Psalms often cry the same way (Ps 79:10). Later, when Babylon falls, heaven announces that God "has avenged on her the blood of his servants" (19:2).

**3. White robes and "a little longer."** "Then they were each given a white robe and told to rest a little longer, until the number of their fellow servants and their brothers should be complete, who were to be killed as they themselves had been" (6:11).
- **The white robe:** on earth they were condemned and put to death; in heaven God first clothes them in white, declaring them victorious and clean (3:4–5).
- **Rest:** they are not waiting in anxiety but resting before God. Many interpreters also see here that between death and resurrection believers' souls are with the Lord, conscious and at rest.
- **"Until the number... should be complete":** this does not mean God must fill a quota of deaths, but that even the church's suffering lies within God's plan, with its limits and its appointed day.
- **Martyrs only?** Some interpreters think so; others (e.g. Beale) take them to represent all believers who suffer for their witness to Christ. Either way, God has not forgotten one person who suffered for his name.

> The point: On earth the martyrs' blood seems wasted; in heaven it is counted as an offering beneath the altar. God may not avenge at once, but he has heard; he gives them white robes and has set the day of justice.

## Going Deeper: 6:12–17 Who Can Stand?

**1. The heavens come apart.** "When he opened the sixth seal, I looked, and behold, there was a great earthquake, and the sun became black as sackcloth, the full moon became like blood, and the stars of the sky fell to the earth as the fig tree sheds its winter fruit when shaken by a gale. The sky vanished like a scroll that is being rolled up, and every mountain and island was removed from its place" (6:12–14). Almost every picture comes from the Old Testament prophets (Joel 2:31; Isa 13:10; 34:4). Interpreters read this in different ways:
- **Literal cosmic catastrophe:** many futurist scholars take this as real changes in the heavens before the Lord returns.
- **Prophetic imagery:** others point out that Isaiah uses the same language for the fall of Babylon and of Edom (Isa 13:10; 34:4), meaning that the whole order people rely on will collapse.
Both views agree that people on earth here recognize that "the great day of their wrath has come" (6:17). Recapitulation readers take this as the last judgment itself; sequential readers (e.g. John Walvoord) take it as the onset of God's wrath, which the trumpets and bowls go on to pour out. Jesus used the same words of his own coming (Matt 24:29–30).

**2. From kings to slaves.** "Then the kings of the earth and the great ones and the generals and the rich and the powerful, and everyone, slave and free, hid themselves in the caves and among the rocks of the mountains" (6:15). John lists seven kinds of people, from the most powerful to the lowest; on that day they hide together, and rank, wealth and force cannot protect them. Isaiah had said that when the LORD rises to terrify the earth, "people shall enter the caves of the rocks and the holes of the ground, from before the terror of the LORD, and from the splendor of his majesty" (Isa 2:19).

**3. "Hide us."** "Calling to the mountains and rocks, 'Fall on us and hide us from the face of him who is seated on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb, for the great day of their wrath has come, and who can stand?'" (6:16–17). The words come from Hosea: "they shall say to the mountains, Cover us, and to the hills, Fall on us" (Hos 10:8). On his way to the cross Jesus quoted them to the women of Jerusalem (Luke 23:30).
- **What they fear is a face:** they would rather be crushed by mountains than see the face of the One on the throne. It began in Eden: after Adam and his wife sinned, they "hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God among the trees of the garden" (Gen 3:8). At the end of the book God's servants "will see his face" (22:4). The same face that some long to see, others would die to escape.
- **"The wrath of the Lamb":** a startling phrase. A lamb is gentle, and this Lamb was slain; yet precisely because he is the one slain for sinners, those who refuse him will at last have to face him. His wrath is not a loss of temper but holy judgment (14:10).

**4. "Who can stand?"** The Old Testament often asks this: "Who can stand before you when once your anger is roused?" (Ps 76:7); "Who can stand before his indignation?" (Nah 1:6); "who can stand when he appears?" (Mal 3:2). The answer comes in the next chapter. The seventh seal is not opened at once; first John sees God's servants sealed (7:3) and then "a great multitude that no one could number... standing before the throne and before the Lamb" (7:9). In chapter 6 people hide in caves; in chapter 7 people stand before the throne. They stand not because they are stronger than others, but because "they have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14). Paul says the same: "Since, therefore, we have now been justified by his blood, much more shall we be saved by him from the wrath of God" (Rom 5:9; cf. 1 Thess 1:10).

> The point: On that day no mountain can hide anyone, and no one can stand before God on his own. The only ones who stand are those who take refuge in the Lamb and are hidden in him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Calling to the mountains and rocks, 'Fall on us and hide us from the face of him who is seated on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb, for the great day of their wrath has come, and who can stand?'" (6:16–17). Every morning at seven Lao Feng leaves home in a neatly ironed shirt and tells his wife he is going to the office. In fact his small renovation company closed six months ago. First he borrowed from relatives to pay the workers what he owed them; then he took loans online; then, hoping to win it all back, he began to gamble. He no longer dares to add up what he owes. Each day he drives to the riverside and sits in the car until evening, then goes home for dinner. When his wife asks, he says, "It's fine, just busy." On Sundays he still sits in the back row at church and is the first to leave, in case someone asks how he is. It is not that he does not know he should tell her. It is that every day he hides makes it harder to speak.

The first thing we do after sinning is often to hide. When Adam and his wife had eaten the fruit, they "hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God among the trees of the garden" (Gen 3:8). When the sixth seal is opened, everyone from kings to slaves is looking for a cave. But the trees could not hide Adam, and the mountains cannot hide the kings. What we hide from is not only our family's eyes; it is the face of God. Each of us has a riverside of our own: money spent that a spouse was never told about, something kept from our parents for years, a sin repeated again and again and never once said aloud. What am I hiding? And what do I fear more: losing face, or the face of God? Yet in the garden, the first thing God said as he came looking for Adam was not a sentence but a question: "Where are you?" (Gen 3:9). He was still seeking the one who had hidden.

"Who can stand?" The answer is: on his own, no one. Not Lao Feng, and not us. But the One whose wrath this chapter dreads is the Lamb of chapter 5, "standing, as though it had been slain" (5:6). What people flee is the wrath of the One on the throne and of the Lamb (6:16–17); yet this Lamb has already borne, on the cross, God's wrath against sin for all who trust him: "For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God" (2 Cor 5:21). The mountains and rocks cannot hide anyone; the only one who can hide a sinner is the Lamb himself. Taking refuge in him is not one more escape; it is coming out into the light, confessing and repenting, and saying to him, "Here I am."

For Lao Feng, coming out will hurt. He needs to tell his wife the truth, preferably with a trusted pastor or brother present; to stop gambling and seek professional help or a recovery group; and to face the debts honestly together with his family. If anyone comes to the door with threats or violent debt collection, he should call the police. If Lao Feng has already said once, "I will never gamble again," and fallen, the Lamb's blood has not lost its power; he can still come back today. If, like him, you have sat by the river and thought, "Better to end it all," or "My family would be better off without me," tell someone today: your spouse, a pastor, a trusted friend, or a local crisis line. Debts can be repaid slowly; your life is not payment for them. The Lamb shed his blood for you; he does not ask you to pay with your own. If you are the wife who has found out, you do not have to keep his secret, and you do not have to sign for his debts under pressure; if there is danger, protect yourself and the children first. His wrong is not your fault, and you too can bring your anger and fear to the Lord.

On that day many will cry to the mountains, "Fall on us." Jesus had already quoted those words on the road out of the city with his cross (Luke 23:30). He did not look for a cave; he walked on to Golgotha and let the wrath that should have fallen on us fall on himself. The Lamb who was slain rose from the dead and now stands at the center of the throne (5:6). So Lao Feng no longer needs to hide in a car by the river, or in the back row of the church, or behind "It's fine, just busy"; and I no longer need to hide behind my own version of that sentence. Lord, I come out from where I have been hiding; "You are a hiding place for me" (Ps 32:7); be my only hiding place.
