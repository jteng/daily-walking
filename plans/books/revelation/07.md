## 本章脉络

第6章末了问：「谁能站得住呢？」（6:17）第七印没有马上揭开，约翰先看见了两个异象，作为这个问题的回答。全章可以分成四段：
- **7:1-3，地的四角和永生神的印：** 四位天使执掌地上的四风，暂时不叫它吹；另一位天使拿着永生神的印，要先印神众仆人的额。
- **7:4-8，十四万四千：** 约翰听见以色列各支派中受印的数目，每支派一万二千。
- **7:9-12，没有人能数过来的许多人：** 约翰转过来观看，见有从各国、各族、各民、各方来的许多人，身穿白衣，手拿棕树枝，站在宝座和羔羊面前，高呼「愿救恩归与……我们的神，也归与羔羊」。
- **7:13-17，这些穿白衣的是谁？** 长老解释：他们是从大患难中出来的，用羔羊的血洗白了衣裳；坐宝座的要用帐幕覆庇他们，羔羊要牧养他们，神要擦去他们一切的眼泪。

第6章的人躲在山洞里，第7章的人站在宝座前。在审判的风吹起以前，神先保护属他的人；在苦难还没有结束的时候，神先让我们看见苦难以后的结局。这一章像是七印中间的一段停顿，好叫正在受苦的教会知道：自己是谁的人，将来要站在哪里。

## 深读 7:1-3 永生神的印

**1. 被拦住的风。** 「此后，我看见四位天使站在地的四角，执掌地上四方的风，叫风不吹在地上、海上，和树上。」（7:1）「地的四角」「四方的风」，都是说全地。在旧约里，四风常常代表神的审判（耶49:36）；撒迦利亚书的四辆车也是「天的四风」（亚6:5；原文也可译作往天的四方去），所以许多释经者认为，这四风和第6章四匹马所带来的灾难有关。这里的重点是：风被拦住了。审判何时开始，不由风决定，也不由执掌风的天使决定，而是由神决定。

**2. 「等我们印了我们神众仆人的额」。** 「我又看见另有一位天使，从日出之地上来，拿着永生神的印。他就向那得着权柄能伤害地和海的四位天使大声喊着说：『地与海并树木，你们不可伤害，等我们印了我们神众仆人的额。』」（7:2-3）这个画面来自以西结书。耶路撒冷受审判以前，神吩咐一个人，在「那些因城中所行可憎之事叹息哀哭的人」额上画记号，又吩咐执行审判的，「凡有记号的人不要挨近他」（结9:4、6）。古时的印表示归属和保护。

**3. 受印是什么意思？**
- **属于神：** 后来约翰看见，跟随羔羊的人额上「有他的名和他父的名」（14:1）；到了新天新地，「他的名字必写在他们的额上」（22:4）。神的印就是神的名，表示这些人是属他的。
- **免受神的忿怒，不是免受一切苦难：** 第五印的殉道者已经被杀，他们的弟兄还要「像他们被杀」（6:11）；兽也要「与圣徒争战」（13:7）。可是到了第五号，蝗虫「惟独要伤害额上没有神印记的人」（9:4）。神的印保护他们不受神忿怒的伤害，也保守他们在逼迫中不至于离弃他。
- **新约的印：** 保罗说，信徒「受了所应许的圣灵为印记」（弗1:13；参林后1:22）。许多释经者认为，启示录的印和保罗所说的是同一件事，只是用异象的语言来说：神把自己的灵赐给他的百姓，作他们属他的记号。
- **兽的印记：** 兽的印记（13:16）是在模仿神的印；第13章会再讨论。

> 要点：在审判的风吹起以前，神先在属他的人身上盖了印。受印的人仍会经历苦难，但他们是属神的，没有任何力量能把他们从神手中夺去。

## 深读 7:4-8 十四万四千

**1. 一份奇特的名单。** 「我听见以色列人各支派中受印的数目有十四万四千。」（7:4）接下来约翰列出十二个支派，每支派一万二千（7:5-8）。这份名单有几个不寻常的地方：
- **不寻常的次序：** 旧约的名单通常以长子吕便开头，这里却是犹大，很可能因为「犹大支派中的狮子」（5:5）是从犹大出来的；不分地业、常常不算在名单中的利未算进去了，约瑟代替了以法莲。
- **没有但：** 但支派不在名单里。爱任纽认为，这是因为敌基督要从但出来（《驳异端》第五卷第三十章第二节）；也有人想到但支派早年拜偶像，后来又有金牛犊安在但（士18:30；王上12:29）。这些都只是推测，经文本身没有说明。
- **像是一次点兵：** 旧约数点以色列人，是要数「能出去打仗的」（民1:3）。包衡等学者认为，这十四万四千是羔羊的军队；可是到了第14章，这支军队得胜的方式，是「羔羊无论往哪里去，他们都跟随他」（14:4）。

**2. 他们是谁？** 释经者有几种看法：
- **以色列民族中的信徒：** 时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，这是将来大灾难期间，从以色列各支派中归信基督的犹太人；这时教会已经被提，所以他们和7:9那从各国来的许多人是两群不同的人。他们的理由是：经文明明说「以色列人各支派」，又一一列出支派的名字；接着的许多人「从各国、各族」来，显然是另一群。持这种看法的人也常常把这里和罗马书11:26连在一起。
- **神全体的百姓，从两个角度看见：** 包衡、毕尔等学者认为，十四万四千和那许多人是同一群人。第5章说过，约翰有时先听见一样，转过来看见的却是另一样，而看见的解释了听见的：他听见「犹大支派中的狮子」，看见的是被杀的羔羊（5:5-6）；在这里，他「听见」十四万四千（7:4），「观看」却见有许多的人，没有人能数过来（7:9）。从旧约应许的角度看，他们是神完整的以色列（12×12×1000）；从应许成就的角度看，他们是从万国来、数不过来的人（创15:5）。新约有时把信基督的人，无论是犹太人还是外邦人，称为亚伯拉罕的后裔、真受割礼的人（罗2:28-29；加3:29；腓3:3）；许多释经者也这样理解加拉太书6:16的「神的以色列民」和雅各书1:1的「十二个支派」，不过这两处都有争议。新耶路撒冷的门上写着「以色列十二个支派的名字」，根基上写着「羔羊十二使徒的名字」（21:12、14）。持这种看法的人中，也有不少仍然相信，神对以色列民族还有将来的怜悯（罗11:26），只是不认为本章是在讲这件事。
- **犹太信徒中的余民：** 也有释经者认为，这是教会中信主的犹太人。过去派（例如肯尼斯·詹特里）认为，这是公元70年耶路撒冷被毁时蒙保守的犹太基督徒；另一些人则认为，这是末世以色列中归主的余民，但不必先有灾前被提。

**3. 耶和华见证人的说法。** 耶和华见证人教导说，只有十四万四千个「受膏」的人要上天与基督一同作王；其他的忠心者，就是那「许多的人」，要永远住在地上的乐园里。这种说法很难成立：
- **前后不一致：** 他们说「十四万四千」是字面的数目，「以色列各支派」却不是字面的以色列人；可是经文中这两样是连在一起的。
- **那许多人也在天上，最后只有一群人：** 那许多人「站在宝座和羔羊面前」（7:9），「昼夜在他殿中事奉他」（7:15）；「殿」这个词在启示录别处指的是天上的圣所（11:19；15:5）。到了新天新地，「他的仆人都要事奉他，也要见他的面」（22:3-4）。圣经没有把神的百姓永远分成两等人。
如果有耶和华见证人来到家门口，我们可以温和、尊重地和他们谈，最好先从耶稣是谁谈起（参第3、5章关于耶稣神性的讨论）。

> 要点：受印的人一个支派一个支派、一个一个都被数算过，神没有忘记属他的任何一个人。许多释经者认为，他对以色列的应许在羔羊里成就，结果是一群数不过来的人。

## 深读 7:9-12 没有人能数过来的许多人

**1. 「没有人能数过来」。** 「此后，我观看，见有许多的人，没有人能数过来，是从各国、各族、各民、各方来的，站在宝座和羔羊面前，身穿白衣，手拿棕树枝」（7:9）。
- **数不过来：** 神曾领亚伯拉罕到外边，说：「你向天观看，数算众星，能数得过来吗？……你的后裔将要如此。」（创15:5）这应许在这里成就了。
- **从各国、各族、各民、各方来：** 这四个词和5:9羔羊「用自己的血……买了人来」的那四个词一样。他们能来，是因为羔羊买了他们。
- **白衣和棕树枝：** 白衣是得胜和洁净（3:4-5；6:11）；棕树枝让人想起住棚节，以色列人要拿着树枝欢喜，记念神领他们出埃及、住在棚里（利23:40-43）；耶稣进耶路撒冷的时候，众人也「拿着棕树枝」出去迎接他（约12:13）。

**2. 「愿救恩归与……我们的神，也归与羔羊」。** 「大声喊着说：『愿救恩归与坐在宝座上我们的神，也归与羔羊！』」（7:10）他们没有夸自己终于熬过来了；他们站在那里，没有一样是自己的功劳。这句话也再一次把坐宝座的和羔羊放在一起（5:13），同样敬拜，同样称颂。

**3. 天使的「阿们」。** 「众天使都站在宝座和众长老并四活物的周围，在宝座前，面伏于地，敬拜神，说：阿们！颂赞、荣耀、智慧、感谢、尊贵、权柄、大力都归与我们的神，直到永永远远。阿们！」（7:11-12）天使用七样称颂回应蒙救赎之人的呼喊。

> 要点：神应许给亚伯拉罕的，是数不过来的后裔；羔羊用血买来的，是从各国来的人。他们一同承认：救恩是神的，也是羔羊的。

## 深读 7:13-17 这些穿白衣的是谁

**1. 「我主，你知道」。** 「长老中有一位问我说：『这些穿白衣的是谁？是从哪里来的？』我对他说：『我主，你知道。』」（7:13-14上）以西结被问「这些骸骨能复活吗」的时候，也这样回答：「主耶和华啊，你是知道的。」（结37:3；参亚4:5）约翰不猜，承认自己不知道；读启示录，这也是好的态度。

**2. 「从大患难中出来的」。** 「他向我说：『这些人是从大患难中出来的』」（7:14）。原文有冠词，就是那大患难。释经者主要有两种看法：
- **末后一段特定的时期：** 但以理书说到「有大艰难，从有国以来直到此时，没有这样的」（但12:1）；主耶稣也说：「那时必有大灾难……后来也必没有。」（太24:21）约翰·华尔伏等时代论学者认为，这是指将来七年的大灾难，这些人是在教会被提以后才信主、在大灾难中蒙拯救或殉道的人。许多历史前千禧年派和其他未来派学者也认为，这是主再来以前最后一段特别的苦难，但他们认为教会也要经过这段时期。过去派则认为这指公元66-70年的犹太战争（参太24:21）。
- **教会在整个时代所经历的患难：** 毕尔等学者认为，这里的「大患难」指的是从基督第一次来到他再来，教会在世上所受的一切患难，到了末了会更加剧烈。约翰说自己和读者「在耶稣的患难……里一同有分」（1:9），用的是同一个词，也带冠词；主耶稣也说：「在世上，你们有苦难」（约16:33）。
关于3:10「保守你免去你的试炼」和被提的问题，见第3章的讨论。无论采用哪一种看法，这些人都不是绕过了患难，而是从患难中出来的。

**3. 「用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了」。** 「曾用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了。」（7:14）血本来会把衣服染红，这里却把衣裳洗白了。他们的白衣不是自己织成的，而是在羔羊的血里洗净的。老底嘉的教会被劝「向我买……白衣穿上」（3:18）；在这里，许多人穿着它站在宝座前。

**4. 帐幕、牧人和泉源。** 「所以，他们在神宝座前，昼夜在他殿中事奉他。坐宝座的要用帐幕覆庇他们。他们不再饥，不再渴；日头和炎热也必不伤害他们。因为宝座中的羔羊必牧养他们，领他们到生命水的泉源；神也必擦去他们一切的眼泪。」（7:15-17）
- **用帐幕覆庇：** 许多释经者指出，这个动词原意是「支搭帐幕」，和「道成了肉身，住在我们中间」（约1:14）的「住」是同一个词，也和「神的帐幕在人间」（21:3）相连。神亲自作他们的遮蔽（赛4:6）。
- **不再饥渴，不受炎热：** 这几句出自以赛亚书：「不饥不渴，炎热和烈日必不伤害他们；因为怜恤他们的必引导他们，领他们到水泉旁边。」（赛49:10）以赛亚说的是神领被掳的人回家；约翰看见，这条回家的路最后通到宝座前。
- **羔羊作牧人：** 羔羊本来是被牧养的，这里却由羔羊作牧人。神曾应许：「我必立一牧人照管他们……就是我的仆人大卫。」（结34:23）主耶稣说：「我是好牧人；好牧人为羊舍命。」（约10:11）他能牧养我们，因为他曾为我们被杀。
- **擦去一切的眼泪：** 以赛亚说：「主耶和华必擦去各人脸上的眼泪」（赛25:8）；第21章会再一次这样应许（21:4）。
- **现在还是将来？** 已经离世的信徒现在就在宝座前，而这一切要在新天新地完全成就（21:3-4）。

> 要点：从大患难中出来的人，不是靠自己站在宝座前，而是被羔羊的血洗净。在那里，那位曾被杀的羔羊亲自作他们的牧人，神亲手擦去他们的眼泪。

## 默想

**默想：** 「因为宝座中的羔羊必牧养他们，领他们到生命水的泉源；神也必擦去他们一切的眼泪。」（7:17）老刘在南方的工地上干了二十年，每年只在春节回家一次。今年夏天，他在脚手架上中暑，摔了下来，再也没有醒过来。工友们把他的骨灰送回村里。他的母亲八十岁了，坐在门口一直问：「他是不是又出去打工了？」他的妻子一边办丧事，一边还要和包工头谈赔偿。他们上初中的儿子，一滴眼泪也没有流，只是把自己关在屋里。上个月父子俩通电话吵了一架，儿子说：「你从来没管过我。」那是他对父亲说的最后一句话。老刘是在工地的团契里信主的，工友们还记得他晚上唱诗的样子，嗓子总是哑的。

我们不必急着劝这一家人不要哭。约翰看见的那许多人，是「从大患难中出来的」（7:14），他们流过泪，所以神才要擦去他们的眼泪。启示录没有假装死亡不痛，也没有叫受苦的人笑着说「没关系」。为赔偿合理地争取，向劳动部门或法律援助求助，也不是不属灵；神是公义的神。

可是这一家人也可以听一听长老对约翰说的话。老刘一辈子在烈日底下干活，夏天的工地常常四十度；可是经上说，在宝座前的人「不再饥，不再渴；日头和炎热也必不伤害他们」（7:16）。他一辈子漂在外面，住在工棚里；坐宝座的却「要用帐幕覆庇他们」（7:15）。他一辈子没有机会好好休息；现在牧养他的，是宝座中的羔羊。这位牧人认识离家的人，因为他自己就曾离开父的荣耀，来到我们中间，住在我们中间（约1:14），最后在城外被杀。老刘能站在宝座前，不是因为他这一生够好，而是因为这位牧人为他舍了命。

那个儿子心里也许最难过的，是那最后一句话。他收不回来了。可是那位牧人也是为这个儿子舍命的。他可以把那句话带到羔羊面前，承认它，哭出来；凡信靠他的，他的罪羔羊已经担当了。如果他愿意，也可以找一位可信的大人陪他谈谈。那位八十岁的母亲每天还在门口等，她的眼泪，神也一滴一滴都记着：「求你把我眼泪装在你的皮袋里。这不都记在你册子上吗？」（诗56:8）对那些不确定离世的亲人是否信主的家庭，我们不替神下结论，只把他们交托给那位公义又满有怜悯的神。

今天，这一家人的眼泪还没有擦干。神的应许不是说他们不会再哭，而是说，最后擦去眼泪的那一位，是神自己。不是时间慢慢冲淡了它，也不是大家劝着劝着就忘了，而是那位记得每一滴眼泪的神，亲自为他们擦去。到那日，宝座中的羔羊要领他们到生命水的泉源；「神也必擦去他们一切的眼泪」（7:17）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 6 ended with the question, "Who can stand?" (6:17). The seventh seal is not opened at once; first John sees two visions that answer the question. The chapter falls into four parts:
- **7:1–3, the four corners of the earth and the seal of the living God:** four angels hold back the four winds of the earth; another angel, with the seal of the living God, must first seal the servants of God on their foreheads.
- **7:4–8, the 144,000:** John hears the number of the sealed from the tribes of Israel, 12,000 from each tribe.
- **7:9–12, the multitude no one could number:** John looks and sees a great multitude from every nation, tribe, people and language, clothed in white robes with palm branches in their hands, standing before the throne and the Lamb and crying, "Salvation belongs to our God... and to the Lamb!"
- **7:13–17, who are these in white robes?** An elder explains: they have come out of the great tribulation and washed their robes white in the blood of the Lamb; the One on the throne will shelter them with his presence, the Lamb will be their shepherd, and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.

In chapter 6 people hide in caves; in chapter 7 people stand before the throne. Before the winds of judgment blow, God first protects those who are his; while suffering has not yet ended, God first shows us how it will end. The chapter is a pause within the seals, so that a suffering church may know whose they are and where they will stand.

## Going Deeper: 7:1–3 The Seal of the Living God

**1. The winds held back.** "After this I saw four angels standing at the four corners of the earth, holding back the four winds of the earth, that no wind might blow on earth or sea or against any tree" (7:1). "The four corners of the earth" and "the four winds" mean the whole earth. In the Old Testament the four winds often stand for God's judgment (Jer 49:36); in Zechariah the four chariots are "going out to the four winds of heaven" (Zech 6:5; the Hebrew can also be read "these are the four winds"), so many interpreters connect these winds with the disasters brought by the four horses of chapter 6. The point here is that the winds are held back. When judgment begins is not decided by the winds, or by the angels who hold them, but by God.

**2. "Until we have sealed the servants of our God."** "Then I saw another angel ascending from the rising of the sun, with the seal of the living God, and he called with a loud voice to the four angels who had been given power to harm earth and sea, saying, 'Do not harm the earth or the sea or the trees, until we have sealed the servants of our God on their foreheads'" (7:2–3). The picture comes from Ezekiel. Before judgment fell on Jerusalem, God told a man to "put a mark on the foreheads of the men who sigh and groan over all the abominations that are committed in it," and told those who carried out the judgment, "touch no one on whom is the mark" (Ezek 9:4, 6). In the ancient world a seal meant ownership and protection.

**3. What does the seal mean?**
- **Belonging to God:** later John sees that those who follow the Lamb have "his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads" (14:1); in the new creation "his name will be on their foreheads" (22:4). The seal of God is God's name, marking these people as his.
- **Protection from God's wrath, not from all suffering:** the martyrs of the fifth seal have already been killed, and their brothers are still "to be killed as they themselves had been" (6:11); the beast will "make war on the saints" (13:7). Yet at the fifth trumpet the locusts are to harm "only those people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads" (9:4). God's seal protects them from his wrath, and keeps them from abandoning him under persecution.
- **The seal in the New Testament:** Paul says believers "were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit" (Eph 1:13; cf. 2 Cor 1:22). Many interpreters think Revelation's seal is the same reality told in the language of vision: God gives his own Spirit to his people as the mark that they belong to him.
- **The mark of the beast:** the beast's mark (13:16) imitates this seal; chapter 13 returns to it.

> The point: Before the winds of judgment blow, God sets his seal on those who are his. The sealed will still suffer, but they belong to God, and no power can snatch them from his hand.

## Going Deeper: 7:4–8 The 144,000

**1. An unusual list.** "And I heard the number of the sealed, 144,000, sealed from every tribe of the sons of Israel" (7:4). John then lists twelve tribes, 12,000 from each (7:5–8). The list has several unusual features:
- **An unusual order:** Old Testament lists usually begin with Reuben, the firstborn; Judah is probably first because "the Lion of the tribe of Judah" (5:5) came from Judah. Levi, who received no land and is often left out of tribal lists, is counted, and Joseph takes Ephraim's place.
- **Dan is missing:** Irenaeus thought this was because the antichrist would come from Dan (*Against Heresies* 5.30.2); others recall Dan's early idolatry and the golden calf later set up at Dan (Judg 18:30; 1 Kgs 12:29). These are only guesses; the text itself does not say.
- **Like a military census:** when Israel was counted in the Old Testament, those counted were "all in Israel who are able to go to war" (Num 1:3). Bauckham and others take the 144,000 as the Lamb's army; yet in chapter 14 this army conquers by following: "It is these who follow the Lamb wherever he goes" (14:4).

**2. Who are they?** Interpreters hold several views:
- **Believers from the nation of Israel:** dispensational scholars (e.g. John Walvoord) take them as Jews from the tribes of Israel who come to faith in Christ during a future tribulation, after the church has been raptured, and so as a group distinct from the multitude of 7:9. They argue that the text plainly says "every tribe of the sons of Israel" and names each tribe, while the multitude that follows comes "from every nation," clearly another group. Those who hold this view often connect the passage with Romans 11:26.
- **The whole people of God, seen from two angles:** Bauckham, Beale and others take the 144,000 and the multitude as the same people. As chapter 5 noted, John sometimes hears one thing, then turns and sees another, and what he sees interprets what he heard: he hears of "the Lion of the tribe of Judah" and sees the slain Lamb (5:5–6); here he "heard" the 144,000 (7:4), but "looked" and saw a great multitude that no one could number (7:9). Seen in terms of the Old Testament promise, they are God's complete Israel (12 × 12 × 1,000); seen in terms of its fulfillment, they are a countless people from every nation (Gen 15:5). The New Testament at times calls those who believe in Christ, Jew and Gentile, Abraham's offspring and "the real circumcision" (Rom 2:28–29; Gal 3:29; Phil 3:3); many read "the Israel of God" (Gal 6:16) and "the twelve tribes" (Jas 1:1) the same way, though both are debated. The gates of the New Jerusalem bear "the names of the twelve tribes of the sons of Israel," and its foundations "the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb" (21:12, 14). Many who hold this view also believe God still has future mercy for the nation of Israel (Rom 11:26), but do not think this chapter is about it.
- **A remnant of Jewish believers:** some take them as Jewish believers within the church. Preterists (e.g. Kenneth Gentry) see the Jewish Christians preserved through the fall of Jerusalem in AD 70; others see an end-time believing remnant of Israel, without requiring a pre-tribulation rapture.

**3. The Jehovah's Witnesses' claim.** Jehovah's Witnesses teach that only 144,000 "anointed" people go to heaven to reign with Christ, while the other faithful, the "great crowd," will live forever in a paradise on earth. This is hard to sustain:
- **It is inconsistent:** they take "144,000" as a literal number, but "every tribe of the sons of Israel" as not literal Israel; yet in the text the two go together.
- **The multitude is in heaven too, and in the end there is one people:** the multitude is "standing before the throne and before the Lamb" (7:9) and serves God "day and night in his temple" (7:15); the word for "temple" (*naos*) elsewhere in Revelation is the heavenly sanctuary (11:19; 15:5). In the new creation "his servants will worship him. They will see his face" (22:3–4). Scripture does not divide God's people into two classes forever.
If Jehovah's Witnesses come to our door, we can speak with them gently and respectfully, and it is best to begin with who Jesus is (see the discussions of his deity in chapters 3 and 5).

> The point: The sealed are counted tribe by tribe, one by one; God forgets none who are his. Many interpreters see here his promise to Israel fulfilled in the Lamb, in a people no one can number.

## Going Deeper: 7:9–12 A Multitude No One Could Number

**1. "That no one could number."** "After this I looked, and behold, a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages, standing before the throne and before the Lamb, clothed in white robes, with palm branches in their hands" (7:9).
- **Beyond counting:** God once brought Abraham outside and said, "Look toward heaven, and number the stars, if you are able to number them... So shall your offspring be" (Gen 15:5). Here that promise is fulfilled.
- **From every nation, tribe, people and language:** these four words match the four in 5:9, where the Lamb "by your blood... ransomed people for God." They are here because the Lamb bought them.
- **White robes and palm branches:** white robes mean victory and cleansing (3:4–5; 6:11); palm branches recall the Feast of Booths, when Israel rejoiced with branches in remembrance of God bringing them out of Egypt and making them live in booths (Lev 23:40–43), and the crowds who "took branches of palm trees and went out to meet" Jesus as he entered Jerusalem (John 12:13).

**2. "Salvation belongs to our God... and to the Lamb!"** "And crying out with a loud voice, 'Salvation belongs to our God who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb!'" (7:10). They do not boast that they made it through at last; nothing about their standing there is their own achievement. The cry once again places the One on the throne and the Lamb together (5:13), with the same worship and the same praise.

**3. The angels' "Amen."** "And all the angels were standing around the throne and around the elders and the four living creatures, and they fell on their faces before the throne and worshiped God, saying, 'Amen! Blessing and glory and wisdom and thanksgiving and honor and power and might be to our God forever and ever! Amen'" (7:11–12). The angels answer the cry of the redeemed with a sevenfold praise.

> The point: God promised Abraham offspring beyond counting; the Lamb's blood bought a people from every nation. Together they confess that salvation belongs to God and to the Lamb.

## Going Deeper: 7:13–17 Who Are These in White Robes?

**1. "Sir, you know."** "Then one of the elders addressed me, saying, 'Who are these, clothed in white robes, and from where have they come?' I said to him, 'Sir, you know'" (7:13–14a). Ezekiel answered the same way when asked whether the dry bones could live: "O Lord GOD, you know" (Ezek 37:3; cf. Zech 4:5). John does not guess; he admits he does not know, which is a good posture for reading Revelation too.

**2. "Coming out of the great tribulation."** "And he said to me, 'These are the ones coming out of the great tribulation'" (7:14). The Greek has the article: "the great tribulation." Interpreters hold two main views:
- **A particular period at the end:** Daniel speaks of "a time of trouble, such as never has been since there was a nation till that time" (Dan 12:1), and Jesus said, "then there will be great tribulation, such as has not been from the beginning of the world until now, no, and never will be" (Matt 24:21). John Walvoord and other dispensational scholars take this as the future seven-year tribulation, and these people as those who come to faith after the rapture of the church and are delivered or martyred in it. Many historic premillennialists and other futurists also see a final period of special suffering before the Lord returns, but hold that the church goes through it. Preterists take it as the Jewish War of AD 66–70 (cf. Matt 24:21).
- **The tribulation of the church through the whole age:** Beale and others take "the great tribulation" as all the suffering the church endures in the world from Christ's first coming to his return, growing more intense at the end. John calls himself the readers' "partner in the tribulation" (1:9), the same word, also with the article; and Jesus said, "In the world you will have tribulation" (John 16:33).
On 3:10, "I will keep you from the hour of trial," and the rapture, see the discussion in chapter 3. Whichever view we take, these people did not go around the tribulation; they came out of it.

**3. "Washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb."** "They have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14). Blood would normally stain a garment red; here it washes it white. Their white robes were not woven by themselves; they were washed in the Lamb's blood. The church in Laodicea was urged to "buy from me... white garments" (3:18); here a multitude stands before the throne wearing them.

**4. Shelter, shepherd and springs.** "Therefore they are before the throne of God, and serve him day and night in his temple; and he who sits on the throne will shelter them with his presence. They shall hunger no more, neither thirst anymore; the sun shall not strike them, nor any scorching heat. For the Lamb in the midst of the throne will be their shepherd, and he will guide them to springs of living water, and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes" (7:15–17).
- **"Shelter them with his presence":** many note that the verb means to pitch a tent, the same verb as in "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14) and is tied to "the dwelling place of God is with man" (21:3). God himself becomes their covering (Isa 4:6).
- **No hunger, no thirst, no scorching heat:** these lines come from Isaiah: "they shall not hunger or thirst, neither scorching wind nor sun shall strike them, for he who has pity on them will lead them, and by springs of water will guide them" (Isa 49:10). Isaiah spoke of God bringing exiles home; John sees that the road home ends before the throne.
- **The Lamb as shepherd:** a lamb is normally the one shepherded; here the Lamb is the shepherd. God promised, "I will set up over them one shepherd, my servant David" (Ezek 34:23). Jesus said, "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep" (John 10:11). He can shepherd us because he was slain for us.
- **Every tear wiped away:** Isaiah said, "the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from all faces" (Isa 25:8); chapter 21 repeats the promise (21:4).
- **Now or later?** Those who have died are before the throne now; all this is fully realized in the new creation (21:3–4).

> The point: Those who come out of the great tribulation do not stand before the throne by their own strength; they are washed in the blood of the Lamb. There the Lamb who was slain becomes their shepherd, and God himself wipes away their tears.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For the Lamb in the midst of the throne will be their shepherd, and he will guide them to springs of living water, and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes" (7:17). For twenty years Lao Liu worked on building sites in the south, going home only once a year at Spring Festival. This summer he collapsed from heatstroke on the scaffolding, fell, and never woke up. His workmates brought his ashes back to the village. His mother, who is eighty, sits at the door and keeps asking, "Has he gone off to work again?" His wife arranges the funeral while negotiating compensation with the labor contractor. Their son, in junior high, has not shed a tear; he just shuts himself in his room. Last month father and son quarreled on the phone, and the boy said, "You never cared about me." Those were his last words to his father. Lao Liu came to faith in a fellowship on the building site; his workmates still remember him singing hymns in the evenings, his voice always hoarse.

We need not hurry to tell this family not to cry. The multitude John saw are "the ones coming out of the great tribulation" (7:14); they have wept, and that is why God wipes away their tears. Revelation does not pretend that death does not hurt, or ask those who suffer to smile and say, "It's fine." Pressing for fair compensation, and asking the labor department or legal aid for help, is not unspiritual either; God is a God of justice.

But this family can also listen to what the elder told John. Lao Liu worked his whole life under the blazing sun, on summer sites that often reached forty degrees; yet Scripture says of those before the throne, "the sun shall not strike them, nor any scorching heat" (7:16). He spent his life far from home, sleeping in site dormitories; but the One on the throne "will shelter them with his presence" (7:15). He never had the chance to rest properly; now the one who shepherds him is the Lamb in the midst of the throne. This shepherd understands those who leave home, for he himself left his Father's glory and came to dwell among us (John 1:14), and in the end was slain outside the city. Lao Liu stands before the throne not because his life was good enough, but because this shepherd laid down his life for him.

What may hurt the son most is that last sentence. He cannot take it back. But the same shepherd laid down his life for the son too. The son can bring those words to the Lamb, confess them and weep; for all who trust him, the Lamb has already carried their sin. If he is willing, he can also talk with a trusted adult. The eighty-year-old mother still waits at the door each day, and God keeps every one of her tears: "put my tears in your bottle. Are they not in your book?" (Ps 56:8). For families unsure whether a loved one who died had come to faith, we do not pronounce in God's place; we entrust them to the God who is both just and full of mercy.

Today this family's tears are not yet dry. God's promise is not that they will never weep again, but that the one who at last wipes the tears away is God himself. Not time slowly wearing them down, not friends talking them out of their grief, but the God who has kept count of every tear, wiping them away himself. On that day the Lamb in the midst of the throne will guide them to springs of living water, "and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes" (7:17).
