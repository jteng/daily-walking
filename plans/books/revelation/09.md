## 本章脉络

第8章末了，鹰宣告还有三样「祸哉」。第9章记载了其中的两样，就是第五枝号和第六枝号。全章可以分成三段：
- **9:1-12，第五号，第一样灾祸：** 一个从天落到地上的星打开无底坑，蝗虫从烟中出来，叫额上没有神印记的人受痛苦五个月；它们的王名叫亚巴顿，又叫亚玻伦。
- **9:13-19，第六号，第二样灾祸：** 声音从金坛的四角出来，释放捆绑在幼发拉底大河的四个使者；无数的马军杀了人的三分之一。
- **9:20-21，仍旧不悔改：** 其余没有被杀的人，仍旧拜鬼魔和偶像，也不悔改他们的凶杀、邪术、奸淫、偷窃。

前四枝号击打的是受造之物，这两枝号直接临到人身上，而且越来越重：第五号叫人受痛苦，却不许害死他们；第六号却杀了人的三分之一。可是全章最叫人心惊的，不是蝗虫和马军，而是末了那一句：「仍旧不悔改」。

## 深读 9:1-6 无底坑和蝗虫

**1. 「一个星从天落到地上」。** 「第五位天使吹号，我就看见一个星从天落到地上，有无底坑的钥匙赐给它。」（9:1）启示录常用星来代表天使（1:20），这里的星显然是一个有位格的使者，因为他接过钥匙，打开了坑。他是谁？
- **堕落的天使或撒但：** 许多释经者认为，这是一个已经堕落的天使，甚至就是撒但。原文的「落」是完成式，可以理解为已经落下来的；以赛亚书说到「明亮之星」「从天坠落」（赛14:12），主耶稣也说：「我曾看见撒但从天上坠落，像闪电一样。」（路10:18）
- **奉差遣的好天使：** 也有释经者指出，20:1也有「一位天使从天降下，手里拿着无底坑的钥匙」；「落」也可以只是「降下来」的意思。
无论是哪一种，钥匙都是「赐给它」的。无底坑不是由撒但自己管的；什么时候打开，打开多久，都在神的手中。

**2. 无底坑。** 「无底坑」（*abyssos*）在路加福音和启示录中是关押邪灵的地方。被赶出的鬼「央求耶稣，不要吩咐他们到无底坑里去」（路8:31）；后来的兽也是「从无底坑里上来的」（11:7）；到了第20章，撒但要被扔在无底坑里（20:1-3）。保罗也用这个词指死人的去处（罗10:7）。

**3. 蝗虫。** 蝗虫在旧约中是神审判的工具：埃及的第八灾就是蝗虫，「遮满地面，甚至地都黑暗了」（出10:15）；约珥书描写蝗虫像一支军队，在耶和华的日子来临以前侵犯神的地（珥1:6；2:1-11）。可是这里的蝗虫很奇特：它们「不可伤害地上的草和各样青物，并一切树木」（9:4）。蝗虫本来就是吃青物的，这些蝗虫却只伤害人。所以多数释经者认为，它们代表从无底坑出来的邪灵势力，不是一般的昆虫。

**4. 审判的界限。** 这一段经文一再说到界限：
- **只伤害没有印记的人：** 它们「惟独要伤害额上没有神印记的人」（9:4）。这正是第7章受印的意义：「等我们印了我们神众仆人的额」（7:3）。就像在埃及的灾难中，神「将我的百姓和你的百姓分别出来」（出8:23）。神的百姓仍然活在这个世界上，也会经历各样的苦难，可是邪灵不能夺去他们属神的身份。
- **不许害死，只受痛苦：** 「但不许蝗虫害死他们，只叫他们受痛苦五个月。」（9:5）
- **五个月：** 有些释经者指出，蝗虫一生大约就是五个月左右；也有人认为，这只是表示一段有限的时间。
这些「不可」和「不许」都是谁说的呢？是神。连无底坑的力量，也要听从他的命令。

**5. 「人要求死，决不得死」。** 「在那些日子，人要求死，决不得死；愿意死，死却远避他们。」（9:6）约伯在痛苦中也说过同样的话：「他们切望死，却不得死」（伯3:21）。邪灵所带来的不是自由，而是叫人活着却没有盼望的痛苦。不过，如果你此刻也有求死的念头，那并不表示你在这审判之下；约伯是一个敬畏神的人，他也说过这样的话。请把你的心事告诉一位可以信任的人，或者联系当地的心理危机干预热线、到医院求助。

> 要点：无底坑的力量是真实的，可是钥匙在神手里。受了神印记的人，邪灵不能夺去他们属神的身份。

## 深读 9:7-12 亚巴顿，亚玻伦

**1. 一连串的「好像」。** 约翰描写这些蝗虫的时候，几乎每一句都用「好像」：「好像预备出战的马」「好像金冠冕」「好像男人的脸面」（9:7）；头发「像女人的头发」，牙齿「像狮子的牙齿」（9:8）；翅膀的声音「好像许多车马奔跑上阵的声音」（9:9）。这些画面大多来自约珥书：蝗虫「形状如马，奔跑如马兵」（珥2:4），「牙齿如狮子的牙齿」（珥1:6）。约翰是在用人类的语言描写一种超乎寻常、叫人恐惧的势力。「好像金冠冕」也值得留意：它们看起来有权柄，却不是真正的王冠。

**2. 「亚巴顿」和「亚玻伦」。** 「有无底坑的使者作它们的王，按着希伯来话，名叫亚巴顿，希腊话，名叫亚玻伦。」（9:11）
- **亚巴顿：** 希伯来文的意思是「毁灭」，在旧约中常和阴间、死亡并列，指死人的去处：「在神面前，阴间显露；灭亡也不得遮掩。」（伯26:6）和合本把这个词译作「灭亡」或「灭没」（伯28:22；诗88:11）。
- **亚玻伦：** 希腊文的意思是「毁灭者」。约翰把两种语言的名字都写出来。有些学者（例如大卫·奥尼、毕尔）认为，这个名字也暗暗讽刺了希腊的阿波罗神：蝗虫是阿波罗的标记之一，多米田等皇帝也很推崇这位神。
这位王是谁？有人认为就是撒但，有人认为是撒但手下一位邪灵的首领。约翰的重点是：这些蝗虫有一个王，他的名字就叫「毁灭」。

**3. 四种读法怎样看蝗虫？**
- **未来派：** 约翰·华尔伏等人认为，这些是将来大灾难时从无底坑真实释放出来的邪灵。
- **历史派：** 许多较早的历史派释经者（例如十七世纪的约瑟夫·米德和十九世纪的艾略特）认为，第五号指七世纪起从阿拉伯半岛兴起、像蝗虫一样扩张的撒拉逊人（阿拉伯军队），并按「一日顶一年」的算法，把五个月算成一百五十年。
- **过去派：** 大卫·齐尔顿、肯尼斯·詹特里等人把这些灾难和主后66-70年犹太战争中耶路撒冷内部的恐怖、外部的围困连在一起。
- **理想派：** 威廉·亨德里克森和毕尔等人认为，这些蝗虫描写邪灵在历世历代中对拜偶像之人的辖制：迷惑、空虚、良心的折磨和对死亡的恐惧。
本研读认为，这些画面的重点在于属灵的实际：拜偶像的人以为自己是自由的，其实是在毁灭者的权下受折磨。至于将来是否还有更集中、更激烈的应验，福音派信徒之间有不同的看法。

**4. 毁灭者和赐生命的主。** 这个邪恶的王折磨的，是他自己的臣民。罪的工价不是快乐，而是痛苦；神有时「任凭」人走自己选择的路，这本身就是审判（罗1:24）。主耶稣说：「盗贼来，无非要偷窃，杀害，毁坏；我来了，是要叫羊得生命，并且得的更丰盛。」（约10:10）世上有两个王：一个名叫毁灭，一个为羊舍命。

> 要点：撒但的国度带来的是折磨和毁灭，即使它看起来戴着金冠冕。只有基督赐人生命。

## 深读 9:13-19 第六号：幼发拉底大河

**1. 从金坛的四角出来的声音。** 「第六位天使吹号，我就听见有声音从神面前金坛的四角出来」（9:13）。这座金坛，就是众圣徒的祈祷和香一同献上的地方（8:3-5）。第二样灾祸的命令从那里发出。许多释经者认为，这又一次表明：神的审判和他百姓的祷告连在一起（6:10；8:3-5）。

**2. 幼发拉底大河。** 「把那捆绑在幼发拉底大河的四个使者释放了。」（9:14）
- **应许之地的边界：** 神应许亚伯兰的地，「从埃及河直到幼发拉底大河」（创15:18）。
- **侵略者来的方向：** 亚述和巴比伦的大军都是从幼发拉底河那边来的。
- **罗马的边境：** 对一世纪的读者来说，幼发拉底河是罗马帝国的东方边境，河的那边是帕提亚人，以善于骑射闻名，是罗马人最害怕的敌人。许多学者认为，约翰的读者听到这里，会想到从东方涌来的骑兵。
「捆绑」表示这四个使者一直被约束着；什么时候释放，不由他们自己决定。

**3. 「到某年某月某日某时」。** 「他们原是预备好了，到某年某月某日某时，要杀人的三分之一。」（9:15）这句话的重点是：神早已定好了时候，不早也不迟。历史派（例如十九世纪的艾略特）曾按一日顶一年的算法，把一时、一日、一月、一年加起来，算成约三百九十六年，读作从十一世纪土耳其人越过幼发拉底河到1453年攻陷君士坦丁堡；这在当时很有影响，今天却很少有人这样读。原文四个词前面只有一个冠词，许多释经者认为，较自然的意思是神所定的一个时刻，而不是一段要加起来计算的时间。约翰的意思不是要我们去算日子，而是要我们知道：连最可怕的灾难，也在神所定的时候之内。

**4. 二万万马军。** 「马军有二万万；他们的数目我听见了。」（9:16）
- **一个说不尽的数目：** 原文是两万的万倍。但以理看见「在他面前侍立的有万万」（但7:10），启示录第5章的天使「有千千万万」（5:11）。许多释经者认为，这是表示数目大得无法计算。
- **中国的军队？** 二十世纪七十年代，有些通俗的未来派作者（例如哈尔·林赛）把这二万万马军和16:12「从日出之地所来的众王」连在一起，说这是中国的军队，因为当时有人说中国可以动员两亿民兵。这种说法在华人教会中也流传过。可是约翰说「他们的数目我听见了」，正像他「听见」十四万四千的数目一样（7:4）；这个数目的作用是叫人震惊，而不是叫人去对照哪一个国家的人口。
- **不像人的军队：** 马的头「好像狮子头」，口中出来火、烟和硫磺（9:17）；尾巴「像蛇，并且有头用以害人」（9:19）。这不像任何人间的骑兵，而是邪灵势力的形象。火与硫磺叫人想起所多玛（创19:24），也预示后面的火湖（21:8）；蛇叫人想起「那古蛇」（12:9）。

> 要点：最可怕的灾难，也有神定的时候和界限。约翰不是要我们推算日期、对照国家，而是要我们看见：时候在神手中。

## 深读 9:20-21 仍旧不悔改

**1. 灾难没有带来悔改。** 「其余未曾被这些灾所杀的人仍旧不悔改自己手所做的，还是去拜鬼魔和那些不能看、不能听、不能走，金、银、铜、木、石的偶像，又不悔改他们那些凶杀、邪术、奸淫、偷窃的事。」（9:20-21）
- **法老：** 冰雹的灾难来到时，法老说：「这一次我犯了罪了。」可是「法老见雨和雹与雷止住，就越发犯罪」（出9:27、34）；前面也说，他「见灾祸松缓，就硬着心」（出8:15）。
- **阿摩司：** 神说他降下饥荒、旱灾、蝗虫、瘟疫，每一次都说：「你们仍不归向我。」（摩4:6-11）
「仍旧不悔改」这句话表明，悔改本来是人面对这些灾难时应有的回应。许多释经者认为，这些灾难也有呼召人悔改的作用；毕尔等人则强调，它们像埃及的十灾一样，显明了人心的刚硬。到了第11章，在两个见证人的见证以后，确实有人「恐惧，归荣耀给天上的神」（11:13）。可是这里，人仍旧不悔改；后面七碗倒下时也是这样（16:9、11）。

**2. 「自己手所做的」：偶像。** 这里的偶像清单来自旧约：「他们的偶像是金的，银的，是人手所造的，有口却不能言，有眼却不能看」（诗115:4-5）。偶像本身是虚无的，可是它们背后有邪灵：「外邦人所献的祭是祭鬼，不是祭神」（林前10:20；参申32:17）。今天，偶像可能是财神、算命和风水，也可能是保罗所说的「贪婪」，「贪婪就与拜偶像一样」（西3:5）。

**3. 拜错了神，就活错了路。** 第20节违背的是十诫的前半部（拜别神、雕刻偶像），第21节的凶杀、奸淫、偷窃违背的是后半部。保罗也说，人把神的荣耀换成偶像，接着就落在各样的败坏中（罗1:23-29）。「邪术」（*pharmakeia*）原指用药物、符咒行法术，加拉太书也把它列在肉体的行为里（加5:20）；启示录到了末了还要再提到「行邪术的」（21:8；22:15）。

**4. 苦难本身不会改变人心。** 先知耶利米说：「你击打他们，他们却不伤恸……他们使脸刚硬过于磐石，不肯回头。」（耶5:3）苦难可以叫人软化，也可以叫人更刚硬。主耶稣在财主和拉撒路的比喻中说：「若不听从摩西和先知的话，就是有一个从死里复活的，他们也是不听劝。」（路16:31）能改变人心的，不是灾难本身，而是神的恩典：「我也要赐给你们一个新心……从你们的肉体中除掉石心，赐给你们肉心。」（结36:26）所以在第六号和第七号之间，约翰看见的是小书卷和两个见证人（10-11章）：神不只降下灾难，也差遣他的百姓去作见证。启示录的警告是真实的，不应隐瞒（见第0章），但警告是要叫人转向那位为罪人被杀的羔羊。

> 要点：灾难本身不能使人悔改；人心可以在苦难中越来越刚硬。只有神的恩典能把石心换成肉心。

## 默想

**默想：** 「其余未曾被这些灾所杀的人仍旧不悔改自己手所做的……又不悔改他们那些凶杀、邪术、奸淫、偷窃的事。」（9:20-21）去年冬天，四十多岁的老郑在一次应酬后胸口剧痛，被送进急诊室。等检查结果的那几天，他躺在病床上，跟神说了很多话：只要这次没事，我就戒烟，不再陪客户喝到半夜；那笔见不得光的回扣，我要退回去；我要回教会，也要给多年不说话的弟弟打个电话。结果出来了，只是虚惊一场。半年过去了，烟还在抽，酒还在喝，回扣的事没有再提，弟弟的电话也没有打。有一天他读到启示录第9章，看见「仍旧不悔改」这几个字，心里一沉：这说的不就是我吗？

这场病不一定是神的惩罚。主耶稣说，西罗亚楼倒塌压死的那十八个人，并不比别人更有罪；可是他接着说：「你们若不悔改，都要如此灭亡！」（路13:4-5）病床上那几天，是一个警告，也是一份怜悯。法老在冰雹中说「这一次我犯了罪了」，等到「雨和雹与雷止住」，他就「越发犯罪」（出9:27、34）。人心刚硬，常常不是一下子发生的，而是在一次又一次「以后再说」中慢慢变硬的。

老郑的问题还不只是没有兑现诺言。他在病床上说的那些话，有点像庙里的许愿：神啊，你保我平安，我就还你一个好表现。可是悔改不是还愿，不是用好行为向神交换平安，而是回到他面前，承认我需要的不只是身体的平安，更是一颗新的心。我们没有能力自己换掉石心，可是神应许说：「我也要赐给你们一个新心」（结36:26）。这颗新心的代价，不是我们付的。主耶稣曾经看着耶路撒冷，「就为它哀哭，说：『巴不得你在这日子知道关系你平安的事』」（路19:41-42）；不久以后，他就在那城外被钉在十字架上，「被挂在木头上，亲身担当了我们的罪」（彼前2:24），连那些一再说「以后再说」的人的罪也在其中。

也许你在病床上、在手术室外、在一次车祸以后，也向神许过愿，后来却都忘了；也许这样的事已经不止一次。今天你重新想起这些，本身就是神还在呼召你的记号。主对推雅推喇的那个女人说：「我曾给她悔改的机会」（2:21）；这段时间本身就是恩典。保罗说：「还是你藐视他丰富的恩慈、宽容、忍耐，不晓得他的恩慈是领你悔改呢？」（罗2:4）神没有在急诊室里结束老郑的故事，也没有在那半年里结束。他仍然给时间，不是因为他看不见，而是因为他有怜悯。

主耶稣，我承认我一次又一次地说「以后再说」。谢谢你没有照我的刚硬待我，还给我今天。求你除掉我的石心，赐我一颗肉心；我不再拿好表现来和你交换，只求你接纳我回到你面前。

那位为不肯回转的城哀哭的主，今天还在呼召：「日期满了，神的国近了。你们当悔改，信福音！」（可1:15）

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 8, the eagle announced three more woes. Chapter 9 records two of them, the fifth and sixth trumpets. The chapter falls into three parts:
- **9:1–12, the fifth trumpet, the first woe:** a star fallen from heaven to earth opens the bottomless pit; locusts come out of the smoke and torment for five months those who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads; their king is called Abaddon and Apollyon.
- **9:13–19, the sixth trumpet, the second woe:** a voice from the four horns of the golden altar orders the release of the four angels bound at the great river Euphrates; countless mounted troops kill a third of mankind.
- **9:20–21, no repentance:** the rest, who were not killed, still worship demons and idols, and do not repent of their murders, sorceries, sexual immorality and thefts.

The first four trumpets struck creation; these two fall directly on people, and they grow heavier: the fifth torments people but is not allowed to kill them; the sixth kills a third of mankind. Yet the most frightening thing in the chapter is not the locusts or the horsemen but the last sentence: "they did not repent."

## Going Deeper: 9:1–6 The Bottomless Pit and the Locusts

**1. "A star fallen from heaven to earth."** "And the fifth angel blew his trumpet, and I saw a star fallen from heaven to earth, and he was given the key to the shaft of the bottomless pit" (9:1). Revelation often uses stars for angels (1:20), and this star is clearly a personal being, since he receives a key and opens the pit. Who is he?
- **A fallen angel, or Satan:** many interpreters take him to be an angel who has already fallen, perhaps Satan himself. The Greek "fallen" is a perfect participle and can be read "having fallen"; Isaiah speaks of the "Day Star" "fallen from heaven" (Isa 14:12), and the Lord Jesus said, "I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven" (Luke 10:18).
- **A good angel on God's errand:** other interpreters point out that in 20:1 too "an angel coming down from heaven" holds "the key to the bottomless pit," and that "fallen" may simply mean "come down."
Either way, the key "was given" to him. The bottomless pit is not under Satan's own control; when it is opened, and for how long, is in God's hands.

**2. The bottomless pit.** In Luke and Revelation the "abyss" (*abyssos*) is where evil spirits are confined. The demons "begged him not to command them to depart into the abyss" (Luke 8:31); later the beast "rises from the bottomless pit" (11:7); in chapter 20 Satan is thrown into the pit (20:1–3). Paul uses the word for the realm of the dead (Rom 10:7).

**3. The locusts.** In the Old Testament locusts are an instrument of God's judgment. The eighth plague in Egypt was locusts that "covered the face of the whole land, so that the land was darkened" (Exod 10:15); Joel describes locusts like an army invading God's land before the day of the LORD (Joel 1:6; 2:1–11). But these locusts are strange: "They were told not to harm the grass of the earth or any green plant or any tree" (9:4). Locusts eat green things; these harm only people. So most interpreters take them to represent demonic powers from the abyss, not ordinary insects.

**4. The limits of judgment.** This passage speaks of limits again and again:
- **Only those without the seal:** they harm "only those people who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads" (9:4). This is what the sealing of chapter 7 meant: "until we have sealed the servants of our God on their foreheads" (7:3). So in the plagues of Egypt God said, "I will put a division between my people and your people" (Exod 8:23). God's people still live in this world and go through all kinds of suffering, but evil spirits cannot take away the fact that they belong to God.
- **Torment, not death:** "They were allowed to torment them for five months, but not to kill them" (9:5).
- **Five months:** some interpreters note that this is roughly the life span of a locust; others take it simply as a limited period.
Who says all these "not"s? God does. Even the power of the abyss must obey his command.

**5. "People will seek death and will not find it."** "And in those days people will seek death and will not find it. They will long to die, but death will flee from them" (9:6). Job said the same in his suffering: they "long for death, but it comes not" (Job 3:21). What evil spirits bring is not freedom but the torment of living without hope. But if you are having thoughts of death right now, that does not mean you are under this judgment; Job, a man who feared God, said these words too. Please tell someone you trust, or contact a local crisis line or go to a hospital for help.

> The point: the power of the abyss is real, but the key is in God's hand. Evil spirits cannot take away from those sealed by God the fact that they belong to him.

## Going Deeper: 9:7–12 Abaddon, Apollyon

**1. A string of "likes."** When John describes these locusts, almost every phrase uses "like": "like horses prepared for battle," "what looked like crowns of gold," "faces were like human faces" (9:7); "hair like women's hair," "teeth like lions' teeth" (9:8); "the noise of their wings was like the noise of many chariots with horses rushing into battle" (9:9). Most of these images come from Joel: "Their appearance is like the appearance of horses, and like war horses they run" (Joel 2:4); "its teeth are lions' teeth" (Joel 1:6). John is using human language to describe an extraordinary, terrifying power. "What looked like crowns of gold" is worth noting: they look as if they have authority, but their crowns are not real.

**2. "Abaddon" and "Apollyon."** "They have as king over them the angel of the bottomless pit. His name in Hebrew is Abaddon, and in Greek he is called Apollyon" (9:11).
- **Abaddon:** in Hebrew it means "destruction," and in the Old Testament it often stands beside Sheol and death as the place of the dead: "Sheol is naked before God, and Abaddon has no covering" (Job 26:6; cf. Job 28:22; Ps 88:11).
- **Apollyon:** in Greek it means "destroyer." John gives the name in both languages. Some scholars (e.g. David Aune, Beale) think the name also mocks the god Apollo, whose emblems included the locust and whom some emperors, Domitian among them, favored.
Who is this king? Some say Satan himself; others a chief demon under Satan. John's point is that these locusts have a king, and his name is "Destruction."

**3. How do the four schools read the locusts?**
- **Futurist:** John Walvoord and others take them as demonic beings literally released from the abyss in a future great tribulation.
- **Historicist:** many older historicist interpreters (e.g. Joseph Mede in the seventeenth century and E. B. Elliott in the nineteenth) took the fifth trumpet as the Saracens (Arab armies) who rose from the Arabian peninsula from the seventh century and spread like locusts, and by the "day for a year" principle counted the five months as a hundred and fifty years.
- **Preterist:** David Chilton, Kenneth Gentry and others connect these plagues with the terror inside Jerusalem and the siege outside it during the Jewish War of AD 66–70.
- **Idealist:** William Hendriksen, Beale and others take the locusts as describing the hold that evil spirits have over idolaters in every age: deception, emptiness, torment of conscience and the fear of death.
This study takes the focus of these images to be a spiritual reality: idolaters think they are free, but they are being tormented under the rule of the Destroyer. Whether there will also be a more concentrated, more intense fulfillment in the future is a point on which evangelical believers differ.

**4. The Destroyer and the Lord who gives life.** This evil king torments his own subjects. The wages of sin are not happiness but misery; sometimes God "gave them up" to the path they chose, and that itself is judgment (Rom 1:24). The Lord Jesus said, "The thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy. I came that they may have life and have it abundantly" (John 10:10). There are two kings in the world: one is named Destruction; the other lays down his life for the sheep.

> The point: Satan's kingdom brings torment and destruction, even when it seems to wear crowns of gold. Only Christ gives life.

## Going Deeper: 9:13–19 The Sixth Trumpet: The Great River Euphrates

**1. A voice from the four horns of the golden altar.** "Then the sixth angel blew his trumpet, and I heard a voice from the four horns of the golden altar before God" (9:13). This golden altar is the place where the prayers of the saints were offered with incense (8:3–5). The order for the second woe comes from there. Many interpreters see here, once again, that God's judgments are bound up with the prayers of his people (6:10; 8:3–5).

**2. The great river Euphrates.** "Release the four angels who are bound at the great river Euphrates" (9:14).
- **The border of the promised land:** God promised Abram the land "from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates" (Gen 15:18).
- **The direction of invaders:** the armies of Assyria and Babylon came from beyond the Euphrates.
- **Rome's frontier:** for first-century readers the Euphrates was the eastern frontier of the Roman Empire. Beyond it lay the Parthians, famous mounted archers and Rome's most feared enemy. Many scholars think John's readers would have thought here of cavalry pouring in from the east.
"Bound" means these four angels had been held in check; the time of their release was not theirs to decide.

**3. "For the hour, the day, the month, and the year."** "So the four angels, who had been prepared for the hour, the day, the month, and the year, were released to kill a third of mankind" (9:15). The point is that God fixed the time long ago, neither too early nor too late. Historicists (e.g. E. B. Elliott in the nineteenth century) once added up "the hour, the day, the month, and the year" by the day-for-a-year principle as about 396 years, reading it as the period from the Turks' crossing of the Euphrates in the eleventh century to the fall of Constantinople in 1453; this was influential in its day, but few read it so now. In Greek a single article governs all four words, which, many note, most naturally points to one appointed moment rather than a span to be added up. John does not mean for us to count days, but to know that even the most terrible disaster falls within the time God has set.

**4. Two hundred million mounted troops.** "The number of mounted troops was twice ten thousand times ten thousand; I heard their number" (9:16).
- **A number beyond counting:** Daniel saw "ten thousand times ten thousand" standing before God (Dan 7:10); the angels in chapter 5 numbered "myriads of myriads" (5:11). Many interpreters take it to mean a number too great to count.
- **China's army?** In the 1970s some popular futurist writers (e.g. Hal Lindsey) linked these two hundred million troops with "the kings from the east" (16:12) and said they were China's army, since China was then said to be able to mobilize two hundred million militia. This idea has also circulated in Chinese churches. But John says, "I heard their number," just as he "heard" the number of the 144,000 (7:4); the number is meant to stun, not to be matched against a nation's population.
- **No human army:** the horses' heads are "like lions' heads," and fire, smoke and sulfur come out of their mouths (9:17); their tails are "like serpents with heads, and by means of them they wound" (9:19). This is like no human cavalry; it is an image of demonic power. Fire and sulfur recall Sodom (Gen 19:24) and point ahead to the lake of fire (21:8); the serpent recalls "that ancient serpent" (12:9).

> The point: even the most terrible disaster has a time and a limit set by God. John does not want us to calculate dates or match nations, but to see that the times are in God's hand.

## Going Deeper: 9:20–21 They Did Not Repent

**1. The plagues did not bring repentance.** "The rest of mankind, who were not killed by these plagues, did not repent of the works of their hands nor give up worshiping demons and idols of gold and silver and bronze and stone and wood, which cannot see or hear or walk, nor did they repent of their murders or their sorceries or their sexual immorality or their thefts" (9:20–21).
- **Pharaoh:** when the hail came, Pharaoh said, "This time I have sinned." But "when Pharaoh saw that the rain and the hail and the thunder had ceased, he sinned yet again" (Exod 9:27, 34); earlier, "when Pharaoh saw that there was a respite, he hardened his heart" (Exod 8:15).
- **Amos:** God says he sent famine, drought, locusts and pestilence, and each time: "yet you did not return to me" (Amos 4:6–11).
"They did not repent" shows that repentance was the fitting response to these plagues. Many interpreters hold that the plagues also serve as a call to repentance; Beale and others stress that, like the plagues of Egypt, they expose the hardness of the human heart. In chapter 11, after the testimony of the two witnesses, some indeed "were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven" (11:13). But here, people do not repent, nor will they when the bowls are poured out (16:9, 11).

**2. "The works of their hands": idols.** The list of idols comes from the Old Testament: "Their idols are silver and gold, the work of human hands. They have mouths, but do not speak; eyes, but do not see" (Ps 115:4–5). Idols are nothing in themselves, but there are evil spirits behind them: "what pagans sacrifice they offer to demons and not to God" (1 Cor 10:20; cf. Deut 32:17). Today an idol may be the god of wealth, fortune-telling and feng shui, or what Paul calls "covetousness, which is idolatry" (Col 3:5).

**3. Wrong worship leads to a wrong life.** Verse 20 breaks the first half of the Ten Commandments (other gods and carved images); the murders, sexual immorality and thefts of verse 21 break the second. Paul too says that people exchanged the glory of God for images and then fell into every kind of corruption (Rom 1:23–29). "Sorceries" (*pharmakeia*) originally meant working magic with drugs and spells; Galatians lists it among the works of the flesh (Gal 5:20), and Revelation names "sorcerers" again at its close (21:8; 22:15).

**4. Suffering alone does not change the heart.** Jeremiah says, "You have struck them down, but they felt no anguish... They have made their faces harder than rock; they have refused to repent" (Jer 5:3). Suffering can soften people, and it can harden them. In the parable of the rich man and Lazarus the Lord Jesus said, "If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither will they be convinced if someone should rise from the dead" (Luke 16:31). What changes the heart is not disaster itself but God's grace: "I will give you a new heart... And I will remove the heart of stone from your flesh and give you a heart of flesh" (Ezek 36:26). So between the sixth and seventh trumpets John sees the little scroll and the two witnesses (chs. 10–11): God does not only send plagues; he also sends his people to bear witness. Revelation's warnings are real and should not be hidden (see chapter 0), but they are meant to turn people to the Lamb who was slain for sinners.

> The point: disasters by themselves do not make people repent; the heart can grow harder in suffering. Only God's grace can replace a heart of stone with a heart of flesh.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "The rest of mankind, who were not killed by these plagues, did not repent of the works of their hands... nor did they repent of their murders or their sorceries or their sexual immorality or their thefts" (9:20–21). Last winter, after a business dinner, Lao Zheng, in his forties, was rushed to the emergency room with severe chest pain. In the days of waiting for test results he lay in the hospital bed and said many things to God: if I come through this, I will quit smoking and stop drinking with clients until midnight; I will give back that kickback I can't let anyone see; I will go back to church, and I will call my younger brother, whom I haven't spoken to in years. The results came back: a false alarm. Six months have passed. He still smokes, still drinks, has not mentioned the kickback again, and has not made the call. One day he reads Revelation 9, sees the words "did not repent," and his heart sinks: isn't that me?

His illness was not necessarily God's punishment. The Lord Jesus said that the eighteen killed when the tower in Siloam fell were not worse offenders than others; but he went on, "unless you repent, you will all likewise perish" (Luke 13:4–5). Those days in the hospital bed were a warning, and also a mercy. Pharaoh said in the hail, "This time I have sinned," but when "the rain and the hail and the thunder had ceased, he sinned yet again" (Exod 9:27, 34). A heart seldom hardens all at once; it hardens slowly, through one "later" after another.

Lao Zheng's problem is not only that he broke his promises. What he said in the hospital bed was rather like a vow made at a temple: God, keep me safe, and I will pay you back with good behavior. But repentance is not paying off a vow. It is not trading good works for God's protection. It is coming back to him and admitting that what I need is not only a healthy body but a new heart. We cannot exchange our heart of stone by our own power, but God promises, "I will give you a new heart" (Ezek 36:26). The price of that new heart is not one we pay. The Lord Jesus once looked at Jerusalem and "wept over it, saying, 'Would that you, even you, had known on this day the things that make for peace!'" (Luke 19:41–42). Soon afterward, outside that city, he was nailed to the cross and "himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" (1 Pet 2:24), the sins of people who kept saying "later," mine among them.

Perhaps you too made vows to God in a hospital bed, outside an operating room or after a car accident, and later forgot them all; perhaps this has happened more than once. That you remember them today is itself a sign that God is still calling you. The Lord said of the woman in Thyatira, "I gave her time to repent" (2:21); that time itself is grace. Paul asks, "Do you presume on the riches of his kindness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that God's kindness is meant to lead you to repentance?" (Rom 2:4). God did not end Lao Zheng's story in the emergency room, nor in those six months. He still gives time, not because he does not see, but because he is merciful.

Lord Jesus, I confess that again and again I have said "later." Thank you for not treating me as my hardness deserves, and for giving me today. Take away my heart of stone and give me a heart of flesh. I will no longer offer you good behavior in exchange; I ask only that you receive me back.

The Lord who wept over the city that would not turn is still calling today: "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel" (Mark 1:15).
