## 本章脉络

第8-9章里，七位天使中已经有六位吹了号。读者正等着第七号的时候，叙述却停了下来。正如第六印和第七印之间插进了第7章，第六号和第七号之间也插进了一段（10:1-11:14）：先是小书卷（第10章），然后是丈量圣殿和两个见证人（第11章）。第9章的末了说，其余未被灾所杀的人「仍旧不悔改」（9:20-21）。这段插曲接着回答一个问题：神的百姓在这样的世界里要做什么？答案是：他们要先领受神的话，然后再去说预言。全章可以分成三段：
- **10:1-4，大力的天使与七雷：** 一位披着云彩、头上有虹的天使从天降下，手里拿着展开的小书卷，右脚踏海，左脚踏地；七雷发声，约翰却被吩咐「封上，不可写出来」。
- **10:5-7，天使的誓言：** 天使向天举起右手，指着创造天、地、海的永活者起誓：「不再有时日了」；第七位天使吹号的时候，「神的奥秘就成全了」。
- **10:8-11，吃小书卷：** 约翰从天使手中接过小书卷吃尽了，口中甜如蜜，肚子却发苦；然后他领受了新的差遣：「你必……再说预言」。

七号是接着七印发生，还是从另一个角度重述同一段时期？这个问题在导读和第6、8章已经介绍过，这里不再重复。无论采用哪一种读法，这段插曲的位置都很清楚：在最后的号声响起以前，神先把他的话交给他的仆人。

## 深读 10:1-4 大力的天使与七雷

**1. 天使的样貌。** 「我又看见另有一位大力的天使从天降下，披着云彩，头上有虹，脸面像日头，两脚像火柱。」（10:1）这位天使身上的每一样，前面几乎都出现过：
- **云彩：** 主耶稣「驾云降临」（1:7）；在旧约里，云常常是神同在的记号。
- **虹：** 宝座的周围「有虹」（4:3），让人想起神在挪亚以后立的约。
- **脸面像日头：** 人子的「面貌如同烈日放光」（1:16）。
- **两脚像火柱：** 在旷野里，耶和华「夜间，在火柱中光照他们」（出13:21）。

**2. 这位天使是谁？** 释经者有两种主要的看法：
- **基督自己，或旧约中「耶和华的使者」：** 有些释经者（如毕尔）认为，云彩、虹、日头、火柱都是神和人子的特征，所以这位天使很可能就是基督，或是代表基督的显现。即使采用这种看法，这里的「天使」一词也只是指奉差遣而来的那一位，不是说基督是受造的天使；神的儿子「远超过天使」（来1:4）。
- **一位大能的天使：** 许多释经者（如包衡、格兰特·奥斯本）认为这是一位天使。启示录从来没有称基督为天使；「另有一位大力的天使」让人想起5:2那位「大力的天使」，后面18:21也有一位；而且他「指着」永活的神起誓（10:6），更像是天使的口吻。他身上的荣光，是从差他来的那一位反照出来的。
两种看法都承认：他带着天上的权柄而来，他手中的书卷是神的话。

**3. 「右脚踏海，左脚踏地」。** 「他手里拿着小书卷，是展开的。他右脚踏海，左脚踏地」（10:2）。海和地合起来，就是全世界。这位天使所带来的信息关乎全地，也宣告全地都属于神。到了第13章，一只兽「从海中上来」，另一只兽「从地中上来」（13:1、11）；可是在兽出现以前，神的使者已经站在海上和地上了。海和地看起来属于兽，其实从来不是。

**4. 「好像狮子吼叫」。** 「大声呼喊，好像狮子吼叫。」（10:3）先知阿摩司说：「狮子吼叫，谁不惧怕呢？主耶和华发命，谁能不说预言呢？」（摩3:8）何西阿也说：「耶和华必如狮子吼叫」（何11:10）。狮子的吼声，是神说话的声音；这一章的结尾，正是约翰领受命令，要再说预言（10:11）。

**5. 「七雷所说的，你要封上」。** 「呼喊完了，就有七雷发声。七雷发声之后，我正要写出来，就听见从天上有声音说：『七雷所说的，你要封上，不可写出来。』」（10:3-4）诗篇说「荣耀的神打雷」（诗29:3），雷声在圣经中常是神的声音。约翰一向是看见什么就写什么（1:11），这一次却被禁止写下来。为什么？
- **神决定不施行的审判：** 包衡认为，七雷像七印、七号一样，原是另一组审判；神吩咐把它封上，是因为单单审判并没有叫人悔改（9:20-21）。神不再加上一轮又一轮的灾难，而是转向另一条路，就是他百姓忠心的见证（第11章）。
- **神保留不说的事：** 另一些释经者认为，这节经文只是告诉我们，神并没有把一切都启示出来。「隐秘的事是属耶和华－我们神的；惟有明显的事是永远属我们和我们子孙的」（申29:29）；保罗也曾「听见隐秘的言语，是人不可说的」（林后12:4）。
这两种看法可以并存。无论如何，七雷要「封上」，和这卷书末了的吩咐正好相对：「不可封了这书上的预言，因为日期近了」（22:10；参但12:4）。神没有封上的，是这卷书其余的一切话。

**6. 对喜欢推算的人的提醒。** 启示录本身承认，有一些话连约翰都没有写下来。凡自称能说出七雷的内容、能解开这卷书一切隐秘的人，已经越过了这卷书自己定下的界限（参看导读关于东方闪电的讨论）。我们不必为没有写出来的事焦虑，而要专心领受已经写出来的。

> 要点：在最后的号声响起以前，神的使者已经站在海上和地上。神没有把一切都告诉我们，但他所启示的，足够叫我们听见、遵守。

## 深读 10:5-7 不再有时日了

**1. 举手起誓。** 「我所看见的那踏海踏地的天使向天举起右手来，指着那创造天和天上之物，地和地上之物，海和海中之物，直活到永永远远的，起誓说」（10:5-6）。这一幕的背景是但以理书的末了。在那里，有人问：「这奇异的事到几时才应验呢？」那穿细麻衣的「向天举起左右手，指着活到永远的主起誓说：『要到一载、二载、半载……这一切事就都应验了。』」（但12:6-7）但以理听见的是还要等候一段时间；约翰听见的却是：等候的时候到头了。天使指着创造主起誓：那创造天、地、海的，有权柄也有能力，把他所开始的完成。

**2. 「不再有时日了」。** 英文钦定本把这句译成时间不再存在的意思，有人因此认为，这是说时间本身要结束，进入没有时间的永恒。可是这个词（*chronos*）在这里，许多释经者认为是「耽延」的意思，现代的英文译本（如英文标准版）多这样翻译。启示录别处也用同一个词：主说他给耶洗别「悔改的机会」（2:21）；祭坛下的灵魂被告知「还要安息片时」（6:11）。那些灵魂曾大声问：「要等到几时呢？」（6:10）现在天使起誓回答：不再耽延了。先知哈巴谷也听过类似的话：「虽然迟延，还要等候；因为必然临到，不再迟延。」（哈2:3）

**3. 这不是一个日期。** 「不再有时日了」不是说末日就在明天，也没有给我们一个可以推算的年份。天使说的是：到了「第七位天使吹号发声的时候」（10:7），就再没有什么能拦阻神的旨意成就。主耶稣说过，那日子、那时辰，「没有人知道」（可13:32）。这个誓言要给受苦的教会的，不是一张时间表，而是一个保证。但以理听见的「一载、二载、半载」，在第11-12章又出现了（启11:2-3；12:14）。有释经者因此认为，「不再有时日」不是说中间再没有任何时间，而是说神所量定的最后一段日子不再推迟，已经开始了。

**4. 「神的奥秘就成全了」。** 「但在第七位天使吹号发声的时候，神的奥秘就成全了，正如神所传给他仆人众先知的佳音。」（10:7）
- **奥秘：** 在新约里，「奥秘」不是谁也不能明白的谜，而是神从前隐藏、如今显明的旨意：「这道理就是历世历代所隐藏的奥秘；但如今向他的圣徒显明了」（西1:26）；「这奥秘就是外邦人在基督耶稣里，藉着福音，得以同为后嗣」（弗3:6）。在这里，奥秘是神在全地建立他国度的整个计划，包括审判罪恶，也包括拯救万民。第七号吹响的时候，天上就宣告：「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国」（11:15）。
- **「成全」：** 这个动词（*teleō*）在启示录里也用在见证人「作完见证」（11:7）和神的大怒在七灾中「发尽了」（15:1）。神所定的事，没有一件会半途而止。
- **「佳音」：** 「所传给」原文是传好消息的意思（*euangelizō*），就是「传福音」的那个词。阿摩司说：「主耶和华若不将奥秘指示他的仆人－众先知，就一无所行。」（摩3:7）神的结局，包括审判，对他的仆人来说是好消息，因为恶要被除去，他的国要来到。

> 要点：神指着自己创造的天地起誓，不再耽延。他没有给我们日期，却给了我们保证：他所传给众先知的佳音，必要完全成就。

## 深读 10:8-11 吃小书卷

**1. 「你去，把……小书卷取过来」。** 「我先前从天上所听见的那声音又吩咐我说：『你去，把那踏海踏地之天使手中展开的小书卷取过来。』」（10:8）这卷小书卷和第5章那卷书是什么关系？释经者有不同的看法：
- **同一卷书：** 包衡和毕尔都认为，第5章封严的书卷由羔羊揭开以后，现在「是展开的」（10:2）；书卷真正的内容，就是从这里开始显明的（参第5章的讨论）。而且10:8较早的抄本用的是普通的「书卷」一词（和合本依照后来的抄本作「小书卷」），所以指小词未必有多大分量。
- **另一卷较小的书：** 有些释经者（如大卫·奥尼）认为，原文用的是指小词（*biblaridion*），这是另一卷书，内容大致就是接下来的第11章。
无论采用哪一种看法，这卷书的内容就是约翰接下来要说的预言。

**2. 以西结吃书卷。** 「我就走到天使那里，对他说：『请你把小书卷给我。』他对我说：『你拿着吃尽了，便叫你肚子发苦，然而在你口中要甜如蜜。』」（10:9）这一幕几乎是以西结蒙召的重演。神对以西结说：「你要开口吃我所赐给你的」；「要吃这书卷，好去对以色列家讲说」；以西结「就吃了，口中觉得其甜如蜜」（结2:8；3:1、3）。随后他说：「我心中甚苦，灵性忿激」（结3:14）。耶利米也说：「我得着你的言语就当食物吃了；你的言语是我心中的欢喜快乐」（耶15:16）。先知不能只是把神的话转交出去；他要先把这话吃下去，叫它进到自己里面。主耶稣也说：「人活着，不是单靠食物，乃是靠神口里所出的一切话。」（太4:4）

**3. 先甜后苦。** 「我从天使手中把小书卷接过来，吃尽了，在我口中果然甜如蜜，吃了以后，肚子觉得发苦了。」（10:10）天使先说苦、后说甜（10:9），约翰经历的却是先甜、后苦。甜和苦各指什么？
- **甜：** 神的话本身是好的，「比蜜甘甜，且比蜂房下滴的蜜甘甜」（诗19:10）。神的旨意是要审判罪恶，拯救他的百姓，叫万民敬拜他；这一切都是甜的。
- **苦：** 这卷书的内容也包括审判，包括神的见证人要受苦、被杀（11:7-10）。许多释经者认为，苦的不只是听信息的人，也是传信息的人自己：他要把一个甜美的信息带进一个不肯悔改的世界，而且要为此付上代价。以西结和耶利米都是这样。
所以，肚子发苦不表示约翰做错了什么，也不表示神的话变坏了。苦，是忠心领受神话语的人常有的经历。

**4. 「你必……再说预言」。** 「天使对我说：『你必指着多民、多国、多方、多王再说预言。』」（10:11）
- **「必」：** 这是神所定的必须，和1:1「必要快成的事」的「必」相同。说预言不是约翰自己选的事业，而是神的差遣。
- **「再」：** 约翰在第1章已经蒙召写信给七个教会（1:11、19）；在这里，他像以西结一样，吃了书卷以后再一次被差遣：「你往以色列家那里去，将我的话对他们讲说」（结3:4）。这是一次重新的差遣。
- **「多民、多国、多方、多王」：** 启示录常用「各族、各方、各民、各国」这样四个词的组合（5:9；7:9；13:7），这里却把「族」换成了「王」。后面的章节果然说到地上的君王（17-19章），而到了书末，「地上的君王必将自己的荣耀归与那城」（21:24）。「指着」原文的介词可以译作「关于」，也可以译作「向」或「针对」。包衡强调，这个预言的目的之一，是要叫列国悔改归向神（参11:13；14:6-7；15:4）。

**5. 今天的教会。** 约翰的差遣是独特的，他所写的这卷书是神的话。但吃书卷、再说预言的模式，也是给每一个时代的教会的：先领受神的话，让它在我们里面甜，也在我们里面苦；然后向列国作见证。启示录里的「说预言」，主要不是预测日期，而是为耶稣作见证（19:10）。

> 要点：神的仆人要先把神的话吃下去，才能把它说出来。这话在口中甜如蜜，在肚子里却可能发苦；但神并没有因此撤回差遣，反而说：「你必……再说预言」。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你拿着吃尽了，便叫你肚子发苦，然而在你口中要甜如蜜。」（10:9）林姊妹在教会教儿童主日学已经十二年了。头几年，她每个星期六晚上备课，常常备着备着就自己先被感动了：原来挪亚的方舟是这样的，原来大卫在旷野里也是这样等候神的。那时候，神的话在她口中是甜的。这几年却不一样了。帮忙的老师走了两位，班上的孩子越来越难管，有位家长当面说她讲得太枯燥。最叫她难受的，是一个她从小教到大的孩子，上了高中就不来了，在路上遇见她，只淡淡地说：「那些都是讲给小孩子听的故事。」现在她星期六晚上打开圣经，就像打开一本工作手册，每一节经文都是一项任务。上个月，她在班上对一个男孩大声吼了一句，回家以后很久都睡不着。她心里已经定了：做到年底就退下来。也许神已经用完她了。

约翰吃下小书卷，口中甜如蜜，肚子却发苦了（10:10）。那苦不是因为他不忠心，也不是因为神的话坏了，而是因为一个甜美的信息被带进一个不肯悔改的世界。以西结吃了书卷以后，也是「心中甚苦」地走了出去（结3:14）。林姊妹肚子里的苦，有一部分正是这种苦：为那个离开的孩子流的泪，为孩子们的心祷告却看不见果效。这不是失败，这是忠心服事神的人常有的经历。

可是她也知道，那苦里还混着别的东西：对家长的怨气，对那个男孩的吼叫，看见别人的事工受重视时心里的不平。约翰在他的书信里写过：「他儿子耶稣的血也洗净我们一切的罪」（约一1:7）。「一切的罪」，也包括一个累了、发了脾气、心里有怨气的主日学老师的罪；羔羊的血也是为她流的。她可以把这些都带到他面前，不必先把自己整理好。

最值得留意的是接下来的那一句：「你必……再说预言」（10:11）。约翰的肚子还在发苦，神并没有说：你太苦了，换别人吧。神说的是「再」。可是在差遣以前，神先把书卷交给他吃。林姊妹也许可以在这个星期六，先不打开教师手册，像一个饿了的人那样，自己读一遍下周要讲的经文，不为备课，只为听神对她说话。她也可以老实地告诉牧师：我很累，需要有人和我一同承担。如果她确实需要休息一段时间，那也不是被神开除；差遣她的那一位，也是喂养她的那一位。

那个离开的孩子，那些听不进去的课，那些她觉得白白讲过的故事，都不在她手中结束。天使向天举起右手，「指着那创造天和天上之物，地和地上之物，海和海中之物，直活到永永远远的，起誓说：『不再有时日了。』」「但在第七位天使吹号发声的时候，神的奥秘就成全了，正如神所传给他仆人众先知的佳音。」（10:6-7）她十二年来讲的挪亚、大卫和耶稣，都是这佳音的一部分。故事不需要由她来讲完。保罗说，神的奥秘「就是基督」（西2:2）：那位为她流血、也仍在呼唤那个离开的孩子的主，就是这奥秘的中心。到了第七号吹响的时候，神的奥秘必要成全。天地的创造主、直活到永远的神，已经为此起了誓。

## The Shape of the Chapter

In chapters 8–9, six of the seven angels have blown their trumpets. Just as the reader waits for the seventh, the narrative pauses. As chapter 7 was set between the sixth and seventh seals, so a section is set between the sixth and seventh trumpets (10:1–11:14): first the little scroll (chapter 10), then the measuring of the temple and the two witnesses (chapter 11). Chapter 9 ended with the rest of mankind who "did not repent" (9:20–21). This interlude answers the question that follows: what are God's people to do in such a world? The answer: first receive God's word, and then prophesy again. The chapter falls into three parts:
- **10:1–4, the mighty angel and the seven thunders:** an angel wrapped in a cloud, with a rainbow over his head, comes down from heaven with a little scroll open in his hand, his right foot on the sea and his left on the land; the seven thunders sound, but John is told, "Seal up... and do not write it down."
- **10:5–7, the angel's oath:** the angel raises his right hand to heaven and swears by the One who lives forever, who created heaven, earth and sea, "that there would be no more delay"; when the seventh angel sounds, "the mystery of God would be fulfilled."
- **10:8–11, eating the scroll:** John takes the little scroll from the angel's hand and eats it; it is sweet as honey in his mouth but bitter in his stomach; then he receives a fresh commission: "You must again prophesy."

Do the trumpets follow the seals in sequence, or retell the same period from another angle? The Introduction and chapters 6 and 8 set out that question, and it is not repeated here. On either reading the place of this interlude is clear: before the last trumpet sounds, God first puts his word into the hands of his servant.

## Going Deeper: 10:1–4 The Mighty Angel and the Seven Thunders

**1. The angel's appearance.** "Then I saw another mighty angel coming down from heaven, wrapped in a cloud, with a rainbow over his head, and his face was like the sun, and his legs like pillars of fire" (10:1). Almost every feature of this angel has appeared before:
- **The cloud:** the Lord Jesus "is coming with the clouds" (1:7); in the Old Testament the cloud is often a sign of God's presence.
- **The rainbow:** around the throne there was a rainbow (4:3), recalling God's covenant after Noah.
- **His face like the sun:** the Son of Man's "face was like the sun shining in full strength" (1:16).
- **Legs like pillars of fire:** in the wilderness the LORD went before Israel "by night in a pillar of fire to give them light" (Exod 13:21).

**2. Who is this angel?** Interpreters hold two main views:
- **Christ himself, or the Old Testament "angel of the LORD":** some interpreters (e.g. Beale) argue that the cloud, the rainbow, the sun and the pillars of fire are features of God and of the Son of Man, so that this angel is probably Christ, or an appearance representing him. Even on this view, "angel" means one who is sent, not that Christ is a created angel; the Son is "as much superior to angels as the name he has inherited is more excellent than theirs" (Heb 1:4).
- **A mighty angel:** many interpreters (e.g. Bauckham, Grant Osborne) take him as an angel. Revelation never calls Christ an angel; "another mighty angel" recalls the "strong angel" of 5:2, and another appears in 18:21; and he swears *by* the living God (10:6), which sounds more like an angel. The glory he bears is reflected from the One who sent him.
Both views agree that he comes with heaven's authority and that the scroll in his hand is God's word.

**3. "His right foot on the sea, and his left foot on the land."** "He had a little scroll open in his hand. And he set his right foot on the sea, and his left foot on the land" (10:2). Sea and land together are the whole world. The angel's message concerns all the earth and declares that all the earth belongs to God. In chapter 13 one beast comes "out of the sea" and another "out of the earth" (13:1, 11); but before either beast appears, God's messenger is already standing on sea and land. Sea and land may look as if they belong to the beast. They never have.

**4. "Like a lion roaring."** "And called out with a loud voice, like a lion roaring" (10:3). The prophet Amos says, "The lion has roared; who will not fear? The Lord GOD has spoken; who can but prophesy?" (Amos 3:8). Hosea too says of the LORD, "he will roar like a lion" (Hos 11:10). The lion's roar is the voice of God speaking, and this chapter ends with John commanded to prophesy again (10:11).

**5. "Seal up what the seven thunders have said."** "When he called out, the seven thunders sounded. And when the seven thunders had sounded, I was about to write, but I heard a voice from heaven saying, 'Seal up what the seven thunders have said, and do not write it down'" (10:3–4). The psalm says "the God of glory thunders" (Ps 29:3); in Scripture thunder is often God's voice. John has always written what he saw (1:11), but this time he is forbidden. Why?
- **Judgments God decided not to carry out:** Bauckham argues that the seven thunders, like the seals and trumpets, were another series of judgments, and that God has them sealed because judgment alone has not brought people to repentance (9:20–21). Instead of adding round upon round of plagues, God turns to another way: the faithful witness of his people (chapter 11).
- **Things God keeps back:** other interpreters take the verse simply to tell us that God has not revealed everything. "The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but the things that are revealed belong to us and to our children forever" (Deut 29:29); Paul too "heard things that cannot be told, which man may not utter" (2 Cor 12:4).
The two views can stand together. Either way, the command to "seal up" stands in contrast to the command at the end of the book: "Do not seal up the words of the prophecy of this book, for the time is near" (22:10; cf. Dan 12:4). What God has not sealed is everything else in this book.

**6. A word to those who love to calculate.** Revelation itself admits that some things were not written down, even by John. Anyone who claims to know what the seven thunders said, or to unlock every secret of this book, has stepped beyond the limits the book sets for itself (see the Introduction on Eastern Lightning). We need not be anxious about what was not written; we are to give ourselves to what was.

> The point: Before the last trumpet sounds, God's messenger already stands on sea and land. God has not told us everything, but what he has revealed is enough for us to hear and keep.

## Going Deeper: 10:5–7 No More Delay

**1. The raised hand and the oath.** "And the angel whom I saw standing on the sea and on the land raised his right hand to heaven and swore by him who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and what is in it, the earth and what is in it, and the sea and what is in it" (10:5–6). The background is the end of Daniel. There someone asks, "How long shall it be till the end of these wonders?" and the man clothed in linen "raised his right hand and his left hand toward heaven and swore by him who lives forever that it would be for a time, times, and half a time, and that... all these things would be finished" (Dan 12:6–7). Daniel heard that there was still a time to wait; John hears that the waiting is over. The angel swears by the Creator: the One who made heaven, earth and sea has both the right and the power to finish what he began.

**2. "No more delay."** The KJV rendered this "there should be time no longer," and some have taken it to mean that time itself will end and give way to a timeless eternity. But many interpreters take the word (*chronos*) here to mean "delay," as most modern English versions (e.g. the ESV) translate it. Revelation uses the same word elsewhere: the Lord says of Jezebel, "I gave her time to repent" (2:21), and the souls under the altar are told "to rest a little longer" (6:11). Those souls had cried out, "How long?" (6:10). Now the angel answers on oath: no more delay. The prophet Habakkuk heard something like it: "If it seems slow, wait for it; it will surely come; it will not delay" (Hab 2:3).

**3. This is not a date.** "No more delay" does not mean the end comes tomorrow, and it gives no year to calculate. The angel says that "in the days of the trumpet call to be sounded by the seventh angel" (10:7) nothing will any longer hold back God's purpose. The Lord Jesus said that of that day or hour "no one knows" (Mark 13:32). What this oath gives a suffering church is not a timetable but a guarantee. Daniel's "time, times, and half a time" reappears in chapters 11–12 (Rev 11:2–3; 12:14). Some interpreters therefore take "no more delay" to mean not that no interval remains, but that the final period God has measured will be postponed no longer; it has begun.

**4. "The mystery of God would be fulfilled."** "But that in the days of the trumpet call to be sounded by the seventh angel, the mystery of God would be fulfilled, just as he announced to his servants the prophets" (10:7).
- **Mystery:** in the New Testament a "mystery" is not a riddle no one can solve but God's purpose once hidden and now revealed: "the mystery hidden for ages and generations but now revealed to his saints" (Col 1:26); "This mystery is that the Gentiles are fellow heirs" (Eph 3:6). Here the mystery is God's whole plan to establish his kingdom over all the earth, including both the judgment of evil and the salvation of the nations. When the seventh trumpet sounds, heaven declares, "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ" (11:15).
- **"Fulfilled":** Revelation uses the same verb (*teleō*) when the witnesses have "finished their testimony" (11:7) and when "the wrath of God is finished" in the last plagues (15:1). Nothing God has purposed stops halfway.
- **"Announced":** the Greek verb means to announce good news (*euangelizō*), the word for "preaching the gospel." Amos says, "For the Lord GOD does nothing without revealing his secret to his servants the prophets" (Amos 3:7). God's ending, judgment included, is good news for his servants, because evil will be removed and his kingdom will come.

> The point: God swears by the heaven and earth he made: no more delay. He has not given us a date, but he has given us a guarantee: the good news he announced to the prophets will be fully accomplished.

## Going Deeper: 10:8–11 Eating the Scroll

**1. "Go, take the scroll."** "Then the voice that I had heard from heaven spoke to me again, saying, 'Go, take the scroll that is open in the hand of the angel who is standing on the sea and on the land'" (10:8). How is this little scroll related to the scroll of chapter 5? Interpreters differ:
- **The same scroll:** Bauckham and Beale both identify it with the chapter 5 scroll: once the Lamb has broken its seals, it is now "open" (10:2), and its real content begins to be disclosed here (see the discussion in chapter 5). And in 10:8 the better manuscripts use the ordinary word *biblion* (hence the ESV's "the scroll"), so the diminutive may carry little weight.
- **A different, smaller scroll:** some interpreters (e.g. David Aune) note the diminutive (*biblaridion*) and take this as a different scroll, whose content is roughly what follows in chapter 11.
On either view, the scroll's content is the prophecy John is about to speak.

**2. Ezekiel ate the scroll.** "So I went to the angel and told him to give me the little scroll. And he said to me, 'Take and eat it; it will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be sweet as honey'" (10:9). The scene almost replays Ezekiel's call. God told Ezekiel, "open your mouth and eat what I give you"; "Eat this scroll, and go, speak to the house of Israel"; and Ezekiel "ate it, and it was in my mouth as sweet as honey" (Ezek 2:8; 3:1, 3). Afterward he says, "I went in bitterness in the heat of my spirit" (Ezek 3:14). Jeremiah too says, "Your words were found, and I ate them, and your words became to me a joy and the delight of my heart" (Jer 15:16). A prophet cannot simply pass God's word along; he must first eat it and take it into himself. The Lord Jesus said, "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that comes from the mouth of God" (Matt 4:4).

**3. First sweet, then bitter.** "And I took the little scroll from the hand of the angel and ate it. It was sweet as honey in my mouth, but when I had eaten it my stomach was made bitter" (10:10). The angel names the bitterness first and then the sweetness (10:9); John experiences the sweetness first and then the bitterness. What do they stand for?
- **Sweet:** God's word itself is good, "sweeter also than honey and drippings of the honeycomb" (Ps 19:10). God's purpose to judge evil, save his people and bring the nations to worship him is all sweet.
- **Bitter:** the scroll's content also includes judgment, and the suffering and death of God's witnesses (11:7–10). Many interpreters take the bitterness to belong not only to those who hear the message but to the messenger himself: he must carry a sweet message into an unrepentant world, and pay a price for it. So it was for Ezekiel and Jeremiah.
So a bitter stomach does not mean John did something wrong, or that God's word had gone bad. Bitterness is a common experience of those who receive God's word faithfully.

**4. "You must again prophesy."** "And I was told, 'You must again prophesy about many peoples and nations and languages and kings'" (10:11).
- **"Must":** this is the necessity God has set, the same "must" as in "the things that must soon take place" (1:1). Prophesying is not a career John chose for himself, but God's commission.
- **"Again":** John was first commissioned in chapter 1 to write to the seven churches (1:11, 19); here, like Ezekiel, he is sent again after eating the scroll: "go to the house of Israel and speak with my words to them" (Ezek 3:4). It is a recommissioning.
- **"Many peoples and nations and languages and kings":** Revelation often uses the fourfold "tribe and language and people and nation" (5:9; 7:9; 13:7); here "tribes" gives way to "kings." Later chapters do speak of the kings of the earth (chapters 17–19), and at the end of the book "the kings of the earth will bring their glory into" the city (21:24). The Greek preposition rendered "about" can also mean "to" or "against." Bauckham stresses that one aim of this prophecy is to bring the nations to repentance (cf. 11:13; 14:6–7; 15:4).

**5. The church today.** John's commission was unique; the book he wrote is the word of God. But the pattern of eating the scroll and prophesying again belongs to the church in every age: first receive God's word, let it be sweet in us and bitter in us, and then bear witness to the nations. In Revelation, "prophesying" is not mainly predicting dates; it is bearing witness to Jesus (19:10).

> The point: God's servants must first eat God's word before they can speak it. It is sweet as honey in the mouth and may turn bitter in the stomach, yet God does not withdraw the commission. He says, "You must again prophesy."

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Take and eat it; it will make your stomach bitter, but in your mouth it will be sweet as honey" (10:9). Sister Lin has taught children's Sunday school at her church for twelve years. In the early years she prepared every Saturday night, and often, halfway through, she was moved herself: so that is what Noah's ark was like; so David waited on God like that in the wilderness. In those days God's word was sweet in her mouth. These last few years have been different. Two of the helping teachers have left, the children are harder and harder to manage, and one parent told her to her face that her lessons were dull. What hurts most is a boy she taught from his earliest years who stopped coming once he started high school. When she met him in the street, he said flatly, "Those were just stories for little kids." Now when she opens her Bible on Saturday night, it is like opening a work manual, and every verse is a task. Last month she shouted at a boy in class and lay awake for hours afterward. She has already decided: she will stop at the end of the year. Perhaps God has finished with her.

John ate the little scroll; it was sweet as honey in his mouth, and his stomach was made bitter (10:10). The bitterness was not because he was unfaithful, nor because God's word had gone bad, but because a sweet message was being carried into an unrepentant world. Ezekiel, after eating the scroll, also went out "in bitterness" (Ezek 3:14). Part of the bitterness in Sister Lin's stomach is that kind of bitterness: tears over the boy who left, prayers for children's hearts with no fruit she can see. That is not failure; it is what those who serve God faithfully often know.

Yet she knows the bitterness is mixed with other things too: resentment toward the parent, the shout at that boy, the sting she felt when someone else's ministry was praised. John wrote in his letter, "the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin" (1 John 1:7). "All sin" includes the sin of a tired Sunday-school teacher who lost her temper and carries resentment; the Lamb's blood was shed for her too. She can bring all of it to him without first putting herself in order.

Most striking is the next sentence: "You must again prophesy" (10:11). John's stomach was still bitter, and God did not say, "You are too bitter; I will find someone else." God said "again." But before the sending, God first gave him the scroll to eat. This Saturday, Sister Lin might leave the teacher's manual closed at first, and, like someone who is hungry, read next week's passage for herself—not to prepare a lesson, but to hear God speak to her. She might also tell her pastor honestly: "I am tired. I need someone to carry this with me." And if she truly needs to rest for a season, that is not God dismissing her. The One who sends her is also the One who feeds her.

The boy who left, the lessons no one took in, the stories she thinks she told in vain, do not end in her hands. The angel swore "by him who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and what is in it, the earth and what is in it, and the sea and what is in it, that there would be no more delay, but that in the days of the trumpet call to be sounded by the seventh angel, the mystery of God would be fulfilled, just as he announced to his servants the prophets" (10:6–7). The Noah, the David and the Jesus she has taught for twelve years are all part of that good news. The story does not have to be finished by her. Paul speaks of "God's mystery, which is Christ" (Col 2:2): the Lord who shed his blood for her, and who is still calling the boy who left, is the heart of it. When the seventh trumpet sounds, the mystery of God will be fulfilled. The Creator of heaven and earth, who lives forever, has sworn it.
