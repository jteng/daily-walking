## 本章脉络

约翰吃了小书卷，领受命令要「再说预言」（10:11）；第11章就是他所说的预言。前半章结束第六号和第七号之间的插曲，讲神的百姓怎样在世上作见证；后半章，第七位天使终于吹号了。全章可以分成四段：
- **11:1-2，丈量圣殿：** 神的殿、祭坛和在殿中礼拜的人要被量；殿外的院子却留下不量，交给外邦人践踏四十二个月。
- **11:3-6，两个见证人传道：** 他们穿着毛衣传道一千二百六十天，有以利亚和摩西那样的权柄。
- **11:7-14，被杀、复活、升天：** 兽杀了他们，尸首倒在大城的街上三天半；然后有生气从神那里进入他们，他们上了天，其余的人归荣耀给天上的神。
- **11:15-19，第七号：** 天上宣告「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国」；神天上的殿开了，现出约柜。

这一章很难解释，本研读在每一处列出主要的读法；不过各种读法都同意：神的见证人看起来被打败了，其实并没有输。

## 深读 11:1-2 丈量圣殿

**1. 「将神的殿和祭坛……都量一量」。** 「起来！将神的殿和祭坛，并在殿中礼拜的人都量一量。只是殿外的院子要留下不用量」（11:1-2）。以西结在异象中看见一个人「手拿麻绳和量度的竿」，量了将来的圣殿（结40:3）；撒迦利亚看见有人「要去量耶路撒冷」，耶和华应许：「我要作耶路撒冷四围的火城」（亚2:2、5）。在这两处，丈量都表示神要保守他的居所（旧约有时也用丈量表示审判，如哀2:8，但这里多数人认为是保守）。被量的属于神，受他保护；没有量的却要被践踏。

**2. 这殿指的是什么？** 释经者主要有三种看法：
- **主后70年以前耶路撒冷的圣殿：** 一些过去派学者（例如肯尼斯·詹特里）认为，这里说的是罗马军队围困下的耶路撒冷，外院和圣城被践踏，就是主后66-70年的犹太战争。
- **将来重建的圣殿：** 时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，这是将来大灾难时期在耶路撒冷重建的圣殿。
- **神的百姓，就是教会：** 许多学者（例如毕尔、包衡）认为这是象征的说法。新约称教会为神的殿（林前3:16；弗2:21），启示录也应许得胜的人要在「神殿中作柱子」（3:12）。照这种读法，被量的内殿表示神保守他百姓的属灵生命，没有人能夺去；被践踏的外院表示他们在世上仍要受逼迫。
本研读倾向第三种读法，因为启示录常用旧约的殿、坛和城来指神的百姓。这符合导读所说的综合读法；而且许多持这种读法的人（如毕尔、威廉·亨德里克森）也认为，兽对见证人最后的攻击（11:7）指向基督再来以前最后一次、也更猛烈的逼迫。不过三种看法都肯定：神认得、保守属于他的人，即使他们周围的一切都被践踏。

> 要点：神丈量他的殿，表示他认得、保守属于他的人。他们在世上可能被践踏，但没有人能把他们从神那里夺去。

## 深读 11:2-3 四十二个月和一千二百六十天

**1. 同一段时间的三种说法。** 外邦人践踏圣城「四十二个月」（11:2），兽也「任意而行四十二个月」（13:5）；两个见证人传道「一千二百六十天」（11:3），妇人在旷野也被养活一千二百六十天（12:6），这段日子又称为「一载二载半载」（12:14）。三种说法都是三年半。背景是但以理书：圣民要被交在那角的手中「一载、二载、半载」（但7:25；参12:7）。

**2. 怎样理解这段时间？** 主要有四种读法：
- **将来末了的一段日子：** 时代论的学者（例如华尔伏）认为，这是但以理书所说第七十个七的后一半，就是将来大灾难中字面的三年半；另一些未来派学者（例如乔治·赖德）也认为这是基督再来以前末了的一段苦难时期，但长度可能是象征的。
- **一世纪的事件：** 过去派（例如詹特里）认为，这大约是罗马攻打犹太的那几年。
- **一日顶一年：** 许多历史派的释经者按「一日顶一年」的原则（民14:34；结4:6），把一千二百六十天算作一千二百六十年，常把这段时间和中世纪教廷的权势连在一起。十九世纪英国的埃利奥特（E. B. Elliott）是代表之一。
- **整个教会时代的象征：** 许多学者（例如毕尔、亨德里克森）认为，这象征从基督第一次来到他再来之间的整个时期。三年半是「七」的一半：邪恶有权势，却不完全，而且有结束的时候。
本研读倾向最后一种读法，但承认这是个难题。无论哪一种读法，都可以留意一个对比：见证人传道一千二百六十天，他们的仇敌却只得意了「三天半」（11:9）。这些数字是要告诉受苦的教会，苦难的日子是神所量定的，不是叫我们推算日期（可13:32；参看导读）。

> 要点：四十二个月、一千二百六十天、一载二载半载，指的是神所量定、有限的一段日子。邪恶的权势有它的期限，神的见证却不会被消灭。

## 深读 11:3-6 两个见证人

**1. 「我那两个见证人」。** 「我要使我那两个见证人，穿着毛衣，传道一千二百六十天。」（11:3）律法规定，「总要凭两三个人的口作见证才可定案」（申19:15）；两个见证人，表示他们的见证是确实的。和合本的「毛衣」不是今天的毛线衣，而是用兽毛织成的粗布衣，就是麻衣，是先知和悔改的人所穿的（参王下1:8；拿3:5）。他们穿这样的衣服，表示他们的信息是呼召人悔改。

**2. 「两棵橄榄树，两个灯台」。** 「他们就是那两棵橄榄树，两个灯台，立在世界之主面前的。」（11:4）撒迦利亚看见一个金灯台，旁边「有两棵橄榄树」（亚4:3），就是「两个受膏者站在普天下主的旁边」（亚4:14），通常认为是指当时的大祭司约书亚和省长所罗巴伯。神在那里说：「不是倚靠势力，不是倚靠才能，乃是倚靠我的灵方能成事。」（亚4:6）约翰在这里改了一处：撒迦利亚只看见一个灯台，这里却有两个。在启示录里，「七灯台就是七个教会」（1:20）。

**3. 以利亚和摩西的权柄。** 他们口中出火，又有权柄「叫天闭塞不下雨」「叫水变为血」（11:5-6）。以利亚曾叫天不下雨（王上17:1），也曾求火从天降下（王下1:10）；摩西和亚伦叫「河里的水都变作血了」（出7:20）。不过这里的火是「从他们口中出来」的。神对耶利米说：「我必使我的话在你口中为火」（耶5:14）。许多释经者认为，这是说他们的话语带着神审判的能力。雅各和约翰曾想求「火从天上降下来」烧灭不接待主的撒玛利亚人，「耶稣转身责备两个门徒」（路9:54-55）。这段经文不能拿来支持基督徒用暴力对付反对的人。

**4. 两个见证人是谁？** 释经者主要有三种看法：
- **将来两位个别的先知：** 时代论的学者（例如华尔伏）认为，他们是将来大灾难时期在耶路撒冷传道的两个人。有人认为就是摩西和以利亚，因为他们的权柄相同，又一同在登山变像时显现（太17:3）；早期教会的一些作者（如希坡律陀）则认为是以诺和以利亚，因为这两人都没有经过死。
- **一世纪的见证人：** 一些过去派的释经者（如大卫·奇尔顿）认为，这是耶路撒冷被毁以前，教会在那城中向以色列所作的先知性见证。
- **作见证的教会：** 许多学者（例如毕尔、包衡）认为，两个见证人代表整个教会在世上所作的先知性见证。理由是：他们是「两个灯台」，而灯台就是教会（1:20）；他们的尸首被「各民、各族、各方、各国」的人观看（11:9），可见他们不只在一个地方；他们的一生也和他们的主一样：作见证、被杀、复活、升天。
本研读倾向最后一种读法。照这种读法，教会继承了摩西和以利亚的先知职分，也继承了他们所受的苦。

> 要点：神的见证人不是倚靠势力，也不是倚靠才能，而是倚靠神的灵作见证。他们的能力不是刀剑，而是神所赐的话。

## 深读 11:7-14 被杀、复活、升天

**1. 「那从无底坑里上来的兽」。** 「他们作完见证的时候，那从无底坑里上来的兽必与他们交战，并且得胜，把他们杀了。」（11:7）这是启示录第一次提到「兽」（参第13、17章），背景是但以理书：「这角与圣民争战，胜了他们」（但7:21）。请留意：兽要等到「他们作完见证的时候」才能杀他们。在神所定的日子满了以前，没有人能叫他们闭口。

**2. 「大城」。** 「他们的尸首就倒在大城里的街上；这城按着灵意叫所多玛，又叫埃及，就是他们的主钉十字架之处。」（11:8）这城是哪里？
- **耶路撒冷：** 过去派和时代论的学者（例如詹特里、华尔伏）都这样认为，因为「他们的主钉十字架之处」最直接的意思就是耶路撒冷。
- **代表世界的大城：** 许多学者（例如毕尔）认为，「按着灵意」表示这是象征的说法；启示录其他地方的「大城」多半指巴比伦（16:19；17:18）。所多玛代表败坏，埃及代表压迫神的百姓，加上钉主的地方，这城代表历世历代一切抵挡神的城。
无论哪一种读法，这句话都提醒我们：见证人所走的路，就是他们的主走过的路（约15:20）。

**3. 不许埋葬，互送礼物。** 「不许把尸首放在坟墓里……互相馈送礼物」（11:9-10）。在古代，不许埋葬是极大的羞辱（参诗79:2-3）；华人讲「入土为安」，更能体会这种羞辱。世人互送礼物，庆祝自己摆脱了神的先知。见证人没有害人；可是神的话本身就叫不愿悔改的人「受痛苦」。

**4. 「有生气从神那里进入他们里面」。** 「过了这三天半，有生气从神那里进入他们里面，他们就站起来；看见他们的人甚是害怕。」（11:11）这里让人想起以西结书第37章：「气息就进入骸骨，骸骨便活了，并且站起来」（结37:10）。见证人复活，不是靠自己的力量，也不是因为世人回心转意，而是因为生气「从神那里」来。

**5. 「上到这里来」。** 「他们就驾着云上了天，他们的仇敌也看见了。」（11:12）他们的主也是这样：死了，复活，然后「有一朵云彩把他接去」（徒1:9）。仇敌「也看见了」：被嘲笑、被弃在街上的，正是神所认可的人。

**6. 「其余的都恐惧，归荣耀给天上的神」。** 「城就倒塌了十分之一，因地震而死的有七千人；其余的都恐惧，归荣耀给天上的神。」（11:13）后半句怎样理解？
- **真实的悔改：** 包衡认为，这是全书的一个转折。号灾杀了三分之一的人，其余的「仍旧不悔改」（9:20）；这里只有十分之一的城倒塌，其余的却「归荣耀给天上的神」。以利亚的时候，神只留下七千人（王上19:18）；这里正好相反，七千人死了，其余的都归荣耀给神。审判做不到的事，忠心的见证做到了。启示录别处说「将荣耀归给他」，也正是悔改的意思（14:7；参16:9）。
- **惧怕之下的承认：** 另一些释经者认为，这只是在灾难面前被迫承认神的大能，不一定是得救的信心。
无论哪一种看法，神的见证人所传的，终必叫仇敌也承认天上的神。

> 要点：神的见证人看起来被打败了，被嘲笑，被弃在街上；但他们走的是他们的主走过的路，所以也要像主一样，从死里站起来。

## 深读 11:15-19 第七号

**1. 「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国」。** 「第七位天使吹号，天上就有大声音说：世上的国成了我主和主基督的国；他要作王，直到永永远远。」（11:15）
- **单数的「国」：** 原文的「世上的国」是单数：一直抵挡神的那个国度，现在易主了。
- **「我主和主基督」：** 背景是诗篇第2篇：「世上的君王一齐起来……要敌挡耶和华并他的受膏者」（诗2:2）。「基督」就是受膏者。
- **「他要作王」：** 前面说的是「我主和主基督」两位，这里却用单数的「他」：父与子同坐一个宝座，一同掌权（参第5章的讨论）。
- **「成了」：** 原文是不定过去时，天上把将来的事当作已经成就的事来宣告。这就是10:7所说的「神的奥秘就成全了」。

**2. 「昔在、今在的主神」。** 二十四位长老敬拜说：「昔在、今在的主神－全能者啊，我们感谢你！因你执掌大权作王了。」（11:16-17）从前的称呼是「昔在、今在、以后永在的全能者」（1:8；参4:8），这里却只说「昔在、今在」。许多释经者（如包衡）指出，原因很可能是：那要来的一位，现在已经来了（参16:5）。

**3. 「外邦发怒，你的忿怒也临到了」。** 这一节（11:18）几乎是全书后半部的目录：列国的愤怒（诗2:1；第12-19章），审判死人（20:11-15），神的仆人「连大带小」得赏赐（参22:12），以及神败坏那些败坏他所造之世界的人。神不忘记任何一个默默无闻的仆人；他的审判也是为了救赎受造的世界，不是要毁掉它。

**4. 约柜现出来。** 「当时，神天上的殿开了，在他殿中现出他的约柜。随后有闪电、声音、雷轰、地震、大雹。」（11:19）约柜是神与以色列立约的记号。第一圣殿被巴比伦毁灭以后（主前586年），约柜就不见了，耶利米甚至说，人必「不再提说耶和华的约柜」（耶3:16）。现在约柜在天上显现：神没有忘记他的约。闪电、雷轰、地震让人想起西奈山（出19:16）；同样的记号先从宝座发出（4:5），又出现在第七印、第七号、第七碗的末了（8:5；11:19；16:18-21），一次比一次强烈。许多释经者认为，这表示三组「七」都走到了同一个终点（参看导读和第6、8章的讨论）。

> 要点：第七号吹响时，天上宣告的不是一个新的计划，而是神一直以来的计划成就了：世上的国成了我主和主基督的国。

## 默想

**默想：** 「住在地上的人就为他们欢喜快乐，互相馈送礼物」（11:10）。小吴在一家工程公司做质检。去年他拒绝在一份不合格的检测报告上签字，项目因此拖了两个星期，经理在会上点了他的名。从那以后，同事们常拿他开玩笑：「信耶稣的又来了」「你的神给你发工资吗？」年底评优他落选了，饭桌上有人举杯说：「敬我们公司最诚实的人。」大家都笑了。他所在的那间小教会经过一场纷争，现在只剩八九个人，镇上的人说「那个教会早就散了」。小吴有时觉得，自己和教会的见证，就像倒在大街上的尸首，旁边的人正在庆祝。他知道，这比不上那些为主坐监、丢了工作甚至丢了性命的弟兄姊妹；可是被人当成笑话的滋味，也是真的。

可是小吴心里知道，他并不总是那个诚实的人。同事讲下流的笑话，他也跟着笑过；年会上有人问他是不是信教的，他含糊地说「不算是」；在那份报告以前，他也签过一两份明知有问题的报告。教会闹纷争的时候，他也说过伤人的话。所以每次被人嘲笑，他都暗暗怀疑：也许他的见证不是被别人弄死的，而是他自己弄坏的。

约翰说，两个见证人的尸首倒在「他们的主钉十字架之处」（11:8）。他们不是第一批在那里被嘲笑的人。主被挂在十字架上的时候，祭司长和文士也戏弄他：「他救了别人，不能救自己。」（可15:31）可是他正是在那里，为那些嘲笑他的人舍命，也为那些在嘲笑面前沉默、跟着笑、含糊其辞的门徒舍命。小吴不必把自己的胆怯和妥协藏起来。主的血足以遮盖这一切，所以他可以在这位被钉的主面前认罪。正因为他不必再保住自己的记录，从前签过的那一两份报告，他也可以照实告诉公司和该知道的人，设法补救，因为那关乎别人的安全。这样做也许要付代价，但他不是要赚回清白，而是已经蒙了赦免，不再需要遮掩什么。见证人站起来，不是因为他们的记录完美，而是因为「有生气从神那里进入他们里面」（11:11）。

那间只剩八九个人的教会也是一样。以西结看见的那些骸骨，不是被别人害死的英雄，而是因自己的罪被掳的百姓；他们自己说：「我们的骨头枯干了，我们的指望失去了」（结37:11）。神的气息却进入了他们里面。这间教会的伤，有一些是外面的人给的，也有一些是自己吵出来的；弟兄姊妹需要的不只是安慰，也需要彼此认罪、和好。可是叫这间教会站起来的，仍旧是「从神那里」来的生气。世人庆祝了「三天半」，见证人却传道了「一千二百六十天」（11:3、9）。小吴不需要为了挽回面子去反击那些同事；他可以继续诚实地做他的工作，盼望那些举杯的人有一天也像那「其余的」人一样，「归荣耀给天上的神」（11:13）。

主啊，我的见证常常软弱：有时被人的嘲笑压倒，有时被我自己弄坏。求你把生气吹进我里面，也吹进我们那间小小的教会。当别人庆祝我们的失败时，叫我记得你在那城里被钉，又从死里复活。我等候那一天，天上的大声音要说：「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国；他要作王，直到永永远远。」（11:15）

## The Shape of the Chapter

John has eaten the little scroll and been told to "prophesy again" (10:11); chapter 11 is that prophecy. The first half closes the interlude between the sixth and seventh trumpets, showing how God's people bear witness in the world; in the second half the seventh angel at last blows his trumpet. The chapter falls into four parts:
- **11:1–2, measuring the temple:** the temple of God, the altar and those who worship there are to be measured; the outer court is left out, given over to the nations to trample for forty-two months.
- **11:3–6, the two witnesses prophesy:** clothed in sackcloth, they prophesy for 1,260 days, with powers like those of Elijah and Moses.
- **11:7–14, killed, raised, taken up:** the beast kills them, and their bodies lie in the street of the great city for three and a half days; then a breath of life from God enters them, they go up to heaven, and the rest give glory to the God of heaven.
- **11:15–19, the seventh trumpet:** heaven announces, "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ"; God's temple in heaven is opened and the ark of his covenant is seen.

This chapter is hard to interpret, and this study sets out the main readings at each point; but they all agree that God's witnesses look defeated, and they are not.

## Going Deeper: 11:1–2 Measuring the Temple

**1. "Measure the temple of God and the altar."** "Rise and measure the temple of God and the altar and those who worship there, but do not measure the court outside the temple" (11:1–2). In a vision Ezekiel saw a man "with a linen cord and a measuring reed in his hand," who measured the temple to come (Ezek 40:3); Zechariah saw a man going "to measure Jerusalem," and the LORD promised, "I will be to her a wall of fire all around" (Zech 2:2, 5). In both places measuring signals that God will protect his dwelling (measuring can also mark judgment, e.g. Lam 2:8, but most take it here as protection). What is measured belongs to God and is under his protection; what is not measured is trampled.

**2. What is this temple?** Interpreters hold three main views:
- **The Jerusalem temple before AD 70:** some preterist scholars (e.g. Kenneth Gentry) take this as Jerusalem under Roman siege, with the trampling of the outer court and the holy city referring to the Jewish War of AD 66–70.
- **A future rebuilt temple:** dispensational scholars (e.g. John Walvoord) take it as a temple rebuilt in Jerusalem during the future tribulation.
- **God's people, the church:** many scholars (e.g. Beale, Bauckham) read it symbolically. The New Testament calls the church God's temple (1 Cor 3:16; Eph 2:21), and Revelation promises the one who conquers that he will be "a pillar in the temple of my God" (3:12). On this reading the measured inner temple stands for God's keeping of his people's spiritual life, which no one can take away, while the trampled outer court stands for the persecution they still face in the world.
This study leans toward the third reading, since Revelation often uses the Old Testament temple, altar and city for God's people. This fits the eclectic reading set out in the Introduction, and many who hold this view (e.g. Beale, William Hendriksen) also see in the beast's final attack (11:7) a last, intensified persecution before Christ returns. But all three views see that God knows and keeps those who belong to him, even when everything around them is trampled.

> The point: God measures his temple: he knows and keeps those who belong to him. They may be trampled in the world, but no one can take them from God.

## Going Deeper: 11:2–3 Forty-Two Months and 1,260 Days

**1. Three ways of naming one period.** The nations trample the holy city "for forty-two months" (11:2), and the beast exercises authority "for forty-two months" (13:5); the two witnesses prophesy "for 1,260 days" (11:3), and the woman is nourished in the wilderness for 1,260 days (12:6), also called "a time, and times, and half a time" (12:14). All three come to three and a half years. The background is Daniel: the saints "shall be given into his hand for a time, times, and half a time" (Dan 7:25; cf. 12:7).

**2. How should the period be understood?** There are four main readings:
- **A future final period:** dispensational scholars (e.g. Walvoord) take it as the second half of Daniel's seventieth week, a literal three and a half years within the future tribulation; other futurists (e.g. George Ladd) also see a final period of tribulation before Christ's return, though its length may be symbolic.
- **First-century events:** preterists (e.g. Gentry) take it as roughly the years of Rome's war against Judea.
- **A day for a year:** many historicist interpreters apply the "day for a year" principle (Num 14:34; Ezek 4:6) to read 1,260 days as 1,260 years, often linked with the power of the medieval papacy; the nineteenth-century English writer E. B. Elliott is one example.
- **A symbol of the whole church age:** many scholars (e.g. Beale, Hendriksen) take it as a symbol of the whole period between Christ's first coming and his return. Three and a half is half of seven: evil has power, but not complete power, and its time has an end.
This study leans toward the last reading, while granting that the question is a hard one. On any reading, notice the contrast: the witnesses prophesy for 1,260 days, while their enemies triumph for only "three and a half days" (11:9). These numbers tell a suffering church that its days of suffering are measured by God, not to set us calculating dates (Mark 13:32; see the Introduction).

> The point: Forty-two months, 1,260 days, and a time, times and half a time name a limited period measured by God. Evil's power has its term; God's witness will not be wiped out.

## Going Deeper: 11:3–6 The Two Witnesses

**1. "My two witnesses."** "And I will grant authority to my two witnesses, and they will prophesy for 1,260 days, clothed in sackcloth" (11:3). The law required that "only on the evidence of two witnesses or of three witnesses shall a charge be established" (Deut 19:15); two witnesses mean their testimony is sure. Sackcloth was the coarse clothing of prophets and penitents; their message is a call to repentance.

**2. "The two olive trees and the two lampstands."** "These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth" (11:4). Zechariah saw a golden lampstand with "two olive trees by it" (Zech 4:3), "the two anointed ones who stand by the Lord of the whole earth" (Zech 4:14), usually taken to be Joshua the high priest and Zerubbabel the governor. There God says, "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD of hosts" (Zech 4:6). But John makes one change: Zechariah has one lampstand; here there are two. And in Revelation "the seven lampstands are the seven churches" (1:20).

**3. The powers of Elijah and Moses.** Fire comes from their mouth, and they have power "to shut the sky" and "over the waters to turn them into blood" (11:5–6). Elijah shut the sky (1 Kgs 17:1) and called down fire from heaven (2 Kgs 1:10); through Moses and Aaron "all the water in the Nile turned into blood" (Exod 7:20). But here the fire "pours from their mouth." God told Jeremiah, "I am making my words in your mouth a fire" (Jer 5:14). Many interpreters take this to mean that their words carry the power of God's judgment. James and John once wanted to "tell fire to come down from heaven" on Samaritans who would not receive the Lord, "but he turned and rebuked them" (Luke 9:54–55). This passage gives Christians no warrant for violence against those who oppose them.

**4. Who are the two witnesses?** Interpreters hold three main views:
- **Two individual prophets in the future:** dispensational scholars (e.g. Walvoord) take them as two individuals who will prophesy in Jerusalem during the future tribulation. Some identify them as Moses and Elijah, since their powers match and they appeared together at the transfiguration (Matt 17:3); some early church writers (e.g. Hippolytus) instead named Enoch and Elijah, since neither passed through death.
- **First-century witnesses:** some preterist interpreters (e.g. David Chilton) take them as the church's prophetic witness to Israel in Jerusalem before its fall.
- **The witnessing church:** many scholars (e.g. Beale, Bauckham) take the two witnesses as the prophetic witness of the whole church in the world. The reasons: they are "two lampstands," and the lampstands are churches (1:20); their bodies are watched by people "from the peoples and tribes and languages and nations" (11:9), so they are not in one place only; and their story follows their Lord's: witness, death, resurrection and ascension.
This study leans toward the last reading. On this reading the church inherits the prophetic office of Moses and Elijah, and their sufferings too.

> The point: God's witnesses bear witness not by might nor by power but by God's Spirit. Their power is not a sword but the word God has given them.

## Going Deeper: 11:7–14 Killed, Raised, Taken Up

**1. "The beast that rises from the bottomless pit."** "And when they have finished their testimony, the beast that rises from the bottomless pit will make war on them and conquer them and kill them" (11:7). This is Revelation's first mention of "the beast" (see chapters 13 and 17), with Daniel in the background: "this horn made war with the saints and prevailed over them" (Dan 7:21). Notice that the beast can kill them only "when they have finished their testimony." Until the days God has set are complete, no one can silence them.

**2. "The great city."** "And their dead bodies will lie in the street of the great city that symbolically is called Sodom and Egypt, where their Lord was crucified" (11:8). Which city is this?
- **Jerusalem:** preterist and dispensational scholars (e.g. Gentry, Walvoord) alike take it so, since "where their Lord was crucified" most directly means Jerusalem.
- **The great city that stands for the world:** many scholars (e.g. Beale) take "symbolically" (literally "spiritually") to mark the language as symbolic; elsewhere in Revelation "the great city" is usually Babylon (16:19; 17:18). Sodom stands for corruption, Egypt for the oppression of God's people, and with the place where the Lord was crucified, this city gathers into one every city that has opposed God in every age.
On either reading, the phrase reminds us that the witnesses walk the road their Lord walked (John 15:20).

**3. No burial, and presents exchanged.** They "refuse to let them be placed in a tomb... and exchange presents" (11:9–10). In the ancient world, refusing burial was the deepest disgrace (cf. Ps 79:2–3). The world exchanges presents to celebrate its release from God's prophets. The witnesses harmed no one; but to those who will not repent, God's word is itself "a torment."

**4. "A breath of life from God entered them."** "But after the three and a half days a breath of life from God entered them, and they stood up on their feet, and great fear fell on those who saw them" (11:11). This almost echoes Ezekiel 37: "the breath came into them, and they lived and stood on their feet" (Ezek 37:10). The witnesses rise not by their own strength, nor because the world has changed its mind, but because the breath of life comes "from God."

**5. "Come up here!"** "And they went up to heaven in a cloud, and their enemies watched them" (11:12). So it was with their Lord: he died, rose, and "a cloud took him out of their sight" (Acts 1:9). "Their enemies watched": those mocked and left in the street are the ones God approves.

**6. "The rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven."** "A tenth of the city fell. Seven thousand people were killed in the earthquake, and the rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven" (11:13). How should the second half be understood?
- **Real repentance:** Bauckham sees a turning point of the whole book here. The trumpet plagues killed a third of mankind, and the rest "did not repent" (9:20); here only a tenth of the city falls, and the rest "gave glory to the God of heaven." In Elijah's day God kept only seven thousand (1 Kgs 19:18); here it is the reverse: seven thousand die and the rest give God glory. What judgment could not do, faithful witness did. Elsewhere in Revelation "give him glory" is the language of repentance (14:7; cf. 16:9).
- **Acknowledgment under fear:** other interpreters take it as a forced acknowledgment of God's power in the face of catastrophe, not necessarily saving faith.
On either view, the witness of God's people at last brings even their enemies to acknowledge the God of heaven.

> The point: God's witnesses look defeated, mocked and left in the street; but they walk the road their Lord walked, and so, like him, they will stand up from death.

## Going Deeper: 11:15–19 The Seventh Trumpet

**1. "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ."** "Then the seventh angel blew his trumpet, and there were loud voices in heaven, saying, 'The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever and ever'" (11:15).
- **A singular "kingdom":** "the kingdom of the world" is singular: the one realm that has always opposed God has now changed hands.
- **"Our Lord and... his Christ":** the background is Psalm 2: "The kings of the earth set themselves... against the LORD and against his anointed" (Ps 2:2). "Christ" means the anointed one.
- **"He shall reign":** two are named, yet "he" is singular: the Father and the Son share one throne and reign as one (see chapter 5).
- **"Has become":** an aorist; heaven announces the future as already accomplished. This is what 10:7 called "the mystery of God would be fulfilled."

**2. "Lord God Almighty, who is and who was."** The twenty-four elders worship, saying, "We give thanks to you, Lord God Almighty, who is and who was, for you have taken your great power and begun to reign" (11:16–17). The earlier title was "who is and who was and who is to come, the Almighty" (1:8; cf. 4:8), but here it is only "who is and who was." Many interpreters (e.g. Bauckham) point out the likely reason: the One who was to come has now come (cf. 16:5).

**3. "The nations raged, but your wrath came."** This verse (11:18) is almost a table of contents for the second half of the book: the nations' rage (Ps 2:1; chapters 12–19), the judgment of the dead (20:11–15), the rewarding of God's servants "both small and great" (cf. 22:12), and God's destroying of those who ruin the world he made. God forgets no obscure servant; and his judgment serves the redemption of creation, not its destruction.

**4. The ark is seen.** "Then God's temple in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant was seen within his temple. There were flashes of lightning, rumblings, peals of thunder, an earthquake, and heavy hail" (11:19). The ark was the sign of God's covenant with Israel. After Babylon destroyed the first temple (586 BC) the ark disappeared, and Jeremiah even said that people "shall no more say, 'The ark of the covenant of the LORD'" (Jer 3:16). Now the ark appears in heaven: God has not forgotten his covenant. The lightning, thunder and earthquake recall Sinai (Exod 19:16); the same signs first come from the throne (4:5) and then at the close of the seventh seal, the seventh trumpet and the seventh bowl (8:5; 11:19; 16:18–21), each time more intensely. Many interpreters take this to show that the three series of seven reach the same end (see the Introduction and chapters 6 and 8).

> The point: When the seventh trumpet sounds, heaven announces not a new plan but the fulfillment of God's plan all along: the kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And those who dwell on the earth will rejoice over them and make merry and exchange presents" (11:10). Xiao Wu works in quality inspection for an engineering firm. Last year he refused to sign a test report on a failed component; the project was delayed two weeks, and the manager named him in a meeting. Since then his colleagues often make fun of him: "Here comes the Jesus believer." "Does your God pay your salary?" At the year-end awards he was passed over, and at the dinner someone raised a glass: "To the most honest man in our company!" Everyone laughed. His small church went through a quarrel and now has only eight or nine left; people in town say, "That church broke up long ago." Sometimes Xiao Wu feels that his witness and his church's are like bodies lying in the street while the people around them celebrate. He knows this is nothing beside brothers and sisters who have been imprisoned, or lost their jobs or even their lives, for Christ; but the taste of being made a joke is real too.

But Xiao Wu knows he has not always been the honest one. When colleagues told dirty jokes, he laughed along; at the annual party, when someone asked whether he was religious, he mumbled, "Not really"; before that report there were one or two others he knew were wrong and signed anyway. When the church quarreled, he said hurtful things too. So whenever he is mocked, he half suspects that his witness was not killed by others at all, but spoiled by himself.

John says that the bodies of the two witnesses lie "where their Lord was crucified" (11:8). They were not the first to be mocked there. When the Lord hung on the cross, the chief priests and scribes mocked him: "He saved others; he cannot save himself" (Mark 15:31). Yet it was there that he gave his life for those who mocked him, and for the disciples who fell silent before mockery, laughed along and gave evasive answers. Xiao Wu need not hide his cowardice and compromises. The Lord's blood is enough to cover it all, so he can confess them before the crucified Lord. And because he no longer has to protect his record, he can tell the company, and whoever needs to know, about the earlier reports and try to put them right, since others' safety depends on them. It may cost him, but he is not earning back a clean record; he is already forgiven and has nothing left to hide. The witnesses stand up not because their record was perfect but because "a breath of life from God entered them" (11:11).

So it is with the church of eight or nine. The bones Ezekiel saw were not heroes killed by others but a people exiled for their own sin, who said, "Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost" (Ezek 37:11). Yet God's breath entered them. Some of this church's wounds came from outside, and some it quarreled into itself; its members need not only comfort but confession and reconciliation with one another. Yet what will raise this church is still the breath of life "from God." The world celebrated for "three and a half days," while the witnesses prophesied for "1,260 days" (11:3, 9). Xiao Wu does not need to strike back at his colleagues to save face; he can go on doing his work honestly, hoping that one day those who raised their glasses will, like "the rest," give "glory to the God of heaven" (11:13).

Lord, my witness is often weak: sometimes crushed by others' mockery, sometimes spoiled by me. Breathe your breath of life into me, and into our small church. When others celebrate our failure, help me remember that you were crucified in that city and rose from the dead. I wait for the day when the loud voices of heaven will say, "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever and ever" (11:15).
