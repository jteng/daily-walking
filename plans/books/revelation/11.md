## 本章脉络

约翰吃了小书卷，领受命令要「再说预言」（10:11）；第11章就是他所说的预言。前半章结束第六号和第七号之间的插曲，讲神的百姓怎样在世上作见证；后半章，第七位天使终于吹号了。全章可以分成五段：
- **11:1-2，丈量圣殿：** 神的殿、祭坛和在殿中礼拜的人要被量；殿外的院子却留下不量，交给外邦人践踏四十二个月。
- **11:3-6，两个见证人传道：** 他们穿着毛衣传道一千二百六十天，有以利亚和摩西那样的权柄。
- **11:7-10，见证人被杀：** 从无底坑上来的兽杀了他们；他们的尸首倒在大城的街上三天半，住在地上的人互相馈送礼物。
- **11:11-14，见证人复活升天：** 有生气从神那里进入他们，他们驾着云上了天；其余的人归荣耀给天上的神。
- **11:15-19，第七号：** 天上宣告「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国」；神天上的殿开了，现出约柜。

这一章是启示录中最难解释的经文之一。本研读在每一处列出主要的读法；不过各种读法都同意一点：神的见证人看起来被打败了，却并没有失败。

## 深读 11:1-2 丈量圣殿

**1. 「将神的殿和祭坛……都量一量」。** 「有一根苇子赐给我，当作量度的杖；且有话说：『起来！将神的殿和祭坛，并在殿中礼拜的人都量一量。只是殿外的院子要留下不用量，因为这是给了外邦人的；他们要践踏圣城四十二个月。』」（11:1-2）以西结在异象中看见一个人「手拿麻绳和量度的竿」，量了将来的圣殿（结40:3）；撒迦利亚看见有人「要去量耶路撒冷」，耶和华应许：「我要作耶路撒冷四围的火城」（亚2:2、5）。在这两处，丈量都表示神要保守他的居所。被量的属于神，受他保护；没有量的却要被践踏，正如主耶稣所说：「耶路撒冷要被外邦人践踏，直到外邦人的日期满了」（路21:24）。

**2. 这殿指的是什么？** 释经者主要有三种看法：
- **主后70年以前耶路撒冷的圣殿：** 一些过去派学者（例如肯尼斯·詹特里）认为，这里说的是罗马军队围困下的耶路撒冷，外院和圣城被践踏，就是主后66-70年的犹太战争。他们也用这段经文支持较早的写作年代（参看导读）。
- **将来重建的圣殿：** 时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，这是将来大灾难时期在耶路撒冷重建的圣殿。
- **神的百姓，就是教会：** 许多学者（例如毕尔、包衡）认为这是象征的说法。新约称教会为神的殿（林前3:16；弗2:21），启示录也应许得胜的人要在「神殿中作柱子」（3:12）。照这种读法，被量的内殿表示神保守他百姓的属灵生命，没有人能夺去；被践踏的外院表示他们在世上仍要受逼迫。
本研读倾向第三种读法，因为启示录常用旧约的殿、坛和城来指神的百姓。不过三种看法都看见：神认得、保守属于他的人，即使他们周围的一切都被践踏。

> 要点：神丈量他的殿，表示他认得、保守属于他的人。他们在世上可能被践踏，但没有人能把他们从神那里夺去。

## 深读 11:2-3 四十二个月和一千二百六十天

**1. 同一段时间的三种说法。** 外邦人践踏圣城「四十二个月」（11:2），兽也「任意而行四十二个月」（13:5）；两个见证人传道「一千二百六十天」（11:3），妇人在旷野也被养活一千二百六十天（12:6），又说是「一载二载半载」（12:14）。三种说法都是三年半。背景是但以理书：圣民要交付那角的手「一载、二载、半载」（但7:25；参12:7）；「一七之半」，祭祀要止息（但9:27）。以利亚的时候，也是「天闭塞了三年零六个月」（路4:25）。

**2. 怎样理解这段时间？** 主要有四种读法：
- **将来真实的三年半：** 时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，这是但以理第七十个七的后半，就是将来大灾难中的三年半。
- **一世纪的事件：** 过去派（例如肯尼斯·詹特里）认为，这大约是罗马攻打犹太的那几年。
- **一日顶一年：** 许多历史派的释经者根据「一日顶一年」的原则（民14:34；结4:6），把一千二百六十天算成一千二百六十年，常和中世纪教廷的权势连在一起，十九世纪英国的埃利奥特（E. B. Elliott）就是一个代表。
- **整个教会时代的象征：** 许多学者（例如毕尔、威廉·亨德里克森）认为，这象征从基督第一次来到他再来之间的整个时期。三年半是「七」的一半：邪恶有权势，却不完全，而且有结束的时候。
本研读倾向最后一种读法，但承认这是个难题。无论哪一种读法，都可以留意一个对比：见证人传道一千二百六十天，他们的仇敌却只得意了「三天半」（11:9）。

**3. 不要拿这些数字来推算。** 华人教会中常有人把这些数字和某一年、某件事对在一起，然后宣布末日的日期；东方闪电（自称全能神教会）一类的团体也常借这些经文吓唬人（参看导读）。启示录用这些数字，是要告诉受苦的教会：苦难的日子是神所量定的，有开始，也有结束。这不是叫我们去推算日期（可13:32）。

> 要点：四十二个月、一千二百六十天、一载二载半载，指的是神所量定、有限的一段日子。邪恶的权势有它的期限，神的见证却不会被消灭。

## 深读 11:3-6 两个见证人

**1. 「我那两个见证人」。** 「我要使我那两个见证人，穿着毛衣，传道一千二百六十天。」（11:3）律法规定，「总要凭两三个人的口作见证才可定案」（申19:15）；两个见证人，表示他们的见证是确实的。主耶稣也是两个两个地差遣门徒（路10:1）。毛衣是先知和悔改的人所穿的粗衣，他们的信息是呼召人悔改。

**2. 「两棵橄榄树，两个灯台」。** 「他们就是那两棵橄榄树，两个灯台，立在世界之主面前的。」（11:4）撒迦利亚看见一个金灯台，旁边「有两棵橄榄树」（亚4:3），就是「两个受膏者站在普天下主的旁边」（亚4:14），通常认为是指当时的大祭司约书亚和省长所罗巴伯。神在那里说：「不是倚靠势力，不是倚靠才能，乃是倚靠我的灵方能成事。」（亚4:6）约翰却有一个改变：撒迦利亚只有一个灯台，这里有两个。而在启示录里，「七灯台就是七个教会」（1:20）。

**3. 以利亚和摩西的权柄。** 「若有人想要害他们，就有火从他们口中出来，烧灭仇敌……这二人有权柄，在他们传道的日子叫天闭塞不下雨；又有权柄叫水变为血」（11:5-6）。以利亚曾叫天不下雨（王上17:1），也曾求火从天降下（王下1:10）；摩西和亚伦叫「河里的水都变作血了」（出7:20）。不过这里的火是「从他们口中出来」的。神对耶利米说：「我必使我的话在你口中为火」（耶5:14）。许多释经者认为，这是说他们的话语带着神审判的能力。雅各和约翰曾想求「火从天上降下来」烧灭不接待主的撒玛利亚人，「耶稣转身责备两个门徒」（路9:54-55）。这段经文不能拿来支持基督徒用暴力对付反对的人。

**4. 两个见证人是谁？** 释经者主要有三种看法：
- **将来两位真实的先知：** 时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，他们是将来大灾难时期在耶路撒冷传道的两个人。有人认为就是摩西和以利亚，因为他们的权柄相同，又一同在登山变像时显现（太17:3），而且神应许要差遣以利亚来（玛4:5）；早期教会的一些作者（如希坡律陀）则认为是以诺和以利亚，因为这两人都没有经过死。
- **一世纪的见证人：** 一些过去派的学者认为，他们是耶路撒冷被毁以前在那里作见证的人；有人认为是彼得和保罗，也有人认为泛指当时作见证的教会。
- **作见证的教会：** 许多学者（例如毕尔、包衡）认为，两个见证人代表整个教会在世上先知性的见证。理由是：他们是「两个灯台」，而灯台就是教会（1:20）；他们的尸首被「各民、各族、各方、各国」的人观看（11:9），可见他们不只在一个地方；他们的一生也和他们的主一样：作见证、被杀、复活、升天。毕尔还提出，七个教会中只有士每拿和非拉铁非没有受责备，这也许是「两个」灯台的一个原因。
本研读倾向最后一种读法。照这种读法，教会继承了摩西和以利亚的先知职分，也继承了他们所受的苦。

> 要点：神的见证人不是倚靠势力，也不是倚靠才能，而是倚靠神的灵作见证。他们的能力不是刀剑，而是神所赐的话。

## 深读 11:7-14 被杀、复活、升天

**1. 「那从无底坑里上来的兽」。** 「他们作完见证的时候，那从无底坑里上来的兽必与他们交战，并且得胜，把他们杀了。」（11:7）这是启示录第一次提到「兽」，第13章和第17章会再详细描写它。背景是但以理书：「这角与圣民争战，胜了他们」（但7:21）。请留意：兽要等到「他们作完见证的时候」才能杀他们。在神所定的日子满了以前，没有人能叫他们闭口。

**2. 「大城」。** 「他们的尸首就倒在大城里的街上；这城按着灵意叫所多玛，又叫埃及，就是他们的主钉十字架之处。」（11:8）这城是哪里？
- **耶路撒冷：** 过去派和时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）都这样认为，因为「他们的主钉十字架之处」最直接的意思就是耶路撒冷；以赛亚也曾称耶路撒冷的官长为「所多玛的官长」（赛1:10）。
- **代表世界的大城：** 许多学者（例如毕尔、包衡）认为，「按着灵意」表示这是象征的说法；启示录其他地方的「大城」多半指巴比伦（16:19；17:18）。所多玛代表败坏，埃及代表压迫神的百姓，加上钉主的地方，这城集合了历世历代一切抵挡神的城。
无论哪一种读法，这句话都提醒我们：见证人所走的路，就是他们的主走过的路（约15:20）。

**3. 不许埋葬，互送礼物。** 「从各民、各族、各方、各国中，有人观看他们的尸首三天半，又不许把尸首放在坟墓里。住在地上的人就为他们欢喜快乐，互相馈送礼物，因这两位先知曾叫住在地上的人受痛苦。」（11:9-10）在古代，不许埋葬是极大的羞辱（参诗79:2-3）。有释经者指出，「互相馈送礼物」让人想起普珥节的「彼此馈送礼物」（斯9:22），只是完全颠倒过来：那时神的百姓脱离了仇敌，这里世人却庆祝自己摆脱了神的先知。见证人没有害人；可是对不愿悔改的人，神的话本身就叫他们「受痛苦」。

**4. 「有生气从神那里进入他们里面」。** 「过了这三天半，有生气从神那里进入他们里面，他们就站起来；看见他们的人甚是害怕。」（11:11）这几乎是以西结书第37章的回声：「气息就进入骸骨，骸骨便活了，并且站起来」（结37:10）。那些骸骨说：「我们的骨头枯干了，我们的指望失去了」（结37:11）；神却叫它们活过来。见证人复活，不是靠自己的力量，也不是因为世人回心转意，而是因为生气「从神那里」来。

**5. 「上到这里来」。** 「两位先知听见有大声音从天上来，对他们说：『上到这里来。』他们就驾着云上了天，他们的仇敌也看见了。」（11:12）他们的主也是这样：死了，复活，然后「有一朵云彩把他接去」（徒1:9）。时代论的学者通常把这一节读作将来两位先知真实的复活和升天；把两个见证人看作教会的学者，则认为这是神在末了公开为他忠心的百姓伸冤。这一次，仇敌「也看见了」：原来被嘲笑、被弃在街上的，正是神所认可的人。

**6. 「其余的都恐惧，归荣耀给天上的神」。** 「正在那时候，地大震动，城就倒塌了十分之一，因地震而死的有七千人；其余的都恐惧，归荣耀给天上的神。」（11:13）这句话怎样理解？
- **真实的悔改：** 包衡认为，这是全书的一个转折。号灾杀了三分之一的人，其余的「仍旧不悔改」（9:20）；这里只有十分之一的城倒塌，其余的却「归荣耀给天上的神」。以利亚的时候，神只留下七千人（王上19:18）；这里正好相反，七千人死了，其余的都归荣耀给神。审判做不到的事，忠心的见证做到了。启示录别处说「将荣耀归给他」，也正是悔改的意思（14:7；参16:9）。
- **惧怕之下的承认：** 另一些释经者认为，这只是在灾难面前被迫承认神的大能，不一定是得救的信心。
无论哪一种看法，这节经文都显明：神的心意不只是审判，也是叫人回转。

> 要点：神的见证人看起来被打败了，被嘲笑，被弃在街上；但他们走的是他们的主走过的路，所以也要像主一样，从死里站起来。

## 深读 11:15-19 第七号

**1. 「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国」。** 「第七位天使吹号，天上就有大声音说：世上的国成了我主和主基督的国；他要作王，直到永永远远。」（11:15）
- **单数的「国」：** 原文的「世上的国」是单数。世上看起来有许多国，在启示录看来却只有一个一直抵挡神的国度；现在，这个国度易主了。
- **「我主和主基督」：** 背景是诗篇第2篇：「世上的君王一齐起来……要敌挡耶和华并他的受膏者」（诗2:2）。「基督」就是受膏者。但以理也说：「天上的神必另立一国，永不败坏」（但2:44）。
- **「他要作王」：** 神和他的基督一同作王，却用单数的「他」，又一次显明父与子的合一（参第5章的讨论）。
- **「成了」：** 原文是不定过去时，天上把将来的事当作已经成就的事来宣告。这就是10:7所说的「神的奥秘就成全了」。

**2. 「昔在、今在的主神」。** 「在神面前，坐在自己位上的二十四位长老，就面伏于地，敬拜神，说：昔在、今在的主神－全能者啊，我们感谢你！因你执掌大权作王了。」（11:16-17）从前的称呼是「昔在、今在、以后永在的全能者」（1:8；参4:8），这里却只说「昔在、今在」。许多释经者（如包衡）指出，原因很可能是：那要来的一位，现在已经来了（参16:5）。

**3. 「外邦发怒，你的忿怒也临到了」。** 「外邦发怒，你的忿怒也临到了；审判死人的时候也到了。你的仆人众先知和众圣徒，凡敬畏你名的人，连大带小得赏赐的时候也到了。你败坏那些败坏世界之人的时候也就到了。」（11:18）这一节几乎是全书后半部的目录：列国的愤怒（诗2:1；第12-19章），审判死人（20:11-15），神的仆人「连大带小」得赏赐（参22:12），以及神败坏那些败坏他所造之世界的人。神不忘记任何一个默默无闻的仆人；他的审判也是为了救赎受造的世界，不是要毁掉它。

**4. 约柜现出来。** 「当时，神天上的殿开了，在他殿中现出他的约柜。随后有闪电、声音、雷轰、地震、大雹。」（11:19）约柜是神与以色列立约的记号；神说：「我要在那里与你相会」（出25:22）。圣殿被毁以后约柜就不见了，耶利米甚至说，人必「不再提说耶和华的约柜」（耶3:16）。现在约柜在天上显现：神没有忘记他的约。闪电、雷轰、地震让人想起西奈山（出19:16）；同样的记号出现在第七印、第七号、第七碗的末了（8:5；11:19；16:18），一次比一次强烈。许多释经者认为，这表示三组「七」都走到了同一个终点（参看导读和第8章的讨论）。

> 要点：第七号吹响时，天上宣告的不是一个新的计划，而是神一直以来的计划成就了：世上的国成了我主和主基督的国。

## 默想

**默想：** 「住在地上的人就为他们欢喜快乐，互相馈送礼物」（11:10）。小刘在一家工程公司做质检。去年他拒绝在一份不合格的检测报告上签字，项目因此拖了两个星期，经理在会上点了他的名。从那以后，同事们常拿他开玩笑：「信耶稣的又来了」「你的神给你发工资吗？」年底评优他落选了，饭桌上有人举杯说：「敬我们公司最诚实的人。」大家都笑了。他所在的那间小教会，也好不到哪里去：几年前还有四十多人在一套租来的房子里聚会；后来搬了两次家，又经历了一场纷争，现在只剩下八九个人，多半是老人家。镇上有人提起来，就说：「那个教会早就散了。」小刘有时觉得，自己和教会的见证，就像倒在大街上的尸首，旁边的人正在庆祝。

可是小刘心里知道，他并不总是那个诚实的人。同事讲下流的笑话，他也跟着笑过；年会上有人问他是不是信教的，他含糊地说「不算是」；在那份报告以前，也有过一两份他明知有问题还是签了。教会闹纷争的时候，他也说过伤人的话。所以每次被人嘲笑，他都暗暗怀疑：也许他的见证不是被别人弄死的，而是他自己弄坏的。

约翰说，两个见证人的尸首倒在「他们的主钉十字架之处」（11:8）。他们不是第一批在那里被嘲笑的人。主被挂在十字架上的时候，祭司长和文士也戏弄他：「他救了别人，不能救自己。」（可15:31）众人看着他，好像看着一个失败的人。可是他正是在那里，为那些嘲笑他的人舍命，也为那些在嘲笑面前沉默、跟着笑、含糊其辞的门徒舍命。小刘不必把自己的胆怯和妥协藏起来；他可以在这位被钉的主面前承认，在需要的地方去改正，因为主的血足以遮盖这一切。见证人站起来，不是因为他们的记录完美，而是因为「有生气从神那里进入他们里面」（11:11）。以西结看见的那些枯骨，也不是被仇敌杀死的英雄，而是因自己的罪被掳的百姓；神却应许：「我却要救他们出离一切的住处，就是他们犯罪的地方；我要洁净他们」（结37:23）。

那间只剩八九个人的教会也是一样。世人庆祝了「三天半」，见证人却传道了「一千二百六十天」（11:3、9）。保罗形容神的仆人「似乎要死，却是活着的」（林后6:9）。小刘不需要为了挽回面子去反击那些同事，更不需要求「火从天上降下来」（路9:54）。他可以继续诚实地做他的工作，也可以为那些举杯嘲笑他的人祷告，盼望他们有一天也像那「其余的」人一样，「归荣耀给天上的神」（11:13）。

主啊，我的见证常常软弱：有时被人的嘲笑压倒，有时被我自己弄坏。求你把生气吹进我里面，也吹进我们那间小小的教会。当别人庆祝我们的失败时，叫我记得你在那城里被钉，又从死里复活。我等候那一天，天上的大声音要说：「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国；他要作王，直到永永远远。」（11:15）

## The Shape of the Chapter

John has eaten the little scroll and been told to "prophesy again" (10:11); chapter 11 is that prophecy. The first half closes the interlude between the sixth and seventh trumpets, showing how God's people bear witness in the world; in the second half the seventh angel at last blows his trumpet. The chapter falls into five parts:
- **11:1–2, measuring the temple:** the temple of God, the altar and those who worship there are to be measured; the outer court is left out, given over to the nations to trample for forty-two months.
- **11:3–6, the two witnesses prophesy:** clothed in sackcloth, they prophesy for 1,260 days, with powers like those of Elijah and Moses.
- **11:7–10, the witnesses killed:** the beast from the bottomless pit kills them; their bodies lie in the street of the great city for three and a half days, and those who dwell on the earth exchange presents.
- **11:11–14, the witnesses raised and taken up:** a breath of life from God enters them, and they go up to heaven in a cloud; the rest give glory to the God of heaven.
- **11:15–19, the seventh trumpet:** heaven announces, "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ"; God's temple in heaven is opened and the ark of his covenant is seen.

This chapter is among the hardest in Revelation to interpret. This study sets out the main readings at each point; but they all agree on one thing: God's witnesses look defeated, and they are not.

## Going Deeper: 11:1–2 Measuring the Temple

**1. "Measure the temple of God and the altar."** "Then I was given a measuring rod like a staff, and I was told, 'Rise and measure the temple of God and the altar and those who worship there, but do not measure the court outside the temple; leave that out, for it is given over to the nations, and they will trample the holy city for forty-two months'" (11:1–2). In a vision Ezekiel saw a man "with a linen cord and a measuring reed in his hand," who measured the temple to come (Ezek 40:3); Zechariah saw a man going "to measure Jerusalem," and the LORD promised, "I will be to her a wall of fire all around" (Zech 2:2, 5). In both places measuring signals that God will protect his dwelling. What is measured belongs to God and is under his protection; what is not measured is trampled, as the Lord Jesus said: "Jerusalem will be trampled underfoot by the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled" (Luke 21:24).

**2. What is this temple?** Interpreters hold three main views:
- **The Jerusalem temple before AD 70:** some preterist scholars (e.g. Kenneth Gentry) take this as Jerusalem under Roman siege, with the trampling of the outer court and the holy city referring to the Jewish War of AD 66–70. They also use the passage to support an early date of writing (see the Introduction).
- **A future rebuilt temple:** dispensational scholars (e.g. John Walvoord) take it as a temple rebuilt in Jerusalem during the future tribulation.
- **God's people, the church:** many scholars (e.g. Beale, Bauckham) read it symbolically. The New Testament calls the church God's temple (1 Cor 3:16; Eph 2:21), and Revelation promises the one who conquers that he will be "a pillar in the temple of my God" (3:12). On this reading the measured inner temple stands for God's keeping of his people's spiritual life, which no one can take away, while the trampled outer court stands for the persecution they still face in the world.
This study leans toward the third reading, since Revelation often uses the Old Testament temple, altar and city for God's people. But all three views see that God knows and keeps those who belong to him, even when everything around them is trampled.

> The point: God measures his temple: he knows and keeps those who belong to him. They may be trampled in the world, but no one can take them from God.

## Going Deeper: 11:2–3 Forty-Two Months and 1,260 Days

**1. Three ways of naming one period.** The nations trample the holy city "for forty-two months" (11:2), and the beast exercises authority "for forty-two months" (13:5); the two witnesses prophesy "for 1,260 days" (11:3), and the woman is nourished in the wilderness for 1,260 days (12:6), also called "a time, and times, and half a time" (12:14). All three come to three and a half years. The background is Daniel: the saints "shall be given into his hand for a time, times, and half a time" (Dan 7:25; cf. 12:7); "for half of the week he shall put an end to sacrifice and offering" (Dan 9:27). In Elijah's day, too, "the heavens were shut up three years and six months" (Luke 4:25).

**2. How should the period be understood?** There are four main readings:
- **A literal future three and a half years:** dispensational scholars (e.g. John Walvoord) take it as the second half of Daniel's seventieth week, three and a half years within the future tribulation.
- **First-century events:** preterists (e.g. Kenneth Gentry) take it as roughly the years of Rome's war against Judea.
- **A day for a year:** many historicist interpreters apply the "day for a year" principle (Num 14:34; Ezek 4:6) to read 1,260 days as 1,260 years, often linked with the power of the medieval papacy; the nineteenth-century English writer E. B. Elliott is one example.
- **A symbol of the whole church age:** many scholars (e.g. Beale, William Hendriksen) take it as a symbol of the whole period between Christ's first coming and his return. Three and a half is half of seven: evil has power, but not complete power, and its time has an end.
This study leans toward the last reading, while granting that the question is a hard one. On any reading, notice the contrast: the witnesses prophesy for 1,260 days, while their enemies triumph for only "three and a half days" (11:9).

**3. Do not use these numbers to calculate.** In Chinese churches people often match these numbers with a particular year or event and announce the date of the end; groups such as Eastern Lightning (which calls itself the Church of Almighty God) also use these passages to frighten people (see the Introduction). Revelation uses these numbers to tell a suffering church that the days of suffering are measured by God, with a beginning and an end. They are not given to set us calculating dates (Mark 13:32).

> The point: Forty-two months, 1,260 days, and a time, times and half a time name a limited period measured by God. Evil's power has its term; God's witness will not be wiped out.

## Going Deeper: 11:3–6 The Two Witnesses

**1. "My two witnesses."** "And I will grant authority to my two witnesses, and they will prophesy for 1,260 days, clothed in sackcloth" (11:3). The law required that "only on the evidence of two witnesses or of three witnesses shall a charge be established" (Deut 19:15); two witnesses mean their testimony is sure. The Lord Jesus also sent out his disciples two by two (Luke 10:1). Sackcloth was the coarse clothing of prophets and penitents; their message is a call to repentance.

**2. "The two olive trees and the two lampstands."** "These are the two olive trees and the two lampstands that stand before the Lord of the earth" (11:4). Zechariah saw a golden lampstand with "two olive trees by it" (Zech 4:3), "the two anointed ones who stand by the Lord of the whole earth" (Zech 4:14), usually taken to be Joshua the high priest and Zerubbabel the governor. There God says, "Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD of hosts" (Zech 4:6). But John makes one change: Zechariah has one lampstand; here there are two. And in Revelation "the seven lampstands are the seven churches" (1:20).

**3. The powers of Elijah and Moses.** "And if anyone would harm them, fire pours from their mouth and consumes their foes... They have the power to shut the sky, that no rain may fall during the days of their prophesying, and they have power over the waters to turn them into blood" (11:5–6). Elijah shut the sky (1 Kgs 17:1) and called down fire from heaven (2 Kgs 1:10); through Moses and Aaron "all the water in the Nile turned into blood" (Exod 7:20). But here the fire "pours from their mouth." God told Jeremiah, "I am making my words in your mouth a fire" (Jer 5:14). Many interpreters take this to mean that their words carry the power of God's judgment. James and John once wanted to "tell fire to come down from heaven" on Samaritans who would not receive the Lord, "but he turned and rebuked them" (Luke 9:54–55). This passage gives Christians no warrant for violence against those who oppose them.

**4. Who are the two witnesses?** Interpreters hold three main views:
- **Two real prophets in the future:** dispensational scholars (e.g. John Walvoord) take them as two individuals who will prophesy in Jerusalem during the future tribulation. Some identify them as Moses and Elijah, since their powers match, they appeared together at the transfiguration (Matt 17:3), and God promised to send Elijah (Mal 4:5); some early church writers (e.g. Hippolytus) instead named Enoch and Elijah, since neither passed through death.
- **First-century witnesses:** some preterist scholars take them as those who bore witness in Jerusalem before its fall; some suggest Peter and Paul, others the witnessing church of that time in general.
- **The witnessing church:** many scholars (e.g. Beale, Bauckham) take the two witnesses as the prophetic witness of the whole church in the world. The reasons: they are "two lampstands," and the lampstands are churches (1:20); their bodies are watched by people "from the peoples and tribes and languages and nations" (11:9), so they are not in one place only; and their story follows their Lord's: witness, death, resurrection and ascension. Beale also suggests that, since only two of the seven churches, Smyrna and Philadelphia, receive no rebuke, this may be one reason for "two" lampstands.
This study leans toward the last reading. On this reading the church inherits the prophetic office of Moses and Elijah, and their sufferings too.

> The point: God's witnesses bear witness not by might nor by power but by God's Spirit. Their power is not a sword but the word God has given them.

## Going Deeper: 11:7–14 Killed, Raised, Taken Up

**1. "The beast that rises from the bottomless pit."** "And when they have finished their testimony, the beast that rises from the bottomless pit will make war on them and conquer them and kill them" (11:7). This is Revelation's first mention of "the beast," described more fully in chapters 13 and 17. The background is Daniel: "this horn made war with the saints and prevailed over them" (Dan 7:21). Notice that the beast can kill them only "when they have finished their testimony." Until the days God has set are complete, no one can silence them.

**2. "The great city."** "And their dead bodies will lie in the street of the great city that symbolically is called Sodom and Egypt, where their Lord was crucified" (11:8). Which city is this?
- **Jerusalem:** preterist and dispensational scholars (e.g. John Walvoord) alike take it so, since "where their Lord was crucified" most directly means Jerusalem; Isaiah also addressed Jerusalem's leaders as "rulers of Sodom" (Isa 1:10).
- **The great city that stands for the world:** many scholars (e.g. Beale, Bauckham) take "symbolically" (literally "spiritually") to mark the language as symbolic; elsewhere in Revelation "the great city" is usually Babylon (16:19; 17:18). Sodom stands for corruption, Egypt for the oppression of God's people, and with the place where the Lord was crucified, this city gathers into one every city that has opposed God in every age.
On either reading, the phrase reminds us that the witnesses walk the road their Lord walked (John 15:20).

**3. No burial, and presents exchanged.** "For three and a half days some from the peoples and tribes and languages and nations will gaze at their dead bodies and refuse to let them be placed in a tomb, and those who dwell on the earth will rejoice over them and make merry and exchange presents, because these two prophets had been a torment to those who dwell on the earth" (11:9–10). In the ancient world, refusing burial was the deepest disgrace (cf. Ps 79:2–3). Some interpreters note that "exchange presents" recalls Purim, with its "gifts of food to one another" (Esth 9:22), turned upside down: then God's people were delivered from their enemies; here the world celebrates its release from God's prophets. The witnesses harmed no one; but to those who will not repent, God's word is itself "a torment."

**4. "A breath of life from God entered them."** "But after the three and a half days a breath of life from God entered them, and they stood up on their feet, and great fear fell on those who saw them" (11:11). This almost echoes Ezekiel 37: "the breath came into them, and they lived and stood on their feet" (Ezek 37:10). Those bones said, "Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost" (Ezek 37:11); God made them live. The witnesses rise not by their own strength, nor because the world has changed its mind, but because the breath of life comes "from God."

**5. "Come up here!"** "Then they heard a loud voice from heaven saying to them, 'Come up here!' And they went up to heaven in a cloud, and their enemies watched them" (11:12). So it was with their Lord: he died, rose, and "a cloud took him out of their sight" (Acts 1:9). Dispensational scholars usually read this verse as the literal resurrection and ascension of two future prophets; scholars who take the two witnesses as the church see God's public vindication of his faithful people at the end. This time "their enemies watched": those who were mocked and left in the street turn out to be the ones God approves.

**6. "The rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven."** "And at that hour there was a great earthquake, and a tenth of the city fell. Seven thousand people were killed in the earthquake, and the rest were terrified and gave glory to the God of heaven" (11:13). How should this be understood?
- **Real repentance:** Bauckham sees a turning point of the whole book here. The trumpet plagues killed a third of mankind, and the rest "did not repent" (9:20); here only a tenth of the city falls, and the rest "gave glory to the God of heaven." In Elijah's day God kept only seven thousand (1 Kgs 19:18); here it is the reverse: seven thousand die and the rest give God glory. What judgment could not do, faithful witness did. Elsewhere in Revelation "give him glory" is the language of repentance (14:7; cf. 16:9).
- **Acknowledgment under fear:** other interpreters take it as a forced acknowledgment of God's power in the face of catastrophe, not necessarily saving faith.
On either view, the verse shows that God's purpose is not judgment alone but turning people back.

> The point: God's witnesses look defeated, mocked and left in the street; but they walk the road their Lord walked, and so, like him, they will stand up from death.

## Going Deeper: 11:15–19 The Seventh Trumpet

**1. "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ."** "Then the seventh angel blew his trumpet, and there were loud voices in heaven, saying, 'The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever and ever'" (11:15).
- **A singular "kingdom":** "the kingdom of the world" is singular. The world seems to have many kingdoms, but in Revelation's eyes there is one realm that has always opposed God; now that realm has changed hands.
- **"Our Lord and... his Christ":** the background is Psalm 2: "The kings of the earth set themselves... against the LORD and against his anointed" (Ps 2:2). "Christ" means the anointed one. Daniel also says, "the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed" (Dan 2:44).
- **"He shall reign":** God and his Christ reign together, yet the singular "he" is used, which again shows the unity of the Father and the Son (see the discussion in chapter 5).
- **"Has become":** an aorist; heaven announces the future as already accomplished. This is what 10:7 called "the mystery of God would be fulfilled."

**2. "Lord God Almighty, who is and who was."** "And the twenty-four elders who sit on their thrones before God fell on their faces and worshiped God, saying, 'We give thanks to you, Lord God Almighty, who is and who was, for you have taken your great power and begun to reign'" (11:16–17). The earlier title was "who is and who was and who is to come, the Almighty" (1:8; cf. 4:8), but here it is only "who is and who was." Many interpreters (e.g. Bauckham) point out the likely reason: the One who was to come has now come (cf. 16:5).

**3. "The nations raged, but your wrath came."** "The nations raged, but your wrath came, and the time for the dead to be judged, and for rewarding your servants, the prophets and saints, and those who fear your name, both small and great, and for destroying the destroyers of the earth" (11:18). This verse is almost a table of contents for the second half of the book: the nations' rage (Ps 2:1; chapters 12–19), the judgment of the dead (20:11–15), the rewarding of God's servants "both small and great" (cf. 22:12), and God's destroying of those who ruin the world he made. God forgets no obscure servant; and his judgment serves the redemption of creation, not its destruction.

**4. The ark is seen.** "Then God's temple in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant was seen within his temple. There were flashes of lightning, rumblings, peals of thunder, an earthquake, and heavy hail" (11:19). The ark was the sign of God's covenant with Israel; God said, "There I will meet with you" (Exod 25:22). After the temple was destroyed the ark disappeared, and Jeremiah even said that people "shall no more say, 'The ark of the covenant of the LORD'" (Jer 3:16). Now the ark appears in heaven: God has not forgotten his covenant. The lightning, thunder and earthquake recall Sinai (Exod 19:16); the same signs come at the close of the seventh seal, the seventh trumpet and the seventh bowl (8:5; 11:19; 16:18), each time more intensely. Many interpreters take this to show that the three series of seven reach the same end (see the Introduction and chapter 8).

> The point: When the seventh trumpet sounds, heaven announces not a new plan but the fulfillment of God's plan all along: the kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And those who dwell on the earth will rejoice over them and make merry and exchange presents" (11:10). Xiao Liu works in quality inspection for an engineering firm. Last year he refused to sign a test report on a failed component; the project was delayed two weeks, and the manager named him in a meeting. Since then his colleagues often make fun of him: "Here comes the Jesus believer." "Does your God pay your salary?" At the year-end awards he was passed over, and at the dinner someone raised a glass: "To the most honest man in our company!" Everyone laughed. His small church is not doing much better. A few years ago more than forty people met in a rented flat; then it moved twice and went through a quarrel, and now only eight or nine remain, mostly elderly. When people in town mention it, they say, "That church broke up long ago." Sometimes Xiao Liu feels that his witness and his church's are like bodies lying in the street while the people around them celebrate.

But Xiao Liu knows he has not always been the honest one. When colleagues told dirty jokes, he laughed along; at the annual party, when someone asked whether he was religious, he mumbled, "Not really"; before that report there were one or two others he knew were wrong and signed anyway. When the church quarreled, he said hurtful things too. So whenever he is mocked, he half suspects that his witness was not killed by others at all, but spoiled by himself.

John says that the bodies of the two witnesses lie "where their Lord was crucified" (11:8). They were not the first to be mocked there. When the Lord hung on the cross, the chief priests and scribes mocked him: "He saved others; he cannot save himself" (Mark 15:31). People looked at him as at a failure. Yet it was there that he gave his life for those who mocked him, and for the disciples who fell silent before mockery, laughed along and gave evasive answers. Xiao Liu need not hide his cowardice and compromises; he can confess them before the crucified Lord and put things right where that is needed, because the Lord's blood is enough to cover it all. The witnesses stand up not because their record was perfect but because "a breath of life from God entered them" (11:11). The dry bones Ezekiel saw were not heroes killed by enemies but a people exiled for their own sins; yet God promised, "I will save them from all the backslidings in which they have sinned, and will cleanse them" (Ezek 37:23).

So it is with the church of eight or nine. The world celebrated for "three and a half days," while the witnesses prophesied for "1,260 days" (11:3, 9). Paul describes God's servants "as dying, and behold, we live" (2 Cor 6:9). Xiao Liu does not need to strike back at his colleagues to save face, still less to "tell fire to come down from heaven" (Luke 9:54). He can go on doing his work honestly, and pray for those who raised their glasses to mock him, hoping that one day they too, like "the rest," will give "glory to the God of heaven" (11:13).

Lord, my witness is often weak: sometimes crushed by others' mockery, sometimes spoiled by me. Breathe your breath of life into me, and into our small church. When others celebrate our failure, help me remember that you were crucified in that city and rose from the dead. I wait for the day when the loud voices of heaven will say, "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever and ever" (11:15).
