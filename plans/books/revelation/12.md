## 本章脉络

第11章末了，第七号吹响，天上宣告「世上的国成了我主和主基督的国」（11:15）。读到这里，读者也许会问：既然如此，教会为什么还在受苦？第12-14章好像把幕布往后拉开，让我们看见地上一切逼迫背后的那场争战。教会的仇敌不只是罗马或任何一个地上的政权，而是那条古蛇（参弗6:12）。本章可以分成三段：
- **12:1-6，妇人、龙和男孩子：** 龙要吞吃妇人的孩子，孩子却被提到神的宝座那里，妇人逃到旷野。
- **12:7-12，天上的争战：** 龙被摔在地上，天上宣告控告弟兄的已经被摔下去了。
- **12:13-17，龙追赶妇人：** 妇人得了大鹰的翅膀，龙就去与她其余的儿女争战。
和合本的12:18「那时龙就站在海边的沙上」（英文标准版把这句放在12:17末了；英文钦定本按较后期的抄本作我站在，指约翰，放在13:1），把我们带到第13章：龙不能再上天，就站在海边，等着那兽从海中上来，借着地上的权势作工。

本章先说天上的得胜（12:7-12），再说地上的逼迫（12:13-17）：约翰要读者先知道战争的结局，再去面对龙的怒气。

## 深读 12:1-6 妇人、龙和男孩子

**1. 「天上现出大异象来」。** 「天上现出大异象来：有一个妇人身披日头，脚踏月亮，头戴十二星的冠冕。」（12:1）「异象」这个词（*sēmeion*）的意思是记号、兆头，是一个要人明白其意义的画面。日头、月亮和十一个星，曾出现在约瑟的梦里，代表雅各和他的儿子们（创37:9）；这里加上约瑟自己，正是以色列的十二支派。

**2. 这妇人是谁？** 释经者主要有三种看法：
- **神的子民，就是旧约的以色列和新约的教会：** 她「在生产的艰难中疼痛呼叫」（12:2），先知常用这个画面描写锡安和神的百姓：「锡安一劬劳便生下儿女」（赛66:8；参赛26:17；弥4:10）。弥赛亚是从以色列生出来的；到了本章末了，她「其余的儿女」是「守神诫命、为耶稣作见证的」（12:17），就是教会。毕尔、包衡等许多学者这样读，三世纪的希坡律陀也已经把这妇人解释为教会（《论基督与敌基督》第六十一章）。这种读法认为，从以色列到教会，是神同一个子民的历史。
- **以色列民族：** 时代论的学者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，妇人特指以色列；她逃到旷野，是指将来大灾难的后半期，神要保守犹太人中的余民。这种读法认真看待「十二星」和弥赛亚出于以色列这两点。
- **耶稣的母亲马利亚：** 天主教的传统常常把这妇人看作马利亚，同时也看作教会；天主教在圣母升天节（8月15日）的弥撒中，以这一段（11:19；12:1-6、10）为第一篇读经。这种读法看见，男孩子是从一位真实的母亲生出来的。福音派释经者多半认为，在旷野被养活一千二百六十天、又有「其余的儿女」的，更像是一个群体；不过以色列生产弥赛亚的劳苦，确实是在马利亚身上成就的。她是蒙大恩的女子（路1:28）；福音派尊敬她，也学习她的信心，但只向三一神祷告。

**3. 「一条大红龙，七头十角」。** 「天上又现出异象来：有一条大红龙，七头十角；七头上戴着七个冠冕。」（12:3）
- **七头十角：** 但以理所见的第四兽「头有十角」（但7:7）。第13章从海中上来的兽也是「十角七头」（13:1），可见兽的样式是从龙来的。
- **冠冕：** 这里的「冠冕」（*diadēma*）是君王的冠冕，和妇人头上那胜利者的冠冕（*stephanos*）不同。龙要作王，要与神争夺王权。
- **「天上星辰的三分之一」：** 龙的尾巴把星辰「摔在地上」（12:4）。许多人认为这是指跟随撒但堕落的天使（参12:9「它的使者」）；也有人根据但以理书8:10，认为是指神的百姓受逼迫。

**4. 「要吞吃她的孩子」。** 「龙就站在那将要生产的妇人面前，等她生产之后，要吞吃她的孩子。」（12:4）从创世记开始，神就宣告女人的后裔和蛇的后裔要「彼此为仇」，「女人的后裔要伤你的头」（创3:15）。这条龙一直想除灭那应许的后裔。希律下令杀尽伯利恒两岁以里的男孩（太2:16），就是这种敌意在历史中的一次显露。

**5. 「被提到神宝座那里去了」。** 「妇人生了一个男孩子，是将来要用铁杖辖管万国的；她的孩子被提到神宝座那里去了。」（12:5）「用铁杖辖管」出自诗篇第2篇（诗2:9），这男孩子就是弥赛亚（参19:15）。约翰只用一句话，就从耶稣的降生说到他的升天，中间他的一生、受死和复活都没有提。十字架要到12:11才明说（「因羔羊的血」）；这里要强调的是：龙要吞吃他，却失败了；他已经坐在神的宝座上。

**6. 一千二百六十天。** 「妇人就逃到旷野，在那里有神给她预备的地方，使她被养活一千二百六十天。」（12:6）这段日子和两个见证人传道的日子相同（11:3），又称为「一载二载半载」（12:14）。几种主要的读法，第11章已经介绍过（参11:2-3的讨论）；本研读在那里倾向把它看作从基督升天到再来的整个教会时代。无论采取哪一种看法，这段时间都是有限的，是神量定的。三年半也让人想起以利亚的时候「天闭塞了三年零六个月」（路4:25），那时神在旷野供养了他（王上17:3-4）。旷野是危险的地方，也是神预备的地方；妇人在那里「被养活」，像以色列在旷野吃吗哪一样。

> 要点：教会在地上受的逼迫，背后有一条想要吞吃弥赛亚的古蛇。可是那孩子已经被提到神的宝座那里，神的百姓在旷野中也有他预备的地方。

## 深读 12:7-9 天上的争战

**1. 「米迦勒同他的使者与龙争战」。** 「在天上就有了争战。米迦勒同他的使者与龙争战，龙也同它的使者去争战，并没有得胜，天上再没有它们的地方。」（12:7-8）米迦勒在但以理书里是「保佑你本国之民的天使长」（但12:1；参但10:13）。这里打仗的是米迦勒，不是基督自己。有些较早的释经者认为米迦勒就是基督；耶和华见证人更教导耶稣就是天使长米迦勒，是受造的。可是圣经称米迦勒为「天使长」（犹1:9）、「大君中的一位」（但10:13），而子是「神的使者都要拜他」的那一位（来1:6）。天上的诗歌随即说明，这场胜利的根据是羔羊的血（12:11）。天使执行的，是羔羊已经赢得的判决。

**2. 这场争战在什么时候？** 释经者有三种主要的看法：
- **在创世以前或人类历史的起头：** 教会历史上常有人把这一段和撒但最初的堕落连在一起，又引以赛亚书14:12和以西结书第28章为证（这两段经文首先是指巴比伦王和推罗王）。
- **在基督的十字架和升天：** 现代许多释经者（例如毕尔、包衡）认为，上下文把这场争战放在男孩子「被提到神宝座那里」之后（12:5），天上的大声音又说「现在」救恩来到了，是因为羔羊的血（12:10-11）。主耶稣在十字架以前说：「现在这世界受审判，这世界的王要被赶出去。」（约12:31）他也说：「我曾看见撒但从天上坠落，像闪电一样。」（路10:18）保罗说，基督在十字架上「将一切执政的、掌权的掳来」（西2:15）。
- **在将来大灾难的中间：** 华尔伏等未来派学者认为，这是将来要发生的事：撒但最后被赶出天上，在地上发出最后的大怒。
这几种看法不一定互相排斥：撒但最初的背叛可能是背景，而决定性的胜利是在十字架上。无论如何，约翰要读者知道：龙已经败了。

**3. 龙的名字。** 「大龙就是那古蛇，名叫魔鬼，又叫撒但，是迷惑普天下的。」（12:9）古蛇，就是伊甸园里引诱夏娃的那蛇（创3:1）；「魔鬼」原文（*diabolos*）有毁谤者的意思；「撒但」在希伯来文是敌对者、控告者（参伯1:6-11）。他的武器主要是谎言（约8:44）。这一节原文里「被摔」这个动词（*eblēthē*）出现了三次（和合本译出两次），都是被动的说法：不是龙自己离开，而是被神赶出去。

## 深读 12:10-12 天上的大声音

**1. 「现在都来到了」。** 「我听见在天上有大声音说：『我神的救恩、能力、国度，并他基督的权柄，现在都来到了！』」（12:10）这首诗歌是本章的钥匙，解释了前面那场争战的意义。按许多释经者（例如毕尔、包衡）的读法，「现在」不是指世界末了，而是指羔羊被杀、复活、升天以后的这个时候：神的国度和基督的权柄，已经借着十字架和复活开始了（参诗2:2、8）；未来派则把这首诗歌连于将来的大灾难。

**2. 「昼夜控告我们弟兄的」。** 「因为那在我们神面前昼夜控告我们弟兄的，已经被摔下去了。」（12:10）在约伯记里，撒但在神面前说：「约伯敬畏神，岂是无故呢？」（伯1:9）在撒迦利亚书里，撒但站在大祭司约书亚的右边，「与他作对」（亚3:1）。撒但的控告，有时是谎言（像对约伯）；有时却是事实（约书亚的衣服确实污秽，亚3:3）。所以他的控告能够伤人，正因为里面常常有真的部分。
- **为什么控告失效了？** 不是因为我们的罪不真实，而是因为罪的刑罚已经由羔羊承担了。保罗说：「谁能控告神所拣选的人呢？有神称他们为义了。谁能定他们的罪呢？有基督耶稣已经死了，而且从死里复活」（罗8:33-34）。在神的审判台前，控告者已经没有站立的地方。
- **控告和责备不同：** 主耶稣说，圣灵来了，要叫人「为罪、为义、为审判，自己责备自己」（约16:8；这节首先是说世人，也适用于信徒）。圣灵的责备，是要人认罪、悔改，然后到基督那里得赦免（约一1:9）；控告者的控告，却是要人离开神，把已经认过、已经被赦免的罪一次又一次翻出来，好像羔羊的血不够用。

**3. 「弟兄胜过它」。** 「弟兄胜过它，是因羔羊的血和自己所见证的道。他们虽至于死，也不爱惜性命。」（12:11）
- **羔羊的血：** 这是得胜的根据。主得胜（5:5）是在十字架上，信徒的得胜也在同一个地方。
- **自己所见证的道：** 信徒不是靠力量胜过龙，而是靠忠心地说出关于耶稣的真理（参1:9）。
- **不爱惜性命：** 在世人看来是失败，在天上看来却是得胜，正如主对士每拿说：「你务要至死忠心」（2:10）。

**4. 「气忿忿地下到你们那里去了」。** 「所以，诸天和住在其中的，你们都快乐吧！只是地与海有祸了！因为魔鬼知道自己的时候不多，就气忿忿地下到你们那里去了。」（12:12）
魔鬼在天上已经没有地方，就在地上发怒。教会受逼迫，不是因为撒但赢了，而是因为他输了；他的大怒，是知道自己「时候不多」的仇敌的怒气。

> 要点：控告者在神面前已经失去了地位，因为羔羊的血已经回答了一切的控告。他在地上的大怒是真实的，但那是失败者的怒气。

## 深读 12:13-17 龙追赶妇人

**1. 「大鹰的两个翅膀」。** 「于是有大鹰的两个翅膀赐给妇人，叫她能飞到旷野，到自己的地方，躲避那蛇；她在那里被养活一载二载半载。」（12:14）神在西奈山对以色列说：「我如鹰将你们背在翅膀上，带来归我。」（出19:4；参申32:11）这是新的出埃及：神的百姓在旷野中，正像当年的以色列一样，被神背着、养活。

**2. 「从口中吐出水来，像河一样」。** 「蛇就在妇人身后，从口中吐出水来，像河一样，要将妇人冲去。地却帮助妇人，开口吞了从龙口吐出来的水。」（12:15-16）
- **口中吐出的水：** 16:13-14也说，从龙口中出来的是污秽的灵，行奇事迷惑人。许多释经者认为，这河既指逼迫，也指谎言和异端的洪流。
- **地开口吞了水：** 出埃及时，神「伸出右手，地便吞灭他们」（出15:12）；可拉一党背叛时，地也开口把他们吞下去（民16:32）。连受造的世界也站在神百姓的一边。

**3. 「她其余的儿女」。** 「龙向妇人发怒，去与她其余的儿女争战，这儿女就是那守神诫命、为耶稣作见证的。」（12:17）
- **其余的儿女：** 妇人的长子是弥赛亚，「其余的儿女」就是跟随他的人。创3:15所说「女人的后裔」，在这里扩大到所有属基督的人。
- **守神诫命、为耶稣作见证：** 这是启示录描写信徒的方式（参14:12）。龙打不到坐在宝座上的那一位，就去攻击属他的人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我听见在天上有大声音说：『我神的救恩、能力、国度，并他基督的权柄，现在都来到了！因为那在我们神面前昼夜控告我们弟兄的，已经被摔下去了。』」（12:10）素珍姊妹在教会负责探访，常去医院陪病人祷告，大家都说她心细。很少有人知道，她二十出头在南方打工的时候，曾经怀过一个孩子。男朋友不辞而别，家里人在电话里说，要是生下来，全家的脸都没处放了。她一个人去了镇上的一家小诊所。后来她信了主，在主面前痛哭认罪，多年以前也告诉过一位年长的传道。可是这件事并没有就此过去。每年到了那个月份，每次听见讲台上讲生命的宝贵，每次去喝别人孩子的满月酒，那件事就又被翻出来，像一份旧案卷，重新摆在审判台上。最难的是，她找不到可以为自己辩护的话，因为卷宗上写的都是真的。所以她每次在病床边为人祷告，心里都觉得自己不配为别人求生命；她也从来不敢在姊妹会里分享。

启示录没有说控告者不存在。它说，他「昼夜」控告。这个控告者不一定要说谎；他常常拿真实的事来定人的罪。可是天上那大声音宣告的，不是案卷上的事不是真的，而是控告者「已经被摔下去了」。天上「再没有它们的地方」（12:8）。为什么？因为那孩子已经被提到神的宝座那里（12:5），因为在神的审判台前，已经有一位为她站着：羔羊的血已经为这件事付了代价。主耶稣走向十字架的时候说：「现在这世界受审判，这世界的王要被赶出去。」（约12:31）那份案卷没有被藏起来，而是在十字架上被审判过了。先知说，神要「将我们的一切罪投于深海」（弥7:19）。被摔下去的是控告者，被投进深海的是罪；还留在宝座前的，是那位像是被杀过的羔羊。

这并不是说那件事无关紧要。神看重每一个生命，那个孩子也在审判全地的主手中（创18:25）；为那孩子哀伤，不是信心软弱。圣灵的责备会叫人认罪，然后把人领到基督那里；控告者却叫人觉得自己不配服事，叫已经认过的罪一次次重来。素珍当年也被人亏负了：被抛下，被逼着，一个人去承担。她的哀伤里，不只有罪疚，也有被伤害的痛。也许你那份案卷不是这件事，而是一段破裂的婚姻。如果当年是你离开了忠心的配偶，那是真实的罪，需要真实的悔改，有时还要尽力弥补，例如对孩子的责任；如果你是在暴力和危险中离开的，离开危险不是罪；向警方求助是对的（罗13:4），你也不必回到一个不安全的人身边。如果你是被离弃的一方，控告者也许会说都是你的错；被人离弃，本身不是你的罪。无论是哪一种，控告者都没有权利在神面前再提出已经被羔羊的血回答过的案子。教会也要小心，不要替控告者开口，把肢体的过去在背后翻来覆去地说。

素珍后来找了一位信得过的姊妹，第一次说出这件事。两个人在教会的小房间里一起读这一节，读到「已经被摔下去了」，那位姊妹停下来说：被摔下去的，不是你。下一周，素珍去医院探访一位早产的年轻妈妈，为她和孩子祷告的时候，声音没有再发抖。那份案卷有时候还是会被翻出来，可是她知道，天上的法庭已经宣判了，天上的声音已经宣告了：「我神的救恩、能力、国度，并他基督的权柄，现在都来到了！」

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 11 the seventh trumpet sounds and heaven announces, "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ" (11:15). Readers may well ask: then why is the church still suffering? Chapters 12–14 draw the curtain back, as it were, to show the conflict that lies behind all persecution on earth. The church's enemy is not only Rome or any earthly power, but that ancient serpent (cf. Eph 6:12). The chapter falls into three parts:
- **12:1–6, the woman, the dragon and the male child:** the dragon would devour the woman's child, but the child is caught up to God's throne and the woman flees into the wilderness.
- **12:7–12, war in heaven:** the dragon is thrown down, and heaven announces that the accuser of the brothers has been thrown down.
- **12:13–17, the dragon pursues the woman:** the woman is given the wings of the great eagle, and the dragon makes war on the rest of her offspring.
The last line of 12:17 in the ESV, "And he stood on the sand of the sea" (verse 18 in the Chinese Union Version; the KJV, following later manuscripts, reads "I stood," meaning John, and places it at 13:1), leads into chapter 13: barred from heaven, the dragon stands at the shore, waiting for the beast to rise from the sea and work through earthly power.

The chapter tells of the victory in heaven (12:7–12) before the persecution on earth (12:13–17): John wants his readers to know how the war ends before they face the dragon's fury.

## Going Deeper: 12:1–6 The Woman, the Dragon and the Male Child

**1. "A great sign appeared in heaven."** "And a great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars" (12:1). The word "sign" (*sēmeion*) means a token or portent, a picture whose meaning is to be grasped. The sun, the moon and eleven stars appeared in Joseph's dream, standing for Jacob and his sons (Gen 37:9); add Joseph himself and you have the twelve tribes of Israel.

**2. Who is the woman?** Interpreters hold three main views:
- **The people of God, Israel in the old covenant and the church in the new:** she "was crying out in birth pains" (12:2), a picture the prophets use for Zion and God's people: "as soon as Zion was in labor she brought forth her children" (Isa 66:8; cf. Isa 26:17; Mic 4:10). The Messiah comes from Israel; at the end of the chapter "the rest of her offspring" are "those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus" (12:17), that is, the church. Many scholars, Beale and Bauckham among them, read it this way, and in the third century Hippolytus already took the woman as the church (*On Christ and Antichrist* 61). On this view, Israel and the church are the one history of the one people of God.
- **The nation of Israel:** dispensational scholars (for example John Walvoord) take the woman as Israel specifically, and her flight into the wilderness as God's preservation of a Jewish remnant in the second half of a future great tribulation. This reading takes seriously the "twelve stars" and the Messiah's descent from Israel.
- **Mary, the mother of Jesus:** Catholic tradition often sees the woman as Mary, and also as the church; the Catholic Church uses this passage (11:19; 12:1–6, 10) as the first reading at Mass on the feast of the Assumption (15 August). This reading sees that the male child was born of a real mother. Most evangelical interpreters think that one nourished in the wilderness for 1,260 days, who has "the rest of her offspring," is more like a community; yet Israel's labor to bring forth the Messiah was indeed fulfilled in Mary. She is the one greatly favored (Luke 1:28); evangelicals honor her and learn from her faith, while praying to the triune God alone.

**3. "A great red dragon, with seven heads and ten horns."** "And another sign appeared in heaven: behold, a great red dragon, with seven heads and ten horns, and on his heads seven diadems" (12:3).
- **Seven heads and ten horns:** Daniel's fourth beast "had ten horns" (Dan 7:7). The beast that rises from the sea in chapter 13 also has "ten horns and seven heads" (13:1): the beast's shape comes from the dragon.
- **Diadems:** the word here (*diadēma*) is a royal crown, unlike the victor's crown (*stephanos*) on the woman's head. The dragon wants to be king; he contests God's rule.
- **"A third of the stars of heaven":** the dragon's tail "cast them to the earth" (12:4). Many take these as the angels who fell with Satan (cf. "his angels," 12:9); others, following Dan 8:10, take them as God's people under persecution.

**4. "So that... he might devour it."** "And the dragon stood before the woman who was about to give birth, so that when she bore her child he might devour it" (12:4). From Genesis on, God declared enmity between the woman's offspring and the serpent's: "he shall bruise your head" (Gen 3:15). The dragon has always tried to destroy the promised offspring. Herod's order to kill all the boys of Bethlehem two years old or under (Matt 2:16) was one surfacing of that hatred in history.

**5. "Caught up to God and to his throne."** "She gave birth to a male child, one who is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron, but her child was caught up to God and to his throne" (12:5). "Rule... with a rod of iron" comes from Psalm 2 (Ps 2:9); the male child is the Messiah (cf. 19:15). In one sentence John moves from Jesus' birth to his ascension, saying nothing of his life, death and resurrection between. The cross is named at 12:11 ("by the blood of the Lamb"); the stress here is that the dragon meant to devour him and failed: he is seated on God's throne.

**6. 1,260 days.** "And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared by God, in which she is to be nourished for 1,260 days" (12:6). This is the same period as the two witnesses' prophesying (11:3), also called "a time, and times, and half a time" (12:14). The main readings are set out at 11:2–3; there this study leans toward taking it as the whole church age from Christ's ascension to his return. On any view the time is limited and measured out by God. Three and a half years also recall Elijah's day, "when the heavens were shut up three years and six months" (Luke 4:25), when God fed him in the wilderness (1 Kgs 17:3–4). The wilderness is a place of danger and also a place God has prepared; the woman is "nourished" there, as Israel ate manna in the wilderness.

> The point: Behind the persecution of the church on earth stands the ancient serpent who wanted to devour the Messiah. But the child has been caught up to God's throne, and God's people have a place he has prepared for them, even in the wilderness.

## Going Deeper: 12:7–9 War in Heaven

**1. "Michael and his angels fighting against the dragon."** "Now war arose in heaven, Michael and his angels fighting against the dragon. And the dragon and his angels fought back, but he was defeated and there was no longer any place for them in heaven" (12:7–8). In Daniel, Michael is "the great prince who has charge of your people" (Dan 12:1; cf. Dan 10:13). It is Michael who fights here, not Christ himself. Some older interpreters took Michael to be Christ, and Jehovah's Witnesses teach that Jesus is the archangel Michael, a created being. But Scripture calls Michael "the archangel" (Jude 9) and "one of the chief princes" (Dan 10:13), while of the Son it says, "Let all God's angels worship him" (Heb 1:6). Yet heaven's song immediately explains that the ground of the victory is the blood of the Lamb (12:11). The angels carry out a verdict the Lamb has already won.

**2. When is this war?** Interpreters hold three main views:
- **Before creation or at the dawn of human history:** many in church history have linked this passage with Satan's original fall, citing Isa 14:12 and Ezekiel 28 (passages addressed first to the kings of Babylon and Tyre).
- **At Christ's cross and ascension:** many modern interpreters (for example Beale and Bauckham) note that the context places the war after the male child is "caught up to God and to his throne" (12:5), and that the voice in heaven says salvation has come "now" because of the blood of the Lamb (12:10–11). Before the cross the Lord Jesus said, "Now is the judgment of this world; now will the ruler of this world be cast out" (John 12:31). He also said, "I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven" (Luke 10:18). Paul says Christ "disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame" (Col 2:15).
- **In the middle of a future tribulation:** Walvoord and other futurist scholars take this as a future event: Satan's final expulsion from heaven and his last great fury on earth.
These views need not exclude one another: Satan's first rebellion may lie in the background while the decisive victory is won at the cross. Either way, John wants his readers to know that the dragon has been defeated.

**3. The dragon's names.** "And the great dragon was thrown down, that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world" (12:9). The ancient serpent is the one who tempted Eve in Eden (Gen 3:1); "devil" (*diabolos*) carries the sense of slanderer; "Satan" in Hebrew means adversary or accuser (cf. Job 1:6–11). His chief weapon is the lie (John 8:44). In this verse the Greek verb "thrown down" (*eblēthē*) occurs three times, and it is passive: the dragon did not leave; God threw him out.

## Going Deeper: 12:10–12 The Loud Voice in Heaven

**1. "Now... have come."** "And I heard a loud voice in heaven, saying, 'Now the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our God and the authority of his Christ have come'" (12:10). This song is the key to the chapter; it explains what the war meant. On the reading many take (for example Beale and Bauckham), "now" is not the end of the world but the time after the Lamb has been slain, raised and exalted: God's kingdom and the authority of his Christ have already been inaugurated through the cross and resurrection (cf. Ps 2:2, 8). Futurists link the song to a future tribulation.

**2. "The accuser of our brothers... who accuses them day and night."** "For the accuser of our brothers has been thrown down, who accuses them day and night before our God" (12:10). In Job, Satan says before God, "Does Job fear God for no reason?" (Job 1:9). In Zechariah, Satan stands at the right hand of Joshua the high priest "to accuse him" (Zech 3:1). Satan's accusations are sometimes lies (as against Job), and sometimes facts (Joshua's garments really were filthy, Zech 3:3). That is why his accusations wound: there is often truth in them.
- **Why has the accusation failed?** Not because our sins are not real, but because the Lamb has borne their penalty. Paul says, "Who shall bring any charge against God's elect? It is God who justifies. Who is to condemn? Christ Jesus is the one who died—more than that, who was raised" (Rom 8:33–34). Before God's judgment seat, the accuser has no place left to stand.
- **Accusation is not conviction:** the Lord Jesus said that the Spirit "will convict the world concerning sin and righteousness and judgment" (John 16:8; said first of the world, but true for believers too). The Spirit's conviction leads a person to confess, repent and come to Christ for forgiveness (1 John 1:9). The accuser's charges aim to drive a person away from God, dragging up again and again sins already confessed and forgiven, as if the Lamb's blood were not enough.

**3. "They have conquered him."** "And they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, for they loved not their lives even unto death" (12:11).
- **The blood of the Lamb:** this is the ground of victory. The Lord conquered (5:5) at the cross, and believers conquer at the same place.
- **The word of their testimony:** believers do not overcome the dragon by force, but by faithfully speaking the truth about Jesus (cf. 1:9).
- **Loving not their lives:** what the world calls defeat, heaven calls victory, as the Lord told Smyrna: "Be faithful unto death" (2:10).

**4. "Come down to you in great wrath."** "Therefore, rejoice, O heavens and you who dwell in them! But woe to you, O earth and sea, for the devil has come down to you in great wrath, because he knows that his time is short!" (12:12).
The devil has no place left in heaven, so he rages on earth. The church is persecuted not because Satan has won, but because he has lost; his fury is that of an enemy who knows "his time is short."

> The point: The accuser has lost his standing before God, because the Lamb's blood has answered every charge. His fury on earth is real, but it is the fury of the defeated.

## Going Deeper: 12:13–17 The Dragon Pursues the Woman

**1. "The two wings of the great eagle."** "But the woman was given the two wings of the great eagle so that she might fly from the serpent into the wilderness, to the place where she is to be nourished for a time, and times, and half a time" (12:14). At Sinai God said to Israel, "I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself" (Exod 19:4; cf. Deut 32:11). This is a new exodus: in the wilderness God's people are carried and fed by God, just as Israel was.

**2. "Water like a river out of his mouth."** "The serpent poured water like a river out of his mouth after the woman, to sweep her away with a flood. But the earth came to the help of the woman, and the earth opened its mouth and swallowed the river that the dragon had poured from his mouth" (12:15–16).
- **Water from the mouth:** in 16:13–14 too, what comes from the dragon's mouth is unclean spirits that deceive by signs. Many interpreters take the river as both persecution and a flood of lies and false teaching.
- **The earth swallows the river:** at the exodus, "You stretched out your right hand; the earth swallowed them" (Exod 15:12); when Korah rebelled, the earth opened its mouth and swallowed them up (Num 16:32). Even the created world takes the side of God's people.

**3. "The rest of her offspring."** "Then the dragon became furious with the woman and went off to make war on the rest of her offspring, on those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus" (12:17).
- **The rest of her offspring:** the woman's firstborn is the Messiah; "the rest of her offspring" are those who follow him. "Her offspring" in Gen 3:15 now widens to include all who belong to Christ.
- **Keeping the commandments of God and holding to the testimony of Jesus:** this is how Revelation describes believers (cf. 14:12). The dragon cannot reach the one seated on the throne, so he attacks those who belong to him.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And I heard a loud voice in heaven, saying, 'Now the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our God and the authority of his Christ have come, for the accuser of our brothers has been thrown down, who accuses them day and night before our God.'" (12:10) Sister Suzhen leads the visiting team at her church. She often goes to the hospital to pray with the sick, and everyone says how thoughtful she is. Few people know that in her early twenties, working in a factory in the south, she became pregnant. Her boyfriend disappeared without a word, and on the phone her family said that if she had the baby the whole family would have no face left. She went alone to a small clinic in the town. Later she came to faith, wept and confessed it before the Lord, and years ago she told an older preacher. But it has not gone away. Every year when that month comes round, every time the pulpit speaks of the value of life, every time she goes to a celebration for someone's new baby, the thing is brought out again, like an old case file laid once more before the bench. The hardest part is that she can find nothing to say in her own defense, because everything written in the file is true. So every time she prays at a bedside, she feels unfit to ask God for someone else's life; and she has never dared share at the women's meeting.

Revelation does not say there is no accuser. It says he accuses "day and night." This accuser does not always need lies; he often condemns people with what is true. But the loud voice in heaven does not announce that the file is false. It announces that the accuser "has been thrown down." There is "no longer any place for them in heaven" (12:8). Why? Because the child has been caught up to God's throne (12:5), and before God's judgment seat someone already stands for her: the Lamb's blood has paid for this very thing. As the Lord Jesus went toward the cross he said, "Now is the judgment of this world; now will the ruler of this world be cast out" (John 12:31). The file has not been hidden; it has been judged at the cross. The prophet says, "You will cast all our sins into the depths of the sea" (Mic 7:19). The accuser is thrown down, the sins are thrown into the depths of the sea, and what remains before the throne is the Lamb, standing as though it had been slain.

This does not mean what happened does not matter. God treasures every life, and that child too is in the hands of the Judge of all the earth (Gen 18:25); grieving for the child is not weak faith. The Spirit's conviction brings a person to confess and then leads her to Christ; the accuser tells her she is unfit to serve and makes sins already confessed come back again and again. Suzhen was also wronged back then: abandoned, pressured, left to bear it alone. Her grief holds not only guilt but the pain of having been hurt. Perhaps your file is not this, but a broken marriage. If you were the one who left a faithful spouse, that is real sin and calls for real repentance, and sometimes for doing what you can to make amends, for example in your duty to your children. If you left because of violence and danger, leaving danger is not sin; going to the police is right (Rom 13:4), and you need not go back to someone unsafe. If you were the one who was left, the accuser may tell you it was all your fault; being abandoned is not in itself your sin. Either way, the accuser has no right to bring again before God a case the Lamb's blood has already answered. The church too must take care not to speak for the accuser, turning over a member's past behind their back.

In the end Suzhen went to a sister she trusted and told her for the first time. The two of them read this verse together in a small room at church. When they came to "has been thrown down," her friend stopped and said, "The one thrown down is not you." The next week Suzhen visited a young mother whose baby had come early, and when she prayed for the mother and child her voice did not shake. The file is still sometimes brought out. But she knows that the court of heaven has given its verdict, and the voice from heaven has made its announcement: "Now the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our God and the authority of his Christ have come."
