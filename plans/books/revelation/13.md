## 本章脉络

第12章末了，龙站在海边的沙上（和合本12:18）。它在天上已经没有地方，就要借着地上的权势作工。第13章介绍它的两个助手：
- **13:1-10，从海中上来的兽：** 龙把大权柄给了这兽；兽的死伤医好了，全地的人都跟从它，它又与圣徒争战四十二个月。
- **13:11-18，从地中上来的兽：** 它像羊羔，说话却像龙，叫众人拜头一个兽、受兽的印记；兽的数目是六百六十六。

许多学者指出，龙、海中的兽和地中的兽，好像在冒充父、子、圣灵（参16:13）。龙把权柄给兽，像子从父领受权柄（2:27「像我从我父领受的权柄一样」）；兽「似乎受了死伤」又活了，像羔羊「像是被杀过的」却站立着（5:6）；地中的兽叫人拜头一个兽，像圣灵叫人尊荣基督。撒但不能创造，只能模仿，而且模仿得永远不像。这一章要读者学会分辨：真羔羊和假羔羊，神的印和兽的印记。

## 深读 13:1-4 从海中上来的兽

**1. 「从海中上来」。** 「我又看见一个兽从海中上来，有十角七头，在十角上戴着十个冠冕，七头上有亵渎的名号。」（13:1）但以理看见「四个大兽从海中上来」（但7:3）：像狮子、像熊、像豹，第四兽「头有十角」（但7:4-7）。约翰所见的这一只兽，把但以理四兽的特征集于一身：「形状像豹，脚像熊的脚，口像狮子的口」（13:2）。许多释经者认为，这表示它集历史上一切与神为敌的帝国于一身。

**2. 「那龙将自己的能力、座位，和大权柄都给了它」。** （13:2）这兽的权柄是从龙来的。魔鬼在旷野试探主耶稣的时候，也提出过同样的交易：「这一切权柄、荣华，我都要给你，因为这原是交付我的，我愿意给谁就给谁。你若在我面前下拜，这都要归你。」（路4:6-7）主耶稣拒绝了；兽却接受了。

**3. 「似乎受了死伤，那死伤却医好了」。** 「我看见兽的七头中，有一个似乎受了死伤，那死伤却医好了。」（13:3）「似乎受了死伤」原文和5:6「像是被杀过的」用的是同一个分词。许多学者（例如包衡）认为，约翰是故意这样写的：兽在模仿羔羊的死和复活。至于这死伤在历史上指什么，有几种看法：
- **尼禄的传说：** 许多学者认为约翰借用了尼禄的传说：先是说他没有死，还要回来；后来更说他要从死里复生归来（第17章会再讨论）。
- **帝国的复原：** 尼禄死后罗马一年换了四个皇帝，到维斯帕先即位，才重新稳定下来，好像受了死伤又活过来。
- **将来的敌基督：** 约翰·华尔伏等未来派学者认为，这是将来一位世界领袖或一个复兴的罗马帝国，要显出好像从死里复活的样子。
无论哪一种看法，重点是：兽假冒复活，叫「全地的人都希奇跟从」。

**4. 「谁能比这兽，谁能与它交战呢？」** 「又拜那龙－因为它将自己的权柄给了兽，也拜兽，说：『谁能比这兽，谁能与它交战呢？』」（13:4）这句话模仿了摩西的歌：「耶和华啊，众神之中，谁能像你？」（出15:11）许多人也注意到，米迦勒（12:7）这个名字本身就是一个问句，意思是谁像神呢？人拜兽，是因为它看起来不可战胜。拜兽的人其实是在拜龙。

**5. 兽是谁？** 历代的读法（参看导读）大致如下：
- **历史派：** 许多宗教改革家和清教徒把兽和当时的罗马教廷连在一起。马丁·路德这样看；1646年的《威斯敏斯德信条》第二十五章第六节，根据帖撒罗尼迦后书第2章的用语，称罗马教皇为那敌基督、大罪人、沉沦之子。这是在经历了一个多世纪的逼迫和激烈对立之后写成的。美国长老会（PCUSA）在1903年修订信条时删去了这一句，许多长老会今天采用修订后的文字；许多福音派信徒也不再这样认定，不过也有一些改革宗信徒仍持守原文。爱任纽曾提到拉丁人（*Lateinos*）这个希腊词的数值也是六百六十六，却不肯断定（《驳异端》第五卷第三十章第三节）；后来许多更正教释经者用它来指罗马教廷。
- **其他读法：** 过去派（肯尼斯·詹特里）、未来派（华尔伏）、理想派（威廉·亨德里克森）的读法，参看导读。
本研读和许多学者（例如毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）一样，采取综合的看法：对最初的读者，兽就是要求敬拜的罗马帝国；这兽的样式在历史中一再出现（参约一2:18）；到了末了，它还要有最后、最完全的显现（参帖后2:3-8）。

**6. 罗马书第13章和启示录第13章。** 保罗说：「在上有权柄的，人人当顺服他，因为没有权柄不是出于神的。」（罗13:1）启示录第13章却把一个帝国画成从龙得权柄的兽。这两章并不矛盾。二十世纪的奥斯卡·库尔曼在《新约中的国家》里指出，政权本是神设立的，为要赏善罚恶；政权一旦要人把只属于神的敬拜归给它，就成了兽。所以信徒平常顺服掌权的；可是若有人要我们向它下拜，我们就要像使徒一样说：「顺从神，不顺从人，是应当的。」（徒5:29）

> 要点：兽的权柄是从龙来的，它的力量在于叫人以为它不可战胜。它假冒羔羊的死和复活，要人把只属于神的敬拜归给它。

## 深读 13:5-10 四十二个月和圣徒的忍耐

**1. 「有权柄赐给它」。** 「又赐给它说夸大亵渎话的口，又有权柄赐给它，可以任意而行四十二个月。」（13:5）本段一再说「赐给它」「任凭它」（13:5、7）。许多释经者认为，这种被动的说法是在表明神的主权：兽的权柄是有限的，是被容许的，时间也是神量定的。四十二个月就是一千二百六十天（参11:2-3的讨论）。

**2. 「与圣徒争战，并且得胜」。** 「又任凭它与圣徒争战，并且得胜；也把权柄赐给它，制伏各族、各民、各方、各国。」（13:7）但以理看见那小角「与圣民争战，胜了他们」（但7:21）。在地上看，兽是得胜的。可是在天上看，真正得胜的是那些「胜了兽和兽的像并它名字数目的人」（15:2）。

**3. 「被杀之羔羊生命册」。** 「凡住在地上、名字从创世以来没有记在被杀之羔羊生命册上的人，都要拜它。」（13:8）
- **「从创世以来」连在哪里？** 和合本和英文标准版把它连在「记在」，就是名字从创世以来记在册上，这和17:8的说法一致。英文钦定本按原文的词序连在「被杀」，读作从创世以来被杀的羔羊（参彼前1:20）。许多现代译本采用前一种读法，也有一些（例如新国际版）采用后一种。
- **安慰：** 拜兽的人和属羔羊的人，分别在于名字是否记在羔羊的生命册上。属羔羊的人得保守，不是因为自己特别刚强，而是因为被杀的羔羊早已把他们的名字写上了。

**4. 「圣徒的忍耐和信心就是在此」。** 「凡有耳的，就应当听！掳掠人的，必被掳掠；用刀杀人的，必被刀杀。圣徒的忍耐和信心就是在此。」（13:9-10）
- **抄本的差异：** 和合本按一些抄本，读作「用刀杀人的，必被刀杀」，意思是兽和逼迫者终必受报应（参太26:52）。英文标准版按另一些抄本，读成：人若该被刀杀，就必被刀杀。这是回应耶利米书的话：「定为刀杀的，必交刀杀……定为掳掠的，必被掳掠」（耶15:2）。照这个读法，神的百姓要接受神所容许的苦难，不以刀还刀。
- **共同的意思：** 无论哪一种读法，圣徒都不是靠刀剑胜过兽，而是靠「忍耐和信心」：忍耐，是因为知道兽的日子是神量定的。

## 深读 13:11-15 从地中上来的兽

**1. 「两角如同羊羔，说话好像龙」。** 「我又看见另有一个兽从地中上来，有两角如同羊羔，说话好像龙。」（13:11）这兽后来被称为「假先知」（16:13；19:20；20:10）。它看起来像羔羊，说的却是龙的话。它的工作是宗教性的：「叫地和住在地上的人拜那死伤医好的头一个兽」（13:12）。

**2. 「叫火从天降在地上」。** 「又行大奇事，甚至在人面前，叫火从天降在地上。」（13:13）以利亚在迦密山上求告，「耶和华降下火来」（王上18:38）。这兽在模仿神的真先知所行的神迹。可是神迹不能证明真理：摩西早已警告，若有先知显神迹奇事，叫人随从别神，「你也不可听那先知」（申13:1-3；参太24:24）。分辨的标准，不是看能力有多大，而是看它叫人拜谁。这也不是叫我们怀疑每一位讲道的人：许多忠心的牧者正是在压力中劝信徒不向兽下拜，我们当敬重他们（帖前5:12-13）。

**3. 「给那受刀伤还活着的兽作个像」。** 「又有权柄赐给它，叫兽像有生气，并且能说话，又叫所有不拜兽像的人都被杀害。」（13:15）这让人想起尼布甲尼撒所立的金像：「凡不俯伏敬拜的，必立时扔在烈火的窑中」（但3:6）。
- **一世纪的背景：** 在亚细亚省，各城争相为罗马和皇帝建庙、立像：别迦摩早在主前29年就为罗马和奥古斯都建了庙，士每拿后来为提庇留建庙，以弗所在多米田时也建了皇帝的庙（参看导读）。许多学者（例如包衡、毕尔）认为，地中的兽代表推动皇帝崇拜的地方祭司和城中的权贵；有些学者（例如毕尔）认为，也包括教会里那些劝信徒随从的人，像别迦摩的巴兰和推雅推喇的耶洗别（2:14、20）。
- **今天的样式：** 假先知不一定穿着宗教的衣服。凡是把地上的权势、财富或某个领袖神化，又用好听的话为它祝福，都带着这兽的样式。

## 深读 13:16-18 印记和六百六十六

**1. 「在右手上或是在额上受一个印记」。** 「它又叫众人，无论大小、贫富、自主的、为奴的，都在右手上或是在额上受一个印记。」（13:16）
- **神的印的冒充：** 在第7章，天使「印了我们神众仆人的额」（7:3）；第14章，跟随羔羊的人「有他的名和他父的名写在额上」（14:1）。兽的印记是这个印的冒充。
- **手和额：** 摩西吩咐以色列人把神的话「系在手上为记号，戴在额上为经文」（申6:8）。许多人认为，额关乎人所认信的，手关乎人所做的。也有许多释经者认为，正像神仆人额上的印不是肉眼可见的，兽的印记也主要表示人归属谁、敬拜谁；未来派学者（例如华尔伏）认为，到了末了还会有一个可见的记号。无论如何，启示录把受印记和「拜兽和兽像」连在一起（14:9）：这不是人在不知不觉中受的，而是敬拜的选择。

**2. 「都不得做买卖」。** 「除了那受印记、有了兽名或有兽名数目的，都不得做买卖。」（13:17）对亚细亚的信徒，这是很实际的压力。城中的行会和生意，常和偶像的宴席、皇帝的节期连在一起；不参加的人，生意会受影响。兽的权势不一定用刀，常常是用饭碗。

**3. 六百六十六。** 「在这里有智慧：凡有聪明的，可以算计兽的数目；因为这是人的数目，它的数目是六百六十六。」（13:18）释经者主要有几种看法：
- **尼禄该撒：** 古代的希伯来文和希腊文都用字母代表数字。把尼禄该撒这个名字的希腊文写法用希伯来字母拼出来，各字母的数值加起来正好是六百六十六；若按拉丁文的写法去掉一个字母，就是六百一十六。爱任纽已经知道有些抄本作六百一十六，认为是抄错了（《驳异端》第五卷第三十章第一节）；现存的抄本中也有这个读法，主要是约三世纪的蒲草纸残片P115和五世纪的以法莲抄本。许多学者（例如包衡、詹特里）认为，这两个数字都能对上尼禄，是这数目指向尼禄的有力证据。
- **象征的数字：** 七是完全的数目，六比七少一；三个六，好像是一直想要达到完全，却永远差一点。亨德里克森、毕尔等认为，这表示兽和它的整个体系，冒充神却永远不是神。
- **将来的一个人：** 华尔伏等认为，这是将来敌基督的名字，到他显露的时候才能认出来。

**4. 「在这里有智慧」。** 这几种看法可以并存：对最初的读者，数字可能指向尼禄；它的意思却不止于尼禄，而是揭露一切冒充神的权势。历代把这数目套在某个人物、某种科技上的人，没有一个说对过（参看导读）。「在这里有智慧」是要信徒看穿兽的真面目，不是要人去破解密码。真正的问题是：我的额上写着谁的名？我的手在为谁做工？

> 要点：兽的印记是神的印的冒充，关乎人敬拜谁、属于谁。六百六十六是一个人的数目，永远达不到神的完全；智慧不在于算出它是谁，而在于记得自己额上已经有羔羊的名（14:1），因此不向它下拜。

## 默想

**默想：** 「它又叫众人，无论大小、贫富、自主的、为奴的，都在右手上或是在额上受一个印记。除了那受印记、有了兽名或有兽名数目的，都不得做买卖。」（13:16-17）小何师范毕业三年，一直在一所私立学校代课，工资不高，合同一年一签。今年她终于考上了县里一所公办小学的编制，只差最后一步：交一张入职考察表。表上有一栏是宗教信仰。经办的老师私下对她说，大家都填「无」，你也填「无」就行了，没有人会去查；你要是填了别的，这个编制恐怕就保不住了。她妈妈在电话里也急了：你辛苦了三年，难道要为了一个字全丢掉？那天晚上，她把表摊在桌上，手里拿着笔，想到三年来每个月都剩不下几百块的工资，想到父母逢人就说女儿考上了时那份高兴，也想到那一栏里小小的空格。

约翰最初的读者，住在推雅推喇、别迦摩这样的城里，生意常和行会的宴席、守护神的节期连在一起：不随大家，就没有饭碗。启示录说，跟随羔羊的人「有他的名和他父的名写在额上」（14:1）。兽的印记不一定是一块芯片；有时候，兽只是要人把额上的那个名擦掉，换上一个「无」字。小何知道，这一栏问的不只是一项资料，而是她属于谁。我也许从来没有填过这样的表，可是我知道，为了一个职位、一张执照、一笔生意，我曾经怎样一点一点地让步。

这一章最叫人惊讶的，不是兽有多可怕，而是它所提出的条件我们早就听过。魔鬼曾把天下的万国指给主耶稣看，说：「这一切权柄、荣华，我都要给你……你若在我面前下拜，这都要归你。」（路4:6-7）这正是龙给兽的东西（13:2）。主耶稣说：「当拜主－你的神，单要事奉他。」（路4:8）他没有为了得着万国而下拜，宁可走那条往十字架去的路。后来，人群在彼拉多面前喊着说：「除了凯撒，我们没有王。」（约19:15）他就在那地上的权势之下被钉死。他拒绝了不经过十字架的国度，为的是把真正的国度白白赐给属他的人。

交表的前一天是周四，小何在小组里讲了这张表的事。她也承认，上大学的时候，她在同样的一栏里填过「无」，那时觉得不过是一张表。小组里没有人指着她说她受了兽的印记。大家一起读13:8：名字记在「被杀之羔羊生命册上」的人，不是靠自己的刚强站立的。他们没有替她作决定，却为她祷告，也约好，如果她因此失去这个编制，大家一同承担。第二天，她在那一栏里写上「基督教」，把表交了上去。她不知道结果会怎样。启示录14:9-11严厉警告拜兽、受印记的人；那是指始终甘心归属兽、至死不肯回头的人。至于一个曾经软弱、又回转悔改的门徒，羔羊并没有因为当年那一个字，就把她的名字从生命册上涂抹。主耶稣的国从来不是赏给自以为站得稳的人的；屈过膝、又被他的血带回来的人，他也照样白白地赐给。

小何不知道这个编制最后能不能保住。我也常常不知道，不肯弯腰要付上什么代价。可是那位不肯为万国的荣华向龙下拜的主，并没有叫我们自己去争一个国。兽拿饭碗来换人额上的名；父却不用我们拿什么去换。主耶稣对他那些害怕的门徒说：「你们这小群，不要惧怕，因为你们的父乐意把国赐给你们。」（路12:32）

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 12 the dragon stands on the sand of the sea (the last line of 12:17 in the ESV). With no place left in heaven, he will work through earthly power. Chapter 13 introduces his two helpers:
- **13:1–10, the beast from the sea:** the dragon gives the beast great authority; its mortal wound is healed, the whole earth follows it, and it makes war on the saints for forty-two months.
- **13:11–18, the beast from the earth:** it looks like a lamb but speaks like a dragon, and makes all worship the first beast and take its mark; the number of the beast is 666.

Many scholars point out that the dragon, the beast from the sea and the beast from the earth form a kind of counterfeit of the Father, the Son and the Spirit (cf. 16:13). The dragon gives his authority to the beast, as the Son receives authority from the Father ("even as I myself have received authority from my Father," 2:27); the beast "seemed to have a mortal wound" and lives, as the Lamb stands "as though it had been slain" (5:6); the beast from the earth makes people worship the first beast, as the Spirit glorifies Christ. Satan cannot create; he can only imitate, and the imitation is never a likeness. This chapter trains its readers to tell the difference: the true Lamb from the false, the seal of God from the mark of the beast.

## Going Deeper: 13:1–4 The Beast from the Sea

**1. "Rising out of the sea."** "And I saw a beast rising out of the sea, with ten horns and seven heads, with ten diadems on its horns and blasphemous names on its heads" (13:1). Daniel saw "four great beasts came up out of the sea" (Dan 7:3): like a lion, like a bear, like a leopard, and a fourth with "ten horns" (Dan 7:4–7). The one beast John sees gathers up the features of all four: "like a leopard; its feet were like a bear's, and its mouth was like a lion's mouth" (13:2). Many interpreters take this to mean that it sums up every empire in history that has set itself against God.

**2. "And to it the dragon gave his power and his throne and great authority."** (13:2) The beast's authority comes from the dragon. When the devil tempted the Lord Jesus in the wilderness he made the same offer: "To you I will give all this authority and their glory, for it has been delivered to me, and I give it to whom I will. If you, then, will worship me, it will all be yours" (Luke 4:6–7). The Lord Jesus refused; the beast accepted.

**3. "Seemed to have a mortal wound, but its mortal wound was healed."** "One of its heads seemed to have a mortal wound, but its mortal wound was healed" (13:3). "Seemed to have a mortal wound" is in Greek the same participle as "as though it had been slain" in 5:6. Many scholars (for example Bauckham) think John wrote this deliberately: the beast imitates the Lamb's death and resurrection. As for what the wound refers to in history, there are several views:
- **The Nero legend:** many scholars think John drew on the legend about Nero: first that he had not died and would return, later that he would come back from the dead (chapter 17 returns to it).
- **The empire's recovery:** after Nero's death Rome had four emperors in a year, then became stable again when Vespasian came to power, as if mortally wounded and alive again.
- **A future antichrist:** John Walvoord and other futurist scholars take this as a future world leader or a revived Roman empire that will appear to rise from the dead.
On any of these views the point is that the beast fakes a resurrection, and "the whole earth marveled as they followed the beast."

**4. "Who is like the beast, and who can fight against it?"** "And they worshiped the dragon, for he had given his authority to the beast, and they worshiped the beast, saying, 'Who is like the beast, and who can fight against it?'" (13:4). The words mimic the song of Moses: "Who is like you, O LORD, among the gods?" (Exod 15:11). Many also note that the name "Michael" means "Who is like God?" (12:7). People worship the beast because it looks unbeatable. Those who worship the beast are in fact worshiping the dragon.

**5. Who is the beast?** The main readings through the centuries (see the Introduction) are roughly these:
- **Historicist:** many Reformers and Puritans linked the beast with the papacy of their day. Martin Luther took this view, and the Westminster Confession of 1646 (25.6), using the language of 2 Thessalonians 2, called the pope "that Antichrist, that man of sin, and son of perdition." It was written after more than a century of persecution and bitter conflict. The Presbyterian Church in the U.S.A. struck the clause in its 1903 revision, and many Presbyterian churches today use the revised text; many evangelicals no longer make the identification, though some Reformed believers still hold the original wording. Irenaeus mentioned that the Greek word *Lateinos* ("Latin man") also totals 666, though he would not commit to it (*Against Heresies* 5.30.3); many later Protestant interpreters applied it to papal Rome.
- **Other readings:** for the preterist (Kenneth Gentry), futurist (Walvoord) and idealist (William Hendriksen) readings, see the Introduction.
Like many scholars (for example Beale and Grant Osborne), this study takes an eclectic view: for the first readers, the beast was the Roman Empire demanding worship; the beast's pattern recurs throughout history (cf. 1 John 2:18); and at the end it will have a final and fullest appearance (cf. 2 Thess 2:3–8).

**6. Romans 13 and Revelation 13.** Paul says, "Let every person be subject to the governing authorities. For there is no authority except from God" (Rom 13:1). Yet Revelation 13 portrays an empire as a beast that receives its authority from the dragon. The two chapters do not contradict each other. As Oscar Cullmann argued in *The State in the New Testament* (1956), the state is instituted by God to reward good and punish evil; but once it demands the worship that belongs to God alone, it becomes the beast. So believers ordinarily submit to the authorities; but when we are told to bow down, we say with the apostles, "We must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29).

> The point: The beast's authority comes from the dragon, and its strength lies in making people think it cannot be beaten. It counterfeits the Lamb's death and resurrection, and demands the worship that belongs to God alone.

## Going Deeper: 13:5–10 Forty-Two Months and the Endurance of the Saints

**1. "It was allowed."** "And the beast was given a mouth uttering haughty and blasphemous words, and it was allowed to exercise authority for forty-two months" (13:5). The passage keeps saying "was given" and "was allowed" (13:5, 7). Many interpreters take this as a passive that points to God's sovereignty: the beast's authority is limited and permitted, and its time is measured out by God. Forty-two months are 1,260 days (see the discussion at 11:2–3).

**2. "To make war on the saints and to conquer them."** "Also it was allowed to make war on the saints and to conquer them. And authority was given it over every tribe and people and language and nation" (13:7). Daniel saw the little horn that "made war with the saints and prevailed over them" (Dan 7:21). Seen from earth, the beast wins. Seen from heaven, the true conquerors are "those who had conquered the beast and its image and the number of its name" (15:2).

**3. "The book of life of the Lamb that was slain."** "And all who dwell on earth will worship it, everyone whose name has not been written before the foundation of the world in the book of life of the Lamb that was slain" (13:8).
- **What does "before the foundation of the world" attach to?** The ESV and the CUV attach it to "written": names written in the book from the foundation of the world, which matches 17:8. The KJV follows the Greek word order and attaches it to "slain": "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world" (cf. 1 Pet 1:20). Many modern translations take the first reading; some (e.g. NIV) take the second.
- **Comfort:** what separates those who worship the beast from those who belong to the Lamb is whether their names are written in the Lamb's book of life. Those who belong to the Lamb are kept, not because they are especially strong, but because the slain Lamb wrote their names there long ago.

**4. "Here is a call for the endurance and faith of the saints."** "If anyone has an ear, let him hear: If anyone is to be taken captive, to captivity he goes; if anyone is to be slain with the sword, with the sword must he be slain. Here is a call for the endurance and faith of the saints" (13:9–10).
- **A textual variant:** the CUV follows manuscripts that read "whoever kills with the sword must be killed with the sword," meaning that the beast and the persecutors will be repaid (cf. Matt 26:52). The ESV follows manuscripts that read "if anyone is to be slain with the sword," echoing Jeremiah: "those who are for the sword, to the sword... those who are for captivity, to captivity" (Jer 15:2); the sense is that God's people are to accept the suffering God permits, and not answer sword with sword.
- **The shared sense:** on either reading the saints do not overcome the beast by the sword, but by "endurance and faith": they endure because they know the beast's days are numbered by God.

## Going Deeper: 13:11–15 The Beast from the Earth

**1. "Two horns like a lamb and it spoke like a dragon."** "Then I saw another beast rising out of the earth. It had two horns like a lamb and it spoke like a dragon" (13:11). This beast is later called "the false prophet" (16:13; 19:20; 20:10). It looks like a lamb, but it speaks the dragon's words. Its work is religious: it "makes the earth and its inhabitants worship the first beast, whose mortal wound was healed" (13:12).

**2. "Making fire come down from heaven."** "It performs great signs, even making fire come down from heaven to earth in front of people" (13:13). When Elijah called on God on Mount Carmel, "the fire of the LORD fell" (1 Kgs 18:38). This beast imitates the signs done by God's true prophets. But signs do not prove truth: Moses warned long ago that if a prophet gives a sign and says, "Let us go after other gods," "you shall not listen to the words of that prophet" (Deut 13:1–3; cf. Matt 24:24). The test is not how great the power is, but whom it calls people to worship. Nor does this ask us to suspect every preacher: many faithful pastors under pressure are the very ones urging believers not to bow to the beast, and we should honor them (1 Thess 5:12–13).

**3. "Make an image for the beast that was wounded by the sword and yet lived."** "And it was allowed to give breath to the image of the beast, so that the image of the beast might even speak and might cause those who would not worship the image of the beast to be slain" (13:15). This recalls Nebuchadnezzar's golden image: "whoever does not fall down and worship shall immediately be cast into a burning fiery furnace" (Dan 3:6).
- **The first-century background:** the cities of the province of Asia competed to build temples and statues to Rome and the emperors: Pergamum built a temple to Rome and Augustus as early as 29 BC, Smyrna later built one to Tiberius, and Ephesus built an imperial temple under Domitian (see the Introduction). Many scholars (for example Bauckham and Beale) think the beast from the earth stands for the local priests and city elites who promoted the imperial cult; some (for example Beale) extend it to those in the churches who urged believers to go along, like Balaam at Pergamum and Jezebel at Thyatira (2:14, 20).
- **Its form today:** the false prophet does not always wear religious clothes. Any voice that makes an earthly power, wealth or a leader into a god and blesses it with fine words bears this beast's likeness.

## Going Deeper: 13:16–18 The Mark and 666

**1. "To be marked on the right hand or the forehead."** "Also it causes all, both small and great, both rich and poor, both free and slave, to be marked on the right hand or the forehead" (13:16).
- **A counterfeit of God's seal:** in chapter 7 the angels sealed "the servants of our God on their foreheads" (7:3); in chapter 14 those who follow the Lamb have "his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads" (14:1). The mark of the beast counterfeits that seal.
- **Hand and forehead:** Moses told Israel to bind God's words "as a sign on your hand, and they shall be as frontlets between your eyes" (Deut 6:8). Many take the forehead to stand for what a person confesses and the hand for what a person does. Many also think that, just as the seal on the foreheads of God's servants is not visible, the mark of the beast is chiefly a sign of whom a person belongs to and worships; futurist scholars (for example Walvoord) expect a visible mark as well at the end. Either way, Revelation ties receiving the mark to worshiping "the beast and its image" (14:9): it is not taken unknowingly, but is a choice of worship.

**2. "So that no one can buy or sell."** "So that no one can buy or sell unless he has the mark, that is, the name of the beast or the number of its name" (13:17). For the believers of Asia this was a very practical pressure. Trade guilds and business in the cities were often bound up with idol feasts and imperial festivals; those who stayed away lost trade. The beast's power does not always come by the sword; often it comes through a person's livelihood.

**3. 666.** "This calls for wisdom: let the one who has understanding calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a man, and his number is 666" (13:18). Interpreters hold several main views:
- **Nero Caesar:** in the ancient world Hebrew and Greek letters also served as numbers. The Greek form of the name "Nero Caesar," spelled in Hebrew letters, adds up to exactly 666; spelled after the Latin form, without one letter, it comes to 616. Irenaeus already knew of copies that read "616" and thought it a scribal error (*Against Heresies* 5.30.1); surviving manuscripts also have it, chiefly a papyrus fragment of about the third century (P115) and the fifth-century Codex Ephraemi. Many scholars (for example Bauckham and Gentry) find the fact that both numbers fit Nero strong evidence that the number points to him.
- **A symbolic number:** seven is the number of completeness, and six falls one short; three sixes suggest something always striving for completeness and always falling short. Hendriksen, Beale and others take it to mean that the beast and its whole system imitate God but are never God.
- **A future individual:** Walvoord and others take it as the name of the future antichrist, to be recognized when he appears.

**4. "This calls for wisdom."** These views can stand together: for the first readers the number may have pointed to Nero, but its meaning does not stop at Nero; it exposes every power that poses as God. Of all who have pinned the number on some person or technology through the centuries, none has been right (see the Introduction). "This calls for wisdom" means seeing through the beast, not cracking a code. The real questions are: whose name is written on my forehead? For whom is my hand working?

> The point: The mark of the beast counterfeits God's seal; it concerns whom a person worships and belongs to. 666 is the number of a man, forever short of God's completeness; wisdom lies not in working out who it is, but in remembering that the Lamb's name is already on our foreheads (14:1), and so refusing to bow to it.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Also it causes all, both small and great, both rich and poor, both free and slave, to be marked on the right hand or the forehead, so that no one can buy or sell unless he has the mark" (13:16–17). Xiao He has been out of teacher training for three years, teaching on one-year contracts at a private school for low pay. This year she finally won a permanent post at a public primary school in her county. One step is left: handing in the personnel form. One line on it asks for her religious belief. The teacher handling it tells her quietly, "Everyone writes 'none.' Just write 'none'; nobody checks. If you write anything else, I doubt the post will hold." On the phone her mother is upset too: "Three years of hard work, and you'd throw it all away over one word?" That night she sits with the form spread on the table and the pen in her hand, thinking of three years of pay with barely a few hundred yuan left each month, of how glad her parents were telling everyone their daughter got the job, and of the small blank on that line.

John's first readers lived in cities like Thyatira and Pergamum, where trade was bound up with guild feasts and the festivals of patron gods: refuse to go along and you lose your livelihood. Revelation says those who follow the Lamb have "his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads" (14:1). The mark of the beast need not be a chip; sometimes the beast simply demands that a person rub that name off the forehead and write "none" in its place. Xiao He knows that the line is asking for more than a piece of information; it is asking whom she belongs to. I may never have filled in a form like hers, but I know how, for a position, a licence or a deal, I have given way little by little.

What is most startling in this chapter is not how terrible the beast is, but that we have heard its terms before. The devil showed the Lord Jesus all the kingdoms of the world and said, "To you I will give all this authority and their glory... If you, then, will worship me, it will all be yours" (Luke 4:6–7). That is exactly what the dragon gave the beast (13:2). The Lord Jesus answered, "You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve" (Luke 4:8). He would not bow down to gain the kingdoms; he chose instead the road to the cross. Later the crowd before Pilate cried, "We have no king but Caesar" (John 19:15), and he was crucified under that earthly power. He refused a kingdom without the cross in order to give the true kingdom freely to those who are his.

The day before the form was due was a Thursday, and Xiao He told her small group about it. She also admitted that at university she had written "none" on the same line, thinking it was only a form. No one pointed at her and said she had taken the mark of the beast. Together they read 13:8: those whose names are written "in the book of life of the Lamb that was slain" do not stand by their own strength. They did not make the decision for her, but they prayed for her, and agreed that if she lost the post over this, they would carry it with her. The next day she wrote "Christian" on the line and handed the form in. She does not know how it will turn out. Revelation 14:9–11 warns in the strongest terms those who worship the beast and receive its mark: those who give themselves to the beast and never turn back. But the Lamb has not blotted out of the book of life the name of a disciple who was weak and has turned back, not over that one word years ago. The Lord Jesus' kingdom was never a prize for those who think they stand firm; he gives it just as freely to those who bent the knee and have been brought back by his blood.

Xiao He does not know whether she will keep the post. I often do not know what refusing to bend will cost me either. But the Lord who would not bow to the dragon for the glory of the kingdoms has not left us to win a kingdom for ourselves. The beast trades a livelihood for the name on a person's forehead; the Father asks nothing of us in exchange. The Lord Jesus said to his frightened disciples, "Fear not, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom" (Luke 12:32).
