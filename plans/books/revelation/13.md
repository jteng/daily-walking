## 本章脉络

第12章末了，龙站在海边的沙上（和合本12:18）。它在天上已经没有地方，就要借着地上的权势作工。第13章介绍它的两个助手：
- **13:1-10，从海中上来的兽：** 它有十角七头，形状像豹、熊、狮子；龙把能力、座位和大权柄给了它；它的一个头受了死伤又医好了，全地的人都希奇跟从它；它说亵渎的话四十二个月，与圣徒争战。
- **13:11-18，从地中上来的兽：** 它有两角如同羊羔，说话却像龙；它行大奇事，叫人给头一个兽作像，又叫众人在右手上或额上受印记，不然就不能做买卖；兽的数目是六百六十六。

许多学者指出，龙、海中的兽和地中的兽，好像是对父、子、圣灵的一种冒充（参16:13）。龙把权柄给兽，像父把书卷交给羔羊（5:7）；兽「似乎受了死伤」又活了，像羔羊「像是被杀过的」却站立着（5:6）；地中的兽叫人拜头一个兽，像圣灵叫人尊荣基督。撒但不能创造，只能模仿。这一章要读者学会分辨：真羔羊和假羔羊，神的印和兽的印记。

## 深读 13:1-4 从海中上来的兽

**1. 「从海中上来」。** 「我又看见一个兽从海中上来，有十角七头，在十角上戴着十个冠冕，七头上有亵渎的名号。」（13:1）但以理看见「四个大兽从海中上来」（但7:3）：像狮子、像熊、像豹，第四兽「头有十角」（但7:4-7）。约翰所见的这一只兽，把但以理四兽的特征集于一身：「形状像豹，脚像熊的脚，口像狮子的口」（13:2）。许多释经者认为，这表示它总结了历史上一切与神为敌的帝国。在旧约里，海常是混乱和邪恶的象征（参赛27:1）。

**2. 「那龙将自己的能力、座位，和大权柄都给了它」。** （13:2）这兽的权柄是从龙来的。魔鬼在旷野试探主耶稣的时候，也说过同样的话：「这一切权柄、荣华，我都要给你，因为这原是交付我的，我愿意给谁就给谁。你若在我面前下拜，这都要归你。」（路4:6-7）主耶稣拒绝了；兽却接受了。

**3. 「似乎受了死伤，那死伤却医好了」。** 「我看见兽的七头中，有一个似乎受了死伤，那死伤却医好了。」（13:3）「似乎受了死伤」原文和5:6「像是被杀过的」用的是同一个说法（*hōs esphagmenēn*）。许多学者（例如包衡）认为，约翰是故意这样写的：兽在模仿羔羊的死和复活。至于这死伤在历史上指什么，有几种看法：
- **尼禄的传说：** 尼禄在主后68年自杀以后，民间流传他没有真死，或将要复活回来（参塔西佗《历史》第二卷第八章；苏维托尼乌《罗马十二帝王传·尼禄传》第五十七章）。许多学者认为约翰借用了这个传说的画面；第17章会再讨论。
- **帝国的复原：** 尼禄死后，罗马一年之内换了四个皇帝，随后在维斯帕先手下重新稳定，好像帝国受了死伤又活过来了。
- **将来的敌基督：** 约翰·华尔伏等未来派学者认为，这是将来一位世界领袖，或一个复兴的罗马帝国，要显出好像从死里复活的样子。
无论哪一种看法，重点是：兽假冒复活，叫「全地的人都希奇跟从」。

**4. 「谁能比这兽，谁能与它交战呢？」** 「又拜那龙－因为它将自己的权柄给了兽，也拜兽，说：『谁能比这兽，谁能与它交战呢？』」（13:4）这句话模仿了摩西的歌：「耶和华啊，众神之中，谁能像你？」（出15:11）许多人也注意到，米迦勒（12:7）这名字的意思就是：谁像神呢？。人拜兽，是因为它看起来不可战胜。拜兽的人其实是在拜龙。

**5. 兽是谁？** 历代的读法（参看导读）大致如下：
- **过去派：** 尼禄和一世纪的罗马帝国（例如肯尼斯·詹特里）。
- **历史派：** 许多宗教改革家和清教徒把兽和当时的罗马教廷连在一起。马丁·路德这样看；1646年的《威斯敏斯德信条》第二十五章第六节，根据帖撒罗尼迦后书第2章的用语，称罗马教皇为那敌基督、大罪人、沉沦之子。这是在宗教改革时期受逼迫、彼此严重对立的处境中写成的；后来有些长老会（例如二十世纪初美国的长老会）删去了这一句，今天许多福音派信徒也不再这样认定。
- **未来派：** 将来一位敌基督，带领一个世界性的帝国（例如华尔伏）。
- **理想派：** 历世历代要人敬拜它的政治权势（例如威廉·亨德里克森）。
本研读和许多学者一样（例如毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）采取综合的看法：对最初的读者，兽就是要求敬拜的罗马帝国；这兽的样式在历史中一再出现（参约一2:18）；到了末了，它还要有最后、最完全的显现（参帖后2:3-8）。

**6. 罗马书第13章和启示录第13章。** 保罗说：「在上有权柄的，人人当顺服他，因为没有权柄不是出于神的。」（罗13:1）彼得也说：「敬畏神，尊敬君王。」（彼前2:17）启示录第13章却把一个帝国画成从龙得权柄的兽。这两章并不矛盾。二十世纪的奥斯卡·库尔曼指出，政权本是神设立的，为要赏善罚恶；可是当它要求人把只属于神的敬拜归给它，它就成了兽。基督徒尊敬掌权的，按时纳税，为君王祷告；但当人要我们向它下拜，我们要像使徒一样说：「顺从神，不顺从人，是应当的。」（徒5:29）

> 要点：兽的权柄是从龙来的，它的力量在于叫人以为它不可战胜。它假冒羔羊的死和复活，要人把只属于神的敬拜归给它。

## 深读 13:5-10 四十二个月和圣徒的忍耐

**1. 「有权柄赐给它」。** 「又赐给它说夸大亵渎话的口，又有权柄赐给它，可以任意而行四十二个月。」（13:5）本段一再说「赐给它」「任凭它」（13:5、7）。许多释经者认为这是一种表示神主权的被动说法：兽的权柄是有限的，是被容许的，时间也是神量定的。四十二个月就是一千二百六十天（参12:6的讨论），和但以理书那「一载、二载、半载」（但7:25）相同。

**2. 「与圣徒争战，并且得胜」。** 「又任凭它与圣徒争战，并且得胜；也把权柄赐给它，制伏各族、各民、各方、各国。」（13:7）但以理看见那小角「与圣民争战，胜了他们」（但7:21）；第11章那从无底坑上来的兽，也杀了两个见证人（11:7）。在地上看，兽是得胜的。可是在天上看，真正得胜的是那些「胜了兽和兽的像并它名字数目的人」（15:2），他们「是因羔羊的血」得胜的（12:11）。注意这里「各族、各民、各方、各国」：羔羊用自己的血从这些人中买了人来（5:9），兽却要制伏他们。

**3. 「被杀之羔羊生命册」。** 「凡住在地上、名字从创世以来没有记在被杀之羔羊生命册上的人，都要拜它。」（13:8）
- **「从创世以来」连在哪里？** 和合本和英文标准版把它连在「记在」，就是名字从创世以来记在册上，这和17:8的说法一致。英文钦定本按原文的词序，连在「被杀」，译作从创世以来被杀的羔羊；这也合乎圣经的教导（参彼前1:20）。多数现代译本采用前一种读法。
- **安慰：** 拜兽的人和属羔羊的人，分别在于名字是否记在羔羊的生命册上。属羔羊的人得保守，不是因为自己特别刚强，而是因为被杀的羔羊早已把他们的名字写上了。

**4. 「圣徒的忍耐和信心就是在此」。** 「凡有耳的，就应当听！掳掠人的，必被掳掠；用刀杀人的，必被刀杀。圣徒的忍耐和信心就是在此。」（13:9-10）
- **抄本的差异：** 和合本按一些抄本，读作「用刀杀人的，必被刀杀」，意思是兽和逼迫者终必受报应（参太26:52）。英文标准版按另一些抄本，读作该被刀杀的，必被刀杀，回应耶利米书：「定为刀杀的，必交刀杀……定为掳掠的，必被掳掠」（耶15:2）；意思是神的百姓要接受神所容许的苦难，不以刀还刀。
- **共同的意思：** 无论哪一种读法，圣徒都不是靠刀剑胜过兽，而是靠「忍耐和信心」。「凡有耳的」这句话，和七封信的末了一样（2:7），这是向众教会说的。

> 要点：兽的权柄是被容许的，日子是被限定的。圣徒在地上看起来被打败了，可是他们的名字记在被杀之羔羊的生命册上；他们用忍耐和信心胜过兽。

## 深读 13:11-15 从地中上来的兽

**1. 「两角如同羊羔，说话好像龙」。** 「我又看见另有一个兽从地中上来，有两角如同羊羔，说话好像龙。」（13:11）这兽后来被称为「假先知」（16:13；19:20；20:10）。它看起来像羔羊，说的却是龙的话。主耶稣警告过：「他们到你们这里来，外面披着羊皮，里面却是残暴的狼。」（太7:15）它的工作是宗教性的：「叫地和住在地上的人拜那死伤医好的头一个兽」（13:12）。

**2. 「叫火从天降在地上」。** 「又行大奇事，甚至在人面前，叫火从天降在地上。」（13:13）以利亚在迦密山上求告，「耶和华降下火来」（王上18:38）；两个见证人口中也有火出来（11:5）。这兽模仿神真先知的记号。
- **神迹不能证明真理：** 摩西早已警告，若有先知显神迹奇事，叫人随从别神，「你也不可听那先知」（申13:1-3）。主耶稣也说，假基督、假先知要「显大神迹、大奇事」（太24:24；参帖后2:9）。
- **分辨的标准：** 不是看能力有多大，而是看它叫人拜谁。

**3. 「给那受刀伤还活着的兽作个像」。** 「又有权柄赐给它，叫兽像有生气，并且能说话，又叫所有不拜兽像的人都被杀害。」（13:15）这让人想起尼布甲尼撒所立的金像：「凡不俯伏敬拜的，必立时扔在烈火的窑中」（但3:6）。
- **一世纪的背景：** 在亚细亚省，各城争相为罗马和皇帝建庙、立像：别迦摩早在主前29年就为罗马和亚古士都建了庙，士每拿后来为提庇留建庙，以弗所在多米田时也有皇帝的庙（参导读）。许多学者（例如包衡、毕尔）认为，地中的兽代表推动皇帝崇拜的地方祭司和城中的权贵，甚至也包括教会里那些劝信徒随从的人，像别迦摩的巴兰和推雅推喇的耶洗别（2:14、20）。
- **今天的样式：** 假先知不一定穿着宗教的衣服。凡是把地上的权势、财富或某个领袖神化，又用好听的话为它祝福的声音，都带着这兽的样式。

**4. 牧者的提醒。** 这一段不是叫我们怀疑每一位讲道的人。许多忠心的牧者正是在压力中劝信徒不向兽下拜，我们当敬重他们（帖前5:12-13）。要看的是：他所传的是不是那位被杀的羔羊。

> 要点：假先知看起来像羔羊，说话却像龙。神迹和能力不能证明真理；真先知叫人敬拜神，假先知叫人拜兽。

## 深读 13:16-18 印记和六百六十六

**1. 「在右手上或是在额上受一个印记」。** 「它又叫众人，无论大小、贫富、自主的、为奴的，都在右手上或是在额上受一个印记。」（13:16）
- **神的印的冒充：** 在第7章，天使「印了我们神众仆人的额」（7:3）；第14章，跟随羔羊的人「有他的名和他父的名写在额上」（14:1）；到了新天新地，「他的名字必写在他们的额上」（22:4）。兽的印记是这个印的冒充。
- **手和额：** 摩西吩咐以色列人把神的话「系在手上为记号，戴在额上为经文」（申6:8）。额关乎人所认信的，手关乎人所做的。许多释经者认为，正像神仆人额上的印不是肉眼可见的记号，兽的印记也主要是表示人归属谁、敬拜谁。未来派学者（例如华尔伏）认为，到了末了，还会有一个真实可见的记号。无论如何，启示录把受印记和「拜兽和兽像」连在一起（14:9）；这不是一个人在不知不觉中会受的，而是敬拜的选择。

**2. 「都不得做买卖」。** 「除了那受印记、有了兽名或有兽名数目的，都不得做买卖。」（13:17）对亚细亚的信徒，这是很实际的压力。城中的行会和生意，常和偶像的宴席、皇帝的节期连在一起；不参加的人，生意会受影响，也容易被人怀疑不忠。士每拿的信徒「贫穷」（2:9），可能和这有关。兽的权势不一定用刀，常常是用饭碗。

**3. 六百六十六。** 「在这里有智慧：凡有聪明的，可以算计兽的数目；因为这是人的数目，它的数目是六百六十六。」（13:18）释经者主要有几种看法：
- **尼禄该撒：** 古代的希伯来文和希腊文都用字母代表数字。尼禄该撒这个名字的希腊文写成希伯来字母，数值加起来正好是六百六十六；若按拉丁文的写法去掉一个字母，就是六百一十六。爱任纽已经知道有些抄本作六百一十六，认为是抄错了（《驳异端》第五卷第三十章第一节）；现存的一些抄本也有这个读法，包括一份三世纪的蒲草纸残片。许多学者（例如包衡、詹特里）认为，这两个数字都能对上尼禄，是有力的证据。按较晚的写作年代，尼禄可以看作兽的典型，这和兽「受了死伤」又活了的画面相合。
- **象征的数字：** 七是完全的数目，六比七少一；三个六，好像是一直想要达到完全，却永远差一点。亨德里克森、毕尔等认为，这表示兽和它的整个体系，冒充神却永远不是神。「人的数目」也可以译作普通人用的数目（参21:17「人的尺寸」）。
- **将来的一个人：** 华尔伏等认为，这是将来敌基督的名字，到他显露的时候才能认出来。
- **历史派的计算：** 爱任纽曾提到拉丁人（*Lateinos*）这个希腊字的数值也是六百六十六，却不肯断定（《驳异端》第五卷第三十章第三节）；后来许多更正教的释经者用它来指罗马教廷。

**4. 「在这里有智慧」。** 这几种看法可以并存：对最初的读者，数字可能指向尼禄；它的意思却不止于尼禄，而是揭露一切冒充神的权势。爱任纽劝人不要贸然说出敌基督的名字；历代把这数目套在某个人物、某种科技上的人，没有一个说对过（参看导读）。约翰说「在这里有智慧」，和17:9一样，是要信徒看穿兽的真面目，不是要人去破解密码。真正的问题是：我的额上写着谁的名？我的手在为谁做工？

> 要点：兽的印记是神的印的冒充，关乎人敬拜谁、属于谁。六百六十六是一个人的数目，永远达不到神的完全；智慧不在于算出它是谁，而在于不向它下拜。

## 默想

**默想：** 「它又叫众人，无论大小、贫富、自主的、为奴的，都在右手上或是在额上受一个印记。除了那受印记、有了兽名或有兽名数目的，都不得做买卖。」（13:16-17）小陈在一家建筑公司做工程师，在教会的小组里是出了名的启示录专家。他能讲清楚尼禄该撒怎样算成六百六十六，也知道六百一十六的抄本问题，小组查经常常被他带到这些题目上，一讲就是一个晚上。这个月，公司要提他做项目经理，条件是在几份验收报告上签字。他知道有几处钢筋的用量不够，可是大家都这样做，上司也说，不签就别想在这一行干下去。周四晚上他还在小组里讲兽的数目，周一早上，他的手已经停在那几份报告上。

约翰写下「不得做买卖」的时候，他想的不是一块芯片，而是推雅推喇和别迦摩的信徒：不去行会的宴席，就接不到生意；不跟着大家，就不能在城里立足。兽的印记先是写在人敬拜的心里，然后才在手所做的事上显出来。小陈能算出兽的数目，却差一点把兽的印记签在自己的手上。我也许不像他那样懂六百六十六，可是我也知道，为了一个职位、一张执照、一次升迁，我曾经怎样一点一点地让步。

这一章最叫人惊讶的，不是兽有多可怕，而是它所提出的条件我们早就听过。魔鬼曾把天下的万国指给主耶稣看，说：「这一切权柄、荣华，我都要给你……你若在我面前下拜，这都要归你。」（路4:6-7）这正是龙给兽的东西（13:2）。主耶稣说：「当拜主－你的神，单要事奉他。」（路4:8）他没有为了得着万国而下拜，宁可走那条往十字架去的路。后来，人群在彼拉多面前喊着说：「除了凯撒，我们没有王。」（约19:15）他就在那地上的权势之下被钉死。他拒绝了不经过十字架的国度，为的是把真正的国度白白赐给属他的人。

那个周四，小陈第一次没有在小组里讲六百六十六，而是讲了那几份报告。他也承认，去年已经签过一份差不多的。小组里没有人指着他说他受了兽的印记；大家一起读13:8，名字记在「被杀之羔羊生命册上」的人，不是靠自己的刚强站立的。他们为他祷告，也说好，如果他因此丢了这个职位，大家一同承担。小陈决定不签，又把去年那份报告的问题告诉了该知道的人，因为那关乎住在楼里的人的安全。去年那一次，羔羊并没有因此把他的名字划掉。主耶稣的国不是赏给从来没有屈过膝的人，而是赐给那些屈过膝、又被他的血带回来的人。

主耶稣，你没有为万国的荣华向那龙下拜，我却常常为了一点点好处就弯了腰。求你在我的额上写你的名，在我的手所做的事上作王。你对你那些害怕的门徒说过：「你们这小群，不要惧怕，因为你们的父乐意把国赐给你们。」（路12:32）你拒绝了魔鬼所给的国，为要把父的国赐给我们。阿们。

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 12 the dragon stands on the sand of the sea (the last line of 12:17 in the ESV). With no place left in heaven, he will work through earthly power. Chapter 13 introduces his two helpers:
- **13:1–10, the beast from the sea:** it has ten horns and seven heads and looks like a leopard, a bear and a lion; the dragon gives it his power, his throne and great authority; one of its heads seems to have a mortal wound that is healed, and the whole earth marvels and follows it; it utters blasphemies for forty-two months and makes war on the saints.
- **13:11–18, the beast from the earth:** it has two horns like a lamb but speaks like a dragon; it performs great signs, has people make an image for the first beast, and causes all to be marked on the right hand or the forehead, so that no one can buy or sell without it; the number of the beast is 666.

Many scholars point out that the dragon, the beast from the sea and the beast from the earth form a kind of counterfeit of the Father, the Son and the Spirit (cf. 16:13). The dragon gives his authority to the beast, as the Father gives the scroll to the Lamb (5:7); the beast "seemed to have a mortal wound" and lives, as the Lamb stands "as though it had been slain" (5:6); the beast from the earth makes people worship the first beast, as the Spirit glorifies Christ. Satan cannot create; he can only imitate. This chapter trains its readers to tell the difference: the true Lamb from the false, the seal of God from the mark of the beast.

## Going Deeper: 13:1–4 The Beast from the Sea

**1. "Rising out of the sea."** "And I saw a beast rising out of the sea, with ten horns and seven heads, with ten diadems on its horns and blasphemous names on its heads" (13:1). Daniel saw "four great beasts came up out of the sea" (Dan 7:3): like a lion, like a bear, like a leopard, and a fourth with "ten horns" (Dan 7:4–7). The one beast John sees gathers up the features of all four: "like a leopard; its feet were like a bear's, and its mouth was like a lion's mouth" (13:2). Many interpreters take this to mean that it sums up every empire in history that has set itself against God. In the Old Testament the sea is often a symbol of chaos and evil (cf. Isa 27:1).

**2. "And to it the dragon gave his power and his throne and great authority."** (13:2) The beast's authority comes from the dragon. When the devil tempted the Lord Jesus in the wilderness he said the same thing: "To you I will give all this authority and their glory, for it has been delivered to me, and I give it to whom I will. If you, then, will worship me, it will all be yours" (Luke 4:6–7). The Lord Jesus refused; the beast accepted.

**3. "Seemed to have a mortal wound, but its mortal wound was healed."** "One of its heads seemed to have a mortal wound, but its mortal wound was healed" (13:3). "Seemed to have a mortal wound" is in Greek the same expression (*hōs esphagmenēn*) as "as though it had been slain" in 5:6. Many scholars (for example Bauckham) think John wrote this deliberately: the beast imitates the Lamb's death and resurrection. As for what the wound refers to in history, there are several views:
- **The Nero legend:** after Nero's suicide in AD 68, rumors spread that he had not really died, or would return to life (cf. Tacitus, *Histories* 2.8; Suetonius, *Nero* 57). Many scholars think John drew on the imagery of this legend; chapter 17 returns to it.
- **The empire's recovery:** after Nero's death Rome had four emperors in one year, then regained stability under Vespasian, as if the empire had taken a mortal wound and lived.
- **A future antichrist:** John Walvoord and other futurist scholars take this as a future world leader, or a revived Roman empire, that will appear to rise from the dead.
On any of these views the point is that the beast fakes a resurrection, and "the whole earth marveled as they followed the beast."

**4. "Who is like the beast, and who can fight against it?"** "And they worshiped the dragon, for he had given his authority to the beast, and they worshiped the beast, saying, 'Who is like the beast, and who can fight against it?'" (13:4). The words mimic the song of Moses: "Who is like you, O LORD, among the gods?" (Exod 15:11). Many also note that the name "Michael" means "Who is like God?" (12:7). People worship the beast because it looks unbeatable. Those who worship the beast are in fact worshiping the dragon.

**5. Who is the beast?** The main readings through the centuries (see the Introduction) are roughly these:
- **Preterist:** Nero and the first-century Roman Empire (for example Kenneth Gentry).
- **Historicist:** many Reformers and Puritans linked the beast with the papacy of their day. Martin Luther took this view, and the Westminster Confession of 1646 (25.6), using the language of 2 Thessalonians 2, called the pope "that Antichrist, that man of sin, and son of perdition." It was written amid persecution and bitter conflict in the Reformation era; some Presbyterian churches later removed the clause (for example in America in the early twentieth century), and many evangelicals today no longer make that identification.
- **Futurist:** a future antichrist ruling a worldwide empire (for example Walvoord).
- **Idealist:** political power in every age that demands worship (for example William Hendriksen).
Like many scholars (for example Beale and Grant Osborne), this study takes an eclectic view: for the first readers, the beast was the Roman Empire demanding worship; the beast's pattern recurs throughout history (cf. 1 John 2:18); and at the end it will have a final and fullest appearance (cf. 2 Thess 2:3–8).

**6. Romans 13 and Revelation 13.** Paul says, "Let every person be subject to the governing authorities. For there is no authority except from God" (Rom 13:1). Peter says, "Fear God. Honor the emperor" (1 Pet 2:17). Yet Revelation 13 portrays an empire as a beast that receives its authority from the dragon. The two chapters do not contradict each other. As Oscar Cullmann argued in the twentieth century, the state is instituted by God to reward good and punish evil; but when it demands the worship that belongs to God alone, it becomes the beast. Christians honor those in authority, pay their taxes and pray for rulers; but when we are told to bow down, we say with the apostles, "We must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29).

> The point: The beast's authority comes from the dragon, and its strength lies in making people think it cannot be beaten. It counterfeits the Lamb's death and resurrection, and demands the worship that belongs to God alone.

## Going Deeper: 13:5–10 Forty-Two Months and the Endurance of the Saints

**1. "It was allowed."** "And the beast was given a mouth uttering haughty and blasphemous words, and it was allowed to exercise authority for forty-two months" (13:5). The passage keeps saying "was given" and "was allowed" (13:5, 7). Many interpreters take this as a passive that points to God's sovereignty: the beast's authority is limited and permitted, and its time is measured out by God. Forty-two months are 1,260 days (see the discussion at 12:6), the same as Daniel's "time, times, and half a time" (Dan 7:25).

**2. "To make war on the saints and to conquer them."** "Also it was allowed to make war on the saints and to conquer them. And authority was given it over every tribe and people and language and nation" (13:7). Daniel saw the little horn that "made war with the saints and prevailed over them" (Dan 7:21); in chapter 11 the beast that rises from the bottomless pit kills the two witnesses (11:7). Seen from earth, the beast wins. Seen from heaven, the true conquerors are "those who had conquered the beast and its image and the number of its name" (15:2), and they conquered "by the blood of the Lamb" (12:11). Notice "every tribe and people and language and nation": the Lamb ransomed people from all of these by his blood (5:9); the beast seeks to rule them.

**3. "The book of life of the Lamb that was slain."** "And all who dwell on earth will worship it, everyone whose name has not been written before the foundation of the world in the book of life of the Lamb that was slain" (13:8).
- **What does "before the foundation of the world" attach to?** The ESV and the CUV attach it to "written": names written in the book from the foundation of the world, which matches 17:8. The KJV follows the Greek word order and attaches it to "slain": "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world," which is also true to Scripture (cf. 1 Pet 1:20). Most modern translations take the first reading.
- **Comfort:** what separates those who worship the beast from those who belong to the Lamb is whether their names are written in the Lamb's book of life. Those who belong to the Lamb are kept, not because they are especially strong, but because the slain Lamb wrote their names there long ago.

**4. "Here is a call for the endurance and faith of the saints."** "If anyone has an ear, let him hear: If anyone is to be taken captive, to captivity he goes; if anyone is to be slain with the sword, with the sword must he be slain. Here is a call for the endurance and faith of the saints" (13:9–10).
- **A textual variant:** the CUV follows manuscripts that read "whoever kills with the sword must be killed with the sword," meaning that the beast and the persecutors will be repaid (cf. Matt 26:52). The ESV follows manuscripts that read "if anyone is to be slain with the sword," echoing Jeremiah: "those who are for the sword, to the sword... those who are for captivity, to captivity" (Jer 15:2); the sense is that God's people are to accept the suffering God permits, and not answer sword with sword.
- **The shared sense:** on either reading the saints do not overcome the beast by the sword, but by "endurance and faith." "If anyone has an ear" echoes the close of the seven letters (2:7): this is spoken to all the churches.

> The point: The beast's authority is permitted and its days are numbered. On earth the saints seem defeated, but their names are written in the book of life of the Lamb that was slain, and they overcome the beast by endurance and faith.

## Going Deeper: 13:11–15 The Beast from the Earth

**1. "Two horns like a lamb and it spoke like a dragon."** "Then I saw another beast rising out of the earth. It had two horns like a lamb and it spoke like a dragon" (13:11). This beast is later called "the false prophet" (16:13; 19:20; 20:10). It looks like a lamb, but it speaks the dragon's words. The Lord Jesus warned, "Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves" (Matt 7:15). Its work is religious: it "makes the earth and its inhabitants worship the first beast, whose mortal wound was healed" (13:12).

**2. "Making fire come down from heaven."** "It performs great signs, even making fire come down from heaven to earth in front of people" (13:13). When Elijah called on God on Mount Carmel, "the fire of the LORD fell" (1 Kgs 18:38); fire also pours from the mouths of the two witnesses (11:5). This beast imitates the signs of God's true prophets.
- **Signs do not prove truth:** Moses warned long ago that if a prophet gives a sign or wonder and says, "Let us go after other gods," "you shall not listen to the words of that prophet" (Deut 13:1–3). The Lord Jesus also said that false christs and false prophets "will arise and perform great signs and wonders" (Matt 24:24; cf. 2 Thess 2:9).
- **The test:** not how great the power is, but whom it calls people to worship.

**3. "Make an image for the beast that was wounded by the sword and yet lived."** "And it was allowed to give breath to the image of the beast, so that the image of the beast might even speak and might cause those who would not worship the image of the beast to be slain" (13:15). This recalls Nebuchadnezzar's golden image: "whoever does not fall down and worship shall immediately be cast into a burning fiery furnace" (Dan 3:6).
- **The first-century background:** the cities of the province of Asia competed to build temples and statues to Rome and the emperors: Pergamum built a temple to Rome and Augustus as early as 29 BC, Smyrna later built one to Tiberius, and Ephesus had an imperial temple under Domitian (see the Introduction). Many scholars (for example Bauckham and Beale) think the beast from the earth stands for the local priests and city elites who promoted the imperial cult, and even for those in the churches who urged believers to go along, like Balaam at Pergamum and Jezebel at Thyatira (2:14, 20).
- **Its form today:** the false prophet does not always wear religious clothes. Any voice that makes an earthly power, wealth or a leader into a god and blesses it with fine words bears this beast's likeness.

**4. A word about pastors.** This passage does not ask us to suspect every preacher. Many faithful pastors under pressure are the very ones urging believers not to bow to the beast, and we should honor them (1 Thess 5:12–13). The question is whether a preacher proclaims the Lamb who was slain.

> The point: The false prophet looks like a lamb but speaks like a dragon. Signs and power do not prove truth; the true prophet calls people to worship God, the false prophet to worship the beast.

## Going Deeper: 13:16–18 The Mark and 666

**1. "To be marked on the right hand or the forehead."** "Also it causes all, both small and great, both rich and poor, both free and slave, to be marked on the right hand or the forehead" (13:16).
- **A counterfeit of God's seal:** in chapter 7 the angels sealed "the servants of our God on their foreheads" (7:3); in chapter 14 those who follow the Lamb have "his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads" (14:1); in the new creation "his name will be on their foreheads" (22:4). The mark of the beast counterfeits that seal.
- **Hand and forehead:** Moses told Israel to bind God's words "as a sign on your hand, and they shall be as frontlets between your eyes" (Deut 6:8). The forehead concerns what a person confesses; the hand concerns what a person does. Many interpreters think that, just as the seal on the foreheads of God's servants is not a visible mark, the mark of the beast is chiefly a sign of whom a person belongs to and worships. Futurist scholars (for example Walvoord) expect that at the end there will also be a literal, visible mark. Either way, Revelation ties receiving the mark to worshiping "the beast and its image" (14:9); it is not something taken unknowingly, but a choice of worship.

**2. "So that no one can buy or sell."** "So that no one can buy or sell unless he has the mark, that is, the name of the beast or the number of its name" (13:17). For the believers of Asia this was a very practical pressure. Trade guilds and business in the cities were often bound up with idol feasts and imperial festivals; those who stayed away lost trade and were easily suspected of disloyalty. The "poverty" of the believers in Smyrna (2:9) may have had something to do with this. The beast's power does not always come by the sword; often it comes through a person's livelihood.

**3. 666.** "This calls for wisdom: let the one who has understanding calculate the number of the beast, for it is the number of a man, and his number is 666" (13:18). Interpreters hold several main views:
- **Nero Caesar:** in the ancient world Hebrew and Greek letters also served as numbers. The Greek name "Nero Caesar" written in Hebrew letters adds up to exactly 666; spelled after the Latin form, without one letter, it comes to 616. Irenaeus already knew of copies that read "616" and thought it a scribal error (*Against Heresies* 5.30.1); some surviving manuscripts also read it, including a third-century papyrus fragment. Many scholars (for example Bauckham and Gentry) find it telling that both numbers fit Nero. On the later date of writing, Nero can be seen as the type of the beast, which fits the picture of a beast with a mortal wound that lives again.
- **A symbolic number:** seven is the number of completeness, and six falls one short; three sixes suggest something always striving for completeness and always falling short. Hendriksen, Beale and others take it to mean that the beast and its whole system imitate God but are never God. "The number of a man" can also mean a human number (cf. "human measurement," 21:17).
- **A future individual:** Walvoord and others take it as the name of the future antichrist, to be recognized when he appears.
- **Historicist calculations:** Irenaeus mentioned that the Greek word *Lateinos* ("Latin man") also totals 666, though he would not commit to it (*Against Heresies* 5.30.3); many later Protestant interpreters applied it to papal Rome.

**4. "This calls for wisdom."** These views can stand together: for the first readers the number may have pointed to Nero, but its meaning does not stop at Nero; it exposes every power that poses as God. Irenaeus urged people not to name the antichrist rashly; of all who have pinned the number on some person or technology through the centuries, none has been right (see the Introduction). "This calls for wisdom," like 17:9, means seeing through the beast, not cracking a code. The real questions are: whose name is written on my forehead? For whom is my hand working?

> The point: The mark of the beast counterfeits God's seal; it concerns whom a person worships and belongs to. 666 is the number of a man, forever short of God's completeness; wisdom lies not in working out who it is, but in refusing to bow to it.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Also it causes all, both small and great, both rich and poor, both free and slave, to be marked on the right hand or the forehead, so that no one can buy or sell unless he has the mark" (13:16–17). Xiao Chen is an engineer at a construction company, and in his church small group he is known as the "Revelation expert." He can explain exactly how "Nero Caesar" adds up to 666, and he knows about the manuscripts that read 616; Bible studies often drift onto these topics with him, and a whole evening goes by. This month the company wants to make him a project manager, on one condition: that he sign several inspection reports. He knows that in several places the steel reinforcement falls short, but everyone does it, and his superior has told him that if he won't sign, he can forget about working in this industry. On Thursday night he was explaining the number of the beast in his small group; on Monday morning his hand is hovering over those reports.

When John wrote "no one can buy or sell," he was not thinking of a microchip but of the believers of Thyatira and Pergamum: stay away from the guild feast and the contracts dry up; refuse to go along with everyone and you cannot keep your place in the city. The mark of the beast is written first on the heart's worship, and only then shows itself in what the hand does. Xiao Chen can calculate the number of the beast, yet he has nearly signed its mark onto his own hand. I may not know as much about 666 as he does, but I know how, for a position, a licence or a promotion, I have given way little by little.

What is most startling in this chapter is not how terrible the beast is, but that we have heard its terms before. The devil showed the Lord Jesus all the kingdoms of the world and said, "To you I will give all this authority and their glory... If you, then, will worship me, it will all be yours" (Luke 4:6–7). That is exactly what the dragon gave the beast (13:2). The Lord Jesus answered, "You shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve" (Luke 4:8). He would not bow down to gain the kingdoms; he chose instead the road to the cross. Later the crowd before Pilate cried, "We have no king but Caesar" (John 19:15), and he was crucified under that earthly power. He refused a kingdom without the cross in order to give the true kingdom freely to those who are his.

That Thursday, for the first time, Xiao Chen did not talk about 666 at small group. He talked about the reports. He also admitted that last year he had signed one much like them. No one pointed at him and said he had taken the mark of the beast. Together they read 13:8: those whose names are written "in the book of life of the Lamb that was slain" do not stand by their own strength. They prayed for him, and agreed that if he lost the position over this, they would carry it with him. Xiao Chen decided not to sign, and he told those who needed to know about last year's report, because the safety of the people living in that building depended on it. The Lamb had not crossed out his name over last year's signature. The Lord Jesus does not give his kingdom as a prize to those who have never bent the knee. He gives it to those who bent the knee and have been brought back by his blood.

Lord Jesus, you did not bow to the dragon for the glory of the kingdoms, but I have often bent for very little gain. Write your name on my forehead, and reign over the work of my hands. You said to your frightened disciples, "Fear not, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom" (Luke 12:32). You refused the kingdom the devil offered, so that you could give us your Father's kingdom. Amen.
