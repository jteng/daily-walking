## 本章脉络

第12-13章揭开了地上争战背后的真相：大龙和两只兽用逼迫、迷惑和印记，要叫「凡住在地上」的人都拜兽（13:8）。读到这里，忠心的信徒难免要问：跟随羔羊的人会怎样？第14章就是天上的回答。全章可以分成四段：
- **14:1-5，锡安山上的羔羊和十四万四千人：** 他们额上写着羔羊和他父的名，唱只有他们能学的新歌。
- **14:6-11，三位天使的宣告：** 永远的福音；巴比伦倾倒了；拜兽的人必喝神大怒的酒。
- **14:12-13，圣徒的忍耐和在主里死去的福。**
- **14:14-20，地上的两次收割：** 人子收割地上的庄稼；地上的葡萄被丢在神忿怒的大酒榨中。

第13章结束时，兽好像掌管了一切；第14章开头，站在锡安山上的却是羔羊。这一章把两条路摆在读者面前：额上写着羔羊的名，还是受兽的印记；在主里面「息了自己的劳苦」（14:13），还是「昼夜不得安宁」（14:11）。

## 深读 14:1-5 跟随羔羊的人

**1. 「羔羊站在锡安山」。** 「我又观看，见羔羊站在锡安山，同他又有十四万四千人，都有他的名和他父的名写在额上。」（14:1）兽从海中上来，羔羊却站在神所立的地方：「我已经立我的君在锡安－我的圣山上了。」（诗2:6）这锡安山在哪里？许多释经者（如毕尔、包衡）认为，这是「天上的耶路撒冷」（来12:22），因为歌声是「从天上」来的，又是在「宝座前」唱的（14:2-3）。许多时代论的学者（如约翰·华尔伏）则认为，这是预先描绘羔羊在千禧年国度开始时，和这群人一同站在地上的耶路撒冷。两种读法都看见：兽的权势是暂时的，羔羊和他的百姓终必站立得住。

**2. 额上的名和兽的印记。** 兽叫众人「都在右手上或是在额上受一个印记」（13:16）；跟随羔羊的人，额上写着「他的名和他父的名」。这和7:3神众仆人额上的印相对，也指向末了的应许：「他的名字必写在他们的额上」（22:4）。人人额上都有一个名字：不是属于兽，就是属于羔羊。十四万四千人是谁，本研读在第7章已经列出主要的看法（同一群人从两个角度来看，包衡、毕尔；或末日蒙保守的以色列人，华尔伏），这里不再重复。

**3. 「仿佛是新歌」。** 「除了从地上买来的那十四万四千人以外，没有人能学这歌。」（14:3）这新歌就是5:9「因为你曾被杀」的歌；「买」也是5:9的同一个词。别人学不会，不是因为曲调太难，而是因为这首歌唱的是自己被买赎的经历。

**4. 「未曾沾染妇女，他们原是童身」。** 这句话很容易误读。
- **象征的读法：** 多数释经者（如包衡、毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）认为，这是借用旧约「圣战」军队的洁净规矩：以色列人出兵时要「远避诸恶」（申23:9），大卫的少年人出征时「没有亲近妇人」（撒上21:5）。十四万四千人在第7章按支派被数点，像一支军队；这里他们是羔羊的军兵。在启示录里，淫乱又常是拜偶像的比喻：巴比伦「叫万民喝邪淫、大怒之酒」（14:8）。保罗也要把教会「如同贞洁的童女，献给基督」（林后11:2）。照这种读法，「童身」是指没有与兽和巴比伦行属灵的淫乱，男女都包括在内。
- **较字面的读法：** 教会历史上有人从禁欲的角度读，把这节当作独身比婚姻更圣洁的证据；也有一些未来派作者认为，这是指末日一群在性方面守身的信徒。
不论哪一种读法，这一节都不是说婚姻或女人本身不洁：「婚姻，人人都当尊重」（来13:4）。

**5. 跟随、初熟的果子、没有谎言。** 「羔羊无论往哪里去，他们都跟随他」（14:4）：羔羊走的是被杀的路，跟随他的人也愿意这样走（12:11）。「初熟的果子」是献给神的（耶2:3），也表示后面还有更大的收成；包衡认为这正预备了14:14-16的收割。「在他们口中察不出谎言来」（14:5）呼应西番雅书：所剩下的人「不说谎言」（番3:13）。兽靠谎言迷惑人，跟随羔羊的人却不向这谎言屈服。他们「没有瑕疵」，不是因为本来无罪，而是因为「曾用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了」（7:14）。

> 要点：在兽好像掌权的时候，羔羊仍然站在锡安山上。跟随他的人额上写着他的名；他们是被他的血买来的，也跟着他走他所走的路。

## 深读 14:6-11 三位天使的宣告

**1. 「永远的福音」。** 「我又看见另有一位天使飞在空中，有永远的福音要传给住在地上的人，就是各国、各族、各方、各民。他大声说：『应当敬畏神，将荣耀归给他！因他施行审判的时候已经到了。应当敬拜那创造天地海和众水泉源的。』」（14:6-7）这很像保罗在路司得对外邦人所传的：要「归向那创造天、地、海，和其中万物的永生神」（徒14:15）。这是好消息，还是审判的宣告？
- **最后的悔改呼召：** 包衡等人认为，这是神在审判以先向万民发出的恩典呼召；启示录盼望列国悔改，归荣耀给神（11:13；15:4）。
- **审判的宣告：** 毕尔等人认为，重点在宣告审判已经到了：对拒绝的人是审判的消息，对属神的人是神国得胜的好消息。
- **未来派的读法：** 华尔伏等人认为，这是大灾难期间天使真实的宣讲，应验了「这天国的福音要传遍天下」（太24:14）。
这几种读法并不全然对立：审判还没有临到以前，悔改的呼召仍然发出。基督复临安息日会把14:6-12称为三天使的信息，认为这是交给他们这个运动的末世使命，并把14:7的「创造天地海」与守星期六为安息日连在一起；其他福音派教会一般认为，这三位天使的宣告是对历世历代众教会说的。

**2. 「巴比伦大城倾倒了！倾倒了！」** （14:8）这是启示录第一次提到巴比伦，到第17-18章才详细描写。这句话出自以赛亚书21:9（参耶51:7-8）。尼布甲尼撒曾夸口「这大巴比伦不是我用大能大力建为京都」（但4:30）。巴比伦代表以财富、权势和享乐引诱万民离开神的世界体系。她的倾倒到第18章才描写，这里却已经说「倾倒了」：许多释经者认为，这是先知的说法，把必定要发生的事说成已经发生。

**3. 「这人也必喝神大怒的酒」。** 「若有人拜兽和兽像，在额上或在手上受了印记，这人也必喝神大怒的酒；此酒斟在神忿怒的杯中纯一不杂。他要在圣天使和羔羊面前，在火与硫磺之中受痛苦。」（14:9-10）
- **两种酒：** 喝了巴比伦淫乱之酒的人（14:8），最后要喝神忿怒的酒。旧约多次说到神手中的杯：「地上的恶人必都喝这酒的渣滓，而且喝尽」（诗75:8；参耶25:15）。「纯一不杂」，是说这酒没有掺水冲淡。
- **火与硫磺：** 这是所多玛的画面（创19:24）。
- **「在羔羊面前」：** 审判人的，就是那曾被杀的羔羊（参6:16）。

**4. 「昼夜不得安宁」。** 「他受痛苦的烟往上冒，直到永永远远。那些拜兽和兽像，受它名之印记的，昼夜不得安宁。」（14:11）「烟往上冒」来自以赛亚书论以东：「昼夜总不熄灭，烟气永远上腾」（赛34:10）。福音派对这审判的性质有不同的理解（本研读在帖撒罗尼迦后书第1章已经介绍过）：
- **永远有知觉的刑罚：** 教会历来的主流看法（如奥古斯丁、乔纳森·爱德华兹，今天的巴刻、卡森、毕尔）认为，这里的「昼夜不得安宁」和20:10的「必昼夜受痛苦，直到永永远远」，都说明不信的人要永远有知觉地承受刑罚。
- **有条件不朽论（又称灭绝论）：** 少数福音派学者（如爱德华·富奇；约翰·斯托得也曾有保留地提出）认为，以赛亚书第34章描写的是以东永远荒废，不是以东人永远受苦，所以「永永远远」可能是指审判的结果永远不能挽回。多数福音派神学家认为，这很难与「昼夜」的说法相合。
约翰写这段话，首先是写给受压、可能向兽妥协的众教会。包衡指出，这个警告叫信徒看清楚：向兽屈膝看起来能保住饭碗和性命，结局却是永远失丧。中国教会有过只用地狱吓人的讲道，也有从来不提审判的讲台；这两样都不是启示录的做法。启示录讲审判极其严肃，指出路也极其清楚：那要审判人的羔羊，就是「曾被杀」、用血买人归于神的那一位（5:9）。耶稣是「救我们脱离将来忿怒的」（帖前1:10）；「如今，那些在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了」（罗8:1）。

> 要点：福音的呼召和审判的警告出于同一位神。拒绝敬拜他的人，最后要喝他忿怒的杯；投靠羔羊的人，不必喝这杯。

## 深读 14:12-13 在主里面而死的人有福了

**1. 「圣徒的忍耐就在此」。** 「圣徒的忍耐就在此；他们是守神诫命和耶稣真道的。」（14:12）这句话第13章已经说过（13:10）。有了第三位天使的警告，就看得出圣徒为什么要忍耐：宁可在地上受兽的逼迫，也不要受神永远的审判。「耶稣真道」原文可以理解为对耶稣的信靠，也可以理解为耶稣所传、所守的信仰；两种意思都指向忠心跟随他。

**2. 「你要写下」。** 「我听见从天上有声音说：『你要写下：从今以后，在主里面而死的人有福了！』」（14:13）这是全书七个「有福」中的第二个（第一个在1:3）。天上特别吩咐约翰写下来，好叫众教会都能念、能听、能记住。今天许多华人教会在追思礼拜上读这一节，墓碑上也常刻着「安息主怀」。
- **「从今以后」：** 原文可以连到「有福」，也可以连到「死」。许多释经者认为，约翰的意思是：自从羔羊得胜，特别是在兽的逼迫越来越重的时候，信徒的死不再是失败，而是蒙福。
- **「在主里面而死的」：** 上下文首先想到那些不拜兽像而被杀害的人（13:15），但这句话不限于殉道者；保罗也说到「那在基督里死了的人」（帖前4:16）。关键不在怎样死，而在死在谁里面。

**3. 「是的，他们息了自己的劳苦」。** 「圣灵说：『是的，他们息了自己的劳苦，做工的果效也随着他们。』」（14:13）天上的声音说完，圣灵亲自应声说「是的」。
- **「息了」：** 拜兽的人「昼夜不得安宁」（14:11），在主里死的人却得了安息。这不是死后没有知觉，而是「离世与基督同在，因为这是好得无比的」（腓1:23），等候身体复活的那一日。
- **「劳苦」：** 这个词（*kopos*）是辛苦、劳累的意思，主称赞以弗所教会时也用过：「我知道你的行为、劳碌、忍耐」（2:2）。忠心的劳苦是真实的，也会有尽头。

**4. 「做工的果效也随着他们」。** 「果效」原文是行为、工作（*erga*）。这些行为是「随着」他们，不是走在前面替他们买进门的票。
- **不是靠行为得救：** 他们有福，是因为「在主里面」；他们的衣裳是用羔羊的血洗白的（7:14）。我们原是神「在基督耶稣里造成的，为要叫我们行善」（弗2:10）。
- **但行为不会白白失落：** 「你们的劳苦在主里面不是徒然的」（林前15:58）。地上的人会忘记，神却不会忘记。

> 要点：信徒的死不是失败，而是安息。他们有福，是因为在主里面；他们在主里所做的，一样也不会失落。

## 深读 14:14-20 地上的两次收割

**1. 「云上坐着一位好像人子」。** 「我又观看，见有一片白云，云上坐着一位好像人子，头上戴着金冠冕，手里拿着快镰刀。」（14:14）这是但以理所见「驾着天云而来」的那一位（但7:13），也是第1章那位「好像人子」的（启1:13）。多数释经者认为他就是基督。少数人认为这是一位天使，因为另有一位天使吩咐他动手（14:15）；不过那位天使是「从殿中出来」的，传达的是神所定的时候（参可13:32）。

**2. 庄稼的收割：救恩还是审判？** 「伸出你的镰刀来收割；因为收割的时候已经到了，地上的庄稼已经熟透了。」（14:15）
- **收聚属神的人：** 包衡等人认为，第一次收割是收聚蒙救赎的人，第二次（葡萄）才是审判。主耶稣常用收割比喻收聚人归神（可4:29；约4:35）；十四万四千人是「初熟的果子」（14:4），也预备了这次大收成。
- **两次都是审判：** 毕尔等人认为两次都是审判，因为约珥书把镰刀和酒榨放在同一节里：「开镰吧！因为庄稼熟了；践踏吧！因为酒榨满了。」（珥3:13）
不论哪一种读法，到了神所定的时候，地上的历史要被收割，没有一个人能留在田里。

**3. 「神忿怒的大酒榨」。** 「那天使就把镰刀扔在地上，收取了地上的葡萄，丢在神忿怒的大酒榨中。」（14:19）以赛亚书里那位勇士说：「我独自踹酒榨」（赛63:3）；到了第19章，骑白马的「要踹全能神烈怒的酒榨」（19:15）。踹酒榨的是基督自己，不是圣徒；启示录从来没有叫信徒拿刀为自己伸冤（13:10；罗12:19）。

**4. 「那酒榨踹在城外」。** 「那酒榨踹在城外，就有血从酒榨里流出来，高到马的嚼环，远有六百里。」（14:20）
- **「城外」：** 「城」很可能指神的圣城，审判在城外，城里的人蒙保守。许多讲道的人从这里想到希伯来书：耶稣「要用自己的血叫百姓成圣，也就在城门外受苦」（来13:12）。经文本身没有提到这个关联，但福音的逻辑正是这样：将来要踹酒榨的那一位，先在城门外为我们受了苦。
- **「六百里」：** 原文是一千六百斯塔迪，大约三百公里，和合本换算成了中国的里数。一千六百是四乘四再乘一百，四常代表全地的四方（7:1）；许多释经者（如毕尔）认为这是象征审判遍及全地。「高到马的嚼环」是犹太启示文学描写大审判的夸张说法（参《以诺一书》100.3）。经文要我们看见审判的彻底和可怕，不是要我们去计算数字和地点。

> 要点：历史有收成的一天，人子要亲自收割，也亲自执行审判；那位将来踹酒榨的，就是先在城门外为我们受苦的那一位。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我听见从天上有声音说：『你要写下：从今以后，在主里面而死的人有福了！』圣灵说：『是的，他们息了自己的劳苦，做工的果效也随着他们。』」（14:13）刘妈妈八十六岁过世。她在这间小城教会里扫地、擦椅子、烧开水，前后四十年。冬天礼拜天一早，她总是第一个到，先把炉子生好，等大家进来时屋子已经暖了。她没有讲过道，也没有上过台；会友名单上，她的名字后面只写着「执事」两个字。追思礼拜那天来的人不多，几个年轻人在门口小声问：「刘妈妈是谁？」坐在后排的我，心里冒出一个念头：一个人这样过一辈子，值得吗？接着又是另一个念头：我呢？我没有她那样的四十年。我服事过，也半途放下过；有些服事是为了面子，有些是一边做一边抱怨。中国人说「人过留名，雁过留声」。到我走的那一天，会留下什么？会有什么随着我吗？

天上的声音没有说做工多的人有福，而是说「在主里面而死的人有福了」。福的根基不在做了多少，而在死在谁里面。和主一同钉十字架的那个强盗，没有一件善行可以随着他；他只说：「耶稣啊，你得国降临的时候，求你记念我！」主就对他说：「今日你要同我在乐园里了。」（路23:42-43）刘妈妈若在这里，大概也会说：她的四十年不是她的门票。她进入安息，和那个强盗一样，是靠着那位为罪人死的主。我那些半途而废、动机不纯的服事，也不能替我买什么；但它们并不决定我有没有福。我若在主里面，羔羊的血已经把我的衣裳洗白了（7:14）。

正因为这样，在主里面所做的事才不会白白失落。圣灵说「做工的果效也随着他们」：是随着，不是走在前面开路。地上的人已经忘了是谁生的炉子，天上却吩咐约翰「写下」。神「并非不公义，竟忘记你们所做的工」（来6:10）。那些冬天的清早、那些擦过的椅子，连同我那些不完全、却真是为他做的小事，他都记着。那些为面子、带着抱怨的部分，他已经赦免了。

这一切都是因为，有一位先为我们做完了工。主耶稣在十字架上说：「成了！」（约19:30）他的工作没有一样留给我们补上；安息日那天，他的身体安放在坟墓里，第三天他复活了。他的工作走在我们前面，我们的才能跟在后面。刘妈妈四十年的劳苦已经过去了；她不必再在冬天摸黑出门，不必再为谁没有来聚会而挂心。有一天，我的劳苦也要过去。在那位说「成了」的主里面，圣灵亲口说「是的」：他们息了自己的劳苦。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapters 12–13 drew back the curtain on the conflict behind events on earth: the dragon and two beasts use persecution, deception and a mark to make "all who dwell on earth" worship the beast (13:8). By now a faithful believer is bound to ask: what will become of those who follow the Lamb? Chapter 14 is heaven's answer. It falls into four parts:
- **14:1–5, the Lamb and the 144,000 on Mount Zion:** they bear the name of the Lamb and of his Father on their foreheads, and sing a new song only they can learn.
- **14:6–11, three angels' proclamations:** an eternal gospel; Babylon has fallen; those who worship the beast will drink the wine of God's wrath.
- **14:12–13, the endurance of the saints and the blessing of dying in the Lord.**
- **14:14–20, two harvests of the earth:** the Son of Man reaps the harvest of the earth; the grapes of the earth are thrown into the great winepress of the wrath of God.

At the end of chapter 13 the beast seems to control everything; at the start of chapter 14 the one standing on Mount Zion is the Lamb. The chapter sets two ways before the reader: the Lamb's name on the forehead or the beast's mark; resting from one's labors in the Lord (14:13), or having "no rest, day or night" (14:11).

## Going Deeper: 14:1–5 Those Who Follow the Lamb

**1. "On Mount Zion stood the Lamb."** "Then I looked, and behold, on Mount Zion stood the Lamb, and with him 144,000 who had his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads" (14:1). The beast rose from the sea; the Lamb stands where God has set him: "As for me, I have set my King on Zion, my holy hill" (Ps 2:6). Where is this Mount Zion? Many interpreters (e.g. Beale, Bauckham) take it as "the heavenly Jerusalem" (Heb 12:22), since the singing comes "from heaven" and is sung "before the throne" (14:2–3). Many dispensational scholars (e.g. John Walvoord) take it as an anticipatory picture of the Lamb standing with this company in the earthly Jerusalem at the start of the millennial kingdom. Both readings see that the beast's power is temporary and that the Lamb and his people will stand at the end.

**2. The name on the forehead and the mark of the beast.** The beast causes all "to be marked on the right hand or the forehead" (13:16); those who follow the Lamb bear "his name and his Father's name." This matches the seal on the foreheads of God's servants (7:3), and looks ahead to the final promise: "his name will be on their foreheads" (22:4). Every forehead bears a name: the beast's or the Lamb's. This study set out the main views of who the 144,000 are in chapter 7 (the same people seen from two angles, Bauckham, Beale; or Israelites preserved at the end, Walvoord); they are not repeated here.

**3. "A new song."** "No one could learn that song except the 144,000 who had been redeemed from the earth" (14:3). This new song is the song of 5:9, "for you were slain"; "redeemed" is the same verb as "ransomed" there. Others cannot learn it, not because the tune is too hard, but because it is a song about one's own redemption.

**4. "Who have not defiled themselves with women, for they are virgins."** This phrase is easily misread.
- **A symbolic reading:** most interpreters (e.g. Bauckham, Beale, Grant Osborne) see the purity rules of Israel's army in "holy war": when Israel went to battle they were to "keep yourself from every evil thing" (Deut 23:9), and David's young men kept from women on campaign (1 Sam 21:5). In chapter 7 the 144,000 were counted by tribe like an army; here they are the Lamb's soldiers. In Revelation sexual immorality is also a frequent picture of idolatry: Babylon "made all nations drink the wine of the passion of her sexual immorality" (14:8). Paul too would present the church "as a pure virgin to Christ" (2 Cor 11:2). On this reading "virgins" means those who have not committed spiritual adultery with the beast and Babylon, men and women alike.
- **A more literal reading:** some in church history read it ascetically, as evidence that celibacy is holier than marriage; some futurist writers take it of a group of believers at the end who are sexually pure.
On either reading, the verse does not say that marriage or women are unclean: "Let marriage be held in honor among all" (Heb 13:4).

**5. Following, firstfruits, no lie.** "It is these who follow the Lamb wherever he goes" (14:4): the Lamb's road was the road of being slain, and those who follow him are willing to take it (12:11). Firstfruits were offered to God (Jer 2:3) and promised a larger harvest to come; Bauckham thinks this prepares for the harvest of 14:14–16. "In their mouth no lie was found" (14:5) echoes Zephaniah, where the remnant "speak no lies" (Zeph 3:13). The beast deceives by lies; those who follow the Lamb do not yield to its lie. They are "blameless," not because they were sinless by nature, but because they "have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14).

> The point: when the beast seems to rule, the Lamb is still standing on Mount Zion. Those who follow him bear his name; they were bought by his blood, and they walk the road he walked.

## Going Deeper: 14:6–11 Three Angels' Proclamations

**1. "An eternal gospel."** "Then I saw another angel flying directly overhead, with an eternal gospel to proclaim to those who dwell on earth, to every nation and tribe and language and people. And he said with a loud voice, 'Fear God and give him glory, because the hour of his judgment has come, and worship him who made heaven and earth, the sea and the springs of water'" (14:6–7). This is much like Paul's word to the Gentiles at Lystra, to "turn... to a living God, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and all that is in them" (Acts 14:15). Is it good news or an announcement of judgment?
- **A last call to repentance:** Bauckham and others take it as God's gracious call to the nations before judgment; Revelation hopes that the nations will repent and give glory to God (11:13; 15:4).
- **An announcement of judgment:** Beale and others think the emphasis is on proclaiming that judgment has come: for those who refuse it is news of judgment, for God's people the good news that his kingdom wins.
- **A futurist reading:** Walvoord and others take it as a real angelic proclamation during the great tribulation, fulfilling "this gospel of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the whole world" (Matt 24:14).
These readings are not wholly opposed: until judgment falls, the call to repent still goes out. The Seventh-day Adventist Church calls 14:6–12 "the three angels' messages," holds that they are the end-time commission given to its movement, and links "him who made heaven and earth, the sea" (14:7) with keeping Saturday as the Sabbath; other evangelical churches generally take the three proclamations as addressed to all the churches in every age.

**2. "Fallen, fallen is Babylon the great."** (14:8) This is Revelation's first mention of Babylon, described at length in chapters 17–18. The cry comes from Isaiah 21:9 (cf. Jer 51:7–8). Nebuchadnezzar boasted of "this great Babylon, which I have built by my mighty power" (Dan 4:30). Babylon stands for the world-system that lures the nations away from God with wealth, power and pleasure. Her fall is described only in chapter 18, yet here she is already "fallen": many interpreters see the prophets' habit of speaking of what is certain as though it had already happened.

**3. "He also will drink the wine of God's wrath."** "If anyone worships the beast and its image and receives a mark on his forehead or on his hand, he also will drink the wine of God's wrath, poured full strength into the cup of his anger, and he will be tormented with fire and sulfur in the presence of the holy angels and in the presence of the Lamb" (14:9–10).
- **Two wines:** those who drink Babylon's wine of immorality (14:8) will at last drink the wine of God's wrath. The Old Testament speaks often of the cup in God's hand: "all the wicked of the earth shall drain it down to the dregs" (Ps 75:8; cf. Jer 25:15). "Full strength" means unmixed, not diluted with water.
- **Fire and sulfur:** this is the picture of Sodom (Gen 19:24).
- **"In the presence of the Lamb":** the one who judges is the Lamb who was slain (cf. 6:16).

**4. "They have no rest, day or night."** "And the smoke of their torment goes up forever and ever, and they have no rest, day or night, these worshipers of the beast and its image, and whoever receives the mark of its name" (14:11). The rising smoke comes from Isaiah on Edom: "Night and day it shall not be quenched; its smoke shall go up forever" (Isa 34:10). Evangelicals understand the nature of this judgment differently (this study introduced the views at 2 Thessalonians 1):
- **Eternal conscious punishment:** the church's mainstream view through history (e.g. Augustine, Jonathan Edwards, and today J. I. Packer, D. A. Carson, Beale) holds that "no rest, day or night" here and "tormented day and night forever and ever" in 20:10 show that unbelievers will consciously bear punishment forever.
- **Conditional immortality (annihilationism):** a minority of evangelical scholars (e.g. Edward Fudge; John Stott also suggested it tentatively) argue that Isaiah 34 describes Edom's lasting ruin, not the endless suffering of Edomites, so "forever and ever" may mean that the outcome of judgment can never be reversed. Most evangelical theologians find this hard to square with "day and night."
John writes this first of all to churches under pressure and tempted to compromise with the beast. Bauckham notes that the warning makes believers see clearly: bowing to the beast may seem to save one's job and one's life, but its end is eternal loss. Chinese churches have known preaching that used hell only to frighten, and pulpits that never mention judgment; neither is Revelation's way, which speaks of judgment with the utmost seriousness and points to the way out just as clearly: the Lamb who will judge is the one who "was slain" and by his blood "ransomed people for God" (5:9). Jesus is the one "who delivers us from the wrath to come" (1 Thess 1:10); "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Rom 8:1).

> The point: the call of the gospel and the warning of judgment come from the same God. Those who refuse to worship him will at last drink the cup of his wrath; those who take refuge in the Lamb will not have to drink it.

## Going Deeper: 14:12–13 Blessed Are the Dead Who Die in the Lord

**1. "Here is a call for the endurance of the saints."** "Here is a call for the endurance of the saints, those who keep the commandments of God and their faith in Jesus" (14:12). Chapter 13 has already said this (13:10). After the third angel's warning, the saints' endurance comes into focus: they would rather suffer the beast's persecution on earth than God's eternal judgment. "Their faith in Jesus" can be read as trust in Jesus, or as the faith Jesus taught and kept; both point to faithfully following him.

**2. "Write this."** "And I heard a voice from heaven saying, 'Write this: Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on'" (14:13). This is the second of the book's seven beatitudes (the first is 1:3). Heaven tells John to write it down, so that all the churches can read, hear and remember it. Many Chinese churches read this verse at memorial services, and Chinese Christian gravestones often carry the words "at rest in the Lord's bosom."
- **"From now on":** the phrase can be joined to "blessed" or to "die." Many interpreters take John's point to be that since the Lamb's victory, and especially as the beast's persecution grows heavier, a believer's death is no longer defeat but blessing.
- **"Who die in the Lord":** the context first brings to mind those slain for refusing to worship the beast's image (13:15), but the words are not limited to martyrs; Paul speaks of "the dead in Christ" (1 Thess 4:16). What matters is not how one dies but in whom.

**3. "Blessed indeed... that they may rest from their labors."** "'Blessed indeed,' says the Spirit, 'that they may rest from their labors, for their deeds follow them!'" (14:13). When the voice from heaven has spoken, the Spirit himself answers, "Yes."
- **"Rest":** worshipers of the beast "have no rest, day or night" (14:11), but those who die in the Lord enter rest. This is not unconscious oblivion but "to depart and be with Christ, for that is far better" (Phil 1:23), waiting for the day of the body's resurrection.
- **"Labors":** the word (*kopos*) means toil, weariness; the Lord used it in commending Ephesus: "I know your works, your toil and your patient endurance" (2:2). Faithful toil is real, and it will end.

**4. "For their deeds follow them."** The deeds (*erga*; the CUV renders it "the fruit of their work") "follow" them; they do not go ahead to buy their way in.
- **Not saved by deeds:** they are blessed because they are "in the Lord"; their robes were washed in the blood of the Lamb (7:14). We are God's "workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works" (Eph 2:10).
- **Yet the deeds are not lost:** "in the Lord your labor is not in vain" (1 Cor 15:58). People on earth forget; God does not.

> The point: a believer's death is not defeat but rest. They are blessed because they are in the Lord, and nothing they did in the Lord will be lost.

## Going Deeper: 14:14–20 Two Harvests of the Earth

**1. "Seated on the cloud one like a son of man."** "Then I looked, and behold, a white cloud, and seated on the cloud one like a son of man, with a golden crown on his head, and a sharp sickle in his hand" (14:14). This is the one Daniel saw coming "with the clouds of heaven" (Dan 7:13), and the "one like a son of man" of chapter 1 (Rev 1:13). Most interpreters take him to be Christ. A few think he is an angel, because another angel tells him to act (14:15); but that angel comes "out of the temple" and conveys the time God has set (cf. Mark 13:32).

**2. The grain harvest: salvation or judgment?** "Put in your sickle, and reap, for the hour to reap has come, for the harvest of the earth is fully ripe" (14:15).
- **Gathering God's people:** Bauckham and others take the first harvest as the gathering of the redeemed, and the second (the grapes) as judgment. The Lord Jesus often used harvest for gathering people to God (Mark 4:29; John 4:35); the 144,000 as "firstfruits" (14:4) also prepare for this great harvest.
- **Both as judgment:** Beale and others take both as judgment, because Joel puts sickle and winepress in one verse: "Put in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe. Go in, tread, for the winepress is full" (Joel 3:13).
On any reading, when God's time comes, the history of the earth will be harvested, and no one will be left in the field.

**3. "The great winepress of the wrath of God."** "So the angel swung his sickle across the earth and gathered the grape harvest of the earth and threw it into the great winepress of the wrath of God" (14:19). Isaiah's warrior says, "I have trodden the winepress alone" (Isa 63:3); in chapter 19 the rider on the white horse "will tread the winepress of the fury of the wrath of God the Almighty" (19:15). The one who treads the winepress is Christ himself, not the saints; Revelation never tells believers to take up the sword to avenge themselves (13:10; Rom 12:19).

**4. "The winepress was trodden outside the city."** "And the winepress was trodden outside the city, and blood flowed from the winepress, as high as a horse's bridle, for 1,600 stadia" (14:20).
- **"Outside the city":** "the city" is probably God's holy city; judgment falls outside it, and those inside are kept safe. Many preachers have thought here of Hebrews: "Jesus also suffered outside the gate in order to sanctify the people through his own blood" (Heb 13:12). The text does not draw that link, but the logic of the gospel runs that way: the one who will tread the winepress first suffered for us outside the gate.
- **"1,600 stadia":** about 300 kilometres; the CUV converts it into Chinese *li* ("six hundred *li*"). Sixteen hundred is four times four times a hundred, and four often stands for the four corners of the earth (7:1); many interpreters (e.g. Beale) take it as a symbol of judgment reaching the whole earth. "As high as a horse's bridle" is a hyperbole found in Jewish apocalyptic descriptions of the great judgment (cf. *1 Enoch* 100.3). The text means us to see how complete and fearful the judgment is, not to calculate figures and places.

> The point: history will have its harvest. The Son of Man will reap it himself and carry out the judgment himself; the one who will tread the winepress is the one who first suffered for us outside the gate.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And I heard a voice from heaven saying, 'Write this: Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord from now on.' 'Blessed indeed,' says the Spirit, 'that they may rest from their labors, for their deeds follow them!'" (14:13). Mother Liu died at eighty-six. For forty years she swept the floor, wiped the chairs and boiled water in our small-town church. On winter Sunday mornings she was always first to arrive; she lit the stove so the room was warm when everyone came in. She never preached and never stood on the platform; in the membership list the only word after her name was "deaconess." Few came to her memorial service, and at the door some young people asked quietly, "Who was Mother Liu?" Sitting in the back row, I found a thought rising: is a life spent like that worth it? Then another: what about me? I don't have forty years like hers. I have served, and I have put service down halfway; some of it I did to keep face, some of it grumbling as I went. Chinese people say, "A person passing leaves a name, as a wild goose passing leaves its cry." On the day I go, what will be left? Will anything follow me?

The voice from heaven did not say that those who did much work are blessed, but "Blessed are the dead who die in the Lord." The ground of the blessing is not how much we did but the One in whom we die. The criminal crucified beside the Lord had not one good deed to follow him; he said only, "Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom," and the Lord answered, "Today you will be with me in Paradise" (Luke 23:42–43). If Mother Liu were here, she would probably say that her forty years were not her ticket. She entered rest as that criminal did, through the Lord who died for sinners. My service, abandoned halfway and mixed in motive, cannot buy me anything either; but it does not decide whether I am blessed. If I am in the Lord, the Lamb's blood has already washed my robe white (7:14).

That is exactly why nothing done in the Lord is lost. The Spirit says, "their deeds follow them": follow, not go ahead to clear the way. People on earth have already forgotten who lit the stove, but heaven told John to "write." God "is not so unjust as to overlook your work" (Heb 6:10). Those winter mornings and those wiped chairs, together with my own small things done imperfectly but truly for him, he remembers. The parts done for face and with grumbling he has already forgiven.

All this is because someone finished the work for us first. On the cross the Lord Jesus said, "It is finished" (John 19:30). Not one part of his work was left for us to complete; on the Sabbath his body lay in the tomb, and on the third day he rose. His work goes ahead of us, so ours can follow behind. Mother Liu's forty years of toil are over; she need not go out in the dark on winter mornings any more, nor worry over who did not come to worship. One day my toil will be over too. In the Lord who said "It is finished," the Spirit himself says "Yes": they rest from their labors.
