## 本章脉络

第15章是启示录最短的一章，只有八节，却是七碗审判的序幕。全章可以分成三段：
- **15:1，天上的大异象：** 七位天使掌管末了的七灾，「因为神的大怒在这七灾中发尽了」。
- **15:2-4，玻璃海上的歌：** 胜了兽的人站在玻璃海上，拿着神的琴，唱摩西的歌和羔羊的歌。
- **15:5-8，殿开了：** 七位天使从天上的殿中出来，领受盛满神大怒的七个金碗；殿中充满了神荣耀的烟，没有人能进去。

奇妙的是，约翰在15:1刚刚介绍了七位天使，还没有说到他们倒碗，就先让我们听见一首歌。在最后的审判倾倒以先，我们先看见那些已经得胜、站在海边的人。审判的结局还没有描写出来，敬拜已经开始了。

## 深读 15:1 末了的七灾

**1. 「在天上有异象，大而且奇」。** 「我又看见在天上有异象，大而且奇」（15:1）。这是第12-15章里天上现出的第三个异象：先是身披日头的妇人（12:1），再是大红龙（12:3），现在是七位掌管末了七灾的天使。「大而且奇」这几个字，在15:3的歌里又出现了：「你的作为大哉！奇哉！」审判本身也是神奇妙作为的一部分。

**2. 「神的大怒在这七灾中发尽了」。** 「就是七位天使掌管末了的七灾，因为神的大怒在这七灾中发尽了。」（15:1）
- **「末了的」：** 七印、七号以后，七碗是最后一组审判。七号的审判还只临到「三分之一」（8:7-12），七碗却没有这样的限制；到第七碗倒出时，宝座上有声音说：「成了！」（16:17）
- **「发尽了」：** 这个词（*teleō*）的意思是完成、成就，和10:7「神的奥秘就成全了」是同一个词。神的忿怒不是无止境的脾气发作，而是有定数、有目的、会完成的公义审判。

**3. 七碗和印、号的关系。** 导读和第6章已经介绍过重述、先后套叠和介乎两者之间的读法，这里只补充七碗特有的一点：七号和七碗的次序非常相似，都临到地、海、江河、日头，都有黑暗、伯拉大河和末了的大震动（8:7-11:19；16:2-21）。主张重述的人认为，这说明七碗是更强烈地重讲同一段时期；主张先后发生的人认为，第七号「包含」了七碗，是主再来以前最后的一连串审判。无论哪一种读法，七碗都是最后、最完全的审判，好像出埃及的十灾（第16章会再看到这些对照），而且是从宝座和天上的殿发出来的。

> 要点：神的忿怒有定数，也会发尽。审判还没有倾倒，天上已经把它的结局定下了。

## 深读 15:2 玻璃海上得胜的人

**1. 「仿佛有玻璃海，其中有火搀杂」。** 第4章的宝座前「好像一个玻璃海，如同水晶」（4:6）；这里玻璃海中「有火搀杂」（15:2）。这火是什么意思？
- **审判的火：** 许多释经者（如毕尔）认为，这火指神的审判，正要从宝座前发出来。
- **试炼的火：** 也有人认为，这火让人想到圣徒刚刚经过的火一般的试炼。
- **出埃及的海：** 许多释经者都看见这里有红海的背景。在启示录里，海常是邪恶和混乱的所在，兽就是从海中上来的（13:1）；可是在宝座前，这海平静得像玻璃，被神掌管。
这几种理解可以互相补充：神的子民经过了海，经过了火，现在站在神掌管的海边。

**2. 「站在玻璃海上」。** 和合本作「站在玻璃海上」。原文的介词（*epi*）也可以译作在海边，许多英文译本就是这样译的。若译作在海边，就更清楚地呼应出埃及记：以色列人过了红海，「看见埃及人的死尸都在海边了」（出14:30），然后摩西和以色列人唱歌。神的百姓现在站在新的出埃及的对岸。

**3. 「那些胜了兽……的人」。** 他们「胜了兽和兽的像并它名字数目」（15:2）。第13章却说，兽「与圣徒争战，并且得胜」（13:7）。到底谁胜了？在地上看，兽杀了他们，好像得胜了；在天上看，他们没有拜兽像，没有受印记，至死忠心，才是真正得胜的人。他们得胜的方法，就是羔羊得胜的方法：「弟兄胜过它，是因羔羊的血和自己所见证的道。他们虽至于死，也不爱惜性命。」（12:11）
- **对中国教会的提醒：** 「得胜」在启示录里不是指事业顺利、身体健康、教会兴旺，而是在兽的压力下仍然敬拜神、跟随羔羊。在地上看来失败的人，天上可能称他为得胜者。

**4. 「拿着神的琴」。** 琴是「神的」，是神赐给他们的（参5:8；14:2）。他们手里的乐器和口中的歌，都是领受来的。

> 要点：在兽看来，他们是被打败的人；在神看来，他们是得胜的人。他们站在海的对岸，手里拿着神所赐的琴。

## 深读 15:3-4 摩西的歌和羔羊的歌

**1. 一首歌还是两首歌？** 「唱神仆人摩西的歌和羔羊的歌」（15:3）。多数释经者（如包衡、毕尔）认为这是同一首歌：摩西在红海边所唱的拯救之歌，在羔羊身上得着更大的成全。出埃及是旧约里最大的救赎；羔羊的死和复活，是那更大的出埃及。逾越节的羔羊救以色列人脱离埃及；被杀的羔羊用血买人归于神（5:9）。
- **哪一首「摩西的歌」？** 出埃及记第15章是在红海边唱的：「我要向耶和华歌唱，因他大大战胜，将马和骑马的投在海中。」（出15:1）申命记第32章也是「摩西的歌」，里面说：「他所行的无不公平，是诚实无伪的神」（申32:4），和15:3的「义哉！诚哉！」很相近。许多释经者认为两首都在背景里，而玻璃海的画面更接近出埃及记第15章。

**2. 一首用旧约织成的歌。** 「主神－全能者啊，你的作为大哉！奇哉！万世之王啊，你的道途义哉！诚哉！主啊，谁敢不敬畏你，不将荣耀归与你的名呢？因为独有你是圣的。万民都要来在你面前敬拜，因你公义的作为已经显出来了。」（15:3-4）这首歌几乎每一句都来自旧约：
- **「你的作为大哉！奇哉！……独有你是圣的」：** 「耶和华啊，众神之中，谁能像你？谁能像你－至圣至荣，可颂可畏，施行奇事？」（出15:11）
- **「你的作为大哉」：** 「耶和华的作为本为大」（诗111:2）。
- **「你的道途义哉」：** 「耶和华在他一切所行的，无不公义」（诗145:17；参申32:4）。
- **「谁敢不敬畏你」：** 「万国的王啊，谁不敬畏你？」（耶10:7）
- **「万民都要来在你面前敬拜」：** 「主啊，你所造的万民都要来敬拜你；他们也要荣耀你的名。」（诗86:9）
包衡指出一件很值得留意的事：这首歌一句也没有提到唱歌的人自己，没有说他们怎样忍耐、怎样得胜，也没有提到兽。出埃及记第15章大部分在唱「将马和骑马的投在海中」，这首歌却只唱神：他的作为、他的道路、他的圣洁、他的名。得胜的人在海边，眼睛只看着神。

**3. 万世之王还是万国之王？** 这里有一个抄本的差异。有些抄本作万世（或万代）之王，和合本采用这个读法；另有些抄本作万国之王，英文标准版采用这个读法；英文钦定本作众圣徒之王，但这个读法的抄本证据很弱。许多现代学者倾向万国之王，因为它和下一节「万民都要来」相合，也和耶利米书10:7「万国的王」一致。两种读法都是真的：他是永世的王，也是列国的王。

**4. 「万民都要来在你面前敬拜」。** 这句话是什么意思？
- **列国归向神的盼望：** 包衡等人认为，这首歌表达启示录对列国悔改归向神的盼望：神藉着审判和教会忠心的见证，使列国「归荣耀给天上的神」（11:13），将来「列国要在城的光里行走」（21:24）。
- **万膝都要跪拜：** 另一些释经者认为，这是说到了末了，所有的人都要承认神是公义的，不论甘心还是不甘心，正如「因耶稣的名无不屈膝」（腓2:10）。
这两种读法都不是普救论：启示录也清楚说，「凡不洁净的……总不得进那城；只有名字写在羔羊生命册上的才得进去」（21:27）。可是它们都看见，神的拯救远比以色列一个民族更大，从万国中都有人来敬拜他（5:9；7:9）。

**5. 「因为独有你是圣的」。** 这里的「圣」（*hosios*）用在神身上，指他完全圣洁、守约可靠；七十士译本的申命记32:4（摩西之歌）正用这个词形容神。神的审判之所以是「义」的、「诚」的，是因为他自己是圣的。他的「公义的作为」（*dikaiōmata*）已经显出来了：他不放过罪恶，也不忘记他受苦的百姓。这首歌叫我们在看见七碗以前，先知道倒碗的是怎样的一位神。

> 要点：得胜的人唱的不是自己的胜利，而是神的作为。摩西在海边所唱的救恩，在羔羊身上成全了，而且要扩展到万国。

## 深读 15:5-8 殿开了，七个金碗

**1. 「那存法柜的殿开了」。** 「此后，我看见在天上那存法柜的殿开了。」（15:5）原文直译是见证的帐幕的殿。旷野的会幕又称为「法柜的帐幕」（出38:21），因为里面存着刻有十诫的两块法版，就是神的「见证」。和合本把它译作「存法柜的殿」，点明了这层意思。11:19也说，天上的殿开了，「现出他的约柜」。神按着他自己的约和律法施行审判：七碗不是任意的报复，而是出于那位守约的神。

**2. 七位天使的装束。** 「那掌管七灾的七位天使从殿中出来，穿着洁白光明的细麻衣，胸间束着金带。」（15:6）这很像第1章人子的装束：「胸间束着金带」（1:13）；细麻衣也是祭司的衣服。这些天使像祭司一样，从神的圣所出来执行他的旨意。
- **抄本的差异：** 这里有两个只差一个字母的希腊词：细麻（*linon*）和石头（*lithon*）。几部重要的古抄本作石头，现代通行的希腊文校勘本也采用这个较难的读法，有人联想到以西结书28:13的宝石；可是穿着石头很难理解，所以和合本和多数译本都译作「细麻衣」。

**3. 「七个金碗」。** 「四活物中有一个把盛满了活到永永远远之神大怒的七个金碗给了那七位天使。」（15:7）这里的「金碗」（*phialē*），和5:8「盛满了香的金炉」原文是同一个词；那里说「这香就是众圣徒的祈祷」。和合本把两处译成不同的字，读者不容易看出这个关联。圣徒曾在祭坛下呼求：「给我们伸流血的冤，要等到几时呢？」（6:10）在5:8，拿着这些盛满祈祷之金炉的，正是四活物和二十四位长老；在这里，把盛满神大怒之金碗交给天使的，也是四活物中的一个。8:3-5也有类似的次序：众圣徒的祈祷和香一同升到神面前，随后天使把同一个香炉盛满坛上的火，倒在地上。许多释经者（如毕尔）因此认为，七碗的审判是神对他百姓祷告的回应。神没有忘记他百姓的祷告。

**4. 「殿中充满了烟……没有人能以进殿」。** 「因神的荣耀和能力，殿中充满了烟。于是没有人能以进殿，直等到那七位天使所降的七灾完毕了。」（15:8）这让人想起旧约的几个时刻：会幕立好的时候，「耶和华的荣光就充满了帐幕」，「摩西不能进会幕」（出40:34-35）；所罗门献殿的时候，「祭司不能站立供职」（王上8:11）；以赛亚看见异象时，「殿充满了烟云」（赛6:4）。为什么没有人能进殿？
- **神的荣耀不可靠近：** 许多释经者认为，神在审判中显出的圣洁和荣耀，是受造之物不能靠近的。
- **代求的时候已经过去：** 有些释经者认为，这表示审判已经定了，再没有人能进殿为世人代求、挡住这审判。
不论哪一种理解，这一节都叫我们敬畏。可是启示录的结局不是一座关闭的殿：到了新耶路撒冷，「神的帐幕在人间」（21:3），「主神－全能者和羔羊为城的殿」（21:22）。那时神的百姓不再站在殿外，因为羔羊已经把他们带到神面前。

> 要点：审判从神的圣所发出，出于他的圣洁、他的约和他百姓的祷告。那今天没有人能进去的殿，有一天要因羔羊向他的百姓敞开。

## 默想

**默想：** 「唱神仆人摩西的歌和羔羊的歌，说：主神－全能者啊，你的作为大哉！奇哉！万世之王啊，你的道途义哉！诚哉！」（15:3）黄弟兄在诗班唱男高音二十多年了。去年秋天，他九岁的女儿病了十个月，还是走了。今年复活节，诗班在排一首用启示录15:3-4谱成的诗歌。排练那晚，他站在第二排，钢琴前奏响起，大家张口唱「你的作为大哉！奇哉！」，他张了张嘴，却唱不出声音来。唱到「你的道途义哉！诚哉！」，他把歌谱合上了。排练完，有人拍拍他的肩膀说：「弟兄，要用赞美来胜过！」也有人私下说，他这个样子，复活节最好先不要上台。他自己也不知道，是不是还应该站在那里。有时他在心里对神说过很重的话，事后又不敢承认。

启示录15章唱这首歌的人，是已经过了海的人。他们站在玻璃海边，经过了火，经过了兽的逼迫，许多人是经过了死才站到那里的。神没有要求还在海里的人，用海边的声音来唱。圣经的诗歌本里，也有被掳的人问：「我们怎能在外邦唱耶和华的歌呢？」（诗137:4）这句问话，本身也被收进了神百姓的诗歌里。对神说重话，向他倒出心里的苦，也是向他祷告；约伯和诗篇的作者都这样做过；神回答了约伯，也没有丢弃他们。那晚说不出口的「义哉！诚哉！」，黄弟兄不必靠自己的力气唱出来。

而且这首歌本来就不是唱歌的人自己写的。它叫「羔羊的歌」，因为它唱的是羔羊所成就的拯救。而且羔羊自己也曾在最黑的夜里唱过歌：主耶稣被卖的那一夜，在逾越节的筵席上，他和门徒「唱了诗，就出来，往橄榄山去」（可14:26）。按后来犹太文献（《米示拿》）所记的逾越节习惯，饭后唱的是哈利路诗篇的后半部分（约是诗115-118篇）；马可没有说明是哪一首，但这很可能就是当晚所唱的。他是在走向十字架、走进神审判的深水以前唱的。他不是站在对岸才唱；他为我们走过那片海，替我们担当了神的审判，死了，又复活了。那条路，他已经开通了。所以今年复活节，这首歌可以由弟兄姊妹替黄弟兄唱，他站在他们中间，安静地听也可以。有一天，他也要站在玻璃海边，手里拿着神所赐的琴。

到那一天，得胜的人不会唱自己的忍耐，也不会唱自己失去的、或是自己曾经说过的重话；他们的眼睛只看着神。那首歌的最后一句，今天我们也可以一同念出来，哪怕声音很小：「主啊，谁敢不敬畏你，不将荣耀归与你的名呢？因为独有你是圣的。万民都要来在你面前敬拜，因你公义的作为已经显出来了。」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 15 is the shortest chapter in Revelation, only eight verses, yet it opens the judgments of the seven bowls. It falls into three parts:
- **15:1, a great sign in heaven:** seven angels with the seven last plagues, "for with them the wrath of God is finished."
- **15:2–4, the song beside the sea of glass:** those who conquered the beast stand by the sea of glass with harps of God and sing the song of Moses and the song of the Lamb.
- **15:5–8, the sanctuary opened:** the seven angels come out of the heavenly sanctuary and receive seven golden bowls full of God's wrath; the sanctuary fills with the smoke of God's glory, and no one can enter.

Strikingly, having introduced the seven angels in 15:1, John does not yet tell of the bowls being poured out; first he lets us hear a song. Before the final judgments are poured out, we see those who have already conquered, standing beside the sea. The outcome of the judgment has not yet been described, but the worship has already begun.

## Going Deeper: 15:1 The Seven Last Plagues

**1. "Another sign in heaven."** "Then I saw another sign in heaven, great and amazing" (15:1). This is the third sign to appear in heaven in chapters 12–15: first the woman clothed with the sun (12:1), then the great red dragon (12:3), and now seven angels with the seven last plagues. "Great and amazing" returns in the song of 15:3: "Great and amazing are your deeds." Judgment itself is among God's wonderful works.

**2. "With them the wrath of God is finished."** "Seven angels with seven plagues, which are the last, for with them the wrath of God is finished" (15:1).
- **"The last":** after the seals and the trumpets, the bowls are the final series of judgments. The trumpet judgments still struck only "a third" (8:7–12); the bowls have no such limit, and when the seventh is poured out a voice from the throne says, "It is done!" (16:17).
- **"Finished":** the verb (*teleō*) means to complete, to accomplish; it is the same word as in 10:7, "the mystery of God would be fulfilled." God's wrath is not an endless fit of temper but a measured, purposeful, righteous judgment that will be completed.

**3. The bowls, the seals and the trumpets.** The introduction and chapter 6 set out the recapitulation, sequential-telescoping and mixed readings; here we add only what is particular to the bowls. The trumpets and bowls follow a strikingly similar order: both strike the earth, the sea, the rivers and the sun, and both include darkness, the Euphrates and a final great upheaval (8:7–11:19; 16:2–21). Those who read the series as recapitulation take this as a retelling of the same period with greater intensity; those who read them in sequence take the seventh trumpet to "contain" the bowls, the last series before the Lord's return. On any reading the bowls are the last and fullest judgments, like the plagues of the exodus (chapter 16 will show these parallels), and they come from the throne and the heavenly sanctuary.

> The point: God's wrath is measured, and it will be finished. Before the judgment is poured out, heaven has already settled its end.

## Going Deeper: 15:2 Those Who Conquered, Beside the Sea of Glass

**1. "A sea of glass mingled with fire."** Before the throne in chapter 4 was "as it were a sea of glass, like crystal" (4:6); here the sea is "mingled with fire" (15:2). What does the fire mean?
- **The fire of judgment:** many interpreters (e.g. Beale) take it as God's judgment, about to go out from before the throne.
- **The fire of trial:** others think it recalls the fiery trials the saints have just come through.
- **The sea of the exodus:** many interpreters see the Red Sea in the background. In Revelation the sea is often the place of evil and chaos, and the beast rose from it (13:1); but before the throne the sea is calm as glass, under God's rule.
These can complement one another: God's people have come through the sea and through the fire, and now stand by the sea God rules.

**2. "Standing beside the sea of glass."** The ESV has "beside the sea of glass"; the CUV has "standing on the sea of glass." The Greek preposition (*epi*) can bear either sense. "Beside" brings out the echo of Exodus more clearly: after crossing the Red Sea, "Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore" (Exod 14:30), and then Moses and the people sang. God's people now stand on the far shore of a new exodus.

**3. "Those who had conquered the beast."** They "had conquered the beast and its image and the number of its name" (15:2). Yet chapter 13 says the beast was allowed "to make war on the saints and to conquer them" (13:7). Who conquered? Seen from earth, the beast killed them and seemed to win; seen from heaven, those who would not worship its image, would not take its mark, and were faithful unto death are the true conquerors. Their way of conquering was the Lamb's way: "And they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony, for they loved not their lives even unto death" (12:11).
- **A word for Chinese churches:** in Revelation, "conquering" does not mean a successful career, good health or a growing church, but going on worshiping God and following the Lamb under the beast's pressure. Someone who looks defeated on earth may be called a conqueror in heaven.

**4. "With harps of God in their hands."** The harps are "of God," given by him (cf. 5:8; 14:2). The instruments in their hands and the song in their mouths are both received.

> The point: to the beast they are the defeated; to God they are the conquerors. They stand on the far shore, holding harps that God has given.

## Going Deeper: 15:3–4 The Song of Moses and the Song of the Lamb

**1. One song or two?** "And they sing the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb" (15:3). Most interpreters (e.g. Bauckham, Beale) take this as one song: the song of deliverance Moses sang by the Red Sea finds its greater fulfillment in the Lamb. The exodus was the great redemption of the Old Testament; the Lamb's death and resurrection are the greater exodus. The Passover lamb delivered Israel from Egypt; the slain Lamb "by your blood... ransomed people for God" (5:9).
- **Which "song of Moses"?** Exodus 15 was sung by the Red Sea: "I will sing to the LORD, for he has triumphed gloriously; the horse and his rider he has thrown into the sea" (Exod 15:1). Deuteronomy 32 is also "the song of Moses," and says, "all his ways are justice. A God of faithfulness and without iniquity" (Deut 32:4), very close to "just and true" in 15:3. Many interpreters think both lie in the background, though the sea of glass is nearer to Exodus 15.

**2. A song woven from the Old Testament.** "Great and amazing are your deeds, O Lord God the Almighty! Just and true are your ways, O King of the nations! Who will not fear, O Lord, and glorify your name? For you alone are holy. All nations will come and worship you, for your righteous acts have been revealed" (15:3–4). Almost every line comes from the Old Testament:
- **"Great and amazing are your deeds… you alone are holy":** "Who is like you, O LORD, among the gods? Who is like you, majestic in holiness, awesome in glorious deeds, doing wonders?" (Exod 15:11).
- **"Great and amazing are your deeds":** "Great are the works of the LORD" (Ps 111:2).
- **"Just and true are your ways":** "The LORD is righteous in all his ways" (Ps 145:17; cf. Deut 32:4).
- **"Who will not fear":** "Who would not fear you, O King of the nations?" (Jer 10:7).
- **"All nations will come and worship you":** "All the nations you have made shall come and worship before you, O Lord, and shall glorify your name" (Ps 86:9).
Bauckham points out something worth noticing: the song says nothing about the singers themselves, nothing of how they endured or conquered, and it does not mention the beast. Exodus 15 sings at length of "the horse and his rider... thrown into the sea," but this song sings only of God: his deeds, his ways, his holiness, his name. The conquerors by the sea have eyes only for God.

**3. "King of the nations" or "King of the ages"?** There is a manuscript difference here. Some manuscripts read "King of the ages," which the CUV follows; others read "King of the nations," which the ESV follows; the King James Version has "King of saints," but that reading has weak manuscript support. Many modern scholars prefer "nations," since it fits "all nations will come" in the next verse and matches "King of the nations" in Jeremiah 10:7. Both are true: he is the eternal King and the King of the nations.

**4. "All nations will come and worship you."** What does this mean?
- **Hope for the nations' turning to God:** Bauckham and others hear in the song Revelation's hope that the nations will repent and turn to God: through judgment and the church's faithful witness the nations "gave glory to the God of heaven" (11:13), and one day "by its light will the nations walk" (21:24).
- **Every knee will bow:** other interpreters take it to mean that at the end all people will acknowledge that God is just, willingly or not, as "at the name of Jesus every knee should bow" (Phil 2:10).
Neither reading is universalism: Revelation also says plainly that "nothing unclean will ever enter it... but only those who are written in the Lamb's book of life" (21:27). Yet both see that God's salvation is far larger than one nation of Israel: from every nation people come to worship him (5:9; 7:9).

**5. "For you alone are holy."** The word for "holy" here, *hosios*, used of God, means his utter holiness and covenant faithfulness; the Greek Old Testament uses it of God in Deuteronomy 32:4, the song of Moses. God's judgments are "just" and "true" because he himself is holy. His "righteous acts" (*dikaiōmata*) have been revealed: he does not let evil go, and he does not forget his suffering people. Before we see the bowls, this song tells us what kind of God pours them out.

> The point: the conquerors do not sing of their own victory but of God's deeds. The salvation Moses sang of by the sea is fulfilled in the Lamb, and it will reach the nations.

## Going Deeper: 15:5–8 The Sanctuary Opened, the Seven Golden Bowls

**1. "The sanctuary of the tent of witness in heaven was opened."** "After this I looked, and the sanctuary of the tent of witness in heaven was opened" (15:5). The wilderness tabernacle was called "the tabernacle of the testimony" (Exod 38:21), because it held the two tablets of the law, God's "testimony." The CUV renders the phrase "the temple where the ark of the testimony is kept," making that meaning explicit. In 11:19 too, God's temple in heaven was opened, "and the ark of his covenant was seen." God judges according to his own covenant and law: the bowls are not arbitrary vengeance but come from the God who keeps covenant.

**2. The seven angels' clothing.** "And out of the sanctuary came the seven angels with the seven plagues, clothed in pure, bright linen, with golden sashes around their chests" (15:6). This resembles the Son of Man in chapter 1, "with a golden sash around his chest" (1:13); linen is also priestly clothing. Like priests, these angels come out of God's sanctuary to carry out his will.
- **A manuscript difference:** two Greek words here differ by one letter, linen (*linon*) and stone (*lithon*). Several important early manuscripts read "stone," and the standard critical Greek text adopts it as the harder reading; some link it with the precious stones of Ezekiel 28:13. But "clothed in stone" is so difficult that the CUV and most translations read "linen."

**3. "Seven golden bowls."** "And one of the four living creatures gave to the seven angels seven golden bowls full of the wrath of God who lives forever and ever" (15:7). "Bowls" (*phialē*) is the same word as the "golden bowls full of incense" in 5:8, "which are the prayers of the saints." (The CUV uses different words in the two places, which hides the link for Chinese readers.) The saints cried out beneath the altar, "how long before you will judge and avenge our blood?" (6:10). In 5:8 it is the four living creatures and the elders who hold the golden bowls of the saints' prayers; here it is one of the four living creatures who gives the golden bowls of wrath. 8:3–5 has a similar sequence: the prayers of the saints rise with the incense before God, and then the angel fills the same censer with fire from the altar and throws it on the earth. Many interpreters (e.g. Beale) therefore see the bowl judgments as God's answer to his people's prayers. God has not forgotten them.

**4. "The sanctuary was filled with smoke... no one could enter."** "And the sanctuary was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from his power, and no one could enter the sanctuary until the seven plagues of the seven angels were finished" (15:8). This recalls several Old Testament moments: when the tabernacle was set up, "the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle," and "Moses was not able to enter the tent of meeting" (Exod 40:34–35); when Solomon dedicated the temple, "the priests could not stand to minister" (1 Kgs 8:11); in Isaiah's vision "the house was filled with smoke" (Isa 6:4). Why could no one enter?
- **God's glory is unapproachable:** many interpreters think that God's holiness and glory, revealed in judgment, are beyond any creature's approach.
- **The time for intercession has passed:** some interpreters think it means the judgment is now decreed, and no one can enter to intercede and hold it back.
On either reading, the verse calls for awe. But Revelation does not end with a closed sanctuary: in the New Jerusalem "the dwelling place of God is with man" (21:3), and "its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" (21:22). Then God's people no longer stand outside, for the Lamb has brought them to God.

> The point: judgment goes out from God's sanctuary, from his holiness, his covenant and his people's prayers. The sanctuary no one can enter now will one day be open to his people because of the Lamb.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And they sing the song of Moses, the servant of God, and the song of the Lamb, saying, 'Great and amazing are your deeds, O Lord God the Almighty! Just and true are your ways, O King of the nations!'" (15:3). Brother Huang has sung tenor in the choir for more than twenty years. Last autumn his nine-year-old daughter died after ten months of illness. This Easter the choir is rehearsing an anthem set to Revelation 15:3–4. At rehearsal he stood in the second row; the piano introduction began, everyone opened their mouths to sing "Great and amazing are your deeds," and he opened his, but no sound came. When they reached "Just and true are your ways," he closed his music. Afterwards someone patted him on the shoulder: "Brother, overcome it with praise!" Someone else said privately that in his state he had better sit out Easter. He himself did not know whether he should still be standing there. At times he had said hard things to God in his heart, and afterwards did not dare admit them.

Those who sing this song in Revelation 15 have already come through the sea. They stand beside the sea of glass; they have passed through fire and through the beast's persecution, and many came there only through death. God does not ask those still in the sea to sing with the voices of the far shore. In Scripture's own hymnbook the exiles ask, "How shall we sing the LORD's song in a foreign land?" (Ps 137:4), and that very question was taken into the songs of God's people. Saying hard things to God, pouring out bitterness before him, is also prayer; Job and the psalmists did it; God answered Job, and he did not cast them off. Brother Huang does not have to sing by his own strength the "just and true" he could not sing that night.

And the song was never written by those who sing it. It is called "the song of the Lamb" because it sings of the salvation the Lamb accomplished. And the Lamb himself once sang on the darkest night: on the night he was betrayed, at the Passover meal, the Lord Jesus and his disciples "had sung a hymn" and "went out to the Mount of Olives" (Mark 14:26). By the Passover custom recorded later in Jewish sources (the Mishnah), the closing part of the Hallel (roughly Psalms 115–118) was sung after the meal; Mark does not say what they sang, but this is very likely it. He sang before going to the cross, before going down into the deep waters of God's judgment. He did not sing it from the far shore; he went through that sea for us, bore God's judgment in our place, died and rose again. The way through is open. So this Easter the brothers and sisters can sing this song for Brother Huang, and he can stand among them and simply listen. One day he too will stand beside the sea of glass with a harp that God has given.

On that day the conquerors will not sing of their own endurance, nor of what they lost, nor of the hard words they once said; their eyes will be on God alone. Even today we can say the last lines of that song together, however quietly: "Who will not fear, O Lord, and glorify your name? For you alone are holy. All nations will come and worship you, for your righteous acts have been revealed."
