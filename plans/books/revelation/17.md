## 本章脉络

第16章说，神「想起巴比伦大城来，要把那盛自己烈怒的酒杯递给他」（16:19）。第17-18章就停下来，仔细描写这座城和她的倾倒。第17章是一个异象，加上天使的解释：
- **17:1-6，异象：** 约翰在旷野看见一个女人骑在朱红色的兽上，手拿金杯，额上写着「大巴比伦」，喝醉了圣徒的血。
- **17:7-14，兽的奥秘：** 那兽「先前有，如今没有」，将要从无底坑上来，又归于沉沦；七头是七座山，又是七位王；十角是十王，他们要与羔羊争战，羔羊却必胜过他们。
- **17:15-18，淫妇的结局：** 那兽和十角反过来恨这淫妇，用火将她烧尽；因为神使他们遵行他的旨意。那女人就是「管辖地上众王的大城」。

这一章最响亮的一句话在中间：「羔羊必胜过他们，因为羔羊是万主之主、万王之王。」（17:14）巴比伦看起来华丽无比，兽看起来不可战胜，结局却早已定了。

## 深读 17:1-6 坐在众水上的大淫妇

**1. 两个女人，两座城。** 17:1-3和21:9-10几乎逐字相同：同是「拿着七碗的七位天使中」的一位说「你到这里来……指给你看」，同是「我被圣灵感动」；一次被带到旷野看大淫妇，一次被带到高山看新妇。许多学者（例如包衡）指出，这两段是刻意写成平行的；也有人把启示录称为两座城的故事：一座是大淫妇巴比伦，一座是新妇新耶路撒冷。约翰要读者把这两个女人放在一起看，然后问自己：我属于哪一座城？

**2. 「淫乱」是什么意思？** 「地上的君王与她行淫，住在地上的人喝醉了她淫乱的酒」（17:2）。在旧约先知书里，「行淫」常指拜偶像，也指为了利益而和列国结盟、交易。以赛亚说泰尔（推罗）「仍得利息，与地上的万国交易」（赛23:17），原文用的正是卖淫的字眼。耶利米也说：「巴比伦素来是耶和华手中的金杯，使天下沉醉」（耶51:7）。所以大淫妇的「淫乱」，主要不是指个人的性犯罪，而是一整个文化用财富、享乐和权势引诱人离开神。第18章会把这一点说得更清楚：「地上的客商因她奢华太过就发了财」（18:3）。

**3. 华丽的妆饰和金杯。** 「那女人穿着紫色和朱红色的衣服，用金子、宝石、珍珠为妆饰；手拿金杯，杯中盛满了可憎之物，就是她淫乱的污秽。」（17:4）紫色和朱红色是昂贵的颜料，是君王和富人的衣服。金子、宝石、珍珠，正是后来新耶路撒冷的材料（21:18-21）。巴比伦是一个仿冒品：她给人看见的，像是天上那座城的荣耀；她杯子的外面是金的，里面却「盛满了可憎之物」。

**4. 额上的名字。** 「在她额上有名写着说：『奥秘哉！大巴比伦，作世上的淫妇和一切可憎之物的母。』」（17:5）启示录里，人的额上写着什么，表明他属于谁：神的仆人额上有神的印，有羔羊和他父的名（7:3；14:1）；拜兽的人有兽的印记（13:16）。巴比伦也有她的名字。许多释经者认为，「奥秘」表示这名字要按灵意来明白，不是指幼发拉底河边那座古城本身（参11:8「按着灵意」）。

**5. 「就大大地希奇」。** 「我又看见那女人喝醉了圣徒的血和为耶稣作见证之人的血。我看见她，就大大地希奇。」（17:6）约翰为什么希奇？释经者看法不一：有人认为他是困惑，不明白这异象的意思；也有人认为，连约翰也被她的华丽震住了。天使接着问他：「你为什么希奇呢？」（17:7）在17:8和13:3，「希奇」也用在那些跟从兽的人身上。无论是哪一种，天使都不让他停在那里，而是揭开她背后的真相。

> 要点：巴比伦是新耶路撒冷的仿冒品：外面有金子、宝石和珍珠，杯里却满了可憎之物。她最大的力量不是刀剑，而是叫人希奇、叫人沉醉。

## 深读 17:5、18 谁是大巴比伦？

天使最后说：「你所看见的那女人就是管辖地上众王的大城。」（17:18）这座城是哪里？释经者主要有以下几种看法：
- **一世纪的罗马：** 这是多数学者的看法（例如包衡、毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）。罗马城以七座山著称（例如维吉尔的诗）；「七头就是女人所坐的七座山」（17:9）。一世纪管辖「地上众王」的大城，就是罗马。早期的基督徒也用「巴比伦」来称呼罗马：彼得在信末说「在巴比伦与你们同蒙拣选的教会问你们安」（彼前5:13），多数学者认为这是指罗马。
- **背道的耶路撒冷：** 一些过去派（例如肯尼斯·詹特里、大卫·齐尔顿）认为指主后70年被毁的耶路撒冷。他们指出，11:8的「大城」是「他们的主钉十字架之处」；巴比伦城里有「先知和圣徒……的血」（18:24），主耶稣也说耶路撒冷「常杀害先知」（太23:37）；旧约先知也常把不忠的以色列比作淫妇（结16章）。不过多数学者认为，「管辖地上众王的大城」和「七座山」更符合罗马。
- **教皇的罗马：** 历史派把巴比伦看作中世纪以后的罗马教廷。马丁·路德1520年的著作《教会被掳于巴比伦》以以色列被掳到巴比伦比喻教会受教廷辖制，书中也直说教皇制就是巴比伦的国度；许多宗教改革家和后来的新教作者也这样读第17章。这种读法要从改革家们当时所经历的历史来理解；今天许多新教学者已经不这样读，而且我们应当用真诚和尊重对待天主教的朋友。
- **将来重建的巴比伦城：** 一些时代论者（例如查尔斯·戴尔）认为，在末日，伊拉克境内古巴比伦的旧址会被重建，成为世界的经济中心；也有时代论者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，这是指末日一个世界性的宗教和经济体系。
- **一切敌挡神的世界体系：** 理想派（例如威廉·亨德里克森）认为，巴比伦代表这世界用享乐、财富和权势引诱人离开神的中心，在每个时代都有它的样子。本研读认为，约翰的读者首先会想到罗马；但巴比伦的「奥秘」也提醒我们：从巴别塔「要传扬我们的名」（创11:4）起，人类一直在建造这样的城。今天的巴比伦，不一定在某一个地名上，而在每一个叫人拜财富和权势、又逼迫神百姓的文化里。

> 要点：按本研读的读法，大巴比伦首先指一世纪的罗马，也指历代一切用荣华引诱人、又逼迫圣徒的世界之城。问题不只是她在哪里，更是我是否喝了她的酒。

## 深读 17:7-11 兽的奥秘

**1. 「先前有，如今没有，将要……上来」。** 「你所看见的兽，先前有，如今没有，将要从无底坑里上来，又要归于沉沦。」（17:8）这是对神「昔在、今在、以后永在」（1:4、8）和羔羊死而复活（1:18；13:3）的模仿；可是羔羊「直活到永永远远」，兽却「归于沉沦」。

**2. 「名字从创世以来没有记在生命册上的」。** 「凡住在地上、名字从创世以来没有记在生命册上的，见先前有、如今没有、以后再有的兽，就必希奇。」（17:8）跟从兽的人，是被它那仿冒的「复活」迷住了。这一节也给信徒很大的安慰：神的百姓不跟从兽，不是因为他们比别人聪明，而是因为他们的名字「从创世以来」就记在生命册上，就是「被杀之羔羊生命册」（13:8；参弗1:4）。

**3. 「七头」和「七位王」。** 「智慧的心在此可以思想。那七头就是女人所坐的七座山，又是七位王；五位已经倾倒了，一位还在，一位还没有来到；他来的时候，必须暂时存留。」（17:9-10）这是全书最难解的经文之一：
- **从罗马皇帝数起：** 若从犹流·该撒数起，第六位「还在」的就是尼禄，这支持早期的写作年代（例如詹特里）。若从奥古斯都数起，并略过主后68-69年在位很短的三位皇帝，第六位是维斯帕先，第七位提多只在位两年，第八位就是多米田。可是从谁数起、略不略过，学者没有一致的看法。
- **七个世界帝国：** 一些未来派（例如华尔伏、罗伯特·托马斯）认为，七头代表历史上先后的几个大帝国，例如埃及、亚述、巴比伦、波斯、希腊、罗马，以及末日的一个帝国。
- **象征的数字：** 毕尔等人认为，「七」在这里和全书一样表示完全：七位王代表一切敌挡神的世上权势；「五位已经倾倒了，一位还在」，是说约翰已经活在这段历史的后期，「暂时」表示剩下的时间不长。
这几种看法都承认：兽的权势是有期限的。

**4. 「第八位」和尼禄的传说。** 「那先前有如今没有的兽，就是第八位；他也和那七位同列，并且归于沉沦。」（17:11）尼禄在主后68年自杀以后，罗马帝国东部流传一种说法：尼禄并没有死，还要回来；后来出现了几个冒充尼禄的人，其中一个还得到帕提亚人的支持（塔西佗《历史》第二卷第八章；苏埃托尼乌斯《尼禄传》第五十七章）。较晚的犹太和基督教作品，例如《西卜神谕》第四、五卷，更把这说法发展成尼禄要带着东方的军队回来，甚至从死里复活。许多学者（例如包衡）认为，约翰借用了这个众人熟悉的恐惧，来描写兽那假冒的「复活」；不过这并不表示约翰相信这个传说，也有学者认为经文和这个传说没有直接的关系。无论如何，经文的重点很清楚：这第八位是七位的顶点，最后仍要「归于沉沦」。

> 要点：兽模仿羔羊的复活，叫地上的人希奇，结局却是沉沦。神的百姓站立得住，是因为他们的名字早已写在羔羊的生命册上。

## 深读 17:12-14 羔羊必胜过他们

**1. 十角是十王。** 「你所看见的那十角就是十王；他们还没有得国，但他们一时之间要和兽同得权柄，与王一样。」（17:12）十角出自但以理书第四兽的异象（但7:7、24）。有人认为指罗马各省的统治者或附庸王；有些未来派认为指末日与兽联合的十个君王或国家（例如华尔伏）；毕尔等人认为「十」表示完全，代表所有和兽同心的世上权势。他们的权柄只有「一时之间」，第18章三次说巴比伦的审判是在「一时之间」来到的（18:10、17、19）。对受压的小教会，这是安慰：那座城在神的计划里只有「一时之间」。他们「同心合意」（17:13），只是在敌挡羔羊这件事上；从地上看，兽好像赢了（13:7）。

**2. 「羔羊必胜过他们」。** 「羔羊必胜过他们，因为羔羊是万主之主、万王之王。」（17:14）旧约称耶和华是「万神之神，万主之主」（申10:17）；现在这个称号归给了羔羊，第19章骑白马的衣服上也写着同样的名字（19:16）。他得胜的方法和兽不同：他「得了胜」，是借着被杀（3:21；5:5-6、9）。

**3. 「蒙召、被选、有忠心的」。** 「同着羔羊的，就是蒙召、被选、有忠心的，也必得胜。」（17:14）和合本补上了「也必得胜」，原文只说同着羔羊的是这样的人；不过许多释经者认为，意思正是他们在羔羊的得胜中有分。许多释经者指出，三个词的次序很重要：「蒙召」和「被选」是神的作为（参罗8:30；彼前2:9），「有忠心」是蒙召之人的生命。他们不是先忠心，才被拣选；而是蒙了召、被选上了，所以能够忠心。

> 要点：羔羊必胜过兽和十王。同着他的人能得胜，根基在于神的呼召和拣选。

## 深读 17:15-18 兽恨淫妇

**1. 众水就是多民。** 「你所看见那淫妇坐的众水，就是多民、多人、多国、多方。」（17:15）耶利米称古巴比伦「住在众水之上多有财宝的」（耶51:13）；在这里，众水是她所控制、所迷惑的万民。

**2. 兽反过来吞吃她。** 「你所看见的那十角与兽必恨这淫妇，使她冷落赤身，又要吃她的肉，用火将她烧尽。」（17:16）这是很惊人的一幕：驮着她的兽，最后反过来吞吃她。这画面让人想起以西结书：神说要把不忠的耶路撒冷「交在他们手中」，就是她从前相爱的列国，「剥去你的衣服……留下你赤身露体」（结16:37-39）。罪恶的同盟没有真正的爱，最后彼此吞吃。过去派（例如詹特里）认为，这是指罗马军队毁灭耶路撒冷；多数学者认为，这是说兽的政治权势最后要毁掉它自己所驮的经济和宗教的诱惑。

**3. 「因为神使诸王同心合意」。** 「因为神使诸王同心合意，遵行他的旨意，把自己的国给那兽，直等到神的话都应验了。」（17:17）连恶人「同心合意」敌挡神，也在神的掌管之中。这不是说神是罪恶的源头，而是说神能使人的恶意成就他自己的旨意，像约瑟对哥哥们说的：「从前你们的意思是要害我，但神的意思原是好的」（创50:20）；也像早期教会的祷告：希律和彼拉多聚集攻打耶稣，却「成就你手和你意旨所预定必有的事」（徒4:27-28）。

> 要点：巴比伦和兽之间没有真正的爱，罪恶的同盟最后要自相吞吃。连恶人的计谋也在神的掌管之中，直等到神的话都应验。

## 默想

**默想：** 「那女人穿着紫色和朱红色的衣服，用金子、宝石、珍珠为妆饰；手拿金杯……我看见她，就大大地希奇。」（17:4、6）方姊妹五十多岁，在教会里一直很忠心。家里有一个盼望：在省城给儿子买一套房，过年回老家的时候，在亲戚面前也抬得起头。教会里一位弟兄介绍了一个理财平台，年息百分之十八。说明会在五星级酒店里开，台上的人还假借神的名，说神要祝福大家。方姊妹把家里的积蓄投了进去，又介绍了妹妹和团契里两位守寡的老姊妹。头一年利息按时到账，她还在聚会中作见证感谢神。第二年，平台爆雷了，创始人逃到国外。那两位老姊妹失去了丈夫留下的积蓄。从那以后，方姊妹再也没有来聚会；她不敢见她们。

叫我们希奇的，往往不是赤裸裸的罪，而是金杯。约翰看见那穿紫色朱红色、满身金子珍珠的女人，也「大大地希奇」；不论他是困惑，还是一时被震住，天使都不让他停在那里。受吸引的不只是贪心的人，忠心的人也会被这样的光彩迷住。天使问约翰：「你为什么希奇呢？」然后让他看见她背后的兽，看见那兽最后要「恨这淫妇……吃她的肉」（17:16）。巴比伦从来不爱她的客人。那些应许人发财的体系，最后吞吃的，正是信靠它们的人。

这并不是说受骗的人和骗人的人有一样的罪。设局诈骗的人要在神面前、也在法律面前负责。遇到这样的事，应当报警（罗13:4），教会也不可为了面子把事情压下去（「家丑不可外扬」）；如果有同工曾经推荐这类项目，教会也要公开、按真理处理；教会也要实际照顾那两位受损的寡妇（提前5:3）。至于方姊妹，她可以诚实地向那两位姊妹认错，尽力弥补；可是她的盼望并不在于她能赔回多少。

在同一章里，有两本账。巴比伦有她的账：谁投了多少，谁带来了多少人。神也有一本册子，就是17:8所说「从创世以来」写着名字的「生命册」。方姊妹的名字写在那本册子上，比她做那个买房的梦早得多，也不因为平台倒闭而被涂抹；那是「被杀之羔羊生命册」（13:8），是用羔羊的血写上去的。她可以带着羞愧回到主面前，也回到教会里，因为她站立的根基从来不是她的眼光，而是羔羊为她流的血。

巴比伦的金杯会在「一时之间」成为荒场（18:19），那套房子也许永远买不成。可是这一章的结局不是巴比伦，而是羔羊：「他们与羔羊争战，羔羊必胜过他们，因为羔羊是万主之主、万王之王。同着羔羊的，就是蒙召、被选、有忠心的。」（17:14）方姊妹在羔羊身边的位置，是从他的呼召开始的，不是从她的成功开始的；她所缺少的忠心，他也能在她身上造就。在亲戚面前，她也许永远抬不起头；可是她不是同着巴比伦，而是同着羔羊。得胜的，是羔羊，和同着他的人。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 16 said that God "remembered Babylon the great, to make her drain the cup of the wine of the fury of his wrath" (16:19). Chapters 17–18 now pause to describe this city and her fall in detail. Chapter 17 is a vision followed by an angel's explanation:
- **17:1–6, the vision:** in a wilderness John sees a woman sitting on a scarlet beast, holding a golden cup, with "Babylon the great" on her forehead, drunk with the blood of the saints.
- **17:7–14, the mystery of the beast:** the beast "was, and is not," and is about to rise from the bottomless pit and go to destruction; its seven heads are seven mountains and seven kings; its ten horns are ten kings who will make war on the Lamb, and the Lamb will conquer them.
- **17:15–18, the prostitute's end:** the beast and the ten horns turn and hate the prostitute and burn her up with fire, because God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose. The woman is "the great city that has dominion over the kings of the earth."

The loudest sentence in the chapter stands in its middle: "the Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings" (17:14). Babylon looks unrivaled in splendor and the beast looks unbeatable, but the outcome is already settled.

## Going Deeper: 17:1–6 The Great Prostitute Seated on Many Waters

**1. Two women, two cities.** 17:1–3 and 21:9–10 are almost word for word the same: one of "the seven angels who had the seven bowls" says "Come, I will show you...", and John is "carried away in the Spirit", once into a wilderness to see the prostitute, once to a high mountain to see the Bride. Many scholars (for example Bauckham) point out that the two passages are deliberately parallel, and some have called Revelation a tale of two cities: the prostitute Babylon and the Bride, the new Jerusalem. John wants his readers to set the two women side by side and ask: which city do I belong to?

**2. What does "sexual immorality" mean here?** "With whom the kings of the earth have committed sexual immorality, and with the wine of whose sexual immorality the dwellers on earth have become drunk" (17:2). In the Old Testament prophets, "prostitution" often means idolatry, and also alliances and trade with the nations for the sake of gain. Isaiah says that Tyre "will return to her wages and will prostitute herself with all the kingdoms of the world" (Isa 23:17). Jeremiah says, "Babylon was a golden cup in the LORD's hand, making all the earth drunken" (Jer 51:7). So the great prostitute's "immorality" is not mainly individual sexual sin but a whole culture using wealth, pleasure and power to lure people away from God. Chapter 18 makes this plainer still: "the merchants of the earth have grown rich from the power of her luxurious living" (18:3).

**3. Splendid adornment and a golden cup.** "The woman was arrayed in purple and scarlet, and adorned with gold and jewels and pearls, holding in her hand a golden cup full of abominations and the impurities of her sexual immorality." (17:4) Purple and scarlet were costly dyes, the clothing of kings and the rich. Gold, jewels and pearls are the very materials of the new Jerusalem (21:18–21). Babylon is a counterfeit. What she shows people looks like the glory of the heavenly city; her cup is gold on the outside, but it is "full of abominations."

**4. The name on her forehead.** "And on her forehead was written a name of mystery: 'Babylon the great, mother of prostitutes and of earth's abominations.'" (17:5) In Revelation, what is written on a person's forehead shows whom he belongs to: God's servants bear God's seal, the name of the Lamb and his Father (7:3; 14:1); the beast's worshipers bear its mark (13:16). Babylon has her name too. Many take "mystery" to signal that the name is to be understood spiritually, not as the ancient city on the Euphrates itself (compare 11:8, "symbolically").

**5. "I marveled greatly."** "And I saw the woman, drunk with the blood of the saints, the blood of the martyrs of Jesus. When I saw her, I marveled greatly." (17:6) Why does John marvel? Interpreters differ: some think he was perplexed, not understanding the vision; others think that even John was dazzled by her splendor. The angel asks him, "Why do you marvel?" (17:7) In 17:8 and 13:3 the same word "marvel" is used of those who follow the beast. Either way, the angel does not leave him there; he unveils the truth behind her.

> The point: Babylon is a counterfeit of the new Jerusalem: outside are gold, jewels and pearls, but her cup is full of abominations. Her greatest power is not the sword but her ability to dazzle and intoxicate.

## Going Deeper: 17:5, 18 Who Is Babylon the Great?

At the end the angel says, "And the woman that you saw is the great city that has dominion over the kings of the earth" (17:18). Which city is it? Interpreters hold these main views:
- **First-century Rome:** This is the view of most scholars (for example Bauckham, Beale and Grant Osborne). Rome was famed as the city of seven hills (as in Virgil); "the seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman is seated" (17:9). In the first century the great city with dominion over "the kings of the earth" was Rome. Early Christians also called Rome "Babylon": Peter closes his letter, "She who is at Babylon, who is likewise chosen, sends you greetings" (1 Pet 5:13), which most scholars take to mean Rome. 
- **Apostate Jerusalem:** Some preterists (for example Kenneth Gentry and David Chilton) take it to be Jerusalem, destroyed in AD 70. They note that "the great city" of 11:8 is "where their Lord was crucified"; that in Babylon "was found the blood of prophets and of saints" (18:24), and Jesus called Jerusalem "the city that kills the prophets" (Matt 23:37); and that the prophets often pictured unfaithful Israel as a prostitute (Ezek 16). Most scholars, though, think "the great city that has dominion over the kings of the earth" and the "seven mountains" fit Rome better.
- **Papal Rome:** Historicists read Babylon as the Roman papacy from the medieval period onward. Martin Luther's 1520 treatise *On the Babylonian Captivity of the Church* likened the church under the papacy to Israel in exile, and in it he called the papacy "the kingdom of Babylon"; many Reformers and later Protestant writers read chapter 17 this way. This reading should be understood in the setting the Reformers lived through; many Protestant scholars no longer read it this way today, and we should treat our Catholic friends with honesty and respect.
- **A rebuilt city of Babylon in the future:** Some dispensationalists (for example Charles Dyer) expect the ancient site of Babylon in Iraq to be rebuilt in the last days as the world's economic center; other dispensationalists (for example John Walvoord) take it to be a worldwide religious and economic system at the end.
- **Every world system opposed to God:** Idealists (for example William Hendriksen) see Babylon as the world's center of seduction, luring people from God by pleasure, wealth and power, and taking a new form in every age.
This study holds that John's readers would first have thought of Rome; but the "mystery" of Babylon also reminds us that ever since Babel's "let us make a name for ourselves" (Gen 11:4), humanity has kept building such cities. Babylon today is not necessarily at one place on the map, but in every culture that makes people worship wealth and power and persecutes God's people.

> The point: On this study's reading, Babylon the great is first-century Rome, and also every worldly city in every age that lures people with splendor and persecutes the saints. The question is not only "Where is she?" but "Have I drunk her wine?"

## Going Deeper: 17:7–11 The Mystery of the Beast

**1. "Was, and is not, and is about to rise."** "The beast that you saw was, and is not, and is about to rise from the bottomless pit and go to destruction." (17:8) This parodies God, "who is and who was and who is to come" (1:4, 8), and the Lamb's death and resurrection (1:18; 13:3); but the Lamb is "alive forevermore," and the beast goes "to destruction."

**2. "Whose names have not been written in the book of life."** "And the dwellers on earth whose names have not been written in the book of life from the foundation of the world will marvel to see the beast, because it was and is not and is to come." (17:8) The beast's followers are captivated by its counterfeit "resurrection." The verse also gives believers great comfort: God's people do not follow the beast, not because they are cleverer than others, but because their names have been written "from the foundation of the world" in the book of life, "the book of life of the Lamb that was slain" (13:8; see Eph 1:4).

**3. "Seven heads" and "seven kings."** "This calls for a mind with wisdom: the seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman is seated; they are also seven kings, five of whom have fallen, one is, the other has not yet come, and when he does come he must remain only a little while." (17:9–10) This is one of the hardest passages in the book:
- **Counting Roman emperors:** Counting from Julius Caesar, the sixth, the one who "is," would be Nero, which supports the early date (for example Gentry). Counting from Augustus and skipping the three short reigns of AD 68–69, the sixth is Vespasian, the seventh Titus reigned only two years, and the eighth is Domitian. But scholars do not agree on where to start or whether to skip.
- **Seven world empires:** Some futurists (for example Walvoord and Robert Thomas) take the seven heads as successive great empires, such as Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, Persia, Greece, Rome and a final empire.
- **A symbolic number:** Beale and others think "seven" here means completeness, as throughout the book: the seven kings stand for all the worldly powers opposed to God. "Five of whom have fallen, one is" says that John lives late in this history, and "a little while" means that little time remains.
All these views agree that the beast's power has a time limit.

**4. "An eighth" and the Nero legend.** "As for the beast that was and is not, it is an eighth but it belongs to the seven, and it goes to destruction." (17:11) After Nero's suicide in AD 68, a story spread in the eastern empire that he had not really died and would return; several pretenders claiming to be Nero appeared, one of them backed by the Parthians (Tacitus, *Histories* 2.8; Suetonius, *Nero* 57). Later Jewish and Christian writings, such as books 4 and 5 of the *Sibylline Oracles*, developed it into Nero returning at the head of eastern armies, and even returning from the dead. Many scholars (for example Bauckham) think John draws on this widely known fear to portray the beast's counterfeit "resurrection"; that does not mean John believed the legend, and some scholars doubt that the text alludes to it at all. Either way the point is clear: this eighth is the climax of the seven, and it still "goes to destruction."

> The point: The beast imitates the Lamb's resurrection and the earth marvels, but its end is destruction. God's people stand because their names have long been written in the Lamb's book of life.

## Going Deeper: 17:12–14 The Lamb Will Conquer Them

**1. Ten horns are ten kings.** "And the ten horns that you saw are ten kings who have not yet received royal power, but they are to receive authority as kings for one hour, together with the beast." (17:12) The ten horns come from Daniel's vision of the fourth beast (Dan 7:7, 24). Some take them as Rome's provincial rulers or client kings; some futurists as ten rulers or nations allied with the beast at the end (for example Walvoord); Beale and others take "ten" as completeness, standing for all the worldly powers of one mind with the beast. Their authority lasts only "one hour," and chapter 18 says three times that Babylon's judgment came "in a single hour" (18:10, 17, 19). For small, pressured churches this was comfort: in God's plan the city lasts only "one hour." They are "of one mind" (17:13) only in opposing the Lamb; from the earth the beast seems to win (13:7).

**2. "The Lamb will conquer them."** "The Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings." (17:14) The Old Testament calls the LORD "God of gods and Lord of lords" (Deut 10:17); now the title belongs to the Lamb, and the Rider on the white horse bears the same name on his robe (19:16). His way of conquering differs from the beast's: he "conquered" by being slain (3:21; 5:5–6, 9).

**3. "Called and chosen and faithful."** "And those with him are called and chosen and faithful." (17:14) The Chinese Union Version adds "will also conquer"; the Greek simply says who those with the Lamb are, though many interpreters think the sense is that they share his victory. Many note that the order of the three words matters: "called" and "chosen" are God's acts (see Rom 8:30; 1 Pet 2:9), and "faithful" is the life of those he has called. They are not chosen because they were faithful first; they are called and chosen, and so they can be faithful.

> The point: The Lamb will conquer the beast and the ten kings. Those with him overcome, and the ground of it is God's call and choice.

## Going Deeper: 17:15–18 The Beast Hates the Prostitute

**1. The waters are peoples.** "The waters that you saw, where the prostitute is seated, are peoples and multitudes and nations and languages." (17:15) Jeremiah addressed ancient Babylon, "O you who dwell by many waters, rich in treasures" (Jer 51:13); here the waters are the nations she controls and seduces.

**2. The beast turns and devours her.** "And the ten horns that you saw, they and the beast will hate the prostitute. They will make her desolate and naked, and devour her flesh and burn her up with fire" (17:16). This is a startling scene: the beast that carried her turns and devours her. It recalls Ezekiel, where God says he will give unfaithful Jerusalem into the hands of her former lovers, and "they shall strip you of your clothes... and leave you naked and bare" (Ezek 16:37–39). An alliance of evil has no real love; in the end its members devour one another. Preterists (for example Gentry) see here the Roman armies destroying Jerusalem; most scholars take it to mean that the beast's political power finally destroys the economic and religious seduction it once carried.

**3. "For God has put it into their hearts."** "For God has put it into their hearts to carry out his purpose by being of one mind and handing over their royal power to the beast, until the words of God are fulfilled." (17:17) Even the wicked being "of one mind" against God lies within God's rule. This does not make God the author of evil; it means he can make human malice serve his own purpose. As Joseph said to his brothers, "You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good" (Gen 50:20); and as the early church prayed, Herod and Pilate gathered against Jesus "to do whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place" (Acts 4:27–28).

> The point: There is no real love between Babylon and the beast; an alliance of evil ends by devouring itself. Even the schemes of the wicked lie under God's rule, until the words of God are fulfilled.

## Meditation

**Meditation:** "The woman was arrayed in purple and scarlet, and adorned with gold and jewels and pearls, holding in her hand a golden cup... When I saw her, I marveled greatly." (17:4, 6) Sister Fang is in her fifties and has always been faithful at church. Her family has one hope: to buy a flat for their son in the provincial capital, so that when they go back to their hometown for New Year they can hold their heads up before the relatives. A brother at church introduced an investment platform paying eighteen percent a year. At the launch in a five-star hotel, the speaker borrowed God's name: God wanted to bless them all. Sister Fang put the family's savings in, and brought in her younger sister and two widows from her fellowship. For a year the interest came on time; she even testified at a meeting, thanking God. In the second year the platform collapsed, and the founder fled abroad. The two widows lost the savings their husbands had left them. Since then Sister Fang has not come to church. She cannot face them.

What makes us marvel is usually not naked sin but a golden cup. John too, seeing the woman in purple and scarlet, covered in gold and pearls, "marveled greatly"; whether he was perplexed or for a moment dazzled, the angel would not leave him there. It is not only the greedy who are drawn in; faithful people can be dazzled by such splendor too. The angel asked John, "Why do you marvel?" and showed him the beast beneath her, the beast that would at last "hate the prostitute... and devour her flesh" (17:16). Babylon has never loved her guests. The systems that promise wealth end by devouring the very people who trust them.

This does not mean that those deceived are as guilty as those who deceive. The people who set up a fraud must answer before God and before the law. When this happens, it should be reported to the police (Rom 13:4), and the church must not bury it to save face ("don't air the family's dirty linen"); if a church worker promoted such a scheme, the church must deal with it openly and truthfully; and the church should care in practice for the two widows who lost their savings (1 Tim 5:3). As for Sister Fang, she can honestly admit her wrong to the two widows and make up what she can; but her hope does not rest on how much she can repay.

In this chapter there are two ledgers. Babylon keeps hers: who invested how much, who brought in how many. God has a book too: the "book of life" of 17:8, with names written in it "from the foundation of the world." Sister Fang's name was written there long before she dreamed of that flat, and it is not erased because a platform collapsed. It is "the book of life of the Lamb that was slain" (13:8), and her name is written there by the Lamb's blood. She can come back to the Lord, and back to the church, carrying her shame, because the ground she stands on was never her own judgment but the blood the Lamb shed for her.

Babylon's golden cup will be laid waste "in a single hour" (18:19), and the flat may never be bought. But this chapter does not end with Babylon. It ends with the Lamb: "They will make war on the Lamb, and the Lamb will conquer them, for he is Lord of lords and King of kings, and those with him are called and chosen and faithful." (17:14) Sister Fang's place beside the Lamb began with his call, not with her success, and the faithfulness she lacked he can build in her. Before her relatives she may never hold her head up; but she is not with Babylon. She is with the Lamb. The one who conquers is the Lamb, and those who are with him.
