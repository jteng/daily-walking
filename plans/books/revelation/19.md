## 本章脉络

第18章的末了，巴比伦城里一切的声音都止息了；第19章一开头，天上却响起了大声音。地上的君王和客商为巴比伦哭，天上的群众为神的审判唱「哈利路亚」。这一章是全书的一个转折点：前半章结束「巴比伦倾倒」这一大段（17:1-19:10），后半章开始描写基督的得胜和最后的审判（19:11-20:15）。全章可以分成四段：
- **19:1-5，天上的哈利路亚：** 群众、二十四位长老和四活物，为神审判了大淫妇而赞美他。
- **19:6-10，羔羊的婚筵：** 「羔羊婚娶的时候到了」；新妇蒙恩得穿细麻衣；被请赴婚筵的有福了。
- **19:11-16，骑白马的：** 天开了，那称为「诚信真实」、「神之道」、「万王之王，万主之主」的骑马出来。
- **19:17-21，神的大筵席和最后的争战：** 兽和假先知被擒拿，扔在火湖里；其余的被骑白马者口中出来的剑杀了。

这一章里有两个女子、两个筵席：
- **两个女子：** 穿紫色和朱红色衣服的大淫妇是巴比伦（17:4），穿光明洁白细麻衣的新妇是新耶路撒冷（19:8；21:2、9）。
- **两个筵席：** 「羔羊之婚筵」（19:9）和「神的大筵席」（19:17）。
每一个人最后都在这两个城、两个筵席中的一个里面。

## 深读 19:1-5 哈利路亚

**1. 新约中唯一的「哈利路亚」。** 「此后，我听见好像群众在天上大声说：哈利路亚！救恩、荣耀、权能都属乎我们的神！」（19:1）「哈利路亚」是希伯来话，意思是「你们要赞美耶和华」。整本新约只有这一章用这个词，一连四次（19:1、3、4、6）。诗篇有许多诗以这句话开头或结尾（诗106:48；113:1），其中第113-118篇是犹太人在逾越节为出埃及唱的诗；现在天上为神最后的拯救而唱。

**2. 为审判而赞美。** 「他的判断是真实公义的；因他判断了那用淫行败坏世界的大淫妇，并且向淫妇讨流仆人血的罪，给他们伸冤。」（19:2）这里的「伸冤」，和祭坛底下的灵魂所求的「给我们伸流血的冤」（6:10）是同一个词。圣徒的呼求现在得到了回答。天上的群众不是为人的灭亡而高兴，而是为神的公义而赞美：他的判断「真实公义」（参16:7），他没有放过「败坏世界」的，也没有忘记被杀害的仆人。现代的读者对这样的赞美常感到不安；可是对那些被下在监里、甚至被杀害的信徒来说（2:10、13），神必伸冤，正是他们能够不自己报仇的原因（罗12:19）。

**3. 「烧淫妇的烟往上冒，直到永永远远」。** 「又说：哈利路亚！烧淫妇的烟往上冒，直到永永远远。」（19:3）这是以赛亚书描写以东被毁的话：「昼夜总不熄灭，烟气永远上腾」（赛34:10）。地上的君王「远远地站着」，看见烧她的烟就哭（18:9）；天上的群众看见同样的烟，却说「哈利路亚」。同一件事，从地上看是灾难，从天上看是神的公义得胜。

**4. 「无论大小」。** 「那二十四位长老与四活物就俯伏敬拜坐宝座的神，说：阿们！哈利路亚！」（19:4）这是二十四位长老和四活物在书中最后一次出现。第4章他们第一次出现时，就在宝座前俯伏敬拜（4:10）；到了这里，他们仍然在做同一件事。然后「有声音从宝座出来说：神的众仆人哪，凡敬畏他的，无论大小，都要赞美我们的神！」（19:5）「无论大小」（参11:18）：在巴比伦的货单上，人有贵贱；在神的宝座前，小人物和大人物一同被召来赞美。

> 要点：天上为神的审判唱「哈利路亚」，因为他的判断真实公义。圣徒不必自己伸冤，因为神必为他们伸冤。

## 深读 19:6-10 羔羊婚娶的时候到了

**1. 「主－我们的神、全能者作王了」。** 「我听见好像群众的声音，众水的声音，大雷的声音，说：哈利路亚！因为主－我们的神、全能者作王了。」（19:6）「作王了」原文是不定过去时，许多释经者认为意思是已经开始作王：神一直是王，现在他的王权在全地完全显明出来（参11:17）。

**2. 羔羊的新妇。** 「我们要欢喜快乐，将荣耀归给他。因为，羔羊婚娶的时候到了；新妇也自己预备好了」（19:7）。神和他百姓的关系，在旧约里常被比作婚姻：「因为造你的是你的丈夫；万军之耶和华是他的名」（赛54:5）。新约把这个画面用在基督和教会身上：保罗说，基督「爱教会，为教会舍己」，要把她「献给自己，作个荣耀的教会」（弗5:25-27）。在旧约里，以色列常常像不忠的妻子；在这里，新妇终于「预备好了」，要与羔羊完婚。

**3. 「蒙恩得穿」。** 「就蒙恩得穿光明洁白的细麻衣。（这细麻衣就是圣徒所行的义。）」（19:8）这一节有两句话，要放在一起读：
- **「新妇也自己预备好了」：** 新妇不是被动的，她真的预备了自己。
- **「蒙恩得穿」：** 原文的意思是被赐给她（*edothē*），许多释经者认为这是一个神作主语的被动式，意思是：这件衣服是神赐的。和合本译作「蒙恩」，把这一点说得很清楚。
那么，「圣徒所行的义」是什么意思？
- **神使圣徒行出来的义行：** 许多释经者（例如毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）认为，「义」在原文是复数（*dikaiōmata*），指圣徒一件一件的义行，也就是他们在逼迫中忠心的见证和顺服；可是连这些义行也是神「赐」的，正如保罗说，我们原是神的工作，为要叫我们行善，「就是神所预备叫我们行的」（弗2:10）。
- **神为圣徒所下的公义判决：** 也有人认为，这个词可以指神为圣徒伸冤、宣告他们为义的判决（参19:2）。
- **基督所赐的义：** 许多讲道的人把这件细麻衣读作基督归给信徒的义，像以赛亚所说「以公义为袍给我披上」（赛61:10）。经文的重点虽然是「圣徒所行的」，但这种读法提醒我们，圣徒能穿上白衣，根本是因为他们「曾用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了」（7:14）。
无论采取哪一种看法，次序都很清楚：先有羔羊的血和神的恩赐，然后才有圣徒的义行；而圣徒的义行本身，也是神所赐的。大淫妇的华丽，是靠买卖人口、流圣徒的血得来的（18:13、24）；新妇的衣服却是白白赐给她的。

**4. 「凡被请赴羔羊之婚筵的有福了」。** 「天使吩咐我说：『你要写上：凡被请赴羔羊之婚筵的有福了！』又对我说：『这是神真实的话。』」（19:9）这是全书七次「有福」中的第四次。新妇和被请的客人，看起来是两群人；许多释经者认为，其实是同一群人从两个角度来看：整个教会是新妇，一个一个的信徒是被请的客人（有些时代论者，例如约翰·华尔伏，则认为新妇是教会，客人是旧约的圣徒等其他的人）。以赛亚早就预言，万军之耶和华「必为万民用肥甘设摆筵席」（赛25:6）；主耶稣也说，「从东从西，将有许多人来，在天国里与亚伯拉罕、以撒、雅各一同坐席」（太8:11）。天使特别加上一句：「这是神真实的话。」这个邀请不是客套话。

**5. 「你要敬拜神」。** 约翰听见这些，就俯伏在天使脚前要拜他；天使说：「千万不可！……你要敬拜神。」（19:10）这件事在22:8-9又发生了一次，导读（第00章）的默想已经提过。天使说「预言中的灵意乃是为耶稣作见证」（19:10）：一切真预言的中心都是耶稣。连最荣耀的天使也只是和约翰「同是作仆人的」，敬拜只归给神。可是羔羊却和坐宝座的一同受敬拜（5:8-14）：天使不敢领受的敬拜，羔羊理当领受，因为他是神。

> 要点：新妇的细麻衣是她「蒙恩得穿」的。被请赴羔羊婚筵的人有福，是因为请他们的是神，这邀请是「神真实的话」。

## 深读 19:11-16 骑白马的称为诚信真实

**1. 「我观看，见天开了」。** 第4章，约翰看见「天上有门开了」（4:1），他被召上去观看；这里是天本身开了，基督要从天上出来。旧约里，以西结也是看见「天就开了」，然后看见神的异象（结1:1）。多数释经者（例如约翰·华尔伏、乔治·赖德、毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）认为，19:11-16描写的是基督第二次降临；也有些后千禧年派和过去派的释经者（例如华腓德、大卫·齐尔顿）认为，这段经文主要描写基督藉着福音在历史中得胜列国。本研读和多数释经者一样，读作基督真实、身体的再来。

**2. 白马。** 「有一匹白马，骑在马上的称为诚信真实，他审判，争战，都按着公义。」（19:11）第6章第一印也有一匹白马（6:2），那位骑马的是谁，第6章已经讨论过。这里的骑马者是谁，经文却说得清清楚楚。他的名字一个接一个显明出来：
- **「诚信真实」：** 他在第1章被称为「那诚实作见证的」（1:5），在给老底嘉的信中自称「为诚信真实见证的」（3:14）。他所说的都必成就。
- **其他的名字：** 还有一个「除了他自己没有人知道」的名字（19:12）；「神之道」（19:13），新约中只有约翰的著作这样称呼耶稣（约1:1、14；约一1:1）；「万王之王，万主之主」（19:16），第17章已经宣告过（17:14）。

**3. 眼睛、冠冕和衣服。**
- **「他的眼睛如火焰」（19:12）：** 和第1章人子的眼目一样（1:14），没有什么能瞒过他。
- **「他头上戴着许多冠冕」（19:12）：** 大龙七头上戴着七个冠冕（12:3），兽在十角上戴着十个冠冕（13:1）；骑白马的戴着「许多」冠冕。他们的权柄是篡夺来的，有限的；他的权柄是真实的，没有限量。
- **「他穿着溅了血的衣服」（19:13）：** 这是谁的血？释经者有几种看法。许多人（例如约翰·华尔伏、格兰特·奥斯本）根据以赛亚书「他们的血溅在我衣服上」（赛63:3）认为，这是仇敌的血，是神审判的画面。另一些人注意到，这时争战还没有开始，而启示录中的「血」大多指羔羊自己的血（1:5；5:9；7:14；12:11），所以认为是他在十字架上所流的血；也有人（例如乔治·凯尔德，G. B. Caird）认为是殉道者的血。这几种读法不一定互相排斥：施行审判的，正是那位曾经为罪人流血的。

**4. 「有利剑从他口中出来」。** 「在天上的众军骑着白马，穿着细麻衣，又白又洁，跟随他。有利剑从他口中出来，可以击杀列国。」（19:14-15）
- **众军是谁？** 有人认为是天使（太25:31；帖后1:7），有人认为是圣徒，因为他们穿着和新妇一样的细麻衣（19:8），而第17章也说「同着羔羊的，就是蒙召、被选、有忠心的」（17:14）。也许两者都有。值得注意的是，经文没有说他们动手打仗。
- **口中的剑：** 剑不在他手中，而是从他口中出来（1:16；2:16）。这来自以赛亚书：弥赛亚要「以口中的杖击打世界，以嘴里的气杀戮恶人」（赛11:4；参49:2）。保罗也说，主耶稣要「用口中的气灭绝」那不法的人（帖后2:8）。他得胜，是凭他的话。那用一句话创造世界的道，也要用一句话审判世界。
- **铁杖和酒榨：** 「他必用铁杖辖管他们」在第12章已经说过（12:5），出自诗篇第2篇（诗2:9）；「踹全能神烈怒的酒榨」接续第14章的收割（14:19-20）和以赛亚书的「我独自踹酒榨」（赛63:3）。

> 要点：骑白马的称为「诚信真实」。那曾经为我们流血的，要凭着公义审判世界，凭他口中的话得胜。

## 深读 19:17-21 神的大筵席和最后的争战

**1. 另一个筵席。** 「我又看见一位天使站在日头中，向天空所飞的鸟大声喊着说：『你们聚集来赴神的大筵席，可以吃君王与将军的肉……以及大小人民的肉。』」（19:17-18）这个可怕的画面来自以西结书第39章：神召飞鸟和走兽来吃歌革军队的肉（结39:17-20）。这里的「大小」，和19:5的「无论大小」用同样的话：敬畏神的「无论大小」，被召来赞美他；与羔羊为敌的「大小人民」，却成了飞鸟的筵席。人人都被请赴筵席，不是赴这一个，就是那一个。

**2. 没有描写的争战。** 「我看见那兽和地上的君王，并他们的众军都聚集，要与骑白马的并他的军兵争战。」（19:19）可是接下来经文并没有描写任何战斗，只说「那兽被擒拿」（19:20）。人们常说的末日大战，其实没有真正交手。第16章的哈米吉多顿，众王被聚集「在神全能者的大日聚集争战」（16:14-16）；第20章，撒但又迷惑歌革和玛各「叫他们聚集争战」（20:8）。这几处和这一处是什么关系？
- **同一场争战的几次描写：** 许多无千禧年派和理想派的释经者（例如毕尔、威廉·亨德里克森）指出，这三处的「争战」在原文都带冠词（*ton polemon*），而19:17-18和20:8-9又都用了以西结书第38-39章歌革的画面，所以认为它们是从不同角度描写主再来时同一场最后的争战。
- **先后发生的事：** 时代论者（例如约翰·华尔伏）和历史前千禧年派（例如乔治·赖德）则认为，第19章到第20章是按次序发生的：基督先再来，打败兽和假先知，然后才有一千年，一千年以后才有歌革和玛各的争战。
这个问题和千禧年的看法直接相关，第20章会详细讨论。不同看法的人都同意：基督要亲自再来，他要完全得胜。

**3. 兽和假先知的结局。** 「那兽被擒拿；那在兽面前曾行奇事、迷惑受兽印记和拜兽像之人的假先知，也与兽同被擒拿。他们两个就活活地被扔在烧着硫磺的火湖里」（19:20）。这个假先知就是第13章那「从地中上来」、叫人拜头一个兽的（13:11-12）。「火湖」在这里第一次出现，后来魔鬼（20:10）、死亡和阴间（20:14）也要被扔在里面。那些曾经叫全世界惊奇、叫人不敢不拜的势力，在骑白马的面前没有一点抵挡的能力。「其余的被骑白马者口中出来的剑杀了」（19:21）。这一节极其严肃，我们不应该轻描淡写，也不应该带着幸灾乐祸的心去读。可是今天，从他口中出来的话仍然是邀请：「圣灵和新妇都说：『来！』……愿意的，都可以白白取生命的水喝。」（22:17）那将来要审判列国的道，今天仍在呼召人悔改；他曾在十字架上为罪人流血（1:5；5:9），这就是他愿意接纳罪人的凭据。

> 要点：最后的争战还没有开始就结束了，因为得胜的是骑白马的那一位。今天他口中的话仍然在邀请人，来赴羔羊的婚筵。

## 默想

**默想：** 「新妇也自己预备好了，就蒙恩得穿光明洁白的细麻衣。（这细麻衣就是圣徒所行的义。）」（19:7-8）一位姊妹小时候家里很穷。有一年表姐结婚，母亲向邻居借了一件衣服给她穿去喝喜酒。衣服太大，袖子卷了两道。那天在酒席上，她几乎不敢夹菜，怕把借来的衣服弄脏，也怕有人认出这是谁家的衣服。几十年过去了，她信主多年，在教会里也一直服事。可是读到「这细麻衣就是圣徒所行的义」，她心里一紧。她想起这些年对婆婆的冷淡，在单位里为了推卸责任说过的谎，还有好几年几乎没有打开圣经。她想，如果羔羊的婚筵要看各人所行的义，那她去赴席，大概也会像那年一样：穿着一件不合身的衣服，坐在那里提心吊胆，怕被主人看出来，像比喻里那个没有穿礼服、被问得无言可答的人（太22:11-12）。

可是这一节经文里最重要的两个字，是「蒙恩」。新妇的细麻衣不是借来的，也不是她自己织的，而是「赐」给她的。连「圣徒所行的义」，也是神预备叫我们行的（弗2:10）；而圣徒所以能穿白衣，是因为羔羊为她的冷淡和谎言流了血，他们「曾用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了」（7:14）。天使也没有说配得赴羔羊婚筵的人有福了，而是说「凡被请赴羔羊之婚筵的有福了」（19:9）。这个筵席是从邀请开始的，不是从检查开始的。至于比喻里那个被赶出去的人，那天坐满筵席的，本来都是从大路上召来的，「不论善恶」（太22:10）；许多释经者认为，他的问题不在于他穷，或者衣服不够体面，而在于他不把王的筵席当一回事，不肯照着王的意思来赴席。

她对婆婆的冷淡和她说过的谎都是真实的，不必假装没有发生。她可以在主面前承认，也可以找机会向婆婆道歉，去补上那些谎言造成的亏损。但她这样做，不是为了换一件衣服，好叫自己配得上筵席；而是因为她已经穿上了一件不必还的衣服。那些义行，也是在她里面运行的神所成就的（腓2:13）。她不必再像小时候那样，一边坐在席上，一边担心衣服什么时候被收回去。

天使说：「这是神真实的话。」（19:9）接着，约翰看见天开了，那发出邀请的羔羊骑着白马出来，他的名字「称为诚信真实」（19:11）。他穿着溅了血的衣服，头上戴着许多冠冕，口中的话可以击杀列国；可是他也是那曾经在加利利的迦拿被请去赴席、坐在乡村婚宴上的主（约2:1-2）。请帖上写的话是真实的，因为写请帖的那一位，他的名字就叫诚信真实。

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 18 every sound in Babylon fell silent; at the start of chapter 19 heaven rings with a loud voice. The kings and merchants of the earth wept over Babylon; the multitude in heaven sings "Hallelujah" over God's judgment. The chapter is a turning point in the book: its first half closes the long section on Babylon's fall (17:1–19:10), and its second half begins the account of Christ's victory and the last judgment (19:11–20:15). It falls into four parts:
- **19:1–5, hallelujahs in heaven:** the multitude, the twenty-four elders and the four living creatures praise God for judging the great prostitute.
- **19:6–10, the marriage supper of the Lamb:** "the marriage of the Lamb has come"; the Bride is granted fine linen; blessed are those invited to the marriage supper.
- **19:11–16, the rider on the white horse:** heaven opens, and the one called "Faithful and True," "The Word of God," "King of kings and Lord of lords" rides out.
- **19:17–21, the great supper of God and the last battle:** the beast and the false prophet are captured and thrown into the lake of fire; the rest are slain by the sword from the rider's mouth.

The chapter sets two women and two suppers side by side:
- **Two women:** the prostitute in purple and scarlet is Babylon (17:4); the Bride in fine linen, bright and pure, is the new Jerusalem (19:8; 21:2, 9).
- **Two suppers:** "the marriage supper of the Lamb" (19:9) and "the great supper of God" (19:17).
In the end everyone is in one of these two cities, at one of these two suppers.

## Going Deeper: 19:1–5 Hallelujah

**1. The only "Hallelujah" in the New Testament.** "After this I heard what seemed to be the loud voice of a great multitude in heaven, crying out, 'Hallelujah! Salvation and glory and power belong to our God'" (19:1). "Hallelujah" is Hebrew for "Praise the LORD." This is the only chapter in the New Testament that uses the word, four times in quick succession (19:1, 3, 4, 6). Many psalms begin or end with these words (Ps 106:48; 113:1), and Psalms 113–118 were sung by Jews at Passover for the exodus; now heaven sings for God's final deliverance.

**2. Praise for judgment.** "For his judgments are true and just; for he has judged the great prostitute who corrupted the earth with her immorality, and has avenged on her the blood of his servants" (19:2). "Avenged" is the same word as in the cry of the souls under the altar, "avenge our blood" (6:10). The saints' cry has now been answered. The multitude in heaven is not glad that people perish; it praises God's justice: his judgments are "true and just" (see 16:7), he has not let the corrupters of the earth go free, and he has not forgotten his murdered servants. Modern readers are often uneasy with praise like this; but for believers thrown into prison or even killed (2:10, 13), "God will avenge" was exactly why they could refuse to avenge themselves (Rom 12:19).

**3. "The smoke from her goes up forever and ever."** "Once more they cried out, 'Hallelujah! The smoke from her goes up forever and ever'" (19:3). These are Isaiah's words for the ruin of Edom: "Night and day it shall not be quenched; its smoke shall go up forever" (Isa 34:10). The kings of the earth "stand far off" and weep when they see the smoke of her burning (18:9); the multitude in heaven sees the same smoke and says "Hallelujah." The same event, seen from earth, is catastrophe; seen from heaven, it is the triumph of God's justice.

**4. "Small and great."** "And the twenty-four elders and the four living creatures fell down and worshiped God who was seated on the throne, saying, 'Amen. Hallelujah!'" (19:4). This is the last time the elders and the living creatures appear in the book. When they first appeared in chapter 4 they fell down in worship before the throne (4:10); here they are still doing the same thing. Then "from the throne came a voice saying, 'Praise our God, all you his servants, you who fear him, small and great'" (19:5). "Small and great" (see 11:18): on Babylon's cargo list people are cheap or dear; before God's throne, the small and the great are called together to praise.

> The point: heaven sings "Hallelujah" over God's judgment because his judgments are true and just. The saints need not avenge themselves, because God will avenge them.

## Going Deeper: 19:6–10 The Marriage of the Lamb Has Come

**1. "The Lord our God the Almighty reigns."** "Then I heard what seemed to be the voice of a great multitude, like the roar of many waters and like the sound of mighty peals of thunder, crying out, 'Hallelujah! For the Lord our God the Almighty reigns'" (19:6). "Reigns" is an aorist, and many interpreters take it as "has begun to reign": God has always been King, and now his kingship is fully displayed over all the earth (see 11:17).

**2. The Bride of the Lamb.** "Let us rejoice and exult and give him the glory, for the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his Bride has made herself ready" (19:7). In the Old Testament God's relationship with his people is often pictured as a marriage: "your Maker is your husband, the LORD of hosts is his name" (Isa 54:5). The New Testament applies the picture to Christ and the church: Paul says that Christ "loved the church and gave himself up for her," so as to "present the church to himself in splendor" (Eph 5:25–27). In the Old Testament Israel was often like an unfaithful wife; here at last the Bride "has made herself ready" to be united to the Lamb.

**3. "It was granted her."** "It was granted her to clothe herself with fine linen, bright and pure—for the fine linen is the righteous deeds of the saints" (19:8). Two phrases need to be read together:
- **"His Bride has made herself ready":** the Bride is not passive; she truly prepares herself.
- **"It was granted her":** the verb (*edothē*) is passive, and many interpreters take it as a "divine passive": the garment is God's gift. The CUV renders it "by grace she was given to wear," which makes this plain.
What, then, are "the righteous deeds of the saints"?
- **Righteous deeds God works in the saints:** many interpreters (e.g. Beale, Grant Osborne) note that the word is plural (*dikaiōmata*) and refers to the saints' particular righteous acts, their faithful witness and obedience under persecution; yet even these deeds are "granted," as Paul says that we are God's workmanship, created for good works "which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them" (Eph 2:10).
- **God's righteous verdicts for the saints:** some take the word to mean God's judgments vindicating the saints and declaring them in the right (see 19:2).
- **The righteousness Christ gives:** many preachers read the linen as the righteousness of Christ credited to believers, as in Isaiah's "he has covered me with the robe of righteousness" (Isa 61:10). The text's own emphasis is on what the saints do, but this reading rightly reminds us that the saints wear white at all only because "they have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14).
On any of these views the order is clear: first the Lamb's blood and God's gift, then the saints' righteous deeds, and those deeds are themselves God's gift. The prostitute's splendor was built on trading in human lives and shedding the saints' blood (18:13, 24); the Bride's clothing is given to her freely.

**4. "Blessed are those who are invited."** "And the angel said to me, 'Write this: Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb.' And he said to me, 'These are the true words of God'" (19:9). This is the fourth of the book's seven beatitudes. The Bride and the invited guests look like two groups; many interpreters take them as the same people seen from two angles: as a whole, the church is the Bride; one by one, believers are the invited guests (some dispensationalists, such as John Walvoord, distinguish the Bride, the church, from the guests, such as Old Testament saints). Isaiah had foretold that the LORD of hosts "will make for all peoples a feast of rich food" (Isa 25:6), and Jesus said that "many will come from east and west and recline at table with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven" (Matt 8:11). The angel adds: "These are the true words of God." The invitation is not a polite formality.

**5. "Worship God."** Hearing this, John fell at the angel's feet to worship him; the angel said, "You must not do that!... Worship God" (19:10). The same thing happens again in 22:8–9, and the meditation in the introduction (chapter 00) has touched on it. The angel says, "the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy" (19:10): Jesus is the center of all true prophecy. Even the most glorious angel is only a "fellow servant"; worship belongs to God alone. Yet the Lamb is worshiped together with the one seated on the throne (5:8–14): the worship the angel dares not receive is rightly given to the Lamb, because he is God.

> The point: the Bride's fine linen "was granted her." Those invited to the Lamb's marriage supper are blessed because God is the one inviting them, and the invitation is "the true words of God."

## Going Deeper: 19:11–16 The Rider Called Faithful and True

**1. "I saw heaven opened."** In chapter 4 John saw "a door standing open in heaven" (4:1) and was called up to look; here heaven itself opens, and Christ comes out from it. In the Old Testament Ezekiel too says that "the heavens were opened, and I saw visions of God" (Ezek 1:1). Most interpreters (e.g. John Walvoord, George Eldon Ladd, Beale, Grant Osborne) take 19:11–16 as the second coming of Christ; some postmillennial and preterist interpreters (e.g. B. B. Warfield, David Chilton) read it chiefly as Christ conquering the nations through the gospel in history. With most interpreters, this study reads it as Christ's real, bodily return.

**2. The white horse.** "Behold, a white horse! The one sitting on it is called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he judges and makes war" (19:11). The first seal in chapter 6 also brought a white horse (6:2), and who its rider is was discussed in chapter 6. Here there is no doubt who the rider is. His names are revealed one after another:
- **"Faithful and True":** in chapter 1 he is "the faithful witness" (1:5); in the letter to Laodicea he calls himself "the faithful and true witness" (3:14). What he says, he does.
- **His other names:** "a name written that no one knows but himself" (19:12); "The Word of God" (19:13), which in the New Testament only John's writings use for Jesus (John 1:1, 14; 1 John 1:1); and "King of kings and Lord of lords" (19:16), already proclaimed in chapter 17 (17:14).

**3. His eyes, crowns and robe.**
- **"His eyes are like a flame of fire"** (19:12): as in the vision of the Son of Man (1:14); nothing is hidden from him.
- **"On his head are many diadems"** (19:12): the dragon wore seven diadems on his heads (12:3) and the beast ten diadems on its horns (13:1); the rider wears "many." Their authority is usurped and limited; his is real and without limit.
- **"He is clothed in a robe dipped in blood"** (19:13): whose blood? Interpreters differ. Many (e.g. John Walvoord, Grant Osborne), following Isaiah's "their lifeblood spattered on my garments" (Isa 63:3), take it as the blood of his enemies, a picture of God's judgment. Others note that the battle has not yet begun, and that "blood" in Revelation usually means the Lamb's own blood (1:5; 5:9; 7:14; 12:11), and so take it as the blood he shed on the cross; others again (e.g. G. B. Caird) see the blood of the martyrs. These readings need not exclude one another: the one who carries out judgment is the one who once shed his blood for sinners.

**4. "From his mouth comes a sharp sword."** "And the armies of heaven, arrayed in fine linen, white and pure, were following him on white horses. From his mouth comes a sharp sword with which to strike down the nations" (19:14–15).
- **Who are the armies?** Some say angels (Matt 25:31; 2 Thess 1:7); some say the saints, since they wear the same fine linen as the Bride (19:8), and chapter 17 says "those with him are called and chosen and faithful" (17:14). Perhaps both are meant. It is worth noticing that the text never says they fight.
- **The sword from his mouth:** the sword is not in his hand but comes from his mouth (1:16; 2:16). This comes from Isaiah: the Messiah "shall strike the earth with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips he shall kill the wicked" (Isa 11:4; see 49:2). Paul says that the Lord Jesus will kill the lawless one "with the breath of his mouth" (2 Thess 2:8). He conquers by his word. The Word who made the world with a word will judge it with a word.
- **The rod of iron and the winepress:** "he will rule them with a rod of iron," already said in chapter 12 (12:5), comes from Psalm 2 (Ps 2:9); "he will tread the winepress of the fury of the wrath of God the Almighty" continues the harvest of chapter 14 (14:19–20) and Isaiah's "I have trodden the winepress alone" (Isa 63:3).

> The point: the rider on the white horse is called "Faithful and True." The one who shed his blood for us will judge the world in righteousness and conquer by the word of his mouth.

## Going Deeper: 19:17–21 The Great Supper of God and the Last Battle

**1. Another supper.** "Then I saw an angel standing in the sun, and with a loud voice he called to all the birds that fly directly overhead, 'Come, gather for the great supper of God, to eat the flesh of kings, the flesh of captains... and the flesh of all men, both free and slave, both small and great'" (19:17–18). This terrible picture comes from Ezekiel 39, where God summons birds and beasts to eat the flesh of Gog's army (Ezek 39:17–20). "Small and great" repeats the words of 19:5: those who fear God, "small and great," are called to praise him; the "small and great" who oppose the Lamb become a feast for the birds. Everyone is invited to a supper, either the one or the other.

**2. A battle that is never described.** "And I saw the beast and the kings of the earth with their armies gathered to make war against him who was sitting on the horse and against his army" (19:19). But the text describes no fighting at all; it says only, "the beast was captured" (19:20). What people often call the last great battle never really comes to blows. In chapter 16, at Armageddon, the kings are assembled "for battle on the great day of God the Almighty" (16:14–16); in chapter 20 Satan deceives Gog and Magog "to gather them for battle" (20:8). How do these passages relate to this one?
- **One battle described several times:** many amillennial and idealist interpreters (e.g. Beale, William Hendriksen) point out that "the battle" carries the article in the Greek in all three places (*ton polemon*), and that 19:17–18 and 20:8–9 both draw on Ezekiel's picture of Gog (Ezek 38–39); so they take them as different views of the same final battle at the Lord's return.
- **Events in sequence:** dispensationalists (e.g. John Walvoord) and historic premillennialists (e.g. George Eldon Ladd) take chapters 19 and 20 in order: Christ returns first and defeats the beast and false prophet; then come the thousand years; and only after them the battle of Gog and Magog.
This question is bound up with the views of the millennium and will be discussed fully in chapter 20. Those who hold either view agree that Christ will return in person and that his victory will be complete.

**3. The end of the beast and the false prophet.** "And the beast was captured, and with it the false prophet who in its presence had done the signs by which he deceived those who had received the mark of the beast and those who worshiped its image. These two were thrown alive into the lake of fire that burns with sulfur" (19:20). The false prophet is the beast "rising out of the earth" in chapter 13, who made people worship the first beast (13:11–12). The "lake of fire" appears here for the first time; later the devil (20:10) and Death and Hades (20:14) will be thrown into it too. The powers that made the whole world marvel, that no one dared refuse to worship, have no strength at all to resist the rider on the white horse. "And the rest were slain by the sword that came from the mouth of him who was sitting on the horse" (19:21). This verse is deeply solemn; we should neither soften it nor read it with any glee. Yet today the word that comes from his mouth is still an invitation: "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come.'... let the one who desires take the water of life without price" (22:17). The Word who will judge the nations still calls people to repent today; and the blood he shed on the cross for sinners (1:5; 5:9) is the pledge that he is willing to receive them.

> The point: the last battle is over before it begins, because the victor is the rider on the white horse. Today the word of his mouth still invites people to come to the marriage supper of the Lamb.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "His Bride has made herself ready; it was granted her to clothe herself with fine linen, bright and pure—for the fine linen is the righteous deeds of the saints" (19:7–8). A sister grew up in a poor family. One year, when her older cousin got married, her mother borrowed a dress from a neighbor for her to wear to the wedding banquet. It was too big; the sleeves were rolled up twice. At the banquet she hardly dared to reach for food, afraid of staining the borrowed dress, and afraid someone would recognize whose dress it was. Decades have passed. She has been a believer for many years and has served in the church all along. But when she reads "the fine linen is the righteous deeds of the saints," her heart tightens. She thinks of her coldness toward her mother-in-law over these years, the lies she told at work to dodge blame, and the years when she hardly opened her Bible. If the Lamb's marriage supper depends on each person's righteous deeds, she thinks, then she will go to it the way she went to that banquet: in clothes that do not fit, sitting on edge, afraid the host will see through her, like the man in the parable who had no wedding garment and was left speechless (Matt 22:11–12).

But the key words in this verse are "it was granted." The Bride's fine linen is not borrowed, and she did not weave it herself; it is given to her. Even "the righteous deeds of the saints" are works that God prepared beforehand for us to walk in (Eph 2:10); and the saints can wear white because the Lamb shed his blood for her coldness and her lies, and "they have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14). Nor did the angel say that those worthy of the Lamb's marriage supper are blessed, but "Blessed are those who are invited to the marriage supper of the Lamb" (19:9). This feast begins with an invitation, not an inspection. As for the man thrown out in the parable: the guests who filled that hall had all been gathered from the roads, "both bad and good" (Matt 22:10). Many interpreters think his problem was not that he was poor or that his clothes were not respectable, but that he took the king's feast lightly and would not come on the king's terms.

Her coldness to her mother-in-law and the lies she told are real; she need not pretend they did not happen. She can confess them before the Lord, and she can look for a chance to apologize to her mother-in-law and to put right the harm those lies did. But she does this not to earn a garment that would make her fit for the feast; she does it because she is already wearing a garment she will never have to give back. Those righteous deeds too are the work of God, who works in her (Phil 2:13). She no longer has to sit at the table as she did as a child, wondering when the clothes will be taken back.

The angel said, "These are the true words of God" (19:9). Then John saw heaven opened, and the Lamb who sent the invitation rode out on a white horse, "called Faithful and True" (19:11). He wears a robe dipped in blood and many diadems, and the word of his mouth strikes down the nations; yet he is also the Lord who was once invited as a guest to a village wedding at Cana in Galilee (John 2:1–2). What is written on the invitation is true, because the one who wrote it is called Faithful and True.
