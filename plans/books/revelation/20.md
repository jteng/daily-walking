## 本章脉络

第19章的末了，骑白马的那一位得胜，兽和假先知被扔在火湖里（19:20）。第20章接着讲那龙，就是撒但的结局，然后是最后的审判。全章可以分成四段：
- **20:1-3，撒但被捆绑：** 「使它不得再迷惑列国」。
- **20:4-6，与基督一同作王：** 「他们都复活了，与基督一同作王一千年」；这是「头一次的复活」。
- **20:7-10，最后的反叛：** 撒但被释放，聚集歌革和玛各，火从天降下，魔鬼被扔在火湖里。
- **20:11-15，白色的大宝座：** 死了的人都站在宝座前，案卷和生命册都展开了。

这一章是历代教会讨论千禧年的主要经文。导读已经简介了四种看法，本章在20:1-6摆出各派最有力的经文理由。无论怎样读，这一章都把我们带到同一个结局：撒但必定灭亡，死人必定复活，人人都要站在神的宝座前。

## 深读 20:1-3 那龙被捆绑

**1. 钥匙、链子和印。** 一位天使拿着钥匙和大链子，「捉住那龙，就是古蛇，又叫魔鬼，也叫撒但，把它捆绑一千年」（20:1-2）。第12章那「迷惑普天下」的大龙（12:9），在这里被一位天使捉住，连一场争战都没有。在启示录里，撒但从来不是和神平起平坐的对手。

**2. 「使它不得再迷惑列国」。** 这一句说明了捆绑的目的。约翰没有说撒但从此不能做任何事，只说它不能再「迷惑列国」。这个捆绑是什么时候的事？
- **现在的捆绑（无千禧年派和许多后千禧年派）：** 主耶稣说：「人怎能进壮士家里，抢夺他的家具呢？除非先捆住那壮士」（太12:29）；这里的「捆住」，和启示录20:2的「捆绑」在原文是同一个动词。主又说「这世界的王要被赶出去」（约12:31）。奥古斯丁、安东尼·胡克玛等人认为，撒但在基督第一次来时已经被捆绑：它仍然像「吼叫的狮子」（彼前5:8），却不能再拦阻福音传到万国，复活的主才能差门徒「去，使万民作我的门徒」（太28:18-19）。
- **将来的捆绑（历史前千禧年派和时代论前千禧年派）：** 乔治·赖德、约翰·华尔伏等人指出，「扔在无底坑里，将无底坑关闭，用印封上」，说的是完全的禁闭，不只是限制。新约也说，撒但现在仍然「迷惑普天下」（12:9），仍然弄瞎不信之人的心眼（林后4:4）。所以他们认为，这个捆绑要等基督在第19章再来以后才发生。
这两种看法都承认：撒但的权势是有限期的，那位拿钥匙的，比它更大。

**3. 「以后必须暂时释放它」。** 为什么「必须」释放它？经文没有解释。我们只知道，连这一段时间也定在神手里：它只是「暂时」被释放（20:3），并且「知道自己的时候不多」（12:12）。

## 深读 20:4-6 头一次的复活

**1. 宝座和殉道者。** 「我又看见几个宝座，也有坐在上面的，并有审判的权柄赐给他们。」（20:4；参但7:22）约翰看见「那些因为给耶稣作见证，并为神之道被斩者的灵魂」，又说到「那没有拜过兽与兽像」、没有受兽印记的人（20:4）。和合本读作两种人：殉道者，以及所有不肯拜兽的信徒（例如毕尔）；英文标准版读作同一群人，就是殉道者，从两方面来描写。第6章祭坛底下的灵魂曾呼求伸冤（6:9-10）；许多释经者认为，坐在宝座上的正是他们（也有人认为是使徒或二十四位长老）。地上的法庭判他们有罪，天上的宝座却宣告他们得胜。包衡等学者认为，这正是这段经文的主要信息。

**2. 「他们都复活了」。** 这个词原文是 *ezēsan*（活了，不定过去时），和合本译作「复活了」。第5节说「其余的死人还没有复活」，用的是同一个词。争论的焦点就在这里：
- **身体的复活（前千禧年派）：** 两节经文紧挨着，用同一个词，又一个称为「头一次」，许多人认为两次都应当指身体的复活。十九世纪的亨利·阿尔福德有一句常被引用的话，大意是：如果同一段经文里的第一次「活了」是属灵的，第二次却是身体的，语言就失去意义了。
- **属灵的「活」（无千禧年派，许多后千禧年派）：** 奥古斯丁认为，头一次的复活是指人重生，与基督一同活过来（参弗2:5-6；《上帝之城》第二十卷第六章）。胡克玛则认为，这是指信徒死后灵魂在天上与基督同活、同作王，因为约翰看见的是「灵魂」，和6:9一样。前千禧年派回应说，这些人是被斩以后才「复活」的，所以不像是指重生；胡克玛的读法也部分是要回应这一点。无千禧年派也指出，主耶稣在同一段话里，先说「死人要听见神儿子的声音，听见的人就要活了」，这是现在属灵的生命，然后才说「凡在坟墓里的」都要出来，这是将来身体的复活（约5:25、28-29）。

**3. 四种看法的主要理由。** 各派最有力的理由如下：
- **无千禧年派**（奥古斯丁、胡克玛；毕尔也大致这样读）：20:8的「聚集争战」和16:14在原文是同一个说法，19:19的说法也很接近；三处都说「聚集」，「争战」一词都带定冠词（*ton polemon*），好像是同一场最后的争战（参第19章）；第19章和第20章都引用以西结书第38-39章（19:17-18；20:8-9）。所以，第20章是回头重述从第一次降临到再来的整个时期。新约别处也多把义人和不义之人的复活说成同一件事（约5:28-29「时候要到」；徒24:15）。
- **历史前千禧年派**（乔治·赖德；早期教会的游斯丁和爱任纽）：最自然的读法是按次序读，基督在19:11-21再来，然后有一千年。20:10说魔鬼被扔在「兽和假先知所在的地方」，似乎假定19:20已经发生了（原文这里没有动词，无千禧年派认为也可以理解为同时发生）。保罗也说复活「各人是按着自己的次序」：「初熟的果子是基督；以后……是那些属基督的。再后，末期到了」（林前15:23-24），中间可能有一段时期。
- **时代论前千禧年派**（约翰·华尔伏）：他们还强调一千年是字面的时期；旧约许多给以色列的应许，例如以赛亚说「百岁死的仍算孩童」（赛65:20），那时仍有死亡，似乎不是最终的新天新地，而是在地上的国度。「蒙爱的城」（20:9）就是耶路撒冷，基督要在那里坐大卫的位作王。
- **后千禧年派**（约拿单·爱德华兹、罗兰·贝特纳（Loraine Boettner））：天国「好像面酵……直等全团都发起来」（太13:33）；「认识耶和华荣耀的知识要充满遍地」（哈2:14）；基督「必要作王，等神把一切仇敌都放在他的脚下」（林前15:25）。他们把20:3「使它不得再迷惑列国」读作福音在列国中越来越得胜：撒但越来越不能拦阻万民归主，基督藉着他的道和圣灵，从天上治理地上的教会。所以他们盼望福音得胜的时代，然后主才再来。也有后千禧年派学者（如华腓德）把20:4-6读作在天上的圣徒，和无千禧年派相近。

**4. 共同的根基。** 游斯丁本人持前千禧年的看法，却也承认，有许多信仰纯正、敬虔的基督徒不这样看（《与特来弗对话》第八十章）。今天的福音派信徒四种看法都有，大家都相信：主耶稣要亲自、身体地再来；死人要复活；神要审判世界；撒但必定灭亡；神要造新天新地。华人教会受时代论影响很深，也有不少弟兄姊妹持别的看法。这不应该成为分裂、彼此论断的理由；不确定的地方，可以谦卑地承认还在学习。

**5. 「有福了，圣洁了」。** 「在头一次复活有分的有福了，圣洁了！第二次的死在他们身上没有权柄。他们必作神和基督的祭司，并要与基督一同作王一千年。」（20:6）主曾应许士每拿那受苦的教会：「得胜的，必不受第二次死的害」（2:11）。他们作「祭司」，作王，正是1:6和5:10所说的成全。

> 要点：千禧年的细节，信徒可以有不同的看法；但被杀的见证人要与基督一同作王，这一点在每一种看法里都是确定的。

## 深读 20:7-10 歌革和玛各

**1. 最后的迷惑。** 「那一千年完了，撒但必从监牢里被释放，出来要迷惑地上四方的列国，就是歌革和玛各，叫他们聚集争战。他们的人数多如海沙。」（20:7-8）前千禧年派常认为，这表明人心若没有重生，即使在基督公义的统治下活了很久，一旦有机会，仍会跟从迷惑者。无千禧年派常把这一段读作主再来以前短暂而猛烈的逼迫（参11:7；帖后2:3、8）。

**2. 歌革和玛各是谁？** 名字来自以西结书：「人子啊，你要面向玛各地的歌革」（结38:2）。在以西结书里，歌革是从北方来攻击神百姓的王；在启示录里，「歌革和玛各」成了「地上四方的列国」的总称（20:8）。二十世纪有不少讲道把玛各等同于某个现代国家（例如《司可福串注圣经》在以西结书第38章的注释把它和俄罗斯连在一起），局势一紧张，就有人重新推算。约翰关心的却不是地图，而是普天下的人怎样被迷惑，起来攻击神的百姓。

**3. 「圣徒的营与蒙爱的城」。** 「他们上来遍满了全地，围住圣徒的营与蒙爱的城，就有火从天降下，烧灭了他们。」（20:9）「蒙爱的城」，时代论者认为是地上的耶路撒冷，许多释经者则认为是神的百姓本身（参21:2）。这里没有描写任何争战：仇敌围住了，火就从天降下来，正如以西结书所说「我要降火在玛各」（结39:6）。最后的胜利完全是神的作为。

**4. 火湖。** 「那迷惑他们的魔鬼被扔在硫磺的火湖里，就是兽和假先知所在的地方。他们必昼夜受痛苦，直到永永远远。」（20:10）龙、兽和假先知这三个敌人，到这里都有了结局。历代教会多数（例如奥古斯丁、《威斯敏斯德信条》第三十三章）认为，经文所说的是永远、有知觉的刑罚；也有一些福音派学者主张有条件的永生，认为不悔改的人最终会被灭绝（例如约翰·斯托得曾试探性地提出）。无论如何，拒绝神的结局是真实而可怕的（参14:10-11；21:8）。

## 深读 20:11-15 白色的大宝座

**1. 坐在宝座上的。** 「我又看见一个白色的大宝座与坐在上面的；从他面前天地都逃避，再无可见之处了。」（20:11）「白色」表明审判者完全圣洁、公义（参但7:9）。坐在上面的是谁？在启示录里，坐宝座的通常是父神（4:2）；主耶稣却说：「父不审判什么人，乃将审判的事全交与子」（约5:22）；保罗也说，神要「藉着他所设立的人按公义审判天下」（徒17:31）。到了新耶路撒冷，宝座是「神和羔羊的宝座」（22:3；参3:21）。审判我们的，是父与子；而那位子，就是曾经被人审判、被钉十字架的耶稣。天地「再无可见之处」，也没有一个地方可以躲藏（参6:14-17）。

**2. 「无论大小」。** 「我又看见死了的人，无论大小，都站在宝座前。」（20:12）「于是海交出其中的死人；死亡和阴间也交出其中的死人」（20:13）。中国人讲入土为安，最怕尸骨无存；可是连葬身大海的人，也都要站在神面前；没有一个人被遗漏，也没有一个人能用地位逃避。这里的「死了的人」包括哪些人？
- **只是不信的人（时代论者，如约翰·华尔伏）：** 这是「其余的死人」（20:5）的复活；信徒较早已经在「基督台前」按所行的受报（林后5:10），那是关乎赏赐的审判。
- **所有的人（无千禧年派，例如安东尼·胡克玛；改革宗信条；也有一些历史前千禧年派）：** 这就是主耶稣所说万民聚集在他面前、分别绵羊山羊的那一个审判，义人和不义的人都在其中（太25:31-46；参《威斯敏斯德信条》第三十三章第一节）。
这两种看法都同意：信徒永远的结局，不是靠案卷上的行为来决定的。

**3. 案卷和生命册。** 「案卷展开了，并且另有一卷展开，就是生命册。死了的人都凭着这些案卷所记载的，照他们所行的受审判。」（20:12）这里有两样东西：
- **许多案卷：** 但以理也看见「案卷都展开了」（但7:10）。「照他们所行的」在第12、13节说了两次。神的审判不是任意的，「他必照各人的行为报应各人」（罗2:6）。这对受冤屈的人是安慰：世上没有立案、被压下来的事，在神那里都记录在案。
- **一卷生命册：** 保罗说他的同工「名字都在生命册上」（腓4:3）。启示录称它为「被杀之羔羊生命册」（13:8）。
最后决定人往哪里去的，是第15节：「若有人名字没记在生命册上，他就被扔在火湖里。」人按行为受审判，却不是靠行为得救。「你们得救是本乎恩，也因着信……也不是出于行为」；接着保罗又说，我们是「在基督耶稣里造成的，为要叫我们行善」（弗2:8-10）。案卷上的善行不能买到生命册上的名字，却可以显明恩典在一个人身上真实地工作过。

**4. 最后的仇敌。** 「死亡和阴间也被扔在火湖里；这火湖就是第二次的死。」（20:14）保罗说：「尽末了所毁灭的仇敌就是死。」（林前15:26）从伊甸园以来压在人类身上的死亡，在这里被扔掉了。

> 要点：人人都要照所行的受审判，所以这审判是公义的；人却只能靠生命册上的名字进入生命，所以这救恩全是恩典。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我又看见死了的人，无论大小，都站在宝座前。案卷展开了，并且另有一卷展开，就是生命册。」（20:12）一位七十多岁的姊妹，信主四十多年，教过主日学。可是每次参加安息礼拜，听到有人念启示录第20章，她心里就发紧。八十年代初，她在家庭聚会里听一位传道人说：「到那一天，你一生做过的事、心里想过的念头，都要像放电影一样，一幕一幕放给天上地下的人看。」她最怕的是其中一幕。十四岁那年，在一场运动中，她在大会上揭发了自己的父亲。父亲后来被批斗，身体垮了，没等到她开口认错就去世了。这件事她向神认过许多次，眼泪也流过许多次，可是一想到那些案卷，她就觉得，到了那一天，这一幕总要被放出来。

她没有记错：案卷是真实的。神的审判不会把罪说成没有发生过，那件事她也不必替自己辩解。她父亲所受的苦，神也都看见了。可是对在基督里的人，神也说过：「我要宽恕他们的不义，不再记念他们的罪愆」（来8:12）。不是全知的神会忘记，而是他不再向她追讨。那位传道人只讲了一半。约翰看见的，不只是许多案卷，还有「另有一卷」（启20:12）；决定人往哪里去的，正是这一卷（20:15）。坐在宝座上审判的，是那位「父……将审判的事全交与」的子（约5:22），他自己曾经站在彼拉多的审判台前，又在十字架上为罪人受了神的审判。《海德堡要理问答》第52问说，信徒可以安心等候那位审判者从天降临，因为他先为我们站在神的审判之下，除去了我们一切的咒诅。他亲口说过：「那听我话、又信差我来者的，就有永生；不至于定罪，是已经出死入生了。」（约5:24）神已经把那「攻击我们、有碍于我们的字据」「钉在十字架上」（西2:14）。

也许你像这位姊妹，知道自己的案卷里有哪一页最不敢翻开。也许你还不确定自己的名字在不在那册子上。主耶稣说：「信他的人，不被定罪」（约3:18）；今天就可以把那一页带到他面前，不必再自己捂着。这不是去赚一个写上名字的资格，而是因为他已经为这样的罪人被杀。至于那些因你受过伤、而你已经无法再去道歉的人，可以把他们交给那位公义又满有怜悯的审判者；若是还有机会，也可以在牧者陪伴下去认错、弥补，但这些都不是写上名字的价钱。

到了这卷书的末了，那册子有了一个更完整的名字：「只有名字写在羔羊生命册上的才得进去。」（21:27）它不叫好人的册子，也不叫忠心仆人的册子，而叫「羔羊生命册」。那一天，展开的不只是她的案卷，还有羔羊的册子；她的名字在那里，不是因为那一幕从来没有发生，而是因为那羔羊曾被杀。那册子上的每一个名字，都是恩典写上去的。

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the close of chapter 19 the Rider on the white horse has conquered, and the beast and the false prophet have been thrown into the lake of fire (19:20). Chapter 20 turns to the end of the dragon, Satan, and then to the final judgment. It falls into four parts:
- **20:1–3, Satan bound:** "so that he might not deceive the nations any longer."
- **20:4–6, reigning with Christ:** "they came to life and reigned with Christ for a thousand years." This is "the first resurrection."
- **20:7–10, the last rebellion:** Satan is released and gathers Gog and Magog; fire comes down from heaven, and the devil is thrown into the lake of fire.
- **20:11–15, the great white throne:** the dead stand before the throne; the books and the book of life are opened.

This chapter is the main text in the church's long discussion of the millennium. The introduction set out the four views briefly; here, in 20:1–6, we lay out each one's strongest biblical reasons. Whichever view we take, the chapter brings us to the same end: Satan will surely be destroyed, the dead will surely rise, and every person will stand before the throne of God.

## Going Deeper: 20:1–3 The Dragon Bound

**1. Key, chain and seal.** An angel with a key and a great chain "seized the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is the devil and Satan, and bound him for a thousand years" (20:1–2). The great dragon who is "the deceiver of the whole world" (12:9) is seized here by one angel, without so much as a battle. In Revelation Satan is never God's equal.

**2. "So that he might not deceive the nations any longer."** This clause gives the purpose of the binding. John does not say Satan can do nothing at all, only that he can no longer "deceive the nations." When does this binding happen?
- **A present binding (amillennialists and many postmillennialists):** Jesus said, "How can someone enter a strong man's house and plunder his goods, unless he first binds the strong man?" (Matt 12:29); the verb "binds" there is the same Greek verb as "bound" in Revelation 20:2. He also said, "now will the ruler of this world be cast out" (John 12:31). Augustine, Anthony Hoekema and others hold that Satan was bound at Christ's first coming: he still "prowls around like a roaring lion" (1 Pet 5:8), but he can no longer stop the gospel from reaching the nations, which is why the risen Lord could send his disciples to "make disciples of all nations" (Matt 28:18–19).
- **A future binding (historic and dispensational premillennialists):** George Eldon Ladd, John Walvoord and others point out that "threw him into the pit, and shut it and sealed it over him" describes complete confinement, not mere restriction. The New Testament also says Satan still deceives the whole world (12:9) and blinds the minds of unbelievers (2 Cor 4:4). So they hold that this binding comes after Christ returns in chapter 19.
Both views agree that Satan's power has a time limit, and that the One who holds the key is greater than he.

**3. "After that he must be released for a little while."** Why "must" he be released? The text does not say. We know only that even this time is in God's hand: he is released only "for a little while" (20:3), and he "knows that his time is short" (12:12).

## Going Deeper: 20:4–6 The First Resurrection

**1. Thrones and martyrs.** "Then I saw thrones, and seated on them were those to whom the authority to judge was committed" (20:4; cf. Dan 7:22). John sees "the souls of those who had been beheaded for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God, and who had not worshiped the beast or its image and had not received its mark" (20:4). The CUV reads two groups here: the martyrs, and all believers who refused the beast (so Beale). The ESV reads one group, the martyrs, described in two ways. The souls under the altar in chapter 6 cried out for justice (6:9–10); many interpreters hold that they are the ones seated on the thrones (others think of the apostles or the twenty-four elders). Earthly courts found them guilty; heaven's thrones declare them victors. Bauckham and others hold that this vindication is the passage's main message.

**2. "They came to life."** The word is *ezēsan* ("they lived," aorist). The CUV renders it "were resurrected," the ESV "came to life." Verse 5 says "the rest of the dead did not come to life," using the same word. Here is the heart of the debate:
- **Bodily resurrection (premillennialists):** The two verses stand side by side, use the same word, and one is called "first," so many hold that both must mean bodily resurrection. The nineteenth-century commentator Henry Alford has an often-quoted remark to the effect that if the first "came to life" in one passage is spiritual and the second bodily, language loses its meaning.
- **Spiritual life (amillennialists, many postmillennialists):** Augustine held that the first resurrection is the new birth, being made alive together with Christ (cf. Eph 2:5–6; *The City of God* 20.6). Hoekema holds that it is the life and reign of believers' souls with Christ in heaven after death, since John sees "souls," as in 6:9. Premillennialists reply that these people "came to life" after being beheaded, which does not fit the new birth; Hoekema's reading is partly an answer to this. Amillennialists also point out that in one discourse Jesus first says "the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live," a present spiritual life, and then that "all who are in the tombs" will come out, a future bodily resurrection (John 5:25, 28–29).

**3. The main reasons for each view.** Each side's strongest reasons:
- **Amillennial** (Augustine, Hoekema; Beale reads broadly this way): "to gather them for battle" in 20:8 is the same Greek phrase as in 16:14, and 19:19 is very close; all three speak of gathering, and each time "battle" carries the article (*ton polemon*), as if it were the same final battle (see chapter 19); and both chapter 19 and chapter 20 draw on Ezekiel 38–39 (19:17–18; 20:8–9). So chapter 20 goes back over the whole period from the first coming to the return. Elsewhere, too, the New Testament usually speaks of the resurrection of the just and the unjust as one event (John 5:28–29, "an hour is coming"; Acts 24:15).
- **Historic premillennial** (George Eldon Ladd; in the early church Justin Martyr and Irenaeus): the most natural reading is in sequence: Christ returns in 19:11–21, and then come the thousand years. In 20:10 the devil is thrown into the lake "where the beast and the false prophet were," which seems to assume 19:20 has already happened (the Greek has no verb here; amillennialists take it as possibly simultaneous). Paul also speaks of resurrection "each in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, then at his coming those who belong to Christ. Then comes the end" (1 Cor 15:23–24), possibly with a period in between.
- **Dispensational premillennial** (John Walvoord): they also stress that the thousand years is a literal period. Many Old Testament promises to Israel, such as Isaiah's "the young man shall die a hundred years old" (Isa 65:20), still include death and seem to describe not the final new creation but a kingdom on earth. "The beloved city" (20:9) is Jerusalem, where Christ will reign on David's throne.
- **Postmillennial** (Jonathan Edwards, Loraine Boettner): the kingdom of heaven is "like leaven... till it was all leavened" (Matt 13:33); "the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD" (Hab 2:14); Christ "must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet" (1 Cor 15:25). They read 20:3, "so that he might not deceive the nations any longer," as the gospel's growing triumph among the nations: Satan is less and less able to keep the peoples from Christ, who rules his church on earth from heaven by his word and Spirit. So they look for an age of gospel triumph before the Lord returns. Some postmillennial scholars (such as B. B. Warfield) read 20:4–6 as the saints in heaven, close to the amillennial reading.

**4. Common ground.** Justin, himself a premillennialist, granted that many Christians of pure and godly faith thought otherwise (*Dialogue with Trypho* 80). Evangelical believers today hold all four views, and all believe that the Lord Jesus will return in person and in the body; that the dead will rise; that God will judge the world; that Satan will be destroyed; and that God will make a new heaven and a new earth. Chinese churches have been deeply shaped by dispensationalism, and many brothers and sisters hold other views. This should not become a reason to divide or judge one another; where we are unsure, we can humbly admit we are still learning.

**5. "Blessed and holy."** "Blessed and holy is the one who shares in the first resurrection! Over such the second death has no power, but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and they will reign with him for a thousand years" (20:6). The Lord promised the suffering church in Smyrna, "The one who conquers will not be hurt by the second death" (2:11). Their being priests and reigning fulfills what 1:6 and 5:10 said.

> The point: Believers may differ over the details of the millennium, but in every view it is certain that the slain witnesses will reign with Christ.

## Going Deeper: 20:7–10 Gog and Magog

**1. The last deception.** "And when the thousand years are ended, Satan will be released from his prison and will come out to deceive the nations that are at the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them for battle; their number is like the sand of the sea" (20:7–8). Premillennialists often take this to show that the human heart, unless born again, will follow the deceiver as soon as it can, even after living long under Christ's righteous rule. Amillennialists often read it as the short, fierce persecution before the Lord's return (cf. 11:7; 2 Thess 2:3, 8).

**2. Who are Gog and Magog?** The names come from Ezekiel: "Son of man, set your face toward Gog, of the land of Magog" (Ezek 38:2). In Ezekiel, Gog is a king from the north who attacks God's people; in Revelation, "Gog and Magog" has become a name for "the nations that are at the four corners of the earth" (20:8). Much twentieth-century preaching identified Magog with a modern nation (the Scofield Reference Bible's note on Ezekiel 38 linked it with Russia), and whenever tensions rise, someone recalculates. But John's concern is not a map; it is how the whole world is deceived and rises against God's people.

**3. "The camp of the saints and the beloved city."** "And they marched up over the broad plain of the earth and surrounded the camp of the saints and the beloved city, but fire came down from heaven and consumed them" (20:9). Dispensationalists take "the beloved city" as earthly Jerusalem; many others take it as God's people themselves (cf. 21:2). No battle is described at all: the enemy surrounds, and fire comes down from heaven, as in Ezekiel, "I will send fire on Magog" (Ezek 39:6). The final victory is entirely God's work.

**4. The lake of fire.** "And the devil who had deceived them was thrown into the lake of fire and sulfur where the beast and the false prophet were, and they will be tormented day and night forever and ever" (20:10). The three enemies, the dragon, the beast and the false prophet, all meet their end here. Most of the church through history (for example Augustine and the Westminster Confession, chapter 33) has taken the text to speak of everlasting, conscious punishment; some evangelical scholars hold to conditional immortality, that the unrepentant are finally destroyed (John Stott, for example, suggested it tentatively). Either way, the end of rejecting God is real and terrible (cf. 14:10–11; 21:8).

## Going Deeper: 20:11–15 The Great White Throne

**1. The One seated on the throne.** "Then I saw a great white throne and him who was seated on it. From his presence earth and sky fled away, and no place was found for them" (20:11). "White" marks the Judge as perfectly holy and just (cf. Dan 7:9). Who sits on it? In Revelation the One on the throne is usually God the Father (4:2); yet Jesus said, "The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son" (John 5:22), and Paul says God "will judge the world in righteousness by a man whom he has appointed" (Acts 17:31). In the New Jerusalem the throne is "the throne of God and of the Lamb" (22:3; cf. 3:21). The Father and the Son judge us; and that Son is Jesus, who was himself judged by men and crucified. Earth and sky find "no place," and there is nowhere left to hide (cf. 6:14–17).

**2. "Great and small."** "And I saw the dead, great and small, standing before the throne" (20:12). "And the sea gave up the dead who were in it, Death and Hades gave up the dead who were in them" (20:13). Chinese people speak of resting in peace once buried, and dread dying far from home with no body to bury. Yet even those lost at sea will stand before God; no one is left out, and no one escapes by rank. Who are "the dead" here?
- **Unbelievers only (dispensationalists such as John Walvoord):** this is the resurrection of "the rest of the dead" (20:5); believers have already appeared before "the judgment seat of Christ" (2 Cor 5:10), a judgment concerned with rewards.
- **All people (amillennialists such as Anthony Hoekema, the Reformed confessions, and some historic premillennialists):** this is the judgment Jesus described, when all nations are gathered before him and the sheep are separated from the goats, with the righteous and the unrighteous both there (Matt 25:31–46; cf. Westminster Confession 33.1).
Both views agree that a believer's eternal destiny is not decided by the works recorded in the books.

**3. The books and the book of life.** "And books were opened. Then another book was opened, which is the book of life. And the dead were judged by what was written in the books, according to what they had done" (20:12). There are two things here:
- **Many books:** Daniel too saw that "the books were opened" (Dan 7:10). "According to what they had done" is said twice, in verses 12 and 13. God's judgment is not arbitrary: "He will render to each one according to his works" (Rom 2:6). This is a comfort to the wronged: what was never brought to court on earth, what was hushed up, is on record with God.
- **One book of life:** Paul says his fellow workers' "names are in the book of life" (Phil 4:3). Revelation calls it "the book of life of the Lamb that was slain" (13:8).
What finally decides where a person goes is verse 15: "And if anyone's name was not found written in the book of life, he was thrown into the lake of fire." People are judged by their works, but not saved by their works. "For by grace you have been saved through faith... not a result of works"; and Paul goes straight on to say that we are "created in Christ Jesus for good works" (Eph 2:8–10). The good works in the books cannot buy a name in the book of life, but they can show that grace has truly been at work in a life.

**4. The last enemy.** "Then Death and Hades were thrown into the lake of fire. This is the second death, the lake of fire" (20:14). Paul says, "The last enemy to be destroyed is death" (1 Cor 15:26). The death that has pressed on humanity since Eden is thrown away here.

> The point: Everyone will be judged by what they have done, so the judgment is just; yet no one enters life except by a name in the book of life, so salvation is all grace.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And I saw the dead, great and small, standing before the throne, and books were opened. Then another book was opened, which is the book of life" (20:12). A sister in her seventies has believed for over forty years and taught Sunday school. Yet whenever she goes to a funeral service and hears someone read Revelation 20, her chest tightens. In the early eighties, at a house meeting, she heard a preacher say, "On that day everything you have done and every thought you have had will be played like a film, scene by scene, for all heaven and earth to see." There is one scene she dreads most. During a political campaign, when she was fourteen, she denounced her own father at a mass meeting. He was later subjected to struggle sessions, his health broke, and he died before she could tell him she was wrong. She has confessed it to God many times and wept over it many times, yet whenever she thinks of those books, she feels sure that on that day this scene will be played.

She is not mistaken: the books are real. God's judgment will not pretend sin never happened, and she need not defend herself over what she did. God saw what her father suffered, too. Yet to those in Christ God has also said, "I will be merciful toward their iniquities, and I will remember their sins no more" (Heb 8:12). The God who knows all things does not forget; he will not hold it against her. But that preacher told only half of what John saw. John saw not only books but "another book" (Rev 20:12), and it is this book that decides where each person goes (20:15). The One who judges from the throne is the Son, to whom the Father "has given all judgment" (John 5:22), and he himself once stood before Pilate's judgment seat and on the cross bore God's judgment for sinners. The Heidelberg Catechism, Question 52, says that believers can await the Judge's coming from heaven with confidence, because he first stood under God's judgment in our place and removed every curse from us. He said it himself: "Whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life. He does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life" (John 5:24). And God has set aside "the record of debt that stood against us," "nailing it to the cross" (Col 2:14).

Perhaps, like this sister, you know which page in your books you least dare to turn. Perhaps you are not yet sure your name is in that book. Jesus says, "Whoever believes in him is not condemned" (John 3:18); today you can bring that page to him instead of keeping it covered yourself. You do this not to earn a place for your name, but because he was already slain for sinners like this. As for those you hurt and can no longer apologize to, you can entrust them to the Judge who is both just and full of mercy; where there is still a way, you can confess and make amends with a pastor beside you. None of this is the price of having your name written.

By the end of the book, that book is given a fuller name: "only those who are written in the Lamb's book of life" (21:27) will enter the city. It is not called the book of good people, nor the book of faithful servants, but the Lamb's book of life. On that day, it is not only her books that will be opened, but the Lamb's book as well; and her name is there, not because that scene never happened, but because the Lamb was slain. Every name in that book was written there by grace.
