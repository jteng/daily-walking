## 本章脉络

白色大宝座的审判以后（20:11-15），约翰看见的不是一片空荡荡的天，而是「一个新天新地」（21:1）。全章可以分成三段：
- **21:1-8，新天新地和神的帐幕：** 圣城从天而降；神的帐幕在人间；坐宝座的说：「看哪，我将一切都更新了！」
- **21:9-21，新妇，羔羊的妻：** 天使要把新妇指给约翰看，他看见的却是一座城，长宽高都一样，城门是珍珠，街道是精金。
- **21:22-27，城内没有殿：** 主神和羔羊就是城的殿，羔羊是城的灯；只有名字写在羔羊生命册上的才得进去。

第17-18章的巴比伦被称为「大淫妇」，这里的圣城被称为「新妇」（21:2、9）；领约翰去看这两座城的，都是拿着七碗的天使中的一位（17:1；21:9）。新天新地这一段，延续到22:5。

## 深读 21:1-4 新天新地

**1. 「先前的天地已经过去了」。** 「我又看见一个新天新地；因为先前的天地已经过去了，海也不再有了。」（21:1）这句话出自以赛亚的应许：「看哪！我造新天新地；从前的事不再被记念，也不再追想。」（赛65:17）问题是：新天新地是把这个世界消灭以后另造一个，还是把这个世界更新？
- **更新：** 许多释经者（如加尔文、巴文克、安东尼·胡克玛）认为，神要炼净、更新这个受造的世界，就像他要叫我们这同一个身体复活。保罗说：「受造之物仍然指望脱离败坏的辖制，得享神儿女自由的荣耀。」（罗8:21）彼得也说，「我们照他的应许，盼望新天新地，有义居在其中」（彼后3:13）；关于彼得后书3:10的异文，见彼得后书第3章。
- **消灭后另造：** 也有神学家（例如十七世纪的路德宗神学家约翰·格哈德）认为，现在的天地要完全归于无有，神要另造新的。他们看重「过去了」（21:1）和「天地都逃避，再无可见之处」（20:11）这些说法。
本研读采取更新的看法：「过去了」可以指先前那种受咒诅、败坏的光景完全过去，不一定是指受造之物本身化为乌有。

**2. 华人教会的一个提醒。** 我们常唱「这世界非我家」这一类的诗歌，提醒自己在世上是「客旅，是寄居的」（来11:13），这是对的。可是如果只剩下这一面，我们就容易把得救想成灵魂从一个注定烧毁的世界里逃出去，身体、家庭、土地、工作、文化，好像都和神的救恩无关。也有人把天堂想成一个金碧辉煌的仙境，和《阿弥陀经》所讲的西方极乐世界差不多，那里也是黄金为地，有七宝池。启示录的城也有精金宝石，但它的画面来自以赛亚和以西结；它的中心不是金子，而是神和羔羊，人进去也不是靠念佛往生，而是白白蒙恩。它所描写的盼望也不是逃到另一个地方去，而是圣城「由神那里从天而降」（21:2），神亲自来与人同住。这卷书的方向是从天到地，不是从地到天。

**3. 「海也不再有了」。** 这句话不一定是说新地上没有海洋。在启示录里，海常常代表混乱、邪恶和威胁：兽是「从海中上来」的（13:1；参但7:3），海也交出其中的死人（20:13）。许多释经者（例如毕尔）认为，「海也不再有了」主要是说，一切与神为敌、威胁神百姓的势力都不再有了。

**4. 「神的帐幕在人间」。** 「我听见有大声音从宝座出来说：『看哪，神的帐幕在人间。他要与人同住，他们要作他的子民。神要亲自与他们同在，作他们的神。』」（21:3）这是整本圣经一再重复的应许：「我要在你们中间立我的帐幕……我要作你们的神，你们要作我的子民」（利26:11-12；参结37:27）。后来「道成了肉身，住在我们中间」（约1:14），「住」这个词和这里的「帐幕」同一个字根。到了新天新地，神不再只住在一个帐幕或一座殿里，而是与他的百姓同住。

**5. 「神要擦去他们一切的眼泪」。** 「神要擦去他们一切的眼泪；不再有死亡，也不再有悲哀、哭号、疼痛，因为以前的事都过去了。」（21:4）这是第7章应许的成全（7:17）。经文没有说那些眼泪不重要，而是说神要亲自去擦。

> 要点：神的救恩不是把人从一个被丢弃的世界里救出去，而是神亲自来，与人同住，更新他所造的一切。

## 深读 21:5-8 「看哪，我将一切都更新了」

**1. 「一切都更新了」。** 「坐宝座的说：『看哪，我将一切都更新了！』又说：『你要写上；因这些话是可信的，是真实的。』」（21:5）在启示录里，这是坐宝座的神少有的几次亲自开口（另见1:8）。神不是说他要另造一切新的东西，而是要把一切都更新。重点在于「一切」：这个世界、神百姓的身体、他们的历史，都在神更新的工作里面。原文的「更新」是现在式，有人认为，这更新的工作从基督复活起已经开始了（林后5:17），到那日要完全成就。神又吩咐约翰写下来，好叫受压的教会手里有一句可以一读再读的话。

**2. 「都成了！」** 「他又对我说：『都成了！我是阿拉法，我是俄梅戛；我是初，我是终。我要将生命泉的水白白赐给那口渴的人喝。』」（21:6）第七碗倒出的时候，宝座上也有声音说：「成了！」（16:17）那是审判完成；这里是新创造完成。这和约19:30的「成了」原文不同，但神能这样宣告，是因为羔羊在十字架上已经成就了救赎。「阿拉法」和「俄梅戛」是希腊字母的第一个和最后一个字母：历史从神开始，也在神那里结束。

**3. 「白白赐给那口渴的人」。** 进入新天新地的条件，不是付得起代价，而是口渴。「白白」就是不用付钱。这呼应以赛亚的呼召：「没有银钱的也可以来」（赛55:1）。启示录的最后一章还要再说一次（22:17）。

**4. 「我要作他的神，他要作我的儿子」。** 「得胜的，必承受这些为业：我要作他的神，他要作我的儿子。」（21:7）这句话出自神对大卫的应许：「我要作他的父，他要作我的子」（撒下7:14）；启示录把「父」换成了「神」，与本章3节「作他们的神」的立约应许合在一起。第2-3章给七个教会的应许，在这里总结起来：得胜的人所承受的，就是神自己。

**5. 「惟有胆怯的、不信的……」。** 「惟有胆怯的、不信的、可憎的、杀人的、淫乱的、行邪术的、拜偶像的，和一切说谎话的，他们的分就在烧着硫磺的火湖里；这是第二次的死。」（21:8）这个名单的第一项是「胆怯的」。许多释经者（例如毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）认为，在这卷书的处境中，这是指那些在压力下不敢承认基督、转去随从兽的人。这句话是对教会说的严肃警告，不只是对外人。可是这不是说，曾经软弱、跌倒过的信徒就一定没有指望了：彼得曾三次不认主，主却挽回了他。保罗写给哥林多人的一个类似的名单，后面跟着一句话：「你们中间也有人从前是这样；但如今你们……已经洗净，成圣，称义了。」（林前6:11）得胜的人不是从来没有软弱过的人，而是那些「用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了」、一直信靠他的人（7:14；参12:11）。

> 要点：进入那新世界的门路，是白白领受生命的水；拒绝这恩典、宁可随从兽的人，就没有分。

## 深读 21:9-21 新妇，羔羊的妻

**1. 听见新妇，看见一座城。** 天使说：「你到这里来，我要将新妇，就是羔羊的妻，指给你看。」（21:9）约翰接着看见的，却是「由神那里、从天而降的圣城耶路撒冷」（21:10）。这是00篇提到的写法：听见的由看见的来解释，新妇就是这座城。新耶路撒冷到底是一群人，还是一个地方？
- **主要是神的百姓：** 有学者（例如罗伯特·冈德里）认为，新耶路撒冷就是神的百姓，不是百姓所住的地方（参弗2:20-22）。
- **一座真实的城：** 有些时代论的作者（例如约翰·华尔伏）认为，这是一座真实的城，经文所说的尺寸可以照字面来理解。
- **两样都是：** 许多释经者（例如奥斯本）认为，这城既是神的百姓，也是一个真实的地方；毕尔也强调，这城主要描写神的百姓和神的同在，却是在真实的「新地」上（21:1-2），不是飘在空中的一种心境。
本研读采取第三种看法：新耶路撒冷是神的百姓，也是神与他百姓同住的地方。神所预备的不只是一个地方给人住，更是一群人成为他的居所。

**2. 十二个门和十二根基。** 城门上「写着以色列十二个支派的名字」（21:12），城墙的根基上「有羔羊十二使徒的名字」（21:14）。以西结所见那座城的门，也是「按以色列支派的名字」（结48:31）。旧约的百姓和新约的教会，一同成为一座城。许多释经者认为，这表示神的百姓从来只有一个，就是因信归于基督的人；时代论的释经者（例如华尔伏）则认为，以色列和教会在这城里同住，却仍各有分别。无论哪一种看法，他们都是因羔羊得以进城。

**3. 「长、宽、高都是一样」。** 「城是四方的，长宽一样。天使用苇子量那城，共有四千里，长、宽、高都是一样」（21:16）。原文作一万二千斯他迪，约合二千二百公里，和合本换算成了「四千里」。一万二千和城墙的「一百四十四肘」（21:17），都是和神百姓相连的十二的倍数。最值得注意的是城的形状：长宽高都一样，是一个正立方体。旧约中唯一这样的地方，是圣殿的至圣所：「内殿长二十肘，宽二十肘，高二十肘，墙面都贴上精金」（王上6:20）。从前只有大祭司一年一次可以进去，现在整座城都是至圣所，城也是「精金的」（21:18）。神的同在不再被关在一个小房间里，而是充满全城。

**4. 宝石、珍珠和精金。** 城墙的十二根基用十二样宝石修饰（21:19-20）。以赛亚早已应许那受困苦的锡安：「我必以彩色安置你的石头，以蓝宝石立定你的根基」（赛54:11）。许多人指出，这十二样宝石多数也出现在刻着十二支派名字的大祭司胸牌上（出28:17-20），只是古代宝石的名称不易一一对上。「十二个门是十二颗珍珠……城内的街道是精金，好像明透的玻璃」（21:21）。巴比伦用来夸耀的，正是「金子、宝石，和珍珠」（18:16）。巴比伦的财富是抢来卖去的，新耶路撒冷的荣美却是神所赐的，精金在那里只是铺路用的；成功神学（健康财富福音）拿黄金街道作追求财富的理由，是把方向弄反了。这城宝贵，是因为「城中有神的荣耀」（21:11）。

> 要点：新耶路撒冷是羔羊的新妇。整座城像至圣所一样，充满神的同在；它的荣美不是人的财富，而是神的荣耀。

## 深读 21:22-27 城内没有殿

**1. 「我未见城内有殿」。** 「我未见城内有殿，因主神－全能者和羔羊为城的殿。」（21:22）对一个犹太读者来说，没有圣殿的耶路撒冷简直难以想象；以西结所见的复兴异象用了九章描写圣殿（以西结书第40至48章），那城的名字叫「耶和华的所在」（结48:35）。约翰所见的城却没有殿，因为殿所指向的那一位，已经亲自在那里。值得注意的是，「主神－全能者和羔羊」一同是城的殿：羔羊与父同受敬拜，也与父同为神百姓的居所。

**2. 「羔羊为城的灯」。** 「那城内又不用日月光照；因有神的荣耀光照，又有羔羊为城的灯。」（21:23）这是以赛亚的应许：「日头不再作你白昼的光；月亮也不再发光照耀你。耶和华却要作你永远的光」（赛60:19）。

**3. 「列国要在城的光里行走」。** 「列国要在城的光里行走；地上的君王必将自己的荣耀归与那城。」（21:24；参26节）这又是以赛亚书第60章的画面：「万国要来就你的光」（赛60:3）。在启示录前面，「地上的君王」一直和巴比伦行淫（17:2；18:9）；现在他们却把荣耀带进圣城。释经者有几种理解：
- **列国归向神：** 包衡等学者认为，这里显出启示录的一条主线：被杀的羔羊不但审判列国，也要赢得列国，使他们悔改归向神（参15:4）。
- **列国的文化被洁净后带进城：** 有些学者（例如理查德·穆欧）认为，人类历史中一切美好的创造，例如艺术、学问和各民族的文化，经过洁净以后，都要以某种方式被带进新天新地。
这两种理解并不互相排斥。但无论哪一种，这都不是说人人最后都要得救。下一节立刻说明了界限。

**4. 「只有名字写在羔羊生命册上的才得进去」。** 「城门白昼总不关闭，在那里原没有黑夜。……凡不洁净的，并那行可憎与虚谎之事的，总不得进那城；只有名字写在羔羊生命册上的才得进去。」（21:25、27）城门永不关闭，因为再没有仇敌要防备；可是这不等于什么都可以进去。决定谁进去的，不是人自己的功绩，而是他的名字是不是写在「羔羊」的生命册上。这册子见第20章。

> 要点：那城不需要殿，因为神和羔羊就在那里；不需要日月，因为羔羊就是它的灯。城门一直开着，进去的却只有属于羔羊的人。

## 默想

**默想：** 「坐宝座的说：『看哪，我将一切都更新了！』又说：『你要写上；因这些话是可信的，是真实的。』」（21:5）秀兰三十四岁，七年前被货车撞了，从此坐轮椅。那一年家里也散了：父亲做生意欠了一大笔债，跑到外地，几年没有音信；母亲一个人照顾她，脾气越来越急，母女俩常为小事吵架。她长大的村子前年拆了修路，只剩一片推平的黄土。每次教会唱「这世界非我家」，她都唱得很大声，心里想：好在这一切都要过去，总有一天我要离开这个身体、这个家、这块地，到天上去。可是有一次，她对小组里的姊妹说了一句实话：「我有时候觉得，神好像只是要把我从一堆废墟里捞出去；至于那堆废墟，他就不管了。」她也承认，这几年她对神说过很多难听的话，对母亲也是。

坐宝座的那一位，不是说他要另造一些新的东西，而是说「我将一切都更新了」。他起初看他所造的「都甚好」（创1:31），到末了也没有放弃。秀兰的身体不是要被丢掉的壳子。复活的主让门徒摸他，说「实在是我了」（路24:39）；保罗说，我们的身体「所种的是软弱的，复活的是强壮的」（林前15:43）。将来复活的，就是秀兰自己。她的家也不是神不屑一顾的废墟。圣城的十二个门上，写的是雅各十二个儿子的名字：那是一个满了偏心、嫉妒和欺骗的家，哥哥们恨约瑟，「不与他说和睦的话」（创37:4）。神没有把这些名字抹掉，而是把它们刻在他永远的城门上。至于那片推平的黄土，我们不知道新地上会有怎样的山川村落；但我们知道，主说「温柔的人有福了！因为他们必承受地土」（太5:5），神没有说他要丢弃这地。

这一切的根据，不在秀兰身上。坐宝座的接着说：「都成了！」（21:6）羔羊已经在十字架上成就了救赎，所以神能宣告新创造已经成就。生命泉的水是「白白」赐给口渴的人的。对神说过难听话的人可以来，对母亲说过伤人话的人也可以来，连那位欠了债、一走了之的父亲，如果读到这里，也可以来；不是先把自己修好了再来，而是口渴的时候就来。也许秀兰今天能做的，只是把那句实话原原本本地告诉神，再找一个时机，对母亲说一声对不起。可是更新不是从她的努力开始的，而是从坐宝座的那一位开始的，他的话「是可信的，是真实的」。

约翰最后看见，那城「不用日月光照；因有神的荣耀光照，又有羔羊为城的灯」，「在那里原没有黑夜」（21:23、25）。照亮那城的，不是它的碧玉，也不是它的精金，而是羔羊。在十字架上，他曾经走进「遍地都黑暗了」的那几个时辰（太27:45），好让秀兰，也让我，不再永远住在黑夜里。那一天，她要用复活的身体，在羔羊的光中行走（21:24）。那里没有黑夜，因为羔羊就是那城的灯。

## The Shape of the Chapter

After the judgment at the great white throne (20:11–15), what John sees is not an empty heaven but "a new heaven and a new earth" (21:1). The chapter falls into three parts:
- **21:1–8, the new heaven and new earth and the dwelling place of God:** the holy city comes down out of heaven; God's dwelling place is with man; the One on the throne says, "Behold, I am making all things new."
- **21:9–21, the Bride, the wife of the Lamb:** the angel will show John the Bride, and what he sees is a city, its length, width and height equal, its gates pearl, its street pure gold.
- **21:22–27, no temple in the city:** the Lord God and the Lamb are its temple, and its lamp is the Lamb; only those written in the Lamb's book of life enter.

In chapters 17–18 Babylon is called "the great prostitute"; here the holy city is called "the Bride" (21:2, 9), and John is shown each city by one of the angels with the seven bowls (17:1; 21:9). The vision of the new heaven and new earth runs on to 22:5.

## Going Deeper: 21:1–4 A New Heaven and a New Earth

**1. "The first heaven and the first earth had passed away."** "Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea was no more." (21:1) This comes from Isaiah's promise: "For behold, I create new heavens and a new earth, and the former things shall not be remembered or come into mind" (Isa 65:17). The question is whether the new heaven and earth replace this world after it is destroyed, or renew it.
- **Renewal:** Many interpreters (such as Calvin, Herman Bavinck and Anthony Hoekema) hold that God will purify and renew this created world, just as he will raise this same body of ours. Paul says, "the creation itself will be set free from its bondage to decay and obtain the freedom of the glory of the children of God" (Rom 8:21). Peter likewise says, "according to his promise we are waiting for new heavens and a new earth in which righteousness dwells" (2 Pet 3:13); on the variant in 2 Peter 3:10, see the study of 2 Peter 3.
- **Annihilation and new creation:** Some theologians (for example the seventeenth-century Lutheran Johann Gerhard) have held that the present heaven and earth will be reduced to nothing and God will create new ones. They give weight to expressions like "passed away" (21:1) and "earth and sky fled away, and no place was found for them" (20:11).
This study takes the renewal view: "passed away" can mean that the former condition, cursed and decaying, is wholly gone, not necessarily that creation itself vanishes.

**2. A word for Chinese churches.** We often sing songs like "This World Is Not My Home," which remind us that we are "strangers and exiles on the earth" (Heb 11:13), and that is right. But if that is all we hear, we easily picture salvation as souls escaping a world doomed to burn, as if the body, family, land, work and culture had nothing to do with God's salvation. Some picture heaven as a glittering fairyland, much like the Western Pure Land of the Buddhist *Amitabha Sutra*, which also has ground of gold and pools of seven jewels. Revelation's city also has gold and jewels, but its imagery comes from Isaiah and Ezekiel; its center is not the gold but God and the Lamb, and entry is not by rebirth through reciting a name but by free grace. Nor is the hope it describes an escape to somewhere else. The holy city comes "down out of heaven from God" (21:2), and God himself comes to live with people. The book moves from heaven to earth, not from earth to heaven.

**3. "The sea was no more."** This need not mean the new earth has no oceans. In Revelation the sea often stands for chaos, evil and threat: the beast rises "out of the sea" (13:1; see Dan 7:3), and the sea gives up its dead (20:13). Many interpreters (for example G. K. Beale) take "the sea was no more" mainly to mean that every power hostile to God and threatening his people is gone.

**4. "The dwelling place of God is with man."** "And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, 'Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God.'" (21:3) This is the promise the whole Bible keeps repeating: "I will make my dwelling among you... and will be your God, and you shall be my people" (Lev 26:11–12; see Ezek 37:27). Later "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14), and "dwelt" there shares a root with "dwelling place" (tent) here. In the new heaven and earth God no longer lives in one tent or one temple; he lives with his people.

**5. "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes."** "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning nor crying nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away." (21:4) This fulfills the promise of chapter 7 (7:17). The text does not say those tears did not matter; it says God himself will wipe them away.

> The point: God's salvation does not rescue people out of a discarded world. God himself comes to live with them and renews all that he has made.

## Going Deeper: 21:5–8 "Behold, I Am Making All Things New"

**1. "All things new."** "And he who was seated on the throne said, 'Behold, I am making all things new.' Also he said, 'Write this down, for these words are trustworthy and true.'" (21:5) This is one of the very few times in Revelation that God on the throne speaks in his own voice (see also 1:8). God does not say he is making all new things, but that he is making all things new. The emphasis falls on "all things": this world, the bodies of God's people, their history, all lie within God's work of renewal. "Am making" is a present tense, and some think the renewal has already begun with Christ's resurrection (2 Cor 5:17) and will be complete on that day. God has it written down so that churches under pressure have a sentence they can read again and again.

**2. "It is done!"** "And he said to me, 'It is done! I am the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end. To the thirsty I will give from the spring of the water of life without payment.'" (21:6) When the seventh bowl was poured out, a voice from the throne also said, "It is done!" (16:17). That was judgment completed; this is new creation completed. The Greek verb is not the one in "It is finished" (John 19:30), but God can declare it because the Lamb has already accomplished redemption on the cross. Alpha and Omega are the first and last letters of the Greek alphabet: history begins with God and ends in him.

**3. "To the thirsty... without payment."** The condition for entering the new heaven and earth is not being able to pay but being thirsty. "Without payment" means free. It echoes Isaiah's call: "he who has no money, come" (Isa 55:1). The last chapter of the book will say it once more (22:17).

**4. "I will be his God and he will be my son."** "The one who conquers will have this heritage, and I will be his God and he will be my son." (21:7) This comes from God's promise to David: "I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son" (2 Sam 7:14); Revelation turns "father" into "God," joining it to the covenant promise of verse 3. The promises to the seven churches in chapters 2–3 are gathered up here: what the conqueror inherits is God himself.

**5. "But as for the cowardly, the faithless..."** "But as for the cowardly, the faithless, the detestable, as for murderers, the sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars, their portion will be in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur, which is the second death." (21:8) The first item is "the cowardly." Many interpreters (for example Beale and Grant Osborne) think that in this book's setting it means those who under pressure will not confess Christ and go over to the beast. This is a serious warning to the church, not only to outsiders. But it does not mean that a believer who has been weak or has fallen has no hope: Peter denied the Lord three times, and the Lord restored him. A similar list in Paul's letter to the Corinthians is followed by this: "And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified" (1 Cor 6:11). Those who conquer are not those who were never weak, but those who "have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" and keep trusting him (7:14; see 12:11).

> The point: The way into that new world is to receive the water of life without payment; those who refuse this grace and prefer to follow the beast have no share in it.

## Going Deeper: 21:9–21 The Bride, the Wife of the Lamb

**1. He hears of a Bride and sees a city.** The angel says, "Come, I will show you the Bride, the wife of the Lamb" (21:9). What John then sees is "the holy city Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God" (21:10). This is the pattern noted in the introduction (00): what John sees interprets what he heard, and the Bride is this city. So is the new Jerusalem a people or a place?
- **Mainly the people of God:** Some scholars (for example Robert Gundry) argue that the new Jerusalem is God's people, not a place where his people live (see Eph 2:20–22).
- **A real city:** Some dispensational writers (for example John Walvoord) take it as a real city whose stated dimensions can be read literally.
- **Both:** Many interpreters (for example Osborne) hold that the city is both a people and a place; Beale likewise stresses that it chiefly portrays God's people and his presence, yet set on a real "new earth" (21:1–2), not a state of mind floating in the air.
This study takes the third view: the new Jerusalem is God's people, and it is also the place where God lives with his people. What God prepares is not only a place for people to live in, but a people who become his dwelling.

**2. Twelve gates and twelve foundations.** On the gates "the names of the twelve tribes of the sons of Israel were inscribed" (21:12), and on the foundations of the wall "the twelve names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb" (21:14). The gates of the city Ezekiel saw were also "named after the tribes of Israel" (Ezek 48:31). The Old Testament people and the New Testament church together form one city. Many interpreters take this to mean God has only ever had one people, those who belong to Christ by faith; dispensational interpreters (for example Walvoord) see Israel and the church sharing the city while remaining distinct. On either reading, both enter through the Lamb.

**3. "Its length and width and height are equal."** "The city lies foursquare; its length the same as its width. And he measured the city with his rod, 12,000 stadia. Its length and width and height are equal." (21:16) Twelve thousand stadia is about 2,200 kilometers; the Chinese Union Version converts it to "four thousand li." Twelve thousand and the wall's "144 cubits" (21:17) are multiples of twelve, the number tied to God's people. Most striking is the city's shape: its length, width and height are equal, a perfect cube. The only such place in the Old Testament is the Most Holy Place of the temple: "The inner sanctuary was twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and twenty cubits high, and he overlaid it with pure gold" (1 Kgs 6:20). Once only the high priest could enter it, once a year; now the whole city is the Most Holy Place, and the city is "pure gold" too (21:18). God's presence is no longer shut in one small room. It fills the whole city.

**4. Jewels, pearls and gold.** The twelve foundations of the wall are adorned with twelve kinds of jewel (21:19–20). Isaiah had already promised afflicted Zion, "I will set your stones in antimony, and lay your foundations with sapphires" (Isa 54:11). Many note that most of these jewels also appear on the high priest's breastpiece, which bore the names of the twelve tribes (Exod 28:17–20), though ancient gem names do not map neatly. "And the twelve gates were twelve pearls... and the street of the city was pure gold, transparent as glass" (21:21). Babylon boasted of exactly this: "gold, with jewels, and with pearls" (18:16). Babylon's wealth was seized and traded; the new Jerusalem's beauty is God's gift, and gold there is only for paving. The prosperity gospel, which makes the streets of gold a reason to pursue wealth, turns the picture upside down. The city is precious because it has "the glory of God" (21:11).

> The point: The new Jerusalem is the Bride of the Lamb. The whole city, like the Most Holy Place, is filled with God's presence; its beauty is not human wealth but God's glory.

## Going Deeper: 21:22–27 No Temple in the City

**1. "I saw no temple in the city."** "And I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb." (21:22) To a Jewish reader a Jerusalem without a temple was almost unthinkable; Ezekiel's vision of the restored city gives nine chapters to its temple (Ezekiel chapters 40–48), and that city was named "The LORD is there" (Ezek 48:35). The city John sees has no temple, because the One the temple pointed to is there himself. Notice that "the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" together are the city's temple: the Lamb is worshiped with the Father, and with the Father he is the dwelling place of his people.

**2. "Its lamp is the Lamb."** "And the city has no need of sun or moon to shine on it, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb." (21:23) This is Isaiah's promise: "The sun shall be no more your light by day, nor for brightness shall the moon give you light; but the LORD will be your everlasting light" (Isa 60:19).

**3. "By its light will the nations walk."** "By its light will the nations walk, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it" (21:24; see v. 26). Again this is the picture of Isaiah 60: "nations shall come to your light" (Isa 60:3). Earlier in Revelation "the kings of the earth" committed immorality with Babylon (17:2; 18:9); now they bring their glory into the holy city. Interpreters read this in two main ways:
- **The nations turn to God:** Bauckham and others see here one of Revelation's main threads: the slain Lamb not only judges the nations but wins them, so that they repent and turn to God (see 15:4).
- **The cultures of the nations, purified, are brought in:** Some scholars (for example Richard Mouw) think that whatever is good in human history, such as art, learning and the cultures of the peoples, will in some way be purified and brought into the new creation.
The two readings do not exclude each other. On either reading, this does not mean that everyone will be saved in the end; the next verse at once sets the boundary.

**4. "Only those who are written in the Lamb's book of life."** "And its gates will never be shut by day—and there will be no night there.... But nothing unclean will ever enter it, nor anyone who does what is detestable or false, but only those who are written in the Lamb's book of life." (21:25, 27) The gates are never shut because there is no longer any enemy to guard against; but that does not mean anything at all may enter. What decides who enters is not a person's own merit, but whether his name is written in the book of life that belongs to "the Lamb." On this book, see chapter 20.

> The point: The city needs no temple, because God and the Lamb are there; it needs no sun or moon, because the Lamb is its lamp. Its gates stand open, yet those who enter are those who belong to the Lamb.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "And he who was seated on the throne said, 'Behold, I am making all things new.' Also he said, 'Write this down, for these words are trustworthy and true.'" (21:5) Xiulan is thirty-four. Seven years ago she was hit by a truck, and she has used a wheelchair ever since. That same year her family fell apart: her father's business ran up a large debt, and he went off to another province and has not been heard from in years; her mother cares for her alone, grows ever more short-tempered, and the two often quarrel over small things. Her home village was razed two years ago for a road, leaving only flattened yellow earth. Whenever the church sings "This World Is Not My Home," she sings loudest, thinking: thank God all this will pass; one day I will leave this body, this family, this land behind and go to heaven. But once she told a sister in her small group the truth: "Sometimes I feel God only wants to fish me out of a heap of ruins. As for the ruins, he doesn't care." She also admitted that in these years she has said many ugly things to God, and to her mother.

The One on the throne does not say he is making some other new things, but "I am making all things new." At the beginning he saw that what he had made was "very good" (Gen 1:31), and at the end he has not given up on it. Xiulan's body is not a shell to be thrown away. The risen Lord let his disciples touch him and said, "it is I myself" (Luke 24:39); Paul says of our bodies, "It is sown in weakness; it is raised in power" (1 Cor 15:43). The one who will be raised is Xiulan herself. Her family is not a ruin beneath God's notice either. On the twelve gates of the holy city are the names of the twelve sons of Jacob: a family full of favoritism, jealousy and deceit, whose brothers hated Joseph "and could not speak peacefully to him" (Gen 37:4). God did not erase those names; he carved them on the gates of his everlasting city. As for the flattened yellow earth, we do not know what hills and villages the new earth will hold; but we know the Lord said, "Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth" (Matt 5:5). God has not said he will throw this earth away.

None of this rests on Xiulan. The One on the throne goes on: "It is done!" (21:6). The Lamb has accomplished redemption on the cross, and so God can declare the new creation accomplished. The water of the spring of life is given "without payment" to the thirsty. The one who has said ugly things to God may come; the one who has said wounding things to her mother may come; even the father who ran up debts and walked away, if he reads this, may come. Not after first mending himself, but when he is thirsty. Perhaps all Xiulan can do today is tell God that honest sentence just as it is, and find a moment to say sorry to her mother. But renewal does not begin with her effort. It begins with the One on the throne, whose words "are trustworthy and true."

At the last John saw that the city "has no need of sun or moon to shine on it, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb," and "there will be no night there" (21:23, 25). What lights the city is not its jasper or its gold, but the Lamb. On the cross he went into those hours when "there was darkness over all the land" (Matt 27:45), so that Xiulan, and I, need not live in the night forever. On that day she will walk in the Lamb's light in her risen body (21:24). There will be no night there, for the Lamb is the city's lamp.
