## 本章脉络

第22章是启示录的末了，也是整本圣经的末了。全章可以分成两大段：
- **22:1-5，生命水的河和生命树：** 这是第21章新耶路撒冷异象的结尾（分章是后来才加上的）。河从神和羔羊的宝座流出来，河两岸有生命树；不再有咒诅；神的仆人要见他的面，作王直到永远。
- **22:6-21，结语：** 主三次说「我必快来」（22:7、12、20）；圣灵和新妇都说「来」；不可加添，不可删去；最后是恩惠的祝福。

圣经从一个园子开始，有河「从伊甸流出来」，园中有生命树（创2:9-10）；人犯罪以后被赶出去，有基路伯「把守生命树的道路」（创3:24）。圣经在一座城里结束，城中有河，有生命树，神的面不再隐藏。启示录一开始就是一封信（1:4），所以它也像书信一样，以祝福结束（22:21）。

## 深读 22:1-5 「也要见他的面」

**1. 生命水的河。** 「天使又指示我在城内街道当中一道生命水的河，明亮如水晶，从神和羔羊的宝座流出来。」（22:1）这是以西结所见的那道河：水从圣殿的门槛下流出来，「这河水所到之处，百物都必生活」（结47:1、9）。主耶稣也在节期的末日高声说：「人若渴了，可以到我这里来喝。」（约7:37）这河是从「神和羔羊的宝座」流出来的。生命的源头不在城的街道，也不在城里的人，而在宝座上。

**2. 生命树。** 「在河这边与那边有生命树，结十二样果子，每月都结果子；树上的叶子乃为医治万民。」（22:2）以西结也说，河两岸的树「每月必结新果子」，「叶子乃为治病」（结47:12）。在伊甸园里，生命树被把守起来，人不能再到它那里去；到了新耶路撒冷，生命树却在河的两边，向神所有的百姓敞开（参22:14）。主早已应许以弗所教会：「得胜的，我必将神乐园中生命树的果子赐给他吃」（2:7）。「医治万民」也不是说那里还有疾病，而是说列国一切的创伤，包括彼此仇恨、分裂的伤，都完全医好了。

**3. 「以后再没有咒诅」。** 「以后再没有咒诅；在城里有神和羔羊的宝座；他的仆人都要事奉他」（22:3）。这呼应撒迦利亚的应许：「人必住在其中，不再有咒诅」（亚14:11），也把我们带回创世记第3章：地因人的缘故受了咒诅。基督「为我们受了咒诅」（加3:13），所以咒诅能有完全除去的一天。这里「事奉」这个词，常用在祭司在圣殿里的事奉上。在那城里，神所有的仆人都是祭司（参1:6）。

**4. 「也要见他的面」。** 「也要见他的面。他的名字必写在他们的额上。」（22:4）这是全本圣经最大的盼望之一。主耶稣说：「清心的人有福了！因为他们必得见神。」（太5:8）保罗也说：「到那时就要面对面了」（林前13:12）。这里还有一个值得注意的地方：经文一面说「神和羔羊的宝座」，分明是两位；一面又说「他的仆人」「他的面」「他的名字」，用的都是单数。许多释经者认为，这表示父与羔羊同坐一个宝座，同受敬拜，同是一位神，而不是两位神。

**5. 「他的名字必写在他们的额上」。** 第13章，跟从兽的人在额上或右手上受了兽的印记（13:16）；神的仆人额上却有神的印（7:3）和羔羊与父的名（14:1）。旧约的大祭司额上戴着一面金牌，上面刻着「归耶和华为圣」（出28:36）。到那日，神所有的仆人都像大祭司一样，带着他的名，站在他面前。

**6. 「他们要作王，直到永永远远」。** 「不再有黑夜；他们也不用灯光、日光，因为主神要光照他们。他们要作王，直到永永远远。」（22:5）第20章说到圣徒与基督一同作王一千年（20:4、6）；这里说的是「直到永永远远」。人起初被造，就是要在神面前管理他所造的世界（创1:28）；这个呼召在新天新地要完全成就。可是他们作王，不是在神以外另立一个国，而是在他的光中，与他一同作王。

> 要点：圣经的结局是伊甸的恢复，而且比伊甸更好：咒诅除去，生命树向人敞开，神的百姓要见他的面。

## 深读 22:6-11 「不可封了这书」

**1. 「这些话是真实可信的」。** 「天使又对我说：『这些话是真实可信的。主就是众先知被感之灵的神，差遣他的使者，将那必要快成的事指示他仆人。』」（22:6）这句话几乎重复了全书的第一节（1:1），好像把整卷书前后封起来。接着主说：「看哪，我必快来！凡遵守这书上预言的有福了！」（22:7）这是全书七次「有福」中的第六次；和1:3一样，福是给「遵守」的人，不是给最会推算的人。关于「必要快成」和「日期近了」（22:6、10），见00篇。

**2. 约翰又俯伏下拜。** 约翰又要拜天使，天使却说「千万不可」，又说「你要敬拜神」（22:8-9；参19:10）；00篇的默想已经讲过这一段。

**3. 「不可封了这书」。** 「他又对我说：『不可封了这书上的预言，因为日期近了。』」（22:10）但以理曾受吩咐：「你要隐藏这话，封闭这书，直到末时。」（但12:4）约翰却受吩咐不可封。许多学者认为，这表示但以理所等候的「末时」，已经因基督的死和复活开始了。这卷书不是锁起来留给末世少数人解密的，而是现在就要在众教会中念、听、遵守的。

**4. 「不义的，叫他仍旧不义」。** 「不义的，叫他仍旧不义；污秽的，叫他仍旧污秽；为义的，叫他仍旧为义；圣洁的，叫他仍旧圣洁。」（22:11）这不是说神不要人悔改；整卷书一再呼召人悔改（2:5；9:20-21）。这句话很像但以理书12:10：「恶人仍必行恶……惟独智慧人能明白」。许多释经者认为，它的意思是：时候近了，人的选择正在定型；到了那日，就再没有改变的机会。所以悔改的时候是现在。

> 要点：这卷书不是密封的密码，而是要在今天打开、宣读、遵守的话；它最后仍然说：你要敬拜神。

## 深读 22:12-17 「圣灵和新妇都说：『来！』」

**1. 「赏罚在我」。** 「看哪，我必快来！赏罚在我，要照各人所行的报应他。」（22:12）这句话出自以赛亚：「他的赏赐在他那里；他的报应在他面前。」（赛40:10）圣经一致地说，人要照所行的受审判；可是人得救是「本乎恩，也因着信」，名字写在羔羊的生命册上（见第20章的讨论；参弗2:8-10）。

**2. 「我是阿拉法，我是俄梅戛」。** 「我是阿拉法，我是俄梅戛；我是首先的，我是末后的；我是初，我是终。」（22:13）在第1章和第21章，说「我是阿拉法，我是俄梅戛」的是主神（1:8；21:6）；以赛亚书中，说「我是首先的，我是末后的」的是耶和华（赛44:6）。主耶稣在1:17和2:8已经说过「我是首先的，我是末后的」；现在，那位「必快来」的耶稣把三组称号都用在自己身上，连主神所用的阿拉法和俄梅戛也在其中。启示录把只属于神的称号，毫不犹豫地用在耶稣身上。

**3. 「那些洗净自己衣服的有福了」。** 「那些洗净自己衣服的有福了！可得权柄能到生命树那里，也能从门进城。」（22:14）这是七次「有福」的最后一次。这里有一个经文异文：最古老的抄本（如西奈抄本、亚历山大抄本）作「洗净自己衣服」，和合本和英文标准版都照这个读法；后来大多数的抄本作遵守他诫命的，英文钦定本就是照这个读法译的。两种读法在希腊文中写起来很相近。照最古老的读法，进城的权柄不是靠自己的功劳，而是像第7章那许多人一样，「用羔羊的血把衣裳洗白净了」（7:14）。

**4. 「城外有那些犬类」。** 「城外有那些犬类、行邪术的、淫乱的、杀人的、拜偶像的，并一切喜好说谎言、编造虚谎的。」（22:15）「犬类」在古代犹太人口中指不洁净、在神百姓以外的人。这个名单和21:8相似，不是说城外还住着一群人，而是用画面说明：凡不肯悔改、喜爱谎言的人，都与生命无分。

**5. 「我－耶稣」。** 「我－耶稣差遣我的使者为众教会将这些事向你们证明。我是大卫的根，又是他的后裔。我是明亮的晨星。」（22:16）这卷书好像是由耶稣亲自签名的。他是「大卫的根」，又是「他的后裔」：许多释经者认为这两个称号都强调他是从大卫家出来的弥赛亚君王（参赛11:1、10；见第5章），也有人看出，大卫的子孙同时是大卫的主（可12:35-37）。「明亮的晨星」，见00篇。

**6. 两种「来」。** 「圣灵和新妇都说：『来！』听见的人也该说：『来！』口渴的人也当来；愿意的，都可以白白取生命的水喝。」（22:17）这一节有好几个「来」，释经者有两种主要的理解：
- **向主说的「来」和向人说的「来」：** 许多释经者（例如毕尔、格兰特·奥斯本）认为，前两个「来」是教会在圣灵感动下回应主「我必快来」（22:7、12）的祷告，像22:20那样；后半节才转过来，邀请口渴的人来喝生命的水。
- **都是向人发出的邀请：** 也有释经者认为，这一节的「来」都是向世人说的，圣灵和教会一同呼召人来就近基督。
无论哪一种，这一节都把两件事放在一起：教会盼望主来，教会也呼召人来。盼望主再来的教会，不能不向口渴的人发出邀请，而且是「白白」的邀请（参21:6）。

> 要点：说「我必快来」的，是与父同为阿拉法和俄梅戛的耶稣；在他来以前，生命的水白白赐给口渴的人。

## 深读 22:18-21 不可加添，不可删去

**1. 「若有人在这预言上加添什么」。** 「我向一切听见这书上预言的作见证，若有人在这预言上加添什么，神必将写在这书上的灾祸加在他身上；这书上的预言，若有人删去什么，神必从这书上所写的生命树和圣城删去他的分。」（22:18-19）这警告来自摩西的话：「所吩咐你们的话，你们不可加添，也不可删减」（申4:2；参申12:32）。古代的文献也有类似的咒诅，例如《亚里斯提亚书信》311记载，七十士译本译成以后，众人宣布咒诅任何更改这译本的人。

**2. 这警告指的是什么？** 约翰最直接说的，是「这书上的预言」，就是启示录这卷书。当时新约的书卷还没有收集成一本书，所以这两节经文本身，不是在宣告整本圣经的正典已经封闭。不过启示录放在圣经的末了，许多信徒也把这原则应用在整本圣经上；这样应用有它的道理，因为摩西（申4:2）和箴言（箴30:6）都有同样的警告。改革宗教会的《威斯敏斯德信条》（1646年）也承认：神关于他自己的荣耀、人的得救、信仰和生活所必需的全部旨意，都已记在圣经中，或可从圣经中正当推论出来，无论是借着圣灵的新启示，或是人的遗传，任何时候都不可再加添什么（第一章第六节）。这警告针对的是两种危险：
- **加添：** 自称得了新的启示，把它放在圣经之上，或者宣称这卷书的印要由某个新的人来揭开。00篇已经讲过东方闪电（自称全能神教会）的这类说法，以及遇到这种情形时应当怎样做。
- **删去：** 把这卷书中我们不喜欢的部分拿掉，例如神的审判，或者主真实的再来。
这并不是说，每一位和我们解释不同的传道人都受了咒诅。许多忠心的牧者劳苦传讲神的话，我们应当敬重他们（提前5:17）。

**3. 一个历史上的小插曲。** 英文钦定本22:19作从生命册上删去他的分，和合本和英文标准版却作「生命树」。布鲁斯·麦慈格等学者指出，十六世纪伊拉斯谟编订希腊文新约时，手上唯一一份启示录抄本缺了最后几节，他便从拉丁文武加大译本译回希腊文；而他所用的拉丁文抄本，正好把树（*ligno*）误抄成了册（*libro*）。这个读法就这样进入了英文钦定本所根据的经文。经文鉴别正是谨慎地寻找原文，免得在神的话上加添或删去。

**4. 「主耶稣啊，我愿你来！」** 「证明这事的说：『是了，我必快来！』阿们！主耶稣啊，我愿你来！」（22:20）这是主在圣经中最后的一句话，也是教会在圣经中最后的一个祷告。主说「我必快来」，教会说「阿们」，然后祷告「我愿你来」。关于亚兰文的 *maranatha*（林前16:22），见00篇。整卷书满了兽、碗和巴比伦的画面，可是读完以后，教会不是只剩下害怕，而是开口求主来。

**5. 「愿主耶稣的恩惠常与众圣徒同在」。** 「愿主耶稣的恩惠常与众圣徒同在。阿们！」（22:21）这一节的抄本有几种读法：有的作与众人同在（英文标准版照这个读法），有的作「与众圣徒同在」（和合本照这个读法），也有的作与圣徒同在或与你们众人同在。无论哪一种，圣经最后的一句话不是吩咐，也不是警告，而是恩惠。

> 要点：神的话不可加添，也不可删去，因为神「在这末世藉着他儿子晓谕我们」（来1:2），也在圣经中把基督完全地启示出来了。圣经以主的应许、教会的祷告和主耶稣的恩惠结束。

## 默想

**默想：** 「圣灵和新妇都说：『来！』听见的人也该说：『来！』口渴的人也当来；愿意的，都可以白白取生命的水喝。」「证明这事的说：『是了，我必快来！』阿们！主耶稣啊，我愿你来！」（22:17、20）一个读经小组用了几年的时间，从约翰福音的「太初有道」一直读到启示录的最后一章。最后一次聚会那天晚上，组长请大家一同祷告：「主耶稣啊，我愿你来！」八十岁的王奶奶马上就说了，她每天早上都这样祷告。二十六岁的小周却一直没有开口。散会以后，她留下来对组长说：「我说不出口。我还没结婚，工作刚有点起色，爸妈也还没信主。说实话，我不太希望主现在就来。」组长没有马上回答，因为他心里也有一件说不出口的事：这几年他带着大家读，自己却常常只是为了读完，好几章是前一晚匆匆翻过的，心里早就不渴了。

先看这两节经文的次序。在教会祷告「主耶稣啊，我愿你来」（22:20）以前，先有另一个「来」：「口渴的人也当来；愿意的，都可以白白取生命的水喝」（22:17）。主没有先要求我们热切地盼望他，才肯给我们水喝；他先把水白白地给口渴的人，给「愿意的」人，哪怕那份愿意还很小。组长那颗不渴的心，不必先装出渴来，他可以把这份干渴、这份麻木原原本本地带到主面前。那些能「到生命树那里」的人，不是读得最完整的人，而是「洗净自己衣服」的人（22:14），他们是在羔羊的血里洗净的。

那么，为什么要祷告「我愿你来」？不是因为结婚、工作、父母这些不重要；新耶路撒冷还要接受「列国的荣耀、尊贵」（21:26）。可是这一切里面，没有一样比得上这一句：「也要见他的面」（22:4）。摩西求见神的面，神说「人见我的面不能存活」（出33:20）；以色列的祭司世世代代为百姓祝福：「愿耶和华使他的脸光照你，赐恩给你」（民6:25）。到那日，这个祝福要完全应验：我们要亲眼看见那为我们被杀的羔羊的面。小周舍不得的那些好东西，都是从这张脸上照出来的光。至于她还没信主的父母，照许多释经者的理解，22:17告诉她：在主来以前，圣灵和新妇向他说「来」，也向口渴的人说「来」。她可以向主说「来」，也可以向父母说「来」。这两个「来」，在同一节经文里。

那天晚上，组长没有逼小周开口。他先承认了自己这几年的干渴，然后请王奶奶念圣经的最后一句。小周后来说，她那天还是没能说出「我愿你来」，只说了一句：「主啊，我愿意先来喝。」也许这就是许多人开始盼望他的方式。我们读完了这卷书，也读完了这本圣经的最后一页。最后一页没有给我们留下一个要通过的考验，而是留下一句祝福。那位说「是了，我必快来」的主，在他来以前，先把这句话留给我们：「愿主耶稣的恩惠常与众圣徒同在。阿们！」（22:21）

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 22 is the end of Revelation and the end of the whole Bible. It falls into two main parts:
- **22:1–5, the river of the water of life and the tree of life:** the close of chapter 21's vision of the new Jerusalem (chapter divisions were added much later). The river flows from the throne of God and of the Lamb, and the tree of life stands on both sides of it; nothing accursed remains; God's servants will see his face and reign forever.
- **22:6–21, the epilogue:** three times the Lord says, "I am coming soon" (22:7, 12, 20); the Spirit and the Bride say "Come"; nothing is to be added or taken away; and last, the blessing of grace.

The Bible begins in a garden, with a river that "flowed out of Eden," and the tree of life in the garden (Gen 2:9–10); after man sinned he was driven out, and cherubim were placed "to guard the way to the tree of life" (Gen 3:24). The Bible ends in a city, with a river, the tree of life, and God's face no longer hidden. Revelation began as a letter (1:4), so like a letter it ends with a blessing (22:21).

## Going Deeper: 22:1–5 "They Will See His Face"

**1. The river of the water of life.** "Then the angel showed me the river of the water of life, bright as crystal, flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb through the middle of the street of the city" (22:1–2). This is the river Ezekiel saw: water flowing from below the threshold of the temple, and "everything will live where the river goes" (Ezek 47:1, 9). On the last day of the feast Jesus cried out, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink" (John 7:37). The river flows "from the throne of God and of the Lamb." The source of life is not in the city's street or in its people, but on the throne.

**2. The tree of life.** "Also, on either side of the river, the tree of life with its twelve kinds of fruit, yielding its fruit each month. The leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations." (22:2) Ezekiel also said that the trees on both banks "will bear fresh fruit every month," and "their leaves for healing" (Ezek 47:12). In Eden the tree of life was guarded so that man could not reach it; in the new Jerusalem it stands on both sides of the river, open to all God's people (see 22:14). The Lord had already promised the church in Ephesus, "To the one who conquers I will grant to eat of the tree of life, which is in the paradise of God" (2:7). "The healing of the nations" does not mean there will still be sickness there, but that every wound of the nations, including the wounds of hatred and division between them, is fully healed.

**3. "No longer will there be anything accursed."** "No longer will there be anything accursed, but the throne of God and of the Lamb will be in it, and his servants will worship him." (22:3) This echoes Zechariah's promise for Jerusalem, "there shall never again be a decree of utter destruction" (Zech 14:11; the Chinese Union Version reads "no more curse"), and takes us back to Genesis 3, where the ground was cursed because of man. Christ "redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us" (Gal 3:13), so the curse can one day be wholly removed. The word translated "worship" here is often used of priestly service in the temple. In that city all God's servants are priests (see 1:6).

**4. "They will see his face."** "They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads." (22:4) This is one of the greatest hopes in the whole Bible. Jesus said, "Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God" (Matt 5:8), and Paul, "then face to face" (1 Cor 13:12). Notice something else: the text speaks of "the throne of God and of the Lamb," plainly two, and then says "his servants," "his face," "his name," all singular. Many interpreters take this to show that the Father and the Lamb, though distinct ("God and the Lamb"), share one throne, receive one worship and are one God, not two gods.

**5. "His name will be on their foreheads."** In chapter 13 the followers of the beast receive its mark on the forehead or right hand (13:16); God's servants instead bear God's seal (7:3) and the name of the Lamb and his Father (14:1) on their foreheads. In the Old Testament the high priest wore a gold plate on his forehead engraved "Holy to the LORD" (Exod 28:36). On that day all God's servants will stand before him like the high priest, bearing his name.

**6. "They will reign forever and ever."** "And night will be no more. They will need no light of lamp or sun, for the Lord God will be their light, and they will reign forever and ever." (22:5) Chapter 20 spoke of the saints reigning with Christ for a thousand years (20:4, 6); here it is "forever and ever." Humanity was created to rule God's world before his face (Gen 1:28); that calling is fulfilled in the new creation. But they do not reign by setting up a kingdom apart from God. They reign with him, in his light.

> The point: The Bible ends with Eden restored and surpassed: the curse removed, the tree of life open, and God's people seeing his face.

## Going Deeper: 22:6–11 "Do Not Seal Up the Words"

**1. "These words are trustworthy and true."** "And he said to me, 'These words are trustworthy and true. And the Lord, the God of the spirits of the prophets, has sent his angel to show his servants what must soon take place.'" (22:6) This almost repeats the book's first verse (1:1), as if closing the whole book at both ends. Then the Lord says, "And behold, I am coming soon. Blessed is the one who keeps the words of the prophecy of this book." (22:7) This is the sixth of the book's seven "blessed" sayings; as in 1:3, the blessing is for those who "keep" it, not for the best calculators. On "must soon take place" and "the time is near" (22:6, 10), see the introduction (00).

**2. John falls down to worship again.** John again falls at the angel's feet, and the angel says, "You must not do that!... Worship God" (22:8–9; see 19:10); the introduction's meditation dwelt on this.

**3. "Do not seal up the words."** "And he said to me, 'Do not seal up the words of the prophecy of this book, for the time is near.'" (22:10) Daniel was told, "shut up the words and seal the book, until the time of the end" (Dan 12:4). John is told not to seal it. Many scholars take this to mean that "the time of the end" Daniel awaited has already begun with Christ's death and resurrection. This book is not locked away for a few to decode at the end of history. It is to be read, heard and kept in the churches now.

**4. "Let the evildoer still do evil."** "Let the evildoer still do evil, and the filthy still be filthy, and the righteous still do right, and the holy still be holy." (22:11) This does not mean God does not want people to repent; the whole book calls people to repent again and again (2:5; 9:20–21). The saying is much like Daniel 12:10: "the wicked shall act wickedly... but those who are wise shall understand." Many interpreters take it to mean that the time is near and people's choices are setting hard; when that day comes there will be no more chance to change. So the time to repent is now.

> The point: This book is not a sealed code but a word to be opened, read aloud and kept today; to the end it says, Worship God.

## Going Deeper: 22:12–17 "The Spirit and the Bride Say, 'Come'"

**1. "Bringing my recompense with me."** "Behold, I am coming soon, bringing my recompense with me, to repay everyone for what he has done." (22:12) This comes from Isaiah: "behold, his reward is with him, and his recompense before him" (Isa 40:10). Scripture consistently says that people will be judged by what they have done; yet people are saved by grace through faith, their names written in the Lamb's book of life (see the discussion in chapter 20; compare Eph 2:8–10).

**2. "I am the Alpha and the Omega."** "I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end." (22:13) In chapters 1 and 21 the one who says "I am the Alpha and the Omega" is the Lord God (1:8; 21:6); in Isaiah the one who says "I am the first and I am the last" is the LORD (Isa 44:6). Jesus already called himself "the first and the last" (1:17; 2:8); now the Jesus who "is coming soon" takes all three pairs to himself, including the "Alpha and Omega" that the Lord God used. Revelation applies to Jesus, without hesitation, titles that belong to God alone.

**3. "Blessed are those who wash their robes."** "Blessed are those who wash their robes, so that they may have the right to the tree of life and that they may enter the city by the gates." (22:14) This is the last of the seven "blessed" sayings. There is a textual variant here: the oldest manuscripts (such as Codex Sinaiticus and Codex Alexandrinus) read "wash their robes," which the Chinese Union Version and the ESV follow; most later manuscripts read "do his commandments," which the King James Version translates. In Greek the two readings look much alike. On the oldest reading, the right to enter the city does not rest on one's own merit; like the great multitude of chapter 7, these "have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (7:14).

**4. "Outside are the dogs."** "Outside are the dogs and sorcerers and the sexually immoral and murderers and idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood." (22:15) "Dogs" was a harsh Jewish word for the unclean, those outside God's people. Like the list in 21:8, it does not describe a population living just outside the walls; it pictures those who will not repent and who love falsehood as having no share in life.

**5. "I, Jesus."** "I, Jesus, have sent my angel to testify to you about these things for the churches. I am the root and the descendant of David, the bright morning star." (22:16) It is as if Jesus signs the book himself. "The root and the descendant of David": many interpreters take both titles to stress that he is the Davidic Messiah-King (see Isa 11:1, 10; chapter 5), and some also see that David's son is David's Lord (Mark 12:35–37). On "the bright morning star," see the introduction (00).

**6. Two kinds of "Come."** "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come.' And let the one who hears say, 'Come.' And let the one who is thirsty come; let the one who desires take the water of life without price." (22:17) Interpreters read the several "Come"s of this verse in two main ways:
- **"Come" to the Lord, and "come" to people:** Many interpreters (for example Beale and Grant Osborne) think the first two are the church's prayer, prompted by the Spirit, answering the Lord's "I am coming soon" (22:7, 12), like 22:20; the second half of the verse then turns to invite the thirsty to drink the water of life.
- **All invitation:** Other interpreters take every "Come" in the verse as addressed to the world: the Spirit and the church together call people to come to Christ.
On either reading the verse holds two things together: the church longs for the Lord to come, and the church calls people to come. A church that longs for the Lord's return cannot help inviting the thirsty, and the invitation is "without price" (see 21:6).

> The point: The One who says "I am coming soon" is Jesus, the Alpha and the Omega with the Father; until he comes, the water of life is free to the thirsty.

## Going Deeper: 22:18–21 Nothing Added, Nothing Taken Away

**1. "If anyone adds to them."** "I warn everyone who hears the words of the prophecy of this book: if anyone adds to them, God will add to him the plagues described in this book, and if anyone takes away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God will take away his share in the tree of life and in the holy city, which are described in this book." (22:18–19) The warning comes from Moses: "You shall not add to the word that I command you, nor take from it" (Deut 4:2; see Deut 12:32). Ancient writings had similar curses; the *Letter of Aristeas* 311, for example, records that once the Septuagint was translated, a curse was pronounced on anyone who altered it.

**2. What does the warning refer to?** What John speaks of most directly is "the words of the prophecy of this book," that is, Revelation itself. The New Testament books had not yet been gathered into one volume, so these verses are not in themselves a declaration that the canon of the whole Bible is closed. Yet since Revelation stands at the end of the Bible, many believers apply the principle to all of Scripture; and there is good reason for doing so, since Moses (Deut 4:2) and Proverbs (Prov 30:6) give the same warning. The Reformed churches also confess in the *Westminster Confession of Faith* (1646) that the whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for his own glory, man's salvation, faith and life is set down in Scripture or deducible from it, "unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men" (1.6). The warning targets two dangers:
- **Adding:** claiming a new revelation and setting it above Scripture, or claiming that the seals of this book are to be opened by some new person. The introduction (00) has already dealt with such claims from Eastern Lightning (which calls itself the Church of Almighty God), and with what to do when one meets them.
- **Taking away:** removing the parts of this book we do not like, such as God's judgment or the Lord's real return.
This does not mean that every preacher who interprets the book differently from us is under a curse. Many faithful pastors labor in preaching God's word, and we should honor them (1 Tim 5:17).

**3. A small episode from history.** The King James Version of 22:19 reads "take away his part out of the book of life," where the Chinese Union Version and the ESV read "tree of life." Bruce Metzger and others note that when Erasmus edited the Greek New Testament in the sixteenth century, his only manuscript of Revelation lacked the last few verses, so he translated them back into Greek from the Latin Vulgate, and the Latin copies he used had miscopied "tree" (*ligno*) as "book" (*libro*). This is how that reading came into the text behind the King James Version. Textual criticism is precisely the careful search for the original wording, so that nothing is added to or taken from God's word.

**4. "Come, Lord Jesus!"** "He who testifies to these things says, 'Surely I am coming soon.' Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!" (22:20) These are the Lord's last words in the Bible, and the church's last prayer. The Lord says, "I am coming soon"; the church says "Amen," and then prays, "Come." On the Aramaic *maranatha* (1 Cor 16:22), see the introduction (00). The book is full of beasts, bowls and Babylon, yet when it is finished the church is not left only with fear. It opens its mouth and asks the Lord to come.

**5. "The grace of the Lord Jesus be with all."** "The grace of the Lord Jesus be with all. Amen." (22:21) The manuscripts read this verse in several ways: "with all" (followed by the ESV), "with all the saints" (followed by the Chinese Union Version), "with the saints," and "with you all." On any reading, the last sentence of the Bible is not a command or a warning, but grace.

> The point: God's word must not be added to or taken from, for "in these last days he has spoken to us by his Son" (Heb 1:2), and in Scripture he has fully revealed Christ. The Bible ends with the Lord's promise, the church's prayer, and the grace of the Lord Jesus.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come.' And let the one who hears say, 'Come.' And let the one who is thirsty come; let the one who desires take the water of life without price." "He who testifies to these things says, 'Surely I am coming soon.' Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!" (22:17, 20) A Bible-reading group spent several years reading from "In the beginning was the Word" in John's Gospel all the way to the last chapter of Revelation. At the last meeting the leader asked everyone to pray together, "Come, Lord Jesus!" Grandma Wang, who is eighty, said it at once; she prays it every morning. Xiao Zhou, who is twenty-six, said nothing. After the meeting she stayed behind and told the leader, "I can't say it. I'm not married yet, my work is just starting to go well, and my parents don't believe. To be honest, I don't really want the Lord to come yet." The leader did not answer at once, because he had something he could not say either. For years he had led the group through these books, but often only to get through them; he had skimmed several chapters the night before, and his heart had long stopped being thirsty.

Look first at the order of these verses. Before the church prays "Come, Lord Jesus" (22:20), there is another "come": "let the one who is thirsty come; let the one who desires take the water of life without price" (22:17). The Lord does not first require us to long for him eagerly before he will give us water. He gives the water first, freely, to the thirsty and to "the one who desires," however small that desire still is. The leader's heart, which no longer thirsts, does not have to fake a thirst first; he can bring his dryness and numbness to the Lord just as they are. Those who may come "to the tree of life" are not those who read most completely, but those who "wash their robes" (22:14), and they are washed in the blood of the Lamb.

Why, then, pray "Come"? Not because marriage, work and parents do not matter; the new Jerusalem will even receive "the glory and the honor of the nations" (21:26). But nothing among them compares with this: "They will see his face" (22:4). When Moses asked to see God's face, God said, "man shall not see me and live" (Exod 33:20); generation after generation Israel's priests blessed the people, "the LORD make his face to shine upon you and be gracious to you" (Num 6:25). On that day the blessing will be fulfilled in full: we will see with our own eyes the face of the Lamb who was slain for us. The good things Xiao Zhou cannot bear to lose are light that shines from that face. As for her parents who do not yet believe, as many read it, 22:17 tells her that until the Lord comes, the Spirit and the Bride say "Come" to him and also say "Come" to the thirsty. She can say "Come" to the Lord, and she can say "Come" to her parents. Both are in the same verse.

That evening the leader did not press Xiao Zhou to speak. He first confessed his own dryness of these years, and then asked Grandma Wang to read the last verse of the Bible. Xiao Zhou said later that she still could not say "Come, Lord Jesus" that night. She only said, "Lord, I want to come and drink first." Perhaps that is how many people begin to long for him. We have finished this book, and the last page of the Bible. The last page does not leave us with a test to pass. It leaves us a blessing. The Lord who says, "Surely I am coming soon," leaves us these words before he comes: "The grace of the Lord Jesus be with all. Amen." (22:21)
