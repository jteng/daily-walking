## 本章脉络

第一章有两大部分。**问安与心愿**（1-15节）：保罗介绍自己和他所传的福音：这福音是论到神的儿子，「按肉体说，是从大卫后裔生的；按圣善的灵说，因从死里复活，以大能显明是神的儿子」；他也告诉罗马的信徒，他多么切切地想见他们。**全书的主题**（16-17节）：「我不以福音为耻；这福音本是神的大能。」**人类的光景**（18-32节）：「神的忿怒从天上显明在一切不虔不义的人身上」；人知道神，却不荣耀他、不感谢他，将神的荣耀换成偶像，神就「任凭」他们。

这一章的顺序很重要：保罗先宣告好消息（16-17节），然后才讲坏消息（18-32节）。不是因为坏消息不重要，而是因为只有在福音的光中，人才能正视自己的罪，而不至于绝望。

## 深读 1:1-7 论到他儿子

**1. 仆人、使徒、特派。** 「耶稣基督的仆人保罗，奉召为使徒，特派传神的福音。」（1:1）三个词说出保罗的身份：他是「仆人」（原文是「奴仆」），属于基督；是「使徒」，是被差遣的；是「特派」的，被分别出来，专为福音而活。「特派」一词和「法利赛人」（意思是「分别出来的人」）字根相近：保罗从前为律法被分别出来，如今为福音被分别出来。

**2. 不是新发明的福音。** 「这福音是神从前藉众先知在圣经上所应许的。」（1:2）保罗一开头就说明，他所传的不是新宗教，而是旧约应许的应验。

**3. 福音的中心是一个人。** 这福音是「论到他儿子」，就是我主耶稣基督：「按肉体说，是从大卫后裔生的；按圣善的灵说，因从死里复活，以大能显明是神的儿子」（1:3-4）。福音首先不是一套方法，而是一个人：他是大卫的后裔，应验了神对大卫的应许（撒下7:12-16）；他也是神的儿子，藉着复活被「以大能显明」。他不是从复活那一刻才成为神的儿子，而是在复活中被公开宣告、高举为有能力的神子。

**4. 恩惠与使命。** 「我们从他受了恩惠并使徒的职分，在万国之中叫人为他的名信服真道。」（1:5）「信服真道」原文是「信心的顺服」：真正的信心，必带出顺服。这句话和全书最后一段（16:26）首尾呼应。

**5. 为神所爱、奉召作圣徒。** 保罗写信「给你们在罗马、为神所爱、奉召作圣徒的众人」（1:7）。罗马的信徒，在世人眼中也许是奴隶、工匠、外邦人；在神眼中，他们是「为神所爱」的，是「圣徒」。圣徒不是少数特别敬虔的人，而是每一个被神呼召、分别归他的人。

> 要点：福音的中心不是我们，而是神的儿子耶稣基督：他是大卫的后裔，又藉着复活被显明是有大能的神子。信他的人，不论在世上是谁，都是「为神所爱」的圣徒。

## 深读 1:8-15 「我都欠他们的债」

**1. 为他们感谢神。** 「第一，我靠着耶稣基督，为你们众人感谢我的神，因你们的信德传遍了天下。」（1:8）保罗还没有见过他们，却已经为他们感谢神。

**2. 彼此的信心。** 他想见他们，「要把些属灵的恩赐分给你们，使你们可以坚固」，又立刻补充：「这样，我在你们中间，因你与我彼此的信心，就可以同得安慰。」（1:11-12）伟大的使徒，也期待从普通信徒那里得着鼓励。

**3. 欠债的人。** 「无论是希腊人、化外人、聪明人、愚拙人，我都欠他们的债，所以情愿尽我的力量，将福音也传给你们在罗马的人。」（1:14-15）保罗没有欠这些人什么，可是他从基督那里领受了福音，就觉得自己欠了所有还没有听见的人一笔债。恩典白白地临到他，也成了他对别人的责任。

## 深读 1:16-17 全书的主题

**1. 「我不以福音为耻」。** 「我不以福音为耻；这福音本是神的大能，要救一切相信的，先是犹太人，后是希腊人。」（1:16）在罗马人眼中，一个被钉十字架的人，是羞耻的极点。保罗却说，这福音不是软弱，而是「神的大能」。它不只是关于救恩的消息，它本身就是神拯救人的能力。

**2. 「先是犹太人，后是希腊人」。** 救恩是给「一切相信的」，不分种族；可是神的应许首先是给以色列的，所以福音也「先」传给犹太人。这个次序，第九至十一章会详细讨论。

**3. 「神的义」。** 「因为神的义正在这福音上显明出来；这义是本于信，以至于信。」（1:17）「神的义」可以指神自己的公义，也可以指神赐给人的义。在罗马书中，两者是连在一起的：公义的神，藉着福音，把他的义赐给信的人，使罪人在他面前被算为义（3:21-26）。「本于信，以至于信」，意思是这义自始至终都是藉着信领受的。

**4. 「义人必因信得生」。** 保罗引用哈巴谷书2:4。先知在巴比伦侵略的黑暗中，听见神说：义人要因信而活。保罗说，这句话在福音里得着完全的意义：人在神面前称义、得生命，都是因着信。

> 要点：福音是神的大能，因为它显明了神的义：神自己是公义的，又把义白白地赐给一切相信的人。人得救，自始至终都是因着信。

## 深读 1:18-32 神的忿怒与人的交换

**1. 神的忿怒。** 「原来，神的忿怒从天上显明在一切不虔不义的人身上，就是那些行不义阻挡真理的人。」（1:18）「原来」原文是「因为」：人需要神的义，是因为人在神的忿怒之下。神的忿怒不是失控的脾气，而是圣洁的神对罪坚定、公正的反应。

**2. 人人都知道。** 「神的事情，人所能知道的，原显明在人心里……自从造天地以来，神的永能和神性是明明可知的，虽是眼不能见，但藉着所造之物就可以晓得，叫人无可推诿。」（1:19-20）神藉着创造向所有人显明自己。人的问题不是不知道，而是「阻挡真理」：把已经知道的压下去。

**3. 根源：不荣耀神，也不感谢他。** 「因为，他们虽然知道神，却不当作神荣耀他，也不感谢他。他们的思念变为虚妄，无知的心就昏暗了。」（1:21）人类堕落的根源，不是先犯了什么大恶，而是不肯把神当作神来荣耀，不肯感谢他。忘恩是一切罪的起点。

**4. 交换。** 「将不能朽坏之神的荣耀变为偶像」（1:23），「将神的真实变为虚谎，去敬拜事奉受造之物，不敬奉那造物的主」（1:25）。「变为」在这段经文里出现三次（1:23，25，26）。人不是不敬拜，而是把敬拜的对象换了。今天的偶像也许不是木头石头，而是金钱、成功、子女、自我，凡是取代了神位置的，都是偶像。

**5. 「神任凭他们」。** 这句话也出现三次：「神任凭他们逞着心里的情欲」（1:24），「神任凭他们放纵可羞耻的情欲」（1:26），「神就任凭他们存邪僻的心」（1:28）。神的忿怒，有一种显明的方式，就是放手，让人得着他们所选择的。罪的本身，就成了对罪的刑罚。

**6. 一份长长的清单。** 保罗列出人在神任凭之下的种种光景：除了性的混乱（1:26-27），还有「贪婪、恶毒……嫉妒、凶杀、争竞、诡诈、毒恨」，还有「谗毁的、背后说人的……违背父母的……不怜悯人的」（1:29-31）。这份清单把每一个人都包括在内：背后说人、违背父母、不怜悯人，和其他的罪一样，都是人离开神的记号。保罗不是要我们去指责「那些人」，而是要我们看见：这是全人类，也是我们自己的光景。

> 要点：人类最根本的罪，不是某一种特别的恶行，而是知道神，却不荣耀他、不感谢他，把神的荣耀换成了别的东西。神的忿怒是真实的，也正因如此，福音才是真正的好消息。

## 默想

**默想：** 保罗说，人堕落的起点，是「不当作神荣耀他，也不感谢他」。我今天有没有为什么事感谢过神？我是否常常把健康、家人、工作、每天的饭食，都看作理所当然，只在缺少的时候才想起神？忘恩看起来是小事，保罗却说它是心「昏暗」的开始。我心里有没有一样东西，已经悄悄地取代了神的位置：一份我离不开的工作、一个我以他为生命中心的孩子、一个我放不下的目标？这正是我需要福音的原因：神的忿怒是真实的，可是神的义也在福音上显明了，那位从死里复活的神子，为我担当了我所当受的。最后，保罗领受了恩典，就说「我都欠他们的债」。我所白白得着的福音，是否也让我觉得，对身边还没有听见的人，有一份甜美的亏欠？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 has two main parts. **Greeting and longing** (vv. 1–15): Paul introduces himself and his gospel, which concerns God's Son, "descended from David according to the flesh and declared to be the Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by his resurrection from the dead"; he also tells the Roman believers how much he longs to see them. **The theme of the letter** (vv. 16–17): "I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God." **The human condition** (vv. 18–32): "the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men"; people know God but neither honor him nor give him thanks, exchange his glory for idols, and God "gives them up."

The order matters: Paul announces the good news first (vv. 16–17) and only then the bad news (vv. 18–32). Not because the bad news is unimportant, but because only in the light of the gospel can people face their sin without despair.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–7 Concerning His Son

**1. Servant, apostle, set apart.** "Paul, a servant of Christ Jesus, called to be an apostle, set apart for the gospel of God" (1:1). Three words describe him: a "servant" (literally "slave"), belonging to Christ; an "apostle," one sent; "set apart," separated to live for the gospel. "Set apart" is close in root to "Pharisee" ("separated one"): Paul was once set apart for the law, and is now set apart for the gospel.

**2. No new invention.** "Which he promised beforehand through his prophets in the holy Scriptures" (1:2). From the outset Paul makes clear that what he preaches is not a new religion but the fulfillment of Old Testament promise.

**3. The gospel's center is a person.** This gospel is "concerning his Son, who was descended from David according to the flesh and was declared to be the Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by his resurrection from the dead, Jesus Christ our Lord" (1:3–4). The gospel is first of all not a method but a person: he is David's descendant, fulfilling God's promise to David (2 Sam 7:12–16); and he is the Son of God, "declared... in power" by the resurrection. He did not become God's Son at the resurrection; he was publicly declared and enthroned as the Son of God in power.

**4. Grace and mission.** "Through whom we have received grace and apostleship to bring about the obedience of faith for the sake of his name among all the nations" (1:5). True faith produces obedience. The phrase "the obedience of faith" returns at the letter's very end (16:26), framing the whole.

**5. Loved by God, called to be saints.** Paul writes "to all those in Rome who are loved by God and called to be saints" (1:7). In the world's eyes the Roman believers may have been slaves, craftsmen, foreigners; in God's eyes they were "loved by God," "saints." Saints are not a few especially devout people; they are everyone God has called and set apart for himself.

> The point: The center of the gospel is not us but God's Son, Jesus Christ: David's descendant, declared by his resurrection to be the Son of God in power. Whoever believes in him, whoever they are in the world, is a saint "loved by God."

## Going Deeper: 1:8–15 "I Am Under Obligation"

**1. Thanking God for them.** "First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you, because your faith is proclaimed in all the world" (1:8). Paul had never met them, yet he already thanked God for them.

**2. Mutual faith.** He longed to see them "that I may impart to you some spiritual gift to strengthen you," and at once added, "that is, that we may be mutually encouraged by each other's faith, both yours and mine" (1:11–12). The great apostle expected to be encouraged by ordinary believers too.

**3. A debtor.** "I am under obligation both to Greeks and to barbarians, both to the wise and to the foolish. So I am eager to preach the gospel to you also who are in Rome" (1:14–15). Paul owed these people nothing; but having received the gospel from Christ, he felt indebted to everyone who had not yet heard it. Grace that came to him freely became his obligation to others.

## Going Deeper: 1:16–17 The Theme of the Letter

**1. "I am not ashamed."** "For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek" (1:16). To Romans, a crucified man was the height of shame. Yet Paul says this gospel is not weakness but "the power of God." It is not only news about salvation; it is itself God's power to save.

**2. "To the Jew first and also to the Greek."** Salvation is for "everyone who believes," regardless of race; yet God's promises were first given to Israel, so the gospel goes "first" to the Jews. Chapters 9–11 will take up this order in detail.

**3. "The righteousness of God."** "For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith for faith" (1:17). "The righteousness of God" can mean God's own righteousness or the righteousness God gives. In Romans the two belong together: the righteous God, through the gospel, gives his righteousness to those who believe, so that sinners are counted righteous before him (3:21–26). "From faith for faith" means that this righteousness is received by faith from first to last.

**4. "The righteous shall live by faith."** Paul quotes Habakkuk 2:4. In the darkness of the Babylonian invasion the prophet heard God say that the righteous would live by faith. Paul says the word reaches its full meaning in the gospel: a person is justified and receives life before God by faith.

> The point: The gospel is the power of God because it reveals the righteousness of God: God himself is righteous, and he freely gives righteousness to all who believe. Salvation is by faith from beginning to end.

## Going Deeper: 1:18–32 God's Wrath and Humanity's Exchange

**1. The wrath of God.** "For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men, who by their unrighteousness suppress the truth" (1:18). "For": people need God's righteousness because they stand under God's wrath. God's wrath is not an uncontrolled temper but the holy God's settled, just response to sin.

**2. Everyone knows.** "For what can be known about God is plain to them, because God has shown it to them. For his invisible attributes, namely, his eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly perceived, ever since the creation of the world, in the things that have been made. So they are without excuse" (1:19–20). God has revealed himself to all people through creation. The human problem is not ignorance but "suppressing the truth": pushing down what we already know.

**3. The root: no honor, no thanks.** "For although they knew God, they did not honor him as God or give thanks to him, but they became futile in their thinking, and their foolish hearts were darkened" (1:21). The root of humanity's fall is not first some great crime, but refusing to honor God as God and refusing to thank him. Ingratitude is where every sin begins.

**4. The exchange.** They "exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images" (1:23), "exchanged the truth about God for a lie and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator" (1:25). "Exchanged" appears three times in this passage (1:23, 25, 26). People don't stop worshiping; they change what they worship. Today's idols may not be wood and stone but money, success, children, the self: whatever takes God's place is an idol.

**5. "God gave them up."** This phrase also appears three times: "God gave them up in the lusts of their hearts" (1:24), "God gave them up to dishonorable passions" (1:26), "God gave them up to a debased mind" (1:28). One way God's wrath is revealed is by letting go, letting people have what they have chosen. Sin itself becomes the punishment for sin.

**6. A long list.** Paul lists what people become when God gives them up: besides sexual disorder (1:26–27), there is "covetousness, malice... envy, murder, strife, deceit, maliciousness," and "gossips, slanderers... disobedient to parents... ruthless" (1:29–31). The list includes everyone: gossip, disobedience to parents and ruthlessness are, like the other sins, signs of humanity turned from God. Paul isn't inviting us to point at "those people" but to see that this is the condition of the whole human race, including us.

> The point: Humanity's most basic sin is not one particular evil deed but knowing God and yet not honoring or thanking him, exchanging his glory for something else. God's wrath is real, and that is exactly why the gospel is truly good news.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** Paul says humanity's fall began when people "did not honor him as God or give thanks to him." Have I thanked God for anything today? Do I take health, family, work and daily food for granted, and remember God only when something is missing? Ingratitude looks small, but Paul calls it the beginning of a "darkened" heart. Is there something in my heart that has quietly taken God's place: a job I can't do without, a child who has become the center of my life, a goal I can't let go of? This is exactly why I need the gospel: God's wrath is real, but God's righteousness is also revealed in the gospel, and the Son of God raised from the dead has borne for me what I deserved. Finally, having received grace, Paul said, "I am under obligation" to everyone. Does the gospel I received freely leave me with a sweet sense of owing something to those around me who haven't yet heard it?
