## 本章脉络

第一章末了，保罗描写了外邦世界的败坏。读到这里，一个敬虔的犹太人（或者一个自以为有道德的人）很可能会点头：「对，那些人就是这样。」第二章就是对着这样的人说的：「你这论断人的，无论你是谁，也无可推诿。」（2:1）

本章分三段。**论断人的也要受审判**（1-11节）：神必照真理审判，「照各人的行为报应各人」，「因为神不偏待人」。**律法与良心**（12-16节）：有律法的要按律法受审判；没有律法的外邦人，心里也有律法的功用和良心的见证。**犹太人与割礼**（17-29节）：有律法、受割礼，却不遵行律法，并不能叫人在神面前站立得住；「真割礼也是心里的，在乎灵，不在乎仪文」。

第一章证明了外邦人在罪中；第二章证明了犹太人也在罪中。到了3:9，保罗就可以下结论：「犹太人和希腊人都在罪恶之下。」

## 深读 2:1-11 神必照真理审判

**1. 指着别人，定了自己的罪。** 「你在什么事上论断人，就在什么事上定自己的罪；因你这论断人的，自己所行却和别人一样。」（2:1）保罗不是说人不可以分辨是非；他是说，我们用来定别人罪的标准，正好也定了我们自己的罪。我们很会看见别人的骄傲、贪心、苛刻，却看不见自己身上同样的东西。

**2. 藐视恩慈。** 「还是你藐视他丰富的恩慈、宽容、忍耐，不晓得他的恩慈是领你悔改呢？」（2:4）神暂时没有审判，不是因为他不在乎，而是因为他有耐心。可是人常常把神的忍耐误会成神的认可：「我做了这事，也没有怎样啊。」保罗说，神的恩慈是一只伸出来的手，要领人回头；若把它当作可以继续犯罪的许可，就是「藐视」它。

**3. 积蓄忿怒。** 「你竟任着你刚硬不悔改的心，为自己积蓄忿怒，以致神震怒，显他公义审判的日子来到。」（2:5）「积蓄」是存钱的字：一个不悔改的人，每一天都在为自己存下审判。这是一个可怕的反讽：人以为自己在积攒什么，其实积攒的是忿怒。

**4. 照各人的行为报应。** 「他必照各人的行为报应各人。凡恒心行善、寻求荣耀、尊贵和不能朽坏之福的，就以永生报应他们；惟有结党、不顺从真理、反顺从不义的，就以忿怒、恼恨报应他们。」（2:6-8；参诗62:12；箴24:12）这是不是和「因信称义」矛盾？解经家主要有两种理解：

- 有人认为，保罗在这里讲的是一个原则：如果有人真能恒心行善，就会得永生；可是第三章会证明，没有人能做到（3:10-12，20）。所以这段话把人逼到绝境，叫人看见需要另一条路。
- 也有人认为，保罗讲的是真信徒的生活：被称义的人，在圣灵的工作下，会「恒心行善」（参2:29），而这样的生命，是信心真实的凭据，不是得救的根据。

两种理解都同意：审判的标准是行为，而审判是公正的；人得救的根据，却始终是神在基督里的恩典（3:24）。

**5. 神不偏待人。** 「因为神不偏待人。」（2:11）不论是犹太人还是希腊人，有特权的还是没有特权的，在神的审判台前，标准都是一样的。

> 要点：神暂时的忍耐，不是对罪的认可，而是对悔改的邀请。若把他的恩慈当作可以继续犯罪的许可，就是为自己积蓄忿怒。

## 深读 2:12-16 刻在心里的律法

**1. 有律法和没有律法的。** 「凡没有律法犯了罪的，也必不按律法灭亡；凡在律法以下犯了罪的，也必按律法受审判。」（2:12）神按照每个人所领受的亮光审判人。外邦人不会因为没有摩西的律法而被定罪，却会因为违背了他们所知道的而被定罪。

**2. 听和行。** 「原来在神面前，不是听律法的为义，乃是行律法的称义。」（2:13）犹太人每个安息日都在会堂里听律法，就以为自己比外邦人强。保罗说，听本身不能叫人称义。

**3. 良心的见证。** 外邦人「若顺着本性行律法上的事」，就显出「律法的功用刻在他们心里，他们是非之心同作见证，并且他们的思念互相较量，或以为是，或以为非」（2:14-15）。「是非之心」，就是良心。孟子说「是非之心，人皆有之」，看见了人人都有道德意识。圣经却告诉我们它从哪里来：是创造主刻在人心里的。而且保罗的重点和孟子不同：孟子把它看作善性的开端，可以培养；保罗却说，良心「或以为是，或以为非」，它见证的是人违背了自己所知道的，所以无可推诿，需要基督。也有解经家认为，这里的外邦人是信了基督的外邦人，神照着耶31:33把律法写在他们心上（参2:26-29）；不过多数人认为，保罗在这里是说一般的外邦人：他们心里有「律法的功用」，有良心，却仍然违背它。

**4. 审判隐秘事的日子。** 这一切要显明「就在神藉耶稣基督审判人隐秘事的日子」（2:16）。神不只审判人看得见的行为，也审判人心里的隐秘；而审判的主，就是耶稣基督。

> 要点：没有人能说自己不知道。神在每个人心里放了良心；每个人都有标准，每个人也都违背了自己的标准。

## 深读 2:17-29 外面的和里面的

**1. 犹太人的特权。** 保罗列出犹太人引以为荣的事：「你称为犹太人，又倚靠律法，且指着神夸口；既从律法中受了教训，就晓得神的旨意……又深信自己是给瞎子领路的，是黑暗中人的光。」（2:17-19）这些都是真实的特权，第三章开头保罗会再说：「凡事大有好处。」（3:2）问题不在于特权，而在于倚靠特权。

**2. 一连串的问题。** 「你既是教导别人，还不教导自己吗？你讲说人不可偷窃，自己还偷窃吗？你说人不可奸淫，自己还奸淫吗？你厌恶偶像，自己还偷窃庙中之物吗？」（2:21-22）保罗不是说每一个犹太人都在偷窃奸淫，而是说，懂得律法和遵行律法是两回事。

**3. 神的名受亵渎。** 「神的名在外邦人中，因你们受了亵渎，正如经上所记的。」（2:24）保罗引用以赛亚书52:5（参结36:20-23）。神的子民本来要作「外邦人的光」（赛42:6；49:6），结果却叫外邦人轻看神。这句话对今天的教会仍是一个严肃的提醒。

**4. 真割礼在乎心。** 「因为外面作犹太人的，不是真犹太人；外面肉身的割礼，也不是真割礼。惟有里面作的，才是真犹太人；真割礼也是心里的，在乎灵，不在乎仪文。」（2:28-29）旧约早就说过：「耶和华－你神必将你心里和你后裔心里的污秽除掉」（申30:6），原文直译就是「给你的心行割礼」；先知也应许神要赐人新心（耶31:33；结36:26-27）。保罗说，这正是圣灵所做的工作。

**5. 从神来的称赞。** 「这人的称赞不是从人来的，乃是从神来的。」（2:29）「犹太人」一词和「犹大」有关，而「犹大」的意思是「赞美」（创29:35）。保罗也许是在用这个字义：真正的「犹太人」，是得着神称赞的人。

> 要点：宗教的身份、外面的仪式、对圣经的知识，都不能叫人在神面前站立得住。神看的是心，而只有圣灵能改变人的心。

## 默想

**默想：** 读第一章的时候，我是否也像保罗设想的那个人一样，心里暗暗地想：「对，那些人就是这样」？第二章对我说：「你这论断人的……也无可推诿。」我最容易看见别人身上的什么毛病？那些毛病，是不是也正在我自己身上？我有没有把神的忍耐当作理所当然，以为某一样罪「做了也没有怎样」？保罗说，神的恩慈不是放任，而是一只伸出来的手，要领我悔改。我也可能像那些倚靠律法的人：信主多年、熟悉圣经、常常服事，就以为自己比别人强。可是福音告诉我，我在神面前站得住，从来不是因为我的知识或服事，而是因为基督。这样，我今天可以放下论断别人的心，不是先去纠正别人，而是先让神的恩慈领我自己回到他面前。

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 1 Paul described the corruption of the Gentile world. A devout Jew (or anyone who thinks of themselves as moral) would likely nod along: "Yes, that's what those people are like." Chapter 2 is addressed to that person: "Therefore you have no excuse, O man, every one of you who judges" (2:1).

The chapter has three parts. **The judge will be judged too** (vv. 1–11): God judges according to truth, "he will render to each one according to his works," "for God shows no partiality." **Law and conscience** (vv. 12–16): those who have the law will be judged by the law; Gentiles without the law still have the work of the law and the witness of conscience in their hearts. **The Jew and circumcision** (vv. 17–29): having the law and being circumcised, without keeping the law, cannot make anyone stand before God; "circumcision is a matter of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter."

Chapter 1 showed that Gentiles are under sin; chapter 2 shows that Jews are under sin as well. By 3:9 Paul can conclude: "all, both Jews and Greeks, are under the power of sin."

## Going Deeper: 2:1–11 God Judges According to Truth

**1. Pointing at others, condemning ourselves.** "For in passing judgment on another you condemn yourself, because you, the judge, practice the very same things" (2:1). Paul isn't saying we may not tell right from wrong; he's saying that the standard we use to condemn others condemns us too. We are quick to see others' pride, greed and harshness, and slow to see the same things in ourselves.

**2. Presuming on kindness.** "Or do you presume on the riches of his kindness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that God's kindness is meant to lead you to repentance?" (2:4). God hasn't judged yet, not because he doesn't care, but because he is patient. Yet people often mistake God's patience for God's approval: "I did that, and nothing happened." Paul says God's kindness is an outstretched hand to lead us back; to treat it as permission to keep sinning is to "presume on" it.

**3. Storing up wrath.** "But because of your hard and impenitent heart you are storing up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath when God's righteous judgment will be revealed" (2:5). "Storing up" is a word for saving money: an unrepentant person is depositing judgment for themselves every day. It is a dreadful irony: people think they are accumulating something, and what they accumulate is wrath.

**4. According to works.** "He will render to each one according to his works: to those who by patience in well-doing seek for glory and honor and immortality, he will give eternal life; but for those who are self-seeking and do not obey the truth, but obey unrighteousness, there will be wrath and fury" (2:6–8; cf. Ps 62:12; Prov 24:12). Does this contradict justification by faith? Interpreters mainly take it in one of two ways:

- Some read it as a principle: if anyone truly persevered in doing good, they would receive eternal life; but chapter 3 will show that no one does (3:10–12, 20). The passage drives us to the end of ourselves, so that we see the need for another way.
- Others read it as describing the life of true believers: those who are justified will, by the Spirit's work, persevere "in well-doing" (cf. 2:29), and such a life is the evidence that faith is real, not the ground of salvation.

Both agree that the standard of judgment is works, and that the judgment is just; yet the ground of salvation is always God's grace in Christ (3:24).

**5. No partiality.** "For God shows no partiality" (2:11). Jew or Greek, privileged or not, the standard at God's judgment seat is the same.

> The point: God's present patience is not approval of sin but an invitation to repent. To treat his kindness as permission to keep sinning is to store up wrath for ourselves.

## Going Deeper: 2:12–16 The Law Written on the Heart

**1. With and without the law.** "For all who have sinned without the law will also perish without the law, and all who have sinned under the law will be judged by the law" (2:12). God judges each person by the light they have received. Gentiles will not be condemned for lacking the law of Moses, but for going against what they knew.

**2. Hearing and doing.** "For it is not the hearers of the law who are righteous before God, but the doers of the law who will be justified" (2:13). Jews heard the law read in the synagogue every Sabbath and assumed that made them better than the Gentiles. Paul says hearing by itself justifies no one.

**3. The witness of conscience.** When Gentiles "by nature do what the law requires," they "show that the work of the law is written on their hearts, while their conscience also bears witness, and their conflicting thoughts accuse or even excuse them" (2:14–15). Mencius observed that "the sense of right and wrong is common to all people." Scripture tells us where it comes from: the Creator wrote it on the heart. But Paul's point differs from Mencius's. Mencius saw it as the sprout of a good nature to be cultivated; Paul says conscience "accuses or even excuses," bearing witness that we have broken what we know, so that we are without excuse and need Christ. Some interpreters take these Gentiles to be Gentile Christians, on whose hearts God has written his law as promised in Jer 31:33 (cf. 2:26–29). Most, however, think Paul means Gentiles generally: they have "the work of the law" and a conscience, yet still break it.

**4. The day when secrets are judged.** All this will come to light "on that day when, according to my gospel, God judges the secrets of men by Christ Jesus" (2:16). God judges not only visible deeds but the secrets of the heart; and the judge is Jesus Christ.

> The point: No one can say they didn't know. God has put a conscience in every heart; everyone has a standard, and everyone has broken it.

## Going Deeper: 2:17–29 Outward and Inward

**1. Jewish privilege.** Paul lists what Jews were proud of: "But if you call yourself a Jew and rely on the law and boast in God and know his will... and if you are sure that you yourself are a guide to the blind, a light to those who are in darkness" (2:17–19). These were real privileges; at the start of chapter 3 Paul will say, "Much in every way" (3:2). The problem isn't the privilege but relying on it.

**2. A string of questions.** "You then who teach others, do you not teach yourself? While you preach against stealing, do you steal? You who say that one must not commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who abhor idols, do you rob temples?" (2:21–22). Paul isn't saying every Jew stole or committed adultery, but that knowing the law and keeping it are two different things.

**3. God's name blasphemed.** "For, as it is written, 'The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you'" (2:24). Paul quotes Isaiah 52:5 (cf. Ezek 36:20–23). God's people were meant to be "a light for the nations" (Isa 42:6; 49:6), and instead they gave the nations reason to despise God. The sentence remains a sobering warning to the church today.

**4. True circumcision is of the heart.** "For no one is a Jew who is merely one outwardly, nor is circumcision outward and physical. But a Jew is one inwardly, and circumcision is a matter of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter" (2:28–29). The Old Testament had said so long before: "the LORD your God will circumcise your heart and the heart of your offspring" (Deut 30:6); and the prophets promised that God would give a new heart (Jer 31:33; Ezek 36:26–27). Paul says this is exactly the Spirit's work.

**5. Praise from God.** "His praise is not from man but from God" (2:29). The word "Jew" comes from "Judah," whose name is tied to "praise" (Gen 29:35). Paul may be playing on it: the true "Jew" is the one who receives praise from God.

> The point: Religious identity, outward ritual and knowledge of Scripture cannot make anyone stand before God. God looks at the heart, and only the Spirit can change the heart.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** When I read chapter 1, did I, like the person Paul imagines, quietly think, "Yes, that's what those people are like"? Chapter 2 says to me: "You have no excuse, O man, every one of you who judges." What faults am I quickest to see in others? Are those same faults in me? Have I taken God's patience for granted, thinking some sin "doesn't really matter, since nothing happened"? Paul says God's kindness is not indulgence but an outstretched hand to lead me to repentance. I may also be like those who relied on the law: a believer for years, at home in the Bible, busy serving, and so quietly sure I'm better than others. But the gospel tells me I have never stood before God because of my knowledge or service, but only because of Christ. So today I can lay down my judging spirit: not first by correcting others, but by letting God's kindness lead me back to him.
