## 本章脉络

第三章是罗马书的转折点。**几个反对的问题**（1-8节）：如果犹太人也在罪中，那么作犹太人还有什么好处？人的不信会不会废掉神的信实？保罗一一回答。**普世都在罪下**（9-20节）：保罗串连了几处旧约经文，作出结论：「没有义人，连一个也没有」，「好塞住各人的口，叫普世的人都伏在神审判之下」。**但如今**（21-26节）：在人类最黑暗的光景中，神的义「在律法以外已经显明出来」：人「蒙神的恩典，因基督耶稣的救赎，就白白地称义」。**没有可夸的了**（27-31节）：既然称义是因着信，人就没有任何可以夸口的地方；神既是一位，他就照同一个方法称犹太人和外邦人为义。

马丁‧路德在他德文圣经的旁注中，称3:21-26为「全书和全本圣经的中心」。从1:18一路下来的黑暗，到这里迎来了光。

## 深读 3:1-8 神的信实不会落空

**1. 犹太人有什么长处？** 「凡事大有好处：第一是神的圣言交托他们。」（3:2）以色列真实的特权，是领受了神的话。圣经不是犹太人自己写的宗教文学，而是神「交托」给他们的「圣言」。

**2. 人的不信与神的信实。** 「即便有不信的，这有何妨呢？难道他们的不信就废掉神的信吗？断乎不能！不如说，神是真实的，人都是虚谎的。」（3:3-4）这里的「神的信」，指的是神的信实。人可能失信，神却不会背弃他的应许。这个问题，保罗在第九至十一章会详细展开。

**3. 一个荒谬的推论。** 有人说：如果我的罪使神的义更加显明，那我犯罪岂不是在荣耀神？「为什么不说，我们可以作恶以成善呢？」（3:8）保罗说，有人就是这样毁谤他所传的福音。他的回答很简短：「这等人定罪是该当的。」这个问题第六章还会再回来（6:1）。

> 要点：人的失信不能废掉神的信实。神的话交托给了人，人却可能不信；可是神永远是真实的。

## 深读 3:9-20 没有义人，连一个也没有

**1. 结论。** 「犹太人和希腊人都在罪恶之下。」（3:9）「在罪恶之下」，就像在一个主人的权势之下（ESV译作「在罪的权势之下」）。罪不只是一些错误的行为，而是一种辖制人的权势。

**2. 一串旧约经文。** 保罗把诗篇和以赛亚书的经文串在一起（主要出自诗14:1-3；5:9；140:3；10:7；赛59:7-8；诗36:1）：「没有义人，连一个也没有。没有明白的；没有寻求神的；都是偏离正路，一同变为无用。」（3:10-12）接着他从人的喉咙、舌头、嘴唇、口、脚一直讲到眼睛（3:13-18）：罪进入了人的每一部分。最后一句是总结：「他们眼中不怕神。」（3:18）一切罪的根源，就是不把神放在眼中。

**3. 「没有寻求神的」。** 人常常说自己在「寻找神」，保罗却说，按人的本性，「没有寻求神的」（3:11）。若不是神先来寻找人，人永远不会真心寻求他。这样看来，一个人愿意寻求神，本身就是恩典的记号。

**4. 每一张口都被塞住。** 「我们晓得律法上的话都是对律法以下之人说的，好塞住各人的口，叫普世的人都伏在神审判之下。」（3:19）在神的审判台前，没有人能为自己辩护。这个画面，就像法庭上被告无话可说。

**5. 律法的功用。** 「所以凡有血气的，没有一个因行律法能在神面前称义，因为律法本是叫人知罪。」（3:20）律法像一面镜子，能照出脸上的污垢，却不能洗净它。律法是好的（7:12），但它的功用是叫人知罪，而不是叫人称义。

> 要点：罪不只是一些行为，而是一种辖制全人类的权势。律法能让我们看见自己的罪，却不能救我们；在神面前，每一张口都被塞住了。

## 深读 3:21-26 但如今

**1. 「但如今」。** 「但如今，神的义在律法以外已经显明出来，有律法和先知为证。」（3:21）这是全本圣经最重要的转折词之一。在「没有义人，连一个也没有」之后，神做了一件新的事。可是这件事并不是新发明的，「有律法和先知为证」。

**2. 因信加给人的义。** 这义「就是神的义，因信耶稣基督加给一切相信的人，并没有分别」（3:22）。「信耶稣基督」原文也可以理解为「耶稣基督的信实」，有些学者认为这是指基督顺服至死的信实。不过多数福音派解经家仍然认为这里是指人对基督的信，因为保罗在加2:16明说「我们也信了基督耶稣」，第四章又以亚伯拉罕的信为例；这里重复「相信」，是在强调信心是唯一的途径。不论哪一种理解，称义的根据都在基督身上，而不在人身上。

**3. 「并没有分别」。** 「因为世人都犯了罪，亏缺了神的荣耀。」（3:23）在罪的事上，犹太人和外邦人「并没有分别」；在恩典的事上，也「并没有分别」（参10:12）。「亏缺」是现在时态：人不只是从前犯了罪，现在仍然达不到神荣耀的标准。人本来是照神的形像造的，要反映他的荣耀，罪却叫人亏缺了这荣耀。

**4. 三个画面。** 保罗用三个词来描写神在基督里所做的事：

- **称义**（法庭的用语）：「如今却蒙神的恩典……就白白地称义」（3:24）。称义不是神使人变得公义，而是神宣告人是义的（神改变人的生命，是第六至八章要讲的成圣）。「白白地」，就是不需要付代价，因为代价已经有人付了。
- **救赎**（出埃及与奴隶市场的用语）：「因基督耶稣的救赎」（3:24）。旧约里，神「救赎」以色列脱离埃及的奴役（出6:6；申7:8）。救赎是付出赎价，买回为奴的人，使他得自由；在这里，赎价就是基督的血（3:25）。
- **挽回祭**（圣殿的用语）：「神设立耶稣作挽回祭，是凭着耶稣的血」（3:25）。这个词在七十士译本里用来指约柜上的「施恩座」，就是赎罪日大祭司洒血的地方（利16:14-15）。「挽回祭」的意思是：神的忿怒因着一个祭物被平息了，罪也被遮盖了；保罗前面讲过神的忿怒（1:18；2:5），所以这里的重点正是平息忿怒。不同的是，在异教中是人献祭来安抚神；在这里，是神自己「设立」耶稣作挽回祭。神亲自预备了平息他自己忿怒的祭物。

**5. 神既是公义的，又称人为义。** 为什么神要这样做？「要显明神的义；因为他用忍耐的心宽容人先时所犯的罪」（3:25）。神在过去没有立刻审判人的罪，这看起来好像他不公义；可是在十字架上，神显明他从来没有轻看罪。结果是：「使人知道他自己为义，也称信耶稣的人为义。」（3:26）这是全书最深的奥秘之一：神没有为了赦免罪人而放下他的公义，也没有为了维护公义而毁灭罪人。在十字架上，公义与慈爱同时完全显明。

> 要点：在「没有义人」之后，是「但如今」。神设立耶稣作挽回祭，在十字架上同时显明了他的公义和他的恩典：他自己是义的，也称信耶稣的人为义。

## 深读 3:27-31 没有可夸的了

**1. 夸口被排除了。** 「既是这样，哪里能夸口呢？没有可夸的了。」（3:27）如果称义是靠行为，人就可以夸口；可是称义既是因着信，信就只是空着手领受，没有什么可以夸口。

**2. 不在乎遵行律法。** 「所以我们看定了：人称义是因着信，不在乎遵行律法。」（3:28）原文没有「只」字，路德在1522年的德文译本中加上了这个字（「只因着信」），引起很大争议；他后来解释，这是为了把保罗的意思用德文说清楚。从上下文看（3:27排除夸口，3:28「不在乎遵行律法」），这确实是保罗的意思；路德以前，也有教父用过「单单因信」的说法。称义不是信心加上行为，而是单单因着信；可是叫人称义的信心从来不是孤单的（参加5:6）。

**3. 神只有一位。** 「难道神只作犹太人的神吗？不也是作外邦人的神吗？是的，也作外邦人的神。神既是一位，他就要因信称那受割礼的为义，也要因信称那未受割礼的为义。」（3:29-30）保罗引用了犹太人每天背诵的信仰宣告（申6:4「耶和华－我们神是独一的主」），推出一个他们可能没有想到的结论：既然神只有一位，他就是所有人的神，也就只有一条得救的路。

**4. 更是坚固律法。** 「这样，我们因信废了律法吗？断乎不是！更是坚固律法。」（3:31）因信称义不是轻看律法；正好相反，它让律法得着应有的地位：律法所要求的义，在基督身上得着成全（参8:4），律法本身也为这福音作见证（3:21）。第四章保罗就要用律法书中的亚伯拉罕来证明这一点。

> 要点：恩典排除了一切夸口。称义是单单因着信，所以没有人可以看不起别人，也没有人可以在神面前抬起头来，除非是因为基督。

## 默想

**默想：** 「没有义人，连一个也没有」，这句话里也包括我。我是否仍然在心里为自己辩护：「我虽然有些毛病，可是我没有那么坏」？第三章告诉我，在神面前，我的口要被塞住，不是为了羞辱我，而是为了让我准备好听那两个字：「但如今」。我靠着什么相信神接纳我：我的读经、我的服事、我比别人好一点？还是单单因为「神设立耶稣作挽回祭」？在十字架上，神没有轻看我的罪，却也没有拒绝我；耶稣替我担当了我所当受的忿怒。若是这样，我还有什么可夸的呢？我今天能不能放下在神面前和在人面前的一切自我辩护，空着手站在他面前，单单因信领受他白白赐下的义？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 is the turning point of Romans. **Some objections** (vv. 1–8): if Jews too are under sin, what advantage is there in being a Jew? Does human unfaithfulness cancel God's faithfulness? Paul answers each in turn. **The whole world under sin** (vv. 9–20): Paul strings together several Old Testament texts and concludes, "None is righteous, no, not one," "so that every mouth may be stopped, and the whole world may be held accountable to God." **But now** (vv. 21–26): into humanity's darkest condition, God's righteousness "has been manifested apart from the law": people "are justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus." **Boasting excluded** (vv. 27–31): since justification is by faith, no one has anything to boast of; and since God is one, he justifies Jew and Gentile in the same way.

In a marginal note in his German Bible, Martin Luther called 3:21–26 "the chief point, and the very central place of the Epistle, and of the whole Bible." The darkness that began in 1:18 now meets the light.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–8 God's Faithfulness Does Not Fail

**1. What advantage has the Jew?** "Much in every way. To begin with, the Jews were entrusted with the oracles of God" (3:2). Israel's real privilege was receiving God's word. Scripture is not Jewish religious literature they wrote for themselves, but "the oracles of God" "entrusted" to them.

**2. Human faithlessness and God's faithfulness.** "What if some were unfaithful? Does their faithlessness nullify the faithfulness of God? By no means! Let God be true though every one were a liar" (3:3–4). People may break faith, but God will not abandon his promises. Paul will develop this question at length in chapters 9–11.

**3. An absurd inference.** Someone says: if my sin makes God's righteousness shine more brightly, isn't my sinning a way of glorifying God? "And why not do evil that good may come?" (3:8). Paul says some were slandering his gospel in exactly these terms. His answer is brief: "Their condemnation is just." The question returns in chapter 6 (6:1).

> The point: Human faithlessness cannot cancel God's faithfulness. God entrusted his word to people, and people may disbelieve; but God remains true forever.

## Going Deeper: 3:9–20 None Is Righteous, No, Not One

**1. The verdict.** "All, both Jews and Greeks, are under the power of sin" (3:9). "Under sin" means under a master's rule. Sin is not only a set of wrong deeds; it is a power that enslaves.

**2. A chain of Old Testament texts.** Paul strings together passages from the Psalms and Isaiah (mainly Ps 14:1–3; 5:9; 140:3; 10:7; Isa 59:7–8; Ps 36:1): "None is righteous, no, not one; no one understands; no one seeks for God. All have turned aside; together they have become worthless" (3:10–12). He then moves from throat, tongue, lips and mouth to feet and eyes (3:13–18): sin has entered every part of a person. The last line sums it up: "There is no fear of God before their eyes" (3:18). The root of all sin is not keeping God in view.

**3. "No one seeks for God."** People often say they are "searching for God," yet Paul says that by nature "no one seeks for God" (3:11). If God did not first seek us, we would never truly seek him. Seen this way, a person's desire to seek God is itself a sign of grace.

**4. Every mouth stopped.** "Now we know that whatever the law says it speaks to those who are under the law, so that every mouth may be stopped, and the whole world may be held accountable to God" (3:19). Before God's judgment seat no one can defend themselves. The picture is a courtroom where the accused has nothing left to say.

**5. What the law does.** "For by works of the law no human being will be justified in his sight, since through the law comes knowledge of sin" (3:20). The law is like a mirror: it shows the dirt on your face but cannot wash it off. The law is good (7:12), but its role is to make sin known, not to justify.

> The point: Sin is not only a set of deeds but a power that rules the whole human race. The law can show us our sin but cannot save us; before God, every mouth is stopped.

## Going Deeper: 3:21–26 But Now

**1. "But now."** "But now the righteousness of God has been manifested apart from the law, although the Law and the Prophets bear witness to it" (3:21). These are some of the most important words in the Bible. After "none is righteous, no, not one," God has done something new. Yet it is no novelty: "the Law and the Prophets bear witness to it."

**2. Righteousness given through faith.** It is "the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe. For there is no distinction" (3:22). "Faith in Jesus Christ" could also be translated "the faithfulness of Jesus Christ," and some scholars take it to mean Christ's faithful obedience unto death. Most evangelical interpreters still take it as our faith in Christ, since in Gal 2:16 Paul says plainly, "we also have believed in Christ Jesus," and chapter 4 builds on Abraham's faith; the repetition here underlines that faith is the only way. Either way, the ground of justification lies in Christ, not in us.

**3. "No distinction."** "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (3:23). In sin there is "no distinction" between Jew and Gentile; in grace too there is "no distinction" (cf. 10:12). "Fall short" is in the present tense: people not only sinned in the past but still fall short of God's glory now. Made in God's image to reflect his glory, humanity has fallen short of it through sin.

**4. Three pictures.** Paul uses three words to describe what God has done in Christ:

- **Justified** (a courtroom word): "and are justified by his grace as a gift" (3:24). Justification is not God making a person righteous but God declaring a person righteous (God's transforming work in the believer is sanctification, which chs. 6–8 take up). "As a gift" means free of charge, because someone else has paid.
- **Redemption** (a word from the exodus and the slave market): "through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (3:24). In the Old Testament God "redeemed" Israel from slavery in Egypt (Exod 6:6; Deut 7:8). Redemption means paying a price to buy back a slave and set them free; here the price is Christ's blood (3:25).
- **Propitiation** (a temple word): "whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood" (3:25). In the Septuagint this word was used for the "mercy seat" on the ark, where the high priest sprinkled blood on the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:14–15). "Propitiation" means that God's wrath is turned aside by a sacrifice, and sin is covered; since Paul has already spoken of God's wrath (1:18; 2:5), turning aside wrath is the point here. The difference is this: in pagan religion people offer sacrifices to appease the gods; here it is God himself who "put forward" Jesus as the propitiation. God himself provided the sacrifice that satisfies his own wrath.

**5. God both just and justifier.** Why did God do this? "This was to show God's righteousness, because in his divine forbearance he had passed over former sins" (3:25). God had not judged sins immediately in the past, which could make him look unjust; but at the cross God showed that he had never taken sin lightly. The result: "so that he might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (3:26). This is one of the deepest mysteries of the letter: God did not set aside his justice to forgive sinners, nor destroy sinners to uphold his justice. At the cross justice and love are both fully displayed.

> The point: After "none is righteous" comes "but now." God put forward Jesus as a propitiation, displaying at the cross both his justice and his grace: he is just, and he justifies the one who has faith in Jesus.

## Going Deeper: 3:27–31 Boasting Excluded

**1. Boasting is shut out.** "Then what becomes of our boasting? It is excluded" (3:27). If justification were by works, people could boast; but since it is by faith, and faith simply receives with empty hands, there is nothing to boast of.

**2. Apart from works of the law.** "For we hold that one is justified by faith apart from works of the law" (3:28). The word "alone" is not in the Greek. Luther added it in his 1522 German New Testament ("by faith alone"), which caused great controversy; he later explained that German needed it to convey Paul's sense. The context (boasting excluded in 3:27; "apart from works of the law" in 3:28) shows that this is indeed Paul's meaning, and some church fathers before Luther had used "faith alone" in the same way. Justification is not faith plus works but by faith alone; yet justifying faith is never alone (cf. Gal 5:6).

**3. God is one.** "Or is God the God of Jews only? Is he not the God of Gentiles also? Yes, of Gentiles also, since God is one. He will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith" (3:29–30). Paul cites the confession Jews recited daily (Deut 6:4, "The LORD our God, the LORD is one") and draws a conclusion they may not have expected: since there is only one God, he is the God of all, and there is only one way of salvation.

**4. Upholding the law.** "Do we then overthrow the law by this faith? By no means! On the contrary, we uphold the law" (3:31). Justification by faith doesn't belittle the law; on the contrary, it gives the law its proper place: the righteousness the law requires is fulfilled in Christ (cf. 8:4), and the law itself bears witness to this gospel (3:21). In chapter 4 Paul will prove this from Abraham in the books of the law.

> The point: Grace excludes all boasting. Justification is by faith alone, so no one may look down on anyone else, and no one may lift their head before God, except because of Christ.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "None is righteous, no, not one": that includes me. Am I still defending myself in my heart: "I have my faults, but I'm not that bad"? Chapter 3 tells me that before God my mouth must be stopped, not to humiliate me, but to get me ready to hear two words: "But now." What am I relying on to believe that God accepts me: my Bible reading, my service, being a little better than others? Or simply that "God put forward Jesus as a propitiation"? At the cross God did not take my sin lightly, and yet he did not reject me; Jesus bore the wrath I deserved. If so, what do I have to boast of? Can I lay down, today, all my self-defense before God and before people, and stand before him with empty hands, receiving by faith alone the righteousness he freely gives?
