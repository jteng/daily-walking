## 本章脉络

5:20说：「罪在哪里显多，恩典就更显多了。」这句话很容易被人误用：既然这样，我们多犯罪，岂不是叫恩典更多吗？第六章就是回答这个问题。保罗两次提出类似的问题，两次都回答「断乎不可」。

**第一个问题：可以仍在罪中吗？**（1-14节）保罗的回答不是「你应该更努力」，而是「你已经和基督联合了」：我们受洗归入基督的死，旧人和他同钉十字架，所以我们「在罪上死了」；我们现在就要过新生的生活，将来也要与他一同复活。所以：「你们向罪也当看自己是死的；向神在基督耶稣里，却当看自己是活的。」**第二个问题：既在恩典之下，就可以犯罪吗？**（15-23节）保罗用主人和奴仆的比喻：每个人都是某一位主人的奴仆，不是罪的奴仆，就是义（神）的奴仆。最后是那句著名的话：「罪的工价乃是死；惟有神的恩赐，在我们的主基督耶稣里，乃是永生。」

第三至五章主要讲称义和称义的果效：神宣告我们为义。第六章开始讲成圣：被称义的人怎样活出新的生命。两者不能分开，也不能混淆。

## 深读 6:1-4 受洗归入他的死

**1. 一个合理的问题。** 「这样，怎么说呢？我们可以仍在罪中、叫恩典显多吗？」（6:1）如果有人从来没有被这个问题困扰过，也许是因为他还没有真正听明白恩典。保罗所传的恩典是如此白白、如此彻底，以致人会以为它鼓励犯罪（参3:8）。

**2. 「在罪上死了」。** 「断乎不可！我们在罪上死了的人岂可仍在罪中活着呢？」（6:2）保罗不是说信徒不会再犯罪，也不是说罪的试探对信徒已经没有吸引力。他说的是一个身份的改变：罪曾经是我们的主人，可是我们和那个主人之间的关系已经因着死而结束了。

**3. 受洗归入基督。** 「岂不知我们这受洗归入基督耶稣的人是受洗归入他的死吗？所以，我们藉着洗礼归入死，和他一同埋葬，原是叫我们一举一动有新生的样式，像基督藉着父的荣耀从死里复活一样。」（6:3-4）在初期教会，洗礼和信主是紧紧连在一起的（参徒2:38，41）。保罗用洗礼来说明一个属灵的事实：信徒和基督「联合」了，他的死成了我们的死，他的复活也成了我们新生命的源头。洗礼不是离开信心就自动生效的仪式（参4:11；西2:12「因信」）；可是保罗能说我们是「藉着洗礼」与基督同埋葬，因为洗礼是神所设立的记号和印证，标明信的人已经与基督联合。叫人得救的是基督，是藉着信与他联合，而洗礼正是这联合可见的印记。

> 要点：恩典不是犯罪的许可证。信徒已经和基督联合，与他同死、同埋葬，所以我们和罪之间的旧关系已经结束；我们要过的，是复活的新生命。

## 深读 6:5-11 看自己是死的，也是活的

**1. 联合。** 「我们若在他死的形状上与他联合，也要在他复活的形状上与他联合。」（6:5）「联合」一词原文有「一同生长」「长在一起」的意思，表示一种活的、紧密的联合。「与基督联合」是保罗神学的核心之一：称义、成圣、得荣耀，都是在基督里得着的。

**2. 旧人同钉十字架。** 「因为知道我们的旧人和他同钉十字架，使罪身灭绝，叫我们不再作罪的奴仆。」（6:6）「旧人」不是指我们里面「旧的那一部分」，而是指我们在亚当里的那个旧身份（参5:12-21）。那个人已经死了。「罪身灭绝」的「灭绝」，原文的意思是「失去效力」，不是说我们的身体被毁灭了，而是说罪不能再藉着我们的身体辖制我们。

**3. 「只有一次」。** 「他死是向罪死了，只有一次；他活是向神活着。」（6:10）基督「向罪死」不是因为他自己有罪，而是他为我们担当了罪的刑罚，进入罪和死的权势之下，并且一次永远地胜过了它（8:3；林后5:21）。他的死是一次而永远有效的。他已经彻底解决了罪的问题，死不能再作他的主。我们与他联合，这也成了我们的事实。

**4. 「当看自己」。** 「这样，你们向罪也当看自己是死的；向神在基督耶稣里，却当看自己是活的。」（6:11）这是本章第一个命令。「看」（*logizomai*）就是第四章「算为」的那个字。神已经把基督的义算给我们；现在，我们也要照着神所说的来「算」自己。这不是自我暗示，不是假装自己不再有罪的倾向，而是相信神所宣告的事实，并且按着这个事实来生活。

> 要点：成圣不是从我们的努力开始，而是从相信一个事实开始：在基督里，我们向罪已经是死的，向神是活的。我们要照着神所说的来「看」自己。

## 深读 6:12-14 不要容罪作王

**1. 从直述到命令。** 「所以，不要容罪在你们必死的身上作王，使你们顺从身子的私欲。」（6:12）保罗先讲「你们是什么」（6:1-11），然后才讲「所以你们要怎样」。这是整本罗马书，也是整本新约伦理的顺序：命令建立在恩典的事实上。神不是说「你要成为新人」，而是说「你已经是新人了，所以要活出来」。

**2. 献上。** 「也不要将你们的肢体献给罪作不义的器具；倒要像从死里复活的人，将自己献给神，并将肢体作义的器具献给神。」（6:13）「器具」原文也可以译作「兵器」。我们的眼睛、舌头、手、脚，每天都在为某一方作战。成圣是很具体的：我的舌头今天要说什么？我的眼睛今天要看什么？

**3. 不在律法之下，乃在恩典之下。** 「罪必不能作你们的主；因你们不在律法之下，乃在恩典之下。」（6:14）这是一个应许，不只是命令。为什么罪不能再作主？不是因为律法更严厉了，而是因为恩典掌权了。律法能指出罪，却不能胜过罪；恩典却能。「不在律法之下」不是说信徒可以不理会神的旨意（6:15；8:4），而是说我们不再活在摩西律法那个旧的统治之下：它吩咐人，也定人的罪，却不能赐人生命（7:5-6；8:3）。如今我们在恩典的统治之下，恩典藉着基督和圣灵，真正改变人。

> 要点：神的命令建立在恩典的事实上：你们已经是新人，所以要活出新人的样子。罪不能再作主，不是因为律法更严，而是因为我们在恩典之下。

## 深读 6:15-23 两种奴仆

**1. 没有中立的地带。** 「岂不晓得你们献上自己作奴仆，顺从谁，就作谁的奴仆吗？或作罪的奴仆，以至于死；或作顺命的奴仆，以至成义。」（6:16）现代人以为自由就是不受任何人的约束。保罗说，没有人是完全「自主」的：每个人都在服侍某一位主人。问题不是要不要作奴仆，而是作谁的奴仆。

**2. 从心里顺服。** 「感谢神！因为你们从前虽然作罪的奴仆，现今却从心里顺服了所传给你们道理的模范。」（6:17）保罗为他们的改变「感谢神」，因为这是神的工作。「模范」原文就是5:14「预像」的那个字，可能是指一个模子：福音像一个模子，把信徒塑造成它的样式。和合本译作「所传给你们」，原文直译却是「你们被交付给它」：不只是教训交给了他们，更是他们被交给了这教训，由它来塑造。这是神亲自的作为，正与1:24-28神「任凭」（原文是「交给」）人相反。

**3. 「照人的常话」。** 保罗承认，用「奴仆」来形容与神的关系，并不完全恰当（6:19）。作神的奴仆，其实是真正的自由；而作罪的奴仆，看起来自由（「不被义约束」，6:20），结局却是死。

**4. 果子与结局。** 「你们现今所看为羞耻的事，当日有什么果子呢？那些事的结局就是死。但现今，你们既从罪里得了释放，作了神的奴仆，就有成圣的果子，那结局就是永生。」（6:21-22）罪应许我们快乐，给我们的却是羞耻和死亡。

**5. 工价与恩赐。** 「因为罪的工价乃是死；惟有神的恩赐，在我们的主基督耶稣里，乃是永生。」（6:23）「工价」原文常指士兵的粮饷，也泛指工钱，是挣来的、应得的；「恩赐」是白白的礼物。罪是一个付工钱的主人，而它付的工钱就是死。神却不是付工钱，而是白白赐给我们永生。即使在讲成圣的这一章，保罗最后一句话仍然是恩典：永生不是成圣的工价，而是神的恩赐。

> 要点：每个人都是某一位主人的奴仆。罪付给我们的工钱是死；神却白白地把永生赐给我们。作神的奴仆，才是真正的自由。

## 默想

**默想：** 「你们向罪也当看自己是死的；向神在基督耶稣里，却当看自己是活的。」当我又一次在同一个罪上跌倒时，我心里怎样「看」自己？我是否说：「我就是这样的人，改不了」？保罗说，那不是神眼中的我：在基督里，我和罪的旧关系已经因着死结束了。今天，有没有一个具体的「肢体」，我需要重新把它献给神：我的舌头，不再用来抱怨、批评或说谎；我的眼睛，不再停留在不洁的画面上；我的手机和时间，不再被我拿来逃避神？这不是靠意志力咬紧牙关，而是相信基督的死和复活真的改变了我，然后按着这个事实迈出一步。罪今天仍会对我说它能给我什么，可是我知道它的工价是死；而神已经白白地把永生赐给我了。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Romans 5:20 said, "where sin increased, grace abounded all the more." That sentence is easily abused: if so, shouldn't we sin more, so that there is more grace? Chapter 6 answers that question. Paul raises a similar question twice, and twice answers, "By no means!"

**First question: may we continue in sin?** (vv. 1–14). Paul's answer is not "you should try harder" but "you have been united with Christ": we were baptized into his death, our old self was crucified with him, so we "died to sin"; so we now walk in newness of life, and shall share in his resurrection. Therefore: "consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus." **Second question: since we are under grace, may we sin?** (vv. 15–23). Paul uses the picture of masters and slaves: everyone is a slave of some master, either of sin or of righteousness (God). The chapter ends with the famous words: "For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord."

Chapters 3–5 have dealt mainly with justification and its fruits: God declares us righteous. Chapter 6 begins to deal with sanctification: how justified people live out the new life. The two must never be separated, nor confused.

## Going Deeper: 6:1–4 Baptized into His Death

**1. A reasonable question.** "What shall we say then? Are we to continue in sin that grace may abound?" (6:1). If someone has never been troubled by this question, perhaps they haven't really heard grace yet. The grace Paul preached was so free and so complete that people thought it encouraged sin (cf. 3:8).

**2. "We died to sin."** "By no means! How can we who died to sin still live in it?" (6:2). Paul isn't saying believers no longer sin, or that sin's temptations no longer attract them. He is speaking of a change of identity: sin was once our master, but our relationship to that master ended in a death.

**3. Baptized into Christ.** "Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death, in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life" (6:3–4). In the early church, baptism and coming to faith were closely bound together (cf. Acts 2:38, 41). Paul uses baptism to explain a spiritual reality: believers are "united" with Christ; his death became our death, and his resurrection the source of our new life. Baptism does not work automatically apart from faith (cf. 4:11; Col 2:12, "through faith"); yet Paul can say we were buried with Christ "by baptism," because baptism is God's appointed sign and seal of the union believers have with him. It is Christ who saves, through faith-union with him, and baptism is the visible mark of that union.

> The point: Grace is not a license to sin. Believers have been united with Christ, dying and buried with him, so our old relationship to sin is over; the life we now live is resurrection life.

## Going Deeper: 6:5–11 Consider Yourselves Dead, and Alive

**1. Union.** "For if we have been united with him in a death like his, we shall certainly be united with him in a resurrection like his" (6:5). "United" carries the sense of "grown together," a living and intimate union. Union with Christ is at the heart of Paul's theology: justification, sanctification and glorification are all received in Christ.

**2. The old self crucified.** "We know that our old self was crucified with him in order that the body of sin might be brought to nothing, so that we would no longer be enslaved to sin" (6:6). The "old self" is not some "old part" of us, but our old identity in Adam (cf. 5:12–21). That person has died. "Brought to nothing" means "rendered powerless": not that our bodies are destroyed, but that sin can no longer rule us through our bodies.

**3. "Once for all."** "For the death he died he died to sin, once for all, but the life he lives he lives to God" (6:10). Christ "died to sin" not because he had any sin of his own, but because he bore sin's penalty for us, entered the realm of sin and death, and broke its power once for all (8:3; 2 Cor 5:21). His death was once for all, effective forever. He has dealt with sin completely; death no longer rules him. Through union with him, this becomes true of us too.

**4. "Consider yourselves."** "So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus" (6:11). This is the chapter's first command. "Consider" (*logizomai*) is the same word as "counted" in chapter 4. God has counted Christ's righteousness to us; now we too are to "count" ourselves as God says we are. This isn't autosuggestion or pretending we no longer have sinful tendencies; it is believing the fact God has declared, and living by it.

> The point: Sanctification doesn't begin with our effort but with believing a fact: in Christ we are dead to sin and alive to God. We are to "consider" ourselves as God says we are.

## Going Deeper: 6:12–14 Do Not Let Sin Reign

**1. From statement to command.** "Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal bodies, to make you obey their passions" (6:12). Paul first says "what you are" (6:1–11), and only then "so this is how you must live." This is the order of all of Romans, and of New Testament ethics as a whole: commands rest on the facts of grace. God does not say "become a new person"; he says "you are a new person, so live it out."

**2. Presenting.** "Do not present your members to sin as instruments for unrighteousness, but present yourselves to God as those who have been brought from death to life, and your members to God as instruments for righteousness" (6:13). "Instruments" can also be translated "weapons." Our eyes, tongue, hands and feet fight for one side or the other every day. Sanctification is very concrete: what will my tongue say today? What will my eyes look at today?

**3. Not under law but under grace.** "For sin will have no dominion over you, since you are not under law but under grace" (6:14). This is a promise, not only a command. Why can sin no longer rule? Not because the law has grown stricter, but because grace now reigns. The law can expose sin but cannot overcome it; grace can. "Not under law" does not mean believers may ignore God's will (6:15; 8:4); it means we no longer live under the old regime of the Mosaic law, which commanded and condemned but could not give life (7:5–6; 8:3). We now live under the reign of grace, which through Christ and the Spirit actually changes us.

> The point: God's commands rest on the facts of grace: you are already new, so live like it. Sin can no longer rule, not because the law is stricter, but because we are under grace.

## Going Deeper: 6:15–23 Two Kinds of Slavery

**1. No neutral ground.** "Do you not know that if you present yourselves to anyone as obedient slaves, you are slaves of the one whom you obey, either of sin, which leads to death, or of obedience, which leads to righteousness?" (6:16). Modern people think freedom means being bound by no one. Paul says no one is fully "self-governing": everyone serves some master. The question is not whether to be a slave, but whose.

**2. Obedient from the heart.** "But thanks be to God, that you who were once slaves of sin have become obedient from the heart to the standard of teaching to which you were committed" (6:17). Paul "thanks God" for their change, because it is God's work. "Standard" is the same word as "type" in 5:14, probably picturing a mold: the gospel is like a mold that shapes believers into its pattern. Notice that Paul says they were "committed to" this teaching: not that the teaching was handed to them, but that they were handed over to the teaching. This is God's own act, the reverse of 1:24–28, where God "gave them up" to impurity.

**3. "In human terms."** Paul admits that "slavery" is not a fully fitting picture for our relationship to God (6:19). Being God's slave is in fact true freedom; being sin's slave looks like freedom ("free in regard to righteousness," 6:20), but it ends in death.

**4. Fruit and end.** "But what fruit were you getting at that time from the things of which you are now ashamed? The end of those things is death. But now that you have been set free from sin and have become slaves of God, the fruit you get leads to sanctification and its end, eternal life" (6:21–22). Sin promises us happiness and pays us in shame and death.

**5. Wages and gift.** "For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord" (6:23). "Wages" (*opsōnia*) was often used of a soldier's pay or rations, and of wages generally: something earned and owed; a "free gift" is given for nothing. Sin is a master who pays wages, and its wage is death. God doesn't pay wages; he freely gives eternal life. Even in this chapter on sanctification, Paul's last word is grace: eternal life is not the wage of sanctification but the gift of God.

> The point: Everyone is a slave of some master. Sin pays us in death; God freely gives us eternal life. To be God's slave is true freedom.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus." When I fall into the same sin yet again, how do I "consider" myself? Do I say, "That's just who I am; I can't change"? Paul says that is not who I am in God's eyes: in Christ my old relationship with sin ended in a death. Today, is there a specific "member" I need to present to God afresh: my tongue, no longer for complaining, criticizing or lying; my eyes, no longer lingering on impure images; my phone and my time, no longer used to hide from God? This is not gritting my teeth by willpower, but believing that Christ's death and resurrection have truly changed me, and taking a step on that basis. Sin will still tell me today what it can give me, but I know its wages are death; and God has already freely given me eternal life.
