## 本章脉络

6:14说：「你们不在律法之下，乃在恩典之下。」读到这里，一个犹太信徒很可能会不安：律法是神赐的，难道律法不好吗？第七章就是回答这个问题。

本章分三段。**脱离律法，归于基督**（1-6节）：保罗用婚姻作比喻。丈夫死了，妻子就脱离了婚姻的律法，可以归于别人；照样，信徒「藉着基督的身体，在律法上也是死了」，归于那从死里复活的主，「结果子给神」。**律法是罪吗？**（7-13节）：「断乎不是！」律法是「圣洁、公义、良善的」；问题在于罪「趁着机会，就藉着诫命」引诱人、杀人。**里面的争战**（14-25节）：「我所愿意的，我并不做；我所恨恶的，我倒去做。」最后保罗呼喊：「我真是苦啊！谁能救我脱离这取死的身体呢？」随即就有了回答：「感谢神，靠着我们的主耶稣基督就能脱离了。」

这一章常常被人单独拿来讲「信徒的挣扎」。可是要记得，它是在第六章和第八章之间：第六章讲我们与基督联合、向罪死了；第八章讲圣灵所赐的生命。第七章的重点，是律法本身没有能力叫人成圣。

## 深读 7:1-6 归于另一位

**1. 律法管人是在活着的时候。** 「弟兄们，我现在对明白律法的人说，你们岂不晓得律法管人是在活着的时候吗？」（7:1）这是一个人人都明白的原则：人死了，律法就管不着他了。

**2. 婚姻的比喻。** 「就如女人有了丈夫，丈夫还活着，就被律法约束；丈夫若死了，就脱离了丈夫的律法。」（7:2）保罗的比喻不必处处对应（比喻里死的是丈夫，应用里死的却是信徒自己，7:4）。他的重点只有一个：死亡结束了一种约束，人就可以进入新的关系。

**3. 归于那从死里复活的。** 「我的弟兄们，这样说来，你们藉着基督的身体，在律法上也是死了，叫你们归于别人，就是归于那从死里复活的，叫我们结果子给神。」（7:4）「藉着基督的身体」，就是藉着基督在十字架上的死。我们与他同死，旧的约束就结束了；如今我们属于另一位，就是复活的基督。救恩不只是脱离一样东西，更是归于一位主。

**4. 心灵的新样。** 「但我们既然在捆我们的律法上死了，现今就脱离了律法，叫我们服事主，要按着心灵的新样，不按着仪文的旧样。」（7:6）「心灵」原文也可以译作「圣灵」：新的服事方式，是靠着圣灵，不是靠着写在石版上的条文。这句话预告了第八章。

> 要点：信徒在律法上已经死了，不是为了无法无天，而是为了归于复活的基督，靠着圣灵结果子给神。救恩不只是脱离旧主人，更是属于一位新的主。

## 深读 7:7-13 律法是好的，罪才是问题

**1. 「律法是罪吗？」** 「这样，我们可说什么呢？律法是罪吗？断乎不是！只是非因律法，我就不知何为罪。非律法说『不可起贪心』，我就不知何为贪心。」（7:7）保罗选了十诫的最后一条（出20:17）。这条诫命很特别：它不是管人的行为，而是管人的心。一个人可以一辈子不偷不抢，却不能说自己从来没有起过贪心。

**2. 罪趁着机会。** 「然而，罪趁着机会，就藉着诫命叫诸般的贪心在我里头发动。」（7:8）「机会」这个词在军事上可以指发动攻击的据点。罪把神良善的诫命当作据点，来攻击人。罪是何等诡诈！就像对孩子说「不要碰那个」，孩子反而更想去碰。

**3. 「我」是谁？** 7:7-13的「我」，解经家有不同的看法：

- 有人认为是保罗自己的经历，也许是他少年时第一次明白诫命的时候。
- 有人认为保罗是在讲亚当：「诫命来到，罪又活了，我就死了」（7:9），像极了伊甸园的故事；罪「藉着诫命引诱我」（7:11），也呼应夏娃说「那蛇引诱我」（创3:13）。
- 也有人认为是讲以色列：律法在西乃山「来到」以后，以色列的罪反而更加显明。

这几种看法不一定彼此排斥：保罗可能是用「我」，把亚当、以色列和他自己的经历都放在一起。

**4. 律法是圣洁的。** 「这样看来，律法是圣洁的，诫命也是圣洁、公义、良善的。」（7:12）问题不在律法，而在罪。律法像一盏灯，照出房间里的污秽；不能因为看见了污秽，就怪罪那盏灯。

**5. 罪显出是恶极了。** 「叫我死的乃是罪。但罪藉着那良善的叫我死，就显出真是罪，叫罪因着诫命更显出是恶极了。」（7:13）罪竟然能利用神良善的诫命来杀人，这正显明罪是何等的邪恶。

> 要点：律法是圣洁、公义、良善的；问题在于罪：罪连神良善的诫命也能拿来攻击人。律法能照出罪，却不能除掉罪。

## 深读 7:14-25 「我真是苦啊！」

**1. 里面的分裂。** 「我们原晓得律法是属乎灵的，但我是属乎肉体的，是已经卖给罪了。因为我所做的，我自己不明白；我所愿意的，我并不做；我所恨恶的，我倒去做。」（7:14-15）这是一个人心里真实的分裂：他认同律法是善的（7:16），甚至「喜欢神的律」（7:22），却发现自己做不到。

**2. 住在我里头的罪。** 「既是这样，就不是我做的，乃是住在我里头的罪做的。」（7:17，20）保罗不是在推卸责任，而是在描写罪的权势：罪像一个房客，住进了人的里面，不肯离开。「立志为善由得我，只是行出来由不得我。」（7:18）

**3. 这个「我」是谁？** 7:14-25是罗马书最有争议的经文之一。主要有三种看法：

- **已经重生的信徒**：奥古斯丁（后期）、马丁·路德、加尔文和许多改革宗解经家都这样看。理由是：这里用的是现在时态；只有重生的人才会「喜欢神的律」（7:22），恨恶自己的罪；而且7:25下半节在感谢神之后，仍然说「我以内心顺服神的律，我肉体却顺服罪的律了」。信徒一生都在经历这样的争战（参加5:17）。不过持不同看法的人指出，敬虔的犹太人同样喜爱神的律法（诗1:2；119:97），保罗也说自己信主以前「就律法上的义说，我是无可指摘的」（腓3:6）。
- **还没有重生的人**：许多早期教父和不少现代解经家认为，「已经卖给罪了」（7:14）和第六章说信徒「从罪里得了释放」（6:18）不合；而且除了7:6的一句预告，7:7-25完全没有提到圣灵，圣灵要到第八章才成为中心。所以这是一个在律法之下、想靠自己遵行律法的人（也许是保罗回头看自己信主以前的光景）。
- 也有人认为，保罗是在描写任何一个想**靠律法、不靠圣灵**来过圣洁生活的人，不论他是否已经信主；有些福音派学者则认为，这是一个被圣灵光照、知罪却还没有重生的人。

无论采取哪一种看法，结论都是一样的：靠律法和自己的意志，不能胜过罪。华人文化相信人可以靠修身、立志成为君子；保罗却承认，人能立志，却行不出来。我们需要的不是更好的修养，而是一位拯救者。

**4. 呼喊与回答。** 「我真是苦啊！谁能救我脱离这取死的身体呢？感谢神，靠着我们的主耶稣基督就能脱离了。」（7:24-25）注意他问的是「谁」，不是「什么」：他需要的不是一个更好的方法，而是一位拯救者。答案立刻就来了，就是耶稣基督。第八章会展开这个答案：「如今，那些在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了。」（8:1）

> 要点：靠律法和自己的意志，不能胜过住在里面的罪。人需要的不是一个更好的方法，而是一位拯救者；而这位拯救者，就是我们的主耶稣基督。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我所愿意的，我并不做；我所恨恶的，我倒去做。」我有没有一样罪，已经下过许多次决心，却仍然一再跌倒？也许不是别人看得见的事，而是像保罗说的「贪心」那样藏在心里的：刷朋友圈时的嫉妒、不停地和别人比较、一股压不下去的怨气？我是否以为，只要再多读一点经、再多祷告一点、再严格一点，就一定能胜过它？第七章告诉我，律法和决心本身都不能救我；连保罗也只能喊：「我真是苦啊！」可是要留意，他问的是「谁能救我」。今天，我能不能不再只问「我还要怎样做」，而是把这份挣扎诚实地带到主耶稣面前？我心里还在争战、还会恨恶自己的罪，这本身也可能是恩典的记号：一个不在乎神的人，是不会这样挣扎的。可是这不是叫我安于跌倒的理由。主耶稣就是那一位「谁」：他已经在十字架上担当了这罪的定罪，所以我跌倒以后不必躲开他（8:1）；他也藉着住在我里面的圣灵，赐我能力去治死它（8:13）。读经、祷告不是我自救的方法，而是我来到这位拯救者面前的路。今天，我能不能把这一样罪的名字，诚实地告诉他，也告诉一位可信的弟兄姊妹（雅5:16），然后和保罗一起说：「感谢神，靠着我们的主耶稣基督就能脱离了。」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Romans 6:14 said, "you are not under law but under grace." A Jewish believer reading this might well be uneasy: the law was given by God, so is the law bad? Chapter 7 answers that question.

The chapter has three parts. **Released from the law, joined to Christ** (vv. 1–6): an illustration from marriage: when a husband dies, his wife is released from the law of marriage and may belong to another; so believers "have died to the law through the body of Christ," and now belong to the risen Lord, "in order that we may bear fruit for God." **Is the law sin?** (vv. 7–13): "By no means!" The law is "holy and righteous and good"; the problem is that sin, "seizing an opportunity through the commandment," deceives and kills. **The war within** (vv. 14–25): "I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate," until the cry, "Wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death?" and the immediate answer, "Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!"

This chapter is often lifted out on its own to talk about "the believer's struggle." But remember that it sits between chapters 6 and 8: chapter 6 speaks of our union with Christ and death to sin; chapter 8 speaks of the life the Spirit gives. The main point of chapter 7 is that the law itself has no power to make us holy.

## Going Deeper: 7:1–6 Belonging to Another

**1. The law binds the living.** "Or do you not know, brothers (for I am speaking to those who know the law), that the law is binding on a person only as long as he lives?" (7:1). Everyone understands this principle: once a person dies, the law no longer has a hold on them.

**2. The marriage illustration.** "Thus a married woman is bound by law to her husband while he lives, but if her husband dies she is released from the law of marriage" (7:2). Not every detail of Paul's illustration needs a match (in the picture the husband dies; in the application it is the believer who dies, 7:4). His point is a single one: death ends one bond and frees a person to enter a new relationship.

**3. Belonging to the one raised from the dead.** "Likewise, my brothers, you also have died to the law through the body of Christ, so that you may belong to another, to him who has been raised from the dead, in order that we may bear fruit for God" (7:4). "Through the body of Christ" means through Christ's death on the cross. In dying with him, the old bond ended; now we belong to another, the risen Christ. Salvation is not only release from something; it is belonging to a Lord.

**4. The new life of the Spirit.** "But now we are released from the law, having died to that which held us captive, so that we serve not under the old written code but in the new life of the Spirit" (7:6). The new life of serving is by the Spirit, not by rules engraved on stone. The verse previews chapter 8.

> The point: Believers have died to the law, not to become lawless, but to belong to the risen Christ and bear fruit for God by the Spirit. Salvation is not only release from an old master but belonging to a new Lord.

## Going Deeper: 7:7–13 The Law Is Good; Sin Is the Problem

**1. "Is the law sin?"** "What then shall we say? That the law is sin? By no means! Yet if it had not been for the law, I would not have known sin. I would not have known what it is to covet if the law had not said, 'You shall not covet'" (7:7). Paul chooses the last of the Ten Commandments (Exod 20:17). This commandment is unusual: it governs not behavior but the heart. A person may go a lifetime without stealing or robbing, but no one can say they have never coveted.

**2. Sin seizes an opportunity.** "But sin, seizing an opportunity through the commandment, produced in me all kinds of covetousness" (7:8). "Opportunity" could be used in military settings for a base of operations. Sin uses God's good commandment as a base from which to attack. How deceitful sin is: tell a child "don't touch that," and the child often wants to touch it all the more.

**3. Who is "I"?** Interpreters read the "I" of 7:7–13 in different ways:

- Some take it as Paul's own experience, perhaps when he first understood the commandment as a boy.
- Some think Paul is speaking of Adam: "when the commandment came, sin came alive and I died" (7:9) sounds very like the Eden story; sin "deceived me" through the commandment (7:11), echoing Eve's "the serpent deceived me" (Gen 3:13).
- Others think it refers to Israel: when the law "came" at Sinai, Israel's sin became all the more evident.

These views need not exclude each other: Paul may be using "I" to gather Adam, Israel and his own experience together.

**4. The law is holy.** "So the law is holy, and the commandment is holy and righteous and good" (7:12). The problem is not the law but sin. The law is like a lamp that shows the dirt in a room; we shouldn't blame the lamp for what it reveals.

**5. Sin shown to be sinful beyond measure.** "It was sin, producing death in me through what is good, in order that sin might be shown to be sin, and through the commandment might become sinful beyond measure" (7:13). That sin can use God's good commandment to kill shows how evil sin truly is.

> The point: The law is holy, righteous and good; the problem is sin, which can even use God's good commandment to attack us. The law can expose sin but cannot remove it.

## Going Deeper: 7:14–25 "Wretched Man That I Am!"

**1. Divided within.** "For we know that the law is spiritual, but I am of the flesh, sold under sin. I do not understand my own actions. For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate" (7:14–15). This is a real division within a person: he agrees that the law is good (7:16), even delights in the law of God (7:22), yet finds he cannot keep it.

**2. Sin that dwells within.** "So now it is no longer I who do it, but sin that dwells within me" (7:17, 20). Paul is not passing the blame; he is describing sin's power: sin is like a tenant who has moved in and won't leave. "For I have the desire to do what is right, but not the ability to carry it out" (7:18).

**3. Who is this "I"?** Romans 7:14–25 is one of the most debated passages in the letter. There are three main views:

- **A regenerate believer:** Augustine (in his later writings), Luther, Calvin and many Reformed interpreters take this view. Their reasons: the verbs are in the present tense; only a regenerate person delights in the law of God (7:22) and hates their own sin; and even after giving thanks, 7:25b still says, "I myself serve the law of God with my mind, but with my flesh I serve the law of sin." Believers face this conflict all their lives (cf. Gal 5:17). Others reply that devout Jews also delighted in God's law (Ps 1:2; 119:97), and that Paul called his pre-Christian self "blameless" as to righteousness under the law (Phil 3:6).
- **An unregenerate person:** many early church fathers and a good number of modern interpreters argue that "sold under sin" (7:14) does not fit chapter 6, which says believers "have been set free from sin" (6:18); and that, apart from the preview in 7:6, the Spirit is not mentioned in 7:7–25, though he is central in chapter 8. So this is a person under the law, trying to keep it by their own strength (perhaps Paul looking back on his life before Christ).
- Others think Paul is describing anyone who tries to live a holy life **by the law rather than by the Spirit**, whether or not they are already a believer; some evangelicals think of an awakened person under conviction, not yet regenerate.

Whichever view one takes, the conclusion is the same: the law and our own will cannot overcome sin. Chinese culture trusts that self-cultivation and resolve can make a person a gentleman; Paul admits that we can resolve but cannot carry it out. What we need is not better cultivation but a deliverer.

**4. The cry and the answer.** "Wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death? Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!" (7:24–25). Notice that he asks "who," not "what": he needs not a better method but a deliverer. The answer comes at once: Jesus Christ. Chapter 8 unfolds it: "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (8:1).

> The point: The law and our own will cannot overcome the sin that dwells within. What we need is not a better method but a deliverer, and that deliverer is our Lord Jesus Christ.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate." Is there a sin I have resolved against many times, yet keep falling into? Perhaps not something others can see, but something hidden in the heart like Paul's "covet": envy as I scroll through other people's lives, constant comparison, a resentment I cannot put down? Do I assume that if I just read a bit more Scripture, prayed a bit more, were a bit stricter, I would surely beat it? Chapter 7 tells me that the law and my resolve cannot save me; even Paul could only cry, "Wretched man that I am!" But notice that he asks, "Who will deliver me?" Today, can I stop asking only "What else must I do?" and bring this struggle honestly to the Lord Jesus? That I still fight, that I still hate my sin, may itself be a sign of grace: a person who does not care about God does not struggle like this. But that is no reason to settle down with my sin. The Lord Jesus is the "who": he has already borne this sin's condemnation on the cross, so I need not hide from him after I fall (8:1); and by his Spirit living in me he gives me power to put it to death (8:13). Scripture and prayer are not my methods of self-rescue but the way I come to this Deliverer. Today, can I name this one sin honestly to him, and to one trusted brother or sister (Jas 5:16), and then say with Paul, "Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!"?
