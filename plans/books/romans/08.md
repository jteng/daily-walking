## 本章脉络

第七章以一声呼喊结束：「谁能救我脱离这取死的身体呢？」第八章就是神的回答。这一章以「不定罪」开始（8:1），以「不能……隔绝」结束（8:39）；圣灵在第七章一次也没有出现，在这一章却出现了将近二十次。

本章分四段。**圣灵所赐的生命**（1-11节）：「如今，那些在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了」；律法做不到的，神藉着差遣他的儿子做成了，又藉着圣灵使律法的义成就在我们身上。**神的儿女**（12-17节）：我们所受的「乃是儿子的心」，可以呼叫「阿爸！父！」**叹息与盼望**（18-30节）：受造之物、我们、和圣灵都在「叹息」；可是「万事都互相效力，叫爱神的人得益处」，神从预知到得荣耀，一步一步完成他的救恩。**谁能隔绝？**（31-39节）：「神若帮助我们，谁能敌挡我们呢？」没有任何受造之物「能叫我们与神的爱隔绝」。

如果说3:21-26是罗马书的中心，第八章就是罗马书的高峰。

## 深读 8:1-11 不定罪，且有生命

**1. 「如今……就不定罪了」。** 「如今，那些在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了。」（8:1）「如今」呼应3:21的「但如今」。「定罪」是法庭的判决，也包括判决之后的刑罚。对那些「在基督耶稣里的」，判决已经宣读了：无罪。这不是说我们不再犯罪，而是说罪的定罪已经不再落在我们身上。

**2. 律法所不能行的。** 「律法既因肉体软弱，有所不能行的，神就差遣自己的儿子，成为罪身的形状，作了赎罪祭，在肉体中定了罪案。」（8:3）律法本身是好的（7:12），可是遇到人软弱的肉体，就无能为力了。神自己做了律法做不到的事：差遣他的儿子。「成为罪身的形状」，意思是他有真实的人性，却没有罪（参林后5:21；来4:15）。「定了罪案」：神在基督的身体上，审判了罪。为什么我们不被定罪（8:1）？因为罪已经在基督身上被定罪了（8:3）。

**3. 律法的义成就在我们身上。** 「使律法的义成就在我们这不随从肉体、只随从圣灵的人身上。」（8:4）福音不是废掉律法，而是使律法真正的要求，借着圣灵在信徒身上得着成就（参3:31）。旧约先知早就应许过：神要将他的灵放在人里面，使人遵行他的律例（结36:27）。

**4. 体贴肉体与体贴圣灵。** 「随从肉体的人体贴肉体的事；随从圣灵的人体贴圣灵的事。体贴肉体的，就是死；体贴圣灵的，乃是生命、平安。」（8:5-6）「体贴」是人心思所关注、所向往的方向。保罗在这里描写的是两种人，而不是一个信徒的两种状态：「如果神的灵住在你们心里，你们就不属肉体，乃属圣灵了。人若没有基督的灵，就不是属基督的。」（8:9）每一个真信徒都有圣灵；没有圣灵，就不是基督徒。

**5. 复活的保证。** 「然而，叫耶稣从死里复活者的灵若住在你们心里，那叫基督耶稣从死里复活的，也必藉着住在你们心里的圣灵，使你们必死的身体又活过来。」（8:11）住在我们里面的圣灵，就是叫耶稣复活的那位灵。他今天在我们里面的工作，是将来身体复活的保证。

> 要点：我们不被定罪，因为罪已经在基督身上被定罪了。住在我们里面的圣灵，不只使我们能顺服神，也保证我们必死的身体将来要复活。

## 深读 8:12-17 阿爸！父！

**1. 不欠肉体的债。** 「弟兄们，这样看来，我们并不是欠肉体的债去顺从肉体活着。你们若顺从肉体活着，必要死；若靠着圣灵治死身体的恶行，必要活着。」（8:12-13）这是信徒的责任：「治死」罪。英国清教徒欧文（John Owen）有一句名言：「你若不治死罪，罪就要治死你。」可是要留意，是「靠着圣灵」，不是靠自己。

**2. 被神的灵引导。** 「因为凡被神的灵引导的，都是神的儿子。」（8:14）这里的「引导」，首先不是指在生活中做决定时得着特别的指引，而是指上文所说的：圣灵引导人治死罪、过圣洁的生活。

**3. 儿子的心。** 「你们所受的，不是奴仆的心，仍旧害怕；所受的，乃是儿子的心，因此我们呼叫：『阿爸！父！』」（8:15）「儿子的心」原文是「儿子名分的灵」，就是使人得着儿子名分的圣灵。在罗马社会，被收养的儿子享有和亲生儿子一样的权利，旧的债务一笔勾销。「阿爸」是亚兰话，是孩子对父亲亲切的称呼；耶稣在客西马尼园就是这样祷告的（可14:36）。神不只是赦免我们的审判官，更是我们的父亲。

**4. 圣灵的见证。** 「圣灵与我们的心同证我们是神的儿女。」（8:16）我们确信自己是神的儿女，不只是从道理上推论出来的，也是圣灵在我们心里亲自作的见证。

**5. 后嗣。** 「既是儿女，便是后嗣，就是神的后嗣，和基督同作后嗣。如果我们和他一同受苦，也必和他一同得荣耀。」（8:17）作神的儿女，意味着要承受产业；可是在得荣耀之前，先要和基督一同受苦。这一句话，把下一段连接起来。

> 要点：我们所受的不是奴仆的心，乃是儿子的心。神不只是赦免我们的审判官，更是我们可以呼叫「阿爸！父！」的父亲。

## 深读 8:18-30 叹息与盼望

**1. 不足介意。** 「我想，现在的苦楚若比起将来要显于我们的荣耀就不足介意了。」（8:18）「我想」原文是一个会计用语，意思是经过计算之后的结论。保罗不是轻看苦难：他自己受过鞭打、下过监、遇过船难（林后11:23-27）。他是把苦难放在天平上，与将来的荣耀相比，发现苦难根本不能与荣耀相比。

**2. 受造之物的叹息。** 「受造之物切望等候神的众子显出来。因为受造之物服在虚空之下，不是自己愿意，乃是因那叫他如此的。但受造之物仍然指望脱离败坏的辖制，得享神儿女自由的荣耀。」（8:19-21）因着人的罪，整个受造界都受了咒诅（创3:17-18）。自然界的灾难、疾病、死亡，都是这「虚空」和「败坏」的记号。可是8:20原文末了还有一句「在盼望中」：有一天，受造界要和神的儿女一同得着释放。救恩最终不是灵魂离开世界，而是整个受造界被更新。

**3. 我们的叹息。** 「我们这有圣灵初结果子的，也是自己心里叹息，等候得着儿子的名分，乃是我们的身体得赎。」（8:23）「初结果子」是收割之前献给神的第一批庄稼，是整个收成的保证。圣灵在我们里面，就是将来完全救赎的保证。可是我们仍然「叹息」：信徒不是不会痛苦的人。

**4. 圣灵的叹息。** 「况且，我们的软弱有圣灵帮助；我们本不晓得当怎样祷告，只是圣灵亲自用说不出来的叹息替我们祷告。」（8:26）这是何等大的安慰：当我们痛苦到不知道怎样祷告的时候，圣灵亲自替我们祈求，而且是「照着神的旨意」（8:27）。

**5. 万事互相效力。** 「我们晓得万事都互相效力，叫爱神的人得益处，就是按他旨意被召的人。」（8:28）这节经文常常被误用。它不是说每一件事本身都是好的，也不是说每一件事的结局都会照我们所希望的。它说的是：神在一切事上掌权，使一切事最终都为他的儿女效力。什么是「益处」？下一节说明了：「效法他儿子的模样」（8:29）。神最大的心意，不是叫我们舒适，而是叫我们像基督。

**6. 金链。** 「因为他预先所知道的人，就预先定下效法他儿子的模样……预先所定下的人又召他们来；所召来的人又称他们为义；所称为义的人又叫他们得荣耀。」（8:29-30）这五个环节，常被称为「救恩的金链」：预知、预定、呼召、称义、得荣耀。每一环都是神的作为，而且没有一环会断掉：凡被神预知的，最后都要得荣耀。最奇妙的是，「得荣耀」用的是过去时态，好像已经成就了一样，因为在神眼中，这是确定的事。「预先所知道」，许多解经家认为不只是预先知道某人会信，而是神预先爱他、拣选他（参摩3:2；耶1:5）。

> 要点：我们仍在叹息，受造之物也在叹息，可是这叹息是在盼望中的。神在万事上掌权，要叫我们效法他儿子的模样；他所开始的救恩，没有一环会断掉。

## 深读 8:31-39 谁能隔绝？

**1. 神若帮助我们。** 「既是这样，还有什么说的呢？神若帮助我们，谁能敌挡我们呢？」（8:31）「帮助」原文是「为我们」：神站在我们这一边。这不是说没有人会反对我们，而是说没有人能真正胜过我们。

**2. 从大到小的推论。** 「神既不爱惜自己的儿子，为我们众人舍了，岂不也把万物和他一同白白地赐给我们吗？」（8:32）这句话可能暗指亚伯拉罕献以撒（创22:12，16「不留下」）。神已经给了最宝贵的，就是他的儿子；他还会吝惜比这更小的东西吗？

**3. 法庭的场面。** 「谁能控告神所拣选的人呢？有神称他们为义了。谁能定他们的罪呢？有基督耶稣已经死了，而且从死里复活，现今在神的右边，也替我们祈求。」（8:33-34）撒但可以控告，我们的良心也可以控告；可是审判官已经宣判我们为义，我们的辩护者就坐在审判官的右边。基督的死、复活、升天和代求，四重保证我们不被定罪。

**4. 谁能隔绝？** 「谁能使我们与基督的爱隔绝呢？难道是患难吗？是困苦吗？是逼迫吗？是饥饿吗？是赤身露体吗？是危险吗？是刀剑吗？」（8:35）保罗列的不是假想的事，而是他自己和初期教会实际经历过的。他引用诗篇44:22：「我们为你的缘故终日被杀；人看我们如将宰的羊。」信徒的苦难，并不表示神不爱他们。

**5. 得胜有余。** 「然而，靠着爱我们的主，在这一切的事上已经得胜有余了。」（8:37）不是「脱离」这一切，而是「在这一切的事上」得胜。得胜的根据，不是我们有多坚强，而是「爱我们的主」。

**6. 「我深信」。** 「因为我深信无论是死，是生，是天使，是掌权的，是有能的，是现在的事，是将来的事，是高处的，是低处的，是别的受造之物，都不能叫我们与神的爱隔绝；这爱是在我们的主基督耶稣里的。」（8:38-39）保罗列了十样东西，涵盖了死亡与生命、属灵的权势、时间、空间，最后加上一句「别的受造之物」，把一切都包括在内。只有一样东西没有列出来：神自己。而神，是不会离弃我们的。

> 要点：神既已为我们舍了他的儿子，就必白白地把一切赐给我们。没有任何控告能定我们的罪，也没有任何受造之物能叫我们与神在基督里的爱隔绝。

## 默想

**默想：** 「如今，那些在基督耶稣里的就不定罪了。」我心里是否仍然常常听见一个定罪的声音：「你这样还算基督徒吗？」「神一定对你很失望」？保罗说，审判官已经宣判了，我的辩护者就坐在他的右边，「谁能定他们的罪呢？」今天，我正在经历的痛苦是什么：一场病、一次失业、一段关系的破裂，或是我说不出来的重担？我可能已经不知道该怎样祷告；保罗说，圣灵正在「用说不出来的叹息」替我祷告。我不需要假装这些事本身是好的，可是我可以相信，那位「不爱惜自己的儿子」的神，正在这一切事上掌权，要叫我更像基督。最后，我能不能把8:38-39慢慢读一遍，把我最怕的那一样东西，放进保罗的清单里，然后对自己说：连这个，也「不能叫我们与神的爱隔绝」？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 7 ended with a cry: "Who will deliver me from this body of death?" Chapter 8 is God's answer. It begins with "no condemnation" (8:1) and ends with "nothing will be able to separate" (8:39); the Holy Spirit, absent from chapter 7, appears here nearly twenty times.

The chapter has four parts. **Life in the Spirit** (vv. 1–11): "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus"; what the law could not do, God did by sending his Son, and by the Spirit the righteous requirement of the law is fulfilled in us. **Children of God** (vv. 12–17): we have received "the Spirit of adoption as sons," and cry, "Abba! Father!" **Groaning and hope** (vv. 18–30): creation, we ourselves and the Spirit all "groan"; yet "for those who love God all things work together for good," and God completes his salvation step by step, from foreknowledge to glory. **Who shall separate us?** (vv. 31–39): "If God is for us, who can be against us?" Nothing in all creation "will be able to separate us from the love of God."

If 3:21–26 is the center of Romans, chapter 8 is its summit.

## Going Deeper: 8:1–11 No Condemnation, and Life

**1. "Now no condemnation."** "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (8:1). "Now" echoes the "but now" of 3:21. "Condemnation" is a court's verdict, including the penalty that follows it. For those "in Christ Jesus" the verdict has been read: not guilty. This doesn't mean we no longer sin; it means sin's condemnation no longer falls on us.

**2. What the law could not do.** "For God has done what the law, weakened by the flesh, could not do. By sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and for sin, he condemned sin in the flesh" (8:3). The law itself is good (7:12), but faced with our weak flesh it is powerless. God himself did what the law could not: he sent his Son. "In the likeness of sinful flesh" means he had a real human nature, yet without sin (cf. 2 Cor 5:21; Heb 4:15). "He condemned sin in the flesh": God judged sin in Christ's body. Why are we not condemned (8:1)? Because sin has already been condemned in Christ (8:3).

**3. The law's requirement fulfilled in us.** "In order that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not according to the flesh but according to the Spirit" (8:4). The gospel does not abolish the law; by the Spirit it brings the law's true requirement to fulfillment in believers (cf. 3:31). The prophets had promised that God would put his Spirit within his people and cause them to walk in his statutes (Ezek 36:27).

**4. The mind set on the flesh, the mind set on the Spirit.** "For those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit set their minds on the things of the Spirit. To set the mind on the flesh is death, but to set the mind on the Spirit is life and peace" (8:5–6). "Set the mind on" describes the direction of a person's thoughts and desires. Paul is describing two kinds of people, not two moods of a believer: "You, however, are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if in fact the Spirit of God dwells in you. Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him" (8:9). Every true believer has the Spirit; without the Spirit, a person is not a Christian.

**5. The guarantee of resurrection.** "If the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, he who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit who dwells in you" (8:11). The Spirit who lives in us is the same Spirit who raised Jesus. His work in us now guarantees the resurrection of our bodies.

> The point: We are not condemned because sin has already been condemned in Christ. The Spirit who lives in us not only enables us to obey God but guarantees that our mortal bodies will be raised.

## Going Deeper: 8:12–17 Abba! Father!

**1. Not debtors to the flesh.** "So then, brothers, we are debtors, not to the flesh, to live according to the flesh. For if you live according to the flesh you will die, but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live" (8:12–13). This is the believer's responsibility: to "put to death" sin. The Puritan John Owen famously said, "Be killing sin or it will be killing you." But notice: "by the Spirit," not by ourselves.

**2. Led by the Spirit.** "For all who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God" (8:14). "Led" here does not first mean receiving special guidance for decisions, but what the context describes: the Spirit leading us to put sin to death and live holy lives.

**3. The Spirit of adoption.** "For you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!'" (8:15). In Roman society an adopted son had the same rights as a natural son, and his old debts were cancelled. "Abba" is Aramaic, a child's warm word for father; it is how Jesus prayed in Gethsemane (Mark 14:36). God is not only the judge who acquits us; he is our Father.

**4. The Spirit's witness.** "The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God" (8:16). Our assurance that we are God's children is not only reasoned from doctrine; it is the Spirit's own witness in our hearts.

**5. Heirs.** "And if children, then heirs, heirs of God and fellow heirs with Christ, provided we suffer with him in order that we may also be glorified with him" (8:17). To be God's children means to inherit; but before glory comes suffering with Christ. This sentence links to the next section.

> The point: We have received not the spirit of slavery but the Spirit of adoption. God is not only the judge who acquits us but the Father to whom we may cry, "Abba! Father!"

## Going Deeper: 8:18–30 Groaning and Hope

**1. Not worth comparing.** "For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worth comparing with the glory that is to be revealed to us" (8:18). "Consider" is an accounting word, a conclusion reached after calculation. Paul doesn't make light of suffering: he had been beaten, imprisoned and shipwrecked (2 Cor 11:23–27). He weighed suffering against the coming glory and found there was no comparison.

**2. Creation groaning.** "For the creation waits with eager longing for the revealing of the sons of God. For the creation was subjected to futility, not willingly, but because of him who subjected it, in hope that the creation itself will be set free from its bondage to decay and obtain the freedom of the glory of the children of God" (8:19–21). Because of human sin, the whole creation came under the curse (Gen 3:17–18). Natural disasters, disease and death are all signs of this "futility" and "decay." Yet all this is "in hope" (8:20): one day creation will be set free together with God's children. Salvation is not finally souls escaping the world, but the whole creation made new.

**3. Our groaning.** "And not only the creation, but we ourselves, who have the firstfruits of the Spirit, groan inwardly as we wait eagerly for adoption as sons, the redemption of our bodies" (8:23). "Firstfruits" were the first sheaves offered to God before the harvest, the pledge of the whole crop. The Spirit within us is the pledge of our full redemption to come. Yet we still "groan": believers are not people who feel no pain.

**4. The Spirit's groaning.** "Likewise the Spirit helps us in our weakness. For we do not know what to pray for as we ought, but the Spirit himself intercedes for us with groanings too deep for words" (8:26). What comfort this is: when we hurt so much we don't know how to pray, the Spirit himself intercedes for us, and "according to the will of God" (8:27).

**5. All things work together for good.** "And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good, for those who are called according to his purpose" (8:28). This verse is often misused. It doesn't say that everything is good in itself, or that everything will turn out as we hope. It says that God rules over all things and makes them all serve his children's good in the end. What is that "good"? The next verse tells us: to be "conformed to the image of his Son" (8:29). God's great purpose is not to make us comfortable but to make us like Christ.

**6. The golden chain.** "For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son... And those whom he predestined he also called, and those whom he called he also justified, and those whom he justified he also glorified" (8:29–30). These five links are often called "the golden chain of salvation": foreknowledge, predestination, calling, justification, glorification. Every link is God's action, and none breaks: all whom God foreknew will in the end be glorified. Most wonderfully, "glorified" is in the past tense, as though already done, because in God's sight it is certain. Many interpreters take "foreknew" not merely as knowing in advance that someone would believe, but as God's setting his love on them beforehand and choosing them (cf. Amos 3:2; Jer 1:5).

> The point: We still groan, and creation groans too, but our groaning is in hope. God rules over all things to conform us to the image of his Son; no link in the salvation he has begun will break.

## Going Deeper: 8:31–39 Who Shall Separate Us?

**1. If God is for us.** "What then shall we say to these things? If God is for us, who can be against us?" (8:31). God is on our side. This doesn't mean no one will oppose us, but that no one can finally prevail against us.

**2. From the greater to the lesser.** "He who did not spare his own Son but gave him up for us all, how will he not also with him graciously give us all things?" (8:32). These words may echo Abraham's offering of Isaac (Gen 22:12, 16, "you have not withheld your son"). God has already given his most precious gift, his Son; will he hold back anything lesser?

**3. A courtroom scene.** "Who shall bring any charge against God's elect? It is God who justifies. Who is to condemn? Christ Jesus is the one who died, more than that, who was raised, who is at the right hand of God, who indeed is interceding for us" (8:33–34). Satan may accuse us, and so may our conscience; but the judge has already declared us righteous, and our advocate sits at the judge's right hand. Christ's death, resurrection, exaltation and intercession are a fourfold guarantee that we will not be condemned.

**4. Who shall separate us?** "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or danger, or sword?" (8:35). Paul isn't listing imaginary things but what he and the early church actually endured. He quotes Psalm 44:22: "For your sake we are being killed all the day long; we are regarded as sheep to be slaughtered." Believers' sufferings do not mean God does not love them.

**5. More than conquerors.** "No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us" (8:37). Not escaping all these things, but conquering "in all these things." The ground of victory is not how strong we are, but "him who loved us."

**6. "I am sure."** "For I am sure that neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers, nor height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord" (8:38–39). Paul lists ten things, covering death and life, spiritual powers, time and space, and adds "anything else in all creation" to leave nothing out. Only one thing is not on the list: God himself. And God will never abandon us.

> The point: Since God gave his Son for us, he will freely give us all things. No accusation can condemn us, and nothing in all creation can separate us from God's love in Christ.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." Do I still often hear a condemning voice inside: "Call yourself a Christian?" "God must be so disappointed in you"? Paul says the judge has already given his verdict, and my advocate sits at his right hand: "Who is to condemn?" What pain am I going through today: an illness, a lost job, a broken relationship, or a burden I can't put into words? I may no longer know how to pray; Paul says the Spirit is interceding for me "with groanings too deep for words." I don't need to pretend these things are good in themselves, but I can trust that the God "who did not spare his own Son" is ruling over all of them to make me more like Christ. Finally, can I read 8:38–39 slowly, put the one thing I fear most onto Paul's list, and say to myself: even this "will not be able to separate us from the love of God"?
