## 本章脉络

第九章从神的主权讲以色列为什么没有得救；第十章从人的责任来讲同一件事。保罗没有用一边去否定另一边。

本章分三段。**热心却不按着真知识**（1-4节）：保罗为以色列人祷告，他们「向神有热心」，却「想要立自己的义，就不服神的义了」，因为「律法的总结就是基督」。**这道离你不远**（5-13节）：出于信心的义不要求人升天下地，只要「口里认耶稣为主，心里信神叫他从死里复活」；而且「凡求告主名的就必得救」，犹太人和希腊人并没有分别。**他们听见了吗？**（14-21节）：人要求告，先要信；要信，先要听；要听，先要有人传；可是以色列人听见了，却没有都听从。最后神说：「我整天伸手招呼那悖逆顶嘴的百姓。」

## 深读 10:1-4 两种义

**1. 保罗的祷告。** 「弟兄们，我心里所愿的，向神所求的，是要以色列人得救。」（10:1）保罗讲完了拣选的奥秘，并没有停止为以色列人祷告。相信神的主权的人，仍然祷告，而且更迫切地祷告。

**2. 热心却不按着真知识。** 「我可以证明，他们向神有热心，但不是按着真知识。」（10:2）保罗自己就曾经是这样的人：「就热心说，我是逼迫教会的」（腓3:6）。真诚和热心本身不能救人；方向错了，越热心就走得越远。

**3. 立自己的义。** 「因为不知道神的义，想要立自己的义，就不服神的义了。」（10:3）「立」是建造、竖立的意思，像是给自己立一座纪念碑。人的问题，不只是做了坏事，也包括想用好事来建立自己在神面前的地位。这样的人，看起来是在顺服神，其实是不肯「服」神所赐的义，因为接受恩典就要承认自己一无所有。

**4. 「律法的总结就是基督」。** 「律法的总结就是基督，使凡信他的都得着义。」（10:4）「总结」原文 *telos*，可以指「终结」，也可以指「目标」。解经家有不同的侧重：

- 有人强调「终结」：律法作为得义的途径，在基督里已经结束了。
- 有人强调「目标」：律法一直指向基督，在他身上得着成全（参太5:17）。

许多解经家认为两个意思都在其中：基督是律法所指向的目标，所以当他来了，律法作为得义之途的时代也就结束了。无论如何，这节经文的重点在下半句：「使凡信他的都得着义。」

> 要点：真诚和热心本身不能救人。人最深的问题，是想立自己的义，不肯接受神所赐的义；而律法所指向的，正是那位使凡信他的都得着义的基督。

## 深读 10:5-13 口里承认，心里相信

**1. 两种说话的声音。** 保罗让两种「义」各自说话。出于律法的义说：「人若行那出于律法的义，就必因此活着。」（10:5；引利18:5）出于信心的义却说：「你不要心里说：谁要升到天上去呢？……谁要下到阴间去呢？」（10:6-7）这是引用申命记30:12-14。摩西原来说的是，神的诫命不是遥不可及的；保罗说，这话在基督身上得着最深的意义：我们不需要上天去把基督领下来，因为他已经道成肉身；也不需要下到阴间把他领上来，因为神已经叫他从死里复活了。

**2. 这道离你不远。** 「这道离你不远，正在你口里，在你心里。」（10:8）福音不要求人去完成一件不可能的任务。基督已经做成了一切，福音就近在咫尺。

**3. 「耶稣为主」。** 「你若口里认耶稣为主，心里信神叫他从死里复活，就必得救。」（10:9）「耶稣是主」是初期教会最早的信仰告白之一（参林前12:3；腓2:11）。在罗马帝国，人被要求说「凯撒是主」；说「耶稣是主」，就是宣告最高的效忠属于耶稣。而且在希腊文旧约中，「主」正是用来翻译神的圣名「耶和华」的。

**4. 心里与口里。** 「因为，人心里相信就可以称义，口里承认就可以得救。」（10:10）这不是两个分开的步骤，也不是说称义和得救是两回事。这是希伯来诗歌式的平行句：里面的信心和外面的承认是同一件事的两面。真正的信心，不会永远藏在心里不说出来。

**5. 凡求告主名的。** 「犹太人和希腊人并没有分别，因为众人同有一位主；他也厚待一切求告他的人。因为『凡求告主名的就必得救』。」（10:12-13；引珥2:32）约珥书原文的「主」就是「耶和华」；保罗把这句话用在耶稣身上（参10:9）。求告耶稣的名，就是求告耶和华的名。这是基督神性的又一个见证。

> 要点：福音不要求人上天下地去完成不可能的任务，因为基督已经做成了一切。凡口里承认耶稣为主、心里信神叫他复活的人，不分是谁，都必得救。

## 深读 10:14-21 信道是从听道来的

**1. 一条链子。** 「然而，人未曾信他，怎能求他呢？未曾听见他，怎能信他呢？没有传道的，怎能听见呢？若没有奉差遣，怎能传道呢？」（10:14-15）这是一条倒过来的链子：差遣、传道、听见、相信、求告。神拯救人，是藉着人去传。「未曾听见他」原文也可以译作「未曾听见他说话」：在传道人的话中，人听见的是基督自己的声音。

**2. 佳美的脚踪。** 「如经上所记：『报福音、传喜信的人，他们的脚踪何等佳美！』」（10:15；引赛52:7）以赛亚原来说的是报信的人从远方跑来，宣告被掳的百姓要得释放、神要作王。脚走了长路，满是尘土，在等候消息的人眼中却是「佳美」的。

**3. 不是都听从。** 「只是人没有都听从福音，因为以赛亚说：『主啊，我们所传的有谁信呢？』」（10:16；引赛53:1）这句话正是在受苦仆人的诗歌开头说的。传福音的人不是每一次都会看见果效，连先知也曾这样叹息。

**4. 信道从听道来。** 「可见，信道是从听道来的，听道是从基督的话来的。」（10:17）信心不是靠人自己努力产生的，而是神藉着他的话在人心里创造的。

**5. 以色列听见了。** 保罗连续引用旧约，说明以色列人不能推说没有听见或不明白：「他们的声音传遍天下」（10:18；引诗19:4）；神早就藉摩西说，他要用「那不成子民的」惹动以色列的愤恨（10:19；引申32:21）；以赛亚也说，神要被「没有寻找我的」人遇见（10:20；引赛65:1）。

**6. 伸手招呼的神。** 「至于以色列人，他说：『我整天伸手招呼那悖逆顶嘴的百姓。』」（10:21；引赛65:2）这是本章最后一句，也是最动人的一句。以色列人「悖逆顶嘴」，神却「整天伸手」。神的主权，从来不是冷冰冰的；他是一位一直向人伸出双手的神。

> 要点：神拯救人，是藉着人去传他的话，而人在传道中听见的是基督的声音。面对悖逆的百姓，神仍然「整天伸手招呼」。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他们向神有热心，但不是按着真知识。」我的热心，是建立在神所赐的义上，还是悄悄地在「立自己的义」：我的出席、我的奉献、我的服事年数，好像一座座小纪念碑，提醒神和别人我有多好？保罗说，福音不需要我上天下地，因为「这道离你不远」；基督已经做成了，我只需要求告他的名。最后，请再读一次10:21：「我整天伸手招呼那悖逆顶嘴的百姓。」在我的生命中，有没有哪一个方面，我一直在对神「顶嘴」：一个我不肯放下的决定、一处我不肯让他进入的地方？神今天仍然向我伸着手，不是要责打我，而是要领我回家。我能不能今天就放下我的争辩，握住那双为我被钉的手？

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 9 explained, from God's sovereignty, why Israel has not been saved; chapter 10 explains the same thing from human responsibility. Paul doesn't let one deny the other.

The chapter has three parts. **Zeal without knowledge** (vv. 1–4): Paul prays for Israel; they have "a zeal for God," but "seeking to establish their own [righteousness], they did not submit to God's righteousness," because "Christ is the end of the law." **The word is near you** (vv. 5–13): the righteousness of faith doesn't ask anyone to scale heaven or descend to the depths, only to "confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead"; and "everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved," with no distinction between Jew and Greek. **Have they not heard?** (vv. 14–21): to call, people must believe; to believe, they must hear; to hear, someone must preach; yet Israel heard and did not all obey. Finally God says, "All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people."

## Going Deeper: 10:1–4 Two Kinds of Righteousness

**1. Paul's prayer.** "Brothers, my heart's desire and prayer to God for them is that they may be saved" (10:1). Having spoken of the mystery of election, Paul has not stopped praying for Israel. Those who believe in God's sovereignty still pray, and pray more urgently.

**2. Zeal without knowledge.** "I bear them witness that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge" (10:2). Paul himself had been such a man: "as to zeal, a persecutor of the church" (Phil 3:6). Sincerity and zeal cannot save by themselves; headed in the wrong direction, the more zealous we are, the farther we go.

**3. Establishing one's own righteousness.** "For, being ignorant of the righteousness that comes from God, and seeking to establish their own, they did not submit to God's righteousness" (10:3). "Establish" means to set up or erect, like raising a monument to oneself. The human problem is not only doing bad things but trying to use good things to build our standing before God. Such people look as though they are submitting to God, but in fact they refuse to "submit" to the righteousness God gives, because receiving grace means admitting we have nothing.

**4. "Christ is the end of the law."** "For Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes" (10:4). "End" (*telos*) can mean "termination" or "goal." Interpreters place the weight differently:

- Some stress "termination": the law as a way to righteousness has come to an end in Christ.
- Some stress "goal": the law always pointed to Christ and is fulfilled in him (cf. Matt 5:17).

Many think both senses are present: Christ is the goal the law pointed to, and so when he came, the era of the law as a way to righteousness ended. Either way, the weight of the verse lies in its second half: "for righteousness to everyone who believes."

> The point: Sincerity and zeal cannot save by themselves. Our deepest problem is trying to establish our own righteousness and refusing the righteousness God gives; and the law points to Christ, who gives righteousness to everyone who believes.

## Going Deeper: 10:5–13 Confessing with the Mouth, Believing in the Heart

**1. Two voices.** Paul lets two kinds of "righteousness" speak. The righteousness based on the law says that "the person who does the commandments shall live by them" (10:5, quoting Lev 18:5). The righteousness based on faith says, "Do not say in your heart, 'Who will ascend into heaven?'... or 'Who will descend into the abyss?'" (10:6–7). This quotes Deuteronomy 30:12–14. Moses originally said that God's commandment was not out of reach; Paul says the words find their deepest meaning in Christ: we don't need to go up to heaven to bring Christ down, for he has become flesh; nor down into the abyss to bring him up, for God has raised him from the dead.

**2. The word is near you.** "The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart" (10:8). The gospel doesn't ask us to accomplish an impossible task. Christ has done it all, so the gospel is close at hand.

**3. "Jesus is Lord."** "Because, if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved" (10:9). "Jesus is Lord" is one of the church's earliest confessions (cf. 1 Cor 12:3; Phil 2:11). In the Roman Empire people were expected to say "Caesar is Lord"; to say "Jesus is Lord" was to declare that one's highest allegiance belonged to Jesus. And in the Greek Old Testament, "Lord" is the word used to render God's holy name, "the LORD."

**4. Heart and mouth.** "For with the heart one believes and is justified, and with the mouth one confesses and is saved" (10:10). These are not two separate steps, nor is Paul saying justification and salvation are different things. This is Hebrew-style parallelism: inner faith and outward confession are two sides of one thing. Real faith does not stay hidden in the heart forever.

**5. Everyone who calls.** "For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek; the same Lord is Lord of all, bestowing his riches on all who call on him. For 'everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved'" (10:12–13, quoting Joel 2:32). In Joel, "the Lord" is "the LORD"; Paul applies the words to Jesus (cf. 10:9). To call on the name of Jesus is to call on the name of the LORD. This is another witness to Christ's deity.

> The point: The gospel doesn't ask us to scale heaven or descend to the depths, for Christ has done it all. Everyone, whoever they are, who confesses with the mouth that Jesus is Lord and believes in the heart that God raised him, will be saved.

## Going Deeper: 10:14–21 Faith Comes from Hearing

**1. A chain.** "But how are they to call on him in whom they have not believed? And how are they to believe in him of whom they have never heard? And how are they to hear without someone preaching? And how are they to preach unless they are sent?" (10:14–15). The chain runs backward: sending, preaching, hearing, believing, calling. God saves people through people who go and tell. "Of whom they have never heard" can also be read "whom they have never heard": in the preacher's words, people hear the voice of Christ himself.

**2. Beautiful feet.** "As it is written, 'How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the good news!'" (10:15, quoting Isa 52:7). Isaiah pictured a messenger running from far away to announce that the exiles would be freed and that God reigns. Feet that had traveled a long road, covered in dust, looked "beautiful" to those waiting for the news.

**3. Not all obeyed.** "But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says, 'Lord, who has believed what he has heard from us?'" (10:16, quoting Isa 53:1). These words open the song of the suffering servant. Those who share the gospel don't always see results; even the prophet sighed like this.

**4. Faith comes from hearing.** "So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ" (10:17). Faith is not produced by our own effort; God creates it in the heart through his word.

**5. Israel heard.** Paul strings together Old Testament texts to show that Israel cannot plead that they had not heard or understood: "Their voice has gone out to all the earth" (10:18, quoting Ps 19:4); God had said through Moses that he would make Israel jealous "of those who are not a nation" (10:19, quoting Deut 32:21); and Isaiah said that God would be found "by those who did not seek me" (10:20, quoting Isa 65:1).

**6. The God who holds out his hands.** "But of Israel he says, 'All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people'" (10:21, quoting Isa 65:2). This is the chapter's last sentence and its most moving. Israel was "disobedient and contrary," yet God held out his hands "all day long." God's sovereignty is never cold; he is a God who keeps stretching out his hands to people.

> The point: God saves people through people who carry his word, and in their preaching people hear the voice of Christ. Even toward a rebellious people, God holds out his hands "all day long."

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "They have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge." Is my zeal resting on the righteousness God gives, or am I quietly "establishing my own": my attendance, my giving, my years of service, like little monuments reminding God and others how good I am? Paul says the gospel doesn't require me to scale heaven or descend to the depths, because "the word is near you"; Christ has done it, and I need only call on his name. Finally, read 10:21 again: "All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people." Is there some area of my life where I keep "answering back" to God: a decision I won't give up, a place I won't let him into? God is still holding out his hands to me today, not to strike me but to lead me home. Can I put down my arguing today and take hold of the hands that were nailed for me?
