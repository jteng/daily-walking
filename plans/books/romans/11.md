## 本章脉络

第十章末了，神对以色列说：「我整天伸手招呼那悖逆顶嘴的百姓。」读者自然会问：那么，神是不是已经放弃以色列了？第十一章的回答是：「断乎没有！」

本章分四段。**余数**（1-10节）：保罗自己就是证据；正如以利亚的时候神留下七千人，「如今也是这样，照着拣选的恩典，还有所留的余数」。**跌倒不是为了倒下**（11-16节）：以色列的过失，使救恩临到外邦人；而他们将来的「丰满」，更要带来「死而复生」。**橄榄树**（17-24节）：外邦信徒像野橄榄枝被接上，就不可向被折下来的旧枝子夸口；「神的恩慈和严厉」都要看见。**奥秘与颂赞**（25-36节）：「以色列人有几分是硬心的，等到外邦人的数目添满了，于是以色列全家都要得救」；「神将众人都圈在不顺服之中，特意要怜恤众人」。最后，保罗不再论证，而是敬拜：「深哉，神丰富的智慧和知识！」

## 深读 11:1-10 照着拣选的恩典

**1. 保罗自己就是证据。** 「我且说，神弃绝了他的百姓吗？断乎没有！因为我也是以色列人，亚伯拉罕的后裔，属便雅悯支派的。」（11:1）保罗曾经是逼迫教会的人；如果神能救他，就证明神没有弃绝以色列。

**2. 以利亚和七千人。** 以利亚以为「只剩下我一个人」（11:3；引王上19:10），神却说：「我为自己留下七千人，是未曾向巴力屈膝的。」（11:4；引王上19:18）在属灵黑暗的时代，信徒常常以为只剩下自己；神却知道他的子民在哪里。

**3. 恩典就不是恩典了。** 「如今也是这样，照着拣选的恩典，还有所留的余数。既是出于恩典，就不在乎行为；不然，恩典就不是恩典了。」（11:5-6）这是全书对恩典最简洁的定义之一：恩典若加上一点点行为，就不再是恩典了。

**4. 其余的成了顽梗。** 「以色列人所求的，他们没有得着。惟有蒙拣选的人得着了；其余的就成了顽梗不化的。」（11:7）保罗引用申命记29:4、以赛亚书29:10和诗篇69:22-23，说明这种属灵的瞎眼，是神对长期悖逆的审判（参9:18）。

> 要点：在最黑暗的时代，神仍然为自己留下余数，而这余数是「照着拣选的恩典」。恩典若掺杂了行为，就不再是恩典。

## 深读 11:11-16 跌倒不是为了倒下

**1. 断乎不是！** 「我且说，他们失脚是要他们跌倒吗？断乎不是！反倒因他们的过失，救恩便临到外邦人，要激动他们发愤。」（11:11）以色列的「失脚」不是故事的结局。神在他们的过失中，把救恩带给了外邦人；而外邦人蒙福，又要叫以色列人「发愤」，就是生出羡慕，回转归向神（参10:19；申32:21）。

**2. 何况他们的丰满呢？** 「若他们的过失为天下的富足，他们的缺乏为外邦人的富足，何况他们的丰满呢？」（11:12）这又是一个「何况」的推论：以色列的失败已经为世界带来了这么大的福气，他们将来的「丰满」，岂不带来更大的福气？「若他们被丢弃，天下就得与神和好，他们被收纳，岂不是死而复生吗？」（11:15）

**3. 保罗的宣教策略。** 「我对你们外邦人说这话；因我是外邦人的使徒，所以敬重我的职分，或者可以激动我骨肉之亲发愤，好救他们一些人。」（11:13-14）保罗向外邦人传福音，心里却一直记挂着他的同胞。

**4. 圣洁的根。** 「所献的新面若是圣洁，全团也就圣洁了；树根若是圣洁，树枝也就圣洁了。」（11:16）「所献的新面」和「树根」，多数解经家认为是指列祖，就是神与亚伯拉罕、以撒、雅各所立的约（参11:28）。

> 要点：以色列的跌倒不是结局。神在他们的过失中把救恩带给外邦人，又要藉着外邦人蒙福，激动以色列人回转。

## 深读 11:17-24 野橄榄枝

**1. 接在其中。** 「若有几根枝子被折下来，你这野橄榄得接在其中，一同得着橄榄根的肥汁。」（11:17）神的百姓只有一棵橄榄树，根是神对列祖的应许。外邦信徒不是另栽了一棵树，而是被接到这棵树上。所以外邦信徒也承受了旧约的应许，亚伯拉罕也成了他们的父（4:16）。

**2. 不可夸口。** 「你就不可向旧枝子夸口；若是夸口，当知道不是你托着根，乃是根托着你。」（11:18）这是对外邦教会的严肃警告。教会历史上，外邦基督徒常常轻看、甚至逼迫犹太人，这正是保罗在这里所禁止的。

**3. 因信立得住。** 「他们因为不信，所以被折下来；你因为信，所以立得住；你不可自高，反要惧怕。」（11:20）信心本身就排除了骄傲，因为信心承认一切都是领受的（参3:27）。

**4. 恩慈和严厉。** 「可见，神的恩慈和严厉向那跌倒的人是严厉的，向你是有恩慈的，只要你长久在他的恩慈里；不然，你也要被砍下来。」（11:22）这是对整个外邦教会的警告，也是对每一个自称信徒的人的提醒。不同传统对「被砍下来」有不同的理解：改革宗传统认为，真信徒必蒙保守到底（8:30，38-39），这样的警告正是神保守他们的方法之一；阿民念传统则认为，真信徒有可能失落救恩。双方都同意：信心是持续地倚靠神的恩慈，不是一次过的决定之后就可以不管。

**5. 重新接上。** 「而且他们若不是长久不信，仍要被接上，因为神能够把他们重新接上。」（11:23）神的门从来没有向以色列关上。

> 要点：外邦信徒是被接上的枝子，不是我们托着根，乃是根托着我们。我们因信立得住，所以不可自高，反要敬畏，长久住在神的恩慈里。

## 深读 11:25-32 以色列全家都要得救

**1. 奥秘。** 「弟兄们，我不愿意你们不知道这奥秘（恐怕你们自以为聪明），就是以色列人有几分是硬心的，等到外邦人的数目添满了，于是以色列全家都要得救。」（11:25-26）在保罗的用语中，「奥秘」不是神秘难解的事，而是从前隐藏、如今由神启示出来的计划。

**2. 「以色列全家」是谁？** 这是本章最多讨论的问题。主要有三种理解：

- **整个神的子民**：加尔文等人认为，「以色列」在这里指由犹太人和外邦人组成的属灵以色列（参加6:16）；「于是」（原文是「这样」）以色列全家都要得救。
- **历世历代蒙拣选的犹太人**：「以色列全家」指历史中所有的余数加起来，神的拣选必定全部成就。
- **将来犹太民族大规模的归信**：许多现代解经家（包括不少改革宗和时代论的学者）认为，在外邦人的数目添满以后，犹太民族整体要回转归向基督。他们指出，整章的「以色列」都是指犹太民族，与「外邦人」对比（11:25），而且下文说他们「为列祖的缘故是蒙爱的」（11:28）。

三种理解都同意：得救是藉着那位「从锡安出来」的救主（11:26；引赛59:20），藉着神除去罪的约（11:27；参耶31:33-34），而不是另外一条不需要基督的路。

**3. 没有后悔的恩赐。** 「就着福音说，他们为你们的缘故是仇敌；就着拣选说，他们为列祖的缘故是蒙爱的。因为神的恩赐和选召是没有后悔的。」（11:28-29）神对以色列的信实，正是我们对他的信实有信心的根据：他既不收回对以色列的选召，也必不收回对我们的应许（参8:28-39）。

**4. 怜恤众人。** 「因为神将众人都圈在不顺服之中，特意要怜恤众人。」（11:32）这句话总结了第一至十一章：外邦人在不顺服之中（第1章），犹太人也在不顺服之中（第2-3章），神这样做，是要让所有的人都只能靠怜恤得救。这不是说每一个人最后都必得救，而是说无论犹太人或外邦人，得救的唯一途径都是怜恤。

> 要点：神没有弃绝以色列；他的恩赐和选召是没有后悔的。神把众人都圈在不顺服之中，是要叫犹太人和外邦人都只能靠他的怜恤得救。

## 深读 11:33-36 深哉！

**1. 论证变成敬拜。** 「深哉，神丰富的智慧和知识！他的判断何其难测！他的踪迹何其难寻！」（11:33）保罗讲了十一章的道理，最后不是说「我已经把一切都解释清楚了」，而是说「何其难测」。真正的神学，最终会带人俯伏敬拜。

**2. 谁作过他的谋士？** 「谁知道主的心？谁作过他的谋士呢？」（11:34；引赛40:13）神的计划不需要人的建议。第九至十一章有许多我们无法完全明白的地方，可是我们可以信靠那位全知的神。

**3. 谁先给了他？** 「谁是先给了他，使他后来偿还呢？」（11:35；参伯41:11）没有人能叫神欠他的债。我们所有的，都是从他领受的；所以救恩从头到尾都是恩典。

**4. 本于他，倚靠他，归于他。** 「因为万有都是本于他，倚靠他，归于他。愿荣耀归给他，直到永远。阿们！」（11:36）万物从神而来（创造），靠神而存（护理），也要归向神（目的）。人生和历史的意义，不在于人，而在于神的荣耀。

> 要点：神的计划深不可测，可是他的智慧和怜悯是可以信靠的。没有人能叫神欠他的债；万有都是本于他，倚靠他，归于他。

## 默想

**默想：** 「谁是先给了他，使他后来偿还呢？」华人社会很讲「人情」：你帮过我，我就欠你一份情，总要找机会还。我对神是否也有这样的心态：我奉献了、服事了、忍耐了，神就该照顾我的家、保守我的工作，好像他欠我一份人情？保罗说，没有人能先给神什么；我手中所有的，连我的信心，都是他先给的。这个真理一面拆掉我的骄傲，一面也释放我：我不需要再跟神算账，可以单单因为恩典而感恩。再想想11:18：「不是你托着根，乃是根托着你。」我对那些还没有信、甚至反对福音的人，是否暗暗有一种「我比你强」的心？我能站立得住，只是因为神的恩慈。今天，我能不能也和保罗一起，把一切说不清楚的事放下，单单说：「愿荣耀归给他，直到永远。阿们！」

## The Shape of the Chapter

At the end of chapter 10 God said of Israel, "All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people." A reader will naturally ask: has God then given up on Israel? Chapter 11 answers, "By no means!"

The chapter has four parts. **The remnant** (vv. 1–10): Paul himself is the evidence; as in Elijah's day God kept seven thousand, "so too at the present time there is a remnant, chosen by grace." **Stumbling, but not to fall** (vv. 11–16): Israel's trespass has brought salvation to the Gentiles, and their future "full inclusion" will mean "life from the dead." **The olive tree** (vv. 17–24): Gentile believers, grafted in like wild olive shoots, must not boast over the branches broken off; they must see both "the kindness and the severity of God." **The mystery and the doxology** (vv. 25–36): "a partial hardening has come upon Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. And in this way all Israel will be saved"; "God has consigned all to disobedience, that he may have mercy on all." At the end Paul stops arguing and worships: "Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God!"

## Going Deeper: 11:1–10 Chosen by Grace

**1. Paul himself is the evidence.** "I ask, then, has God rejected his people? By no means! For I myself am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, a member of the tribe of Benjamin" (11:1). Paul had persecuted the church; if God could save him, God had not rejected Israel.

**2. Elijah and the seven thousand.** Elijah thought "I alone am left" (11:3, quoting 1 Kgs 19:10), but God said, "I have kept for myself seven thousand men who have not bowed the knee to Baal" (11:4, quoting 1 Kgs 19:18). In dark times believers often think they are the only ones left; God knows where his people are.

**3. Otherwise grace would no longer be grace.** "So too at the present time there is a remnant, chosen by grace. But if it is by grace, it is no longer on the basis of works; otherwise grace would no longer be grace" (11:5–6). This is one of the letter's most concise definitions of grace: add even a little bit of works, and grace is no longer grace.

**4. The rest were hardened.** "What then? Israel failed to obtain what it was seeking. The elect obtained it, but the rest were hardened" (11:7). Paul quotes Deuteronomy 29:4, Isaiah 29:10 and Psalm 69:22–23 to show that such spiritual blindness is God's judgment on long rebellion (cf. 9:18).

> The point: Even in the darkest times God keeps a remnant for himself, "chosen by grace." Grace mixed with works is no longer grace.

## Going Deeper: 11:11–16 Stumbling, but Not to Fall

**1. By no means!** "So I ask, did they stumble in order that they might fall? By no means! Rather through their trespass salvation has come to the Gentiles, so as to make Israel jealous" (11:11). Israel's "stumble" is not the end of the story. Through their trespass God brought salvation to the Gentiles, and the Gentiles' blessing is meant to make Israel "jealous," moved with longing to return to God (cf. 10:19; Deut 32:21).

**2. How much more their full inclusion!** "Now if their trespass means riches for the world, and if their failure means riches for the Gentiles, how much more will their full inclusion mean!" (11:12). Another "how much more": if Israel's failure has brought such blessing to the world, how much greater blessing will their future "full inclusion" bring? "For if their rejection means the reconciliation of the world, what will their acceptance mean but life from the dead?" (11:15).

**3. Paul's mission strategy.** "Now I am speaking to you Gentiles. Inasmuch then as I am an apostle to the Gentiles, I magnify my ministry in order somehow to make my fellow Jews jealous, and thus save some of them" (11:13–14). Paul preached to Gentiles, yet his heart never stopped carrying his own people.

**4. A holy root.** "If the dough offered as firstfruits is holy, so is the whole lump, and if the root is holy, so are the branches" (11:16). Most interpreters take "the firstfruits" and "the root" to be the patriarchs, that is, God's covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob (cf. 11:28).

> The point: Israel's stumbling is not the end. Through their trespass God brought salvation to the Gentiles, and through the Gentiles' blessing he means to move Israel to return.

## Going Deeper: 11:17–24 The Wild Olive Shoot

**1. Grafted in among them.** "But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, although a wild olive shoot, were grafted in among the others and now share in the nourishing root of the olive tree" (11:17). God's people are one olive tree, whose root is God's promise to the patriarchs. Gentile believers are not a new tree planted alongside, but shoots grafted into this one. So Gentile believers also inherit the Old Testament promises, and Abraham becomes their father too (4:16).

**2. Do not boast.** "Do not be arrogant toward the branches. If you are, remember it is not you who support the root, but the root that supports you" (11:18). This is a solemn warning to the Gentile church. In church history Gentile Christians have often despised and even persecuted Jews, exactly what Paul forbids here.

**3. Standing by faith.** "They were broken off because of their unbelief, but you stand fast through faith. So do not become proud, but stand in awe" (11:20). Faith itself excludes pride, because faith confesses that everything is received (cf. 3:27).

**4. Kindness and severity.** "Note then the kindness and the severity of God: severity toward those who have fallen, but God's kindness to you, provided you continue in his kindness. Otherwise you too will be cut off" (11:22). This is a warning to the whole Gentile church, and a reminder to everyone who claims to believe. Traditions understand "cut off" differently: the Reformed tradition holds that true believers will be kept to the end (8:30, 38–39), and that warnings like this are one of the means by which God keeps them; the Arminian tradition holds that true believers can lose their salvation. Both agree that faith means continuing to rely on God's kindness, not a one-time decision that can then be ignored.

**5. Grafted in again.** "And even they, if they do not continue in their unbelief, will be grafted in, for God has the power to graft them in again" (11:23). God's door has never been shut to Israel.

> The point: Gentile believers are grafted branches: it is not we who support the root, but the root that supports us. We stand by faith, so we must not be proud but stand in awe, continuing in God's kindness.

## Going Deeper: 11:25–32 All Israel Will Be Saved

**1. A mystery.** "Lest you be wise in your own conceits, I want you to understand this mystery, brothers: a partial hardening has come upon Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. And in this way all Israel will be saved" (11:25–26). In Paul's usage, a "mystery" is not something obscure but a plan once hidden and now revealed by God.

**2. Who is "all Israel"?** This is the chapter's most debated question. There are three main views:

- **The whole people of God:** Calvin and others take "Israel" here as the spiritual Israel of Jews and Gentiles together (cf. Gal 6:16); "in this way" all Israel will be saved.
- **The elect Jews of every generation:** "all Israel" is the sum of the remnant through history; God's election will be fully accomplished.
- **A future large-scale turning of the Jewish people:** many modern interpreters (including a good number of Reformed and dispensational scholars) think that after the fullness of the Gentiles comes in, the Jewish people as a whole will turn to Christ. They note that "Israel" throughout the chapter means the Jewish people, in contrast to "the Gentiles" (11:25), and that they are "beloved for the sake of their forefathers" (11:28).

All three agree that salvation comes through the Deliverer "from Zion" (11:26, quoting Isa 59:20) and through God's covenant to take away sins (11:27; cf. Jer 31:33–34), not through some other way that bypasses Christ.

**3. Irrevocable gifts.** "As regards the gospel, they are enemies of God for your sake. But as regards election, they are beloved for the sake of their forefathers. For the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable" (11:28–29). God's faithfulness to Israel is the very ground of our confidence in his faithfulness: he will not withdraw his calling of Israel, nor his promises to us (cf. 8:28–39).

**4. Mercy on all.** "For God has consigned all to disobedience, that he may have mercy on all" (11:32). This sums up chapters 1–11: Gentiles are in disobedience (ch. 1), and Jews too (chs. 2–3); God has done this so that everyone can be saved only by mercy. This does not mean every person will finally be saved, but that for Jew and Gentile alike, the only way of salvation is mercy.

> The point: God has not rejected Israel; his gifts and calling are irrevocable. God has consigned all to disobedience so that Jew and Gentile alike can be saved only by his mercy.

## Going Deeper: 11:33–36 Oh, the Depth!

**1. Argument becomes worship.** "Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways!" (11:33). After eleven chapters of argument, Paul doesn't say, "Now I have explained everything," but "how unsearchable." True theology ends in worship.

**2. Who has been his counselor?** "For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor?" (11:34, quoting Isa 40:13). God's plan needs no human advice. Much in chapters 9–11 lies beyond our full understanding, but we can trust the all-knowing God.

**3. Who has given to him first?** "Or who has given a gift to him that he might be repaid?" (11:35; cf. Job 41:11). No one can put God in their debt. Everything we have, we have received from him; so salvation is grace from first to last.

**4. From him, through him, to him.** "For from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be glory forever. Amen" (11:36). All things come from God (creation), are sustained by God (providence) and return to God (purpose). The meaning of life and history lies not in us but in God's glory.

> The point: God's plan is beyond our searching out, but his wisdom and mercy can be trusted. No one can put God in their debt; from him and through him and to him are all things.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Who has given a gift to him that he might be repaid?" Chinese society runs on *renqing*, the debt of favors: if you have helped me, I owe you, and I will look for a way to repay. Do I treat God the same way: I have given, served and endured, so God ought to look after my family and protect my job, as though he owed me a favor? Paul says no one can give God anything first; everything in my hands, even my faith, he gave me first. This truth tears down my pride and also sets me free: I no longer need to keep accounts with God, and can simply give thanks for grace. Think too of 11:18: "it is not you who support the root, but the root that supports you." Toward those who don't yet believe, or even oppose the gospel, do I secretly feel "I'm better than you"? I stand only by God's kindness. Today, can I lay down everything I cannot explain and say with Paul, "To him be glory forever. Amen"?
