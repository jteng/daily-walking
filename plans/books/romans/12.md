## 本章脉络

第十二章开始了罗马书的最后一大段（12–16章）。前十一章讲神为我们做了什么，从这里开始讲我们因此怎样生活。第一个字就是「所以」：基督徒的生活，是对神慈悲的回应。

本章分四段。**活祭**（1-2节）：「我以神的慈悲劝你们，将身体献上，当作活祭」；「不要效法这个世界，只要心意更新而变化」。**一个身体，许多肢体**（3-8节）：「不要看自己过于所当看的」；我们在基督里成为一身，各人照所得的恩赐彼此服事。**真实的爱**（9-16节）：「爱人不可虚假」；彼此亲热、彼此推让，与喜乐的人同乐，与哀哭的人同哭。**以善胜恶**（17-21节）：「不要以恶报恶」；「伸冤在我，我必报应」；「你不可为恶所胜，反要以善胜恶。」

## 深读 12:1-2 理所当然的事奉

**1. 「所以」与「神的慈悲」。** 「所以，弟兄们，我以神的慈悲劝你们，将身体献上，当作活祭。」（12:1）「慈悲」原文是复数，可能是沿用希伯来文的说法，表达神怜悯的丰盛（参林后1:3「发慈悲的父」）；而「所以」把它连到前面所讲的一切怜悯：称义、与神相和、儿子的名分、圣灵的内住、不能隔绝的爱（3–8章），一直到11:30-32神对犹太人和外邦人的怜恤。保罗劝勉的根据不是惧怕，而是神的慈悲。基督徒的顺服，不是为了换取神的恩典，而是因为已经领受了恩典。

**2. 活祭。** 旧约的祭物被杀、献在坛上；如今神要的是「活祭」：不是一次性的献上，而是一生每一天把整个人交给他。「身体」表示这献上是具体的：在家里、在工作中、在我们怎样说话、怎样用时间上。

**3. 「理所当然的」事奉。** 「你们如此事奉乃是理所当然的。」（12:1）原文 *logikē latreia*，有不同的翻译：和合本作「理所当然的」，英文ESV译本作「属灵的敬拜」，也有人译作「合理的事奉」。几个意思可以并存：在神这样的慈悲面前，把自己献上是唯一合理的回应；而且这敬拜不只在礼拜堂里，也在整个生活中。

**4. 不要效法，只要变化。** 「不要效法这个世界，只要心意更新而变化，叫你们察验何为神的善良、纯全、可喜悦的旨意。」（12:2）「效法」是被压进一个模子里；「变化」原文就是福音书描写耶稣「变了形象」的那个字（太17:2），保罗在林后3:18也用这个字描写信徒被神改变。这个世界不断地塑造我们：什么才算成功，什么才值得追求：名校、高薪、买房、让父母有面子。神的方法，是从里面更新我们的心意，叫我们能「察验」，也就是在实际的生活中分辨并认同神的旨意。

> 要点：基督徒的生活是对神慈悲的回应。在这样的慈悲面前，把整个人献给神，是唯一合理的敬拜；而神从里面更新我们的心意，叫我们不再被这个世界的模子塑造。

## 深读 12:3-8 一个身体，许多肢体

**1. 看得合乎中道。** 「我凭着所赐我的恩对你们各人说：不要看自己过于所当看的；要照着神所分给各人信心的大小，看得合乎中道。」（12:3）这是心意更新后的第一个表现：对自己有清醒的认识。不是过高，也不是过低，而是照着神所给的来看自己。

**2. 一个身子。** 「正如我们一个身子上有好些肢体，肢体也不都是一样的用处。我们这许多人，在基督里成为一身，互相联络作肢体，也是如此。」（12:4-5）要留意「互相联络作肢体」：我们不只是基督的肢体，也是「互相」的肢体。我需要你，你也需要我。

**3. 不同的恩赐。** 保罗列出七种恩赐：说预言、作执事、教导、劝化、施舍、治理、怜悯人（12:6-8）。这份清单并不完整（参林前12章；弗4:11），里面有看起来很显眼的恩赐，也有不显眼的。每一种都要用心去做：施舍要「诚实」（原文也有「慷慨、单纯」的意思），治理要「殷勤」，怜悯人要「甘心」。

**4. 恩赐是恩典。** 「按我们所得的恩赐，各有不同。」（12:6）「恩赐」（*charismata*）和「恩典」（*charis*）在原文同出一个字根。恩赐不是我们的成就，而是神的礼物，所以没有人可以因为恩赐而骄傲，也没有人可以因为自己的恩赐不显眼而自卑。

> 要点：恩赐是神的恩典，不是我们的成就。在基督的身体里，我们不只属于基督，也彼此相属；没有人可以骄傲，也没有人是多余的。

## 深读 12:9-16 爱人不可虚假

**1. 不可虚假。** 「爱人不可虚假。恶，要厌恶；善，要亲近。」（12:9）「不可虚假」原文是「没有假冒」（*anypokritos*），与「假冒为善」同一字根；这个字原指舞台上戴面具演戏的人。爱不能是一场表演。真实的爱也不是不分是非，而是厌恶恶、亲近善。

**2. 彼此推让。** 「爱弟兄，要彼此亲热；恭敬人，要彼此推让。」（12:10）「亲热」原文是家人之间的爱。「彼此推让」原文的意思是「在尊重别人上要争先」：基督徒若要争，就争着尊重别人。华人很懂得「给面子」，也讲究谦让；可是我们给人面子，常常是为了保住自己的面子，或者换取别人将来还我面子，表面推让，心里却在计较排位。保罗说的不是客套，而是真心看重别人，连那些不能回报我们的人也包括在内（参12:16）。

**3. 火热、喜乐、忍耐、恒切。** 「殷勤，不可懒惰；要心里火热，常常服事主。在指望中要喜乐；在患难中要忍耐；祷告要恒切。」（12:11-12）「心里火热」原文也可以理解为「在圣灵里火热」。这几句话描写的是一个被神的慈悲点燃的生命。

**4. 款待。** 「圣徒缺乏，要帮补；客，要一味地款待。」（12:13）「款待」原文是「爱陌生人」。在当时，旅行的信徒常常要靠各地的弟兄姊妹接待。

**5. 同乐与同哭。** 「与喜乐的人要同乐；与哀哭的人要同哭。」（12:15）有人说，与哀哭的人同哭比较容易，与喜乐的人同乐反而更难，因为别人的喜乐常常引起我们的嫉妒。真实的爱，能为别人的成功真心高兴，比如亲戚的孩子考上了名校。

**6. 俯就卑微的人。** 「要彼此同心；不要志气高大，倒要俯就卑微的人。不要自以为聪明。」（12:16）在罗马的教会里，有奴隶也有自由人，有穷人也有富人。爱的意思，是愿意和那些在社会上没有地位的人坐在一起。

> 要点：真实的爱不是一场表演，它的源头是神在基督里先真实地爱了我们。基督既替我们担当了羞辱，又把神儿女的尊荣赐给我们，我们就不必再争着要面子，反而可以争着尊重别人：厌恶恶、亲近善，能与哀哭的人同哭，也能为别人的喜乐真心高兴。

## 深读 12:17-21 以善胜恶

**1. 不要以恶报恶。** 「不要以恶报恶；众人以为美的事要留心去做。若是能行，总要尽力与众人和睦。」（12:17-18）「若是能行」承认和睦不总是可能的，因为它需要双方；可是我们要尽到自己那一方的力。和睦也不是假装没事，或者让人继续伤害自己。

**2. 伸冤在我。** 「亲爱的弟兄，不要自己伸冤，宁可让步，听凭主怒；因为经上记着：『主说：「伸冤在我，我必报应。」』」（12:19；引申32:35）保罗没有说恶不重要，也没有说公义不会来到。他是说，审判属于神，不属于我们。把伸冤交给神，不是软弱，而是信心：相信神是公义的审判者，任何恶都逃不过他。这里禁止的是私人报复，并没有否定公义的审判：保罗紧接着说，掌权的「是神的用人，是伸冤的，刑罚那作恶的」（13:4）。所以，受害的人报警、寻求法律保护，并不违背这节经文。

**3. 炭火堆在头上。** 「所以，『你的仇敌若饿了，就给他吃，若渴了，就给他喝；因为你这样行就是把炭火堆在他的头上。』」（12:20；引箴25:21-22）「把炭火堆在他的头上」，解经家有不同的理解：

- 多数解经家认为，这是指叫对方感到羞愧、良心受责，以致可能悔改。
- 也有人认为，这是指若对方始终不悔改，他所受的审判就更重；他们指出旧约中炭火多表示审判（诗140:10），而上一节刚讲到「听凭主怒」。

从下一节「以善胜恶」来看，前一种理解更合乎上下文：我们的目的，是要胜过恶，而不是要胜过人。

**4. 以善胜恶。** 「你不可为恶所胜，反要以善胜恶。」（12:21）以恶报恶，看起来是赢了，其实是被恶胜过了，因为我们变得和那个伤害我们的人一样。基督自己就是这样胜过恶的：「他被骂不还口；受害不说威吓的话，只将自己交托那按公义审判人的主。」（彼前2:23）

> 要点：把伸冤交给神不是软弱，而是信心。以恶报恶，就是被恶胜过；基督在十字架上以善胜恶，而我们自己就是这样被他的善胜过的；因着他，我们也能走同一条路。

## 默想

**默想：** 「所以，弟兄们，我以神的慈悲劝你们。」我对神的顺服，是出于害怕、出于责任，还是出于被他的慈悲感动？请慢慢回想前十一章的「慈悲」：我原是神的仇敌，他却为我舍了儿子；我原该被定罪，他却称我为义；我原是罪的奴仆，他却让我叫他「阿爸」。再想想12:19：「不要自己伸冤。」有没有一个人，我心里一直在等他得报应：一个出卖我的同事、一个伤害过我的亲人、一个从来没有道歉的朋友？神没有叫我假装不痛，也没有说这件事不要紧；正因为他是公义的，我才可以把伸冤的事交给他。如果我受过虐待、暴力或犯罪的伤害，向警察、法院、社工或可信任的人求助，寻求保护和公道，并不是「自己伸冤」，而是神所设立的保护（13:4）；不报复，也不等于要继续留在危险之中，或者必须马上和一个不安全的人和好。我也可以为那个人祷告，因为我自己就是一个蒙了怜悯的仇敌（5:10）。今天，我能不能为那个人做一件小小的善事？如果对方对我并不安全，这件善事不需要靠近他：可以是为他祷告，也可以是放下想要他受苦的念头。不是为了让他知道我有多好，而是因为基督也是这样待我的。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 12 begins the final major section of Romans (chs. 12–16). The first eleven chapters told what God has done for us; from here Paul tells how we therefore live. The chapter opens with "therefore": the Christian life is a response to God's mercy.

The chapter has four parts. **A living sacrifice** (vv. 1–2): "I appeal to you therefore, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice"; "do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind." **One body, many members** (vv. 3–8): "not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think"; we are one body in Christ, serving one another with the gifts each has received. **Genuine love** (vv. 9–16): "Let love be genuine"; brotherly affection, outdoing one another in showing honor, rejoicing with those who rejoice and weeping with those who weep. **Overcoming evil with good** (vv. 17–21): "Repay no one evil for evil"; "Vengeance is mine, I will repay"; "Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good."

## Going Deeper: 12:1–2 Spiritual Worship

**1. "Therefore" and "the mercies of God."** "I appeal to you therefore, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice" (12:1). "Mercies" is plural, probably following Hebrew idiom for the fullness of God's compassion (cf. 2 Cor 1:3, "Father of mercies"); and "therefore" ties it to all the mercy Paul has described: justification, peace with God, adoption, the indwelling Spirit, the love nothing can separate us from (chs. 3–8), and God's plan to have mercy on all (11:30–32). Paul does not ground his appeal in fear but in God's mercies. Christian obedience is not a way to earn grace but a response to grace already received.

**2. A living sacrifice.** Old Testament sacrifices were killed and placed on the altar; now God wants a "living sacrifice": not a one-time offering, but giving our whole selves to him every day of our lives. "Bodies" makes the offering concrete: at home, at work, in how we speak and spend our time.

**3. "Spiritual worship."** "Which is your spiritual worship" (12:1). The phrase *logikē latreia* is translated in different ways: the ESV has "spiritual worship," the CUV "service that is only right" (理所当然的), and others "reasonable service." The senses can stand together: in the face of such mercy, offering ourselves is the only reasonable response; and this worship is not only in the church building but in the whole of life.

**4. Not conformed, but transformed.** "Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect" (12:2). "Conformed" means pressed into a mold; "transformed" is the word the Gospels use for Jesus being "transfigured" (Matt 17:2), and Paul uses it in 2 Cor 3:18 of believers being changed by God. This world constantly shapes us: what counts as success, what is worth pursuing: the top school, the high salary, the apartment, making our parents proud. God's way is to renew our minds from within, so that we can "discern," that is, test and approve God's will in actual life.

> The point: The Christian life is a response to God's mercies. In the face of such mercy, offering our whole selves to God is the only reasonable worship; and God renews our minds from within, so that the world's mold no longer shapes us.

## Going Deeper: 12:3–8 One Body, Many Members

**1. Sober judgment.** "For by the grace given to me I say to everyone among you not to think of himself more highly than he ought to think, but to think with sober judgment, each according to the measure of faith that God has assigned" (12:3). The first sign of a renewed mind is a clear-eyed view of oneself: not too high and not too low, but according to what God has given.

**2. One body.** "For as in one body we have many members, and the members do not all have the same function, so we, though many, are one body in Christ, and individually members one of another" (12:4–5). Notice "members one of another": we are not only members of Christ but members of each other. I need you, and you need me.

**3. Different gifts.** Paul lists seven gifts: prophecy, service, teaching, exhortation, giving, leading and showing mercy (12:6–8). The list is not complete (cf. 1 Cor 12; Eph 4:11); it includes prominent gifts and quiet ones. Each is to be exercised wholeheartedly: giving "in generosity" (the word also carries the sense of "single-heartedly"), leading "with zeal," acts of mercy "with cheerfulness."

**4. Gifts are grace.** "Having gifts that differ according to the grace given to us" (12:6). "Gifts" (*charismata*) and "grace" (*charis*) share a root. Gifts are not our achievements but God's presents, so no one may be proud of their gift, and no one need feel inferior because their gift is unnoticed.

> The point: Gifts are God's grace, not our achievements. In the body of Christ we belong not only to Christ but to one another; no one may be proud, and no one is superfluous.

## Going Deeper: 12:9–16 Let Love Be Genuine

**1. Genuine.** "Let love be genuine. Abhor what is evil; hold fast to what is good" (12:9). "Genuine" is literally "unhypocritical"; a "hypocrite" was originally a masked actor on the stage. Love cannot be a performance. Nor is genuine love without moral clarity: it hates evil and clings to good.

**2. Outdoing one another in honor.** "Love one another with brotherly affection. Outdo one another in showing honor" (12:10). "Brotherly affection" is the love of family. If Christians compete, it should be to honor others. Chinese culture knows well how to "give face" and to defer politely; but we often give face to protect our own, or to be repaid later, deferring outwardly while still counting rank inwardly. Paul means not etiquette but genuinely valuing others, including those who can never repay us (cf. 12:16).

**3. Zeal, joy, patience, prayer.** "Do not be slothful in zeal, be fervent in spirit, serve the Lord. Rejoice in hope, be patient in tribulation, be constant in prayer" (12:11–12). "Fervent in spirit" may also be read "aglow with the Spirit." These lines describe a life set alight by God's mercies.

**4. Hospitality.** "Contribute to the needs of the saints and seek to show hospitality" (12:13). "Hospitality" is literally "love of strangers." In those days traveling believers often depended on being welcomed by brothers and sisters in each place.

**5. Rejoicing and weeping together.** "Rejoice with those who rejoice, weep with those who weep" (12:15). It has been said that weeping with those who weep is easier than rejoicing with those who rejoice, because others' joy often stirs our envy. Genuine love can be truly glad at someone else's success, such as a relative's child getting into a top school.

**6. Associating with the lowly.** "Live in harmony with one another. Do not be haughty, but associate with the lowly. Never be conceited" (12:16). The Roman church included slaves and free, poor and rich. Love means being willing to sit with those who have no standing in society.

> The point: Genuine love is no performance; its source is the genuine love God first showed us in Christ. Because Christ bore our shame and gave us the honor of God's children, we no longer need to compete for face but can compete to honor others; such love hates evil and clings to good, weeps with those who weep, and is truly glad at others' joy.

## Going Deeper: 12:17–21 Overcoming Evil with Good

**1. Repay no one evil for evil.** "Repay no one evil for evil, but give thought to do what is honorable in the sight of all. If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all" (12:17–18). "If possible" admits that peace is not always achievable, because it takes two; but we must do our part. Peace does not mean pretending nothing happened, or letting someone keep harming us.

**2. Vengeance is mine.** "Beloved, never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God, for it is written, 'Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord'" (12:19, quoting Deut 32:35). Paul doesn't say that evil doesn't matter, or that justice will never come. He says judgment belongs to God, not to us. Leaving vengeance to God is not weakness but faith: trusting that God is the righteous judge and that no evil will escape him. What is forbidden is personal revenge, not the just judgment carried out by lawful authority: Paul goes on to call the governing authority "the servant of God, an avenger who carries out God's wrath on the wrongdoer" (13:4). So a victim who reports a crime or seeks legal protection is not disobeying this verse.

**3. Burning coals on his head.** "To the contrary, 'if your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him something to drink; for by so doing you will heap burning coals on his head'" (12:20, quoting Prov 25:21–22). Interpreters understand "burning coals" in different ways:

- Most take it to mean causing the enemy to feel shame and a stricken conscience, which may lead to repentance.
- Others take it to mean that if the enemy never repents, his judgment will be heavier, noting that coals in the OT usually signal judgment (Ps 140:10) and that v. 19 has just spoken of God's wrath.

The next verse, "overcome evil with good," makes the first reading fit the context better: our aim is to overcome evil, not to beat a person.

**4. Overcoming evil with good.** "Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good" (12:21). Repaying evil for evil looks like winning, but in fact it means being overcome by evil, because we become like the one who hurt us. This is how Christ himself overcame evil: "When he was reviled, he did not revile in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but continued entrusting himself to him who judges justly" (1 Pet 2:23).

> The point: Leaving vengeance to God is not weakness but faith. To repay evil with evil is to be overcome by evil; Christ overcame evil with good at the cross, and we ourselves were won over by that goodness; because of him, we can walk the same road.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "I appeal to you therefore, brothers, by the mercies of God." Does my obedience to God come from fear, from duty, or from being moved by his mercy? Slowly recall the "mercies" of chapters 1–11: I was God's enemy, yet he gave his Son for me; I deserved condemnation, yet he justified me; I was a slave of sin, yet he lets me call him "Abba." Think too of 12:19: "never avenge yourselves." Is there someone I have been quietly waiting to see get what they deserve: a coworker who betrayed me, a relative who hurt me, a friend who never apologized? God does not ask me to pretend it didn't hurt or didn't matter; it is because he is just that I can hand vengeance over to him. If I have suffered abuse, violence or crime, going to the police, the courts, a social worker or someone I trust for protection and justice is not "avenging myself"; it is part of the protection God has provided (13:4). Refusing revenge does not mean staying in danger, or reconciling at once with someone who is not safe. And I can pray for that person, because I myself am an enemy who received mercy (5:10). Today, can I do one small good thing for that person? If they are not safe for me, that good thing need not bring me near them: it may be a prayer for them, or letting go of my wish to see them suffer. Not to show them how good I am, but because this is how Christ has treated me.
