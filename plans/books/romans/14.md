## 本章脉络

第十二章说「爱人不可虚假」，第十三章说「爱就完全了律法」。第十四章就把这爱带进罗马教会一个具体的难题里：信徒对一些次要的事看法不同，怎样彼此相处？

本章分三段。**不可论断，也不可轻看**（1-12节）：有人「信百物都可吃」，有人「只吃蔬菜」；有人「看这日比那日强」，有人「看日日都是一样」。保罗说，「吃的人不可轻看不吃的人；不吃的人不可论断吃的人；因为神已经收纳他了」，而且「我们都要站在神的台前」。**不要叫弟兄跌倒**（13-18节）：「凡物本来没有不洁净的」，可是「你若因食物叫弟兄忧愁，就不是按着爱人的道理行」；「神的国不在乎吃喝，只在乎公义、和平，并圣灵中的喜乐。」**追求和睦，彼此建立**（19-23节）：「不可因食物毁坏神的工程」；「凡不出于信心的都是罪。」

## 深读 14:1-12 神已经收纳他了

**1. 谁是「软弱的」？** 「信心软弱的，你们要接纳，但不要辩论所疑惑的事。有人信百物都可吃；但那软弱的，只吃蔬菜。」（14:1-2）从上下文看，「软弱的」多半是一些犹太背景的信徒（也可能包括一些外邦信徒），他们的良心仍然受摩西律法中饮食和节期的约束；在外邦城市里，他们无法确定市场上的肉是否洁净，或者是否祭过偶像，所以只吃蔬菜。「刚强的」则明白在基督里「凡物本来没有不洁净的」（14:14）。保罗自己属于「刚强的」（15:1），可是他把大部分的劝勉都给了刚强的人。

**2. 两种不同的试探。** 「吃的人不可轻看不吃的人；不吃的人不可论断吃的人；因为神已经收纳他了。」（14:3）刚强的人容易「轻看」软弱的人，觉得他们幼稚、守旧；软弱的人容易「论断」刚强的人，觉得他们随便、不属灵。保罗对双方都有提醒，而理由只有一个：神已经收纳了他。我怎能拒绝神已经接纳的人？

**3. 别人的仆人。** 「你是谁，竟论断别人的仆人呢？他或站住或跌倒，自有他的主人在；而且他也必要站住，因为主能使他站住。」（14:4）每一个信徒都是基督的仆人，不是我的仆人。主人是基督，不是我。

**4. 守日。** 「有人看这日比那日强；有人看日日都是一样。只是各人心里要意见坚定。」（14:5）这里的「日」，多半是指犹太的安息日和节期。至于这是否也包括主日，解经家有不同看法：有人认为主日不在争论之列，因为它是初期教会共同的敬拜日（徒20:7；林前16:2）；也有人认为保罗在这里给予更大的自由。无论如何，保罗的重点是：在这类事上，要在神面前有清楚的良心。

**5. 为主而活。** 「我们没有一个人为自己活，也没有一个人为自己死。我们若活着，是为主而活；若死了，是为主而死。所以，我们或活或死总是主的人。」（14:7-8）吃的人是「为主吃的」，不吃的人是「为主不吃的」（14:6）。关键不在于吃不吃，而在于是否「为主」。

**6. 都要站在神的台前。** 「你这个人，为什么论断弟兄呢？又为什么轻看弟兄呢？因我们都要站在神的台前。」（14:10）保罗引用以赛亚书45:23：「万膝必向我跪拜；万口必向我承认。」（14:11）我们不是审判官，而是将来要站在审判台前的人。「我们各人必要将自己的事在神面前说明」（14:12），不是替别人说明。

> 要点：在次要的事上，刚强的人不可轻看软弱的人，软弱的人也不可论断刚强的人，因为神已经收纳了他。我们不是别人的主人，而是同样要站在神台前的仆人。

## 深读 14:13-18 神的国不在乎吃喝

**1. 不放绊脚石。** 「所以，我们不可再彼此论断，宁可定意谁也不给弟兄放下绊脚跌人之物。」（14:13）保罗用同一个字玩了一个文字游戏：不要「论断」别人，倒要「定意」（原文是同一个字）不叫人跌倒。

**2. 凡物本来没有不洁净的。** 「我凭着主耶稣确知深信，凡物本来没有不洁净的；惟独人以为不洁净的，在他就不洁净了。」（14:14）这呼应了耶稣的教导：入口的不能污秽人（可7:19）。可是保罗立刻补充：一个人若认为某样东西不洁净，他吃了就违背了自己的良心。

**3. 基督已经替他死。** 「你若因食物叫弟兄忧愁，就不是按着爱人的道理行。基督已经替他死，你不可因你的食物叫他败坏。」（14:15）这是全章最有力的一句话。基督为了这位软弱的弟兄连生命都舍了，我难道连一顿饭都不肯为他放下吗？「败坏」是很重的词：保罗关心的不只是对方的感受，而是他属灵的生命，就是被引诱去做违背自己良心的事。

**4. 神的国在乎什么。** 「因为神的国不在乎吃喝，只在乎公义、和平，并圣灵中的喜乐。在这几样上服事基督的，就为神所喜悦，又为人所称许。」（14:17-18）教会很容易为次要的事争吵，却忘了神国真正的中心：与神和好而有的义，与人之间的和平，以及圣灵所赐的喜乐。

> 要点：基督既为软弱的弟兄舍了生命，我也应当为他放下自己的自由。神的国不在乎吃喝，只在乎公义、和平，并圣灵中的喜乐。

## 深读 14:19-23 凡不出于信心的都是罪

**1. 追求和睦，彼此建立。** 「所以，我们务要追求和睦的事与彼此建立德行的事。不可因食物毁坏神的工程。」（14:19-20）「建立」是盖房子的用语，「神的工程」就是神在弟兄姊妹身上的工作。我们可能为了一点小事，拆毁神正在建造的人。

**2. 自由的限制。** 「无论是吃肉是喝酒，是什么别的事，叫弟兄跌倒，一概不做才好。」（14:21）基督徒的自由是真实的，可是爱会自愿地限制自由。这不是说教会要被最挑剔的人控制，而是说刚强的人要在具体的关系中，为一个真实可能跌倒的弟兄着想。

**3. 在神面前守着。** 「你有信心，就当在神面前守着。人在自己以为可行的事上能不自责，就有福了。」（14:22）刚强的人不需要到处宣扬自己的自由。

**4. 凡不出于信心的都是罪。** 「若有疑心而吃的，就必有罪，因为他吃不是出于信心。凡不出于信心的都是罪。」（14:23）这里的「信心」，是指在神面前确信这事是可行的。违背良心去做一件本身不是罪的事，仍然是罪，因为它是在不信靠神的心态下做的。所以，不要逼一个良心软弱的弟兄去做他还不能坦然去做的事。

**5. 什么属于「次要的事」？** 罗马书第十四章所讲的，是圣经没有明确规定、真信徒可以有不同看法的事。今天华人教会的例子可能包括：信徒可不可以喝酒、崇拜要用诗歌还是传统圣诗、能不能过某些节日。可是要注意，福音的核心不在此列：保罗在加拉太书中对那些要求外邦人守割礼才能得救的人，毫不让步（加1:8-9；2:3-5）。分辨哪些是可以有不同看法的事，哪些是福音的核心，本身就需要智慧。

> 要点：爱会自愿地限制自由，不在小事上拆毁神正在建造的人。可是保罗所讲的是次要的事；在福音的核心上，教会不能让步。

## 默想

**默想：** 「吃的人不可轻看不吃的人；不吃的人不可论断吃的人；因为神已经收纳他了。」在教会里，我比较像「刚强的」还是「软弱的」？我是否轻看过那些我觉得「太保守、太死板」的弟兄姊妹？或者，我是否论断过那些我觉得「太随便、不够属灵」的人？也许是为了一首诗歌、一种崇拜方式、一杯酒、一个节日。保罗没有叫我们假装没有不同，而是叫我们记得：神已经收纳了他，基督也已经替他死。今天，我能不能想起一位和我在这些事上看法不同的弟兄或姊妹，为他感谢神，甚至主动约他吃一顿饭？吃什么不要紧，因为「神的国不在乎吃喝」；要紧的是，我们是被同一位主收纳的人。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 12 said "let love be genuine"; chapter 13 said "love is the fulfilling of the law." Chapter 14 brings that love into a concrete problem in the Roman church: how should believers live together when they disagree about secondary matters?

The chapter has three parts. **Neither despise nor judge** (vv. 1–12): one "believes he may eat anything," another "eats only vegetables"; one "esteems one day as better than another," another "esteems all days alike." Paul says, "Let not the one who eats despise the one who abstains, and let not the one who abstains pass judgment on the one who eats, for God has welcomed him," and "we will all stand before the judgment seat of God." **Don't make a brother stumble** (vv. 13–18): "nothing is unclean in itself," but "if your brother is grieved by what you eat, you are no longer walking in love"; "the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking but of righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit." **Pursue peace and build up** (vv. 19–23): "Do not, for the sake of food, destroy the work of God"; "whatever does not proceed from faith is sin."

## Going Deeper: 14:1–12 God Has Welcomed Him

**1. Who are "the weak"?** "As for the one who is weak in faith, welcome him, but not to quarrel over opinions. One person believes he may eat anything, while the weak person eats only vegetables" (14:1–2). From the context, "the weak" were probably mostly believers of Jewish background (perhaps including some Gentiles) whose consciences were still bound by the food and festival laws of Moses; in a Gentile city they could not be sure whether meat in the market was clean or had been offered to idols, so they ate only vegetables. "The strong" understood that in Christ "nothing is unclean in itself" (14:14). Paul counts himself among the strong (15:1), yet he gives most of his counsel to the strong.

**2. Two different temptations.** "Let not the one who eats despise the one who abstains, and let not the one who abstains pass judgment on the one who eats, for God has welcomed him" (14:3). The strong are tempted to "despise" the weak as naive and old-fashioned; the weak are tempted to "judge" the strong as careless and unspiritual. Paul warns both, and for one reason: God has welcomed him. How can I reject someone God has accepted?

**3. Another's servant.** "Who are you to pass judgment on the servant of another? It is before his own master that he stands or falls. And he will be upheld, for the Lord is able to make him stand" (14:4). Every believer is Christ's servant, not mine. The master is Christ, not me.

**4. Observing days.** "One person esteems one day as better than another, while another esteems all days alike. Each one should be fully convinced in his own mind" (14:5). The "days" here are most likely the Jewish Sabbath and festivals. Whether the Lord's Day is included, interpreters differ: some think it is not in dispute, since it was the early church's common day of worship (Acts 20:7; 1 Cor 16:2); others think Paul allows greater freedom here. Either way, Paul's point is that in such matters each person should have a clear conscience before God.

**5. Living to the Lord.** "For none of us lives to himself, and none of us dies to himself. If we live, we live to the Lord, and if we die, we die to the Lord. So then, whether we live or whether we die, we are the Lord's" (14:7–8). The one who eats "eats in honor of the Lord," and the one who abstains abstains "in honor of the Lord" (14:6). The key is not whether one eats but whether one does it "to the Lord."

**6. All before the judgment seat.** "Why do you pass judgment on your brother? Or you, why do you despise your brother? For we will all stand before the judgment seat of God" (14:10). Paul quotes Isaiah 45:23: "every knee shall bow to me, and every tongue shall confess to God" (14:11). We are not the judges but people who will one day stand before the judgment seat. "Each of us will give an account of himself to God" (14:12), not of someone else.

> The point: In secondary matters, the strong must not despise the weak, and the weak must not judge the strong, for God has welcomed each. We are not one another's masters, but fellow servants who will all stand before God.

## Going Deeper: 14:13–18 The Kingdom Is Not Food and Drink

**1. No stumbling blocks.** "Therefore let us not pass judgment on one another any longer, but rather decide never to put a stumbling block or hindrance in the way of a brother" (14:13). Paul plays on a single word: don't "judge" others; rather "decide" (the same Greek word) never to make them stumble.

**2. Nothing unclean in itself.** "I know and am persuaded in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean in itself, but it is unclean for anyone who thinks it unclean" (14:14). This echoes Jesus' teaching that what goes into the mouth does not defile a person (Mark 7:19). But Paul adds at once that if someone believes a thing is unclean, eating it goes against their conscience.

**3. Christ died for him.** "For if your brother is grieved by what you eat, you are no longer walking in love. By what you eat, do not destroy the one for whom Christ died" (14:15). This is the most powerful sentence in the chapter. Christ gave his very life for this weak brother; will I not even give up a meal for him? "Destroy" is a strong word: Paul is concerned not only with the brother's feelings but with his spiritual life, being drawn into acting against his own conscience.

**4. What the kingdom is about.** "For the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking but of righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit. Whoever thus serves Christ is acceptable to God and approved by men" (14:17–18). Churches easily quarrel over secondary things and forget the true center of God's kingdom: the righteousness of being right with God, peace among people, and the joy the Spirit gives.

> The point: Since Christ gave his life for a weak brother, I should give up my freedom for him. The kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking but of righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit.

## Going Deeper: 14:19–23 Whatever Is Not from Faith Is Sin

**1. Pursue peace and upbuilding.** "So then let us pursue what makes for peace and for mutual upbuilding. Do not, for the sake of food, destroy the work of God" (14:19–20). "Upbuilding" is a construction word, and "the work of God" is what God is doing in our brothers and sisters. We can tear down someone God is building over a small matter.

**2. The limits of freedom.** "It is good not to eat meat or drink wine or do anything that causes your brother to stumble" (14:21). Christian freedom is real, but love willingly limits freedom. This doesn't mean the church should be controlled by its most easily offended members; it means the strong should think of a real brother who might really stumble, within an actual relationship.

**3. Keep it before God.** "The faith that you have, keep between yourself and God. Blessed is the one who has no reason to pass judgment on himself for what he approves" (14:22). The strong don't need to advertise their freedom.

**4. Whatever is not from faith is sin.** "But whoever has doubts is condemned if he eats, because the eating is not from faith. For whatever does not proceed from faith is sin" (14:23). "Faith" here means being confident before God that something is permitted. To act against conscience in something that is not sin in itself is still sin, because it is done without trust in God. So don't press a brother with a weak conscience to do what he cannot yet do with a clear heart.

**5. What counts as a "secondary matter"?** Romans 14 concerns things Scripture does not explicitly command, on which true believers may differ. In Chinese churches today the examples might include whether believers may drink alcohol, whether worship uses contemporary songs or traditional hymns, and whether to observe certain festivals. But note that the heart of the gospel is not on this list: in Galatians Paul would not give an inch to those who required Gentiles to be circumcised to be saved (Gal 1:8–9; 2:3–5). Telling secondary matters from the heart of the gospel itself takes wisdom.

> The point: Love willingly limits its freedom, rather than tearing down over small things someone God is building. But Paul is speaking of secondary matters; on the heart of the gospel the church cannot give way.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Let not the one who eats despise the one who abstains, and let not the one who abstains pass judgment on the one who eats, for God has welcomed him." In church, am I more like "the strong" or "the weak"? Have I looked down on brothers and sisters I think are "too conservative, too rigid"? Or have I judged those I think are "too casual, not spiritual enough"? Perhaps over a song, a style of worship, a glass of wine, a festival. Paul doesn't ask us to pretend our differences away; he asks us to remember that God has welcomed this person, and Christ has died for him. Today, can I think of a brother or sister who sees these things differently from me, thank God for them, and perhaps invite them for a meal? What we eat doesn't matter, for "the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking"; what matters is that we have been welcomed by the same Lord.
