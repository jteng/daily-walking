## 本章脉络

第十五章前半继续第十四章的话题，后半转到保罗自己的计划。

本章分四段。**基督不求自己的喜悦**（1-6节）：「我们坚固的人应该担代不坚固人的软弱，不求自己的喜悦」，因为「基督也不求自己的喜悦」。**彼此接纳**（7-13节）：「你们要彼此接纳，如同基督接纳你们一样」；基督作了受割礼人的执事，也叫外邦人因他的怜悯荣耀神；最后是一个祝福：「但愿使人有盼望的神……使你们藉着圣灵的能力大有盼望！」**保罗的祭司职分**（14-21节）：他为外邦人「作神福音的祭司」，「从耶路撒冷，直转到以利哩古，到处传了基督的福音」。**往西班牙去**（22-33节）：保罗先要把捐项送到耶路撒冷，然后经过罗马往西班牙；他请罗马的信徒「与我一同竭力，为我祈求神」。

## 深读 15:1-6 基督不求自己的喜悦

**1. 担代软弱。** 「我们坚固的人应该担代不坚固人的软弱，不求自己的喜悦。我们各人务要叫邻舍喜悦，使他得益处，建立德行。」（15:1-2）「担代」不只是容忍，而是像背重担一样承担。这里的「叫邻舍喜悦」不是讨好人，而是为了「使他得益处，建立德行」。

**2. 基督的榜样。** 「因为基督也不求自己的喜悦，如经上所记：『辱骂你人的辱骂都落在我身上。』」（15:3；引诗69:9）这是一首受苦的义人的诗篇，新约多次用它来描写基督（约2:17；19:28-29；罗11:9-10；徒1:20）。基督不只是给我们一个榜样：人对神的敌意与辱骂，都落在了他身上；他甘心承受，不求自己的喜悦。而圣经在别处更告诉我们，他也担当了我们的罪（赛53:4-6；彼前2:24）。我们能担代别人的软弱，是因为他先这样担代了我们。

**3. 圣经的用处。** 「从前所写的圣经都是为教训我们写的，叫我们因圣经所生的忍耐和安慰可以得着盼望。」（15:4）旧约不是过时的书，而是神为我们写的，要叫我们在忍耐和安慰中得着盼望。

**4. 一心一口。** 「但愿赐忍耐安慰的神叫你们彼此同心，效法基督耶稣，一心一口荣耀神－我们主耶稣基督的父！」（15:5-6）彼此接纳的目的，不只是教会和平，而是叫犹太人和外邦人「一心一口」地敬拜神。

> 要点：基督不求自己的喜悦，反而为我们承担了辱骂。我们能担代别人的软弱，是因为他先担代了我们；而这一切的目的，是要叫教会一心一口地荣耀神。

## 深读 15:7-13 如同基督接纳你们

**1. 彼此接纳。** 「所以，你们要彼此接纳，如同基督接纳你们一样，使荣耀归与神。」（15:7）这是第十四至十五章的总结。「如同」既是标准，也是理由：基督怎样接纳了我们，我们也要照样接纳别人；而基督接纳我们的时候，我们并不配得。

**2. 基督作了执事。** 「我说，基督是为神真理作了受割礼人的执事，要证实所应许列祖的话，并叫外邦人因他的怜悯荣耀神。」（15:8-9）基督先是为犹太人而来，证实神向列祖的应许是真实的（参9:4-5）；同时也是为外邦人而来，叫他们因神的怜悯荣耀神。犹太人看见神的信实，外邦人看见神的怜悯。

**3. 四处旧约经文。** 保罗一连引用了四处经文，分别出自律法书、先知书和诗篇（15:9-12；引诗18:49；申32:43；诗117:1；赛11:10），证明外邦人与以色列一同敬拜神，早就是神的计划。最后一处说：「将来有耶西的根，就是那兴起来要治理外邦的；外邦人要仰望他。」（15:12）大卫的后裔基督，正是万民的盼望（参1:3）。

**4. 盼望的神。** 「但愿使人有盼望的神，因信将诸般的喜乐、平安充满你们的心，使你们藉着圣灵的能力大有盼望！」（15:13）这是第十二至十五章的高峰，几乎是全书主题的总结：信心、喜乐、平安、圣灵、盼望。神被称为「使人有盼望的神」，盼望不是我们自己鼓起来的，而是他所赐的。

> 要点：基督怎样接纳了不配的我们，我们也要照样彼此接纳。基督使犹太人看见神的信实，使外邦人看见神的怜悯；而这位盼望的神，要藉着圣灵叫我们大有盼望。

## 深读 15:14-21 福音的祭司

**1. 满有良善。** 「弟兄们，我自己也深信你们是满有良善，充足了诸般的知识，也能彼此劝戒。」（15:14）保罗写了一封很直接的信，却仍然真心肯定罗马的信徒。劝勉与鼓励可以并存。

**2. 福音的祭司。** 「使我为外邦人作基督耶稣的仆役，作神福音的祭司，叫所献上的外邦人，因着圣灵成为圣洁，可蒙悦纳。」（15:16）保罗用圣殿的语言描写他的宣教工作：他像一位祭司，所献上的「祭物」就是信主的外邦人。他们从前被看为不洁净，如今「因着圣灵成为圣洁」。这回应了12:1：信徒献上自己为活祭，而保罗的工作，就是把外邦人带到神面前。

**3. 只提基督所做的。** 「除了基督藉我做的那些事，我什么都不敢提。」（15:18）保罗一生的工作非常惊人，可是他只「夸」基督藉他所做的。

**4. 从耶路撒冷到以利哩古。** 「甚至我从耶路撒冷，直转到以利哩古，到处传了基督的福音。」（15:19）以利哩古在今天巴尔干半岛的西部（大约是阿尔巴尼亚和前南斯拉夫一带）。「到处传了」原文是「传满了」「完成了」的意思；这不是说每一个人都听见了，而是说保罗在这些地区的主要城市建立了教会，福音可以从那里继续传开。

**5. 先锋的志向。** 「我立了志向，不在基督的名被称过的地方传福音，免得建造在别人的根基上。」（15:20）保罗引用以赛亚书52:15：「未曾闻知他信息的，将要看见。」（15:21）这句话出自第四首仆人之歌的开头部分（赛52:13–53:12）。保罗的心，是要让那些从来没有听过基督名字的人听见。

> 要点：保罗把宣教看作祭司的事奉：把外邦人献给神，因圣灵成为圣洁。他只夸基督藉他所做的，而他的心，是要让那些从未听过基督名字的人听见。

## 深读 15:22-33 计划与祷告

**1. 往西班牙去。** 「但如今，在这里再没有可传的地方，而且这好几年，我切心想望到西班牙去的时候，可以到你们那里，盼望从你们那里经过，得见你们……然后蒙你们送行。」（15:23-24）西班牙是当时罗马帝国的西端。保罗希望罗马教会成为他向西宣教的基地。

**2. 捐项。** 「但现在，我往耶路撒冷去供给圣徒。因为马其顿和亚该亚人乐意凑出捐项给耶路撒冷圣徒中的穷人。这固然是他们乐意的，其实也算是所欠的债；因外邦人既然在他们属灵的好处上有分，就当把养身之物供给他们。」（15:25-27）这捐项不只是救济，更是犹太和外邦教会合一的记号。外邦信徒从犹太人那里领受了福音，现在用物质回报（参林后8-9章）。

**3. 一同竭力祈求。** 「弟兄们，我藉着我们主耶稣基督，又藉着圣灵的爱，劝你们与我一同竭力，为我祈求神，叫我脱离在犹太不顺从的人，也叫我为耶路撒冷所办的捐项可蒙圣徒悦纳，并叫我顺着神的旨意，欢欢喜喜地到你们那里。」（15:30-32）「一同竭力」原文是「一同竭力奋斗、一同争战」的意思（参西4:12以巴弗「竭力祈求」）：代祷是一场属灵的争战。

**4. 神怎样回答这祷告。** 从使徒行传我们知道后来发生了什么：保罗到了耶路撒冷，在圣殿被捉拿（徒21章），在该撒利亚被关了两年多（徒24:27），最后不是作为一位自由的宣教士，而是作为一个被锁着的囚犯，经过船难，才到了罗马（徒27-28章）。他的祷告蒙应允了吗？他确实没有被那些不顺从的人杀害，也确实到了罗马，却是用他没有想到的方式。至于他是否到过西班牙，早期教会有一些传说（如《革利免一书》5:7说他「到了西方的尽头」），可是圣经没有记载。

> 要点：保罗有清楚的计划，也恳切地请人为他代祷；神应允了，却是用他没有想到的方式。我们的计划要「顺着神的旨意」，而神的旨意常常比我们的计划更深。

## 默想

**默想：** 「劝你们与我一同竭力，为我祈求神……叫我顺着神的旨意，欢欢喜喜地到你们那里。」保罗请人为他的旅程代祷，神也应允了：他到了罗马，却是戴着锁链去的。我是否也有一个祷告，神似乎是用我没有想到、甚至不想要的方式回答的？我能不能相信，那位「使人有盼望的神」并没有忘记我的祷告，他只是比我看得更远？保罗在捆锁中写下了腓立比书、以弗所书、歌罗西书和腓利门书（信中都提到他的捆锁）；按传统的看法，这些信写于他在罗马被囚期间（徒28:30），也有学者认为写于该撒利亚或以弗所；神用他没有想到的方式，成就了更大的事。今天，我也可以学保罗，请一两位弟兄姊妹与我「一同竭力」祷告，也问问他们：我可以为你祷告什么？最后，请把15:13当作今天的祷告：「但愿使人有盼望的神，因信将诸般的喜乐、平安充满你们的心，使你们藉着圣灵的能力大有盼望！」

## The Shape of the Chapter

The first half of chapter 15 continues the theme of chapter 14; the second half turns to Paul's own plans.

The chapter has four parts. **Christ did not please himself** (vv. 1–6): "We who are strong have an obligation to bear with the failings of the weak, and not to please ourselves," for "Christ did not please himself." **Welcome one another** (vv. 7–13): "welcome one another as Christ has welcomed you"; Christ became a servant to the circumcised and caused the Gentiles to glorify God for his mercy; and it closes with a blessing: "May the God of hope... so that by the power of the Holy Spirit you may abound in hope." **Paul's priestly service** (vv. 14–21): he is a minister to the Gentiles "in the priestly service of the gospel of God," and "from Jerusalem and all the way around to Illyricum I have fulfilled the ministry of the gospel of Christ." **On to Spain** (vv. 22–33): Paul must first take the collection to Jerusalem, then pass through Rome on the way to Spain; he asks the Roman believers "to strive together with me in your prayers to God on my behalf."

## Going Deeper: 15:1–6 Christ Did Not Please Himself

**1. Bearing with the weak.** "We who are strong have an obligation to bear with the failings of the weak, and not to please ourselves. Let each of us please his neighbor for his good, to build him up" (15:1–2). "Bear with" is not mere tolerance, but carrying something like a heavy load. "Please his neighbor" doesn't mean flattering people, but seeking "his good, to build him up."

**2. Christ's example.** "For Christ did not please himself, but as it is written, 'The reproaches of those who reproached you fell on me'" (15:3, quoting Ps 69:9). This is a psalm of the suffering righteous one, which the New Testament often applies to Christ (John 2:17; 19:28–29; cf. Rom 11:9–10; Acts 1:20). Christ doesn't only give us an example: the hostility and insults aimed at God fell on him, and he willingly bore them rather than please himself. And Scripture elsewhere tells us he also bore our sins (Isa 53:4–6; 1 Pet 2:24). We can bear with others because he first bore with us like this.

**3. What Scripture is for.** "For whatever was written in former days was written for our instruction, that through endurance and through the encouragement of the Scriptures we might have hope" (15:4). The Old Testament is not out of date; God wrote it for us, to give us hope through endurance and encouragement.

**4. With one voice.** "May the God of endurance and encouragement grant you to live in such harmony with one another, in accord with Christ Jesus, that together you may with one voice glorify the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ" (15:5–6). The goal of welcoming one another is not only peace in the church, but that Jews and Gentiles might worship God "with one voice."

> The point: Christ did not please himself but bore reproach for us. We can bear with others' weaknesses because he first bore ours; and the goal of it all is a church glorifying God with one voice.

## Going Deeper: 15:7–13 As Christ Has Welcomed You

**1. Welcome one another.** "Therefore welcome one another as Christ has welcomed you, for the glory of God" (15:7). This sums up chapters 14–15. "As" gives both the standard and the reason: as Christ welcomed us, so we welcome others; and when Christ welcomed us, we did not deserve it.

**2. Christ became a servant.** "For I tell you that Christ became a servant to the circumcised to show God's truthfulness, in order to confirm the promises given to the patriarchs, and in order that the Gentiles might glorify God for his mercy" (15:8–9). Christ came first for the Jews, confirming that God's promises to the patriarchs were true (cf. 9:4–5); and he came for the Gentiles too, that they might glorify God for his mercy. The Jews see God's faithfulness; the Gentiles see God's mercy.

**3. Four Old Testament texts.** Paul quotes four texts in a row, from the Law, the Prophets and the Psalms (15:9–12, quoting Ps 18:49; Deut 32:43; Ps 117:1; Isa 11:10), to show that the Gentiles worshiping God alongside Israel was God's plan all along. The last says, "The root of Jesse will come, even he who arises to rule the Gentiles; in him will the Gentiles hope" (15:12). Christ, the son of David, is the hope of all nations (cf. 1:3).

**4. The God of hope.** "May the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, so that by the power of the Holy Spirit you may abound in hope" (15:13). This is the high point of chapters 12–15, almost a summary of the whole letter's themes: faith, joy, peace, the Spirit, hope. God is called "the God of hope": hope is not something we work up ourselves, but something he gives.

> The point: As Christ welcomed us who did not deserve it, so we welcome one another. Christ showed the Jews God's faithfulness and the Gentiles God's mercy; and the God of hope makes us abound in hope by the Spirit.

## Going Deeper: 15:14–21 A Priest of the Gospel

**1. Full of goodness.** "I myself am satisfied about you, my brothers, that you yourselves are full of goodness, filled with all knowledge and able to instruct one another" (15:14). Paul has written a very direct letter, yet he sincerely affirms the Roman believers. Exhortation and encouragement can go together.

**2. Priestly service.** "To be a minister of Christ Jesus to the Gentiles in the priestly service of the gospel of God, so that the offering of the Gentiles may be acceptable, sanctified by the Holy Spirit" (15:16). Paul describes his mission in temple language: he is like a priest, and his "offering" is the Gentiles who have believed. Once regarded as unclean, they are now "sanctified by the Holy Spirit." This echoes 12:1: believers present themselves as living sacrifices, and Paul's work is to bring the Gentiles before God.

**3. Speaking only of Christ's work.** "For I will not venture to speak of anything except what Christ has accomplished through me" (15:18). Paul's life's work was astonishing, yet he "boasts" only of what Christ did through him.

**4. From Jerusalem to Illyricum.** "So that from Jerusalem and all the way around to Illyricum I have fulfilled the ministry of the gospel of Christ" (15:19). Illyricum lay in the western Balkans (roughly modern Albania and the former Yugoslavia). "Fulfilled" doesn't mean everyone had heard, but that Paul had planted churches in the key cities of these regions, from which the gospel could keep spreading.

**5. A pioneer's ambition.** "And thus I make it my ambition to preach the gospel, not where Christ has already been named, lest I build on someone else's foundation" (15:20). Paul quotes Isaiah 52:15: "Those who have never been told of him will see" (15:21), words from the opening of the fourth Servant Song (Isa 52:13–53:12). Paul's heart was for those who had never heard Christ's name.

> The point: Paul saw mission as priestly service: offering the Gentiles to God, sanctified by the Spirit. He boasted only of what Christ did through him, and his heart was for those who had never heard Christ's name.

## Going Deeper: 15:22–33 Plans and Prayer

**1. On to Spain.** "But now, since I no longer have any room for work in these regions, and since I have longed for many years to come to you, I hope to see you in passing as I go to Spain, and to be helped on my journey there by you" (15:23–24). Spain was the western end of the Roman Empire. Paul hoped the Roman church would become a base for his mission to the west.

**2. The collection.** "At present, however, I am going to Jerusalem bringing aid to the saints. For Macedonia and Achaia have been pleased to make some contribution for the poor among the saints at Jerusalem. They were pleased to do it, and indeed they owe it to them. For if the Gentiles have come to share in their spiritual blessings, they ought also to be of service to them in material blessings" (15:25–27). The collection was not only relief, but a sign of unity between Jewish and Gentile churches. Gentile believers had received the gospel from the Jews, and now gave material help in return (cf. 2 Cor 8–9).

**3. Striving together in prayer.** "I appeal to you, brothers, by our Lord Jesus Christ and by the love of the Spirit, to strive together with me in your prayers to God on my behalf, that I may be delivered from the unbelievers in Judea, and that my service for Jerusalem may be acceptable to the saints, so that by God's will I may come to you with joy" (15:30–32). "Strive together" means to struggle or contend alongside, like fellow athletes or soldiers (cf. Col 4:12, Epaphras "struggling" in prayer): intercession is a spiritual battle.

**4. How God answered.** From Acts we know what happened next: Paul reached Jerusalem, was seized in the temple (Acts 21), was held in Caesarea for more than two years (Acts 24:27), and finally came to Rome not as a free missionary but as a prisoner in chains, after a shipwreck (Acts 27–28). Was his prayer answered? He was indeed not killed by his opponents, and he did reach Rome, but in a way he had not imagined. As for whether he ever reached Spain, there are early church traditions (e.g. *1 Clement* 5:7, which says he reached "the limits of the west"), but Scripture does not record it.

> The point: Paul made clear plans and earnestly asked others to pray; God answered, but in a way Paul had not imagined. Our plans must be "by God's will," and God's will often runs deeper than our plans.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Strive together with me in your prayers to God on my behalf... so that by God's will I may come to you with joy." Paul asked others to pray for his journey, and God answered: he reached Rome, but in chains. Do I have a prayer that God seems to have answered in a way I didn't expect, or even want? Can I trust that "the God of hope" has not forgotten my prayer, but sees farther than I do? In chains Paul wrote Philippians, Ephesians, Colossians and Philemon (each mentions his imprisonment); by the traditional view these were written during his Roman imprisonment (Acts 28:30), though some scholars place them in Caesarea or Ephesus; God did something greater in a way Paul never imagined. Today I too can follow Paul and ask one or two brothers or sisters to "strive together" with me in prayer, and ask them what I can pray for them. Finally, take 15:13 as today's prayer: "May the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, so that by the power of the Holy Spirit you may abound in hope."
