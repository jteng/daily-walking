## 本章脉络

许多人读罗马书，读到第十六章就跳过去了：一长串难念的名字，好像和我们没有关系。可是正是这些名字，让我们看见福音在一个真实的城市里，建立了一个怎样的家。

本章分四段。**举荐非比**（1-2节）：「我对你们举荐我们的姊妹非比；她是坚革哩教会中的女执事。」**问安**（3-16节）：保罗问候了二十多位信徒，有男有女，有犹太人也有外邦人，有自由人，看来也有奴隶。**警告**（17-20节）：要躲避那些「离间你们、叫你们跌倒、背乎所学之道的人」；「赐平安的神快要将撒但践踏在你们脚下。」**同工的问安与颂赞**（21-27节）：提摩太、德提、该犹等人问安，最后是全书的颂赞：「愿荣耀，因耶稣基督，归与独一全智的神，直到永远。阿们！」

## 深读 16:1-2 非比

**1. 带信的人。** 「我对你们举荐我们的姊妹非比；她是坚革哩教会中的女执事。」（16:1）坚革哩是哥林多东边的港口。多数解经家认为，非比就是把这封信带到罗马的人；在当时，带信的人常常也要向收信人解释信的内容。罗马书这封伟大的信，是交在一位姊妹手里送去的。

**2. 「女执事」。** 「女执事」原文 *diakonos*，与「执事」是同一个字，也可以译作「仆人」（英文ESV译本正文作「仆人」，小字注作「女执事」）。解经家对这个词的理解有不同：有人认为非比担任了教会正式的执事职分（参腓1:1；提前3:8-13）；也有人认为这里是一般性地说她服事教会。无论哪一种理解，保罗都高度肯定她的服事。

**3. 帮助许多人。** 「请你们为主接待她，合乎圣徒的体统……因她素来帮助许多人，也帮助了我。」（16:2）「帮助」原文有「保护者、资助者」的意思：非比很可能是一位有资财、用她的家和资源支持教会和宣教的姊妹。

> 要点：这封伟大的信，是交在一位姊妹手里送去的。在神的家里，那些用资源、接待和劳苦默默支持福音的人，都被保罗记念并且举荐。

## 深读 16:3-16 一长串名字

**1. 百基拉和亚居拉。** 「问百基拉和亚居拉安。他们在基督耶稣里与我同工，也为我的命将自己的颈项置之度外……又问在他们家中的教会安。」（16:3-5）这对夫妇曾因革老丢的命令离开罗马（徒18:2），在哥林多和以弗所与保罗同工（徒18:18-26），如今又回到了罗马。他们家里有一个教会。初期教会没有礼拜堂，信徒在家中聚会；罗马的信徒很可能分散在好几个家庭教会里（参16:5，10，11，14，15）。

**2. 犹尼亚。** 「又问我亲属与我一同坐监的安多尼古和犹尼亚安；他们在使徒中是有名望的，也是比我先在基督里。」（16:7）这里有两个讨论：

- 「犹尼亚」是女性的名字，这是今天大多数学者的看法；安多尼古和犹尼亚可能是一对夫妇。
- 「在使徒中是有名望的」，原文可以理解为「在使徒当中（作为使徒）是有名望的」，也可以理解为「在使徒眼中是有名望的」（英文ESV译本作「为使徒所熟知」）。主张前一种理解的人认为，「使徒」在这里是广义的用法，指被差遣的宣教士（参徒14:14；林后8:23），不是十二使徒那样的职分。

这些问题牵涉到华人教会中关于女性事奉的不同立场。无论如何，保罗名单中的女性都被他尊重，而且是真实地参与福音的工作：非比、百基拉、马利亚、犹尼亚、土非拿氏、土富撒氏、彼息氏、鲁孚的母亲、犹利亚、尼利亚的姊妹。

**3. 为主劳苦。** 「又问马利亚安；她为你们多受劳苦……又问为主劳苦的土非拿氏和土富撒氏安。问可亲爱为主多受劳苦的彼息氏安。」（16:6，12）「劳苦」这个词在名单里出现了好几次。保罗没有记下他们做了什么大事，只记下他们「劳苦」。神记念的，常常就是这些没有人看见的辛劳。

**4. 鲁孚和他的母亲。** 「又问在主蒙拣选的鲁孚和他母亲安；他的母亲就是我的母亲。」（16:13）马可福音说替耶稣背十字架的古利奈人西门，是「亚历山大和鲁孚的父亲」（可15:21）。马可福音很可能是写给罗马教会的，所以有解经家认为，这里的鲁孚就是那个鲁孚。若是这样，那个被迫为耶稣背十字架的人，后来全家都信了主。鲁孚的母亲在某个时候照顾过保罗，像母亲一样。一个离家漂泊的宣教士，在神的家里找到了母亲。

**5. 不同背景的人。** 这份名单里，有犹太人（保罗称他们为「亲属」，16:7，11，21），也有外邦人；有些名字（如暗伯利、腓罗罗古、黑米）是当时奴隶和被释放的奴隶常用的名字；「亚利多布家里的人」和「拿其数家在主里的人」（16:10-11），可能是这些显贵家中的奴仆。在社会上，这些人分属不同的阶层；在基督里，他们是彼此问安的家人。这正是第十四至十五章「彼此接纳」的样子。

**6. 圣洁的亲嘴。** 「你们亲嘴问安，彼此务要圣洁。」（16:16）在当时的文化中，亲嘴是家人之间的问候。保罗要信徒用家人的方式彼此相待。今天各地教会可以用适合自己文化的方式表达同样的亲情。

> 要点：保罗的名单，是福音所建立的家：男女老少、犹太人和外邦人、自由人和奴仆。神记念每一个名字，也记念那些没有人看见的劳苦。

## 深读 16:17-20 在善上聪明，在恶上愚拙

**1. 留意躲避。** 「弟兄们，那些离间你们、叫你们跌倒、背乎所学之道的人，我劝你们要留意躲避他们。」（16:17）第十四章说要接纳在次要的事上看法不同的人；这里却说要躲避那些「背乎所学之道」的人。这两者并不矛盾：在次要的事上要宽容，在福音的核心上要警醒。

**2. 花言巧语。** 「因为这样的人不服事我们的主基督，只服事自己的肚腹，用花言巧语诱惑那些老实人的心。」（16:18）假教师的特征：表面动听，骨子里是为自己。

**3. 在善上聪明。** 「我愿意你们在善上聪明，在恶上愚拙。」（16:19）基督徒不需要对一切罪恶都见多识广，但要在行善上有智慧。

**4. 践踏撒但。** 「赐平安的神快要将撒但践踏在你们脚下。」（16:20）这呼应创世记3:15：女人的后裔要伤蛇的头。基督已经在十字架上胜过了撒但，而神要藉着他的教会，最终完成这个胜利。要注意，践踏撒但的是「赐平安的神」：教会的和平与合一，本身就是对撒但的打击。

> 要点：在次要的事上要彼此接纳，在福音的核心上要警醒分辨。赐平安的神必在教会的脚下践踏撒但，完成基督已经得着的胜利。

## 深读 16:21-27 颂赞

**1. 同工的问安。** 提摩太、路求、耶孙、所西巴德、代笔的德提、接待全教会的该犹、城内管银库的以拉都、兄弟括土（16:21-23）。德提是保罗的代笔人，他在这里亲自加了一句问安（16:22）。以拉都是哥林多城的官员；考古学家在哥林多发现一块刻有「以拉都」名字的铭文，有人认为可能就是这个人，但学者对此看法不一。福音已经进入了社会的不同阶层。

**2. 颂赞的位置。** 16:25-27这段颂赞，在不同的古抄本中出现在不同的位置（有的在14:23之后，有的在15:33之后），所以有些学者讨论它原来的位置。可是它的内容与全书紧紧相连。

**3. 全书的总结。** 「惟有神能照我所传的福音和所讲的耶稣基督，并照永古隐藏不言的奥秘，坚固你们的心。这奥秘如今显明出来，而且按着永生神的命，藉众先知的书指示万国的民，使他们信服真道。」（16:25-26）这里几乎重述了第一章的开头：福音、先知的书（1:2）、万国（1:5）、「信服真道」（1:5）。整封信首尾呼应。

**4. 愿荣耀归与神。** 「愿荣耀，因耶稣基督，归与独一全智的神，直到永远。阿们！」（16:27）罗马书以人的罪开始（1:18），以神的荣耀结束。保罗写这封信的最终目的，不是要建立一套神学体系，而是要叫万国的民「因耶稣基督」把荣耀归给神。

> 要点：罗马书以福音开始，也以福音结束：神按着众先知的应许，藉着耶稣基督，叫万国的民信服真道。一切的终点，是荣耀归给独一全智的神。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他的母亲就是我的母亲。」保罗离开家乡，四处奔波，却在神的家里找到了母亲、兄弟和姊妹。在我的教会里，有没有一位像非比、百基拉、马利亚、鲁孚的母亲那样默默付出的人，我从来没有好好谢过他？今天，我能不能像保罗一样，写几句话或打一个电话，叫着他的名字向他道谢？再想想：有没有一位离乡背井的人：留学生、外地来打工的人、新移民、刚来的信徒，正需要有人像鲁孚的母亲那样待他？最后，罗马书十六章读完了。保罗用十一章讲福音，用五章讲福音所带来的生活，最后用一长串名字告诉我们：福音不是抽象的道理，而是在真实的人中间建立起来的家。愿我读完这卷书，也和保罗一起说：「愿荣耀，因耶稣基督，归与独一全智的神，直到永远。阿们！」

## The Shape of the Chapter

Many readers of Romans skip chapter 16: a long list of hard-to-pronounce names that seems to have nothing to do with us. Yet it is exactly these names that show us what kind of family the gospel built in a real city.

The chapter has four parts. **Commending Phoebe** (vv. 1–2): "I commend to you our sister Phoebe, a servant of the church at Cenchreae." **Greetings** (vv. 3–16): Paul greets more than twenty believers, men and women, Jews and Gentiles, free people and apparently slaves. **A warning** (vv. 17–20): watch out for "those who cause divisions and create obstacles contrary to the doctrine that you have been taught"; "the God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet." **Greetings from co-workers and a doxology** (vv. 21–27): greetings from Timothy, Tertius, Gaius and others, and finally the letter's doxology: "to the only wise God be glory forevermore through Jesus Christ! Amen."

## Going Deeper: 16:1–2 Phoebe

**1. The letter carrier.** "I commend to you our sister Phoebe, a servant of the church at Cenchreae" (16:1). Cenchreae was Corinth's eastern port. Most interpreters think Phoebe carried this letter to Rome; in those days a letter carrier often also explained the letter to its recipients. The great letter to the Romans was entrusted to a sister.

**2. "Servant."** The word is *diakonos*, the same word as "deacon"; it can also be translated "servant" (the ESV has "servant" in the text and "deaconess" in a footnote). Interpreters differ: some think Phoebe held the recognized office of deacon in her church (cf. Phil 1:1; 1 Tim 3:8–13); others think Paul speaks more generally of her service to the church. On either view, Paul warmly commends her service.

**3. A patron of many.** "That you may welcome her in the Lord in a way worthy of the saints, and help her in whatever she may need from you, for she has been a patron of many and of myself as well" (16:2). "Patron" suggests a protector or benefactor: Phoebe was probably a woman of means who used her home and resources to support the church and its mission.

> The point: This great letter was entrusted to a sister. In God's family, those who quietly support the gospel with their resources, hospitality and labor are remembered and commended by Paul.

## Going Deeper: 16:3–16 A Long List of Names

**1. Prisca and Aquila.** "Greet Prisca and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus, who risked their necks for my life... Greet also the church in their house" (16:3–5). This couple had left Rome because of Claudius's edict (Acts 18:2), worked with Paul in Corinth and Ephesus (Acts 18:18–26), and had now returned to Rome. A church met in their home. The early church had no church buildings; believers met in homes, and the Roman believers were probably spread across several house churches (cf. 16:5, 10, 11, 14, 15).

**2. Junia.** "Greet Andronicus and Junia, my kinsmen and my fellow prisoners. They are well known to the apostles, and they were in Christ before me" (16:7). Two questions arise here:

- "Junia" is a woman's name, as most scholars today agree; Andronicus and Junia may have been a married couple.
- The phrase rendered "well known to the apostles" can be read either as "outstanding among the apostles (as apostles)" or "well known to the apostles." Those who take the first reading think "apostle" is used in a broad sense of sent missionaries (cf. Acts 14:14; 2 Cor 8:23), not an office like that of the Twelve.

These questions touch on the different positions held in Chinese churches about women's ministry. Either way, the women on Paul's list are honored by him and genuinely involved in gospel work: Phoebe, Prisca, Mary, Junia, Tryphaena, Tryphosa, Persis, Rufus's mother, Julia and Nereus's sister.

**3. Working hard for the Lord.** "Greet Mary, who has worked hard for you... Greet those workers in the Lord, Tryphaena and Tryphosa. Greet the beloved Persis, who has worked hard in the Lord" (16:6, 12). "Worked hard" appears several times in the list. Paul doesn't record any great deeds, only that they "labored." What God remembers is often the unseen toil.

**4. Rufus and his mother.** "Greet Rufus, chosen in the Lord; also his mother, who has been a mother to me as well" (16:13). Mark's Gospel says that Simon of Cyrene, who was made to carry Jesus' cross, was "the father of Alexander and Rufus" (Mark 15:21). Mark was quite likely written for the church in Rome, so some interpreters think this Rufus is the same man. If so, the man forced to carry Jesus' cross later saw his whole family come to faith. At some point Rufus's mother cared for Paul like a mother. A wandering missionary far from home found a mother in God's family.

**5. People of different backgrounds.** The list includes Jews (Paul calls them "kinsmen," 16:7, 11, 21) and Gentiles; some names (such as Ampliatus, Philologus and Hermes) were common among slaves and freed slaves; "those who belong to the family of Aristobulus" and "those in the Lord who belong to the family of Narcissus" (16:10–11) may have been servants in these prominent households. In society, these people belonged to different classes; in Christ they were family, greeting one another. This is what the "welcome one another" of chapters 14–15 looks like.

**6. A holy kiss.** "Greet one another with a holy kiss" (16:16). In that culture a kiss was a family greeting. Paul wants believers to treat one another as family. Churches today may express the same affection in ways suited to their own culture.

> The point: Paul's list is the family the gospel built: men and women, old and young, Jews and Gentiles, free and slave. God remembers every name, and every unseen labor.

## Going Deeper: 16:17–20 Wise as to Good, Innocent as to Evil

**1. Watch out and avoid.** "I appeal to you, brothers, to watch out for those who cause divisions and create obstacles contrary to the doctrine that you have been taught; avoid them" (16:17). Chapter 14 told us to welcome those who differ on secondary matters; here Paul says to avoid those who go "contrary to the doctrine that you have been taught." There is no contradiction: be generous on secondary matters, and watchful over the heart of the gospel.

**2. Smooth talk.** "For such persons do not serve our Lord Christ, but their own appetites, and by smooth talk and flattery they deceive the hearts of the naive" (16:18). The marks of false teachers: pleasing on the surface, self-serving underneath.

**3. Wise as to good.** "I want you to be wise as to what is good and innocent as to what is evil" (16:19). Christians don't need to be experts in every kind of evil, but they do need wisdom in doing good.

**4. Crushing Satan.** "The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet" (16:20). This echoes Genesis 3:15: the woman's offspring will bruise the serpent's head. Christ has already defeated Satan at the cross, and God will complete that victory through his church. Note that it is "the God of peace" who crushes Satan: the peace and unity of the church is itself a blow against Satan.

> The point: Welcome one another on secondary matters, and stay watchful over the heart of the gospel. The God of peace will crush Satan under the church's feet, completing the victory Christ has already won.

## Going Deeper: 16:21–27 Doxology

**1. Greetings from co-workers.** Timothy, Lucius, Jason, Sosipater, Tertius the scribe, Gaius the host of the whole church, Erastus the city treasurer, and our brother Quartus (16:21–23). Tertius, Paul's scribe, adds his own greeting here (16:22). Erastus was an official of Corinth; archaeologists have found an inscription in Corinth bearing the name Erastus, and some think it may be the same man, though scholars are divided. The gospel had reached different levels of society.

**2. Where the doxology belongs.** The doxology of 16:25–27 appears in different places in different ancient manuscripts (some after 14:23, some after 15:33), so some scholars discuss where it originally stood. Yet its content is closely tied to the whole letter.

**3. Summing up the letter.** "Now to him who is able to strengthen you according to my gospel and the preaching of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery that was kept secret for long ages but has now been disclosed and through the prophetic writings has been made known to all nations, according to the command of the eternal God, to bring about the obedience of faith" (16:25–26). This almost restates the opening of chapter 1: the gospel, the prophetic writings (1:2), all the nations (1:5), "the obedience of faith" (1:5). The whole letter is framed.

**4. Glory to God.** "To the only wise God be glory forevermore through Jesus Christ! Amen" (16:27). Romans begins with human sin (1:18) and ends with the glory of God. Paul's final purpose in writing was not to build a theological system but to bring the nations to give glory to God "through Jesus Christ."

> The point: Romans begins and ends with the gospel: according to the prophets' promise, through Jesus Christ, God brings the nations to the obedience of faith. The end of it all is glory to the only wise God.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "His mother, who has been a mother to me as well." Paul left home and traveled constantly, yet in God's family he found mothers, brothers and sisters. In my church, is there someone like Phoebe, Prisca, Mary or Rufus's mother who serves quietly and whom I have never properly thanked? Today, can I follow Paul's example and write a few lines or make a call, thanking them by name? Think too: is there someone far from home, an international student, a migrant worker, a new immigrant, a new believer, who needs someone to treat them the way Rufus's mother treated Paul? Finally, we have finished the sixteen chapters of Romans. Paul spent eleven chapters on the gospel and five on the life it brings, and ended with a long list of names to tell us that the gospel is not an abstract doctrine but a family built among real people. As I finish this book, may I say with Paul, "To the only wise God be glory forevermore through Jesus Christ! Amen."
