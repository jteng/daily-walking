## 这卷书在讲什么

提多书是保罗写给同工提多的一封短信。保罗曾和提多一同在克里特，后来保罗离开，把提多留下：「我从前留你在克里特，是要你将那没有办完的事都办整齐了，又照我所吩咐你的，在各城设立长老。」（1:5）

克里特的教会还很年轻，问题却不少。有人「不服约束，说虚空话欺哄人」，「败坏人的全家」（1:10-11）；有人「说是认识神，行事却和他相背」（1:16）。提多要在这样的地方设立长老，教导各样的人怎样生活，又要提醒众人怎样在社会上作见证。

这封信只有三章，却把两件事紧紧连在一起：
- **纯正的道理：** 信中一再讲到「真实的道理」「纯正的教训」（1:9）和「纯正的道理」（2:1）。
- **好的行为：** 「善事」「善行」「为善」这类说法，在这封短信里出现了好几次（1:16；2:7、14；3:1、8、14；其中3:8、14和合本译作「正经事业」）。保罗要信徒「凡事尊荣我们救主－神的道」（2:10）。

把这两件事连起来的，是福音。信中有两段福音的总结，是全书的核心：
- **恩典的教导：** 「因为神救众人的恩典已经显明出来，教训我们除去不敬虔的心和世俗的情欲，在今世自守、公义、敬虔度日。」（2:11-12）
- **怜悯的拯救：** 「他便救了我们；并不是因我们自己所行的义，乃是照他的怜悯，藉着重生的洗和圣灵的更新。」（3:5）

信徒行善，不是为了得救，而是因为已经得救了：恩典在教导他，圣灵在更新他。

## 作者与写作背景

**1. 提多是谁。** 使徒行传没有提到提多的名字，我们对他的认识主要来自保罗的书信。
- **一个外邦人：** 「与我同去的提多，虽是希腊人，也没有勉强他受割礼。」（加2:3）保罗带他上耶路撒冷，他成了外邦人不必受割礼就能得救的活见证。
- **处理难事的同工：** 哥林多教会和保罗关系紧张的时候，保罗派提多去。提多带回好消息，「那安慰丧气之人的神藉着提多来安慰了我们」（林后7:6）。后来保罗又派他去，把为耶路撒冷信徒捐钱的事办成（林后8:6、16-17）。保罗称他为「我的同伴，一同为你们劳碌的」（林后8:23）。
- **后来的行踪：** 提摩太后书说「提多往挞马太去」（提后4:10），挞马太在今天的巴尔干半岛一带。优西比乌的《教会史》（第三卷第四章）记载，按照传统，提多后来作了克里特众教会的监督。

**2. 克里特。** 克里特是地中海东部的一个大岛，在希腊南边。
- **五旬节时已有克里特人：** 五旬节那天在耶路撒冷，有「克里特和阿拉伯人，都听见他们用我们的乡谈，讲说神的大作为」（徒2:11）。福音可能很早就传到了岛上。
- **保罗曾经路过：** 保罗被押往罗马时，船曾「贴着克里特」航行，停在一个叫「佳澳」的地方（徒27:7-8）。但那时他是囚犯，不太可能在岛上建立教会。
- **岛上的名声：** 当时的希腊人常常取笑克里特人说谎。保罗在1:12引了一位克里特诗人的话：「克里特人常说谎话，乃是恶兽，又馋又懒。」古代的作者大多认为这句话出自主前六世纪的诗人埃庇米尼得斯。

**3. 写作时间。** 使徒行传没有记载保罗在克里特传道。所以许多学者认为，保罗在罗马第一次被囚后获释，和提多一同到了克里特，然后把提多留在那里；这封信大约写于主后62-64年，和提摩太前书差不多同时。罗马的革利免在主后一世纪末说，保罗到了西方的尽头（《革利免一书》5.7），许多人认为这是保罗获释后再出外旅行的线索。也有少数学者（如约翰·罗宾逊）认为，克里特的工作可以放在使徒行传的时期之内。本研读采用前一种看法。保罗说他「已经定意在那里过冬」（3:12）。这个尼哥坡里很可能是希腊西北部的那座同名城市。

**4. 作者问题。** 提多书和提摩太前书、提摩太后书合称教牧书信，关于作者的讨论可参看提摩太前书的导读。本研读照信本身所说的（1:1），把它当作保罗的书信来读。

**5. 克里特的问题。** 从信中看，克里特的假教师有几个特点：
- **犹太的色彩：** 「那奉割礼的更是这样」（1:10），他们讲「犹太人荒谬的言语」（1:14），又有「家谱的空谈」和「因律法而起的争竞」（3:9）。
- **洁净的规条：** 保罗回答说：「在洁净的人，凡物都洁净」（1:15），可见他们可能在食物或洁净的事上给人加上规条。
- **贪财：** 「他们因贪不义之财，将不该教导的教导人」（1:11）。
- **言行不一：** 「他们说是认识神，行事却和他相背」（1:16）。

## 钥匙经文：1:1-2；2:11-14；3:4-7

**1. 「那无谎言的神」。** 「神的仆人，耶稣基督的使徒保罗，凭着神选民的信心与敬虔真理的知识，盼望那无谎言的神在万古之先所应许的永生。」（1:1-2）在一个以说谎出名的地方，保罗一开头就讲到「那无谎言的神」。有些解经家提到，克里特有一个传说，说宙斯死了，坟墓就在岛上；保罗所传的神却是永活、信实、从不说谎的神。

**2. 「神救众人的恩典已经显明出来」。** 「因为神救众人的恩典已经显明出来，教训我们除去不敬虔的心和世俗的情欲，在今世自守、公义、敬虔度日，等候所盼望的福，并等候至大的神和我们救主耶稣基督的荣耀显现。他为我们舍了自己，要赎我们脱离一切罪恶，又洁净我们，特作自己的子民，热心为善。」（2:11-14）恩典不只是赦免我们，也像一位老师，一天一天地「教训我们」怎样生活。

**3. 「照他的怜悯」。** 「但到了神－我们救主的恩慈和他向人所施的慈爱显明的时候，他便救了我们；并不是因我们自己所行的义，乃是照他的怜悯，藉着重生的洗和圣灵的更新。圣灵就是神藉着耶稣基督－我们救主厚厚浇灌在我们身上的，好叫我们因他的恩得称为义，可以凭着永生的盼望成为后嗣。」（3:4-7）这几节经文里，父、子、圣灵都在救恩中作工：父施怜悯，子是我们的救主，圣灵使我们重生更新。

## 全书结构

提多书是一封书信，大致可以这样分：
- **问安**（1:1-4）：那无谎言的神所应许的永生。
- **设立长老**（1:5-9）：长老要无可指责，坚守真实的道理。
- **抵挡假教师**（1:10-16）：堵住说虚空话之人的口；在洁净的人，凡物都洁净。
- **各样的人当怎样生活**（2:1-10）：老年人、老年妇人、少年妇人、少年人、提多自己、作仆人的。
- **恩典的教导**（2:11-15）：神救众人的恩典已经显明出来。
- **在社会上作见证**（3:1-8）：顺服作官的，向众人大显温柔；因为我们从前也是这样，神却照他的怜悯救了我们。
- **最后的嘱咐和问安**（3:9-15）：远避无益的辩论；警戒分门结党的人；要学习正经事业。

## 怎样读这卷书

**1. 留意「救主」。** 这封短信六次称神或基督为「救主」，常常成对出现：1:3「神－我们救主」，1:4「我们的救主基督耶稣」；2:10和2:13；3:4和3:6。父与子同被称为救主。2:13的「至大的神和我们救主耶稣基督」，和合本的译法可以读成指两位；但许多学者根据原文的语法认为，「至大的神」和「救主」都是指耶稣基督。英文标准译本就是这样译的（参第二章）。

**2. 留意吩咐和「因为」。** 保罗常常先讲要怎样生活，然后用「因为」讲出福音的根据（2:11；3:3。3:3和合本没有译出，但原文开头也有一个表示原因的连词）。读的时候，可以问：这条吩咐的根据，是基督做了什么？

**3. 留意外人的眼光。** 保罗好几次提到外人怎样看信徒：「免得神的道理被毁谤」（2:5），「叫那反对的人……便自觉羞愧」（2:8），「凡事尊荣我们救主－神的道」（2:10）。信徒的生活会被外人看见；我们要人看见的，不是我们自己有多体面，而是我们救主的美。

**4. 读难解的经文时要谦卑。** 「儿女也是信主的」（1:6）、少年妇人「料理家务……顺服自己的丈夫」（2:5）、「重生的洗」（3:5），历代信徒有不同的理解。我们要把不同的看法公平地摆出来，在意见不同时彼此尊重。

## 默想

**默想：** 「盼望那无谎言的神在万古之先所应许的永生。」（1:2）我这一生听过多少没有兑现的应许？小时候，父亲说这个周末一定带我出去，后来又说工作太忙；有人说会一直爱我，后来却离开了；老板说明年一定加薪，朋友说钱下个月就还。慢慢地，我学会了不太相信别人的话，也不太敢指望什么。我自己也一样，许过很多愿，答应过很多事，有的忘了，有的做不到，有的根本是为了应付才说的。克里特人以说谎出名，保罗却在信的第一句就说：有一位「无谎言的神」。希伯来书也说：「神决不能说谎。」（来6:18）他应许的事，在「万古之先」就定下了，「到了日期」，他就藉着福音显明出来（多1:3）。他没有只在口头上应许，而是差他的儿子来；「神的应许，不论有多少，在基督都是是的」（林后1:20）。基督为我们舍了自己（多2:14），那位说话算话的神，是付上了代价来守他的应许的。我失信的地方，他没有因此收回他的应许；基督也为我这些失信的罪舍了自己。今天，我可以把一个让我失望过的应许，或者一个我自己没有守住的应许，带到他面前。我不必再用别人的失信去量他的信实。我可以对他说：主啊，人会失信，我也会失信；但你的应许在基督里都是是的。我今天藉着他向你说阿们，「叫神因我们得荣耀」（林后1:20）。

## What This Book Is About

Titus is a short letter Paul wrote to his coworker Titus. Paul and Titus had been in Crete together; then Paul left and Titus stayed behind: "This is why I left you in Crete, so that you might put what remained into order, and appoint elders in every town as I directed you" (1:5).

The church in Crete was young, and it had many problems. Some were "insubordinate, empty talkers and deceivers," "upsetting whole families" (1:10–11); some "profess to know God, but they deny him by their works" (1:16). Titus was to appoint elders in such a place, teach people of every kind how to live, and remind everyone how to bear witness in society.

The letter has only three chapters, but it binds two things tightly together:
- **Sound doctrine:** the letter keeps speaking of "sound doctrine" (1:9; 2:1) and "the trustworthy word as taught" (1:9).
- **Good works:** "good works" or "good deeds" come up again and again in this short letter (1:16; 2:7, 14; 3:1, 8, 14). Paul wants believers to "adorn the doctrine of God our Savior" in everything (2:10).

What joins the two is the gospel. The letter contains two summaries of the gospel, which are the heart of the book:
- **Grace that trains:** "For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people, training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in the present age" (2:11–12).
- **Mercy that saves:** "He saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit" (3:5).

Believers do good works not in order to be saved, but because they have been saved, trained by grace and renewed by the Spirit.

## Author and Setting

**1. Who was Titus?** Acts never mentions Titus by name; what we know of him comes mainly from Paul's letters.
- **A Gentile:** "But even Titus, who was with me, was not forced to be circumcised, though he was a Greek" (Gal 2:3). Paul took him to Jerusalem, and he became living proof that Gentiles are saved without circumcision.
- **A coworker for hard tasks:** when relations between Paul and the Corinthian church were strained, Paul sent Titus. Titus brought back good news: "But God, who comforts the downcast, comforted us by the coming of Titus" (2 Cor 7:6). Later Paul sent him to complete the collection for the believers in Jerusalem (2 Cor 8:6, 16–17). Paul calls him "my partner and fellow worker for your benefit" (2 Cor 8:23).
- **Later movements:** Second Timothy says that Titus had gone "to Dalmatia" (2 Tim 4:10), in today's Balkans. Eusebius's *Church History* (book 3, chapter 4) records the tradition that Titus later became overseer of the churches in Crete.

**2. Crete.** Crete is a large island in the eastern Mediterranean, south of Greece.
- **Cretans at Pentecost:** on the day of Pentecost in Jerusalem there were "Cretans and Arabians" who heard the apostles "telling in our own tongues the mighty works of God" (Acts 2:11). The gospel may have reached the island early.
- **Paul had passed by:** when Paul was being taken to Rome, his ship "sailed under the lee of Crete" and stopped at a place called Fair Havens (Acts 27:7–8). But he was a prisoner then and is unlikely to have founded churches on the island.
- **The island's reputation:** Greeks of the time often mocked Cretans as liars. In 1:12 Paul quotes a Cretan poet: "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons." Most ancient writers attributed the line to the sixth-century BC poet Epimenides.

**3. Date.** Acts does not record Paul preaching in Crete. So many scholars think that after his release from his first Roman imprisonment, Paul went to Crete with Titus and then left him there, and that the letter was written around AD 62–64, about the same time as 1 Timothy. At the end of the first century, Clement of Rome says Paul reached "the limits of the West" (*1 Clement* 5.7), which many take as a hint of travels after his release. A few scholars (such as John A. T. Robinson) place the Cretan mission within the period covered by Acts. This study follows the first view. Nicopolis, where Paul says he has "decided to spend the winter" (3:12), was most likely the city of that name in northwestern Greece.

**4. Authorship.** Titus, 1 Timothy and 2 Timothy are together called the Pastoral Letters; for the authorship debate, see the introduction to 1 Timothy. This study reads the letter as it presents itself (1:1), as a letter of Paul.

**5. The problems in Crete.** From the letter, the false teachers in Crete had several marks:
- **A Jewish flavor:** "especially those of the circumcision party" (1:10); they taught "Jewish myths" (1:14), "genealogies" and "quarrels about the law" (3:9).
- **Purity rules:** Paul answers, "To the pure, all things are pure" (1:15), which suggests they were imposing rules about food or purity.
- **Greed:** they were "teaching for shameful gain what they ought not to teach" (1:11).
- **Words and deeds at odds:** "They profess to know God, but they deny him by their works" (1:16).

## Key Verses: 1:1–2; 2:11–14; 3:4–7

**1. "God, who never lies."** "Paul, a servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, for the sake of the faith of God's elect and their knowledge of the truth, which accords with godliness, in hope of eternal life, which God, who never lies, promised before the ages began" (1:1–2). In a place famous for lying, Paul begins by speaking of "God, who never lies." Some commentators note the Cretan legend that Zeus had died and that his tomb was on the island; the God Paul preached is the living, faithful God who never lies.

**2. "The grace of God has appeared."** "For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people, training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in the present age, waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ, who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works" (2:11–14). Grace does not only forgive us; like a teacher, it trains us day by day in how to live.

**3. "According to his own mercy."** "But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit, whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior, so that being justified by his grace we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life" (3:4–7). In these verses Father, Son and Holy Spirit all work in salvation: the Father shows mercy, the Son is our Savior, and the Spirit regenerates and renews us.

## Structure of the Book

Titus is a letter, and it can be outlined like this:
- **Greeting** (1:1–4): eternal life, promised by God, who never lies.
- **Appointing elders** (1:5–9): elders must be above reproach and hold firm to the trustworthy word.
- **Opposing false teachers** (1:10–16): silence the empty talkers; to the pure, all things are pure.
- **How people of every kind should live** (2:1–10): older men, older women, younger women, younger men, Titus himself, slaves.
- **Grace that trains** (2:11–15): the grace of God has appeared.
- **Witness in society** (3:1–8): be submissive to rulers and show perfect courtesy toward all, for we too were once like this, but God saved us according to his mercy.
- **Final instructions and greetings** (3:9–15): avoid foolish controversies; warn a divisive person; learn to devote yourselves to good works.

## How to Read This Book

**1. Watch for "Savior."** This short letter calls God or Christ "Savior" six times, often in pairs: 1:3 "God our Savior" and 1:4 "Christ Jesus our Savior"; 2:10 and 2:13; 3:4 and 3:6. The Father and the Son are both called Savior. In 2:13, "our great God and Savior Jesus Christ," many scholars take both titles, on the basis of the Greek grammar, to refer to Jesus Christ (see the discussion in chapter 2).

**2. Watch for the command and the "for."** Paul often first says how to live, and then gives the gospel ground with "for" (2:11; 3:3; in 3:3 the Greek has "for," though not every translation shows it). As you read, ask: what has Christ done that grounds this command?

**3. Watch for how outsiders see us.** Paul mentions several times how outsiders look at believers: "that the word of God may not be reviled" (2:5); "so that an opponent may be put to shame" (2:8); "so that in everything they may adorn the doctrine of God our Savior" (2:10). A believer's life will be seen by outsiders; what we want them to see is not our respectability but the beauty of our Savior.

**4. Read the difficult passages humbly.** "His children are believers" (1:6), younger women "working at home... and submissive to their own husbands" (2:5), and "the washing of regeneration" (3:5) have been understood differently by faithful believers through the centuries. We will set out the different views fairly and respect one another when we disagree.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "In hope of eternal life, which God, who never lies, promised before the ages began" (1:2). How many promises have I heard in my life that were never kept? When I was a child, my father said he would definitely take me out this weekend, and then said work was too busy. Someone said, "I will always love you," and then left. A boss said there would definitely be a raise next year; a friend said the money would be paid back next month. Slowly I learned not to believe people's words too much, and not to dare hope for much. I am the same: I have made many vows and agreed to many things, and some I forgot, some I could not do, and some I said only to get out of the moment. Cretans were famous for lying, yet in the very first sentence of his letter Paul says there is a "God, who never lies." Hebrews says too that "it is impossible for God to lie" (Heb 6:18). What he promised was settled "before the ages began," and "at the proper time" he made it known through the gospel (Titus 1:3). He did not promise with words only; he sent his Son. "For all the promises of God find their Yes in him" (2 Cor 1:20). Christ "gave himself for us" (Titus 2:14): the God whose word holds paid a price to keep his promise. Where I broke my word, he did not withdraw his; Christ gave himself for these broken promises of mine too. Today I can bring to him a promise that once let me down, or a promise I myself did not keep. I no longer need to measure his faithfulness by other people's broken words. I can say to him: Lord, people break their word, and so do I; but all your promises find their Yes in Christ, and today, through him, I say my Amen to you, for your glory (2 Cor 1:20).
