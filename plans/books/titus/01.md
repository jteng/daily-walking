## 本章脉络

第一章分成三段。**问安**（1-4节）：保罗是神的仆人、耶稣基督的使徒，为了神选民的信心和敬虔真理的知识；他盼望那无谎言的神在万古之先所应许的永生；提多是照着共信之道作他真儿子的。**设立长老**（5-9节）：保罗留提多在克里特，要他把没有办完的事办整齐，在各城设立长老；长老要无可指责，只作一个妇人的丈夫，儿女也是信主的；监督是神的管家，不任性，不暴躁，乐意接待远人，坚守所教真实的道理。**堵住说虚空话之人的口**（10-16节）：有许多人不服约束，说虚空话欺哄人，为不义之财败坏人的全家；一位克里特诗人说「克里特人常说谎话」，这个见证是真的；在洁净的人，凡物都洁净；他们说是认识神，行事却和他相背。

这一章从「那无谎言的神」（2节）开始，以「说是认识神，行事却和他相背」的人（16节）结束。神说话算话，他的应许必定成就；他的百姓，特别是带领他们的长老，也要言行一致。长老的品格和假教师的样子，正好成了对比。

## 深读 1:1-4 那无谎言的神所应许的永生

**1. 「神的仆人，耶稣基督的使徒」。** 「神的仆人，耶稣基督的使徒保罗，凭着神选民的信心与敬虔真理的知识。」（1:1）
- **仆人和使徒：** 保罗在别的书信中称自己为仆人的时候，都说是「基督耶稣的仆人」（罗1:1；腓1:1），只有这里称为「神的仆人」。这是旧约对摩西、大卫、众先知的称呼（参代上6:49；但9:11）。保罗站在他们的行列中。
- **「凭着」：** 这个词在这里的意思大概是为了（也有人理解为按照）：保罗作使徒，是为了叫神的选民有信心，并认识那合乎敬虔的真理。
- **「敬虔真理的知识」：** 真理和敬虔是连在一起的。真正认识真理的人，生活也会合乎敬虔；这是全书的主题，也正和16节那些「说是认识神，行事却和他相背」的人相反。

**2. 「那无谎言的神在万古之先所应许的永生」。** 「盼望那无谎言的神在万古之先所应许的永生，到了日期，藉着传扬的工夫把他的道显明了；这传扬的责任是按着神－我们救主的命令交托了我。」（1:2-3）
- **「无谎言的神」：** 希伯来书说，在神的应许和起誓这两件不更改的事上，「神决不能说谎」（来6:18）。神不说谎，不是因为有什么更高的规矩管着他，而是因为他自己就是真实的。
- **在克里特说这句话：** 克里特以说谎出名（12节）。保罗在这样的地方，先讲这位从不说谎的神。人的话靠不住，神的话却靠得住。
- **「万古之先」和「到了日期」：** 永生的应许是在创世以前就定下的（参提后1:9），到了神所定的时候，藉着传福音显明出来。救恩不是神临时想出的办法，而是他永远的计划。

**3. 「照着我们共信之道作我真儿子的」。** 「现在写信给提多，就是照着我们共信之道作我真儿子的。愿恩惠、平安从父神和我们的救主基督耶稣归与你！」（1:4）
- **「共信之道」：** 保罗是犹太人，提多是希腊人（加2:3）。他们本来分属两个世界，却在同一个信仰里成了父子。
- **两位救主：** 第3节称「神－我们救主」，第4节称「我们的救主基督耶稣」。父与子都被称为救主。

> 要点：在一个以说谎出名的地方，保罗先讲那无谎言的神。他在万古之先应许了永生，到了日期就藉着福音显明出来；人的话会落空，他的应许必定成就。

## 深读 1:5-9 在各城设立长老

**1. 「将那没有办完的事都办整齐了」。** 「我从前留你在克里特，是要你将那没有办完的事都办整齐了，又照我所吩咐你的，在各城设立长老。」（1:5）
- **没有办完的事：** 教会已经建立，但还缺少秩序和带领的人。保罗没有自己留下来做完，而是把这件事交给提多。
- **「在各城」：** 克里特有许多城市。每一处的教会都需要本地的长老来牧养。
- **长老和监督：** 第5节说「长老」，第7节说「监督」，讲的是同一群人。新约中这两个称呼常常指同一个职分（参徒20:17、28）：「长老」着重这人成熟可敬，「监督」着重他照管教会的工作。

**2. 「只作一个妇人的丈夫，儿女也是信主的」。** 「若有无可指责的人，只作一个妇人的丈夫，儿女也是信主的，没有人告他们是放荡不服约束的，就可以设立。」（1:6）
- **「无可指责」：** 这不是说长老要完美无罪，而是说没有人能拿出确实的事来控告他。这个词在6、7节出现了两次。
- **「只作一个妇人的丈夫」：** 和提摩太前书3:2的说法一样。多数现代解经家（如奈特、孟恩斯、汤纳）认为，重点是在婚姻中对妻子忠诚；另外的理解可参看提摩太前书第3章的讨论。
- **「儿女也是信主的」：** 原文这个词可以译作「信主的」，也可以译作忠心的、可靠的。有人认为是指儿女已经信主（和合本和许多现代译本都这样译）；他们指出，在教牧书信里这个词常常指信主的人（提前4:3、10、12；5:16；6:2），而且克里特的教会刚刚建立，长老的家是否已经归主，自然是一个问题。也有人（如奈特）认为是指儿女忠心、靠得住，和提摩太前书3:4「使儿女凡事端庄顺服」相合，因为父母不能替孩子信主；他们也指出，紧接着的「没有人告他们是放荡不服约束的」，讲的正是儿女的行为。
- **不是要求完美的家庭：** 无论哪一种理解，这都是在讲家里还有孩子、在父亲带领之下的情形；不是说孩子长大成人以后的每一个选择，都要由父母负责。

**3. 「监督既是神的管家」。** 「监督既是神的管家，必须无可指责，不任性，不暴躁，不因酒滋事，不打人，不贪无义之财；乐意接待远人，好善，庄重，公平，圣洁自持。」（1:7-8）
- **「神的管家」：** 管家管理的是主人的家，不是自己的家。教会是神的家，长老只是替神管理，将来要向神交账。
- **五个「不」：** 不任性（不固执己见、只顾自己），不暴躁，不因酒滋事，不打人，不贪无义之财。这几样都是滥用权力的人常有的样子。
- **「不打人」：** 这包括在家中。一个殴打妻子、虐待儿女的人，不能作长老。家暴和虐待儿童是罪行，向警方举报是正当的（罗13:4），教会也应当帮助受害的人这样做；受害的人不必留在危险中，也不必和不安全的人和好。教会不可为了面子、「家丑不可外扬」而包庇。即使这人后来认罪悔改，饶恕也不会恢复他的职分，也不等于让他再接近受害的人或孩子。
- **六个「要」：** 乐意接待远人，好善，庄重，公平，圣洁，自持。当时的客店不多，名声也常常不好，旅途又危险；接待来往的信徒是很实际的爱。
- **和耶稣的对比：** 耶稣说：「外邦人有尊为君王的，治理他们……只是在你们中间，不是这样。」（可10:42-43）带领神家的人，是照着那位「来，并不是要受人的服事，乃是要服事人」（可10:45）的主的样式。

**4. 「坚守所教真实的道理」。** 「坚守所教真实的道理，就能将纯正的教训劝化人，又能把争辩的人驳倒了。」（1:9）
- **两件工作：** 长老要用纯正的教训劝勉人，也要指出错误、驳倒争辩的人。只会安慰不会纠正，羊群会被狼吃掉；只会驳斥不会劝勉，羊群会饿死。
- **「坚守」：** 长老自己先要紧紧抓住神的道，才能教导别人。他不是在推销自己的意见，而是传讲所领受的。

> 要点：教会需要品格可靠、坚守真道的长老。他们是神的管家，不是主人；他们的样式，是那位来服事人、为羊舍命的好牧人（约10:11）。

## 深读 1:10-16 他们说是认识神，行事却和他相背

**1. 「这些人的口总要堵住」。** 「因为有许多人不服约束，说虚空话欺哄人；那奉割礼的更是这样。这些人的口总要堵住。他们因贪不义之财，将不该教导的教导人，败坏人的全家。」（1:10-11）
- **「那奉割礼的」：** 大概指有犹太背景的教师；从1:14-15和3:9看，他们讲的是犹太人的传说、家谱和洁净的规条，和加拉太的问题相近，但不完全一样（参徒15:1、5；加2:12）。
- **「败坏人的全家」：** 原文的意思是推翻、颠覆。当时的教会在家中聚会，假教师一家一家地把人带偏（参提后3:6）。
- **「堵住」：** 这不是用暴力，而是用纯正的道理驳倒他们（9节），不让他们继续在教会里教导。

**2. 「克里特人常说谎话」。** 「有克里特人中的一个本地先知说：『克里特人常说谎话，乃是恶兽，又馋又懒。』这个见证是真的。」（1:12-13上）
- **这位「先知」：** 古代的作者（如亚历山大的革利免、耶柔米）大多认为，这句话出自主前六世纪的克里特诗人埃庇米尼得斯，当时有人称他为先知（金口约翰则认为出自卡利马科斯）。主前三世纪的诗人卡利马科斯也用过前半句同样的话，说克里特人连宙斯的坟墓都造了出来。保罗称他为「本地先知」，是用克里特人自己的话，不是说他是神的先知。保罗在雅典也引用过希腊诗人的话（徒17:28）。
- **「这个见证是真的」：** 保罗说这句话，是针对那些在克里特说谎、贪财的假教师，指出他们正好应验了本地人自己的评语。这不是叫我们给一个民族或一个地方的人贴标签。保罗在3:3也说「我们从前也是……」，他没有把自己放在克里特人之上。
- **今天怎样应用？** 每一种文化都有它习以为常的罪。福音不是叫我们去讥笑别的地方的人，而是叫我们诚实地看自己的文化里那些大家都觉得「正常」的罪。

**3. 「在洁净的人，凡物都洁净」。** 「所以，你要严严地责备他们，使他们在真道上纯全无疵，不听犹太人荒谬的言语和离弃真道之人的诫命。在洁净的人，凡物都洁净；在污秽不信的人，什么都不洁净，连心地和天良也都污秽了。」（1:13下-15）
- **「严严地责备」：** 目的是「使他们在真道上纯全无疵」。责备是为了挽回，不是为了羞辱。
- **洁净的问题：** 假教师可能在食物或洁净的礼仪上给人加上规条（参提前4:3）。耶稣说：「入口的不能污秽人，出口的乃能污秽人。」（太15:11）马可也说：「这是说，各样的食物都是洁净的。」（可7:19）
- **问题在心里：** 心若污秽，什么都不洁净；心若被基督洗净（参徒15:9「藉着信洁净了他们的心」），神所造的物就都可以感谢着领受（参提前4:4）。这不是说信徒可以做任何事，而是说洁净不是靠守外面的规条得来的。

**4. 「他们说是认识神，行事却和他相背」。** 「他们说是认识神，行事却和他相背；本是可憎恶的，是悖逆的，在各样善事上是可废弃的。」（1:16）
- **言行不一：** 他们口里承认神，生活却在否认神。约翰也说：「人若说『我认识他』，却不遵守他的诫命，便是说谎话的。」（约一2:4）
- **「可废弃的」：** 这个词原指经过试验、被证明不合格的东西。他们在善事上通不过试验。
- **对照第1节：** 保罗作使徒，是为了叫人认识「敬虔真理」；假教师却「说是认识神」，行事却相背。真正的认识神，会结出敬虔的果子。

> 要点：假教师的问题，是口里认识神，生活却否认神。洁净不是靠外面的规条，而是靠被基督洗净的心；真正认识神的人，会在善事上显出来。

## 默想

**默想：** 「有克里特人中的一个本地先知说：『克里特人常说谎话，乃是恶兽，又馋又懒。』这个见证是真的。」（1:12-13）我们很容易拿这句话去说别人：哪个地方的人最会骗人，哪个民族的人最懒。可是我想先问自己：在我自己的文化里，有哪些罪是大家都觉得「正常」的？生意场上收一点回扣，酒桌上硬劝人喝酒，对外人一套、对自己人一套，为了面子说一些客套的假话。我也常对自己说：我们这里都是这样的，不这样就办不成事。保罗没有替克里特人找借口，他说「这个见证是真的」。可是他也没有放弃克里特人。神把提多留在那里，要在那些「常说谎话」的人中间，建立一个说真话的教会。五旬节那天，在耶路撒冷的人群中就有克里特人，他们听见门徒「用我们的乡谈，讲说神的大作为」（徒2:11）。福音没有绕过任何一种文化，而是进到每一种文化里面，用那里的话说话，也洗净那里的罪。羔羊「曾被杀，用自己的血从各族、各方、各民、各国中买了人来，叫他们归于神」（启5:9）。他用自己的血把我买回来，也包括我那些习以为常、连自己都不再觉得是罪的地方。今天，我可以在主面前说出一样我们这里都是这样的罪，承认它，不再替它找借口。我不必靠自己的文化有多好来站立，也不必因自己的文化有多坏而绝望；我是被那位无谎言的神，用他儿子的血买回来的人。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 1 falls into three parts. **Greeting** (vv. 1–4): Paul is a servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, for the sake of the faith of God's elect and their knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness; he hopes for eternal life, promised before the ages began by God, who never lies; Titus is his true child in a common faith. **Appointing elders** (vv. 5–9): Paul left Titus in Crete to put what remained into order and appoint elders in every town; an elder must be above reproach, the husband of one wife, with believing children; an overseer is God's steward, not arrogant or quick-tempered, but hospitable, holding firm to the trustworthy word. **Silencing the empty talkers** (vv. 10–16): many are insubordinate, empty talkers and deceivers, upsetting whole families for shameful gain; a Cretan poet said, "Cretans are always liars," and this testimony is true; to the pure, all things are pure; they profess to know God but deny him by their works.

This chapter begins with "God, who never lies" (v. 2) and ends with those who "profess to know God, but they deny him by their works" (v. 16). God keeps his word, and his promises will surely be fulfilled. His people, and especially the elders who lead them, are to be people whose words and lives match. The character of the elders and the conduct of the false teachers stand in deliberate contrast.

## Going Deeper: 1:1–4 Eternal Life, Promised by God, Who Never Lies

**1. "A servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ."** "Paul, a servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, for the sake of the faith of God's elect and their knowledge of the truth, which accords with godliness" (1:1).
- **Servant and apostle:** when Paul elsewhere calls himself a servant, it is "of Christ Jesus" (Rom 1:1; Phil 1:1); only here does he call himself a "servant of God." This is the Old Testament title for Moses, David and the prophets (see 1 Chr 6:49; Dan 9:11). Paul stands in their line.
- **"For the sake of":** here the word probably means "for the sake of" (some read "in accordance with"): Paul is an apostle so that God's elect may have faith and come to know the truth that accords with godliness.
- **"The truth, which accords with godliness":** truth and godliness belong together. Those who truly know the truth will live godly lives. This is the theme of the whole letter, and the opposite of those in verse 16 who "profess to know God, but they deny him by their works."

**2. "Eternal life, which God, who never lies, promised before the ages began."** "In hope of eternal life, which God, who never lies, promised before the ages began and at the proper time manifested in his word through the preaching with which I have been entrusted by the command of God our Savior" (1:2–3).
- **"God, who never lies":** Hebrews says that in the two unchangeable things of God's promise and oath, "it is impossible for God to lie" (Heb 6:18). God does not lie, not because some higher rule binds him, but because he himself is true.
- **Said in Crete:** Crete was famous for lying (v. 12). In such a place Paul first speaks of the God who never lies. People's words cannot be relied on; God's word can.
- **"Before the ages began" and "at the proper time":** the promise of eternal life was settled before creation (see 2 Tim 1:9) and, at the time God appointed, made known through the preaching of the gospel. Salvation is not a plan God thought up on the spot; it is his eternal purpose.

**3. "My true child in a common faith."** "To Titus, my true child in a common faith: Grace and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior" (1:4).
- **"A common faith":** Paul was a Jew, Titus a Greek (Gal 2:3). They came from two different worlds, yet in one faith they became father and son.
- **Two Saviors named:** verse 3 says "God our Savior," and verse 4 says "Christ Jesus our Savior." The Father and the Son are both called Savior.

> The point: In a place famous for lying, Paul first speaks of the God who never lies. He promised eternal life before the ages began and at the proper time made it known through the gospel. People's words can fail; his promise will surely be fulfilled.

## Going Deeper: 1:5–9 Appoint Elders in Every Town

**1. "Put what remained into order."** "This is why I left you in Crete, so that you might put what remained into order, and appoint elders in every town as I directed you" (1:5).
- **What remained:** the churches had been founded but still lacked order and leaders. Paul did not stay to finish the work himself; he entrusted it to Titus.
- **"In every town":** Crete had many towns. Each church needed local elders to shepherd it.
- **Elders and overseers:** verse 5 says "elders" and verse 7 says "overseer," and they refer to the same people. In the New Testament the two titles often name one office (see Acts 20:17, 28): "elder" stresses the person's maturity and standing, "overseer" his work of watching over the church.

**2. "The husband of one wife, and his children are believers."** "If anyone is above reproach, the husband of one wife, and his children are believers and not open to the charge of debauchery or insubordination" (1:6).
- **"Above reproach":** this does not mean an elder must be sinless, but that no one can bring a well-founded charge against him. The word appears twice, in verses 6 and 7.
- **"The husband of one wife":** the same phrase as in 1 Timothy 3:2. Most modern commentators (such as Knight, Mounce and Towner) think the point is faithfulness to one's wife in marriage; for the other readings, see the discussion in 1 Timothy 3.
- **"His children are believers":** the Greek word can mean "believing" or "faithful, trustworthy." Some take it to mean the children have come to faith (so the CUV and many modern translations, including the ESV); they note that in the Pastoral Letters this word usually means "believer" (1 Tim 4:3, 10, 12; 5:16; 6:2), and that in a newly planted church whether an elder's household had come to faith was a natural question. Others (such as Knight) take it to mean the children are faithful and trustworthy, which fits 1 Timothy 3:4, "keeping his children submissive," since parents cannot believe on their children's behalf; they also note that the next phrase, "not open to the charge of debauchery or insubordination," is about the children's conduct.
- **Not a demand for a perfect family:** on either reading, this concerns children still at home under their father's care. It does not mean that every choice a grown child makes is the parents' responsibility.

**3. "An overseer, as God's steward."** "For an overseer, as God's steward, must be above reproach. He must not be arrogant or quick-tempered or a drunkard or violent or greedy for gain, but hospitable, a lover of good, self-controlled, upright, holy, and disciplined" (1:7–8).
- **"God's steward":** a steward manages his master's household, not his own. The church is God's household; elders manage it on God's behalf and will give account to him.
- **Five "nots":** not arrogant (self-willed, insisting on his own way), not quick-tempered, not a drunkard, not violent, not greedy for gain. These are the marks of people who abuse power.
- **"Not violent":** this includes at home. A man who beats his wife or abuses his children cannot be an elder. Domestic violence and child abuse are crimes; reporting them to the police is right (Rom 13:4), and the church should help victims do so. Victims need not stay in danger or reconcile with someone unsafe. The church must not cover it up to save face ("don't air the family's dirty laundry"). Even if he later confesses and repents, forgiveness does not restore his office or give him access to those he harmed or to children.
- **Six positives:** hospitable, a lover of good, self-controlled, upright, holy, disciplined. In an age when inns were few and often disreputable, and roads were dangerous, welcoming traveling believers was practical love.
- **Jesus' contrast:** Jesus said, "those who are considered rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them... But it shall not be so among you" (Mark 10:42–43). Those who lead God's household follow the pattern of the Lord who "came not to be served but to serve" (Mark 10:45).

**4. "He must hold firm to the trustworthy word as taught."** "He must hold firm to the trustworthy word as taught, so that he may be able to give instruction in sound doctrine and also to rebuke those who contradict it" (1:9).
- **Two tasks:** the elder must encourage with sound doctrine, and also expose error and answer those who contradict it. A shepherd who only comforts and never corrects leaves the flock to the wolves; one who only refutes and never encourages leaves the flock to starve.
- **"Hold firm":** the elder must first hold tightly to God's word himself before he can teach others. He is not selling his own opinions but passing on what he has received.

> The point: The church needs elders of trustworthy character who hold firm to the true word. They are God's stewards, not owners, and their pattern is the good shepherd who came to serve and laid down his life for the sheep (John 10:11).

## Going Deeper: 1:10–16 They Profess to Know God, but Deny Him by Their Works

**1. "They must be silenced."** "For there are many who are insubordinate, empty talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision party. They must be silenced, since they are upsetting whole families by teaching for shameful gain what they ought not to teach" (1:10–11).
- **"The circumcision party":** probably teachers of Jewish background; judging from 1:14–15 and 3:9, they taught Jewish myths, genealogies and purity rules, related to the Galatian problem but not identical (see Acts 15:1, 5; Gal 2:12).
- **"Upsetting whole families":** the word means to overturn. The churches met in homes, and the false teachers led people astray one household at a time (see 2 Tim 3:6).
- **"Silenced":** not by force, but by answering them with sound doctrine (v. 9) and not letting them go on teaching in the church.

**2. "Cretans are always liars."** "One of the Cretans, a prophet of their own, said, 'Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons.' This testimony is true" (1:12–13a).
- **This "prophet":** most ancient writers (such as Clement of Alexandria and Jerome) attributed the line to the sixth-century BC Cretan poet Epimenides, whom some called a prophet (Chrysostom credited it to Callimachus). The third-century BC poet Callimachus also used the same words as the first part, saying that Cretans had even made a tomb for Zeus. When Paul calls him "a prophet of their own," he is using the Cretans' own words, not saying he was a prophet of God. Paul also quoted Greek poets in Athens (Acts 17:28).
- **"This testimony is true":** Paul says this about the false teachers in Crete who lied and were greedy, pointing out that they fit the islanders' own description exactly. It is not a license for us to put labels on a whole people or region. In 3:3 Paul says "we ourselves were once..."; he does not set himself above the Cretans.
- **How does this apply today?** Every culture has its accepted sins. The gospel does not invite us to mock people from other places, but to look honestly at the sins in our own culture that everyone treats as "normal."

**3. "To the pure, all things are pure."** "Therefore rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith, not devoting themselves to Jewish myths and the commands of people who turn away from the truth. To the pure, all things are pure, but to the defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure; but both their minds and their consciences are defiled" (1:13b–15).
- **"Rebuke them sharply":** the aim is "that they may be sound in the faith." Rebuke is meant to restore, not to humiliate.
- **The question of purity:** the false teachers were probably imposing rules about food or ritual purity (see 1 Tim 4:3). Jesus said, "it is not what goes into the mouth that defiles a person, but what comes out of the mouth" (Matt 15:11). Mark adds, "(Thus he declared all foods clean.)" (Mark 7:19).
- **The problem is in the heart:** if the heart is defiled, nothing is pure; if the heart has been cleansed by Christ (see Acts 15:9, "having cleansed their hearts by faith"), everything God made can be received with thanksgiving (see 1 Tim 4:4). This does not mean believers may do anything at all; it means purity is not gained by keeping outward rules.

**4. "They profess to know God, but they deny him by their works."** "They profess to know God, but they deny him by their works. They are detestable, disobedient, unfit for any good work" (1:16).
- **Words and deeds at odds:** they confess God with their mouths and deny him with their lives. John says the same: "Whoever says 'I know him' but does not keep his commandments is a liar" (1 John 2:4).
- **"Unfit":** the word was used for something tested and found to fail. They fail the test when it comes to good works.
- **Compare verse 1:** Paul is an apostle so that people may know "the truth, which accords with godliness"; the false teachers "profess to know God" but deny him by their works. Truly knowing God bears the fruit of godliness.

> The point: The false teachers' problem was that they knew God with their mouths and denied him with their lives. Purity does not come from outward rules but from a heart cleansed by Christ, and those who truly know God show it in good works.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "One of the Cretans, a prophet of their own, said, 'Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy gluttons.' This testimony is true" (1:12–13). It is easy to turn this verse on others: which region's people are the biggest cheats, which people are the laziest. But I want to ask myself first: in my own culture, which sins does everyone treat as "normal"? A little kickback in business, pressing people to drink at banquets, one face for outsiders and another for our own, the polite untruths I tell to save face. I often tell myself: this is just how things are done here; without it nothing gets done. Paul did not make excuses for the Cretans; he said, "This testimony is true." But he did not give up on them either. God left Titus there to build, among people who were "always liars," a church that told the truth. On the day of Pentecost, in the crowd in Jerusalem, there were Cretans, and they heard the disciples "telling in our own tongues the mighty works of God" (Acts 2:11). The gospel does not bypass any culture; it comes into each one, speaks its language, and cleanses its sins. The Lamb was "slain, and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation" (Rev 5:9). He bought me back with his own blood, including the places in me so familiar that I no longer even feel they are sin. Today I can name before the Lord one sin of the "that's just how it's done here" kind, confess it, and stop making excuses for it. I do not need to stand on how good my culture is, nor despair at how bad it is; I am someone bought back by the blood of his Son, by the God who never lies.
