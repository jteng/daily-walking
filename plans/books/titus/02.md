## 本章脉络

第二章分成三段。**各样的人当怎样生活**（1-10节）：提多所讲的要合乎纯正的道理；老年人要有节制、端庄、自守；老年妇人要举止恭敬，用善道指教少年妇人；少年人要谨守；提多自己要作善行的榜样；作仆人的要显为忠诚，凡事尊荣我们救主－神的道。**恩典的教导**（11-14节）：神救众人的恩典已经显明出来，教训我们除去不敬虔的心，在今世自守、公义、敬虔度日，等候至大的神和我们救主耶稣基督的荣耀显现；他为我们舍了自己，要赎我们脱离一切罪恶，特作自己的子民，热心为善。**用权柄讲明**（15节）：这些事要讲明，劝戒人，责备人，不可叫人轻看。

这一章的转折在第11节的「因为」。前十节讲了许多具体的生活，好像一张很长的清单；第11节却说出这一切的根据：不是因为我们要做好人，而是「因为神救众人的恩典已经显明出来」。恩典不只救了我们，也一天一天地「教训我们」。

## 深读 2:1-6 合乎纯正道理的生活

**1. 「但你所讲的总要合乎那纯正的道理」。** 「但你所讲的总要合乎那纯正的道理。」（2:1）
- **「但你」：** 和第一章那些「说是认识神，行事却和他相背」的假教师相对（1:16）。
- **「合乎」：** 保罗接着讲的不是教义条文，而是怎样生活。纯正的道理一定会生出纯正的生活；生活也显明人所信的道理是不是纯正。

**2. 老年人和老年妇人。** 「劝老年人要有节制、端庄、自守，在信心、爱心、忍耐上都要纯全无疵。又劝老年妇人，举止行动要恭敬，不说谗言，不给酒作奴仆，用善道教训人。」（2:2-3）
- **年纪大不等于成熟：** 保罗没有因为他们年长就不提醒他们。老年人也会有老年人的试探：固执、尖刻、灰心。
- **「纯全」：** 原文就是健康的意思，和「纯正的道理」（1节）同一个词根。健康的道理，叫人在信心、爱心、忍耐上也健康。
- **老年妇人的服事：** 她们要「用善道教训人」，特别是指教少年妇人（4节）。在许多华人教会里，有经验的姊妹陪伴年轻的妻子和母亲，是很宝贵的服事。
- **「不说谗言，不给酒作奴仆」：** 这些在当时是很实际的问题。说别人闲话的人，最不适合作别人信得过的导师。

**3. 「好指教少年妇人」。** 「好指教少年妇人，爱丈夫，爱儿女，谨守，贞洁，料理家务，待人有恩，顺服自己的丈夫，免得神的道理被毁谤。」（2:4-5）
- **爱是可以学的：** 「爱丈夫，爱儿女」要人「指教」，可见爱不只是天生的感情，也是可以学习、需要操练的。
- **「免得神的道理被毁谤」：** 保罗关心外人怎样看教会。在当时的社会，一个基督徒妻子若轻看丈夫、不顾家庭，外人就会说这个新宗教破坏家庭。
- **对「料理家务」和「顺服」的不同理解：** 历代忠心的信徒对这里有不同的看法。认为家庭中夫妻角色男女有别的看法（常称为互补派，如乔治·奈特、托马斯·施莱纳）认为，这是神为婚姻所定的次序，丈夫要像基督爱教会那样爱妻子（弗5:25），妻子要顺服丈夫，这原则适用于各个时代。认为家庭中夫妻角色不分男女的看法（常称为平等派，如戈登·费依、克雷格·基纳）认为，保罗是照着当时的家庭结构，教导信徒怎样不让福音被毁谤，今天应用时要看处境；他们也强调以弗所书5:21「彼此顺服」。大多数两边的解经家都同意，「料理家务」不是一概禁止妇女在外工作（参箴31:16、24的才德妇人），不过互补派一般认为这节经文把家庭定为妻子首要的责任；两种看法也都同意丈夫和妻子同样尊贵（彼前3:7）。
- **顺服不是什么：** 顺服不是说妻子比丈夫低一等，不是说她要顺从丈夫去犯罪（徒5:29），也绝不是说她要忍受虐待。如果婚姻中有家暴、性暴力或其他虐待，向警察或法院求助是对的（罗13:4）；受害的人可以离开，到安全的地方去，不必为了「顺服」而留在危险中，也不必与不安全的人和好。教会的责任是保护受害者，绝不可为了面子、「家丑不可外扬」而劝她忍耐。

**4. 「又劝少年人要谨守」。** 「又劝少年人要谨守。」（2:6）
- **只有一个词：** 给少年人的吩咐最短，只有「谨守」，就是自制、头脑清醒。少年人最需要的，往往是管住自己的脾气、欲望、言语和冲动。
- **「谨守」这个词的家族：** 这个词根在这一章出现了好几次（2、4、5、6、12节）。无论老少男女，恩典都要教导人自守、有节制。

> 要点：纯正的道理会长出纯正的生活。老年人、少年人、男人、女人，各在自己的处境中活出福音，使神的道理不被毁谤。

## 深读 2:7-10 叫神的道理得尊荣

**1. 「你自己凡事要显出善行的榜样」。** 「你自己凡事要显出善行的榜样；在教训上要正直、端庄，言语纯全，无可指责，叫那反对的人，既无处可说我们的不是，便自觉羞愧。」（2:7-8）
- **提多自己：** 提多要劝少年人（6节），他自己先要作榜样。他不只要教导别人，自己先要活出来。
- **「无处可说我们的不是」：** 反对福音的人总会找把柄。保罗要信徒的生活和言语叫人挑不出毛病；即使有人仍然反对，他们也只能「自觉羞愧」。

**2. 「凡事尊荣我们救主－神的道」。** 「劝仆人要顺服自己的主人，凡事讨他的喜欢，不可顶撞他，不可私拿东西，要显为忠诚，以致凡事尊荣我们救主－神的道。」（2:9-10）
- **当时的处境：** 早期教会中有许多信徒是奴隶。保罗在这里没有讨论奴隶制度本身（这个问题可参看提摩太前书第6章），而是教导在这样处境中的信徒怎样作见证。
- **「凡事讨他的喜欢」：** 这当然不包括顺从主人去犯罪（徒5:29）。
- **「私拿东西」：** 这在当时的家奴中很常见，是人们对家奴常有的抱怨。基督徒奴隶却要「显为忠诚」，成为主人家中最可信的人。
- **「尊荣」：** 原文的意思是装饰、使美丽。信徒的生活，就像给神的道理加上装饰，叫人看见福音的美。
- **最卑微的人，最尊贵的任务：** 社会上最没有地位的人，却被交托了装饰「我们救主－神的道」的任务。
- **今天怎样应用？** 员工在工作中诚实、尽心，不私拿公司的东西，也是在装饰福音。但这不是叫人忍受违法的对待：遭受剥削、欠薪、虐待或性骚扰的人，可以寻求法律保护，向警察或有关部门举报（罗13:4），也可以离开不安全的工作环境。

> 要点：信徒的生活是给外人看的。即使是社会上最没有地位的人，也能用忠诚和善行装饰神的道，叫人看见我们救主的美。

## 深读 2:11-14 恩典的教导

**1. 「神救众人的恩典已经显明出来」。** 「因为神救众人的恩典已经显明出来。」（2:11）
- **「因为」：** 前面一切吩咐的根据就在这里。保罗不是只给信徒一张道德的清单，而是告诉他们：这样生活，是因为恩典已经来了。
- **「显明出来」：** 这主要指基督第一次来到世上，他的降生、受死和复活。「道成了肉身，住在我们中间，充充满满地有恩典有真理。」（约1:14）恩典不是一个抽象的观念，而是一个人：耶稣基督。
- **「救众人」：** 在上下文中，保罗刚刚讲到老年人、少年人、妇女、奴隶，各样的人；神的恩典不分年龄、性别、身份，都向他们显明。至于「众人」是否指每一个人都可以得救，历代信徒有不同的理解：改革宗传统（如加尔文对本节的注释）多理解为各样的人；许多改革宗神学家进一步认为，基督的死足够拯救所有人，却特别为选民成就救恩；阿米念传统（如卫斯理）理解为基督为每一个人死，救恩向每一个人敞开。两种看法都同意：福音要传给所有的人。

**2. 「教训我们除去……度日」。** 「教训我们除去不敬虔的心和世俗的情欲，在今世自守、公义、敬虔度日。」（2:12）
- **「教训」：** 原文是一个教养孩子的词，包括教导，也包括管教和训练（参来12:10-11）。恩典不只是在我们得救的那一天赦免了我们，它还像一位耐心的父亲，一天一天地教养我们。
- **两方面：** 「除去」不敬虔和世俗的情欲；又「自守、公义、敬虔度日」。自守是对自己，公义是对别人，敬虔是对神。
- **恩典不是放纵的借口：** 有人以为强调恩典，人就会随便犯罪。保罗说正好相反：真正的恩典会教导人离开罪。罗马书也说：「我们可以仍在罪中、叫恩典显多吗？断乎不可！」（罗6:1-2）

**3. 「至大的神和我们救主耶稣基督」。** 「等候所盼望的福，并等候至大的神和我们救主耶稣基督的荣耀显现。」（2:13）
- **两次显现：** 第11节说恩典「已经显明出来」，第13节说我们「等候」他的荣耀显现。信徒的生活，是在基督第一次来和第二次来之间，「在今世」敬虔度日。
- **一位还是两位？** 和合本「至大的神和我们救主耶稣基督」可以读成两位，也可以读成一位。英文钦定本的译法常被理解为两位。也有学者认为，「耶稣基督」是解释「荣耀」的，就是说基督本身是那位至大之神的荣耀。不过，许多学者（如默里·哈里斯）认为，按照原文的语法，「至大的神」和「救主」是同一位，就是耶稣基督；英文标准译本等现代译本也这样译。他们也指出，在教牧书信中，「显现」总是指基督的显现（提后4:1、8）。若是这样，这节经文直接称耶稣为「至大的神」，和新约其他承认基督是神的经文相合（约1:1；20:28；罗9:5；彼后1:1）。

**4. 「他为我们舍了自己」。** 「他为我们舍了自己，要赎我们脱离一切罪恶，又洁净我们，特作自己的子民，热心为善。」（2:14）
- **「为我们舍了自己」：** 恩典的核心是基督的十字架。他「来，不是要受人的服事，乃是要服事人，并且要舍命，作多人的赎价」（太20:28）。他代替我们受死，担当了我们罪的刑罚。
- **「赎我们脱离一切罪恶」：** 这叫人想起诗篇：「他必救赎以色列脱离一切的罪孽。」（诗130:8）基督救赎我们，不只是免去刑罚，也是要把我们从罪的辖制中买回来。
- **「特作自己的子民」：** 这是旧约称以色列的话：「耶和华从地上的万民中拣选你特作自己的子民。」（申14:2；参出19:5）彼得也用类似的话称呼教会（彼前2:9）。神看他的百姓为他自己的珍宝。
- **「热心为善」：** 被买回来的人，会热心行善。善行不是买回自己的价钱，而是被买回来以后的样子。

> 要点：恩典不只是赦免，也是一位老师。那位为我们舍了自己的基督，买我们作他自己的子民，又一天一天地教导我们离开罪、热心行善，等候他荣耀的显现。

## 深读 2:15 不可叫人轻看你

**「这些事你要讲明，劝戒人，用各等权柄责备人；不可叫人轻看你。」**（2:15）
- **「这些事」：** 指第2章所讲的一切，特别是11-14节的恩典福音。提多的权柄，不是来自他的年纪或口才，而是来自他所传讲的道。
- **「不可叫人轻看你」：** 和提摩太前书4:12相似，不过这里没有提到提多的年纪。提多不需要靠强硬的个性来压人，而是忠心讲明神的话，让神的话本身带着权柄。

## 默想

**默想：** 「他为我们舍了自己，要赎我们脱离一切罪恶，又洁净我们，特作自己的子民，热心为善。」（2:14）我常常觉得，自己在神面前像一件怎么修也修不好的旧东西。他应该对我很失望吧：信主这么多年，脾气还是这样，有些习惯改了又犯，读经祷告时冷时热。我好像总是在想，神什么时候会放下我，不再管我了。可是这节经文说的不是一件旧东西，而是「子民」，而且是「特作自己的子民」。旧约用这个词，形容神从万民中拣选以色列，看他们为自己的珍宝（申14:2）。神不是在摆弄一件修不好的东西，而是在教养他用重价买回来的子民。他为我「舍了自己」；他在我还作罪人的时候，就为我死了（罗5:8）。他付出的代价那么大，就不会因为我学得慢而放弃我。恩典「教训我们」（多2:12），原文这个词可以用在父亲教养孩子身上（参来12:7-11）。好的父亲教孩子，会纠正，会管教，却不会把孩子赶出家门。今天，我可以把那一件我改了又犯、最让我灰心的事，告诉这位教养我的主，求他继续教我。我不必先把自己修好，才配作他的子民；我已经是他的子民，所以他要教我。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 2 falls into three parts. **How people of every kind should live** (vv. 1–10): Titus is to teach what accords with sound doctrine; older men are to be sober-minded, dignified and self-controlled; older women are to be reverent and to train the young women; younger men are to be self-controlled; Titus himself is to be a model of good works; slaves are to show all good faith, so that in everything they may adorn the doctrine of God our Savior. **Grace that trains** (vv. 11–14): the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people, training us to renounce ungodliness and to live self-controlled, upright and godly lives in the present age, waiting for the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ, who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works. **Declare with authority** (v. 15): declare these things, exhort and rebuke; let no one disregard you.

The turning point of this chapter is the "for" of verse 11. The first ten verses describe many concrete ways of living, like a long list. Verse 11 gives the ground for all of it: not that we must be good people, but "for the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation." Grace does not only save us; day by day it is "training us."

## Going Deeper: 2:1–6 A Life That Accords with Sound Doctrine

**1. "But as for you, teach what accords with sound doctrine."** "But as for you, teach what accords with sound doctrine" (2:1).
- **"But as for you":** in contrast to the false teachers of chapter 1, who "profess to know God, but they deny him by their works" (1:16).
- **"Accords with":** what Paul goes on to describe is not doctrinal statements but ways of living. Sound doctrine always produces a sound life, and a life shows whether the doctrine a person holds is sound.

**2. Older men and older women.** "Older men are to be sober-minded, dignified, self-controlled, sound in faith, in love, and in steadfastness. Older women likewise are to be reverent in behavior, not slanderers or slaves to much wine. They are to teach what is good" (2:2–3).
- **Older is not the same as mature:** Paul does not spare the older men because of their age. Older people have their own temptations: stubbornness, sharpness, discouragement.
- **"Sound":** literally "healthy," the same root as "sound doctrine" (v. 1). Healthy doctrine makes people healthy in faith, love and steadfastness too.
- **Older women's ministry:** they are to "teach what is good," especially by training the younger women (v. 4). In many Chinese churches, experienced sisters who walk alongside young wives and mothers offer a precious ministry.
- **"Not slanderers or slaves to much wine":** these were very practical problems then. A person who gossips about others is the last person fit to be a trusted mentor.

**3. "And so train the young women."** "And so train the young women to love their husbands and children, to be self-controlled, pure, working at home, kind, and submissive to their own husbands, that the word of God may not be reviled" (2:4–5).
- **Love can be learned:** "to love their husbands and children" is something they are to be "trained" in. Love is not only a natural feeling; it can be learned and must be practiced.
- **"That the word of God may not be reviled":** Paul cares how outsiders see the church. In that society, if a Christian wife despised her husband and neglected her home, outsiders would say this new religion destroyed families.
- **Different readings of "working at home" and "submissive":** faithful believers have understood these words differently. The view that the roles of husband and wife in the home are distinguished by sex (often called complementarian; for example, George Knight and Thomas Schreiner) holds that this is the order God established for marriage: the husband is to love his wife as Christ loved the church (Eph 5:25), and the wife is to submit to her husband, a principle for every age. The view that the roles of husband and wife in the home are not distinguished by sex (often called egalitarian; for example, Gordon Fee and Craig Keener) holds that Paul is teaching believers within the family structure of their time how to keep the gospel from being reviled, and that today's application depends on the setting; they also stress Ephesians 5:21, "submitting to one another." Most interpreters on both sides agree that "working at home" is not a blanket ban on work outside the home (see the woman of strength in Prov 31:16, 24), though complementarians generally take it to make the home the wife's primary responsibility; both views agree that husband and wife are equally honored (1 Pet 3:7).
- **What submission is not:** submission does not mean a wife is inferior to her husband. It does not mean following her husband into sin (Acts 5:29). And it never means enduring abuse. If there is domestic violence, sexual violence or other abuse in a marriage, seeking help from the police or the courts is right (Rom 13:4). The victim may leave for a safe place; she need not stay in danger for the sake of "submission," nor reconcile with someone unsafe. The church's duty is to protect the victim, and it must never urge her to endure in order to save face ("don't air the family's dirty laundry").

**4. "Likewise, urge the younger men to be self-controlled."** "Likewise, urge the younger men to be self-controlled" (2:6).
- **Just one word:** the instruction to younger men is the shortest: "self-controlled," that is, disciplined and clear-headed. What young men most often need is to govern their tempers, desires, words and impulses.
- **A family of words:** this root appears several times in the chapter (vv. 2, 4, 5, 6, 12). Old and young, men and women, all are to be taught self-control by grace.

> The point: Sound doctrine grows into a sound life. Old and young, men and women, each lives out the gospel in their own setting, so that the word of God may not be reviled.

## Going Deeper: 2:7–10 Adorning the Doctrine of God

**1. "Show yourself in all respects to be a model of good works."** "Show yourself in all respects to be a model of good works, and in your teaching show integrity, dignity, and sound speech that cannot be condemned, so that an opponent may be put to shame, having nothing evil to say about us" (2:7–8).
- **Titus himself:** Titus is to urge the younger men (v. 6), and he must first be an example himself. He is not only to teach others, but first to live it.
- **"Having nothing evil to say about us":** those who oppose the gospel will always look for something to use. Paul wants believers' lives and words to leave no opening; even if some still oppose, they can only "be put to shame."

**2. "Adorn the doctrine of God our Savior."** "Slaves are to be submissive to their own masters in everything; they are to be well-pleasing, not argumentative, not pilfering, but showing all good faith, so that in everything they may adorn the doctrine of God our Savior" (2:9–10).
- **The setting:** many believers in the early church were slaves. Paul does not discuss slavery itself here (see 1 Timothy 6 on that question); he teaches believers in that situation how to bear witness.
- **"Well-pleasing... in everything":** this of course does not include following a master into sin (Acts 5:29).
- **"Pilfering":** this was common among household slaves, a common complaint about household slaves. Christian slaves were to show "all good faith," becoming the most trustworthy people in their master's house.
- **"Adorn":** the word means to decorate or make beautiful; the English word "cosmetic" comes from the same source. A believer's life is like ornament set on God's doctrine, so that people can see the beauty of the gospel.
- **The lowliest people, the highest task:** those with the least standing in society were entrusted with adorning "the doctrine of God our Savior."
- **How does it apply today?** Employees who are honest and wholehearted at work, who do not take company property, are also adorning the gospel. But this does not mean enduring unlawful treatment: those who suffer exploitation, unpaid wages, abuse or sexual harassment may seek the protection of the law and report it to the police or proper authorities (Rom 13:4), and may leave an unsafe workplace.

> The point: A believer's life is seen by outsiders. Even those with the least standing in society can adorn the doctrine of God with faithfulness and good works, so that people see the beauty of our Savior.

## Going Deeper: 2:11–14 Grace That Trains

**1. "For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people."** "For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people" (2:11).
- **"For":** here is the ground of all the instructions before it. Paul does not just hand believers a moral checklist; he tells them they live this way because grace has come.
- **"Has appeared":** this refers mainly to Christ's first coming: his birth, death and resurrection. "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us... full of grace and truth" (John 1:14). Grace is not an abstract idea but a person: Jesus Christ.
- **"For all people":** in context, Paul has just spoken of older men, younger men, women and slaves, people of every kind; God's grace has appeared to them all, whatever their age, sex or status. Whether "all people" means that every person may be saved has been understood differently. The Reformed tradition (for example, Calvin on this verse) generally reads "all kinds of people"; many Reformed theologians add that Christ's death is sufficient to save all, yet accomplishes salvation in particular for the elect. The Arminian tradition (for example, Wesley) understands it as Christ dying for every person, with salvation open to every person. Both agree that the gospel is to be preached to all.

**2. "Training us to renounce... and to live."** "Training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in the present age" (2:12).
- **"Training":** the Greek word is used for bringing up a child. It includes teaching, and also discipline and training (see Heb 12:10–11). Grace did not only forgive us on the day we were saved; like a patient father, it brings us up day by day.
- **Two sides:** "renounce" ungodliness and worldly passions; and "live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives." Self-control concerns ourselves, uprightness our neighbors, godliness God.
- **Grace is not an excuse for license:** some think that if grace is stressed, people will sin freely. Paul says the opposite: real grace trains people to leave sin. Romans says, "Are we to continue in sin that grace may abound? By no means!" (Rom 6:1–2).

**3. "Our great God and Savior Jesus Christ."** "Waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ" (2:13).
- **Two appearings:** verse 11 says grace "has appeared"; verse 13 says we are "waiting for" the appearing of his glory. Believers live "in the present age," between Christ's first coming and his second, living godly lives.
- **One person or two?** The Chinese Union Version can be read as two persons or one. The KJV wording ("the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ") is commonly read as two persons. Some scholars take "Jesus Christ" as explaining "the glory": Christ himself is the glory of the great God who appears. But many modern scholars (such as Murray Harris) hold that, by the grammar of the Greek, "great God" and "Savior" refer to one person, Jesus Christ, and the ESV and other modern translations render it this way. They also note that in the Pastoral Letters the "appearing" is always Christ's (2 Tim 4:1, 8). If so, this verse directly calls Jesus "our great God," in keeping with the other New Testament texts that confess Christ as God (John 1:1; 20:28; Rom 9:5; 2 Pet 1:1).

**4. "Who gave himself for us."** "Who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works" (2:14).
- **"Gave himself for us":** the heart of grace is Christ's cross. He "came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many" (Matt 20:28). He died in our place, bearing the penalty of our sin.
- **"To redeem us from all lawlessness":** this recalls the psalm: "And he will redeem Israel from all his iniquities" (Ps 130:8). Christ redeems us not only from the penalty but from the power of sin, buying us back from its rule.
- **"A people for his own possession":** these are Old Testament words for Israel: "the LORD has chosen you to be a people for his treasured possession, out of all the peoples who are on the face of the earth" (Deut 14:2; see Exod 19:5). Peter uses similar words of the church (1 Pet 2:9). God regards his people as his own treasure.
- **"Zealous for good works":** those who have been bought back are eager to do good. Good works are not the price of buying ourselves back; they are what the bought-back look like.

> The point: Grace is not only forgiveness but a teacher. Christ, who gave himself for us, bought us to be his own people, and day by day trains us to leave sin and to be zealous for good works, as we wait for the appearing of his glory.

## Going Deeper: 2:15 Let No One Disregard You

**"Declare these things; exhort and rebuke with all authority. Let no one disregard you"** (2:15).
- **"These things":** everything in chapter 2, especially the gospel of grace in verses 11–14. Titus's authority does not come from his age or his eloquence, but from the word he proclaims.
- **"Let no one disregard you":** similar to 1 Timothy 4:12, though nothing here mentions Titus's age. Titus does not need a forceful personality to press people down; he faithfully declares God's word, and lets the word itself carry its authority.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "Who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works" (2:14). I often feel like an old thing before God that never quite gets fixed. He must be very disappointed in me: after all these years of faith, my temper is still the same, some habits I break and then fall back into, my Bible reading and prayer run hot and cold. I seem always to be waiting for the day God puts me down and stops bothering with me. But this verse does not speak of an old thing. It speaks of a "people," "a people for his own possession." The Old Testament used these words for Israel, chosen out of all the peoples to be God's treasured possession (Deut 14:2). God is not tinkering with something that will not stay fixed; he is bringing up a people he bought at great cost. He "gave himself" for me; "while we were still sinners, Christ died for us" (Rom 5:8). The price he paid was so great that he will not give up on me because I learn slowly. Grace is "training us" (Titus 2:12), a word that can be used of a father bringing up his child (see Heb 12:7–11). A good father teaches, corrects and disciplines his child, but does not throw him out of the house. Today I can tell this Lord who is bringing me up about the one thing I keep falling back into, the thing that discourages me most, and ask him to keep teaching me. I do not have to fix myself first in order to qualify as one of his people. I am already his, and that is why he is teaching me.
