## 本章脉络

第三章分成四段。**在社会上作见证**（1-2节）：要提醒众人顺服作官的、掌权的，预备行各样的善事；不要毁谤，不要争竞，总要和平，向众人大显温柔。**神的恩慈和慈爱显明**（3-7节）：我们从前也是无知、悖逆、彼此相恨；但到了神我们的救主显明他恩慈和慈爱的时候，他便救了我们，不是因我们所行的义，乃是照他的怜悯，藉着重生的洗和圣灵的更新；好叫我们因他的恩得称为义，成为后嗣。**这话是可信的**（8-11节）：要切切实实地讲明这些事，使信神的人留心做正经事业；要远避无知的辩论；分门结党的人，警戒过一两次，就要弃绝他。**最后的嘱咐**（12-15节）：保罗要提多到尼哥坡里去见他；要为西纳和亚波罗送行；我们的人要学习正经事业，免得不结果子；愿恩惠常与众人同在。

这一章的中心是3-7节。保罗吩咐信徒「向众人大显温柔」（2节），然后马上说出原因：「我们从前也是」那样的人（3节）。信徒能温柔地对待外人，是因为记得自己从前是怎样的人，也记得神是怎样照他的怜悯救了我们。

## 深读 3:1-2 向众人大显温柔

**1. 「顺服作官的、掌权的」。** 「你要提醒众人，叫他们顺服作官的、掌权的，遵他的命，预备行各样的善事。」（3:1）
- **「提醒」：** 这不是新的教导，克里特的信徒以前已经听过。保罗在别处也这样教导（罗13:1；参彼前2:13-14）。
- **为什么要顺服？** 掌权的是神所设立的，为要维持秩序、罚恶赏善（罗13:1-4）。保罗也叫提摩太「为君王和一切在位的」祷告（提前2:1-2）。
- **顺服的界限：** 顺服掌权的，不是说他们的命令高过神的命令。当掌权的禁止传福音、要人拜偶像或作恶的时候，使徒说：「顺从神，不顺从人，是应当的。」（徒5:29）在什么情况下可以不顺从、怎样不顺从，历代信徒看法不一。但大家都同意：即使不顺从，信徒也要存温柔的心，不毁谤，不用暴力报复，甘心为主受苦。这并不排除依法申诉、寻求法律保护，或者躲避逼迫（徒16:37；25:11；太10:23）。
- **「预备行各样的善事」：** 信徒在社会上不只是守法的人，也是主动行善的人：照顾邻舍，帮助有需要的人，为城市求平安。

**2. 「不要毁谤，不要争竞」。** 「不要毁谤，不要争竞，总要和平，向众人大显温柔。」（3:2）
- **「向众人」：** 不只是对信徒，也包括不信的人，包括反对我们、与我们政见不同的人。
- **「毁谤」：** 原文就是「亵渎」这个词，用在人身上，就是用恶毒的话攻击人。今天在网上，信徒也很容易跟着别人一起骂人、嘲笑人、给人贴标签。但不毁谤不等于不说真话：如实说出自己受到的伤害，向警方或有关方面举报（罗13:4），不是毁谤；温柔也不是要受害的人继续留在危险中。
- **「大显温柔」：** 温柔不是没有立场，而是坚持真理，同时尊重、体谅人。彼得说，要「以温柔、敬畏的心」回答问我们盼望缘由的人（彼前3:15）。

> 要点：信徒在社会上要顺服掌权的、预备行善，向众人大显温柔。我们能这样，不是因为自己比别人好，而是因为「我们从前也是」那样，神却照他的怜悯救了我们（3:3-5）。温柔不是软弱，而是蒙了怜悯的人对待别人的样子。

## 深读 3:3-7 照他的怜悯救了我们

**1. 「我们从前也是」。** 「我们从前也是无知、悖逆、受迷惑、服事各样私欲，和宴乐，常存恶毒嫉妒的心，是可恨的，又是彼此相恨。」（3:3）
- **「我们」：** 保罗把自己也算在内。他从前是热心的法利赛人，在别人眼中是很有道德的人；可是他说，自己从前也是「无知、悖逆」的。以弗所书也说：「我们从前也都在他们中间……和别人一样。」（弗2:3）
- **为什么在这里说？** 第2节刚叫信徒「向众人大显温柔」。信徒看不信的人，很容易生出优越感。保罗提醒他们：你们和他们本来是一样的；你们今天不同，不是因为你们比他们好，而是因为神救了你们。
- **「彼此相恨」：** 罪不只是个人的问题，也会破坏人与人的关系。

**2. 「神－我们救主的恩慈和他向人所施的慈爱显明」。** 「但到了神－我们救主的恩慈和他向人所施的慈爱显明的时候，他便救了我们。」（3:4-5上）
- **「但」：** 这是全段的转折。人的光景是那样黑暗，「但」神的恩慈显明了。
- **「向人所施的慈爱」：** 原文的意思是对人的爱。当时的人常用这个词称赞君王和施恩的权贵。保罗说，真正爱人的，是神我们的救主。
- **「显明」：** 和多2:11一样，主要指基督的降生、受死和复活。神的爱不是停留在言语上，而是在基督身上显明出来。

**3. 「并不是因我们自己所行的义，乃是照他的怜悯」。** 「并不是因我们自己所行的义，乃是照他的怜悯，藉着重生的洗和圣灵的更新。」（3:5）
- **不是因为行为：** 保罗说得非常清楚：神救我们，不是因为我们「所行的义」，连我们自以为做得最好的事，也不能算数。神救我们，完全是出于他的怜悯（参弗2:4-5）。
- **「重生的洗和圣灵的更新」：** 「重生」就是从神而生，得着新的生命（参约3:3、5）。「洗」叫人想起以西结书的应许：「我必用清水洒在你们身上，你们就洁净了」（结36:25），接着神应许赐下新心和新灵（结36:26-27）。

**4. 「重生的洗」是指洗礼吗？** 历代信徒对这一点有不同的理解：
- **认为神藉着洗礼使人重生的看法：** 天主教（《天主教教理》1215直接引用这节经文称洗礼为「重生的洗」）认为，洗礼本身带来重生；信义宗（马丁·路德的《小要理问答》论洗礼时引用多3:5-8）也认为神藉着洗礼使人重生，但强调不是水本身，而是与水同在的神的话，以及信靠这话的信心。
- **认为洗礼是重生的记号和印证的看法：** 改革宗（如《威斯敏斯德信条》第二十八章）认为，这里的「洗」和洗礼有关，洗礼表明并印证圣灵洗净人、使人重生的工作；但洗礼的功效不一定和施洗的那一刻相连，人得救是因着信。
- **认为这里主要指圣灵洁净的看法：** 浸信会和许多福音派解经家认为，「洗」主要是比喻圣灵洁净人心的工作，洗礼是信徒对这工作的公开见证。
- **共同点：** 无论哪一种看法，都同意这里的重点不是人做了什么，而是神做了什么：是神「照他的怜悯」救了我们，是圣灵使人重生、更新。

**5. 「好叫我们因他的恩得称为义」。** 「圣灵就是神藉着耶稣基督－我们救主厚厚浇灌在我们身上的，好叫我们因他的恩得称为义，可以凭着永生的盼望成为后嗣。」（3:6-7）
- **「厚厚浇灌」：** 这叫人想起约珥的应许：「我要将我的灵浇灌凡有血气的」（珥2:28）。五旬节那天，彼得说复活升天的基督「从父受了所应许的圣灵，就把你们所看见所听见的，浇灌下来」（徒2:33）。圣灵不是一点一点地给，而是「厚厚」地浇灌。
- **三位一体的救恩：** 父「照他的怜悯」救我们，子是我们的「救主」，圣灵使我们重生更新。
- **「因他的恩得称为义」：** 我们在神面前被宣告为义，不是因为我们的义，而是因为他的恩典，因为基督为我们舍了自己（多2:14）；我们是藉着信领受这恩典（罗3:24-25；5:1）。
- **「后嗣」：** 我们不只是被赦免的罪人，还成了神家的儿女和后嗣（参罗8:17；加4:7），盼望承受永生。

> 要点：我们从前和别人一样，无知、悖逆、彼此相恨。神救我们，不是因为我们所行的义，而是照他的怜悯：父施怜悯，子为我们舍己，圣灵使我们重生；我们因他的恩得称为义，成为后嗣。

## 深读 3:8-11 这话是可信的

**1. 「留心做正经事业」。** 「这话是可信的。我也愿你把这些事切切实实地讲明，使那些已信神的人留心做正经事业。这都是美事，并且与人有益。」（3:8）
- **第五句「可信的话」：** 按新约书卷的排列次序，这是教牧书信中的第五句「可信的话」（参提前1:15；3:1；4:9；提后2:11），很可能指的是前面3-7节的福音总结。
- **「正经事业」：** 原文就是「善事」。和合本在这里和14节译作「正经事业」；这个词也可以理解为好的行为、正当的工作。
- **次序：** 先讲神的怜悯和恩典（3-7节），再讲行善（8节）。恩典是根，善行是果；倒过来就成了靠行为得救。

**2. 「要远避无知的辩论」。** 「要远避无知的辩论和家谱的空谈，以及纷争，并因律法而起的争竞，因为这都是虚妄无益的。」（3:9）
- **对比：** 第8节说善事「与人有益」，第9节说这些辩论「虚妄无益」。保罗不是反对思考和讨论，而是反对那些不叫人更爱神、更爱人，只叫人更会争吵的辩论。

**3. 「分门结党的人」。** 「分门结党的人，警戒过一两次，就要弃绝他。因为知道这等人已经背道，犯了罪，自己明知不是，还是去做。」（3:10-11）
- **「分门结党的人」：** 原文就是后来「异端」一词的来源，但在这里主要指在教会中制造分裂、拉帮结派的人（参罗16:17；林前1:10）。
- **先警戒，再弃绝：** 教会不是一发现问题就把人赶出去，而是先警戒，给他悔改的机会；这和耶稣在马太福音18:15-17所教导的步骤相合。若他仍不肯回头，就不再与他来往，免得他继续拆毁教会。
- **哪些不算分门结党：** 这节经文常被误用。指出教会中的错误教导，或者说出教会里的性侵、虐待、财务不正，不是分门结党，而是光明的子女当做的事（参弗5:11）。教会不可用破坏合一的罪名，去压制举报的人、保护施害的人。受害的人向警方举报是正当的（罗13:4）。

> 要点：福音的根产生善行的果子。教会要切切实实地讲明神的怜悯，使信徒留心行善；也要远避无益的争论，警戒分裂教会的人，却不可把说出真相的人当作分裂者。

## 深读 3:12-15 免得不结果子

**1. 最后的安排。** 「我打发亚提马或是推基古到你那里去的时候，你要赶紧往尼哥坡里去见我，因为我已经定意在那里过冬。你要赶紧给律师西纳和亚波罗送行，叫他们没有缺乏。」（3:12-13）
- **亚提马和推基古：** 亚提马只在这里出现。推基古常为保罗送信、传消息（弗6:21；西4:7；提后4:12）。保罗会派其中一位去接替提多在克里特的工作。
- **尼哥坡里：** 很可能是希腊西北部的尼哥坡里，是冬天船只停航时适合停留的城市。
- **西纳和亚波罗：** 西纳是一位「律师」，可能是精通犹太律法的人，也可能是罗马法律方面的专家。亚波罗是一位很有学问、「最能讲解圣经」的人（徒18:24），曾在哥林多浇灌保罗所栽种的（林前3:6）。他们可能就是带这封信到克里特的人。
- **「叫他们没有缺乏」：** 为路过的传道人提供旅费和所需的，是早期教会实际的爱心。

**2. 「我们的人要学习正经事业」。** 「并且我们的人要学习正经事业，预备所需用的，免得不结果子。」（3:14）
- **「学习」：** 行善是要学习的。克里特人素有「又馋又懒」的名声（多1:12）；信徒如今却要学习为别人「预备所需用的」。
- **「免得不结果子」：** 有信心的人会结果子。善行不是得救的条件，却是生命的证据。果子是慢慢长的；为自己结果子太少而不安的人，可以回头看根：神「照他的怜悯」救了我们（3:5）。

**3. 「愿恩惠常与你们众人同在」。** 「同我在一处的人都问你安。请代问那些因有信心爱我们的人安。愿恩惠常与你们众人同在！」（3:15）
- **「你们众人」：** 这封信写给提多，看来也是要让克里特各处的教会听到的。
- **以恩惠结束：** 这封讲了许多善行的信，最后一句仍是恩惠。善行从恩惠开始，也靠着恩惠持续。

## 默想

**默想：** 「我们从前也是无知、悖逆、受迷惑、服事各样私欲，和宴乐，常存恶毒嫉妒的心，是可恨的，又是彼此相恨。但到了神－我们救主的恩慈和他向人所施的慈爱显明的时候，他便救了我们；并不是因我们自己所行的义，乃是照他的怜悯。」（3:3-5）我有时候看不起不信的人。同事在饭桌上讲下流的笑话，邻居为了一点小事骂街，有个亲戚一辈子只为吃喝玩乐活着，我心里就会冒出一句：这些人真是……有时我嘴上没说，心里却像那个法利赛人一样祷告：「神啊，我感谢你，我不像别人。」（路18:11）保罗在这里没有叫我去比较，而是叫我回头看：「我们从前也是」。我也曾经是无知的，也曾被私欲牵着走，心里也有嫉妒和恨。我今天不一样，不是因为我比他们聪明、比他们有道德，而是因为神「照他的怜悯」救了我。我连自己做得最好的那些事，也不能拿来在神面前邀功。基督为我舍了自己，神把圣灵厚厚地浇灌在我身上，赐我新的生命。今天，我可以想起一个我看不起的人，先为自己的骄傲向主认罪；然后为那个人祷告，求神也向他显明他的恩慈和慈爱，就像当初向我显明的一样。明天我可能又会看不起人；但救我的那怜悯，本来就不是因我所行的义，也不会因我明天的骄傲收回。如果神也向那个人显明恩慈，将来我们要一同作后嗣，一同承受永生（3:7）。

## The Shape of the Chapter

Chapter 3 falls into four parts. **Witness in society** (vv. 1–2): remind them to be submissive to rulers and authorities and ready for every good work; to speak evil of no one, to avoid quarreling, to be gentle, and to show perfect courtesy toward all people. **The goodness and loving kindness of God appeared** (vv. 3–7): we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, hating one another; but when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit, so that being justified by his grace we might become heirs. **The saying is trustworthy** (vv. 8–11): insist on these things, so that those who have believed in God may devote themselves to good works; avoid foolish controversies; warn a divisive person once and twice, then have nothing more to do with him. **Final instructions** (vv. 12–15): Paul asks Titus to come to him at Nicopolis; speed Zenas and Apollos on their way; let our people learn to devote themselves to good works, so as not to be unfruitful; grace be with you all.

The center of this chapter is verses 3–7. Paul tells believers to "show perfect courtesy toward all people" (v. 2), and immediately gives the reason: "we ourselves were once" like that (v. 3). Believers can be gentle toward outsiders because they remember what they once were, and how God saved them according to his own mercy.

## Going Deeper: 3:1–2 Perfect Courtesy Toward All People

**1. "Be submissive to rulers and authorities."** "Remind them to be submissive to rulers and authorities, to be obedient, to be ready for every good work" (3:1).
- **"Remind":** this was not new teaching; the believers in Crete had heard it before. Paul teaches the same elsewhere (Rom 13:1; see 1 Pet 2:13–14).
- **Why submit?** Governing authorities are instituted by God to keep order, punish evil and praise good (Rom 13:1–4). Paul also told Timothy to pray "for kings and all who are in high positions" (1 Tim 2:1–2).
- **The limits of submission:** submitting to the authorities does not mean their commands rank above God's. When authorities forbid the preaching of the gospel, or require idolatry or evil, the apostles said, "We must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29). Believers have differed over when and how they may refuse to obey; but all agree that when believers do refuse, they are to keep a gentle spirit, not slander, not retaliate with violence, and be willing to suffer for Christ's sake; this does not rule out lawful appeal, seeking legal protection, or fleeing persecution (Acts 16:37; 25:11; Matt 10:23).
- **"Ready for every good work":** believers in society are not only law-abiding but actively do good: caring for neighbors, helping those in need, seeking the welfare of the city.

**2. "Speak evil of no one, avoid quarreling."** "To speak evil of no one, to avoid quarreling, to be gentle, and to show perfect courtesy toward all people" (3:2).
- **"Toward all people":** not only toward believers, but toward unbelievers too, including those who oppose us and those whose politics differ from ours.
- **"Speak evil":** the Greek is the word for "blaspheme"; used of people, it means attacking someone with vicious words. Online today it is easy for believers to join others in insulting, mocking and labeling people. But not slandering is not the same as staying silent: telling the truth about harm done to you, or reporting it to the police or proper authorities (Rom 13:4), is not slander; and gentleness does not require a victim to remain in danger.
- **"Perfect courtesy":** gentleness is not having no convictions; it is treating people with respect and consideration while holding to the truth. Peter says to answer those who ask about our hope "with gentleness and respect" (1 Pet 3:15–16).

> The point: Believers in society are to submit to the authorities, be ready for every good work and show perfect courtesy toward all. We can do this not because we are better than others but because "we ourselves were once" like them, and God saved us according to his mercy (3:3–5). Gentleness is not weakness; it is how people who have received mercy treat others.

## Going Deeper: 3:3–7 According to His Own Mercy, He Saved Us

**1. "We ourselves were once."** "For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, slaves to various passions and pleasures, passing our days in malice and envy, hated by others and hating one another" (3:3).
- **"We":** Paul includes himself. He had been a zealous Pharisee, a very moral man in others' eyes, yet he says that he too was once "foolish, disobedient." Ephesians says the same: "among whom we all once lived... like the rest of mankind" (Eph 2:3).
- **Why say it here?** Verse 2 has just told believers to show courtesy toward all people. Believers can easily feel superior to unbelievers. Paul reminds them: you were just like them; you are different today not because you are better, but because God saved you.
- **"Hating one another":** sin is not only a private matter; it breaks relationships between people.

**2. "When the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared."** "But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved us" (3:4–5a).
- **"But":** this is the turning point of the passage. The human condition was that dark, "but" God's goodness appeared.
- **"Loving kindness":** the Greek is *philanthrōpia*, the source of the English "philanthropy," meaning love for humankind. People then often used the word to praise the kindness of kings and benefactors. Paul says the one who truly loves humankind is God our Savior.
- **"Appeared":** as in Titus 2:11, this refers mainly to Christ's birth, death and resurrection. God's love did not stay in words; it appeared in Christ.

**3. "Not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy."** "Not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit" (3:5).
- **Not because of works:** Paul could not be clearer: God saved us not because of "works done by us in righteousness," not even the things we think we did well. The ground lies wholly in his mercy (see Eph 2:4–5).
- **"The washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit":** "regeneration" means being born of God, receiving new life (see John 3:3, 5). "Washing" recalls Ezekiel's promise: "I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean" (Ezek 36:25), followed by the promise of a new heart and a new spirit (Ezek 36:26–27).

**4. Is "the washing of regeneration" baptism?** Believers have understood this differently:
- **The view that God regenerates through baptism:** the Catholic Church (the *Catechism of the Catholic Church* 1215 quotes this verse in calling baptism "the washing of regeneration") holds that baptism itself brings about new birth; the Lutheran tradition (Luther's *Small Catechism* quotes Titus 3:5–8 on baptism) also holds that God regenerates through baptism, but stresses that it is not the water itself but the Word of God in and with the water, received by faith.
- **The view that baptism is the sign and seal of regeneration:** the Reformed tradition (for example, the Westminster Confession, chapter 28) holds that the "washing" here is connected with baptism, which signifies and seals the Spirit's work of cleansing and regeneration; but the efficacy of baptism is not tied to the moment it is administered, and people are saved through faith.
- **The view that this refers mainly to the Spirit's cleansing:** Baptists and many evangelical commentators hold that "washing" is chiefly a picture of the Spirit's cleansing of the heart, and that baptism is the believer's public witness to that work.
- **What they share:** on every view, the emphasis here is not on what we have done but on what God has done: God saved us "according to his own mercy," and the Spirit regenerates and renews.

**5. "So that being justified by his grace."** "Whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior, so that being justified by his grace we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life" (3:6–7).
- **"Poured out on us richly":** this recalls Joel's promise: "I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh" (Joel 2:28). At Pentecost Peter said that the risen and exalted Christ, "having received from the Father the promise of the Holy Spirit, he has poured out this that you yourselves are seeing and hearing" (Acts 2:33). The Spirit is not given sparingly but poured out "richly."
- **A Trinitarian salvation:** the Father saves us "according to his own mercy," the Son is our "Savior," and the Spirit regenerates and renews us.
- **"Justified by his grace":** we are declared righteous before God not because of our righteousness but because of his grace, because Christ gave himself for us (Titus 2:14); we receive this grace through faith (Rom 3:24–25; 5:1).
- **"Heirs":** we are not only forgiven sinners but children and heirs in God's household (see Rom 8:17; Gal 4:7), waiting in hope to inherit eternal life.

> The point: We were once like everyone else, foolish, disobedient, hating one another. God saved us not because of works done in righteousness, but according to his own mercy: the Father showed mercy, the Son gave himself for us, the Spirit gave us new birth. We are justified by his grace and made heirs.

## Going Deeper: 3:8–11 The Saying Is Trustworthy

**1. "Devote themselves to good works."** "The saying is trustworthy, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed in God may be careful to devote themselves to good works. These things are excellent and profitable for people" (3:8).
- **The fifth "trustworthy saying":** in canonical order, this is the fifth of the "trustworthy sayings" in the Pastoral Letters (see 1 Tim 1:15; 3:1; 4:9; 2 Tim 2:11), and it most likely refers to the gospel summary in verses 3–7.
- **"Good works":** the Chinese Union Version renders this here and in verse 14 as "proper occupations" (正经事业); it can be understood as good deeds or honest work.
- **The order:** first God's mercy and grace (vv. 3–7), then good works (v. 8). Grace is the root, good works the fruit; reverse the order and it becomes salvation by works.

**2. "Avoid foolish controversies."** "But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, dissensions, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and worthless" (3:9).
- **A contrast:** verse 8 says good works are "profitable for people"; verse 9 says these controversies are "unprofitable and worthless." Paul is not against thinking and discussion, but against debates that do not make people love God and others more, only argue better.

**3. "A person who stirs up division."** "As for a person who stirs up division, after warning him once and then twice, have nothing more to do with him, knowing that such a person is warped and sinful; he is self-condemned" (3:10–11).
- **"A person who stirs up division":** the Greek word is the source of the later word "heretic," but here it mainly means someone who causes division and forms factions in the church (see Rom 16:17; 1 Cor 1:10).
- **First warn, then refuse:** the church does not expel someone as soon as a problem appears. It warns first, giving him a chance to repent, in keeping with the steps Jesus taught in Matthew 18:15–17. If he still will not turn back, the church stops associating with him, so that he does not keep tearing it down.
- **Who is not "a person who stirs up division":** this verse is often misused. Pointing out false teaching in the church, or speaking up about sexual abuse, other abuse or financial wrongdoing in the church, is not stirring up division; it is what children of light should do (see Eph 5:11). The church must not use the charge of "destroying unity" to silence those who report wrongdoing and protect those who do harm. It is right for victims to report to the police (Rom 13:4).

> The point: The root of the gospel bears the fruit of good works. The church must insist on God's mercy so that believers devote themselves to good works; it must avoid worthless quarrels and warn those who divide the church, but never treat those who speak the truth as the dividers.

## Going Deeper: 3:12–15 So as Not to Be Unfruitful

**1. Final arrangements.** "When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, do your best to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there. Do your best to speed Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their way; see that they lack nothing" (3:12–13).
- **Artemas and Tychicus:** Artemas appears only here. Tychicus often carried Paul's letters and news (Eph 6:21; Col 4:7; 2 Tim 4:12). Paul would send one of them to take over Titus's work in Crete.
- **Nicopolis:** most likely the Nicopolis in northwestern Greece, a suitable city to stay in when winter stopped shipping.
- **Zenas and Apollos:** Zenas was a "lawyer," perhaps an expert in Jewish law, perhaps in Roman law. Apollos was a learned man, "competent in the Scriptures" (Acts 18:24), who had watered what Paul planted in Corinth (1 Cor 3:6). They may have carried this letter to Crete.
- **"See that they lack nothing":** providing travel funds and necessities for passing preachers was practical love in the early church.

**2. "Let our people learn to devote themselves to good works."** "And let our people learn to devote themselves to good works, so as to help cases of urgent need, and not be unfruitful" (3:14).
- **"Learn":** doing good has to be learned. Cretans had a reputation as "lazy gluttons" (Titus 1:12); believers are now to learn to meet others' "urgent need."
- **"Not be unfruitful":** people of faith bear fruit. Good works are not the condition of salvation, but they are the evidence of life. Fruit grows slowly; whoever is troubled by how little fruit they see should look back to the root: God saved us "according to his own mercy" (3:5).

**3. "Grace be with you all."** "All who are with me send greetings to you. Greet those who love us in the faith. Grace be with you all" (3:15).
- **"You all":** the letter is addressed to Titus, but it was evidently meant also for the churches throughout Crete.
- **Ending with grace:** a letter that has said so much about good works ends, once again, with grace. Good works begin from grace and continue by grace.

## Meditation

**Reflect:** "For we ourselves were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, slaves to various passions and pleasures, passing our days in malice and envy, hated by others and hating one another. But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy" (3:3–5). Sometimes I look down on unbelievers. A colleague tells a crude joke over lunch, a neighbor shouts curses in the street over something small, a relative lives only for eating, drinking and fun, and a thought rises in me: these people are just... Sometimes I say nothing aloud, but in my heart I pray like the Pharisee: "God, I thank you that I am not like other men" (Luke 18:11). Paul does not ask me to compare here; he asks me to look back: "we ourselves were once." I too was once foolish, led along by my desires, with envy and hatred in my heart. I am different today not because I am smarter or more moral than they are, but because God saved me "according to his own mercy." Even the things I did best I cannot bring before God as credit. Christ gave himself for me, and God poured out the Spirit richly on me, giving me new life. Today I can think of one person I look down on, first confess my pride to the Lord, and then pray for that person, asking God to show them his goodness and loving kindness, as he once showed it to me. Tomorrow I may look down on someone again; but the mercy that saved me was never because of works done by me in righteousness, and it will not be withdrawn when my pride shows up again. If God shows that person his kindness too, one day we will be heirs together of eternal life (3:7).
